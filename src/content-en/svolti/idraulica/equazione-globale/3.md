## Exercise 1

A water jet of diameter $d = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ leaves a nozzle at speed $v = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and strikes a stationary flat plate perpendicular to its axis. The water spreads in sheets along the plate. Find the discharge and the force exerted by the jet on the plate shown.

::schema{acqua="0,1.1 4.3,1.1 4.3,1.5 0,1.5;4.1,1.5 4.3,1.5 4.3,3 4.1,3;4.1,1.1 4.3,1.1 4.3,-0.4 4.1,-0.4" solidi="4.3,-0.6 4.7,-0.6 4.7,3.2 4.3,3.2" frecce="0.6,1.3,1.8,1.3,v = 12 m/s" etichette="1.2,1.75,d = 50 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Jet striking a stationary flat plate at normal incidence"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi d^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.050^2}{4} = 0.001963\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
Q = A\,v = 0.001963 \cdot 12 = 0.02356\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 23.6\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Take $x$ along the incoming jet. Gauge pressure is zero at the flow sections, so the pressure forces there vanish. Neglect the weight. The water sheets leave parallel to the plate and carry no momentum in the $x$ direction.

$$
\rho\,Q\,(0 - v) = R_x
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
R_x = -\rho\,Q\,v
$$
$$
S = -R_x = \rho\,Q\,v = 1000 \cdot 0.02356 \cdot 12 = 283\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Check using the equivalent expression $\rho A v^2$.
$$
\rho\,A\,v^2 = 1000 \cdot 0.001963 \cdot 12^2 = 283\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The control volume encloses the end of the jet and the sheets of water on the plate. Its boundary is in contact with air or the plate, and the gauge pressure at every flow section is zero. The momentum balance therefore contains only the momentum flux and the force exerted by the plate.

The incoming momentum flux in the $x$ direction is $\rho Q v$. The outgoing sheets flow along the plate, so their velocities have no component along $x$ and their momentum flux in that direction is zero. The net outward momentum flux is therefore $-\rho Q v$.

The jet exerts $283\,\mathrm{N}$ on the plate in the direction of the incoming flow. Holding the plate stationary requires an external force of equal magnitude in the opposite direction.

The same force can be written as $\rho A v^2$, which shows that it varies with speed squared. At fixed jet diameter, doubling the speed quadruples the force.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A water jet of diameter $d = 40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes a smooth, stationary flat plate inclined at angle $\alpha = 30^\circ$ to the jet axis. Neglecting friction on the plate, determine the force exerted by the jet and the discharges in the two sheets of water that leave the plate, as shown.

::schema{acqua="0,1 3.65,1 4.35,1.4 0,1.4;4.35,1.40 5.65,2.15 5.51,2.38 4.21,1.63;3.65,1.00 2.92,0.58 2.86,0.68 3.59,1.10" solidi="2.98,0.27 5.75,1.87 5.60,2.12 2.83,0.53" frecce="0.6,1.2,1.8,1.2,v = 15 m/s" etichette="1.2,1.65,d = 40 mm;4.9,0.75,α = 30°" riferimento=0 titolo="Jet striking a flat plate inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$ to the jet axis"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.040^2}{4} = 0.001257\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
Q = 0.001257 \cdot 15 = 0.01885\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 18.85\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,v = 1000 \cdot 0.01885 \cdot 15 = 283\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Take $\xi$ parallel to the plate in the direction of the larger outgoing sheet, and $\eta$ normal to the plate. The smooth plate exerts no force in the $\xi$ direction.

$$
\begin{cases}
\rho\,Q_1\,v - \rho\,Q_2\,v = \rho\,Q\,v\,\cos\alpha \\[4pt]
Q_1 + Q_2 = Q
\end{cases}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \frac{Q}{2}\,(1 + \cos\alpha) = \frac{18.85}{2}\,(1 + 0.866) = 17.6\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
Q_2 = \frac{Q}{2}\,(1 - \cos\alpha) = \frac{18.85}{2}\,(1 - 0.866) = 1.26\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Resolve the momentum balance along $\eta$. The incoming velocity component is $-v\sin\alpha$ and the outgoing component is zero.
$$
0 - \big(-\rho\,Q\,v\,\sin\alpha\big) = R_\eta
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
F = R_\eta = 283 \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 141\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Check the component parallel to the plate using the calculated discharges.
$$
\rho\,v\,(Q_1 - Q_2) = 1000 \cdot 15 \cdot (0.0176 - 0.00126) = 245\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,v\,\cos\alpha = 283 \cdot 0.866 = 245\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose axes parallel and normal to the smooth plate, since the plate exerts only a normal force on the water. The momentum balance parallel to the plate contains no unknown force and directly gives the flow split. The normal balance gives the force.

Both sheets leave at the original jet speed $v$. The incident jet and outgoing sheets are at atmospheric pressure and at the same elevation. With friction neglected, Bernoulli’s equation therefore gives the same velocity head throughout.

The discharges are $17.6$ and $1.26\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At this small angle of incidence, nearly all the water leaves in the direction of the incident velocity component parallel to the plate. Their sum, $18.86\,\mathrm{L/s}$, equals the incident discharge as continuity requires.

The force of $141\,\mathrm{N}$ acts **normal to the plate**. It is half the force on a plate perpendicular to the jet because $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vertical flat plate of height $L = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is hinged along its upper edge. A horizontal jet of diameter $d = 45\,\mathrm{mm}$ and speed $v = 14\,\mathrm{m/s}$ strikes the plate normally at a point $b = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ below the hinge. Find the jet force, the horizontal force $H$ required at the lower edge to hold the plate vertical, and the hinge reaction, as shown.

::schema{nodi="A,4.3,2.4;B,4.3,0" aste="A-B" acqua="0,1.5 4.3,1.5 4.3,1.9 0,1.9;4.1,1.9 4.3,1.9 4.3,3.1 4.1,3.1;4.1,1.5 4.3,1.5 4.3,0.3 4.1,0.3" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" frecce="0.6,1.7,1.8,1.7,v = 14 m/s" quote="4.3 2.4,4.3 1.7,b = 0.30 m,-0.75;4.3 2.4,4.3 0,L = 0.80 m,-1.7" etichette="1.2,2.15,d = 45 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plate hinged at $A$ along its upper edge. The jet strikes the plate at a distance $b = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.045^2}{4} = 0.001590\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
Q = 0.001590 \cdot 14 = 0.02227\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 22.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
S = \rho\,Q\,v = 1000 \cdot 0.02227 \cdot 14 = 312\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Take moments about hinge $A$.
$$
S\,b - H\,L = 0
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
H = S\,\frac{b}{L} = 312 \cdot \frac{0.30}{0.80} = 117\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Apply horizontal force equilibrium.
$$
R_A = S - H = 312 - 117 = 195\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Check moment equilibrium about the lower edge.
$$
S\,(L - b) - R_A\,L = 312 \cdot 0.50 - 195 \cdot 0.80 = 156 - 156 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Solve the problem in two steps. The integral momentum equation for the jet and outgoing sheets gives the force of the water on the plate, $S = \rho Q v = 312\,\mathrm{N}$, acting horizontally in the jet direction. Applying static equilibrium to the plate then gives the force $H$ and the hinge reaction. See the chapter on rigid-body equilibrium and support reactions.

For a plate in equilibrium, the resultant force is zero and the moments about any point sum to zero. Taking moments about the hinge eliminates the hinge reaction because its line of action passes through that point. Only the jet force, with moment arm $b = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, and the unknown force $H$, with moment arm $L = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, remain.

The results are $H = 117\,\mathrm{N}$ and $R_A = 195\,\mathrm{N}$, both opposite to the incident jet. The required force at the lower edge is smaller than the jet force because it acts through a longer moment arm.

For an independent check, take moments about the lower edge. The jet and the hinge reaction then have moment arms $L - b = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and $L = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, respectively, and their moments sum to zero.

</div>
