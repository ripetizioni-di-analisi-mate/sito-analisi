## Exercise 1

A flat vertical sluice gate spans a rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a horizontal bed. The upstream depth is $h_1 = 2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and the downstream depth is $h_2 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglect the head loss beneath the gate and bed friction. Calculate the discharge and the force exerted by the water on the gate shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0 3,0 3,2.4 0,2.4;3,0 3.3,0 3.3,0.5 3,0.5;3.3,0 8,0 8,0.5 3.3,0.5" solidi="0,-0.4 8,-0.4 8,0 0,0;3,0.5 3.3,0.5 3.3,3.3 3,3.3" pelo="0,3,2.4,p_a;3.3,8,0.5" quote="0 0,0 2.4,h_1 = 2.40 m,-1.0" frecce="0.6,1.1,1.8,1.1,Q" etichette="2,2.8,1;5.2,1.35,2;7.3,0.95,h_2 = 0.50 m;5,2.3,b = 3.00 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Flat gate in a rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Apply Bernoulli’s equation between the two sections, taking the bed as horizontal and neglecting head loss.
$$
h_1 + \frac{Q^2}{2g\,b^2 h_1^2} = h_2 + \frac{Q^2}{2g\,b^2 h_2^2}
$$
$$
h_1 - h_2 = \frac{Q^2}{2g\,b^2}\left(\frac{1}{h_2^2} - \frac{1}{h_1^2}\right)
$$
$$
1.90 = \frac{Q^2}{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 9.00}\,\left(\frac{1}{0.250} - \frac{1}{5.760}\right) = \frac{Q^2}{176.6} \cdot 3.826
$$
$$
Q^2 = \frac{1.90 \cdot 176.6}{3.826} = 87.71
\qquad
Q = 9.36\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{Q}{b\,h_1} = \frac{9.36}{7.20} = 1.30\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{Q}{b\,h_2} = \frac{9.36}{1.50} = 6.24\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = \frac{\gamma\,b\,h_1^2}{2} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 5.760}{2} = 84.76\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\Pi_2 = \frac{\gamma\,b\,h_2^2}{2} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 0.250}{2} = 3.68\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 9.36 \cdot (6.24 - 1.30) = 46.24\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S = \Pi_1 - \Pi_2 - \rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 84.76 - 3.68 - 46.24 = 34.8\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the specific energy at both sections, which should be equal, and calculate the Froude numbers.
$$
E_1 = h_1 + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 2.400 + 0.086 = 2.486\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
E_2 = h_2 + \frac{v_2^2}{2g} = 0.500 + 1.985 = 2.485\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Fr_1 = \frac{v_1}{\sqrt{g\,h_1}} = \frac{1.30}{4.85} = 0.27 < 1
\qquad
Fr_2 = \frac{v_2}{\sqrt{g\,h_2}} = \frac{6.24}{2.21} = 2.82 > 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First, apply Bernoulli’s equation between the upstream and downstream sections. Expressing both velocities by continuity leaves $Q$ as the sole unknown and gives the discharge. Then apply the momentum balance to the same control volume to find the force.

At the vertical sections the streamlines are straight and parallel, so the pressure distribution is hydrostatic. Its resultant is $\dfrac{\gamma b h^2}{2}$ and acts one third of the water depth above the bed. Only the resultant forces, rather than their lines of action, enter the linear momentum balance.

The bed is horizontal and bed friction is neglected, so the gate exerts the only horizontal force from a solid boundary.

The force on the gate is $34.8\,\mathrm{kN}$ **downstream**, much less than the upstream hydrostatic force of $84.76\,\mathrm{kN}$. Downstream water exerts an opposing force of $3.68\,\mathrm{kN}$. More importantly, the flow accelerates and part of the net pressure force supplies the increase in momentum flux.

The two specific energies agree to within a millimeter, consistent with negligible head loss. The two depths $2.40$ and $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ are therefore *alternate depths*. The Froude numbers show that flow changes from **subcritical** upstream to **supercritical** downstream, as expected for free underflow beneath a sluice gate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A flat sluice gate in a rectangular channel of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and horizontal bed reduces the water depth from $h_1 = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ to $h_2 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglect losses. Calculate the discharge and the force on the gate shown, and express that force as a fraction of the force for still water at the same depths.

::schema{acqua="0,0 3,0 3,1.8 0,1.8;3,0 3.3,0 3.3,0.4 3,0.4;3.3,0 8,0 8,0.4 3.3,0.4" solidi="0,-0.35 8,-0.35 8,0 0,0;3,0.4 3.3,0.4 3.3,2.6 3,2.6" pelo="0,3,1.8,p_a;3.3,8,0.4" quote="0 0,0 1.8,h_1 = 1.80 m,-1.0" frecce="0.6,0.85,1.8,0.85,Q" etichette="2,2.15,1;5.2,1.15,2;7.3,0.78,h_2 = 0.40 m;5,1.8,b = 2.00 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate in a channel of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
h_1 - h_2 = \frac{Q^2}{2g\,b^2}\left(\frac{1}{h_2^2} - \frac{1}{h_1^2}\right)
$$
$$
1.40 = \frac{Q^2}{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 4.00}\,\left(\frac{1}{0.160} - \frac{1}{3.240}\right) = \frac{Q^2}{78.48} \cdot 5.941
$$
$$
Q^2 = \frac{1.40 \cdot 78.48}{5.941} = 18.50
\qquad
Q = 4.30\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{4.30}{3.60} = 1.19\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{4.30}{0.80} = 5.38\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = \frac{9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 3.240}{2} = 31.78\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = \frac{9.81 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 0.160}{2} = 1.57\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 4.30 \cdot 4.19 = 18.02\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S = 31.78 - 1.57 - 18.02 = 12.2\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Calculate the net hydrostatic force for still water at the same depths and compare the two forces.
$$
S_0 = \Pi_1 - \Pi_2 = 31.78 - 1.57 = 30.21\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\frac{S}{S_0} = \frac{12.2}{30.21} = 0.40
$$

Check the specific energies and Froude numbers.
$$
E_1 = 1.800 + \frac{1.19^2}{19.62} = 1.872\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
E_2 = 0.400 + \frac{5.38^2}{19.62} = 1.875\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Fr_1 = \frac{1.19}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.80}} = 0.28
\qquad
Fr_2 = \frac{5.38}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.40}} = 2.72
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Follow the procedure in the preceding exercise. Writing Bernoulli’s equation with the difference in depths on the left isolates $Q^2$, giving the discharge directly without iteration.

The still-water comparison shows the contribution of the momentum flux. At the same depths, still water would exert $30.2\,\mathrm{kN}$, whereas the flowing water exerts only $12.2\,\mathrm{kN}$, or forty percent as much. Sizing the gate-operating mechanism from the difference in hydrostatic forces alone would more than double the predicted force.

The two specific energies differ by $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ out of $1.87\,\mathrm{m}$ because the velocities were rounded to three significant figures. The Froude numbers confirm the transition from **subcritical** to **supercritical** flow.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and horizontal bed carries discharge $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A flat sluice gate maintains an upstream depth of $h_1 = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglect head loss and find the downstream water depth and the force on the gate shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0 3,0 3,2 0,2;3,0 3.3,0 3.3,0.25 3,0.25;3.3,0 8,0 8,0.25 3.3,0.25" solidi="0,-0.35 8,-0.35 8,0 0,0;3,0.25 3.3,0.25 3.3,2.8 3,2.8" pelo="0,3,2,p_a;3.3,8,0.25" quote="0 0,0 2,h_1 = 2.00 m,-1.0" frecce="0.6,0.95,1.7,0.95,Q" etichette="2.2,2.35,1;5.2,1.0,2;7.3,0.6,h_2 = ?;5,1.9,b = 4.00 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Gate with specified discharge and upstream depth. The downstream depth is unknown"}

$$
v_1 = \frac{Q}{b\,h_1} = \frac{6.00}{8.00} = 0.750\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
E = h_1 + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 2.000 + \frac{0.750^2}{19.62} = 2.000 + 0.0287 = 2.0287\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The downstream depth must give the same specific energy.
$$
h_2 + \frac{Q^2}{2g\,b^2 h_2^2} = E
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
h_2 = \frac{Q}{b\,\sqrt{2g\,(E - h_2)}}
$$

Iterate using the second form, starting from $h_2 = 0.200\,\mathrm{m}$. Stop when successive values differ by less than $1\,\mathrm{mm}$.

| Iteration | Initial $h_2$ (m) | $E - h_2$ (m) | Calculated $h_2$ (m) | Difference (mm) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.2000 | 1.8287 | 0.2504 | 50.4 |
| 2 | 0.2504 | 1.7782 | 0.2540 | 3.5 |
| 3 | 0.2540 | 1.7747 | 0.2542 | 0.2 |

$$
h_2 = 0.254\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{6.00}{4.00 \cdot 0.254} = 5.90\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 2.000^2}{2} = 78.48\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 0.254^2}{2} = 1.27\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 6.00 \cdot (5.90 - 0.75) = 30.90\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S = 78.48 - 1.27 - 30.90 = 46.3\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the critical depth and the downstream Froude number.
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{Q^2}{g\,b^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{36.0}{9.81 \cdot 16.0}} = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
Fr_2 = \frac{5.90}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.254}} = 3.74
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the given discharge the downstream depth is the alternate depth corresponding to the upstream depth. The specific-energy equation is cubic in $h_2$. Rewrite it as $h_2 = \dfrac{Q}{b\sqrt{2g(E - h_2)}}$ and use the calculated value as the next trial until successive depths agree within the prescribed tolerance.

The iteration converges in three steps. The difference falls from $50.4$ to $0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$, meeting the stopping criterion of a difference smaller than $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ at the third iteration. The starting depth $0.200\,\mathrm{m}$ is below the critical depth because free underflow beneath a sluice gate is supercritical.

The force on the gate is $46.3\,\mathrm{kN}$ downstream.

The critical depth is $0.612\,\mathrm{m}$. The upstream depth is greater and the downstream depth smaller, confirming subcritical flow upstream and supercritical flow downstream. The downstream Froude number $3.74$ also confirms supercritical flow.

</div>
