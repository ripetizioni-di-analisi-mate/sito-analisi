## Exercise 1

A horizontal pipe tee receives discharge $Q_1 = 180\,\mathrm{L/s}$ through section $1$ of diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$. The straight-through branch has diameter $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharge $Q_2 = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and the side branch turns through $90^\circ$ and has diameter $D_3 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharge $Q_3 = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The gauge pressure at section $1$ is $p_1 = 250\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglect junction losses and calculate the components of the force exerted by the water on the tee shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3,0,-;C,6,0,-;D,3,2.6,-" condotte="A-C,0.7;B-D,0.45" frecce="0.3,0,1.3,0,Q_1;4.4,0,5.4,0,Q_2;3,1.5,3,2.4,Q_3" tratteggi="1.2,-0.6 1.2,0.6;4.8,-0.6 4.8,0.6;2.6,1.9 3.4,1.9" etichette="1.05,0.95,1;5.2,0.95,2;3.5,1.55,3;0.3,-0.9,D_1 = 300 mm;4.6,-0.9,D_2 = 250 mm;4.3,2.3,D_3 = 150 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of a pipe tee with its side branch at $90^\circ$"}

$$
A_1 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_2 = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2},
\qquad
A_3 = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.180}{0.0707} = 2.546\,\mathrm{m/s},
\quad
v_2 = \frac{0.120}{0.0491} = 2.445\,\mathrm{m/s},
\quad
v_3 = \frac{0.060}{0.01767} = 3.395\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Apply Bernoulli’s equation from section $1$ to each outlet, taking all sections at the same elevation and neglecting losses.
$$
p_2 = p_1 + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) = 250\,000 + 500 \cdot (6.482 - 5.978) = 250\,252\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
p_3 = p_1 + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(v_1^2 - v_3^2) = 250\,000 + 500 \cdot (6.482 - 11.526) = 247\,478\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = 250.0 \cdot 0.0707 = 17.671\,\mathrm{kN},
\quad
\Pi_2 = 250.3 \cdot 0.0491 = 12.284\,\mathrm{kN},
\quad
\Pi_3 = 247.5 \cdot 0.01767 = 4.373\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Take $x$ along the incident flow and $y$ along the side branch.
$$
\begin{cases}
\rho\,(Q_2 v_2 - Q_1 v_1) = \Pi_1 - \Pi_2 + R_x \\[4pt]
\rho\,Q_3 v_3 = -\Pi_3 + R_y
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\rho\,(Q_2 v_2 - Q_1 v_1) = 1000\,(0.120 \cdot 2.445 - 0.180 \cdot 2.546) = 293 - 458 = -165\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q_3 v_3 = 1000 \cdot 0.060 \cdot 3.395 = 204\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= -0.165 - 17.671 + 12.284 = -5.552\,\mathrm{kN} \\[6pt]
R_y &= 0.204 + 4.373 = 4.577\,\mathrm{kN}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F_x = 5.55\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F_y = -4.58\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F = \sqrt{5.552^2 + 4.577^2} = 7.20\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check junction continuity and the total heads.
$$
Q_2 + Q_3 = 120 + 60 = 180\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q_1
$$
$$
H_1 = \frac{250.0}{9.81} + \frac{6.482}{19.62} = 25.484 + 0.330 = 25.814\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_3 = \frac{247.5}{9.81} + \frac{11.526}{19.62} = 25.229 + 0.587 = 25.816\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The control volume has three flow sections, so the momentum balance includes three pressure forces directed inward. The inlet force $\vec{\Pi}_1$ acts along the incoming flow. The outlet forces $\vec{\Pi}_2$ and $\vec{\Pi}_3$ act opposite to the respective outgoing flows.

The outgoing momentum flux is the sum of the two branch contributions. Only the straight-through branch contributes along $x$ because the side branch is perpendicular to it. Only the side branch contributes along $y$.

Apply Bernoulli’s equation from section $1$ to find the outlet pressures. Velocity decreases slightly in the straight-through branch, so pressure rises by $0.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In the side branch, velocity rises to $3.395\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and pressure falls to $247.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

The resultant force on the tee is $7.20\,\mathrm{kN}$, with component $5.55\,\mathrm{kN}$ along the incoming flow and component $4.58\,\mathrm{kN}$ away from the side branch. The differences between the end pressure forces account for nearly all of the force because the momentum-flux terms amount to only a few hundred newtons.

The check verifies continuity at the junction and conservation of total head along the side branch, where the velocity changes most.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A symmetric horizontal Y-junction receives discharge $Q_1 = 200\,\mathrm{L/s}$ through an inlet pipe of diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and divides it equally between two branches of diameter $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, each inclined at $45^\circ$ to the inlet axis. The inlet gauge pressure is $p_1 = 240\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglect losses and find the force exerted by the water on the junction shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,2.6,0,-;C,5,2.4,-;D,5,-2.4,-" condotte="A-B,0.65;B-C,0.45;B-D,0.45" frecce="0.3,0,1.3,0,Q_1;3.9,1.3,4.6,2,Q_2;3.9,-1.3,4.6,-2,Q_3" tratteggi="1,-0.65 1,0.65;3.7,2.3 4.3,1.7;3.7,-2.3 4.3,-1.7" etichette="0.85,0.95,1;5.5,2.3,2;5.5,-2.4,3;0.3,-0.95,D_1 = 300 mm;2.6,2.6,D = 200 mm;3.7,0.3,45°" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of a symmetrical Y-junction. Both outlet branches make an angle $45^\circ$ with the inlet axis"}

$$
A_1 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
Q_2 = Q_3 = \frac{Q_1}{2} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.200}{0.0707} = 2.829\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = v_3 = \frac{0.100}{0.0314} = 3.183\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
p_2 = p_3 = p_1 + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) = 240\,000 + 500 \cdot (8.003 - 10.131) = 238\,936\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = 240 \cdot 0.0707 = 16.965\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = \Pi_3 = 238.9 \cdot 0.0314 = 7.506\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

The system is symmetric about $x$, so $R_y = 0$. Only the balance along $x$ is needed, and both outlet branches contribute with projection factor $\cos 45^\circ$.
$$
\rho\,(2\,Q_2 v_2 \cos 45^\circ - Q_1 v_1) = \Pi_1 - 2\,\Pi_2 \cos 45^\circ + R_x
$$
$$
\rho\,(2 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 3.183 \cdot 0.707 - 0.200 \cdot 2.829) = 450 - 566 = -116\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
2\,\Pi_2 \cos 45^\circ = 2 \cdot 7.506 \cdot 0.707 = 10.616\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
R_x = -0.116 - 16.965 + 10.616 = -6.465\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
F = 6.46\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the total head in the inlet pipe and one outlet branch.
$$
H_1 = \frac{240}{9.81} + \frac{8.003}{19.62} = 24.465 + 0.408 = 24.873\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 = \frac{238.9}{9.81} + \frac{10.131}{19.62} = 24.353 + 0.516 = 24.869\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The branches have equal discharges and diameters and make equal angles with the inlet axis. Their contributions along $y$ cancel by symmetry, while their contributions along $x$ are equal and each includes the factor $\cos 45^\circ$.

The branch velocity $3.183\,\mathrm{m/s}$ exceeds the inlet velocity because the combined branch area $0.0628\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is smaller than the inlet area $0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Bernoulli’s equation therefore gives a lower branch pressure of $238.9\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

The force on the junction is $6.46\,\mathrm{kN}$ **in the direction of the incoming flow**. The inlet pressure force $16.97\,\mathrm{kN}$ exceeds $10.62\,\mathrm{kN}$, the sum of the axial components of the outlet pressure forces. Each outlet pressure force is inclined, so its projection on $x$ is reduced by the factor $\cos 45^\circ$.

Rounding the velocities accounts for the difference of $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ between the two total heads.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A fitting in a horizontal pipe combines a $90^\circ$ elbow with a reducer. The inlet and outlet diameters are $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $D_2 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the fitting turns the flow through $90^\circ$. The discharge is $Q = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the inlet gauge pressure is $p_1 = 260\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglect losses and find the components, magnitude, and direction of the force on the fitting shown.

::schema{nodi="A,0,0,-;B,3.2,0,-;C,3.2,3.2,-" condotte="A-B,0.7;B-C,0.47" tratteggi="0.9,-0.65 0.9,0.65;2.85,2.2 3.55,2.2" frecce="0.2,0,1,0,Q" etichette="0.75,1,1;3.75,2.2,2;0.2,-1.05,D_1 = 300 mm;3.55,3.45,D_2 = 200 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Plan view of a $90^\circ$ reducing elbow with diameter decreasing from $300$ to $200\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A_1 = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.120}{0.0707} = 1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.120}{0.0314} = 3.82\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
p_2 = p_1 + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) = 260\,000 + 500 \cdot (2.890 - 14.592) = 254\,149\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = 260 \cdot 0.0707 = 18.38\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = 254.1 \cdot 0.0314 = 7.98\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,v_1 = 1000 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 1.70 = 204\,\mathrm{N}
\qquad
\rho\,Q\,v_2 = 1000 \cdot 0.120 \cdot 3.82 = 458\,\mathrm{N}
$$

Take $x$ along the inlet velocity and $y$ along the outlet velocity.
$$
\begin{cases}
\rho\,Q\,(0 - v_1) = \Pi_1 + R_x \\[4pt]
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - 0) = -\Pi_2 + R_y
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= -0.204 - 18.38 = -18.58\,\mathrm{kN} \\[6pt]
R_y &= 0.458 + 7.98 = 8.44\,\mathrm{kN}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F_x = 18.58\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F_y = -8.44\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
F = \sqrt{18.58^2 + 8.44^2} = 20.4\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{8.44}{18.58} = 0.454
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
\varphi = 24.4^\circ
$$

Check the total head at both sections.
$$
H_1 = \frac{260}{9.81} + \frac{2.890}{19.62} = 26.504 + 0.147 = 26.651\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 = \frac{254.1}{9.81} + \frac{14.592}{19.62} = 25.902 + 0.744 = 26.646\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fitting turns the flow and changes its cross-sectional area. The pressure forces in the two component balances therefore act over different areas, and the resultant no longer lies on the $45^\circ$ bisector for a constant-diameter elbow.

The area falls by more than half, so the outlet velocity is more than twice the inlet velocity. The outlet pressure falls to $254.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the outlet pressure force $7.98\,\mathrm{kN}$ is less than half the inlet pressure force $18.38\,\mathrm{kN}$.

The force on the fitting is $20.4\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $24.4^\circ$ to the inlet axis. It lies much closer to the inlet direction than to the angle bisector because the inlet area is larger and its pressure force dominates.

In a constant-diameter $90^\circ$ elbow the two force components have equal magnitudes and the force makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the axis. Here the component along the inlet axis is more than twice the transverse component.

</div>
