## Exercise 1

A horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ ends in a nozzle of diameter $d_2 = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ discharging to the atmosphere. The gauge pressure upstream of the nozzle is $p_1 = 200\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglect losses and calculate the discharge, force on the nozzle, and jet power for the system shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0.7 4,0.7 4.6,1.08 7.8,1.08 7.8,1.52 4.6,1.52 4,1.9 0,1.9" linee="0,0.7 4,0.7 4.6,1.08 6.4,1.08;0,1.9 4,1.9 4.6,1.52 6.4,1.52" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="2.4,0.45 2.4,2.15;6.4,0.83 6.4,1.77" etichette="2.4,2.45,1;6.4,2.1,2;0.3,2.15,D_1 = 250 mm;5.4,0.45,d_2 = 80 mm;0.5,2.75,p_1 = 200 kPa" riferimento=0 titolo="Nozzle of diameter $d_2 = 80\,\mathrm{mm}$ fitted to a pipe of diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.04909\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.080^2}{4} = 0.005027\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$

Apply the energy balance between section $1$ and the outlet, where gauge pressure is zero.
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{v_2^2}{2g}
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
\frac{2\,p_1}{\rho} = Q^2\left(\frac{1}{A_2^2} - \frac{1}{A_1^2}\right)
$$
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 200\,000}{1000 \cdot (39\,578 - 415)}} = \sqrt{\frac{400}{39\,163}} = 0.1011\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 101.1\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.1011}{0.04909} = 2.06\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.1011}{0.005027} = 20.11\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Apply the momentum balance to the same control volume.
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = p_1 A_1 + R_x
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 0.1011 \cdot 18.05 = 1825\,\mathrm{N} = 1.825\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
p_1 A_1 = 200 \cdot 0.04909 = 9.82\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
R_x = 1.825 - 9.82 = -7.99\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
F = 7.99\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Calculate the jet power as the weight flow rate times the velocity head.
$$
P = \gamma\,Q\,\frac{v_2^2}{2g} = \frac{\rho\,Q\,v_2^2}{2} = \frac{1000 \cdot 0.1011 \cdot 404.4}{2} = 20\,440\,\mathrm{W} = 20.4\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

Check the total head at both sections.
$$
H_1 = \frac{200}{9.81} + \frac{2.06^2}{19.62} = 20.387 + 0.216 = 20.603\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
H_2 = \frac{20.11^2}{19.62} = 20.613\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply two balances to the control volume bounded by the pipe section and the nozzle outlet. Each determines a different unknown.

The energy balance relates elevation, pressure, and velocity. At the outlet the gauge pressure is zero, so only the velocity head remains when the outlet elevation is taken as the datum. The balance gives the discharge $101.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and hence both velocities.

The momentum balance also contains the unknown wall reaction. Using $Q$, $v_1$, and $v_2$ from the energy calculation gives the force on the nozzle, $7.99\,\mathrm{kN}$ **downstream**. The flange bolts must resist this force.

The jet power $20.4\,\mathrm{kW}$ follows from energy alone. Multiply the weight flow rate $\gamma Q$ by the available head at the outlet. This is the power the jet can deliver, for example to turbine blades.

The two total heads differ by about one centimeter out of $20.6\,\mathrm{m}$ because the velocities were rounded to three significant figures.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A water tank on frictionless wheels discharges through a circular orifice of diameter $d = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$ in its vertical wall. The orifice center is $h = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, and its coefficients are $\mu = 0.61$ and $C_v = 0.97$. The water level is maintained constant. Find the discharge, jet velocity, and horizontal force needed to hold the tank stationary, as shown.

::schema{acqua="-0.4,-0.4 4,-0.4 4,3 -0.4,3;4,0.5 6.4,0.5 6.4,1 4,1" solidi="-0.8,-0.8 4.4,-0.8 4.4,-0.4 -0.4,-0.4 -0.4,3.4 -0.8,3.4;4,1 4.4,1 4.4,3.4 4,3.4;4,0.5 4.4,0.5 4.4,-0.4 4,-0.4" pelo="-0.4,4,3,p_a" frecce="4.9,0.75,6.1,0.75,v_g" quote="1.2 0.75,1.2 3,h = 3.00 m,-1.0" etichette="5.3,1.55,d = 60 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Wheeled tank with an orifice below the water level in its vertical wall"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.060^2}{4} = 0.002827\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
\sqrt{2\,g\,h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 3.00} = 7.67\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = \mu\,A\,\sqrt{2gh} = 0.61 \cdot 0.002827 \cdot 7.67 = 0.01323\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 13.2\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
v_g = C_v\,\sqrt{2gh} = 0.97 \cdot 7.67 = 7.44\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Use a control volume enclosing the water in the tank, with its outlet at the vena contracta. Take $x$ in the direction of the jet.
$$
\rho\,Q\,v_g - 0 = R_x
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
R_x = 1000 \cdot 0.01323 \cdot 7.44 = 98.4\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F = 98.4\,\mathrm{N}
\;\text{directed opposite to the jet}
$$

Check using the force expression in terms of the available head.
$$
\rho\,Q\,v_g = \rho\,\mu A\sqrt{2gh}\;C_v\sqrt{2gh} = 2\,\gamma\,h\,A\,\mu\,C_v = 2 \cdot 9810 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 0.002827 \cdot 0.592 = 98.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate the discharge and jet velocity as in the chapter on orifice flow. Multiply the theoretical velocity $\sqrt{2gh}$ by the velocity coefficient $C_v$ to obtain the actual velocity. To find discharge, multiply the orifice area and theoretical velocity by the discharge coefficient $\mu$.

The control volume encloses the water in the tank. The level is constant, and the free-surface area is much greater than the orifice area, so the replacement water enters with negligible velocity. The incoming momentum flux along $x$ is therefore zero, while the outgoing momentum flux is $\rho Q v_g$.

Both the free surface and the vena contracta are at zero gauge pressure, so the pressure forces at the flow sections vanish. The tank walls exert $98.4\,\mathrm{N}$ on the water in the jet direction. The water therefore pushes the tank **opposite to the jet**, and holding the wheeled tank stationary requires a force of $98.4\,\mathrm{N}$ in the jet direction.

The force can also be written as $2\gamma h A\,\mu\,C_v$. Without contraction or losses, $\mu = C_v = 1$, giving $2\gamma h A = 166\,\mathrm{N}$, twice the hydrostatic force that would act on a cover closing the orifice. The actual coefficients reduce the force to fifty-nine percent of that ideal value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A horizontal pipe expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 90\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Use the momentum balance to find the pressure rise across the expansion shown and the energy balance to calculate the head loss. Compare that loss with the Borda–Carnot formula.

::schema{acqua="0,0.95 3.4,0.95 3.4,0.6 7,0.6 7,2.0 3.4,2.0 3.4,1.65 0,1.65" linee="0,0.95 3.4,0.95 3.4,0.6 7,0.6;0,1.65 3.4,1.65 3.4,2.0 7,2.0" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="2.2,0.7 2.2,1.9;5.6,0.35 5.6,2.25" etichette="2.2,2.15,1;5.6,2.55,2;0.3,1.9,D_1 = 200 mm;5.2,0.1,D_2 = 300 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden expansion from $200$ to $300\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A_1 = 0.03142\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = 0.07069\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.090}{0.03142} = 2.865\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.090}{0.07069} = 1.273\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Apply the momentum balance, taking the pressure on the annular face to be $p_1$.
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = (p_1 - p_2)\,A_2
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
\frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{v_2\,(v_1 - v_2)}{g}
$$
$$
\frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{1.273 \cdot (2.865 - 1.273)}{9.81} = \frac{2.027}{9.81} = 0.207\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_2 - p_1 = 9810 \cdot 0.207 = 2031\,\mathrm{Pa} = 2.03\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Apply the energy balance between the same two sections.
$$
\Delta H = \frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g} - \frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{8.208 - 1.621}{19.62} - 0.207
$$
$$
\Delta H = 0.336 - 0.207 = 0.129\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check using the Borda–Carnot formula.
$$
\frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(2.865 - 1.273)^2}{19.62} = \frac{2.534}{19.62} = 0.129\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Applied between the same two sections, the momentum and energy balances give two independent pieces of information. Both are needed to determine the pressure rise and head loss.

The momentum balance has no explicit dissipation term. Wall forces are included in the reaction $\vec{R}$. In this short control volume the axial wall shear is neglected, and the pressure $p_1$ on the annular face has already been counted, leaving no additional axial wall force. The momentum balance therefore gives the pressure rise $2.03\,\mathrm{kPa}$ without requiring the head loss in advance.

Use that pressure difference in the energy balance to find the loss. The pressure-head recovery $0.207\,\mathrm{m}$ is smaller than the decrease in velocity head $0.336\,\mathrm{m}$. The difference $0.129\,\mathrm{m}$ is the head dissipated in the eddies of the recirculation zone.

The Borda–Carnot formula gives the same loss, as expected because it follows from the two balances together. Its derivation is given in the chapter on pipe friction and minor losses.

Omitting the loss term $\Delta H$ from Bernoulli’s equation would predict a pressure rise equivalent to $0.336\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, sixty-three percent higher than the actual rise.

</div>
