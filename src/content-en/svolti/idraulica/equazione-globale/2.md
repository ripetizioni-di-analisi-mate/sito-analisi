## Exercise 1

A conical reducer in a horizontal pipe changes the diameter from $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 150\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 300\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglect losses in the reducer and find the force exerted by the water on the reducer shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0.75 2.6,0.75 3.6,1.05 7,1.05 7,1.55 3.6,1.55 2.6,1.85 0,1.85" linee="0,0.75 2.6,0.75 3.6,1.05 7,1.05;0,1.85 2.6,1.85 3.6,1.55 7,1.55" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="1.6,0.5 1.6,2.1;5.8,0.8 5.8,1.8" etichette="1.6,2.4,1;5.8,2.1,2;0.3,2.1,D_1 = 300 mm;5.4,0.5,D_2 = 200 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Conical reducer in a horizontal pipe"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.300^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.150}{0.0707} = 2.12\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.150}{0.0314} = 4.77\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Apply Bernoulli’s equation between the two sections at equal elevation, neglecting head loss.
$$
p_2 = p_1 + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) = 300\,000 + \frac{1000}{2}\,(4.504 - 22.797) = 290\,850\,\mathrm{Pa} = 290.9\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = p_1 A_1 = 300 \cdot 0.0707 = 21.21\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = p_2 A_2 = 290.9 \cdot 0.0314 = 9.14\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 0.150 \cdot (4.77 - 2.12) = 398\,\mathrm{N} = 0.398\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Take $x$ positive in the flow direction. The pipe is horizontal, so the weight has no component along this axis.
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = \Pi_1 - \Pi_2 + R_x
$$
$$
R_x = 0.398 - 21.21 + 9.14 = -11.67\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
F = -R_x = 11.7\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the total head at both sections.
$$
H_1 = \frac{300}{9.81} + \frac{4.504}{19.62} = 30.581 + 0.230 = 30.811\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 = \frac{290.9}{9.81} + \frac{22.797}{19.62} = 29.649 + 1.162 = 30.811\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Continuity gives the two velocities. As the diameter decreases from $300$ to $200\,\mathrm{mm}$, the flow area falls to four ninths of its original value and the velocity more than doubles. Bernoulli’s equation then gives a downstream pressure $9.1\,\mathrm{kPa}$ below the upstream value, as part of the pressure head is converted to velocity head.

The two sections share an axis, so only the axial component of the integral momentum equation is needed. The pressure force at section $1$ acts downstream and that at section $2$ acts upstream. Both act inward on the control volume.

The water exerts a force of $11.7\,\mathrm{kN}$ on the reducer, directed **downstream**. The difference between the end pressure forces, $21.21$ and $9.14\,\mathrm{kN}$, accounts for nearly all of it. The area falls by just over half while the pressure drops by only a little over three percent. The momentum-flux term, $0.398\,\mathrm{kN}$, is about three percent of the total force.

The check confirms that both sections have the same total head, as required when losses are neglected.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A nozzle is fitted to the end of a horizontal pipe of diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ and discharges to the atmosphere through an outlet of diameter $d_2 = 60\,\mathrm{mm}$. The gauge pressure in the pipe upstream of the nozzle is $p_1 = 150\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglect nozzle losses and find the discharge and the force exerted by the water on the nozzle shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0.75 4,0.75 4.6,1.05 7.6,1.05 7.6,1.55 4.6,1.55 4,1.85 0,1.85" linee="0,0.75 4,0.75 4.6,1.05 6.4,1.05;0,1.85 4,1.85 4.6,1.55 6.4,1.55" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="2.4,0.5 2.4,2.1;6.4,0.8 6.4,1.8" etichette="2.4,2.4,1;6.4,2.1,2;0.3,2.1,D_1 = 150 mm;5.6,0.5,d_2 = 60 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Nozzle at the end of a pipe. Section $2$ is the outlet, where the gauge pressure is zero"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.150^2}{4} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.060^2}{4} = 0.002827\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$

Apply Bernoulli’s equation between section $1$ and the outlet, where the gauge pressure is zero.
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{v_2^2}{2g}
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
v_1 = \frac{Q}{A_1},
\quad
v_2 = \frac{Q}{A_2}
$$
$$
\frac{2\,p_1}{\rho} = Q^2\left(\frac{1}{A_2^2} - \frac{1}{A_1^2}\right)
$$
$$
Q = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot 150\,000}{1000 \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{0.002827^2} - \dfrac{1}{0.01767^2}\right)}} = \sqrt{\frac{300}{121\,890}} = 0.0496\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 49.6\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.0496}{0.01767} = 2.81\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.0496}{0.002827} = 17.55\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = p_1 A_1 = 150 \cdot 0.01767 = 2.651\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = 0
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 0.0496 \cdot (17.55 - 2.81) = 731\,\mathrm{N} = 0.731\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = \Pi_1 + R_x
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
R_x = 0.731 - 2.651 = -1.920\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
F = -R_x = 1.92\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the total head at both sections.
$$
H_1 = \frac{150}{9.81} + \frac{2.81^2}{19.62} = 15.291 + 0.402 = 15.693\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 = \frac{17.55^2}{19.62} = 15.697\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discharge is unknown, so first obtain it from Bernoulli’s equation. The jet is exposed to the atmosphere at the outlet, where gauge pressure is zero. Taking the common elevation as the datum leaves only velocity head at the outlet. Replacing each velocity by $\dfrac{Q}{A}$ gives an equation with $Q$ as its only unknown.

The outlet pressure force in the momentum balance vanishes because the gauge pressure there is zero. The remaining forces are the upstream pressure force $\Pi_1$, directed downstream, and the force of the nozzle walls on the water.

The water exerts a force of $1.92\,\mathrm{kN}$ **downstream** on the nozzle. It tends to pull the nozzle away from the flange, so the flange bolts must resist this force in tension.

Here the momentum-flux term is appreciable. It equals $0.731\,\mathrm{kN}$, or twenty-eight percent of the pressure force $\Pi_1$, because the velocity increases from $2.81$ to $17.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Omitting it would give $2.65\,\mathrm{kN}$, thirty-eight percent above the actual nozzle force.

The two total heads agree to the first three significant figures. The small discrepancy comes from rounding the velocities.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A conical reducer in a horizontal pipe changes the diameter from $D_1 = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and carries discharge $Q = 250\,\mathrm{L/s}$. A gauge at the downstream section reads $p_2 = 120\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the local head loss in the reducer is $\Delta H = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the upstream pressure and the force exerted on the reducer shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0.6 2.6,0.6 3.6,0.98 7,0.98 7,1.62 3.6,1.62 2.6,2 0,2" linee="0,0.6 2.6,0.6 3.6,0.98 7,0.98;0,2 2.6,2 3.6,1.62 7,1.62" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="1.6,0.35 1.6,2.25;5.8,0.73 5.8,1.87" etichette="1.6,2.55,1;5.8,2.15,2;0.3,2.25,D_1 = 400 mm;5.3,0.4,D_2 = 250 mm;5.2,2.5,p_2 = 120 kPa" riferimento=0 titolo="Reducer with known downstream pressure and local head loss $\Delta H = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.400^2}{4} = 0.1257\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.250}{0.1257} = 1.99\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.250}{0.0491} = 5.09\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Apply Bernoulli’s equation with head loss between the two sections at equal elevation.
$$
\frac{p_1}{\gamma} + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = \frac{p_2}{\gamma} + \frac{v_2^2}{2g} + \Delta H
$$
$$
p_1 = p_2 + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(v_2^2 - v_1^2) + \gamma\,\Delta H = 120\,000 + 500 \cdot (25.939 - 3.958) + 9810 \cdot 0.15
$$
$$
p_1 = 120\,000 + 10\,990 + 1472 = 132\,462\,\mathrm{Pa} = 132.5\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = 132.5 \cdot 0.1257 = 16.65\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = 120 \cdot 0.0491 = 5.89\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 0.250 \cdot 3.10 = 776\,\mathrm{N} = 0.776\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
R_x = \rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) - \Pi_1 + \Pi_2 = 0.776 - 16.65 + 5.89 = -9.98\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
F = 9.98\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the energy balance using the calculated values.
$$
H_1 = \frac{132.5}{9.81} + \frac{3.958}{19.62} = 13.506 + 0.202 = 13.708\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 + \Delta H = \frac{120}{9.81} + \frac{25.939}{19.62} + 0.15 = 12.232 + 1.322 + 0.150 = 13.704\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the downstream pressure is given, so solve Bernoulli’s equation for $p_1$. Add the head loss to the downstream total head to recover the upstream head, since head falls in the direction of flow. Thus $p_1$ exceeds $p_2$ because of both the difference in velocity heads and the head loss.

Head loss enters the momentum calculation through the pressures. Once $p_1$ and $p_2$ are known, write the momentum balance exactly as in Exercise 1. No separate dissipative term is needed because wall friction is already included in the wall reaction $\vec{R}$ being calculated.

The force on the reducer is $9.98\,\mathrm{kN}$ **downstream**. The total-head check confirms the result. Rounding the velocities to three significant figures accounts for the discrepancy of $4\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>
