## Exercise 1

A diffuser in a horizontal pipe increases the diameter from $D_1 = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ at discharge $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The upstream gauge pressure is $p_1 = 180\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the local head loss is $\Delta H = \xi\,\dfrac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g}$ with $\xi = 0.25$. Find the downstream pressure and the force exerted by the water on the diffuser shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0.9 2.6,0.9 3.8,0.6 7,0.6 7,2.0 3.8,2.0 2.6,1.7 0,1.7" linee="0,0.9 2.6,0.9 3.8,0.6 7,0.6;0,1.7 2.6,1.7 3.8,2.0 7,2.0" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="1.6,0.65 1.6,1.95;5.6,0.35 5.6,2.25" etichette="1.6,2.25,1;5.6,2.55,2;0.3,1.95,D_1 = 200 mm;5.2,0.1,D_2 = 300 mm" riferimento=0 titolo="Diffuser with diameter increasing from $200$ to $300\,\mathrm{mm}$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4} = 0.0314\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.300^2}{4} = 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.080}{0.0314} = 2.546\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.080}{0.0707} = 1.132\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H = 0.25 \cdot \frac{(2.546 - 1.132)^2}{19.62} = 0.25 \cdot \frac{1.999}{19.62} = 0.0255\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_2 = p_1 + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(v_1^2 - v_2^2) - \gamma\,\Delta H = 180\,000 + 500 \cdot (6.482 - 1.281) - 9810 \cdot 0.0255
$$
$$
p_2 = 180\,000 + 2601 - 250 = 182\,351\,\mathrm{Pa} = 182.4\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = 180 \cdot 0.0314 = 5.655\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = 182.4 \cdot 0.0707 = 12.890\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 0.080 \cdot (1.132 - 2.546) = -113\,\mathrm{N} = -0.113\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
R_x = \rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) - \Pi_1 + \Pi_2 = -0.113 - 5.655 + 12.890 = 7.122\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
F = -R_x = -7.12\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check the energy balance.
$$
H_1 = \frac{180}{9.81} + \frac{6.482}{19.62} = 18.349 + 0.330 = 18.679\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_2 + \Delta H = \frac{182.35}{9.81} + \frac{1.281}{19.62} + 0.0255 = 18.588 + 0.065 + 0.026 = 18.679\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The flow area increases, so continuity gives a velocity decrease from $2.546$ to $1.132\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The diffuser converts velocity head into pressure head. Bernoulli’s equation gives a pressure rise from $180$ to $182.4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The local loss reduces the pressure-head recovery by $0.0255\,\mathrm{m}$, about one tenth of the $0.265\,\mathrm{m}$ available in the absence of loss.

The two end pressure forces oppose one another, as in a reducer, but here the downstream force is greater. The area more than doubles, while the pressure rises by just over one percent. Thus $\Pi_2 = 12.89\,\mathrm{kN}$ exceeds $\Pi_1 = 5.655\,\mathrm{kN}$.

The wall reaction component $R_x$ is positive, so the force of the water on the diffuser is negative along $x$. Its magnitude is $7.12\,\mathrm{kN}$ and it acts **upstream**, opposite to the water force on a reducer. A diffuser anchorage must therefore resist the opposite loading direction.

The upstream total head equals the downstream total head plus the head loss, confirming the energy balance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A diffuser in a horizontal pipe increases the diameter from $D_1 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $D_2 = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ at discharge $Q = 150\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Gauges at its upstream and downstream sections read $p_1 = 220.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p_2 = 223.5\,\mathrm{kPa}$, respectively. Find the local head loss, the corresponding loss coefficient $\xi$, and the force on the diffuser shown.

::schema{acqua="0,0.95 2.6,0.95 3.8,0.5 7,0.5 7,2.1 3.8,2.1 2.6,1.65 0,1.65" linee="0,0.95 2.6,0.95 3.8,0.5 7,0.5;0,1.65 2.6,1.65 3.8,2.1 7,2.1" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="1.6,0.7 1.6,1.9;5.6,0.25 5.6,2.35" etichette="1.6,2.2,1;5.6,2.65,2;0.3,1.9,D_1 = 250 mm;5.2,0.0,D_2 = 400 mm;0.5,2.55,p_1 = 220.0 kPa;4.4,2.9,p_2 = 223.5 kPa" riferimento=0 titolo="Diffuser with pressures measured at both end sections"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.400^2}{4} = 0.1257\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.150}{0.0491} = 3.056\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.150}{0.1257} = 1.194\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Apply Bernoulli’s equation with head loss between sections at equal elevation.
$$
\Delta H = \frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g} - \frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{9.339 - 1.426}{19.62} - \frac{3.5}{9.81}
$$
$$
\Delta H = 0.4033 - 0.3568 = 0.0465\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\xi = \frac{\Delta H \cdot 2g}{(v_1 - v_2)^2} = \frac{0.0465 \cdot 19.62}{(3.056 - 1.194)^2} = \frac{0.912}{3.467} = 0.263
$$
$$
\Pi_1 = 220.0 \cdot 0.0491 = 10.80\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
\Pi_2 = 223.5 \cdot 0.1257 = 28.09\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = 1000 \cdot 0.150 \cdot (1.194 - 3.056) = -279\,\mathrm{N} = -0.279\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
R_x = -0.279 - 10.80 + 28.09 = 17.01\,\mathrm{kN}
\qquad
F = 17.0\,\mathrm{kN}
\;\text{upstream}
$$

Check the loss coefficient $\xi$ and the pressure recovery.
$$
\frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g} = 0.4033\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = 0.3568\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{0.3568}{0.4033} = 0.88
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the pressures are measured, so Bernoulli’s equation gives the unknown head loss. The ideal pressure-head recovery $0.4033\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds the measured recovery $0.3568\,\mathrm{m}$ by the local loss $0.0465\,\mathrm{m}$. Keep four decimal places in both heads because they are close in value. Rounding to three significant figures would change their difference by three percent.

The resulting loss coefficient $\xi$ is $0.263$. It lies within the laboratory range $0.15 \div 0.30$ for diffusers with included angles from $7^\circ$ to $10^\circ$, providing a check on the calculation.

The actual pressure recovery is eighty-eight percent of its ideal value. This ratio is the diffuser’s **pressure-recovery efficiency**.

The force on the diffuser is $17.0\,\mathrm{kN}$ upstream, as in the preceding exercise. The momentum-flux term $0.279\,\mathrm{kN}$ is less than two percent of the total force, so the end pressure forces again dominate.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A horizontal pipe expands abruptly from diameter $D_1 = 150\,\mathrm{mm}$ to diameter $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$. The discharge is $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and the gauge pressure immediately upstream of the expansion is $p_1 = 200\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Assume that the pressure on the annular face equals $p_1$. Find the downstream pressure, the axial force on the annular face, and the head loss for the expansion shown.

::schema{acqua="0,1.0 3.4,1.0 3.4,0.5 7,0.5 7,2.1 3.4,2.1 3.4,1.6 0,1.6" linee="0,1.0 3.4,1.0 3.4,0.5 7,0.5;0,1.6 3.4,1.6 3.4,2.1 7,2.1" frecce="0.5,1.3,1.6,1.3,Q" tratteggi="2.2,0.75 2.2,1.85;5.6,0.25 5.6,2.35" etichette="2.2,2.15,1;5.6,2.65,2;0.3,1.85,D_1 = 150 mm;5.2,0.0,D_2 = 250 mm;0.5,2.55,p_1 = 200 kPa" riferimento=0 titolo="Sudden expansion with an annular face of area $A_2 - A_1$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.150^2}{4} = 0.01767\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.250^2}{4} = 0.04909\,\mathrm{m^2}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{0.040}{0.01767} = 2.264\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{0.040}{0.04909} = 0.8149\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Apply the axial momentum balance, with pressure force $p_1 A_2$ at the upstream end of the control volume.
$$
\rho\,Q\,(v_2 - v_1) = (p_1 - p_2)\,A_2
$$
$$
p_2 = p_1 + \rho\,v_2\,(v_1 - v_2) = 200\,000 + 1000 \cdot 0.8149 \cdot (2.264 - 0.8149) = 200\,000 + 1181
$$
$$
p_2 = 201\,181\,\mathrm{Pa} = 201.2\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$
$$
S_{\text{annulus}} = p_1\,(A_2 - A_1) = 200 \cdot (0.04909 - 0.01767) = 200 \cdot 0.03142 = 6.28\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Use Bernoulli’s equation and the calculated pressures to obtain the head loss.
$$
\Delta H = \frac{v_1^2 - v_2^2}{2g} - \frac{p_2 - p_1}{\gamma} = \frac{5.126 - 0.664}{19.62} - \frac{1.181}{9.81} = 0.2274 - 0.1204 = 0.107\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check using the Borda–Carnot head loss.
$$
\frac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g} = \frac{(2.264 - 0.8149)^2}{19.62} = \frac{2.100}{19.62} = 0.107\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At a sudden expansion the flow separates at the edge, producing a recirculation zone beside the annular face of area $A_2 - A_1$. Measurements show that the pressure on the face is close to the upstream pipe pressure. The total pressure force on the upstream end of the control volume is therefore $p_1 A_2$ rather than $p_1 A_1$.

With that assumption the integral momentum equation contains only two pressures and directly gives $p_2$, which is **greater** than $p_1$. Although energy is dissipated, pressure rises as the flow slows down.

The axial force on the annular face is $6.28\,\mathrm{kN}$ upstream because water presses on it from the downstream side.

Using the calculated pressures in Bernoulli’s equation gives the head loss $0.107\,\mathrm{m}$. Compare it with the Borda–Carnot formula $\dfrac{(v_1 - v_2)^2}{2g}$, derived by combining the energy and momentum balances in the chapter on pipe friction and minor losses. The two values agree, confirming both the calculation and the consistency of the balances.

</div>
