## Exercise 1

An open tank contains water to depth $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Taking the bottom as the elevation datum, find the gauge and absolute pressures at point $F$ on the bottom and the piezometric head of the water.

::schema{acqua="0,0 3.5,0 3.5,4.5 0,4.5" solidi="-0.3,-0.3 3.8,-0.3 3.8,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,5 -0.3,5;3.5,0 3.8,0 3.8,5 3.5,5" pelo="0,3.5,4.5,p_atm" punti="1.75,0,F" quote="0 4.5,0 0,4.50 m,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Open tank containing $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ of water"}

The free surface is open to the atmosphere, so gauge pressure there is zero and the hydrostatic head plane coincides with the free surface. The hydrostatic pressure law gives
$$
p_F = \gamma\,h = 9.81 \cdot 4.50 = 44.1\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
Adding atmospheric pressure gives the absolute pressure:
$$
p_{F,\mathrm{abs}} = p_F + p_{\mathrm{atm}} = 44.1 + 101.3 = 145.4\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
With the datum at the bottom, $z_F = 0$, so the piezometric head is
$$
h_F = z_F + \frac{p_F}{\gamma} = 0 + \frac{44.145}{9.81} = 4.50\,\mathrm{m} .
$$

Check the pressure using the weight of the water column above a bottom area of $1\,\mathrm{m^2}$:
$$
G = \gamma\,h\,A = 9.81 \cdot 4.50 \cdot 1 = 44.1\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
\frac{G}{A} = 44.1\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tank is open, so only atmospheric pressure acts on the free surface and the gauge pressure there is zero. The **hydrostatic head plane** therefore passes through the free surface, $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the bottom.

At point $F$, pressure equals the specific weight of water times the vertical distance between $F$ and the head plane, namely the depth $h = 4.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The tank shape drops out because hydrostatic pressure depends only on the difference in elevation.

Piezometric head is the sum of elevation and pressure head. With the tank bottom as the datum, the elevation of $F$ is zero. The piezometric head therefore equals the pressure head, $4.50\,\mathrm{m}$, which is also the elevation of the hydrostatic head plane above that datum.

The water column above one square meter of the tank bottom has volume $4.50\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and weight $44.1\,\mathrm{kN}$. Dividing its weight by the bottom area recovers the same pressure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The closed tank shown contains water to depth $2.60\,\mathrm{m}$ under air at gauge pressure $p_0 = 32\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Find the elevation of the hydrostatic head plane relative to the free surface, the pressure at bottom point $F$, and the piezometric head relative to the bottom.

::schema{acqua="0,0 3.6,0 3.6,2.6 0,2.6" solidi="-0.3,-0.3 3.9,-0.3 3.9,0 -0.3,0;-0.3,0 0,0 0,3.4 -0.3,3.4;3.6,0 3.9,0 3.9,3.4 3.6,3.4;-0.3,3.4 3.9,3.4 3.9,3.7 -0.3,3.7" pelo="0,3.6,2.6" punti="1.8,0,F" etichette="1.8,2.95,air  p_0 = 32 kPa" quote="0 2.6,0 0,2.60 m,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Closed tank with pressurized air above the free surface"}

The air above the water exerts a pressure $p_0$ on the free surface. The hydrostatic head plane therefore lies above the surface by
$$
\frac{p_0}{\gamma} = \frac{32}{9.81} = 3.26\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The bottom pressure equals the specific weight of water times the vertical distance from the bottom to the hydrostatic head plane:
$$
p_F = \gamma\,\left(h + \frac{p_0}{\gamma}\right) = 9.81 \cdot (2.60 + 3.26) = 57.5\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$
With the bottom as the datum, $z_F = 0$, so the piezometric head is
$$
h_F = z_F + \frac{p_F}{\gamma} = \frac{57.506}{9.81} = 5.86\,\mathrm{m} .
$$

Check by adding the two contributions separately:
$$
p_F = p_0 + \gamma\,h = 32 + 9.81 \cdot 2.60 = 32 + 25.5 = 57.5\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressurized air exerts a gauge pressure of $p_0$ on the free surface. The hydrostatic head plane, where gauge pressure is zero, therefore lies $\dfrac{p_0}{\gamma}$ above the free surface.

An increase in pressure at the free surface is transmitted undiminished throughout the water. The bottom pressure is therefore $\gamma$ times the vertical distance from the bottom to the hydrostatic head plane, a distance of $2.60 + 3.26 = 5.86\,\mathrm{m}$.

The piezometric head is **$5.86\,\mathrm{m}$**. It equals the elevation of the hydrostatic head plane above the tank bottom, because gauge pressure is zero throughout that plane.

Adding the air pressure to the pressure due to the water column gives the same result. Both calculations use the hydrostatic pressure law with different starting points.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A closed pipe filled with still water connects points $A$ and $B$ at elevations $z_A = 12.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $z_B = 5.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above a horizontal datum, as shown. The gauge pressure at $A$ is $p_A = 180\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Find the piezometric head, the pressure at $B$, and the elevation of the hydrostatic head plane. If the pipe extends to point $C$ at elevation $32.00\,\mathrm{m}$, find the pressure there.

::schema{nodi="A,0,12;B,7,5.5" condotte="A-B,0.6" linee="-1,0 8.5,0" quote="A,0 0,12.00 m,-0.9;B,7 0,5.50 m,0.9" etichette="3.8,-0.75,datum" riferimento=0 titolo="Closed pipe filled with water at rest between points $A$ and $B$"}

The water at rest forms one continuous body, so its piezometric head is the same throughout:
$$
h = z_A + \frac{p_A}{\gamma} = 12.00 + \frac{180}{9.81} = 12.00 + 18.35 = 30.35\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The hydrostatic head plane is the horizontal plane at elevation $30.35\,\mathrm{m}$. The pressure at $B$ is
$$
p_B = \gamma\,(h - z_B) = 9.81 \cdot (30.35 - 5.50) = 9.81 \cdot 24.85 = 244\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
$$
and at point $C$, above the hydrostatic head plane, it is
$$
p_C = \gamma\,(h - z_C) = 9.81 \cdot (30.35 - 32.00) = -16.2\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
$$
a pressure below atmospheric equivalent to a water column of height $1.65\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check the pressure at $B$ by applying the hydrostatic pressure law directly between $A$ and $B$:
$$
p_B = p_A + \gamma\,(z_A - z_B) = 180 + 9.81 \cdot 6.50 = 180 + 63.8 = 244\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The closed pipe is full of water at rest, so all points belong to one continuous body of liquid. The hydrostatic pressure law gives the same piezometric head everywhere. Evaluate it at $A$, where the pressure is known.

Once the head is known, multiply $\gamma$ by the signed vertical distance below the head plane to find the pressure at any point. Point $B$ lies $24.85\,\mathrm{m}$ below the plane, giving a positive gauge pressure of **$244\,\mathrm{kPa}$**.

Point $C$ lies $1.65\,\mathrm{m}$ above the hydrostatic head plane, so its gauge pressure is negative. This is only a modest vacuum. The equivalent water-column height, $1.65\,\mathrm{m}$, is well below the suction-lift limit of about $-10\,\mathrm{m}$.

The pressure at $B$ can also be found directly from the hydrostatic pressure law. A drop of $6.50\,\mathrm{m}$ in elevation increases the pressure by $\gamma \cdot 6.50 = 63.8\,\mathrm{kPa}$, giving the same result.

</div>
