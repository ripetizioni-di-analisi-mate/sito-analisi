## Exercise 1

Two separate pipes $A$ and $B$ of still water have centerlines at the same elevation. A mercury differential manometer connects them, as shown. Relative to the pipe centerlines, the mercury interface on the $A$ side is at elevation $-0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ and on the $B$ side at $-0.56\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the differences in piezometric head and pressure.

::schema{nodi="P,0,0,-;Q,1.6,0,-;R,3.4,0,-;S,5,0,-" condotte="P-Q,0.35;R-S,0.35" acqua="1,0 1.15,0 1.15,-0.8 1,-0.8;4,0 4.15,0 4.15,-0.56 4,-0.56" poligoni="1,-0.8 1,-1.1 4.15,-1.1 4.15,-0.56 4,-0.56 4,-0.95 1.15,-0.95 1.15,-0.8" tratteggi="1.15,-0.8 2.75,-0.8;4,-0.56 2.45,-0.56" punti="0.8,0,A;4.4,0,B" quote="2.6 -0.8,2.6 -0.56,Δ,0.4" etichette="2.6,-1.4,mercury δ = 13.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Differential mercury manometer connecting two pipes whose centerlines are at the same elevation"}

Trace the tube from $A$ to $B$, applying the hydrostatic relation to each liquid segment. With $z_1 = -0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, $z_2 = -0.56\,\mathrm{m}$, and $\Delta = z_2 - z_1 = 0.24\,\mathrm{m}$, the differential-manometer equation gives
$$
h_A - h_B = \Delta\,(\delta - 1) = 0.24 \cdot (13.6 - 1) = 0.24 \cdot 12.6 = 3.02\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
The pipe centerlines are at the same elevation, so $z_A = z_B$. The difference in piezometric head therefore equals the difference in pressure head.
$$
p_A - p_B = \gamma\,(h_A - h_B) = 9.81 \cdot 3.024 = 29.7\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$

Check by tracing the tube from pipe $A$ to pipe $B$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_1 &= p_A + \gamma \cdot 0.80 = p_A + 7.85\,\mathrm{kPa} \\[6pt]
p_2 &= p_1 - \gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}} \cdot 0.24 = p_1 - 32.02\,\mathrm{kPa} \\[6pt]
p_B &= p_2 - \gamma \cdot 0.56 = p_2 - 5.49\,\mathrm{kPa}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
p_A - p_B = -7.85 + 32.02 + 5.49 = 29.7\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Keep the sign of the level difference. By definition $\Delta$ is the elevation of the meniscus on the $B$ side minus that of the meniscus on the $A$ side. It is positive here, with value $0.24\,\mathrm{m}$.

The relation $h_A - h_B = \Delta\,(\delta - 1)$ converts the observed level difference to water head using the factor $12.6$. Mercury is $13.6$ times as dense as water, and the mercury displaces a water column of the same height, giving the subtraction of one. A level difference of a few centimeters therefore represents a head difference of several tens of centimeters.

The difference in piezometric head is **$3.02\,\mathrm{m}$**. The centerlines are at the same elevation, so the elevation terms cancel. Multiply by $\gamma$ to obtain the pressure difference.

For a check, descend from $A$ to the first meniscus, cross the mercury, and rise to $B$. Adding the three pressure changes gives the same result, $29.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two separate pipes of still water have centerlines at different elevations, with pipe $A$ lying $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ above pipe $B$. The mercury differential manometer shown has its interface on the $A$ side at elevation $-0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and on the $B$ side at $-0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, both relative to the centerline of $B$. Find the differences in piezometric head and pressure.

::schema{nodi="P,0,2.4,-;Q,1.6,2.4,-;R,3.4,0,-;S,5,0,-" condotte="P-Q,0.35;R-S,0.35" acqua="1,2.4 1.15,2.4 1.15,-0.5 1,-0.5;4,0 4.15,0 4.15,-0.15 4,-0.15" poligoni="1,-0.5 1,-0.85 4.15,-0.85 4.15,-0.15 4,-0.15 4,-0.7 1.15,-0.7 1.15,-0.5" tratteggi="1.15,-0.5 2.75,-0.5;4,-0.15 2.45,-0.15" punti="0.8,2.4,A;4.4,0,B" quote="2.6 -0.5,2.6 -0.15,Δ,0.4;0.5 0,0.5 2.4,2.40 m,-0.85" etichette="2.6,-1.15,mercury δ = 13.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Differential manometer connecting two pipes whose centerlines are at different elevations"}

The differential-manometer equation applies at any pair of tap elevations because the terms $z_A$ and $z_B$ cancel in its derivation. With $\Delta = -0.15 - (-0.50) = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ it gives
$$
h_A - h_B = \Delta\,(\delta - 1) = 0.35 \cdot 12.6 = 4.41\,\mathrm{m} .
$$
Use the definition of piezometric head to recover the pressure difference, accounting for the elevation difference between the pipe centerlines.
$$
h_A - h_B = (z_A - z_B) + \frac{p_A - p_B}{\gamma} ,
$$
$$
p_A - p_B = \gamma\,\big[(h_A - h_B) - (z_A - z_B)\big] = 9.81 \cdot (4.41 - 2.40) = 9.81 \cdot 2.01 = 19.7\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$

Check by tracing the tube, using $z_A = 2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and $z_B = 0$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_1 &= p_A + \gamma \cdot \big(2.40 - (-0.50)\big) = p_A + 28.45\,\mathrm{kPa} \\[6pt]
p_2 &= p_1 - \gamma_{\mathrm{Hg}} \cdot 0.35 = p_1 - 46.70\,\mathrm{kPa} \\[6pt]
p_B &= p_2 - \gamma \cdot 0.15 = p_2 - 1.47\,\mathrm{kPa}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
p_A - p_B = -28.45 + 46.70 + 1.47 = 19.7\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The signed level difference is again the meniscus elevation on the $B$ side minus that on the $A$ side. Thus $\Delta = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ and the difference in piezometric head is **$4.41\,\mathrm{m}$**.

The manometer gives the difference in piezometric head. To obtain the pressure difference, subtract the elevation difference before converting the remaining pressure head to pressure.

The centerline elevation difference, $2.40\,\mathrm{m}$, accounts for more than half the head difference, leaving a pressure difference of $19.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With both taps at the same elevation, the same reading would correspond to $43.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

Check by following the tube from one end to the other. The two water columns and the mercury column together give the same pressure difference.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two separate pipes of still water are connected to an inverted U-tube manometer containing oil of specific gravity $\delta = 0.80$, as shown. The centerline of pipe $B$ is $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ above that of $A$. The interfaces on the $A$ and $B$ sides are at elevations $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the centerline of pipe $A$. Given $p_A = 60\,\mathrm{kPa}$, find the piezometric head difference and the pressure at $B$.

::schema{nodi="P,0,0,-;Q,1.6,0,-;R,3.4,0.3,-;S,5,0.3,-" condotte="P-Q,0.35;R-S,0.35" acqua="1,0 1.15,0 1.15,2 1,2;4,0.3 4.15,0.3 4.15,2.5 4,2.5" poligoni="1,2 1,2.8 4.15,2.8 4.15,2.5 4,2.5 4,2.65 1.15,2.65 1.15,2" tratteggi="1.15,2 2.75,2;4,2.5 2.45,2.5" punti="0.8,0,A;4.4,0.3,B" quote="2.6 2,2.6 2.5,Δ,0.45" etichette="2.6,3.05,oil δ = 0.80" riferimento=0 titolo="Inverted U-tube differential manometer containing oil lighter than water"}

The differential-manometer equation also applies when its working fluid is lighter than water, provided both connecting tubes are filled with water. Using $\Delta = 2.50 - 2.00 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\delta = 0.80$ gives
$$
h_A - h_B = \Delta\,(\delta - 1) = 0.50 \cdot (0.80 - 1) = -0.100\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
so the piezometric head at $B$ exceeds that at $A$ by $0.100\,\mathrm{m}$. The head at $A$ is
$$
h_A = z_A + \frac{p_A}{\gamma} = 0 + \frac{60}{9.81} = 6.116\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_B = h_A + 0.100 = 6.216\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
Use the definition of piezometric head to find the pressure at $B$.
$$
p_B = \gamma\,(h_B - z_B) = 9.81 \cdot (6.216 - 0.30) = 9.81 \cdot 5.916 = 58.0\,\mathrm{kPa} .
$$

Check by tracing the tube, using $z_A = 0$ and $z_B = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
p_1 &= p_A - \gamma \cdot 2.00 = 60 - 19.62 = 40.38\,\mathrm{kPa} \\[6pt]
p_2 &= p_1 - 0.80\,\gamma \cdot 0.50 = 40.38 - 3.92 = 36.46\,\mathrm{kPa} \\[6pt]
p_B &= p_2 + \gamma \cdot (2.50 - 0.30) = 36.46 + 21.58 = 58.0\,\mathrm{kPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The manometer fluid is lighter than water, so $\delta - 1$ is negative. The difference in piezometric head has the opposite sign to the meniscus level difference. The higher meniscus is on the $B$ side, so $B$ has the greater head.

An inverted U-tube is useful for measuring small head differences. With oil the conversion factor is $0.20$ instead of $12.6$, so a level difference of $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ corresponds to a head difference of only $0.100\,\mathrm{m}$. The instrument thus magnifies the reading.

The pressure at $B$ is **$58.0\,\mathrm{kPa}$**, lower than at $A$ despite the higher piezometric head, because pipe $B$ lies above $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$.

For a check, trace the tube from $A$ up to the first interface, through the oil, and back down to $B$. The result agrees.

</div>
