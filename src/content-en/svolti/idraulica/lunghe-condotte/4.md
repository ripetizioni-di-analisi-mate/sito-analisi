## Exercise 1

A pipe of length $2400\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.024$ connects reservoirs with free-surface elevations $60\,\mathrm{m}$ and $28\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the datum. The pipe centerline starts at elevation $58\,\mathrm{m}$, reaches elevation $52\,\mathrm{m}$ after $900\,\mathrm{m}$ and then descends more steeply to the outlet, as shown. Find the discharge and the pressure at the section $900\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance.

::schema{nodi="A,1.8,5.05,-;C,5.2,4.3,-;E,8,2.7,-;U,11,1,-" acqua="0,5.4 1.8,5.4 1.8,3.7 0,3.7;11,1 12.6,1 12.6,-0.6 11,-0.6" solidi="0,3.4 1.8,3.4 1.8,3.7 0,3.7;11,-0.9 12.6,-0.9 12.6,-0.6 11,-0.6" condotte="A-C-E-U,0.16" pelo="0,1.8,5.4,60.0 m;11,12.6,1,28.0 m" etichette="6.4,5.05,52.0 m at 900 m;2.6,4.15,58.0 m;6.4,1.7,D=300 mm;6.4,1.1,L=2400 m" titolo="Pipeline profile showing the section $900\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance"}

$$
J = \frac{60 - 28}{2400} = 0.01333
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300 \cdot 0.01333}{0.024}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{3.270}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.81\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 1.81 \cdot 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.128\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 128\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
h(900) = z_1 - J \cdot 900 = 60 - 0.01333 \cdot 900\,\mathrm{m} = 60 - 12.0\,\mathrm{m} = 48.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = h(900) - z(900) = 48.0 - 52.0\,\mathrm{m} = -4.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p = \gamma\,\frac{p}{\gamma} = 9.81 \cdot (-4.00)\,\mathrm{kPa} = -39.2\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check at the outlet section and calculate the Reynolds number:
$$
h(2400) = 60 - 0.01333 \cdot 2400\,\mathrm{m} = 28.0\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
Re = \frac{1.81 \cdot 0.300}{10^{-6}} = 5.4 \cdot 10^{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate discharge as for any long pipeline connecting two reservoirs. Their elevation difference of $32\,\mathrm{m}$ gives the energy gradient, the Darcy–Weisbach equation gives the velocity, and continuity gives the discharge. The intervening elevations do not enter this calculation because the energy balance between reservoir surfaces contains only the end elevations and total head loss.

The pipe profile determines the pressures. The hydraulic grade line descends from elevation $60\,\mathrm{m}$ at gradient $J$. At a distance of $900\,\mathrm{m}$ the piezometric head is therefore $48.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Subtract the pipe centerline elevation to obtain pressure head $-4.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The centerline is above the hydraulic grade line here, so the **gauge pressure is negative**.

The gauge pressure is $-39.2\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Its magnitude is far smaller than the pressure reduction of $10.09\,\mathrm{m}$ of water at which vaporization would occur, so operation is satisfactory. As a check, the piezometric head at the outlet equals the downstream reservoir water level, as required by the energy balance between the free surfaces.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pipe of length $3000\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$ connects reservoirs whose free surfaces are at $85\,\mathrm{m}$ and $50\,\mathrm{m}$. The diagram gives centerline elevations at the indicated sections. Find the discharge and gauge pressures at $800\,\mathrm{m}$, $1600\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2400\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance. Identify the section of lowest pressure.

::schema{nodi="A,1.8,4.1,-;B,4.2,3.6,-;C,6.5,3.2,-;E,8.9,0.9,-;U,11.2,0.6,-" acqua="0,5 1.8,5 1.8,3.7 0,3.7;11.2,0.6 12.8,0.6 12.8,-1 11.2,-1" solidi="0,3.4 1.8,3.4 1.8,3.7 0,3.7;11.2,-1.3 12.8,-1.3 12.8,-1 11.2,-1" condotte="A-B-C-E-U,0.18" pelo="0,1.8,5,85.0 m;11.2,12.8,0.6,50.0 m" etichette="4.2,4.2,76.0 m;6.5,3.8,73.0 m;9.6,1.5,52.0 m;2.6,4.7,80.0 m;4.3,2.6,D=400 mm" titolo="Profile with centerline elevations at sections $800$, $1600$ and $2400\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
J = \frac{85 - 50}{3000} = 0.01167
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.400 \cdot 0.01167}{0.022}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 2.04 \cdot 0.1257\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.256\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 256\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

| $x$ (m) | $z$ (m) | $h = 85 - J x$ (m) | $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = h - z$ (m) | $z + J x$ (m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0$ | $80.0$ | $85.00$ | $+5.00$ | $80.00$ |
| $800$ | $76.0$ | $75.67$ | $-0.33$ | $85.33$ |
| $1600$ | $73.0$ | $66.33$ | $-6.67$ | $91.67$ |
| $2400$ | $52.0$ | $57.00$ | $+5.00$ | $80.00$ |
| $3000$ | $50.0$ | $50.00$ | $0.00$ | $85.00$ |

$$
\left(\frac{p}{\gamma}\right)_{\min} = -6.67\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad\text{at}\qquad
x = 1600\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check using the quantity $z + J x$:
$$
\max_x \big(z + J x\big) = 91.67\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{p_{\min}}{\gamma} = 85 - 91.67 = -6.67\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discharge calculation depends only on the elevation difference $35\,\mathrm{m}$ between the free surfaces. It gives a discharge of $256\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and a velocity of $2.04\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The Reynolds number is $8.2 \cdot 10^{5}$, so the flow is turbulent.

The third column gives piezometric head, the hydraulic grade line elevation at each section. The fourth gives pressure head, the signed vertical distance from the pipe centerline to the grade line. Gauge pressure is negative at $800\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1600\,\mathrm{m}$, where the pipe rises above the grade line, and positive at the other sections.

The last column identifies the minimum pressure. Since $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = z_1 - (z + Jx)$, pressure is lowest where $z + Jx$ reaches its maximum. This occurs at $1600\,\mathrm{m}$, the **critical point** of the pipeline, where the pressure head is $-6.67\,\mathrm{m}$ of water.

This is above the practical lower limit of $-7\,\mathrm{m}$, so the pipe can operate with a small margin. Calculating the minimum pressure directly from the maximum of $z + Jx$ gives the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pipe of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.026$ leads from a reservoir whose water surface is at $45\,\mathrm{m}$ and discharges freely into the atmosphere at elevation $12\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. At its midpoint the pipe centerline is at elevation $30\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the discharge, the midpoint pressure and the outlet velocity head.

::schema{nodi="A,1.8,4.2,-;C,6.4,2.6,-;U,11,1.2,-" acqua="0,5.4 1.8,5.4 1.8,3.8 0,3.8" solidi="0,3.5 1.8,3.5 1.8,3.8 0,3.8" condotte="A-C-U,0.16" pelo="0,1.8,5.4,45.0 m" frecce="11.2,1.2,12.1,1.2,v" etichette="6.4,3.35,30.0 m;6.4,1.7,D=250 mm;6.4,1.1,L=1500 m;11.5,0.7,12.0 m" titolo="Pipe discharging freely into the atmosphere at elevation $12\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
J = \frac{45 - 12}{1500} = 0.02200
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250 \cdot 0.02200}{0.026}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 2.04 \cdot 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.100\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
h(750) = 45 - 0.02200 \cdot 750\,\mathrm{m} = 45 - 16.5\,\mathrm{m} = 28.5\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 28.5 - 30.0 = -1.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{2.04^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.212\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{0.212}{33} = 0.0064
$$

Check with the full energy balance, including outlet velocity head:
$$
33 = \left(\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + 1\right)\frac{v^2}{2g} = (156 + 1)\,\frac{v^2}{2g}
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 33}{157}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.03\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pipe discharges directly to the atmosphere, so its outlet has zero gauge pressure and its piezometric head equals the centerline elevation, $12\,\mathrm{m}$. The available head is therefore $45 - 12 = 33\,\mathrm{m}$, measured between the reservoir surface and the outlet centerline. There is no second free surface in this balance.

The hydraulic grade line falls from elevation $45\,\mathrm{m}$ to $12\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant gradient $J = 0.0220$. At midlength the piezometric head is $28.5\,\mathrm{m}$ and the pipe centerline is at $30.0\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a gauge pressure head of $-1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, or a gauge pressure of $-14.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

The outlet velocity head is $0.212\,\mathrm{m}$, only $0.64\,\%$ of the available head, so it can be neglected. Retaining it adds $1$ to the term $\lambda\,\dfrac{L}{D} = 156$, giving $v = 2.03\,\mathrm{m/s}$ instead of $2.04\,\mathrm{m/s}$, a difference of three parts in a thousand. The discharge is **$100\,\mathrm{L/s}$** toward the outlet.

</div>
