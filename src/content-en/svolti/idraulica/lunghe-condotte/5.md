## Exercise 1

A pipe of length $2500\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.025$ connects reservoirs whose free surfaces are at $70\,\mathrm{m}$ and $40\,\mathrm{m}$. At a distance $1000\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance, its centerline passes over a summit at elevation $68\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Check the summit pressure with unrestricted flow. If it falls below $-7\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, calculate the maximum allowable discharge and the gate-valve loss coefficient needed to obtain it.

::schema{nodi="A,1.8,4.3,-;C,5.64,5.13,-;V,10.8,1.5,-;U,11.4,1.4,-" acqua="0,5.4 1.8,5.4 1.8,3.9 0,3.9;11.4,1.4 13,1.4 13,-0.2 11.4,-0.2" solidi="0,3.6 1.8,3.6 1.8,3.9 0,3.9;11.4,-0.5 13,-0.5 13,-0.2 11.4,-0.2" condotte="A-C-V-U,0.16" pelo="0,1.8,5.4,70.0 m;11.4,13,1.4,40.0 m" etichette="5.64,5.6,high point 68.0 m;6.6,1.9,D=300 mm;6.6,1.2,L=2500 m;11.9,2.3,gate valve" titolo="Pipe with a summit $1000\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance and a downstream gate valve"}

Unthrottled pipeline:
$$
J_0 = \frac{70 - 40}{2500} = 0.01200
\qquad
v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300 \cdot 0.01200}{0.025}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.68\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q_0 = 1.68 \cdot 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.119\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
h(1000) = 70 - 0.01200 \cdot 1000\,\mathrm{m} = 58.0\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 58.0 - 68.0 = -10.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Allowable hydraulic gradient and corresponding discharge:
$$
J_{\mathrm{allow}} = \frac{z_1 - z_c - \dfrac{p_{\min}}{\gamma}}{x_c} = \frac{70 - 68 - (-7)}{1000} = \frac{9}{1000} = 0.00900
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300 \cdot 0.00900}{0.025}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.46\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
Q = 1.46 \cdot 0.0707\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.103\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{cont}} = J_{\mathrm{allow}}\,L = 0.00900 \cdot 2500\,\mathrm{m} = 22.5\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\Delta H_{\mathrm{sar}} = 30 - 22.5 = 7.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.46^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.108\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\xi = \frac{7.5}{0.108} = 69.4
$$

Check using the overall energy balance:
$$
\left(\lambda\,\frac{L}{D} + \xi\right)\frac{v^2}{2g}
= \left(0.025 \cdot \frac{2500}{0.300} + 69.4\right) \cdot 0.108\,\mathrm{m}
= (208.3 + 69.4) \cdot 0.108\,\mathrm{m} = 30.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the pipe unthrottled, the pressure head at the summit is $-10.0\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, effectively the value at which the water reaches its vapor pressure. This operating condition is unacceptable and the discharge must be reduced.

Reducing the discharge reduces the hydraulic gradient. The hydraulic grade line drops less and the summit pressure rises. Imposing $\dfrac{p}{\gamma} = -7\,\mathrm{m}$ at the summit gives the allowable gradient $J_{\mathrm{allow}} = 0.00900$ and the maximum discharge, $103\,\mathrm{L/s}$ compared with $119\,\mathrm{L/s}$ for the unthrottled pipe.

At this gradient, pipe friction uses $22.5\,\mathrm{m}$ of the available $30\,\mathrm{m}$. The remaining head of $7.5\,\mathrm{m}$ must be dissipated by a **gate valve** with loss coefficient $\xi = 69.4$. Install it downstream of the summit because a minor loss lowers the hydraulic grade line only downstream of the fitting. Placing it upstream would reduce the already low summit pressure.

The check adds pipe friction and valve loss, both expressed as multiples of the same velocity head, recovering the elevation difference of $30\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pipe of length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.0245$ connects reservoirs with free surfaces at $55\,\mathrm{m}$ and $25\,\mathrm{m}$. Its route crosses a ridge $600\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance, as shown. Find the discharge and the maximum pipe centerline elevation at the ridge that maintains pressure of at least $-7\,\mathrm{m}$ of water.

::schema{nodi="A,1.8,4.3,-;C,4.4,4.8,-;U,11.2,1.2,-" acqua="0,5.4 1.8,5.4 1.8,3.9 0,3.9;11.2,1.2 12.8,1.2 12.8,-0.4 11.2,-0.4" solidi="0,3.6 1.8,3.6 1.8,3.9 0,3.9;11.2,-0.7 12.8,-0.7 12.8,-0.4 11.2,-0.4" condotte="A-C-U,0.16" pelo="0,1.8,5.4,55.0 m;11.2,12.8,1.2,25.0 m" etichette="4.4,5.4,high point at 600 m;6.3,1.9,D=250 mm;6.3,1.2,L=2000 m" titolo="Ridge crossing $600\,\mathrm{m}$ from the entrance"}

$$
J = \frac{55 - 25}{2000} = 0.01500
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250 \cdot 0.01500}{0.0245}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.73\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 1.73 \cdot 0.0491\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0851\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 85.1\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
h(600) = 55 - 0.01500 \cdot 600\,\mathrm{m} = 55 - 9.0\,\mathrm{m} = 46.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
z_{\max} = h(600) - \frac{p_{\min}}{\gamma} = 46.0 - (-7.0)\,\mathrm{m} = 53.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check using a pipe centerline elevation of $48.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at the same section:
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 46.0 - 48.0\,\mathrm{m} = -2.00\,\mathrm{m} > -7.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discharge depends only on the elevation difference between the reservoir surfaces and can be calculated before setting the alignment. It is $85.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at a velocity of $1.73\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with Reynolds number $4.3 \cdot 10^{5}$.

The hydraulic grade line is therefore fixed. At the ridge the piezometric head is $46.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The pressure condition is $h - z \geq -7.0\,\mathrm{m}$, giving $z \leq h + 7.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and a maximum centerline elevation of $53.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The centerline may lie at most $7\,\mathrm{m}$ above the hydraulic grade line.

The maximum permissible centerline elevation is $2\,\mathrm{m}$ above the upstream reservoir level. Negative gauge pressure allows the pipe to rise above the hydrostatic head plane as long as the pressure stays within its allowable range. At the lower ridge crossing tested, $48.0\,\mathrm{m}$, the pressure is $-2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, leaving ample margin above the minimum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pipe of length $2800\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 400\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.0225$ connects reservoirs whose free surfaces are at $92\,\mathrm{m}$ and $62\,\mathrm{m}$. The diagram shows centerline elevations at the marked sections. Identify the critical point, check its pressure and find the highest discharge that maintains pressure of at least $-7\,\mathrm{m}$ of water.

::schema{nodi="A,1.8,4.1,-;B,4,4.4,-;C,6.2,4,-;E,8.4,2.8,-;U,10.6,1,-" acqua="0,5 1.8,5 1.8,3.7 0,3.7;10.6,1 12.2,1 12.2,-0.6 10.6,-0.6" solidi="0,3.4 1.8,3.4 1.8,3.7 0,3.7;10.6,-0.9 12.2,-0.9 12.2,-0.6 10.6,-0.6" condotte="A-B-C-E-U,0.18" pelo="0,1.8,5,92.0 m;10.6,12.2,1,62.0 m" etichette="3.5,5,86.0 m at 700 m;7.2,4.5,84.0 m at 1400 m;9.3,3.8,78.0 m at 2100 m;4.5,1.8,D=400 mm" titolo="Profile showing three intermediate sections in addition to the entrance and exit"}

$$
J = \frac{92 - 62}{2800} = 0.01071
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.400 \cdot 0.01071}{0.0225}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.93\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 1.93 \cdot 0.1257\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.243\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

| $x$ (m) | $z$ (m) | $z + J x$ (m) | $h = 92 - J x$ (m) | $\dfrac{p}{\gamma}$ (m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $700$ | $86.0$ | $93.50$ | $84.50$ | $-1.50$ |
| $1400$ | $84.0$ | $99.00$ | $77.00$ | $-7.00$ |
| $2100$ | $78.0$ | $100.50$ | $69.50$ | $-8.50$ |

The critical point is at $x = 2100\,\mathrm{m}$. The allowable gradient is
$$
J_{\mathrm{allow}} = \frac{92 - 78 - (-7)}{2100} = \frac{21}{2100} = 0.01000
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.400 \cdot 0.01000}{0.0225}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.87\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
Q = 1.87 \cdot 0.1257\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.235\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{sar}} = 30 - 0.01000 \cdot 2800\,\mathrm{m} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{v^2}{2g} = 0.178\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\xi = \frac{2.00}{0.178} = 11.2
$$

Check the section at $1400\,\mathrm{m}$ using the allowable gradient:
$$
\frac{p}{\gamma} = 92 - 0.01000 \cdot 1400 - 84.0\,\mathrm{m} = -6.00\,\mathrm{m} > -7.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The highest point of the alignment is at $1400\,\mathrm{m}$, with elevation $84.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The **critical point** is at $2100\,\mathrm{m}$, six meters lower. The accumulated head loss $J x$ increases by $7.5\,\mathrm{m}$ between the two sections, while the centerline elevation decreases by only $6\,\mathrm{m}$. The sum $z + Jx$ is therefore greater at the second section. Its minimum pressure head, $-8.50\,\mathrm{m}$ of water, violates the allowable limit.

Impose the limiting pressure at the critical section to obtain the allowable gradient $J_{\mathrm{allow}} = 0.01000$. The discharge becomes $235\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in place of $243\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The reduction is small because discharge varies as the square root of the gradient.

The head left after pipe friction is $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. A gate valve with $\xi = 11.2$ downstream of the critical section dissipates it.

Check the section at $1400\,\mathrm{m}$ using the new gradient. The pressure head of $-6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is also within the limit, as expected because the critical point is the location of minimum pressure.

</div>
