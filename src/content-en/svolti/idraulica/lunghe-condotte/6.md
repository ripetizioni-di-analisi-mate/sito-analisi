## Exercise 1

A pump delivers $Q = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from a well to a reservoir whose free surface lies $42\,\mathrm{m}$ above the well water level. The discharge pipe shown has length $900\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.023$. The overall efficiency is $\eta = 0.72$. Find the pump head, hydraulic output power and input power.

::schema{nodi="A,1.6,1,-;P,3.6,1,-;C,7.2,4.4,-;U,10.4,4.4,-" acqua="0,1.4 1.6,1.4 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,5.6 12,5.6 12,4 10.4,4" solidi="0,-0.7 1.6,-0.7 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,3.7 12,3.7 12,4 10.4,4" condotte="A-P-C-U,0.16" pompa="3.6,1,P" pelo="0,1.6,1.4,well;10.4,12,5.6,reservoir" tratteggi="1.6,1.4 13,1.4;10.4,5.6 13,5.6" quote="13 1.4,13 5.6,42.0 m" etichette="8.6,3.4,D=200 mm;8.6,2.8,L=900 m;8.6,2.2,Q=40 L/s" titolo="Pumping from a well to a reservoir"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 0.200^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.03142\,\mathrm{m^2}
\qquad
v = \frac{0.040}{0.03142}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.273\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.273^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0826\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
J = \frac{\lambda}{D}\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{0.023}{0.200} \cdot 0.0826 = 9.50 \cdot 10^{-3}
$$
$$
\Delta H = J\,L = 9.50 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 900\,\mathrm{m} = 8.55\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_p = (z_2 - z_1) + \Delta H = 42 + 8.55\,\mathrm{m} = 50.6\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
P_u = \gamma\,Q\,H_p = 9.81 \cdot 0.040 \cdot 50.6\,\mathrm{kW} = 19.8\,\mathrm{kW}
\qquad
P = \frac{P_u}{\eta} = \frac{19.8}{0.72}\,\mathrm{kW} = 27.6\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

Check by separating the two contributions to pump input power:
$$
\frac{\gamma Q (z_2 - z_1)}{\eta} + \frac{\gamma Q \Delta H}{\eta}
= \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.040 \cdot 42}{0.72} + \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.040 \cdot 8.55}{0.72}\,\mathrm{kW}
= 22.9 + 4.66\,\mathrm{kW} = 27.6\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The specified discharge gives the velocity and head loss directly. In the energy balance between the two free surfaces, the head rises by $H_p$ at the pump. The **pump head** therefore equals the static lift plus the total pipeline loss.

The static lift accounts for $42\,\mathrm{m}$ and the loss for $8.55\,\mathrm{m}$, or $17\,\%$ of the total. Most of the energy lifts the water, while the remainder overcomes resistance. The Reynolds number is $2.5 \cdot 10^{5}$, so the flow is turbulent.

Hydraulic power is the weight of water raised per unit time times the pump head, giving $19.8\,\mathrm{kW}$. The pump requires greater input because its overall efficiency is $0.72$. The **input power** is $27.6\,\mathrm{kW}$.

Separating the input power into lift and resistance components reproduces the total. Pipe friction accounts for $4.66\,\mathrm{kW}$, about one sixth of the power.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pump draws an input power of $P = 15\,\mathrm{kW}$ at efficiency $\eta = 0.70$ and raises water through an elevation difference of $25\,\mathrm{m}$ in a pipe of length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.024$, as shown. Find the discharge.

::schema{nodi="A,1.6,1,-;P,3.6,1,-;C,7.2,3.8,-;U,10.4,3.8,-" acqua="0,1.4 1.6,1.4 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,5 12,5 12,3.4 10.4,3.4" solidi="0,-0.7 1.6,-0.7 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,3.1 12,3.1 12,3.4 10.4,3.4" condotte="A-P-C-U,0.16" pompa="3.6,1,P" pelo="0,1.6,1.4,upstream;10.4,12,5,downstream" tratteggi="1.6,1.4 13,1.4;10.4,5 13,5" quote="13 1.4,13 5,25.0 m" etichette="8.6,2.9,D=250 mm;8.6,2.3,L=1200 m;5.8,0.4,P=15 kW" titolo="Pumping system with specified input power"}

$$
r = \frac{8\,\lambda\,L}{\pi^2 g\,D^5} = \frac{8 \cdot 0.024 \cdot 1200}{9.87 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250^5}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 2437\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
H_p = 25 + 2437\,Q^2 \\[6pt]
H_p = \dfrac{\eta\,P}{\gamma\,Q} = \dfrac{0.70 \cdot 15}{9.81\,Q} = \dfrac{1.0703}{Q}
\end{cases}
$$

Start the iteration at $Q_0 = 0.0400\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and stop when successive values differ by less than $0.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$:

| Step | Initial $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) | $H_p$ (m) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $0.04000$ | $28.90$ | $0.03704$ |
| 2 | $0.03704$ | $28.34$ | $0.03776$ |
| 3 | $0.03776$ | $28.48$ | $0.03759$ |
| 4 | $0.03759$ | $28.44$ | $0.03763$ |
| 5 | $0.03763$ | $28.45$ | $0.03762$ |

$$
Q = 0.0376\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 37.6\,\mathrm{L/s}
\qquad
H_p = 28.4\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
v = \frac{0.0376}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.766\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

Check the pump input power:
$$
P = \frac{\gamma\,Q\,H_p}{\eta} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.0376 \cdot 28.4}{0.70}\,\mathrm{kW} = 15.0\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Discharge enters both equations. In the first, head loss increases with discharge squared. In the second, the definition of input power makes pump head inversely proportional to discharge at fixed power. Both equations must hold simultaneously.

Eliminating $H_p$ would give the cubic $2437\,Q^3 + 25\,Q = 1.0703$. Successive approximation is more convenient. Choose a trial discharge, calculate the pump head from the first relation and update the discharge using the second.

The iteration converges in five steps, with diminishing oscillations because the two relations vary in opposite directions with $Q$. The discharge is **$37.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$** and the pump head is $28.4\,\mathrm{m}$, made up of $25\,\mathrm{m}$ of static lift and $3.4\,\mathrm{m}$ of head loss.

The calculated values reproduce the specified input power of $15\,\mathrm{kW}$. The velocity is $0.77\,\mathrm{m/s}$, low but still within the usual range.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pumping station delivers $Q = 60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ through a static lift of $30\,\mathrm{m}$ in the pipe shown, of length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.0225$. Its overall efficiency is $\eta = 0.75$. It runs $16$ hours per day for $300$ days per year, and the electricity price is $0.20\,\text{€/kWh}$. Calculate the input power, annual energy consumption and annual energy cost.

::schema{nodi="A,1.6,1,-;P,3.6,1,-;C,7.2,4.2,-;U,10.4,4.2,-" acqua="0,1.4 1.6,1.4 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,5.4 12,5.4 12,3.8 10.4,3.8" solidi="0,-0.7 1.6,-0.7 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,3.5 12,3.5 12,3.8 10.4,3.8" condotte="A-P-C-U,0.16" pompa="3.6,1,P" pelo="0,1.6,1.4,upstream;10.4,12,5.4,downstream" tratteggi="1.6,1.4 13,1.4;10.4,5.4 13,5.4" quote="13 1.4,13 5.4,30.0 m" etichette="8.6,3.2,D=250 mm;8.6,2.6,L=1500 m;8.6,2,Q=60 L/s" titolo="Pumping system operating $16$ hours per day"}

$$
v = \frac{0.060}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.222\,\mathrm{m/s}
\qquad
\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{1.222^2}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0761\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
J = \frac{0.0225}{0.250} \cdot 0.0761 = 6.85 \cdot 10^{-3}
\qquad
\Delta H = 6.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1500\,\mathrm{m} = 10.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
H_p = 30 + 10.3\,\mathrm{m} = 40.3\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
P = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.060 \cdot 40.3}{0.75}\,\mathrm{kW} = 31.6\,\mathrm{kW}
$$
$$
E = P \cdot 16 \cdot 300 = 31.6 \cdot 4800\,\mathrm{kWh} = 1.52 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{kWh}
$$
$$
C = 1.52 \cdot 10^{5} \cdot 0.20\,\text{€} = 3.03 \cdot 10^{4}\,\text{€}
$$

Check using hydraulic resistance:
$$
r = \frac{8 \cdot 0.0225 \cdot 1500}{9.87 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250^5}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 2856\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
\qquad
\Delta H = 2856 \cdot 0.060^2\,\mathrm{m} = 10.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate head and input power as in the first exercise. The head loss is $10.3\,\mathrm{m}$, compared with $30\,\mathrm{m}$ of static lift, so about a quarter of the pump head overcomes pipe resistance.

The annual energy consumption equals input power multiplied by annual operating hours, $4800$, giving $152\,000\,\mathrm{kWh}$. At the stated electricity price the annual cost is about $30\,300\,\text{€}$. Head loss alone accounts for $\dfrac{10.3}{40.3}$ of the total, about $7700\,\text{€}$ per year. This is the energy cost affected by the choice of diameter.

The check recalculates the loss from hydraulic resistance, based only on geometry and roughness, and gives $10.3\,\mathrm{m}$ again.

</div>
