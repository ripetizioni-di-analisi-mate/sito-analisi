## Exercise 1

A pump has characteristic curve $H_p = 55 - 900\,Q^2$, with $Q$ in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and $H_p$ in meters. The static lift is $30\,\mathrm{m}$ and the pipe shown has length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.025$. Find the discharge and head at the operating point and the input power for $\eta = 0.76$.

::schema{nodi="A,1.6,1,-;P,3.6,1,-;C,7.2,4,-;U,10.4,4,-" acqua="0,1.4 1.6,1.4 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,5.2 12,5.2 12,3.6 10.4,3.6" solidi="0,-0.7 1.6,-0.7 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,3.3 12,3.3 12,3.6 10.4,3.6" condotte="A-P-C-U,0.16" pompa="3.6,1,P" pelo="0,1.6,1.4,upstream;10.4,12,5.2,downstream" tratteggi="1.6,1.4 13,1.4;10.4,5.2 13,5.2" quote="13 1.4,13 5.2,30.0 m" etichette="8.6,3.1,D=250 mm;8.6,2.5,L=1000 m" titolo="Pumping system with characteristic curve $H_p = 55 - 900\,Q^2$"}

$$
r = \frac{8 \cdot 0.025 \cdot 1000}{9.87 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.250^5}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 2115\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
H = 55 - 900\,Q^2 \\[6pt]
H = 30 + 2115\,Q^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
55 - 900\,Q^2 = 30 + 2115\,Q^2
\qquad
3015\,Q^2 = 25
\qquad
Q^2 = 8.29 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^6/s^2}
$$
$$
Q = 0.0911\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 91.1\,\mathrm{L/s}
\qquad
H = 30 + 2115 \cdot 8.29 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} = 47.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
P = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.0911 \cdot 47.5}{0.76}\,\mathrm{kW} = 55.9\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

::grafico{f="30+2115*x^2;55-900*x^2" xmin=0 xmax=0.14 ymin=20 ymax=60 punti="0.0911,47.5,pieno,F" nomi="Q,H" etichette="0.1,32,pipe;0.03,52,pump" titolo="The two characteristic curves intersect at point $F$"}

Check against the pump curve:
$$
H = 55 - 900 \cdot 8.29 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} = 55 - 7.46\,\mathrm{m} = 47.5\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
v = \frac{0.0911}{0.0491}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 1.86\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system curve gives the pump head required to deliver a given discharge, $H_c = 30 + r Q^2$. The constant term is the static lift and the quadratic term is head loss. The pump curve gives the head the machine can deliver. The operating point satisfies both curves, where the two heads coincide.

Equating the two right-hand sides gives an equation in $Q^2$ alone, yielding $Q = 91.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Substitution in the system curve gives the operating head, $47.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

The pump curve falls and the system curve rises. They intersect at only one point, so the **operating point** is unique.

Substituting the discharge into the pump curve gives the same head, confirming that the point lies on both curves. The velocity is $1.86\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the pump input power is $55.9\,\mathrm{kW}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The discharge gate valve in the preceding installation is partially closed, raising its loss coefficient to $\xi = 20$, as shown. Calculate the new discharge, new pump head and head loss across the gate valve.

::schema{nodi="A,1.6,1,-;P,3.6,1,-;V,5.4,2.6,-;C,7.2,4,-;U,10.4,4,-" acqua="0,1.4 1.6,1.4 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,5.2 12,5.2 12,3.6 10.4,3.6" solidi="0,-0.7 1.6,-0.7 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10.4,3.3 12,3.3 12,3.6 10.4,3.6" condotte="A-P-V-C-U,0.16" pompa="3.6,1,P" pelo="0,1.6,1.4,upstream;10.4,12,5.2,downstream" tratteggi="1.6,1.4 13,1.4;10.4,5.2 13,5.2" quote="13 1.4,13 5.2,30.0 m" etichette="7.6,0.5,gate valve ξ=20;8.6,3.1,D=250 mm;8.6,2.5,L=1000 m" titolo="The same system with its discharge gate valve partially closed"}

$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{sar}} = \xi\,\frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{\xi}{2 g A^2}\,Q^2
\qquad
2 g A^2 = 19.62 \cdot 0.0491^2\,\mathrm{m^4} = 4.728 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^4}
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{sar}} = \frac{20}{4.728 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 423\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{tot}} = 2115 + 423 = 2538\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
55 - 900\,Q^2 = 30 + 2538\,Q^2
\qquad
3438\,Q^2 = 25
\qquad
Q^2 = 7.27 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^6/s^2}
$$
$$
Q = 0.0853\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 85.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
\qquad
H = 30 + 2538 \cdot 7.27 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} = 48.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta H_{\mathrm{sar}} = 423 \cdot 7.27 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} = 3.08\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
\frac{91.1 - 85.3}{91.1} = 0.064
$$

Check against the pump curve:
$$
H = 55 - 900 \cdot 7.27 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} = 48.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate valve adds a loss proportional to discharge squared and therefore increases the hydraulic resistance of the pipe. The system curve retains the form $H_c = 30 + r_{\mathrm{tot}} Q^2$ but becomes steeper. The pump curve is unchanged.

The operating point moves up and to the left. The discharge falls from $91.1$ to $85.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$, a decrease of $6.4\,\%$, while the head increases from $47.5$ to $48.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The pump curve requires this lower-discharge, higher-head operation.

Of the pump head $48.5\,\mathrm{m}$, the valve dissipates $3.08\,\mathrm{m}$ without providing useful lift. Throttling with a gate valve is therefore simple but costly to operate.

Substituting the new discharge into the pump curve gives the same head.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pump with characteristic curve $H_p = 48 - 700\,Q^2$ fills a reservoir through the pipe shown, of length $800\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.024$. The static lift is $20\,\mathrm{m}$ at the start of filling and $34\,\mathrm{m}$ when the reservoir is full. Find the discharge in each condition and the input power for $\eta = 0.78$.

::schema{nodi="A,1.6,1,-;P,3.4,1,-;C,7,3.4,-;U,10,3.4,-" acqua="0,1.4 1.6,1.4 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10,3.4 11.8,3.4 11.8,3 10,3" solidi="0,-0.7 1.6,-0.7 1.6,-0.4 0,-0.4;10,2.7 11.8,2.7 11.8,3 10,3" condotte="A-P-C-U,0.18" pompa="3.4,1,P" pelo="0,1.6,1.4,upstream;10,11.8,3.4,start" tratteggi="1.6,1.4 12.8,1.4;10,3.4 12.8,3.4;10,4.8 12.8,4.8" quote="12.8 1.4,12.8 3.4,20.0 m" etichette="8.3,2.5,D=300 mm;8.3,1.9,L=800 m;11.4,5.1,when full: 34.0 m" titolo="Static lift changes from $20$ to $34\,\mathrm{m}$ as the reservoir fills"}

$$
r = \frac{8 \cdot 0.024 \cdot 800}{9.87 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.300^5}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 653\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$

At the start of filling, $\Delta z_g = 20\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
48 - 700\,Q^2 = 20 + 653\,Q^2
\qquad
1353\,Q^2 = 28
\qquad
Q = 0.144\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 144\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
H = 20 + 653 \cdot 0.0207\,\mathrm{m} = 33.5\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
P = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.144 \cdot 33.5}{0.78}\,\mathrm{kW} = 60.6\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

With the reservoir full, $\Delta z_g = 34\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
48 - 700\,Q^2 = 34 + 653\,Q^2
\qquad
1353\,Q^2 = 14
\qquad
Q = 0.102\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 102\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
H = 34 + 653 \cdot 0.0103\,\mathrm{m} = 40.8\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
P = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.102 \cdot 40.8}{0.78}\,\mathrm{kW} = 52.3\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

Check both operating conditions against the pump curve:
$$
48 - 700 \cdot 0.0207\,\mathrm{m} = 33.5\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad
48 - 700 \cdot 0.0103\,\mathrm{m} = 40.8\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the reservoir fills, the downstream water surface rises and the static lift increases. The system curve shifts upward without changing slope because resistance $r$ depends only on the pipe geometry and roughness.

The operating point moves to the left along the pump curve. Discharge falls from $144$ to $102\,\mathrm{L/s}$, a decrease of $29\,\%$, while head rises from $33.5$ to $40.8\,\mathrm{m}$. The corresponding velocities are $2.04$ and $1.44\,\mathrm{m/s}$, both within the usual range.

The input power falls from $60.6$ to $52.3\,\mathrm{kW}$. The head rises, but the discharge decreases enough to reduce the product $Q H$. Calculate the filling time using the varying discharge throughout filling.

Substitution of the two discharges in the pump curve gives the two calculated heads.

</div>
