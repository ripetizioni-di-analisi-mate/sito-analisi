## Exercise 1

Two reservoirs whose water surfaces differ in elevation by $\Delta z = 18.00\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by the two pipes in series shown. Section $1$ has length $L_1 = 800\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $D_1 = 300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and Darcy friction factor $\lambda_1 = 0.022$. Section $2$ has length $L_2 = 600\,\mathrm{m}$ with $D_2 = 250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.024$. Calculate the discharge and the head loss in each section.

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,17;N,15,12,-;V,28,0,-;P,30.5,20,-;R,30.5,3,-" condotte="A-N,0.7,;N-V,0.6," acqua="0,13 3.5,13 3.5,20 0,20;26,-2 30.5,-2 30.5,3 26,3" solidi="0,12 3.5,12 3.5,13 0,13;26,-3 30.5,-3 30.5,-2 26,-2" pelo="0,3.5,20,upstream;26,30.5,3,downstream" etichette="14,19.6,segment 1: L = 800 m - D = 300 mm;14,18.2,segment 2: L = 600 m - D = 250 mm;15.95,12.95,N;28.95,-0.95,V" quote="R,P,18.00 m,1.2" titolo="Two pipes in series between two reservoirs"}

$$
r_i = \frac{8\,\lambda_i\,L_i}{g\,\pi^2 D_i^5} ,
\qquad
\begin{cases}
Q_1 = Q_2 = Q \\[4pt]
\Delta z = r_1 Q^2 + r_2 Q^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = \frac{140.8}{0.2353}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 598.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.024 \cdot 600}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = \frac{115.2}{0.09455}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 1218\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 598.4 + 1218 = 1817\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{\Delta z}{r_{\mathrm{eq}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{18.00}{1817}}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.0995\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 99.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H_1 = 598.4 \cdot 0.0995^2 = 5.93\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_2 = 1218 \cdot 0.0995^2 = 12.07\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check each section using the Darcy–Weisbach equation:
$$
v_1 = \frac{4\,Q}{\pi D_1^2} = \frac{4 \cdot 0.0995}{\pi \cdot 0.300^2} = 1.41\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{4 \cdot 0.0995}{\pi \cdot 0.250^2} = 2.03\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H_1 = 0.022 \cdot \frac{800}{0.300}\cdot\frac{1.41^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 5.94\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_2 = 0.024 \cdot \frac{600}{0.250}\cdot\frac{2.03^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 12.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **long-pipeline model** neglects velocity heads and minor losses. The difference between reservoir surface elevations therefore equals the sum of the friction losses. The fluid is incompressible, flow is steady and the Darcy friction factors are given, so each pipe section is represented by its hydraulic resistance $r_i$.

There is no withdrawal at junction $N$. Continuity therefore gives the same discharge in the two pipes. The pipes are **in series**, so their resistances add.

The relation $\Delta z = r_{\mathrm{eq}} Q^2$ gives $Q = 99.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The velocities are $1.41\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $2.03\,\mathrm{m/s}$, within the usual operating range. The Reynolds numbers are $4.2 \cdot 10^5$ and $5.1 \cdot 10^5$. The flow is turbulent, so taking $\lambda$ as constant is reasonable.

Although section $2$ is shorter than section $1$, its head loss is more than twice as large. Resistance varies inversely with the fifth power of diameter, so the reduction from $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ outweighs the difference in length.

For an independent check, use the Darcy–Weisbach equation and the velocities to calculate the losses directly. The resulting $5.94\,\mathrm{m}$ and $12.10\,\mathrm{m}$ agree with the resistance calculation to within the error from rounding velocities to two decimal places.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Replace the system in the preceding exercise with the single pipe shown, of length $L_e = 1400\,\mathrm{m}$ and Darcy friction factor $\lambda_e = 0.023$. It must carry the same discharge under the same head difference. Find the equivalent diameter and the discharge that would result if the standard diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ were selected.

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,17;V,28,0,-;P,30.5,20,-;R,30.5,3,-" condotte="A-V,0.65," acqua="0,13 3.5,13 3.5,20 0,20;26,-2 30.5,-2 30.5,3 26,3" solidi="0,12 3.5,12 3.5,13 0,13;26,-3 30.5,-3 30.5,-2 26,-2" pelo="0,3.5,20,upstream;26,30.5,3,downstream" etichette="14,19.6,equivalent pipe;14,18.2,L = 1400 m - D = ?;28.95,-0.95,V" quote="R,P,18.00 m,1.2" titolo="The equivalent pipe must carry the same discharge under the same head difference"}

$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{8\,\lambda_e L_e}{g\,\pi^2 D_e^5}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
D_e = \left(\frac{8\,\lambda_e L_e}{g\,\pi^2\,r_{\mathrm{eq}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}
$$
$$
D_e = \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 1400}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 1817}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}\,\mathrm{m} = \left(1.464 \cdot 10^{-3}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.271\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
D_e = 271\,\mathrm{mm}
$$
$$
r' = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 1400}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = \frac{257.6}{0.2353}\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} = 1095\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
Q' = \sqrt{\frac{18.00}{1095}}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 0.128\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 128\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the equivalent pipe by calculating its head loss directly:
$$
v_e = \frac{4 \cdot 0.0995}{\pi \cdot 0.271^2} = 1.73\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
\Delta H = 0.023 \cdot \frac{1400}{0.271}\cdot\frac{1.73^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 18.1\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By definition an **equivalent pipe** gives the same head loss at a given discharge as the system it replaces. Its resistance must therefore equal $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 1817\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. With $L_e$ and $\lambda_e$ fixed, the only unknown in the resistance formula is $D_e$, found by taking the fifth root.

The required diameter, $271\,\mathrm{mm}$, is not a standard size. With the next larger standard diameter, $300\,\mathrm{mm}$, the resistance drops to $1095\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$, or $60\%$ of the design value. Under the same head difference the discharge therefore rises to $128\,\mathrm{L/s}$, an increase of $29\%$ over the original $99.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

As a check, use the Darcy–Weisbach equation with the velocity corresponding to $99.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in the equivalent pipe. It gives $18.1\,\mathrm{m}$ instead of $18.0\,\mathrm{m}$ because $D_e$ was rounded to the nearest millimeter.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pipeline connects two reservoirs through the three sections in series shown and carries $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The Darcy friction factors are $\lambda_1 = 0.021$ in section $A\!-\!B$, $\lambda_2 = 0.022$ in section $B\!-\!C$ and $\lambda_3 = 0.024$ in section $C\!-\!V$. The upstream reservoir surface is at $120.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The pipe centerline is at $112.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at node $B$ and at $108.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at node $C$. Find the downstream reservoir surface elevation and the pressure at each junction.

::schema{nodi="A,4,19;B,15,15,-;C,26,11,-;V,37,1,-" condotte="A-B,0.6,;B-C,0.5,;C-V,0.42," acqua="0,14 4,14 4,21 0,21;33,-1 38,-1 38,4 33,4" solidi="0,13 4,13 4,14 0,14;33,-2 38,-2 38,-1 33,-1" pelo="0,4,21,120.00 m;33,38,4,z_V = ?" etichette="24,24,segment A-B: L = 600 m - D = 300 mm;24,22.4,segment B-C: L = 500 m - D = 250 mm;24,20.8,segment C-V: L = 300 m - D = 200 mm;13,12.6,z_B = 112.00 m;21,8.0,z_C = 108.00 m;15.0,16.65,B;27.17,12.17,C;38.17,-0.17,V" titolo="Three pipe sections in series carrying $Q = 80\,\mathrm{L/s}$"}

$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 600}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 428.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 500}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 930.7\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_3 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.024 \cdot 300}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^5} = 1859\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\Delta H_1 = 428.4 \cdot 0.080^2 = 2.74\,\mathrm{m} ,
\quad
\Delta H_2 = 930.7 \cdot 0.080^2 = 5.96\,\mathrm{m} ,
\quad
\Delta H_3 = 1859 \cdot 0.080^2 = 11.90\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta H = 2.74 + 5.96 + 11.90 = 20.60\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
z_V = 120.00 - 20.60 = 99.40\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_B = 120.00 - 2.74 = 117.26\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_B}{\gamma} = 117.26 - 112.00 = 5.26\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_C = 120.00 - 2.74 - 5.96 = 111.30\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_C}{\gamma} = 111.30 - 108.00 = 3.30\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_B = 9.81 \cdot 5.26 = 51.6\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
\qquad
p_C = 9.81 \cdot 3.30 = 32.4\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check the final pipe section by working back from the downstream end:
$$
h_C - z_V = 111.30 - 99.40 = 11.90\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta H_3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three pipes are in series, so they carry the same discharge and their losses add. Calculate the resistance of each pipe from its own data. The total loss of $20.60\,\mathrm{m}$ is the head difference required, placing the downstream reservoir surface at $99.40\,\mathrm{m}$.

The long-pipeline model equates total head with piezometric head. To obtain the piezometric head at a node, subtract all preceding section losses from the upstream head. At $B$ only $\Delta H_1$ has been dissipated. At $C$ the cumulative loss is $\Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2$.

The pressure head at a node is the piezometric head minus the pipe centerline elevation. At $B$ it is $5.26\,\mathrm{m}$ of water and at $C$ it is $3.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Both values are **positive**, so the hydraulic grade line lies above the pipe throughout the system shown. The gauge pressure is positive everywhere.

The velocities are $1.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $1.63\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $2.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The final pipe has the smallest diameter and the greatest energy gradient, giving the steepest segment of the hydraulic grade line.

Check the final loss by working from the opposite end. Subtract the downstream free-surface elevation from the piezometric head at $C$. The result agrees with $\Delta H_3$ calculated from the pipe resistance.

</div>
