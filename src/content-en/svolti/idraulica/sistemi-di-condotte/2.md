## Exercise 1

Nodes $M$ and $V$ in the diagram are linked by two parallel pipes. Branch $1$ has length $900\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and Darcy friction factor $\lambda_1 = 0.021$. Branch $2$ has length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.022$. The inflow at node $M$ is $Q = 150\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Find the head loss between the nodes and the discharge in each branch.

::schema{nodi="M,4,8;V,24,8,-;B,14,12,-;C,14,4,-" condotte="M-B-V,0.5,;M-C-V,0.42," frecce="1,8,3.2,8,;24.8,8,27,8," etichette="1.8,9.2,Q;26,9.2,Q;14,16,branch 1: L = 900 m - D = 300 mm;14,0.4,branch 2: L = 1200 m - D = 250 mm;25.0,8.0,V" titolo="Two parallel branches between nodes $M$ and $V$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
\Delta H = r_1 Q_1^2 \\[4pt]
\Delta H = r_2 Q_2^2 \\[4pt]
Q_1 + Q_2 = Q
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_1}} + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r_2}}\right)^{2}}
$$
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 900}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 642.7\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1200}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 2234\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_1}} = \frac{1}{25.35} = 0.03945 ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_2}} = \frac{1}{47.26} = 0.02116
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(0.03945 + 0.02116\right)^2} = \frac{1}{0.06061^2} = 272.3\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\Delta H = r_{\mathrm{eq}}\,Q^2 = 272.3 \cdot 0.150^2 = 6.13\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \sqrt{\frac{6.13}{642.7}} = 0.0976\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 97.6\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_2 = \sqrt{\frac{6.13}{2234}} = 0.0524\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 52.4\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check continuity at junction $V$ and the ratio of branch discharges.
$$
Q_1 + Q_2 = 97.6 + 52.4 = 150.0\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q ,
\qquad
\frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \frac{97.6}{52.4} = 1.86 = \sqrt{\frac{2234}{642.7}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The branches connect the same two junctions and therefore have the same head at each end. Since the piezometric head at a junction has a single value, the head loss is the same in both branches even though their discharges differ. In the initial system of three equations, the first two express energy balance in each branch and the third expresses continuity at junction $M$.

Solving the first two equations for $Q_i = \sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta H}{r_i}}$ and substituting into the third gives the equivalent resistance, $272.3\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. This is less than half the resistance of the less resistive branch. Adding a branch in parallel always lowers the equivalent resistance.

The head loss between $M$ and $V$ is $6.13\,\mathrm{m}$. Applying it to each branch gives the two discharges. Branch $1$ is shorter and has the larger diameter. It carries **$97.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$**, nearly two thirds of the total.

The velocities are $1.38\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $1.07\,\mathrm{m/s}$, both within the usual operating range. The Reynolds numbers are of order $10^5$, so the flow is turbulent.

The check verifies continuity at the downstream junction and compares the discharge ratio with the theoretical flow-split ratio $\sqrt{\dfrac{r_2}{r_1}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Add the third parallel branch shown to the system from the previous exercise. It has length $800\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_3 = 0.023$. The total discharge remains $150\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Find the new head loss from $M$ to $V$ and the three branch discharges.

::schema{nodi="M,4,9;V,26,9,-;B,15,14,-;C,15,9.001,-;E,15,4,-" condotte="M-B-V,0.5,;M-C-V,0.42,;M-E-V,0.34," frecce="1,9,3.2,9,;26.8,9,29,9," etichette="1.8,10.2,Q;28,10.2,Q;15,18,branch 1: L = 900 m - D = 300 mm;15,16.6,branch 2: L = 1200 m - D = 250 mm;15,15.2,branch 3: L = 800 m - D = 200 mm;27.1,9.0,V" titolo="Three parallel branches between nodes $M$ and $V$"}

$$
r_3 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^5} = 4751\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{\sqrt{r_3}} = \frac{1}{68.93} = 0.01451
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}}' = \frac{1}{\left(0.03945 + 0.02116 + 0.01451\right)^2} = \frac{1}{0.07512^2} = 177.2\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\Delta H' = 177.2 \cdot 0.150^2 = 3.99\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \sqrt{\frac{3.99}{642.7}} = 78.8\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\quad
Q_2 = \sqrt{\frac{3.99}{2234}} = 42.3\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\quad
Q_3 = \sqrt{\frac{3.99}{4751}} = 29.0\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check continuity at junction $V$.
$$
78.8 + 42.3 + 29.0 = 150.1\,\mathrm{L/s} \simeq Q
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The third branch adds another term to $\sum r_i^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, so the equivalent resistance falls from $272.3$ to $177.2\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. The head loss between the junctions drops from $6.13\,\mathrm{m}$ to $3.99\,\mathrm{m}$, a reduction of $35\%$.

With total discharge fixed, the first two branches now carry less flow because their common head loss is smaller. The discharge in branch $1$ falls from $97.6$ to $78.8\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and that in branch $2$ falls from $52.4$ to $42.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Their discharge ratio stays at $1.86$ because it depends only on their resistances.

Branch $3$, the smallest of the three, carries $29.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$, less than one fifth of the total. Resistance varies inversely with the fifth power of diameter, so a $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe contributes relatively little alongside a $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe.

The $0.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$ discrepancy in the balance comes from rounding $\Delta H'$ and the resistances to three significant figures.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two identical pipes of length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$ connect in parallel two reservoirs with a head difference of $\Delta z = 12.00\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the diameter of a single equivalent pipe of the same length and friction factor. Find the total discharge and compare it with the discharge through a single one of the pipes.

::schema{nodi="A,4,15;M,11,13;V,30,2,-;B,21,11,-;C,21,5,-" condotte="A-M,0.42,;M-B-V,0.42,;M-C-V,0.42," acqua="0,11 4,11 4,17 0,17;27,0 31,0 31,4 27,4" solidi="0,10 4,10 4,11 0,11;27,-1 31,-1 31,0 27,0" pelo="0,4,17,upstream;27,31,4,downstream" etichette="17,17.5,two identical pipes;17,16.1,L = 1000 m - D = 250 mm;30.92,1.08,V" titolo="Two identical pipes in parallel between two reservoirs"}

$$
r = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1861\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{r}}\right)^2} = \frac{r}{4} = 465.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\frac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D_e^5} = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{8\,\lambda\,L}{g\,\pi^2 D^5}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
D_e^5 = 4\,D^5
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
D_e = 4^{\frac{1}{5}}\,D
$$
$$
D_e = 1.320 \cdot 250 = 330\,\mathrm{mm}
$$
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{12.00}{465.4}} = 0.1606\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 161\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_{\text{single}} = \sqrt{\frac{12.00}{1861}} = 80.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the discharge and head loss in one branch.
$$
\frac{Q}{2} = 80.3\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
\Delta H = 1861 \cdot 0.0803^2 = 12.00\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta z
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two pipes have the same resistance and carry equal discharges. The sum $\sum r_i^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ is therefore $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{r}}$, and the equivalent resistance is one quarter of the resistance of a single pipe.

To find the equivalent diameter, equate the two expressions for resistance. Because the length and friction factor are the same, this leaves $D_e^5 = 4 D^5$ and hence $D_e = 4^{\frac{1}{5}} D = 1.320\,D$. Thus **adding an identical pipe in parallel** is equivalent to increasing the diameter by about one third.

Under the available head of $12.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the two pipes deliver $161\,\mathrm{L/s}$, twice the single-pipe discharge of $80.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Since $\Delta H = r_{\mathrm{eq}} Q^2$, reducing the resistance to one quarter doubles the discharge. The velocity in each branch remains $1.64\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the same as in the original single pipe, because the available head and hence the hydraulic gradient are unchanged.

As a check, each branch carries half the total discharge. The head loss calculated from that discharge reproduces the specified head difference.

</div>
