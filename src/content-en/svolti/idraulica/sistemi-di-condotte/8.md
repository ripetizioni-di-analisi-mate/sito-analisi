## Exercise 1

The system shown pumps $Q = 100\,\mathrm{L/s}$ from a reservoir with free surface at $10.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to one with free surface at $50.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The pump is in common section $A\!-\!M$ of length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$. Two parallel branches lead from node $M$ to the upper reservoir. The first has length $800\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_1 = 0.022$. The second has length $600\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.023$. Find the pump head, the input power at efficiency $\eta = 0.72$, and the two branch discharges.

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,3;M,15,9;V,30,17;B,22,16,-;C,22,9,-" condotte="A-M,0.55,;M-B-V,0.42,;M-C-V,0.36," pompa="8,5.35,P" acqua="0,0 3.5,0 3.5,5 0,5;30,14 34,14 34,19 30,19" solidi="0,-1 3.5,-1 3.5,0 0,0;30,13 34,13 34,14 30,14" pelo="0,3.5,5,10.00 m;30,34,19,50.00 m" etichette="12,22,A-M: 1000 m - 300 mm;12,20.6,branch 1: 800 m - 250 mm;12,19.2,branch 2: 600 m - 200 mm" titolo="Pump in the common section of a series–parallel system"}

$$
r_0 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 1000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 714.1\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1489\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 600}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^5} = 3563\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_p = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{38.59} + \dfrac{1}{59.69}\right)^2} = \frac{1}{0.04267^2} = 549.3\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
H_p = \Delta z + r_0 Q^2 + r_p Q^2 = 40.00 + 714.1 \cdot 0.100^2 + 549.3 \cdot 0.100^2
$$
$$
H_p = 40.00 + 7.14 + 5.49 = 52.63\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
P = \frac{\gamma\,Q\,H_p}{\eta} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 52.63}{0.72}\,\mathrm{kW} = 71.7\,\mathrm{kW}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \sqrt{\frac{5.49}{1489}} = 60.7\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_2 = \sqrt{\frac{5.49}{3563}} = 39.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check continuity at junction $M$ and the energy balance in branch $2$.
$$
Q_1 + Q_2 = 60.7 + 39.3 = 100.0\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q ,
\qquad
3563 \cdot 0.0393^2 = 5.50\,\mathrm{m} \simeq r_p Q^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pump raises the total head by $H_p$ from its inlet to its outlet. In the energy balance between the reservoir surfaces, the pump head on the left therefore equals the static lift plus the head losses on the right.

The losses occur in the common pipe, which carries the total discharge, and in the parallel branches, represented by equivalent resistance $r_p = 549.3\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. The common pipe is the longest and accounts for $7.14\,\mathrm{m}$ of head loss. The parallel branches account for $5.49\,\mathrm{m}$.

The required pump head is **$52.63\,\mathrm{m}$**, of which $40.00\,\mathrm{m}$ overcomes the static lift and $12.63\,\mathrm{m}$ the head losses. The input power is $71.7\,\mathrm{kW}$. The efficiency $0.72$ accounts for losses in both pump and motor.

Both branches have the same head loss $5.49\,\mathrm{m}$. Applying it to the individual branches gives discharges of $60.7\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in branch $1$ and $39.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in branch $2$. The velocities are $1.41\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the common pipe, $1.24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $1$, and $1.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $2$.

The check verifies continuity at junction $M$ and recalculates the head loss across the parallel branches using the discharge and resistance of branch $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the system shown, reservoir $A$ with free surface at $50.00\,\mathrm{m}$ feeds node $N$ through a pipe of length $900\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$. Branch $N\!-\!B$ leads to reservoir $B$ with free surface at $20.00\,\mathrm{m}$. It has length $700\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$. Branch $N\!-\!C$ leads to reservoir $C$ with free surface at $70.00\,\mathrm{m}$. It has length $1100\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$ and contains a pump supplying constant head $H_p = 45.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the nodal head, the three discharges and the pump input power at efficiency $\eta = 0.75$.

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,13;N,16,7,-;B,28,2,-;C,28,19" condotte="A-N,0.5,;N-B,0.42,;N-C,0.42," pompa="21,12,P" acqua="0,10 3.5,10 3.5,16 0,16;28,0 32,0 32,4 28,4;28,17 32,17 32,21 28,21" solidi="0,9 3.5,9 3.5,10 0,10;28,-1 32,-1 32,0 28,0;28,16 32,16 32,17 28,17" pelo="0,3.5,16,50.00 m;28,32,4,20.00 m;28,32,21,70.00 m" etichette="11,24,A-N: 900 m - 300 mm;11,22.6,N-B: 700 m - 250 mm;11,21.2,N-C: 1100 m - 250 mm;11,19.8,pump: H_p = 45.00 m;15.13,6.13,N;28.87,1.13,B" titolo="Pump in the branch supplying the highest reservoir"}

$$
r_A = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 900}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 642.7\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_B = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 700}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1303\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_C = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1100}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 2048\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
Q_A = \sqrt{\dfrac{50.00 - h_N}{642.7}} \\[10pt]
Q_B = \sqrt{\dfrac{h_N - 20.00}{1303}} \\[10pt]
Q_C = \sqrt{\dfrac{h_N + 45.00 - 70.00}{2048}} \\[10pt]
Q_A = Q_B + Q_C
\end{cases}
$$

Trial values of the flow imbalance $\Phi = Q_A - Q_B - Q_C$, with stopping criterion $|\Phi| < 1\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

| $h_N$ (m) | $Q_A$ (L/s) | $Q_B$ (L/s) | $Q_C$ (L/s) | $\Phi$ (L/s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $30.00$ | $176.4$ | $87.6$ | $49.4$ | $+39.4$ |
| $40.00$ | $124.7$ | $123.9$ | $85.6$ | $-84.8$ |
| $35.00$ | $152.8$ | $107.3$ | $69.9$ | $-24.4$ |
| $33.00$ | $162.6$ | $99.9$ | $62.5$ | $+0.3$ |

$$
h_N = 33.02\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Q_A = 162.5\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_B = 100.0\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_C = 62.6\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
P = \frac{\gamma\,Q_C\,H_p}{\eta} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.0626 \cdot 45.00}{0.75}\,\mathrm{kW} = 36.8\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

Check the energy balance in the pump branch and continuity at the junction.
$$
h_N + H_p - r_C Q_C^2 = 33.02 + 45.00 - 2048 \cdot 0.0626^2 = 78.02 - 8.02 = 70.00\,\mathrm{m} = z_C
$$
$$
Q_B + Q_C = 100.0 + 62.6 = 162.6\,\mathrm{L/s} \simeq Q_A
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Reservoir $C$ is higher than the supply reservoir, so the junction could not supply it without a pump. The pump adds $45.00\,\mathrm{m}$ to the head in branch $N\!-\!C$. The head available to overcome loss in that branch is therefore $h_N + H_p - z_C$, or $h_N - 25.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Water flows toward $C$ as long as the junction head exceeds $25.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

The system has the same structure as the three-reservoir problem, with $h_N$ as the sole unknown. The flow direction in branch $N\!-\!B$ is known because reservoir $B$ has the lowest elevation and the pump drives flow toward $C$. The junction supplies both $B$ and $C$.

Use the monotonic decrease of the flow imbalance to choose trial heads. At $40.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the imbalance is strongly negative, so reduce the head. At $33.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the imbalance is $0.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$, meeting the stopping criterion. The result is $h_N = 33.02\,\mathrm{m}$.

The pump carries only the discharge in its own branch, $62.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Use this branch discharge in the power calculation. The velocities are $2.30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $2.04\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

Checking the pump-branch energy balance reproduces the elevation of reservoir $C$. The final discharges also satisfy continuity at the junction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two reservoirs with a head difference of $\Delta z = 40.00\,\mathrm{m}$ are linked by the system shown. It consists of upstream section $A\!-\!M$ ($500\,\mathrm{m}$, $350\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.020$), two parallel branches between nodes $M$ and $V$, the first of length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.023$, the second of length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.023$, and downstream section $V\!-\!S$ ($400\,\mathrm{m}$, $350\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.020$). The second branch contains a booster pump supplying constant head $H_p = 12.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at overall efficiency $\eta = 0.70$. Find the total discharge, branch discharges and pump input power. Compare these discharges with those obtained without the pump.

::schema{nodi="A,3.5,17;M,12,13,-;V,26,4,-;S,33,1,-;B,19,12,-;C,19,4,-" condotte="A-M,0.6,;M-B-V,0.42,;M-C-V,0.36,;V-S,0.6," pompa="15.5,8.5,P" acqua="0,13 3.5,13 3.5,20 0,20;33,-2 37,-2 37,3 33,3" solidi="0,12 3.5,12 3.5,13 0,13;33,-3 37,-3 37,-2 33,-2" pelo="0,3.5,20,upstream;33,37,3,downstream" etichette="13,24,A-M: 500 m - 350 mm;13,22.6,branch 1: 1200 m - 250 mm;13,21.2,branch 2: 1200 m - 200 mm;13,19.8,V-S: 400 m - 350 mm;9.5,6.5,H_p = 12.00 m;10.96,11.96,M;27.04,5.04,V;34.04,-0.04,S" titolo="Booster pump in one of two parallel branches"}

$$
r_0 = 157.3\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\quad
r_1 = 2335\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\quad
r_2 = 7127\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\quad
r_f = 125.9\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
X = r_1 Q_1^2 \\[4pt]
X = r_2 Q_2^2 - H_p \\[4pt]
Q = Q_1 + Q_2 \\[4pt]
\Delta z = \left(r_0 + r_f\right) Q^2 + X
\end{cases}
$$

Trial values for the head loss $X = h_M - h_V$, using residual $\varepsilon = \Delta z - X - \left(r_0 + r_f\right) Q^2$ and stopping criterion $|\varepsilon| < 0.05\,\mathrm{m}$.

| $X$ (m) | $Q_1$ (L/s) | $Q_2$ (L/s) | $Q$ (L/s) | $\varepsilon$ (m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $5.00$ | $46.3$ | $48.8$ | $95.1$ | $+32.44$ |
| $20.00$ | $92.5$ | $67.0$ | $159.6$ | $+12.79$ |
| $30.00$ | $113.3$ | $76.8$ | $190.1$ | $-0.23$ |
| $29.80$ | $113.0$ | $76.6$ | $189.6$ | $+0.03$ |

$$
X = 29.82\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Q = 189.6\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_1 = 113.0\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_2 = 76.6\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
P = \frac{\gamma\,Q_2\,H_p}{\eta} = \frac{9.81 \cdot 0.0766 \cdot 12.00}{0.70}\,\mathrm{kW} = 12.9\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

With the pump removed,
$$
r_p = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{48.32} + \dfrac{1}{84.42}\right)^2} = 944.5\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
Q' = \sqrt{\frac{40.00}{157.3 + 944.5 + 125.9}} = 180.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
Q_1' = \sqrt{\frac{30.77}{2335}} = 114.8\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_2' = \sqrt{\frac{30.77}{7127}} = 65.7\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the energy balance between the reservoirs.
$$
\left(157.3 + 125.9\right) \cdot 0.1896^2 + 29.82 = 10.18 + 29.82 = 40.00\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta z
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Branch $2$ contains the pump, so its energy balance between junctions $M$ and $V$ is $h_M + H_p = h_V + r_2 Q_2^2$, or $X = r_2 Q_2^2 - H_p$. For the same head difference between the junctions, this branch carries more flow with the pump than it would without it.

A convenient unknown is the head loss $X$ between $M$ and $V$. For a trial $X$, the branch equations give $Q_1$ and $Q_2$. Their sum gives $Q$, and the fourth equation gives the residual. The residual decreases as $X$ increases because a larger $X$ produces larger discharges and hence greater losses in the upstream and downstream pipes.

The trial values converge to $X = 29.82\,\mathrm{m}$ with total discharge **$189.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$**. The velocities are $1.97\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the upstream and downstream pipes, $2.30\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $1$, and $2.44\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $2$.

Comparing the two cases shows the effect of the booster pump. The discharge in branch $2$ rises from $65.7$ to $76.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$, an increase of $17\%$, while total discharge rises only from $180.5$ to $189.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$, or $5\%$. The pump draws a larger share of the flow into its own branch. The discharge in branch $1$ consequently falls from $114.8$ to $113.0\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

The check adds the losses in the upstream and downstream pipes, both calculated at the total discharge, to the head loss between the junctions. Their sum reproduces the specified head difference.

</div>
