## Exercise 1

The three reservoirs shown have water-surface elevations $z_A = 100.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $z_B = 85.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $z_C = 60.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and connect to node $N$ via three pipes. Pipe $A\!-\!N$ has length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$. Pipe $B\!-\!N$ has length $800\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.023$. Pipe $N\!-\!C$ has length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $350\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$. Determine the flow direction in branch $B$ and calculate the nodal head and three discharges.

::schema{nodi="N,18,6,-;A,3.5,16;B,13,14;C,26,2,-" condotte="A-N,0.55,;B-N,0.45,;N-C,0.65," acqua="0,13 3.5,13 3.5,20 0,20;10,14 13,14 13,17 10,17;26,-1 30,-1 30,5 26,5" solidi="0,12 3.5,12 3.5,13 0,13;10,13 13,13 13,14 10,14;26,-2 30,-2 30,-1 26,-1" pelo="0,3.5,20,100.00 m;10,13,17,85.00 m;26,30,5,60.00 m" etichette="26,21.5,A-N: 1000 m - 300 mm;26,20.1,B-N: 800 m - 250 mm;26,18.7,N-C: 1200 m - 350 mm;17.2,5.2,N;26.8,1.2,C" titolo="Three reservoirs connected to junction $N$"}

$$
r_A = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 748.1\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_B = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1557\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_C = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 1200}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.350^5} = 396.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$

First test $h_N = z_B = 85.00\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
Q_A = \sqrt{\frac{100.00 - 85.00}{748.1}} = 0.1416\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
\qquad
Q_C = \sqrt{\frac{85.00 - 60.00}{396.4}} = 0.2511\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_A < Q_C
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
h_N < z_B ,
\qquad
\begin{cases}
Q_A = \sqrt{\dfrac{100.00 - h_N}{748.1}} \\[10pt]
Q_B = \sqrt{\dfrac{85.00 - h_N}{1557}} \\[10pt]
Q_C = \sqrt{\dfrac{h_N - 60.00}{396.4}} \\[10pt]
Q_A + Q_B = Q_C
\end{cases}
$$

Trial values of the flow imbalance $\Phi = Q_A + Q_B - Q_C$, with stopping criterion $|\Phi| < 1\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

| $h_N$ (m) | $Q_A$ (L/s) | $Q_B$ (L/s) | $Q_C$ (L/s) | $\Phi$ (L/s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $85.00$ | $141.6$ | $0.0$ | $251.1$ | $-109.5$ |
| $78.00$ | $171.5$ | $67.1$ | $213.1$ | $+25.5$ |
| $80.00$ | $163.5$ | $56.7$ | $224.6$ | $-4.4$ |
| $79.70$ | $164.7$ | $58.3$ | $222.9$ | $+0.2$ |

$$
h_N = 79.71\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Q_A = 164.7\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_B = 58.3\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_C = 223.0\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the energy balance in branch $C$ and continuity at the junction.
$$
60.00 + 396.4 \cdot 0.2230^2 = 60.00 + 19.71 = 79.71\,\mathrm{m} = h_N ,
\qquad
164.7 + 58.3 = 223.0\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q_C
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Junction $N$ has one piezometric head, $h_N$. Each branch can be treated as a long pipe between a known reservoir level and the junction, with head loss $|z_i - h_N|$. The sole unknown is $h_N$.

Test $h_N = z_B$ before solving to determine the flow direction in the intermediate branch. At this head, branch $B$ carries no flow and continuity would require $Q_A = Q_C$. In fact $Q_A = 141.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and $Q_C = 251.1\,\mathrm{L/s}$, so branch $C$ would carry away more water than arrives from $A$. The actual junction head must therefore be **lower** than $z_B$, and reservoir $B$ supplies the junction.

For these directions continuity gives $Q_A + Q_B = Q_C$, leaving one unknown. The function $\Phi$ is strictly decreasing, so raise the trial head if the imbalance is positive and lower it if the imbalance is negative. Starting at $78.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with imbalance $\Phi = +25.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$, the next trial is $80.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with imbalance $\Phi = -4.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. At $79.70\,\mathrm{m}$ the imbalance is $0.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$, within the stopping tolerance.

The velocities are $2.33\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $1.19\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $2.32\,\mathrm{m/s}$, all within the usual operating range.

As a check, recover $h_N$ from branch $C$ by working back from the downstream reservoir, and verify junction continuity using the final discharges.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The three reservoirs shown have surface elevations $z_A = 75.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $z_B = 50.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $z_C = 40.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and connect to node $N$ through pipes $A\!-\!N$ ($800\,\mathrm{m}$, $350\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.021$), $B\!-\!N$ ($1000\,\mathrm{m}$, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.022$) and $C\!-\!N$ ($1500\,\mathrm{m}$, $300\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.022$). Determine the flow direction in branch $B$ and find the three discharges.

::schema{nodi="N,18,6,-;A,3.5,16;B,13,14;C,26,2,-" condotte="A-N,0.65,;B-N,0.45,;N-C,0.55," acqua="0,13 3.5,13 3.5,20 0,20;10,14 13,14 13,17 10,17;26,-1 30,-1 30,5 26,5" solidi="0,12 3.5,12 3.5,13 0,13;10,13 13,13 13,14 10,14;26,-2 30,-2 30,-1 26,-1" pelo="0,3.5,20,75.00 m;10,13,17,50.00 m;26,30,5,40.00 m" etichette="26,21.5,A-N: 800 m - 350 mm;26,20.1,B-N: 1000 m - 250 mm;26,18.7,C-N: 1500 m - 300 mm;17.2,5.2,N;26.8,1.2,C" titolo="Three-reservoir system with a large-diameter upstream branch"}

$$
r_A = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.350^5} = 264.3\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_B = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1861\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_C = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1500}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 1122\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$

First test $h_N = z_B = 50.00\,\mathrm{m}$.
$$
Q_A = \sqrt{\frac{25.00}{264.3}} = 0.3076\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
\qquad
Q_C = \sqrt{\frac{10.00}{1122}} = 0.0944\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$
$$
Q_A > Q_C
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
h_N > z_B ,
\qquad
Q_A = Q_B + Q_C
$$

Trial values of the flow imbalance $\Phi = Q_A - Q_B - Q_C$, with stopping criterion $|\Phi| < 1\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

| $h_N$ (m) | $Q_A$ (L/s) | $Q_B$ (L/s) | $Q_C$ (L/s) | $\Phi$ (L/s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $50.00$ | $307.6$ | $0.0$ | $94.4$ | $+213.2$ |
| $65.00$ | $194.5$ | $89.8$ | $149.3$ | $-44.5$ |
| $62.00$ | $221.8$ | $80.3$ | $140.0$ | $+1.5$ |
| $62.10$ | $220.9$ | $80.6$ | $140.3$ | $0.0$ |

$$
h_N = 62.10\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Q_A = 220.9\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_B = 80.6\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_C = 140.3\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check the energy balance in branch $A$ and continuity at the junction.
$$
75.00 - 264.3 \cdot 0.2209^2 = 75.00 - 12.90 = 62.10\,\mathrm{m} = h_N ,
\qquad
80.6 + 140.3 = 220.9\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q_A
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Branch $A$ is short and has a large diameter, so its resistance is small. At $h_N = z_B$ it would supply $307.6\,\mathrm{L/s}$, while branch $C$ could carry away only $94.4\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Since $Q_A > Q_C$, the actual junction head is **higher** than $z_B$ and the junction also supplies reservoir $B$.

Flow in the intermediate branch therefore reverses relative to the preceding exercise, and continuity gives $Q_A = Q_B + Q_C$. In the discharge equation for branch $B$, the head difference is $h_N - z_B$ rather than $z_B - h_N$.

Start from the trial value and use the same rule as before. At $65.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the imbalance is negative, so reduce the head. At $62.00\,\mathrm{m}$ the imbalance is $1.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and at $62.10\,\mathrm{m}$ it vanishes.

The velocities are $2.30\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $1.64\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $1.99\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Branch $A$ accounts for only $12.90\,\mathrm{m}$ of the total head difference of $35.00\,\mathrm{m}$ toward $C$ because it has the lowest resistance of the three.

As a check, use branch $A$ to recover $h_N$ and verify continuity at the junction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the system shown, reservoir $A$ at an unknown elevation feeds, through node $N$, reservoir $B$ with free surface at $70.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and reservoir $C$ with free surface at $40.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Pipe $A\!-\!N$ has length $900\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$. Pipe $N\!-\!C$ has length $1400\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$. Find the elevation $z_A$ for zero flow in branch $B$ and explain what happens if $z_A$ rises above this value.

::schema{nodi="N,18,6,-;A,3.5,16;B,13,14;C,26,2,-" condotte="A-N,0.55,;B-N,0.45,;N-C,0.55," acqua="0,13 3.5,13 3.5,20 0,20;10,14 13,14 13,17 10,17;26,-1 30,-1 30,5 26,5" solidi="0,12 3.5,12 3.5,13 0,13;10,13 13,13 13,14 10,14;26,-2 30,-2 30,-1 26,-1" pelo="0,3.5,20,z_A = ?;10,13,17,70.00 m;26,30,5,40.00 m" etichette="26,21.5,A-N: 900 m - 300 mm;26,20.1,N-C: 1400 m - 300 mm;17.2,5.2,N;26.8,1.2,C" titolo="Elevation of reservoir $A$ for zero flow in branch $B$"}

$$
Q_B = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
h_N = z_B = 70.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
Q_A = Q_C
$$
$$
r_A = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 900}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 673.3\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_C = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 1400}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 999.7\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
Q_C = \sqrt{\frac{70.00 - 40.00}{999.7}} = 0.1732\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 173.2\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
z_A = h_N + r_A\,Q_A^2 = 70.00 + 673.3 \cdot 0.1732^2 = 70.00 + 20.20 = 90.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the energy balance along the full path $A\!-\!N\!-\!C$, which here consists of two pipes in series.
$$
\left(r_A + r_C\right) Q^2 = \left(673.3 + 999.7\right) \cdot 0.1732^2 = 1673 \cdot 0.03000 = 50.19\,\mathrm{m} = 90.20 - 40.00
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Discharge in branch $B$ is zero when its head loss is zero, namely when the junction head equals the water level in reservoir $B$. Then $h_N = 70.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and all the flow arriving from $A$ continues toward $C$.

The heads at both ends of branch $N\!-\!C$ are known. Its discharge is therefore found directly as $173.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$, at velocity $2.45\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The same discharge passes through branch $A\!-\!N$, producing a head loss of $20.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and a required elevation of $z_A = 90.20\,\mathrm{m}$.

When $z_A$ exceeds $90.20\,\mathrm{m}$, the inflow from $A$ increases. The junction head rises above $70.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and the junction **supplies** reservoir $B$. At smaller $z_A$ the junction head falls below $70.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and reservoir $B$ supplies the junction.

The value $z_A = 90.20\,\mathrm{m}$ separates the two flow regimes in the preceding exercises. It is therefore a convenient first trial when the direction of flow in the intermediate branch is unknown.

To check the result, treat path $A\!-\!N\!-\!C$ as two pipes in series, since both carry the same discharge. Multiplying the sum of their resistances by the discharge squared gives the overall head difference of $50.20\,\mathrm{m}$, within one hundredth of a meter after rounding.

</div>
