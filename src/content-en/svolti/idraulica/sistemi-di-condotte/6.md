## Exercise 1

A reservoir with free surface at $100.00\,\mathrm{m}$ supplies the branched network shown. Pipe $R\!-\!A$ has length $800\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$. At node $A$, flow divides into branches $A\!-\!B$ ($600\,\mathrm{m}$, $200\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.023$) and $A\!-\!C$ ($1000\,\mathrm{m}$, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.022$). The withdrawal at node $B$ is $40\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and the withdrawal at node $C$ is $80\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The two node elevations are $70.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $65.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the three pipe discharges and the pressures at $B$ and $C$.

::schema{nodi="R,3,12;A,13,12;B,24,17;C,24,6,-" condotte="R-A,0.55,;A-B,0.38,;A-C,0.45," acqua="0,9 3,9 3,15 0,15" solidi="0,8 3,8 3,9 0,9" pelo="0,3,15,100.00 m" frecce="24.8,17,26.6,17,;24.8,6,26.6,6," etichette="16,23,R-A: 800 m - 300 mm;16,21.6,A-B: 600 m - 200 mm;16,20.2,A-C: 1000 m - 250 mm;28.6,17,40 L/s;28.6,6,80 L/s;22,18.8,z_B = 70.00 m;22,4.2,z_C = 65.00 m;24.74,5.26,C" titolo="Branched network with two terminal nodes"}

$$
Q_0 = Q_B + Q_C = 40 + 80 = 120\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
r_0 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 571.2\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_B = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 600}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^5} = 3563\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_C = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1861\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
h_A = 100.00 - 571.2 \cdot 0.120^2 = 100.00 - 8.23 = 91.77\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_B = 91.77 - 3563 \cdot 0.040^2 = 91.77 - 5.70 = 86.07\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_C = 91.77 - 1861 \cdot 0.080^2 = 91.77 - 11.91 = 79.86\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p_B}{\gamma} = 86.07 - 70.00 = 16.07\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_C}{\gamma} = 79.86 - 65.00 = 14.86\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
p_B = 9.81 \cdot 16.07 = 158\,\mathrm{kPa} ,
\qquad
p_C = 9.81 \cdot 14.86 = 146\,\mathrm{kPa}
$$

Check the head loss in branch $A\!-\!B$ using the Darcy–Weisbach equation.
$$
v_B = \frac{4 \cdot 0.040}{\pi \cdot 0.200^2} = 1.27\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_B = 0.023 \cdot \frac{600}{0.200}\cdot\frac{1.27^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 5.67\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The network has no loops. Each junction has a unique path back to the reservoir, so continuity alone determines the discharges, working upstream from the terminal junctions. Main $R\!-\!A$ carries the sum of both demands, $120\,\mathrm{L/s}$, and each terminal branch carries its own demand.

Calculate piezometric heads by following the flow from the reservoir. At $A$ subtract the head loss in the common main. At each terminal junction also subtract the loss in its own branch. Junction $A$ lies on both paths, so its head enters both terminal-head calculations.

Subtracting junction elevation from piezometric head gives pressure heads of $16.07\,\mathrm{m}$ of water at $B$ and $14.86\,\mathrm{m}$ at $C$, corresponding to $158\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $146\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Both exceed the $10\,\mathrm{m}$ usually required at water-supply connections.

The head loss in branch $A\!-\!C$ is more than twice that in branch $A\!-\!B$ because it is longer and carries twice the discharge. Its larger diameter only partly compensates. The velocities are $1.70\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the common main, $1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $B$, and $1.63\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $C$.

An independent check uses the velocity in branch $A\!-\!B$ to recalculate the loss directly from the Darcy–Weisbach equation. Rounding the velocity accounts for the difference of three hundredths of a meter.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the network shown, a reservoir with free surface at $130.00\,\mathrm{m}$ feeds a pipe of length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $350\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.021$, which carries $150\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to node $A$. From $A$, branch $A\!-\!B$ of length $800\,\mathrm{m}$ with $\lambda = 0.023$ delivers $60\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to node $B$ at elevation $90.00\,\mathrm{m}$, and branch $A\!-\!C$ of length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ with $\lambda = 0.022$ delivers $90\,\mathrm{L/s}$ to node $C$ at elevation $85.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Both terminal nodes require a pressure of at least $25.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. Select standard diameters for the two branches from $200$, $250$ and $300\,\mathrm{mm}$.

::schema{nodi="R,3,12;A,13,12;B,24,17;C,24,6,-" condotte="R-A,0.6,;A-B,0.4,;A-C,0.4," acqua="0,9 3,9 3,15 0,15" solidi="0,8 3,8 3,9 0,9" pelo="0,3,15,130.00 m" frecce="24.8,17,26.6,17,;24.8,6,26.6,6," etichette="16,23,R-A: 1000 m - 350 mm;16,21.6,A-B: 800 m - D = ?;16,20.2,A-C: 1200 m - D = ?;28.6,17,60 L/s;28.6,6,90 L/s;22,18.8,z_B = 90.00 m;22,4.2,z_C = 85.00 m;24.74,5.26,C" titolo="Sizing the two terminal branches"}

$$
r_0 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.021 \cdot 1000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.350^5} = 330.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
h_A = 130.00 - 330.4 \cdot 0.150^2 = 130.00 - 7.43 = 122.57\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta H_{B,\mathrm{avail}} = 122.57 - 90.00 - 25.00 = 7.57\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_{C,\mathrm{avail}} = 122.57 - 85.00 - 25.00 = 12.57\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
D_B \geq \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 800 \cdot 0.060^2}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 7.57}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(7.234 \cdot 10^{-4}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.235\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
D_C \geq \left(\frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1200 \cdot 0.090^2}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 12.57}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}} = \left(1.406 \cdot 10^{-3}\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.269\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
D_B = 250\,\mathrm{mm} ,
\qquad
D_C = 300\,\mathrm{mm}
$$
$$
r_B = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1557\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_B = 1557 \cdot 0.060^2 = 5.60\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
r_C = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1200}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.300^5} = 897.7\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_C = 897.7 \cdot 0.090^2 = 7.27\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p_B}{\gamma} = 122.57 - 5.60 - 90.00 = 26.97\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_C}{\gamma} = 122.57 - 7.27 - 85.00 = 30.30\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the next smaller standard diameter for branch $A\!-\!C$.
$$
r_C' = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1200}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 2234\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_C'}{\gamma} = 122.57 - 2234 \cdot 0.090^2 - 85.00 = 19.48\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The head at junction $A$ depends only on the common main, so calculate it first. Its value is $122.57\,\mathrm{m}$.

For each branch, subtract the terminal junction's elevation and minimum pressure head from the head at $A$. This leaves $7.57\,\mathrm{m}$ available for head loss in branch $B$ and $12.57\,\mathrm{m}$ in branch $C$. Branch $C$ has more head available because its terminal junction is lower.

Setting the loss equal to the head available gives the minimum diameter, whose fifth power appears in the resistance. The results are $235\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $269\,\mathrm{mm}$. Select the next larger standard diameters, **$250\,\mathrm{mm}$** and **$300\,\mathrm{mm}$**.

With the selected diameters the actual head losses are below the available heads, so the terminal pressure heads, $26.97\,\mathrm{m}$ and $30.30\,\mathrm{m}$, exceed the minimum. The velocities of $1.22\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $1.27\,\mathrm{m/s}$ are within the usual operating range.

For branch $C$, the next smaller diameter, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, would reduce the pressure head at $C$ to $19.48\,\mathrm{m}$, below the required minimum. Thus $300\,\mathrm{mm}$ is the smallest acceptable standard diameter.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The network shown is supplied by a reservoir with free surface at $95.00\,\mathrm{m}$. The point withdrawals are $30\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at $A$, $10\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at $B$, $50\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at $C$ and $40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at $D$. The node elevations are $70.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at $A$, $68.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at $B$, $66.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at $C$ and $64.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at $D$. The pipes are $R\!-\!A$ ($600\,\mathrm{m}$, $300\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.021$), $A\!-\!B$ ($500\,\mathrm{m}$, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.022$), $B\!-\!C$ ($400\,\mathrm{m}$, $200\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.023$) and $B\!-\!D$ ($600\,\mathrm{m}$, $200\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda = 0.023$). Check that each nodal pressure is at least $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$ of water. Where this condition fails, propose the next standard diameter.

::schema{nodi="R,3,11;A,11,11;B,19,11;C,28,16;D,28,5,-" condotte="R-A,0.5,;A-B,0.42,;B-C,0.34,;B-D,0.34," acqua="0,8 3,8 3,14 0,14" solidi="0,7 3,7 3,8 0,8" pelo="0,3,14,95.00 m" frecce="11,10.2,11,8.4,;19,10.2,19,8.4,;28.8,16,30.6,16,;28.8,5,30.6,5," etichette="11,22,R-A: 600 m - 300 mm;11,20.6,A-B: 500 m - 250 mm;11,19.2,B-C: 400 m - 200 mm;11,17.8,B-D: 600 m - 200 mm;11,7.6,30 L/s;19,7.6,10 L/s;32.6,16,50 L/s;32.6,5,40 L/s;28.88,4.12,D" titolo="Branched network with withdrawals at intermediate nodes"}

$$
Q_{RA} = 30 + 10 + 50 + 40 = 130\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_{AB} = 100\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_{BC} = 50\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_{BD} = 40\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
r_{RA} = 428.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\quad
r_{AB} = 930.7\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\quad
r_{BC} = 2376\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\quad
r_{BD} = 3563\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
h_A = 95.00 - 428.4 \cdot 0.130^2 = 95.00 - 7.24 = 87.76\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_A}{\gamma} = 17.76\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_B = 87.76 - 930.7 \cdot 0.100^2 = 87.76 - 9.31 = 78.45\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_B}{\gamma} = 10.45\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_C = 78.45 - 2376 \cdot 0.050^2 = 78.45 - 5.94 = 72.51\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_C}{\gamma} = 6.51\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h_D = 78.45 - 3563 \cdot 0.040^2 = 78.45 - 5.70 = 72.75\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\frac{p_D}{\gamma} = 8.75\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p_C}{\gamma} = 6.51\,\mathrm{m} < 8.00\,\mathrm{m}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
D_{BC} = 250\,\mathrm{mm}
$$
$$
r_{BC}' = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 400}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 778.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
h_C' = 78.45 - 778.4 \cdot 0.050^2 = 76.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{p_C'}{\gamma} = 76.50 - 66.00 = 10.50\,\mathrm{m} > 8.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check overall continuity and the head loss in main $A\!-\!B$.
$$
30 + 10 + 50 + 40 = 130\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q_{RA} ,
\qquad
v_{AB} = \frac{4 \cdot 0.100}{\pi \cdot 0.250^2} = 2.04\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
0.022 \cdot \frac{500}{0.250}\cdot\frac{2.04^2}{2 \cdot 9.81} = 9.33\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Work upstream from the terminal junctions to obtain the discharges. Pipe $B\!-\!C$ carries the demand at $C$ and pipe $B\!-\!D$ carries the demand at $D$. Pipe $A\!-\!B$ supplies the combined demands at $B$, $C$, and $D$, and main $R\!-\!A$ carries all four demands.

Calculate the piezometric heads by following each path downstream from the reservoir. Junctions $C$ and $D$ share the same upstream junction, $B$, so their calculations start from the same head $h_B = 78.45\,\mathrm{m}$.

Pressure head decreases downstream and reaches its minimum at $C$. Its value of $6.51\,\mathrm{m}$ is below the required $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$, while the value at $D$ is $8.75\,\mathrm{m}$ and meets the requirement. Junction $C$ is less favorably situated than $D$ even though its branch is shorter. Its branch carries more flow at the same diameter, and the junction lies at a higher elevation.

Changing pipe $B\!-\!C$ to the next larger standard diameter, $250\,\mathrm{mm}$, reduces its head loss from $5.94\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.95\,\mathrm{m}$. The pressure head at $C$ rises to $10.50\,\mathrm{m}$, so the requirement is **satisfied**. The velocity falls from $1.59\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $1.02\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and remains acceptable.

The check verifies that the inlet main carries the sum of all withdrawals and recalculates the head loss in pipe $A\!-\!B$ with the Darcy–Weisbach equation.

</div>
