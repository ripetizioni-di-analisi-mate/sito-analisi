## Exercise 1

Two reservoirs with a head difference of $\Delta z = 20.00\,\mathrm{m}$ are connected by the system shown. Common section $A\!-\!M$ has length $1000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $350\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.020$. It is followed by two parallel branches from $M$ to the downstream reservoir. The first has length $1500\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_1 = 0.022$. The second has length $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.023$. Find the total discharge, the two branch discharges and the head losses.

::schema{nodi="A,4,17;M,13,13,-;V,30,0,-;B,21,11,-;C,21,3,-" condotte="A-M,0.6,;M-B-V,0.42,;M-C-V,0.36," acqua="0,13 4,13 4,20 0,20;27,-2 31,-2 31,3 27,3" solidi="0,12 4,12 4,13 0,13;27,-3 31,-3 31,-2 27,-2" pelo="0,4,20,upstream;27,31,3,downstream" etichette="20,19.5,common pipe section: L = 1000 m - D = 350 mm;20,18.1,branch 1: L = 1500 m - D = 250 mm;20,16.7,branch 2: L = 1200 m - D = 200 mm;12.08,12.08,M;30.92,-0.92,V" titolo="A common pipe in series with two parallel branches"}

$$
r_c = \frac{8 \cdot 0.020 \cdot 1000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.350^5} = 314.6\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_1 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1500}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 2792\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 1200}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^5} = 7127\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_p = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{52.84} + \dfrac{1}{84.42}\right)^2} = \frac{1}{0.03077^2} = 1056\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{tot}} = r_c + r_p = 314.6 + 1056 = 1371\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
Q = \sqrt{\frac{20.00}{1371}} = 0.1208\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 121\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H_c = 314.6 \cdot 0.1208^2 = 4.59\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\Delta H_p = 1056 \cdot 0.1208^2 = 15.41\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \sqrt{\frac{15.41}{2792}} = 74.3\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_2 = \sqrt{\frac{15.41}{7127}} = 46.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check continuity at junction $M$ and the energy balance between the two reservoirs.
$$
Q_1 + Q_2 = 74.3 + 46.5 = 120.8\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q ,
\qquad
\Delta H_c + \Delta H_p = 4.59 + 15.41 = 20.00\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta z
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Reduce the network in stages. The two branches between $M$ and the downstream reservoir are in parallel, so substitute their equivalent resistance $r_p = 1056\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$. The common pipe and this equivalent branch are in series, and their resistances add.

The relation $\Delta z = r_{\mathrm{tot}} Q^2$ gives the total discharge, $121\,\mathrm{L/s}$, which flows through the common pipe. The common pipe accounts for $4.59\,\mathrm{m}$ of head loss and the parallel branches for $15.41\,\mathrm{m}$. Although the common pipe is the longest, its larger diameter keeps its loss to less than one quarter of the available head.

Both branches have the same loss $\Delta H_p$. Use it to calculate $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ separately. Branch $1$ carries $74.3\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and branch $2$ carries $46.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

The velocities are $1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the common pipe, $1.51\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $1$, and $1.48\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in branch $2$, all within the usual operating range.

Two checks recover the conditions used in the solution. The branch discharges add up to the discharge in the common pipe, and the head losses along either path add up to the difference between the reservoir levels.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pipe of length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$, diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda = 0.022$ connects two reservoirs with a head difference of $\Delta z = 25.00\,\mathrm{m}$. To increase capacity, an identical pipe is added in parallel along the first $1200\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the discharge before and after the addition and the head losses in the two sections of the new system.

::schema{nodi="A,4,16;M,16,12,-;V,32,1,-;B,10,16,-;C,10,11,-" condotte="A-B-M,0.42,;A-C-M,0.42,;M-V,0.42," acqua="0,12 4,12 4,18 0,18;29,-1 33,-1 33,4 29,4" solidi="0,11 4,11 4,12 0,12;29,-2 33,-2 33,-1 29,-1" pelo="0,4,18,upstream;29,33,4,downstream" etichette="18,19.5,twinned pipe section: 1200 m - 250 mm;18,18.1,single-pipe section: 800 m - 250 mm;16.99,12.99,M;32.99,0.01,V" titolo="Adding a parallel pipe along the first $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ of the pipeline"}

$$
r_0 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 2000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 3723\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
Q_0 = \sqrt{\frac{25.00}{3723}} = 0.0819\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 81.9\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
r' = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 1200}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 2234\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5} ,
\qquad
r_p = \frac{r'}{4} = 558.4\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r'' = \frac{8 \cdot 0.022 \cdot 800}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.250^5} = 1489\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{tot}} = r_p + r'' = 558.4 + 1489 = 2048\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
Q_n = \sqrt{\frac{25.00}{2048}} = 0.1105\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 110.5\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
\Delta H_p = 558.4 \cdot 0.1105^2 = 6.82\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\Delta H'' = 1489 \cdot 0.1105^2 = 18.18\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check the energy balance and recalculate the head loss in the duplicated section from the flow in one pipe.
$$
\Delta H_p + \Delta H'' = 6.82 + 18.18 = 25.00\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta z ,
\qquad
2234 \cdot \left(\frac{0.1105}{2}\right)^2 = 6.82\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta H_p
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initially there is a single pipe, so $\Delta z = r_0 Q_0^2$ directly gives a discharge of $81.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and a velocity of $1.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

After the parallel pipe is added, the system has two sections in series. The duplicated section comprises two identical pipes in parallel, with equivalent resistance $\dfrac{r'}{4}$. The remaining single-pipe section has resistance $r''$. Both sections carry the same total discharge, so the two resistances add.

The total resistance falls from $3723$ to $2048\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$ and the discharge rises to **$110.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$**, an increase of $35\%$. Duplicating three fifths of the length does not double the discharge because the remaining single pipe still accounts for $18.18\,\mathrm{m}$ of the available $25.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

In the duplicated section each pipe carries $55.2\,\mathrm{L/s}$ at velocity $1.13\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the remaining single pipe, velocity reaches $2.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the highest in the system.

The check confirms that the two losses sum to the available head and recalculates the loss in the duplicated section using the discharge in one branch, half the total.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pipe in the preceding exercise, of length $2000\,\mathrm{m}$ and diameter $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ under head difference $25.00\,\mathrm{m}$, is now paired over its entire length with a second pipe of diameter $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.023$, as shown. Find the new total discharge and the discharge in each pipe.

::schema{nodi="A,4,16;V,32,1,-;B,18,12,-;C,18,5,-" condotte="A-B-V,0.42,;A-C-V,0.36," acqua="0,12 4,12 4,18 0,18;29,-1 33,-1 33,4 29,4" solidi="0,11 4,11 4,12 0,12;29,-2 33,-2 33,-1 29,-1" pelo="0,4,18,upstream;29,33,4,downstream" etichette="18,19.5,existing pipe: 2000 m - 250 mm;18,18.1,new pipe: 2000 m - 200 mm;32.99,0.01,V" titolo="A second pipe installed in parallel over the full length"}

$$
r_2 = \frac{8 \cdot 0.023 \cdot 2000}{9.81 \cdot \pi^2 \cdot 0.200^5} = 11878\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
r_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{1}{\left(\dfrac{1}{61.02} + \dfrac{1}{108.99}\right)^2} = \frac{1}{0.02557^2} = 1530\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}
$$
$$
Q_n = \sqrt{\frac{25.00}{1530}} = 0.1278\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 128\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$
$$
Q_1 = \sqrt{\frac{25.00}{3723}} = 81.9\,\mathrm{L/s} ,
\qquad
Q_2 = \sqrt{\frac{25.00}{11878}} = 45.9\,\mathrm{L/s}
$$

Check continuity and the head loss in the second pipe.
$$
Q_1 + Q_2 = 81.9 + 45.9 = 127.8\,\mathrm{L/s} = Q_n ,
\qquad
11878 \cdot 0.0459^2 = 25.0\,\mathrm{m} = \Delta z
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both pipes connect the same two reservoirs and are therefore in parallel, each with head loss $25.00\,\mathrm{m}$. Adding the inverse square roots of their resistances gives an equivalent resistance of $1530\,\mathrm{s^2/m^5}$.

The total discharge rises from $81.9$ to **$128\,\mathrm{L/s}$**, an increase of $56\%$. This exceeds the increase in the preceding exercise, where only part of the route was duplicated.

The existing pipe still carries its original discharge of $81.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$ because its resistance and the head difference between the reservoir surfaces are unchanged. The entire increase in discharge flows through the new pipe.

The velocities are $1.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the $250\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe and $1.46\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the $200\,\mathrm{mm}$ pipe.

The check verifies continuity. Recalculating the loss in the new pipe from its discharge reproduces the specified head difference.

</div>
