## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0008$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and ends in a bed drop, as shown. Find the distance between the sections where depths are $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$ using three depth increments of $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$.

::schema{solidi="0,0.90 11,0.47 11,0.07 0,0.50;10.6,0.48 11,0.48 11,-1.30 10.6,-1.30" acqua="0,0.90 10.6,0.48 10.6,1.30 0,2.00" frecce="1.0,1.35,2.8,1.30,Q" etichette="4.5,-0.35,i = 0.0008;12.6,-0.70,bed drop" riferimento=0 titolo="M2 profile in a channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ ending at a bed drop"}

Assume an incompressible fluid, steady gradually varied flow and $\alpha = 1$. Calculate the friction slope using the Gauckler–Strickler equation at the local flow depth.

The characteristic depths are $h_c = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_0 = 1.259\,\mathrm{m}$. Between $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.70\,\mathrm{m}$ the profile lies in zone 2 on a mild slope and is therefore **M2**.

Governing relations in symbolic form:
$$
\begin{cases}
E = h + \dfrac{Q^2}{2 g (b\,h)^2} \\[10pt]
J = \dfrac{v^2}{k_s^2\,R^{\frac{4}{3}}} \\[10pt]
\Delta x = \dfrac{E_{j+1} - E_j}{i - \bar{J}}
\end{cases}
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $v$ ($\mathrm{m/s}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $E$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $J$ | $\bar{J}$ | $\Delta x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.000 | 4.000 | 1.500 | 0.6667 | 1.1147 | 0.001545 |  |  | 0 |
| 0.900 | 3.600 | 1.667 | 0.6207 | 1.0416 | 0.002099 | 0.001822 | 71.5 | 71.5 |
| 0.800 | 3.200 | 1.875 | 0.5714 | 0.9792 | 0.002966 | 0.002532 | 36.0 | 107.5 |
| 0.700 | 2.800 | 2.143 | 0.5185 | 0.9340 | 0.004409 | 0.003687 | 15.6 | 123.2 |

$$
x = 123\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The section with $h = 0.70\,\mathrm{m}$ lies $123\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream of the section with $h = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check with the depth increment halved to $0.075\,\mathrm{m}$:

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $E$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $J$ | $\bar{J}$ | $\Delta x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.000 | 1.1147 | 0.001545 |  |  | 0 |
| 0.925 | 1.0590 | 0.001937 | 0.001741 | 59.1 | 59.1 |
| 0.850 | 1.0087 | 0.002481 | 0.002209 | 35.7 | 94.8 |
| 0.775 | 0.9659 | 0.003257 | 0.002869 | 20.7 | 115.5 |
| 0.700 | 0.9340 | 0.004409 | 0.003833 | 10.5 | 126.0 |

The finer calculation gives $126\,\mathrm{m}$, compared with $123\,\mathrm{m}$, a difference of $2.3\%$. The distance is therefore about $125\,\mathrm{m}$ and the original three increments suffice for an estimate to two significant figures.

<div class="spiegazione">

The direct-step method inverts the usual profile calculation. Specify successive depths and find the distance between them. Each step requires only direct evaluations of $E$ and $J$ at the two depths, with no iteration.

At each depth calculate the area $A = b\,h$, velocity $v = \dfrac{Q}{A}$, hydraulic radius $R = \dfrac{b h}{b + 2h}$, specific energy and friction slope. For two successive depths, take the mean friction slope and divide the difference in specific energy by $i - \bar{J}$.

Depth decreases downstream, so specific energy also decreases while the depth remains above $h_c$. The friction slope exceeds the bed slope because $h < h_0$, making $i - \bar{J}$ negative. The ratio of two negative quantities is positive, so the distances $\Delta x$ are positive and the profile proceeds downstream.

The interval lengths differ: the first spans $71.5\,\mathrm{m}$ and the last $15.6\,\mathrm{m}$. Close to $h_0$ the profile approaches its asymptote and the derivative $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ tends to zero. Small changes in depth therefore require large distances. Farther from $h_0$ the derivative increases in magnitude and the intervals shorten.

Repeating the calculation with half-sized depth increments raises the result from $123\,\mathrm{m}$ to $126\,\mathrm{m}$. The small difference shows that the arithmetic mean adequately approximates the friction slope over these steps. Further refinement would make the value converge toward approximately $130\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0008$ carries $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The depth immediately downstream of a sluice gate is $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find how far downstream the depth reaches $0.320\,\mathrm{m}$, using four depth increments of $0.005\,\mathrm{m}$.

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercise.

The characteristic depths are $h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_0 = 1.445\,\mathrm{m}$. Between $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.320\,\mathrm{m}$ the profile lies in zone 3 and is an **M3**.

$$
x = 3.22\,\mathrm{m}
$$

As a check, use just two steps of $0.010\,\mathrm{m}$. The results are $\Delta x_1 = 1.612\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\Delta x_2 = 1.609\,\mathrm{m}$, giving $x = 3.22\,\mathrm{m}$. The third significant digit is unchanged because over $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ the energy gradient varies almost linearly with depth.

<div class="spiegazione">

The M3 profile increases in depth downstream, so use successive depths in increasing order. Each increment is only $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the total change in depth is $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. This M3 profile develops over a short distance of only a few meters.

The friction slope is about $0.08$, a hundred times the bed slope. The velocity exceeds $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the hydraulic radius is only $0.26\,\mathrm{m}$. The equation $J = \dfrac{v^2}{k_s^2\,R^{\frac{4}{3}}}$ contains the square of the velocity in the numerator and the small hydraulic-radius term in the denominator.

Specific energy decreases downstream. As the velocity drops from $6.67\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $6.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the velocity head falls by $0.27\,\mathrm{m}$ and the depth increases by only $0.02\,\mathrm{m}$. The numerator and denominator in the step formula are both negative, so the distances $\Delta x$ are positive.

The four increments give nearly equal distances of about $0.806\,\mathrm{m}$ each. The overall depth change is small and the profile nearly straight: $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ remains close to $0.0062$ throughout the reach.

A check with just two increments gives the same value, so the choice of increment size makes no discernible difference here.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.030$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. At the first section, following a mildly sloping reach, the depth is $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find how far downstream the depth falls to $0.450\,\mathrm{m}$, using three increments of $0.050\,\mathrm{m}$.

::schema{solidi="0,1.80 3,1.60 12,0.25 12,-0.15 3,1.20 0,1.40" frecce="0.6,2.25,1.7,2.22,Q" tratteggi="3,1.60 3,2.60" etichette="1.4,0.80,mild slope;8.2,0.05,i = 0.030;4.8,2.85,slope change" riferimento=0 titolo="S2 profile on a steep reach immediately downstream of a slope change"}

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercises.

The characteristic values are $h_c = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}$, $i_c = 0.00545$ and $h_0 = 0.352\,\mathrm{m}$. Since $i > i_c$ the slope is steep. From $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0.450\,\mathrm{m}$ the profile lies in zone 2 and is an **S2**.

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $v$ ($\mathrm{m/s}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $E$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $J$ | $\bar{J}$ | $\Delta x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.600 | 2.400 | 2.500 | 0.4615 | 0.9186 | 0.005793 |  |  | 0 |
| 0.550 | 2.200 | 2.727 | 0.4314 | 0.9291 | 0.007544 | 0.006668 | 0.452 | 0.452 |
| 0.500 | 2.000 | 3.000 | 0.4000 | 0.9587 | 0.010095 | 0.008819 | 1.398 | 1.850 |
| 0.450 | 1.800 | 3.333 | 0.3673 | 1.0163 | 0.013961 | 0.012028 | 3.205 | 5.055 |

$$
x = 5.06\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Halving the depth increment to $0.025\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $x = 5.08\,\mathrm{m}$, a difference of less than half a percent. The result is therefore $5.1\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The S2 profile starts at critical depth at the slope break and decreases toward $h_0 = 0.352\,\mathrm{m}$. Start the calculation at $0.600\,\mathrm{m}$, slightly below $h_c$. At $h_c$ itself the derivative $\dfrac{dh}{dx}$ is infinite and the method does not apply.

The signs are the reverse of those in the preceding exercises. The depth exceeds $h_0$, so $J < i$ and the numerator $i - \bar{J}$ of the profile equation is positive. The depth is below $h_c$, making the flow supercritical. Specific energy increases downstream because below critical depth a decrease in depth increases the specific energy. The numerator and denominator of the step formula are both positive, giving positive distances $\Delta x$.

The distance per step increases from $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ to $3.21\,\mathrm{m}$ as the depth approaches $h_0$ and the derivative tends to zero. This reverses the trend of Exercise 1, where the calculation moved away from $h_0$.

The total distance, about $5\,\mathrm{m}$, shows how rapidly the flow approaches uniform conditions on this steep slope. After only a few meters the depth is already close to $h_0$.

</div>
