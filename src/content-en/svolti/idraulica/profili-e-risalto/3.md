## Exercise 1

A horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 12.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The supercritical flow has depth $h_1 = 0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ and a jump forms downstream, as shown. Find the conjugate depth, head loss, fraction of energy dissipated and jump length.

::schema{solidi="0,0 12,0 12,-0.60 0,-0.60" acqua="0,0 12,0 12,1.50 10.4,1.50 9.2,1.44 8.0,1.28 6.8,1.00 5.6,0.68 4.4,0.45 0,0.45" linee="0,0.45 4.4,0.45 5.6,0.68 6.8,1.00 8.0,1.28 9.2,1.44 10.4,1.50 12,1.50" pelo="11,12,1.50," frecce="1.0,0.20,2.4,0.20,Q" etichette="2.4,1.25,h_1 = 0.45 m;9.6,1.95,h_2" riferimento=0 titolo="Jump in a horizontal channel of width $5.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume an incompressible fluid, steady flow, hydrostatic pressures at the two end sections, a horizontal bed, negligible bed resistance over the length of the jump, and $\alpha = \beta = 1$.

$$
v_1 = \frac{12.0}{5.00 \cdot 0.45} = 5.333\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{5.333}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.45}} = 2.538
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.45}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 2.538^2} - 1\right) = 0.225 \cdot \left(\sqrt{52.53} - 1\right) = 0.225 \cdot 6.248 = 1.406\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(h_2 - h_1)^3}{4\,h_1 h_2} = \frac{(1.406 - 0.45)^3}{4 \cdot 0.45 \cdot 1.406} = \frac{0.8738}{2.531} = 0.345\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
P = \gamma\,Q\,\Delta E = 9.81 \cdot 12.0 \cdot 0.345 = 40.6\,\mathrm{kW}
$$
$$
L \simeq 6\,(h_2 - h_1) = 6 \cdot 0.956 = 5.74\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Across the jump the depth rises from $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$. The head loss is $0.345\,\mathrm{m}$ and the power dissipated is $40.6\,\mathrm{kW}$ over a length of about $5.7\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check by calculating each specific energy separately:
$$
E_1 = h_1 + \frac{v_1^2}{2g} = 0.45 + \frac{5.333^2}{19.62} = 0.45 + 1.450 = 1.900\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{12.0}{5.00 \cdot 1.406} = 1.707\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
E_2 = 1.406 + \frac{1.707^2}{19.62} = 1.406 + 0.149 = 1.555\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E_1 - E_2 = 1.900 - 1.555 = 0.345\,\mathrm{m}
$$
The fraction of energy dissipated is $\dfrac{\Delta E}{E_1} = \dfrac{0.345}{1.900} = 0.182$, or $18.2\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate the conjugate depth as in the preceding worksheet. With both depths known, the head loss follows from $\Delta E = \dfrac{(h_2 - h_1)^3}{4 h_1 h_2}$, derived from the momentum balance for a horizontal rectangular channel.

The power dissipated equals the weight of fluid passing per unit time, $\gamma\,Q$, multiplied by the head loss. With $\gamma = 9.81\,\mathrm{kN/m^3}$ and $Q$ in $\mathrm{m^3/s}$, the result is in $\mathrm{kW}$.

The balance equations do not give the jump length. Estimate it using Smetana's empirical formula based on laboratory measurements. A Froude number of $2.54$ gives an **oscillating jump**, whose position depends on tailwater conditions and which leaves surface waves downstream.

For a check, calculate the two specific energies separately and subtract them. Their difference equals the loss obtained from the formula, providing a check independent of the compact conjugate-depth relation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 6.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 18.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ at a supercritical depth of $h_1 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the conjugate depth, head loss and dissipated power. Check the result using the momentum function in force units.

::schema{solidi="0,0 12,0 12,-0.60 0,-0.60" acqua="0,0 12,0 12,1.50 10.4,1.50 9.2,1.44 8.0,1.28 6.8,1.00 5.6,0.68 4.4,0.45 0,0.45" linee="0,0.45 4.4,0.45 5.6,0.68 6.8,1.00 8.0,1.28 9.2,1.44 10.4,1.50 12,1.50" pelo="11,12,1.50," frecce="1.0,0.20,2.4,0.20,Q" etichette="2.4,1.25,h_1 = 0.50 m;9.6,1.95,h_2" riferimento=0 titolo="Jump in a horizontal channel of width $6.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercise.

$$
v_1 = \frac{18.0}{6.00 \cdot 0.50} = 6.00\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{6.00}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.50}} = 2.709
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.50}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 2.709^2} - 1\right) = 0.250 \cdot \left(\sqrt{59.73} - 1\right) = 0.250 \cdot 6.728 = 1.682\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(1.682 - 0.50)^3}{4 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 1.682} = \frac{1.6508}{3.364} = 0.491\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
P = 9.81 \cdot 18.0 \cdot 0.491 = 86.7\,\mathrm{kW} ,
\qquad
L \simeq 6\,(1.682 - 0.50) = 7.09\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The jump raises the depth from $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ to $1.68\,\mathrm{m}$ and dissipates $86.7\,\mathrm{kW}$ over about $7.1\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check using the momentum function in force units:
$$
S(h_1) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 6.00 \cdot 0.50^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 18.0^2}{6.00 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 1000} = 7.36 + 108.0 = 115.4\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S(h_2) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 6.00 \cdot 1.682^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 18.0^2}{6.00 \cdot 1.682 \cdot 1000} = 83.3 + 32.1 = 115.4\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the method from the preceding exercise, now with a larger discharge per unit width, $q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ compared with $2.40\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$.

The Froude number is $2.71$, so this is another oscillating jump. The fraction of energy dissipated, $\dfrac{0.491}{2.335} = 0.210$, exceeds that in Exercise 1, as expected for the higher Froude number.

The momentum-function check is independent of the head-loss formula. Equal momentum-function values confirm that the two depths satisfy the integral momentum equation. Their components also illustrate the change across the jump: momentum flux dominates the supercritical section, and hydrostatic force dominates the subcritical section.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At a dam's low-level outlet, water enters a horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 8.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at depth $h_1 = 0.55\,\mathrm{m}$ and discharge $Q = 40.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, as shown. Find the conjugate depth, head loss, dissipated power and jump length, and classify the jump.

::schema{solidi="0,0 14,0 14,-0.70 0,-0.70" acqua="0,0 14,0 14,2.00 12.0,2.00 10.6,1.92 9.2,1.70 7.8,1.30 6.4,0.80 5.0,0.50 0,0.50" linee="0,0.50 5.0,0.50 6.4,0.80 7.8,1.30 9.2,1.70 10.6,1.92 12.0,2.00 14,2.00" pelo="12.8,14,2.00," frecce="1.0,0.22,2.6,0.22,Q" etichette="2.6,1.35,h_1 = 0.55 m;11.0,2.45,h_2" riferimento=0 titolo="Jump at the foot of an outlet in a channel of width $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercises.

$$
v_1 = \frac{40.0}{8.00 \cdot 0.55} = 9.091\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{9.091}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.55}} = \frac{9.091}{2.323} = 3.914
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.55}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 3.914^2} - 1\right) = 0.275 \cdot \left(\sqrt{123.6} - 1\right) = 0.275 \cdot 10.12 = 2.78\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(2.78 - 0.55)^3}{4 \cdot 0.55 \cdot 2.78} = \frac{11.09}{6.116} = 1.813\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
P = 9.81 \cdot 40.0 \cdot 1.813 = 711\,\mathrm{kW} ,
\qquad
L \simeq 6\,(2.78 - 0.55) = 13.4\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The depth increases from $0.55\,\mathrm{m}$ to $2.78\,\mathrm{m}$, the head loss is $1.81\,\mathrm{m}$ and the power dissipated is $711\,\mathrm{kW}$ over a length of about $13.4\,\mathrm{m}$. With $Fr_1 = 3.91$ this is still an **oscillating jump**, just below the range for a steady jump.

Check using the specific energies:
$$
E_1 = 0.55 + \frac{9.091^2}{19.62} = 0.55 + 4.212 = 4.762\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{40.0}{8.00 \cdot 2.78} = 1.799\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
E_2 = 2.78 + \frac{1.799^2}{19.62} = 2.78 + 0.165 = 2.945\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E_1 - E_2 = 4.762 - 2.945 = 1.817\,\mathrm{m}
$$
The fraction dissipated is $\dfrac{1.813}{4.762} = 0.381$, or $38.1\%$ of the upstream energy.

<div class="spiegazione">

The outlet velocity, $9.09\,\mathrm{m/s}$, is typical at the foot of a bottom outlet and gives a Froude number of $3.91$. Head loss depends on the cube of the difference in depths. Here the loss is $1.81\,\mathrm{m}$, five times that of Exercise 1 for a depth change only slightly more than twice as large.

The power dissipated exceeds $700\,\mathrm{kW}$. At an actual installation this energy must be dissipated inside a lined structure to prevent erosion of the natural channel downstream. The design of the stilling basin starts from $h_2$ and the length $L$.

The Froude number of $3.91$ lies in the oscillating-jump range. Reducing $h_1$, for instance by narrowing the outlet section, would raise the Froude number above $4.5$ and give a steady jump that is less sensitive to tailwater conditions.

The specific-energy check gives $1.817\,\mathrm{m}$, compared with $1.813\,\mathrm{m}$ from the formula. The four-millimeter difference results from rounding $h_2$ to three significant figures.

</div>
