## Exercise 1

A horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 10.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The depth downstream of a sluice gate is $h_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, and a hydraulic jump forms farther downstream, as shown. Find the conjugate depth $h_2$ and the Froude numbers at the two sections.

::schema{solidi="0,0 12,0 12,-0.60 0,-0.60" acqua="0,0 12,0 12,1.50 10.4,1.50 9.2,1.44 8.0,1.28 6.8,1.00 5.6,0.68 4.4,0.45 0,0.45" linee="0,0.45 4.4,0.45 5.6,0.68 6.8,1.00 8.0,1.28 9.2,1.44 10.4,1.50 12,1.50" pelo="11,12,1.50," frecce="1.0,0.20,2.4,0.20,Q" etichette="2.2,1.25,h_1 = 0.40 m;10.0,1.95,section 2;2.2,1.70,section 1" riferimento=0 titolo="Horizontal rectangular channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$, with section 1 upstream of the jump and section 2 downstream"}

Assume an incompressible fluid, steady flow, hydrostatic pressure at sections 1 and 2, a horizontal bed, negligible bed resistance over the length of the jump, and $\alpha = \beta = 1$.

Governing relations in symbolic form:
$$
\begin{cases}
v_1 = \dfrac{Q}{b\,h_1} \\[8pt]
Fr_1 = \dfrac{v_1}{\sqrt{g\,h_1}} \\[8pt]
h_2 = \dfrac{h_1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,Fr_1^2} - 1\right)
\end{cases}
$$

$$
v_1 = \frac{10.0}{4.00 \cdot 0.40} = 6.25\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Fr_1 = \frac{6.25}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.40}} = \frac{6.25}{1.981} = 3.155
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.40}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 3.155^2} - 1\right) = 0.20 \cdot \left(\sqrt{80.63} - 1\right) = 0.20 \cdot 7.980 = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_2 = \frac{10.0}{4.00 \cdot 1.596} = 1.566\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_2 = \frac{1.566}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.60}} = 0.395
$$

The conjugate depth is $h_2 = 1.60\,\mathrm{m}$. The jump changes the flow from supercritical ($Fr_1 = 3.16$) to subcritical ($Fr_2 = 0.395$).

Check using the momentum function in force units:
$$
S(h_1) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 0.40^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 10.0^2}{4.00 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 1000} = 3.14 + 62.5 = 65.6\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S(h_2) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 1.60^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 10.0^2}{4.00 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 1000} = 50.2 + 15.6 = 65.8\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
The two values agree within rounding, as required by the integral momentum equation.

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the discharge and area are known, continuity gives the velocity at section 1. The Froude number compares this velocity with the speed of small disturbances, $\sqrt{g\,h_1}$. In a rectangular section this speed is calculated using water depth because the hydraulic depth $h_m = \dfrac{A}{B}$ equals $h$. Here $3.155$ exceeds $1$, so the flow is supercritical and a jump is possible.

The conjugate-depth equation follows from the integral momentum balance for the water between the two sections. Dissipation does not introduce a separate force term. Substitution of $Fr_1$ gives $h_2$ directly, four times $h_1$.

The downstream Froude number is $0.395$, below $1$, so the flow is subcritical as required downstream of a jump. The two depths lie on opposite sides of the critical depth $h_c = 0.860\,\mathrm{m}$.

For a check, evaluate the **momentum function in force units** at each section. Its terms are the hydrostatic force $\gamma\,\dfrac{h}{2}\,b\,h$ and the momentum flux $\rho\,Q\,v$. The values at the two sections agree, so the depth pair satisfies the integral momentum equation from which the formula was derived.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A similar horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The measured depth downstream of a hydraulic jump is $h_2 = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Find the supercritical depth upstream of the jump.

::schema{solidi="0,0 12,0 12,-0.60 0,-0.60" acqua="0,0 12,0 12,1.50 10.4,1.50 9.2,1.44 8.0,1.28 6.8,1.00 5.6,0.68 4.4,0.45 0,0.45" linee="0,0.45 4.4,0.45 5.6,0.68 6.8,1.00 8.0,1.28 9.2,1.44 10.4,1.50 12,1.50" pelo="11,12,1.50," frecce="1.0,0.20,2.4,0.20,Q" etichette="9.6,1.95,h_2 = 1.50 m;2.2,1.70,section 1;9.6,2.35,section 2" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with known downstream depth"}

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercise.

The conjugate-depth relation is symmetric, so it can be applied starting from the downstream section:
$$
\begin{cases}
v_2 = \dfrac{Q}{b\,h_2} \\[8pt]
Fr_2 = \dfrac{v_2}{\sqrt{g\,h_2}} \\[8pt]
h_1 = \dfrac{h_2}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,Fr_2^2} - 1\right)
\end{cases}
$$

$$
v_2 = \frac{6.00}{3.00 \cdot 1.50} = 1.333\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Fr_2 = \frac{1.333}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.50}} = \frac{1.333}{3.836} = 0.3476
$$
$$
h_1 = \frac{1.50}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 0.3476^2} - 1\right) = 0.750 \cdot \left(\sqrt{1.9667} - 1\right) = 0.750 \cdot 0.4024 = 0.302\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{6.00}{3.00 \cdot 0.302} = 6.62\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{6.62}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.302}} = 3.85 > 1
$$

The depth upstream of the jump is $h_1 = 0.302\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check using the symmetric relation $h_1 h_2 (h_1 + h_2) = \dfrac{2 q^2}{g}$, where $q = \dfrac{Q}{b} = 2.00\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$:
$$
h_1 h_2 (h_1 + h_2) = 0.302 \cdot 1.50 \cdot 1.802 = 0.816\,\mathrm{m^3} ,
\qquad
\frac{2 q^2}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 4.00}{9.81} = 0.815\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The momentum equation treats the two depths symmetrically, so the calculation can start from either section. Here the subcritical depth is known. Calculate the downstream Froude number and use the same expression with the two depths interchanged.

The result, $0.302\,\mathrm{m}$, is less than the critical depth $h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}$. Its Froude number is $3.85$, confirming that the flow at section 1 is supercritical, as required for a jump.

The check uses the symmetric expression $h_1 h_2 (h_1 + h_2) = \dfrac{2 q^2}{g}$, obtained by equating the momentum functions before introducing the Froude number. Its value depends only on discharge per unit width, so it provides a check independent of the use of $Fr_2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a horizontal rectangular channel of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$, the measured depths upstream and downstream of a hydraulic jump are $h_1 = 0.35\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_2 = 1.40\,\mathrm{m}$, respectively, as shown. Determine the channel discharge.

::schema{solidi="0,0 12,0 12,-0.60 0,-0.60" acqua="0,0 12,0 12,1.50 10.4,1.50 9.2,1.44 8.0,1.28 6.8,1.00 5.6,0.68 4.4,0.45 0,0.45" linee="0,0.45 4.4,0.45 5.6,0.68 6.8,1.00 8.0,1.28 9.2,1.44 10.4,1.50 12,1.50" pelo="11,12,1.50," frecce="1.0,0.20,2.4,0.20,Q" etichette="2.4,1.25,h_1 = 0.35 m;9.6,1.95,h_2 = 1.40 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Jump in a channel of width $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with both depths measured"}

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercises.

Rearrange the conjugate-depth equation to express $Fr_1$ in terms of the ratio $\dfrac{h_2}{h_1}$:
$$
\frac{2 h_2}{h_1} + 1 = \sqrt{1 + 8 Fr_1^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Fr_1^2 = \frac{1}{8}\left[\left(\frac{2 h_2}{h_1} + 1\right)^2 - 1\right]
$$
$$
\frac{h_2}{h_1} = \frac{1.40}{0.35} = 4.00
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Fr_1^2 = \frac{1}{8}\left(9^2 - 1\right) = \frac{80}{8} = 10.0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Fr_1 = 3.162
$$
$$
v_1 = Fr_1\sqrt{g\,h_1} = 3.162 \cdot \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.35} = 3.162 \cdot 1.853 = 5.86\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = v_1\,b\,h_1 = 5.86 \cdot 2.50 \cdot 0.35 = 5.13\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

The discharge is $Q = 5.13\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, or $5130\,\mathrm{L/s}$.

Check using the integral momentum equation in the form $h_1 h_2 (h_1 + h_2) = \dfrac{2 q^2}{g}$, which gives
$$
Q = b\,\sqrt{\frac{g\,h_1 h_2 (h_1 + h_2)}{2}} = 2.50 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{9.81 \cdot 0.35 \cdot 1.40 \cdot 1.75}{2}} = 2.50 \cdot \sqrt{4.207} = 5.13\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A hydraulic jump can serve as a discharge meter because the two conjugate depths are readily measured and have a known relation to discharge. Here solve the conjugate-depth equation for $Fr_1$. Isolate the square root and square both sides to obtain the Froude number from the depth ratio alone.

The ratio is exactly $4$, giving $\sqrt{1 + 8Fr_1^2} = 9$ and $Fr_1^2 = 10$. The Froude number gives the velocity at section 1, and continuity then gives discharge.

The symmetric relation between conjugate depths gives the discharge in one step, without calculating the Froude number. Both methods give $5.13\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

The head loss across this jump is $\Delta E = \dfrac{(1.40 - 0.35)^3}{4 \cdot 0.35 \cdot 1.40} = 0.591\,\mathrm{m}$. The next worksheet examines this loss.

</div>
