## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, roughness coefficient $k_s = 60\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0006$ carries $Q = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A sill at its downstream end imposes a depth of $h = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$ at the last section, as shown. Classify the bed slope and water-surface profile and state in which direction the profile should be traced.

::schema{solidi="0,0.90 10,0.40 10,0.00 0,0.50;9.3,0.435 9.8,0.435 9.8,1.70 9.3,1.70" acqua="0,0.90 9.3,0.435 9.3,2.235 0,2.60" pelo="8.3,9.3,2.235," quote="9.05 0.435,9.05 2.235,1.80 m,-0.8" frecce="1.0,1.65,2.8,1.60,Q" etichette="4.0,-0.35,i = 0.0006;10.6,1.05,sill" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a downstream sill"}

Assume incompressible, steady flow with hydrostatic pressure at the sections and $\alpha = 1$.

Critical depth:
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{Q^2}{g\,b^2}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{4.50^2}{9.81 \cdot 3.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.2294}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Find the normal depth by iterating the Gauckler–Strickler equation $Q = k_s\,A\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{i}$ with $A = b h$ and $R = \dfrac{b h}{b + 2h}$:

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $C$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.200 | 3.600 | 5.400 | 0.6667 | 4.038 |
| 1.300 | 3.900 | 5.600 | 0.6964 | 4.503 |
| 1.299 | 3.897 | 5.598 | 0.6961 | 4.499 |

$$
h_0 = 1.30\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The critical slope is the bed slope for which $h_0 = h_c$. At $h = h_c = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}$, the values are $A = 1.836\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $C = 4.224\,\mathrm{m}$, $R = 0.4347\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v = 2.451\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
$$
i_c = \left(\frac{v}{k_s\,R^{\frac{2}{3}}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{2.451}{60 \cdot 0.5739}\right)^2 = 0.00507
$$

Compare the results:
$$
i = 0.0006 < i_c = 0.00507 ,
\qquad
h = 1.80\,\mathrm{m} > h_0 = 1.30\,\mathrm{m} > h_c = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The bed slope is **mild** and the profile lies in **zone 1**. It is therefore an **M1** profile with depth increasing downstream. At the last section the Froude number is
$$
v = \frac{Q}{b\,h} = \frac{4.50}{3.00 \cdot 1.80} = 0.833\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{0.833}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.80}} = 0.198 < 1 ,
$$
so the flow is subcritical and the profile is traced **upstream** from the sill.

Check the calculation by substituting $h_0 = 1.30\,\mathrm{m}$ into the Gauckler–Strickler equation:
$$
Q = 60 \cdot 3.900 \cdot 0.6964^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot \sqrt{0.0006} = 60 \cdot 3.900 \cdot 0.7857 \cdot 0.02449 = 4.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
which reproduces the specified discharge.

<div class="spiegazione">

Critical depth depends only on discharge and width. It can be found first without iteration. Normal depth also depends on roughness and bed slope. It appears in both the flow area and hydraulic radius in the Gauckler–Strickler equation, which has no closed-form solution for depth here. The first trial, with $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, gives a discharge below the specified value, so increase the depth. At $h = 1.30\,\mathrm{m}$, the calculated discharge agrees with $4.50\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ to within one part in a thousand, and the iteration stops.

To determine the **critical slope**, require uniform flow at critical depth. Calculate area, hydraulic radius and velocity for $h = h_c$ and find the corresponding friction slope. This is the required bed slope. Since $i < i_c$, the bed slope is mild and uniform flow is subcritical, with $h_0 > h_c$.

The sill imposes a depth greater than both $h_0$ and $h_c$, placing the final section in zone 1. The numerator $i - J$ in the profile equation is positive because $h > h_0$ implies $J < i$. The denominator $1 - Fr^2$ is positive because flow is subcritical. Thus $\dfrac{dh}{dx} > 0$ and the water-surface profile rises downstream. This is an **M1** profile that approaches $h_0$ asymptotically upstream.

The flow is subcritical, so disturbances can travel against it and the condition imposed by the sill propagates upstream. Start the profile calculation at the sill and proceed upstream.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0008$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and ends in the bed drop shown. Classify the profile in the reach upstream of the drop and identify the control section.

::schema{solidi="0,0.90 9.6,0.47 9.6,0.07 0,0.50;9.2,0.48 9.6,0.48 9.6,-1.30 9.2,-1.30" acqua="0,0.90 9.2,0.48 9.2,1.36 0,2.00" frecce="1.0,1.35,2.8,1.30,Q" etichette="4.5,-0.35,i = 0.0008;10.9,-0.20,bed drop" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ ending in a bed drop"}

Assume incompressible, steady flow with $\alpha = 1$.

$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 4.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.2294}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.200 | 4.800 | 0.7500 | 5.604 |
| 1.300 | 5.200 | 0.7879 | 6.273 |
| 1.260 | 5.040 | 0.7730 | 6.003 |

$$
h_0 = 1.26\,\mathrm{m}
$$

At $h = h_c$, the values are $A = 2.449\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $C = 5.224\,\mathrm{m}$, $R = 0.4688\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v = 2.451\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving
$$
i_c = \left(\frac{2.451}{50 \cdot 0.6035}\right)^2 = 0.00660 > i = 0.0008
$$

The bed slope is **mild**. At the outlet above the bed drop, critical depth is established. This is the **control section**, with $h = h_c = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}$. Moving upstream, depth increases from $h_c$ toward $h_0$, so the profile lies in **zone 2**. It is an **M2** profile with depth decreasing downstream. The flow is subcritical, so the calculation proceeds **upstream** from the outlet.

Check the specific energy at the outlet:
$$
E = h_c + \frac{Q^2}{2 g (b\,h_c)^2} = 0.612 + \frac{36.0}{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 5.996} = 0.612 + 0.306 = 0.918\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
This equals $\dfrac{3}{2}h_c = 1.5 \cdot 0.612 = 0.918\,\mathrm{m}$, the minimum specific energy for the given discharge.

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate $h_c$, $h_0$ and $i_c$ as in the previous exercise. Since $i$ is much smaller than $i_c$, the bed slope is mild.

The bed drop removes the downstream support for the flow. The depth cannot remain above critical depth because subcritical flow needs a downstream level condition, and the channel ends here. The flow therefore passes through critical depth at the outlet section, where specific energy is a minimum for the given discharge. The outlet is a control section because discharge alone determines the depth there.

Upstream of the outlet the depth lies between $h_c$ and $h_0$, so the representative point lies in zone 2. The numerator $i - J$ is negative because $h < h_0$ implies $J > i$. The denominator $1 - Fr^2$ is positive because $h > h_c$. Hence $\dfrac{dh}{dx} < 0$, and the water-surface profile falls downstream from $h_0$ to $h_c$. The **M2** profile is also called an outlet drawdown curve.

Start at the outlet section and trace the profile upstream. The flow is subcritical, so the downstream condition is transmitted upstream.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular channel of width $b = 2.50\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 70\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0005$ carries $Q = 3.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A vertical sluice gate releases water at depth $h_1 = 0.22\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Classify the profile immediately downstream of the gate, state in which direction it should be traced and determine whether the jump can form against the gate.

::schema{solidi="0,0.90 12,0.30 12,-0.20 0,0.40;3.85,0.70 4.15,0.70 4.15,2.90 3.85,2.90" acqua="0,0.90 3.85,0.71 3.85,2.45 0,2.60;4.15,0.69 12,0.30 12,0.52 4.15,0.91" frecce="0.8,1.70,2.4,1.68,Q" etichette="4.0,3.15,gate;9.5,-0.50,i = 0.0005;7.0,1.25,h_1 = 0.22 m" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical sluice gate in a rectangular channel of width $2.50\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Assume incompressible, steady flow with $\alpha = 1$.

$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 2.50^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.1468}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.528\,\mathrm{m}
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.000 | 2.500 | 0.5556 | 2.644 |
| 1.100 | 2.750 | 0.5851 | 3.011 |
| 1.097 | 2.743 | 0.5843 | 3.000 |

$$
h_0 = 1.10\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
i_c = 0.00396 > i = 0.0005
$$

The bed slope is **mild**. At the gate the depth is less than $h_c$, placing the section in **zone 3**. The **M3** profile increases in depth downstream. The flow is supercritical:
$$
v_1 = \frac{3.00}{2.50 \cdot 0.22} = 5.45\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{5.45}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.22}} = 3.71 > 1 ,
$$
so the profile is traced **downstream** from the gate.

Depth conjugate to $h_1$:
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.22}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 3.71^2} - 1\right) = 0.11 \cdot \left(10.55 - 1\right) = 1.05\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Since $h_2 = 1.05\,\mathrm{m} < h_0 = 1.10\,\mathrm{m}$, the tailwater is deeper than the jump requires at the gate. The jump is **forced back against the gate**, leaving it partly submerged.

Check the momentum function in force units at the conjugate sections:
$$
S(h_1) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 2.50 \cdot 0.22^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 3.00^2}{2.50 \cdot 0.22 \cdot 1000} = 0.594 + 16.36 = 16.95\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
$$
S(h_2) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 2.50 \cdot 1.05^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 3.00^2}{2.50 \cdot 1.05 \cdot 1000} = 13.52 + 3.43 = 16.95\,\mathrm{kN} ,
$$
Both values agree, as required by the integral momentum equation.

<div class="spiegazione">

The gate opening determines the depth at the vena contracta downstream, making it a control section. The depth there is $0.22\,\mathrm{m}$, less than the critical depth $0.528\,\mathrm{m}$. The emerging flow is therefore supercritical and lies in zone 3.

In the profile equation the numerator is negative because $h < h_0$ implies $J > i$. The denominator is also negative because the flow is supercritical. Their ratio is positive, so $\dfrac{dh}{dx} > 0$ and the profile rises downstream. It is an **M3** profile.

The flow is supercritical. No disturbance can travel back to the gate, so trace the profile downstream.

To locate the jump, compare the depth conjugate to $h_1$ with the normal depth in the downstream reach. Here the conjugate depth is less than $h_0$, so the tailwater is deeper than the jump requires. The jump is pushed back to the gate, leaving it **submerged**. If the conjugate depth exceeded $h_0$, the jump would move away from the gate, with an M3 profile between them.

The momentum-function check is independent of the conjugate-depth formula because it uses the integral momentum equation directly. The two momentum-function values agree, confirming that $(0.22;\,1.05)$ is a conjugate pair.

</div>
