## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.0008$ carries $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A vertical sluice gate releases water at depth $h_1 = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, and the downstream reach is long enough for uniform flow to develop, as shown. Determine whether the jump forms at the gate or farther downstream.

::schema{solidi="0,0.90 14,0.20 14,-0.30 0,0.40;4.85,0.655 5.15,0.655 5.15,2.90 4.85,2.90" acqua="0,0.90 4.85,0.66 4.85,2.45 0,2.60;5.15,0.655 14,0.20 14,0.50 5.15,0.955" frecce="1.0,1.70,2.6,1.68,Q" etichette="5.0,3.15,gate;7.2,1.30,h_1 = 0.30 m;10.5,-0.60,i = 0.0008" riferimento=0 titolo="Sluice gate in a rectangular channel of width $4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $Q = 8.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$"}

Assume an incompressible fluid, steady flow, $\alpha = \beta = 1$ and hydrostatic pressure at sections with essentially straight, parallel streamlines.

$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{8.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 4.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.4078}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.400 | 5.600 | 0.8235 | 7.654 |
| 1.450 | 5.800 | 0.8406 | 8.036 |
| 1.445 | 5.780 | 0.8389 | 7.998 |

$$
h_0 = 1.445\,\mathrm{m}
$$

With $h = h_c$ the results are $A = 2.966\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $R = 0.5410\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v = 2.697\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Thus $i_c = \left(\dfrac{2.697}{55 \cdot 0.6639}\right)^2 = 0.00546 > i$ and the channel has a **mild slope**.

Conjugate depth of the flow issuing from the gate:
$$
v_1 = \frac{8.00}{4.00 \cdot 0.30} = 6.667\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{6.667}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.30}} = 3.886
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.30}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 3.886^2} - 1\right) = 0.150 \cdot \left(\sqrt{121.8} - 1\right) = 0.150 \cdot 10.04 = 1.506\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Comparison with the depth in the downstream reach:
$$
h_2 = 1.506\,\mathrm{m} > h_0 = 1.445\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The tailwater depth is less than the jump would require at the gate. The supercritical flow continues downstream on an M3 profile, giving a **swept-out jump**.

Check using the momentum function in force units at the two sections the jump would connect:
$$
S(0.30) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 0.30^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 8.00^2}{4.00 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 1000} = 1.77 + 53.3 = 55.1\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S(1.445) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 1.445^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 8.00^2}{4.00 \cdot 1.445 \cdot 1000} = 41.0 + 11.1 = 52.1\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
The supercritical flow has the greater momentum function, driving the jump downstream as predicted by the depth comparison.

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculating $h_c$, $h_0$ and $i_c$ shows that the slope is mild. The downstream reach is long enough for normal depth $h_0 = 1.445\,\mathrm{m}$ to develop. Since this exceeds $h_c$, the downstream flow is subcritical.

The flow issuing from the gate is supercritical, with $Fr_1 = 3.89$. For a jump to begin immediately below the gate, the downstream reach would have to supply the conjugate depth of $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, namely $1.506\,\mathrm{m}$. Its actual depth is $1.445\,\mathrm{m}$.

The available tailwater is therefore **too shallow**. The jump moves downstream, where the supercritical flow has slowed along the M3 profile and its conjugate depth has fallen to $h_0$. This is a swept-out jump. The next exercise finds its distance from the gate.

Compare the two momentum functions. A stationary jump requires them to agree. Here the momentum function of the supercritical flow exceeds that of the tailwater, forcing the jump downstream. This confirms the conclusion obtained by comparing depths.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using the data from the preceding exercise, find the distance from the gate to the start of the jump and the head loss across the jump, as shown.

::schema{solidi="0,0 16,0 16,-0.45 0,-0.45;0.85,0.30 1.15,0.30 1.15,2.60 0.85,2.60" acqua="1.15,0 16,0 16,1.45 14.2,1.45 13.0,1.40 11.8,1.26 10.6,1.00 9.4,0.62 8.2,0.32 1.15,0.30" linee="1.15,0.30 8.2,0.32 9.4,0.62 10.6,1.00 11.8,1.26 13.0,1.40 14.2,1.45 16,1.45" tratteggi="1.15,1.445 16,1.445" etichette="4.6,0.75,M3;11.0,1.80,hydraulic jump;1.0,2.85,gate;16.9,1.45,h_0" riferimento=0 titolo="Supercritical flow follows an M3 profile to the section where its conjugate depth is $h_0$"}

The depth at the start of the jump is the shallower conjugate depth corresponding to $h_0 = 1.445\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
v_0 = \frac{8.00}{4.00 \cdot 1.445} = 1.384\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_0 = \frac{1.384}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.445}} = 0.3676
$$
$$
h_1^* = \frac{1.445}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 0.3676^2} - 1\right) = 0.7225 \cdot \left(\sqrt{2.081} - 1\right) = 0.7225 \cdot 0.4426 = 0.320\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Calculate the distance from the gate, where $h = 0.300\,\mathrm{m}$, to the section where $h = 0.320\,\mathrm{m}$, using the direct-step method with $\Delta x = \dfrac{E_{j+1} - E_j}{i - \bar{J}}$ and depth increments of $0.005\,\mathrm{m}$. Stop at $h_1^*$.

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $v$ ($\mathrm{m/s}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $E$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $J$ | $\bar{J}$ | $\Delta x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.300 | 6.667 | 0.2609 | 2.5653 | 0.08814 |  |  | 0 |
| 0.305 | 6.557 | 0.2646 | 2.4966 | 0.08366 | 0.08590 | 0.807 | 0.81 |
| 0.310 | 6.452 | 0.2684 | 2.4315 | 0.07948 | 0.08157 | 0.806 | 1.61 |
| 0.315 | 6.349 | 0.2721 | 2.3697 | 0.07557 | 0.07752 | 0.806 | 2.42 |
| 0.320 | 6.250 | 0.2759 | 2.3110 | 0.07191 | 0.07374 | 0.805 | 3.22 |

$$
x = 3.22\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Head loss across the jump between $h_1^* = 0.320\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_0 = 1.445\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(1.445 - 0.320)^3}{4 \cdot 0.320 \cdot 1.445} = \frac{1.4238}{1.850} = 0.770\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
L \simeq 6\,(1.445 - 0.320) = 6.75\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The jump starts $3.22\,\mathrm{m}$ from the gate, extends for about $6.8\,\mathrm{m}$ and dissipates $0.770\,\mathrm{m}$ of head.

Check by repeating the calculation with just two increments of $0.010\,\mathrm{m}$. These give $\Delta x_1 = 1.612\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\Delta x_2 = 1.609\,\mathrm{m}$, hence $x = 3.22\,\mathrm{m}$. Doubling the depth increment leaves the result unchanged to three significant figures because the friction slope varies almost linearly with depth over this reach.

<div class="spiegazione">

The jump occurs where the upstream and downstream flows have the same momentum function. The downstream depth is $h_0$, so the initial depth must be the conjugate depth of $h_0$. Apply the formula starting from the subcritical flow. The result, $0.320\,\mathrm{m}$, only slightly exceeds the depth at the gate, so the jump forms nearby.

An M3 profile connects the two sections, with depth increasing from $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$ to $0.320\,\mathrm{m}$. Use the direct-step method to find the distance. For each depth pair calculate the specific energies and friction slopes, average the friction slopes, and divide the specific-energy difference by $i - \bar{J}$.

The table shows specific energy decreasing along the profile because the velocity head drops by more than the depth increases. The friction slope is large, around $0.08$ or a hundred times the bed slope. The velocity exceeds $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the hydraulic radius is only $0.26\,\mathrm{m}$. Both the denominator $i - \bar{J}$ and the numerator are negative, so the distances $\Delta x$ are positive and the profile proceeds downstream.

The four increments contribute almost equally, about $0.806\,\mathrm{m}$ each, giving a total of $3.22\,\mathrm{m}$.

Use the actual conjugate depths at the jump, $0.320\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.445\,\mathrm{m}$, to calculate its head loss. The upstream jump depth differs from the depth at the gate. Smetana's formula gives a length of about $6.8\,\mathrm{m}$, so the jump occupies the reach between $3.2\,\mathrm{m}$ and $10.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the gate.

Repeating the calculation with double the depth increment leaves the result unchanged, confirming that the original increment is adequate.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Use the data from Exercise 1 again. A sill immediately downstream of the gate raises the water level to $h = 1.70\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. Determine where the jump occurs.

::schema{solidi="0,0 14,0 14,-0.45 0,-0.45;2.85,0.30 3.15,0.30 3.15,2.90 2.85,2.90;12.6,0 13.1,0 13.1,1.20 12.6,1.20" acqua="0,0 2.85,0 2.85,2.50 0,2.50;3.15,0 12.6,0 12.6,1.70 3.15,1.70" pelo="10.6,12.6,1.70," frecce="0.8,1.25,2.0,1.25,Q" etichette="3.0,3.15,gate;12.85,1.55,sill;7.5,2.05,h = 1.70 m" riferimento=0 titolo="A sill immediately downstream of the gate raises the water level to $1.70\,\mathrm{m}$"}

Upstream conjugate depth for the water level imposed by the sill:
$$
v = \frac{8.00}{4.00 \cdot 1.70} = 1.176\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{1.176}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.70}} = 0.2881
$$
$$
h_1^* = \frac{1.70}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 0.2881^2} - 1\right) = 0.850 \cdot \left(\sqrt{1.664} - 1\right) = 0.850 \cdot 0.2898 = 0.246\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Comparison with the depth at the gate:
$$
h_1^* = 0.246\,\mathrm{m} < h_1 = 0.300\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Depth on the M3 profile increases downstream from $0.300\,\mathrm{m}$. Nowhere downstream of the gate can it equal $0.246\,\mathrm{m}$. The jump is therefore **forced back against the gate**, submerging the vena contracta and producing a **submerged jump**.

Check using the momentum functions in force units:
$$
S(0.30) = 1.77 + 53.3 = 55.1\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
S(1.70) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 1.70^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 8.00^2}{4.00 \cdot 1.70 \cdot 1000} = 56.7 + 9.41 = 66.1\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
The downstream flow has a greater momentum function than the supercritical flow, so the jump is pushed upstream to the gate.

<div class="spiegazione">

The sill sets the water level immediately downstream of the gate. This condition determines the jump position. The supercritical depth conjugate to $1.70\,\mathrm{m}$ would be $0.246\,\mathrm{m}$, smaller than the actual depth at the gate.

Depth on the M3 profile can only increase downstream, so it never reaches $0.246\,\mathrm{m}$. There is no possible free-jump location downstream of the gate, and the jump rests against it. The vena contracta is then submerged and the free-jump conjugate-depth formula no longer applies. For the same gate opening and upstream level, the gate delivers a smaller discharge.

The momentum comparison confirms the result. The downstream flow exerts the greater force and pushes the jump upstream. The ordering is the reverse of that in Exercise 1.

</div>

## Exercise 4

At the foot of an outlet, water enters a rectangular channel of width $b = 5.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at discharge $Q = 15.0\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and depth $h_1 = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, as shown. The downstream channel depth is $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$. Determine whether the jump stays within the lined reach and calculate how far to lower the stilling basin floor and how long to make the basin.

::schema{solidi="0,0.60 3,0.60 3,0 13,0 13,0.60 17,0.60 17,-0.70 0,-0.70" acqua="0,0.60 3,0.60 3,0 13,0 13,0.60 17,0.60 17,2.20 13,2.20 11.4,2.14 9.8,1.95 8.2,1.60 6.6,1.15 5.0,0.95 3,0.95 0,0.95" linee="0,0.95 5.0,0.95 6.6,1.15 8.2,1.60 9.8,1.95 11.4,2.14 13,2.20 17,2.20" frecce="0.7,0.74,1.7,0.74,Q" etichette="1.5,1.55,h_1 = 0.50 m;15.0,1.60,downstream channel;13.6,0.28,d;8.0,2.50,stilling basin" riferimento=0 titolo="Stilling basin with its floor $d$ below the downstream channel bed"}

Assume an incompressible fluid, steady flow and $\alpha = \beta = 1$. When sizing the basin depression, neglect the change in $h_1$ caused by lowering its floor.

$$
v_1 = \frac{15.0}{5.00 \cdot 0.50} = 6.00\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_1 = \frac{6.00}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.50}} = 2.709
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.50}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 2.709^2} - 1\right) = 0.250 \cdot 6.728 = 1.682\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The jump requires a downstream depth of $1.682\,\mathrm{m}$, while the receiving channel provides $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
1.682\,\mathrm{m} > 1.60\,\mathrm{m}
$$
so without a depression the jump would move beyond the lined reach. Lowering the floor by $d$ increases the available basin depth by the same amount. The required condition is therefore
$$
1.60 + d \geq h_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d \geq 1.682 - 1.60 = 0.082\,\mathrm{m}
$$
Adopt $d = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ to provide a margin.

Basin length:
$$
L \simeq 6\,(h_2 - h_1) = 6 \cdot 1.182 = 7.09\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_{\text{tank}} = 7.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Head loss and power dissipation:
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(1.682 - 0.50)^3}{4 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 1.682} = 0.491\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
P = 9.81 \cdot 15.0 \cdot 0.491 = 72.2\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

Check using the momentum function in force units:
$$
S(0.50) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 5.00 \cdot 0.50^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 15.0^2}{5.00 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 1000} = 6.13 + 90.0 = 96.1\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S(1.682) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 5.00 \cdot 1.682^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 15.0^2}{5.00 \cdot 1.682 \cdot 1000} = 69.4 + 26.8 = 96.2\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Begin by finding the conjugate depth that the jump requires downstream. Here it is $1.682\,\mathrm{m}$, while the receiving channel provides $1.60\,\mathrm{m}$. This would allow the jump to move past the lining and erode the natural bed.

Lowering the basin floor increases the depth measured from that floor for the same tailwater elevation. Set the available depth equal to the conjugate depth to obtain the minimum depression, $0.082\,\mathrm{m}$. Adopt $0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ because the receiving-channel level varies with discharge. The margin keeps the jump inside the basin when the tailwater falls.

Set the basin length equal to the jump length estimated by Smetana's formula, rounded up. The power dissipated, $72\,\mathrm{kW}$, is the energy the basin removes from the flow per unit time.

The momentum functions in force units agree within rounding, confirming that $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.682\,\mathrm{m}$ are conjugate depths.

The Froude number is $2.71$, so the jump is oscillating and its position responds to changes in tailwater level. This provides a further reason for a margin in the basin depression.

</div>
