## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 60\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ carries $Q = 5.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The upstream bed slope is $i_1 = 0.0005$ and the downstream slope is $i_2 = 0.0025$, as shown. Classify the profile in each reach and identify the control section.

::schema{solidi="0,1.30 6,1.15 12,0.85 12,0.45 6,0.75 0,0.90" frecce="1.0,1.95,2.8,1.92,Q" tratteggi="6,1.15 6,2.40" etichette="2.8,0.30,i_1 = 0.0005;9.4,-0.05,i_2 = 0.0025;6.0,2.65,slope change" riferimento=0 titolo="Change of slope between two mildly sloping reaches"}

Assume incompressible, steady flow with $\alpha = 1$.

$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{5.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 3.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.2832}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.657\,\mathrm{m}
$$

With $h = h_c$, the results are $A = 1.970\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $C = 4.313\,\mathrm{m}$, $R = 0.4568\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v = 2.538\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving
$$
i_c = \left(\frac{2.538}{60 \cdot 0.5933}\right)^2 = 0.00509
$$

Normal depths:

| Reach | $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream, $i_1 = 0.0005$ | 1.500 | 4.500 | 0.7500 | 4.984 |
| Upstream, $i_1 = 0.0005$ | 1.504 | 4.512 | 0.7510 | 5.001 |
| Downstream, $i_2 = 0.0025$ | 0.840 | 2.520 | 0.5385 | 5.004 |
| Downstream, $i_2 = 0.0025$ | 0.8396 | 2.519 | 0.5383 | 5.000 |

$$
h_{01} = 1.504\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_{02} = 0.840\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Both slopes are below $i_c = 0.00509$, so **both reaches have mild slopes** and carry subcritical uniform flow. Both $h_{01}$ and $h_{02}$ exceed $h_c = 0.657\,\mathrm{m}$.

The downstream reach is long enough to develop uniform flow at $h_{02} = 0.840\,\mathrm{m}$. Depth at the slope break is therefore $0.840\,\mathrm{m}$, making it the **control section** for the upstream reach. In the upstream reach the depth is between $h_c$ and $h_{01}$, in **zone 2**. The **M2** profile approaches $h_{01} = 1.504\,\mathrm{m}$ in the upstream direction. Since the flow is subcritical, trace the profile **from downstream to upstream**. The downstream reach remains in uniform flow.

Check. The Froude numbers are
$$
Fr_{01} = \frac{5.00}{3.00 \cdot 1.504 \cdot \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.504}} = 0.289 ,
\qquad
Fr_{02} = \frac{5.00}{3.00 \cdot 0.840 \cdot \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.840}} = 0.691 ,
$$
both below $1$. Both flows are therefore subcritical, as expected for uniform flow on mild slopes, and no hydraulic jump forms.

<div class="spiegazione">

First compare each bed slope separately with the critical slope. Here even the steeper slope, $0.0025$, remains below $i_c = 0.00509$, so both reaches have mild slopes.

Flow in both reaches is subcritical, so the downstream condition can influence the upstream reach. If sufficiently long, the downstream reach develops uniform flow at its normal depth $h_{02}$. The depth $h_{02}$ at the slope break supplies the initial condition for the upstream profile.

The depth at the slope break, $0.840\,\mathrm{m}$, is between $h_c = 0.657\,\mathrm{m}$ and $h_{01} = 1.504\,\mathrm{m}$ in the upstream reach. It lies in zone 2, giving an M2 profile that decreases downstream from its asymptotic depth $h_{01}$ to $0.840\,\mathrm{m}$.

The flow never reaches critical depth and no jump forms. This distinguishes the result from the case of a steep downstream slope.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The upstream bed slope is $i_1 = 0.040$ and the downstream slope is $i_2 = 0.0025$, as shown. Determine where the jump forms and find its distance from the change of slope.

::schema{solidi="0,2.20 5,1.30 12,0.95 12,0.55 5,0.90 0,1.80" frecce="1.0,2.80,2.4,2.55,Q" tratteggi="5,1.30 5,2.70" etichette="2.2,0.95,i_1 = 0.040;9.0,0.15,i_2 = 0.0025;5.4,2.95,slope change" riferimento=0 titolo="Transition from a steep reach to a mildly sloping reach"}

Assume an incompressible fluid, steady flow and $\alpha = \beta = 1$.

$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 3.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.4077}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.742\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
i_c = 0.00612
$$

| Reach | $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream, $i_1 = 0.040$ | 0.3948 | 1.1844 | 0.3125 | 6.000 |
| Downstream, $i_2 = 0.0025$ | 1.0159 | 3.0477 | 0.6057 | 6.000 |

$$
h_{01} = 0.395\,\mathrm{m} < h_c ,
\qquad
h_{02} = 1.016\,\mathrm{m} > h_c
$$

The upstream reach has a **steep slope** ($i_1 > i_c$) and carries supercritical flow. The downstream reach has a **mild slope** ($i_2 < i_c$) and carries subcritical flow. A jump connects the two.

Conjugate depth of the upstream flow:
$$
v_{01} = \frac{6.00}{3.00 \cdot 0.395} = 5.063\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_{01} = \frac{5.063}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.395}} = 2.572
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.395}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 2.572^2} - 1\right) = 0.1975 \cdot \left(\sqrt{53.92} - 1\right) = 0.1975 \cdot 6.343 = 1.253\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Since $1.253\,\mathrm{m} > h_{02} = 1.016\,\mathrm{m}$, the tailwater cannot hold the jump at the slope break. The supercritical flow continues onto the mild slope as an **M3** profile and the jump is swept downstream.

Depth at the start of the jump, the upstream conjugate depth of $h_{02}$:
$$
v_{02} = \frac{6.00}{3.00 \cdot 1.016} = 1.969\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_{02} = \frac{1.969}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.016}} = 0.6237
$$
$$
h_1^* = \frac{1.016}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 0.6237^2} - 1\right) = 0.508 \cdot \left(\sqrt{4.112} - 1\right) = 0.508 \cdot 1.0278 = 0.522\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Distance by the direct-step method, using three depth increments of $0.0424\,\mathrm{m}$ on the mild slope:

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $v$ ($\mathrm{m/s}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $E$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $J$ | $\bar{J}$ | $\Delta x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $x$ ($\mathrm{m}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3948 | 5.066 | 0.3125 | 1.7028 | 0.04000 |  |  | 0 |
| 0.4373 | 4.574 | 0.3386 | 1.5034 | 0.02930 | 0.03465 | 6.20 | 6.20 |
| 0.4797 | 4.169 | 0.3635 | 1.3657 | 0.02215 | 0.02573 | 5.93 | 12.13 |
| 0.5220 | 3.831 | 0.3872 | 1.2702 | 0.01719 | 0.01967 | 5.56 | 17.69 |

$$
x \simeq 17.7\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Head loss across the jump:
$$
\Delta E = \frac{(1.016 - 0.522)^3}{4 \cdot 0.522 \cdot 1.016} = \frac{0.1206}{2.121} = 0.0569\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The jump begins about $17.7\,\mathrm{m}$ from the slope break and dissipates $0.057\,\mathrm{m}$ of head.

Check using the momentum function in force units at the two jump sections:
$$
S(0.522) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 0.522^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 6.00^2}{3.00 \cdot 0.522 \cdot 1000} = 4.01 + 22.99 = 27.0\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
S(1.016) = \frac{9.81 \cdot 3.00 \cdot 1.016^2}{2} + \frac{1000 \cdot 6.00^2}{3.00 \cdot 1.016 \cdot 1000} = 15.19 + 11.81 = 27.0\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The upstream bed slope is six times the critical slope, producing supercritical flow with $h_{01}$ below $h_c$. The downstream bed slope is less than critical, giving subcritical flow with $h_{02}$ above $h_c$. The transition from supercritical to subcritical flow requires a hydraulic jump and cannot be continuous.

The jump occurs where the two momentum functions agree, so calculate the depth conjugate to $h_{01}$. If it equaled $h_{02}$, the jump would occur exactly at the slope change. Here the conjugate depth is $1.253\,\mathrm{m}$, greater than $h_{02} = 1.016\,\mathrm{m}$. The supercritical flow enters the mildly sloping reach with depth $h_{01}$ and follows an M3 profile with depth increasing downstream.

The jump begins where the M3 profile reaches the depth conjugate to $h_{02}$, namely $0.522\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the distance with the direct-step method between $0.3948\,\mathrm{m}$ and $0.5220\,\mathrm{m}$ in equal depth increments. The three distances, about $6.2\,\mathrm{m}$, $5.9\,\mathrm{m}$ and $5.6\,\mathrm{m}$, decrease because the friction slope decreases rapidly as depth increases.

The head loss is small, $0.057\,\mathrm{m}$, because the conjugate depths are close together. The Froude number of the downstream flow is only $0.62$ and that of the supercritical flow immediately upstream of the jump is $1.69$.

The momentum function confirms that $0.522\,\mathrm{m}$ and $1.016\,\mathrm{m}$ are conjugate depths.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the channel from Exercise 2 ($b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$, $k_s = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$, $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$), the upstream slope is $i_1 = 0.025$ and the downstream slope is $i_2 = 0.0008$, as shown. Determine on which side of the slope change the jump occurs and which profile accompanies it.

::schema{solidi="0,2.00 5,1.40 12,1.20 12,0.80 5,1.00 0,1.60" frecce="1.0,2.60,2.4,2.44,Q" tratteggi="5,1.40 5,2.70" etichette="2.2,1.00,i_1 = 0.025;9.0,0.45,i_2 = 0.0008;5.4,2.95,slope change" riferimento=0 titolo="Transition from a steep slope to a very gently sloping downstream bed"}

Use the same assumptions as in the preceding exercise.

$$
h_c = 0.742\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
i_c = 0.00612
$$

| Reach | $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream, $i_1 = 0.025$ | 0.4609 | 1.3827 | 0.3526 | 6.001 |
| Downstream, $i_2 = 0.0008$ | 1.5430 | 4.6290 | 0.7606 | 6.000 |

$$
h_{01} = 0.461\,\mathrm{m} < h_c ,
\qquad
h_{02} = 1.543\,\mathrm{m} > h_c
$$

Conjugate depth of the upstream flow:
$$
v_{01} = \frac{6.00}{3.00 \cdot 0.461} = 4.338\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_{01} = \frac{4.338}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.461}} = 2.041
$$
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.461}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 2.041^2} - 1\right) = 0.2305 \cdot \left(\sqrt{34.33} - 1\right) = 0.2305 \cdot 4.859 = 1.120\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Comparison:
$$
h_2 = 1.120\,\mathrm{m} < h_{02} = 1.543\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The tailwater depth exceeds the depth required by the jump. The jump is forced **upstream** onto the steep slope. Upstream of the jump the flow is uniform at $h_{01} = 0.461\,\mathrm{m}$. Downstream the depth exceeds $h_c$, placing it in **zone 1** on the steep slope. The **S1** profile increases in depth downstream and reaches $h_{02} = 1.543\,\mathrm{m}$ at the slope break.

The depth at the downstream end of the jump is the conjugate depth of $h_{01}$, namely $1.120\,\mathrm{m}$. The S1 profile connects it to $1.543\,\mathrm{m}$.

Check. The upstream conjugate depth of $h_{02} = 1.543\,\mathrm{m}$ is
$$
Fr_{02} = \frac{6.00}{3.00 \cdot 1.543 \cdot \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.543}} = 0.333 ,
\qquad
h_1^* = \frac{1.543}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 0.333^2} - 1\right) = 0.289\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which is smaller than $h_{01} = 0.461\,\mathrm{m}$. No section on the mild slope has depth $0.289\,\mathrm{m}$ because the depth cannot fall below $h_{01}$. The jump must therefore lie upstream of the slope break, confirming the depth comparison.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two slopes differ from those in Exercise 2. The upstream slope is smaller, giving a larger $h_{01}$ and lower velocity in the supercritical flow. The downstream slope is considerably smaller, giving a larger $h_{02}$ and deeper subcritical flow. The two changes act in the same direction and reverse the depth comparison.

The conjugate depth of $h_{01}$ is $1.120\,\mathrm{m}$, compared with $1.543\,\mathrm{m}$ in the downstream reach. This tailwater depth **exceeds the required depth**, so the jump moves upstream on the steep reach, where supercritical flow is available farther upstream.

Immediately downstream of the jump the depth exceeds $h_c$. On a steep slope this gives zone 1 and an S1 profile, which increases in depth downstream to meet $h_{02}$ at the slope break. Locate the jump by requiring the downstream jump depth, $1.120\,\mathrm{m}$, to be the starting depth of the S1 profile that reaches $1.543\,\mathrm{m}$ at the break.

For an independent check, find the supercritical depth conjugate to $h_{02}$ and verify that this depth cannot occur in the downstream reach. Both approaches place the jump upstream.

</div>
