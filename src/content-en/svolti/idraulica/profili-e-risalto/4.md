## Exercise 1

A rectangular channel of width $b = 3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 50\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ and bed slope $i = 0.020$ carries $Q = 9.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. A sill imposes $h = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at the downstream end, as shown. Classify the bed slope and the profile upstream of the sill, and locate the jump.

::schema{solidi="0,1.60 10,0.20 10,-0.30 0,1.10;9.2,0.33 9.7,0.33 9.7,1.80 9.2,1.80" acqua="0,1.60 9.2,0.32 9.2,2.32 0,2.70" pelo="8.2,9.2,2.32," quote="8.95 0.32,8.95 2.32,2.00 m,-0.85" frecce="1.0,2.00,2.6,1.90,Q" etichette="4.0,-0.45,i = 0.020;10.5,1.05,sill" riferimento=0 titolo="Steep rectangular channel of width $3.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a downstream sill"}

Assume incompressible, steady flow with $\alpha = 1$.

$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{9.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 3.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.9174}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.972\,\mathrm{m}
$$

| $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.650 | 1.950 | 0.4535 | 8.139 |
| 0.700 | 2.100 | 0.4773 | 9.069 |
| 0.696 | 2.088 | 0.4754 | 8.994 |

$$
h_0 = 0.696\,\mathrm{m}
$$

With $h = h_c = 0.972\,\mathrm{m}$, the results are $A = 2.915\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $C = 4.943\,\mathrm{m}$, $R = 0.5897\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v = 3.087\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving
$$
i_c = \left(\frac{3.087}{50 \cdot 0.7032}\right)^2 = 0.00771 < i = 0.020
$$

The channel has a **steep slope**, with $h_0 = 0.696\,\mathrm{m} < h_c = 0.972\,\mathrm{m}$. The depth imposed by the sill, $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$, exceeds both characteristic depths, putting the profile in **zone 1**. This is an **S1** profile, with depth increasing downstream. At the final section,
$$
v = \frac{9.00}{3.00 \cdot 2.00} = 1.500\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{1.500}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 2.00}} = 0.339 < 1 ,
$$
so the flow is subcritical and the profile is traced **from downstream to upstream**.

Uniform flow prevails in the upstream reach, with $h_0 = 0.696\,\mathrm{m}$ and
$$
v_0 = \frac{9.00}{3.00 \cdot 0.696} = 4.310\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr_0 = \frac{4.310}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.696}} = 1.650 > 1 ,
$$
so the flow is supercritical. The jump forms where the depth of the S1 profile reaches the conjugate depth of $h_0$:
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.696}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 1.650^2} - 1\right) = 0.348 \cdot \left(\sqrt{22.78} - 1\right) = 0.348 \cdot 3.773 = 1.31\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Check by substituting $h_0 = 0.696\,\mathrm{m}$ in the Gauckler–Strickler equation:
$$
Q = 50 \cdot 2.088 \cdot 0.4754^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot \sqrt{0.020} = 50 \cdot 2.088 \cdot 0.6091 \cdot 0.1414 = 8.99\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
$$
which agrees with the specified discharge within rounding.

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare the bed slope with the critical slope to classify the channel. Here $i$ is almost three times $i_c$, so the slope is steep and uniform flow is supercritical, with $h_0$ smaller than $h_c$.

The sill imposes a depth greater than both characteristic depths, so the point is in zone 1. The numerator $i - J$ is positive because $h > h_0$ implies $J < i$. The denominator $1 - Fr^2$ is also positive because the flow is subcritical. The depth therefore increases downstream, giving an **S1** profile.

Upstream of the sill, uniform flow approaches in the supercritical regime, while the S1 profile is subcritical. A jump must connect them. It occurs where the S1 depth is conjugate to $h_0$, namely at depth $1.31\,\mathrm{m}$. Immediately upstream of the jump the depth is $h_0$, while downstream it increases along the S1 profile to $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at the sill.

Substituting the calculated normal depth back into the defining equation reproduces the specified discharge.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular channel of width $b = 4.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with $k_s = 55\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ carries $Q = 6.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. The upstream bed slope is $i_1 = 0.0007$ and the downstream bed slope is $i_2 = 0.030$, as shown. Classify the profiles in the two reaches and identify the control section.

::schema{solidi="0,1.30 6,1.15 12,0.25 12,-0.15 6,0.75 0,0.90" frecce="1.0,1.95,2.8,1.90,Q" tratteggi="6,1.15 6,2.40" etichette="2.8,0.30,i_1 = 0.0007;9.4,-0.20,i_2 = 0.030;6.0,2.65,slope change" riferimento=0 titolo="Change of bed slope from $i_1 = 0.0007$ upstream to $i_2 = 0.030$ downstream"}

Assume incompressible, steady flow with $\alpha = 1$.

$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{6.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 4.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}
$$

With $h = h_c$, the results are $A = 2.449\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $R = 0.4687\,\mathrm{m}$ and $v = 2.451\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving
$$
i_c = \left(\frac{2.451}{55 \cdot 0.6034}\right)^2 = 0.00545
$$

Normal depths in the two reaches:

| Reach | $h$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | $A$ ($\mathrm{m^2}$) | $R$ ($\mathrm{m}$) | Calculated $Q$ ($\mathrm{m^3/s}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream, $i_1 = 0.0007$ | 1.200 | 4.800 | 0.7500 | 5.766 |
| Upstream, $i_1 = 0.0007$ | 1.250 | 5.000 | 0.7692 | 6.108 |
| Upstream, $i_1 = 0.0007$ | 1.234 | 4.936 | 0.7631 | 5.998 |
| Downstream, $i_2 = 0.030$ | 0.340 | 1.360 | 0.2906 | 5.684 |
| Downstream, $i_2 = 0.030$ | 0.360 | 1.440 | 0.3051 | 6.217 |
| Downstream, $i_2 = 0.030$ | 0.352 | 1.408 | 0.2993 | 6.002 |

$$
h_{01} = 1.234\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
h_{02} = 0.352\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Since $i_1 = 0.0007 < i_c$, the upstream reach has a **mild slope** and $h_{01} > h_c$. Since $i_2 = 0.030 > i_c$, the downstream reach has a **steep slope** and $h_{02} < h_c$.

The flow passes from subcritical to supercritical, so $h = h_c = 0.612\,\mathrm{m}$ is established at the slope change, the **control section**. The upstream **M2** profile approaches $h_{01} = 1.234\,\mathrm{m}$ as it is traced upstream. The downstream **S2** profile approaches $h_{02} = 0.352\,\mathrm{m}$ as it is traced downstream.

Check. The Froude numbers at the two normal depths are
$$
Fr_{01} = \frac{6.00}{4.00 \cdot 1.234 \cdot \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.234}} = 0.349 < 1 ,
\qquad
Fr_{02} = \frac{6.00}{4.00 \cdot 0.352 \cdot \sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.352}} = 2.29 > 1 ,
$$
as expected for the slope of each reach.

<div class="spiegazione">

Critical depth depends only on $Q$ and $b$. Neither changes, so critical depth is $0.612\,\mathrm{m}$ in both reaches. The critical slope also stays the same because $k_s$ is unchanged. Normal depth, however, decreases as the bed slope increases.

In the upstream reach $h_{01} = 1.234\,\mathrm{m}$ exceeds $h_c$, so uniform flow is subcritical. In the downstream reach $h_{02} = 0.352\,\mathrm{m}$ is below $h_c$, so uniform flow is supercritical. The transition between the two regimes is continuous through critical depth, established at the change of slope.

Upstream of the control section the depth lies between $h_c$ and $h_{01}$, in zone 2 on a mild slope. The M2 profile decreases in depth downstream. Below the control, depth lies between $h_{02}$ and $h_c$, in zone 2 on a steep slope. The S2 profile also decreases in depth downstream, approaching $h_{02}$.

The flow regime determines the calculation direction. Trace the subcritical upstream profile upstream from the control section. Trace the supercritical downstream profile downstream from the same section. The slope change thus supplies the initial condition for both calculations.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular channel of width $b = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with a **horizontal** bed and $k_s = 60\,\mathrm{m^{1/3}/s}$ carries $Q = 4.00\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Classify the two profiles shown: the profile downstream of a sluice gate that releases water at $h = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, and the profile upstream of an outlet into a reservoir that imposes $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Give the direction in which each profile should be traced.

::schema{solidi="0,0 14,0 14,-0.60 0,-0.60;4.85,0.55 5.15,0.55 5.15,2.70 4.85,2.70" acqua="0,0 4.85,0 4.85,2.30 0,2.30;5.15,0 14,0 14,1.20 5.15,0.30" frecce="1.0,1.00,2.6,1.00,Q" etichette="5.0,3.00,gate;6.6,0.72,h = 0.30 m;12.6,1.55,h = 1.20 m;2.0,-0.95,i = 0" riferimento=0 titolo="Horizontal channel of width $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ with depths imposed by the upstream gate and downstream outlet"}

Assume incompressible, steady flow with $\alpha = 1$.

A horizontal channel has no normal depth because equation $J = i$ with $i = 0$ has no solution for a nonzero discharge. Critical depth remains well defined:
$$
h_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{4.00^2}{9.81 \cdot 2.00^2}}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt[3]{0.4078}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.742\,\mathrm{m}
$$

First case, $h = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
v = \frac{4.00}{2.00 \cdot 0.30} = 6.667\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{6.667}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 0.30}} = 3.89 > 1
$$
The depth is below $h_c$, putting the point in **zone 3** and giving an **H3** profile. The numerator in the profile equation is $0 - J < 0$ and the denominator is $1 - Fr^2 < 0$, so $\dfrac{dh}{dx} > 0$ and the free surface rises downstream. The flow is supercritical, so trace the profile **from upstream to downstream**, starting at the gate.

Second case, $h = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
v = \frac{4.00}{2.00 \cdot 1.20} = 1.667\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
Fr = \frac{1.667}{\sqrt{9.81 \cdot 1.20}} = 0.486 < 1
$$
The depth exceeds $h_c$, putting the point in **zone 2** and giving an **H2** profile. The numerator remains negative and the denominator is positive, so $\dfrac{dh}{dx} < 0$ and the free surface falls downstream. The flow is subcritical, so trace the profile **from downstream to upstream**, starting at the outlet.

A hydraulic jump joins the two profiles because the first is supercritical and the second subcritical. The conjugate depth of $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ is
$$
h_2 = \frac{0.30}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \cdot 3.89^2} - 1\right) = 0.150 \cdot \left(\sqrt{122.1} - 1\right) = 1.51\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
which is greater than $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The jump therefore cannot form immediately downstream of the gate. It forms farther downstream once the H3 profile has risen sufficiently.

Check using the specific energies:
$$
E(0.30) = 0.30 + \frac{6.667^2}{19.62} = 2.565\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
E(1.20) = 1.20 + \frac{1.667^2}{19.62} = 1.342\,\mathrm{m} ,
$$
Both exceed the minimum $E_{\min} = \dfrac{3}{2}h_c = 1.112\,\mathrm{m}$, as required for this discharge to pass through the channel.

<div class="spiegazione">

In a horizontal channel the bed slope is zero, reducing the numerator of the profile equation to $-J$, negative at every depth. There is no normal depth. The line $h_0$ can be regarded as lying infinitely high, leaving just two zones separated by the line $h_c$.

In the first case the gate imposes a depth below critical, so the flow is supercritical. The numerator and denominator are negative, so the free surface rises downstream. This is an **H3** profile, resembling an M3 profile on a mild slope.

In the second case the outlet imposes a depth greater than critical, giving subcritical flow. The numerator stays negative and the denominator becomes positive, so the free surface falls downstream. This is an **H2** profile.

A jump joins the two flow regimes. Compare the conjugate depth of $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ with the tailwater depth to determine the direction in which the jump moves. Here the conjugate depth is greater, so the tailwater cannot hold the jump at the gate and it moves downstream.

With an adverse bed slope, $i < 0$, the numerator would be $i - J$, again negative at all depths. The resulting profiles would be **A2** and **A3**, with the same trends as H2 and H3.

</div>
