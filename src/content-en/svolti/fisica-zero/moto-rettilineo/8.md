## Exercise 1

At $t=0$ object $A$ starts at $x=0$ with velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and object $B$ starts at $x=20\,\mathrm{m}$ with velocity $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Both move uniformly along the same straight line until they meet. Determine when and where they meet.

$$
\begin{aligned}x_A&=(2\,\mathrm{m/s})t\\[6pt]x_B&=20\,\mathrm{m}-(3\,\mathrm{m/s})t\\[6pt]x_A&=x_B\\[6pt](5\,\mathrm{m/s})t&=20\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]t&=\frac{20\,\mathrm{m}}{5\,\mathrm{m/s}}=4\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]x&=2\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot4\,\mathrm{s}=8\,\mathrm{m}\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x;20-3*x" punti="4,8,pieno,I" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=22 nomi="t,x" legenda="x_A=2t;x_B=20-3t" titolo="Time in seconds and position in meters. The intersection of the two lines marks the meeting."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both objects are described using the same origin and clock. Object $B$ moves toward smaller coordinates, so its position equation contains the negative term $-3t$. When the objects **meet**, their positions are equal. The equation gives $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$, a positive time that is valid for both objects. The position equation for $A$ gives $x=8\,\mathrm{m}$. The equation for $B$ gives the same value because $20-3\cdot4=8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A walker $A$ sets off from the origin at $t=0$ with a constant velocity of $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A second walker $B$ sets off from the same origin $4\,\mathrm{s}$ later, moving in the same direction at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting the initial acceleration of each walker, determine when and where $B$ catches up with $A$.

$$
\begin{aligned}x_A&=(1.5\,\mathrm{m/s})t\\[6pt]x_B&=(3\,\mathrm{m/s})(t-4\,\mathrm{s}),\quad t\geq4\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt](1.5\,\mathrm{m/s})t&=(3\,\mathrm{m/s})t-12\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt](1.5\,\mathrm{m/s})t&=12\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]t&=8\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]x&=1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot8\,\mathrm{s}=12\,\mathrm{m}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Walker $A$ has been moving for $t$, while $B$ has been moving for $t-4\,\mathrm{s}$. The position equation for $B$ therefore applies only from $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$ onward. Setting the positions equal gives $t=8\,\mathrm{s}$, which satisfies this condition. The walkers meet $12\,\mathrm{m}$ from the origin. The first has walked for eight seconds and the second for four. For $B$ the calculation also gives $3\cdot4=12\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
