## Exercise 1

At time $3\,\mathrm{s}$ an object is at position $5\,\mathrm{m}$. At time $9\,\mathrm{s}$ it is at position $23\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the average velocity over the interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta x&=23\,\mathrm{m}-5\,\mathrm{m}=18\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]\Delta t&=9\,\mathrm{s}-3\,\mathrm{s}=6\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]v_m&=\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]&=\frac{18\,\mathrm{m}}{6\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object is observed for $6\,\mathrm{s}$ because the initial time is subtracted from the final time. Over the same interval, the position increases by $18\,\mathrm{m}$. The **average velocity** is the ratio of these two changes, so it is $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The positive result means that the overall displacement is in the positive direction. The two readings alone do not establish that the velocity was constant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object moves from $x=40\,\mathrm{m}$ at time $10\,\mathrm{s}$ to $x=-5\,\mathrm{m}$ at time $25\,\mathrm{s}$ without changing direction. Calculate its average velocity and average speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta x&=-5\,\mathrm{m}-40\,\mathrm{m}=-45\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]\Delta t&=25\,\mathrm{s}-10\,\mathrm{s}=15\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]v_m&=\frac{-45\,\mathrm{m}}{15\,\mathrm{s}}=-3\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]s&=|\Delta x|=45\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]u_m&=\frac{45\,\mathrm{m}}{15\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The position decreases by $45\,\mathrm{m}$, so the displacement is negative. The time interval is $15\,\mathrm{s}$ and is positive, so the average velocity is $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The object never reverses direction, so the distance it travels equals the magnitude of the displacement, $45\,\mathrm{m}$. The average speed is the ratio of this positive distance to the time taken, so it is $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>
