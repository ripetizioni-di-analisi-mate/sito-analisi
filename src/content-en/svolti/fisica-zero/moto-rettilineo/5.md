## Exercise 1

An object starts at $x_0=8\,\mathrm{m}$ at time $t=0$ and moves forward with a constant velocity of $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take to reach $x=35\,\mathrm{m}$? The motion remains uniform until the object reaches that position.

$$
\begin{aligned}x-x_0&=vt\\[6pt]t&=\frac{x-x_0}{v}\\[6pt]&=\frac{35\,\mathrm{m}-8\,\mathrm{m}}{3\,\mathrm{m/s}}\\[6pt]&=\frac{27\,\mathrm{m}}{3\,\mathrm{m/s}}\\[6pt]&=9\,\mathrm{s}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To reach the specified position, the object must undergo a displacement of $35-8=27\,\mathrm{m}$. Since it advances $3\,\mathrm{m}$ each second, we find the elapsed time by dividing $27$ by $3$. The result is $9\,\mathrm{s}$ and is positive, so the object arrives after it starts. Substituting into the position equation gives $8+3\cdot9=35\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart starts moving at time $t_0=4\,\mathrm{s}$ from position $x_0=20\,\mathrm{m}$ with constant velocity $v=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what time does it reach $x=6\,\mathrm{m}$? The cart continues moving in this way until it arrives.

$$
\begin{aligned}x&=x_0+v(t-t_0)\\[6pt]t-t_0&=\frac{x-x_0}{v}\\[6pt]&=\frac{6\,\mathrm{m}-20\,\mathrm{m}}{-2\,\mathrm{m/s}}\\[6pt]&=7\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]t&=4\,\mathrm{s}+7\,\mathrm{s}=11\,\mathrm{s}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required displacement is $-14\,\mathrm{m}$. Dividing by the negative velocity gives a positive time interval of $7\,\mathrm{s}$. This is the time spent moving since $t_0=4\,\mathrm{s}$. To find the **arrival time**, we add the seven seconds to the initial time. This gives $t=11\,\mathrm{s}$, which lies in the interval $t\geq4\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
