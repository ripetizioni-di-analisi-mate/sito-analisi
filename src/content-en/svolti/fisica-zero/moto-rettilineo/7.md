## Exercise 1

A person walks $30\,\mathrm{m}$ in $10\,\mathrm{s}$, stands still for $5\,\mathrm{s}$, and then walks another $15\,\mathrm{m}$ in the same direction in $5\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the average velocity and average speed over the entire interval, taking the direction of travel as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}s&=30\,\mathrm{m}+15\,\mathrm{m}=45\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]\Delta x&=45\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]\Delta t&=10\,\mathrm{s}+5\,\mathrm{s}+5\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]&=20\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]v_m&=\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}=\frac{45\,\mathrm{m}}{20\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]&=2.25\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]u_m&=\frac{s}{\Delta t}=2.25\,\mathrm{m/s}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The person always walks in the same direction, so both the displacement and the distance traveled are $45\,\mathrm{m}$. The total time includes the **stop** because the ratio applies to the entire interval from start to finish. The total time is therefore $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Dividing $45$ by $20$ gives both the positive average velocity and the average speed, each equal to $2.25\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A person walks $60\,\mathrm{m}$ along a straight line at a constant speed of $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, then returns to the starting point at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ without stopping. Calculate the average speed and the average velocity for the entire trip. Neglect the time spent turning around.

$$
\begin{aligned}t_1&=\frac{60\,\mathrm{m}}{3\,\mathrm{m/s}}=20\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]t_2&=\frac{60\,\mathrm{m}}{2\,\mathrm{m/s}}=30\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]u_m&=\frac{s_1+s_2}{t_1+t_2}\\[6pt]&=\frac{120\,\mathrm{m}}{50\,\mathrm{s}}=2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]v_m&=\frac{0\,\mathrm{m}}{50\,\mathrm{s}}=0\,\mathrm{m/s}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The time for each leg is the distance divided by the constant speed. The outward leg takes $20\,\mathrm{s}$ and the return leg $30\,\mathrm{s}$, so the trip takes $50\,\mathrm{s}$. The distance traveled is $120\,\mathrm{m}$, so the average speed is $2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The final point coincides with the starting point and the displacement is zero, so the **average velocity is zero**. The model neglects the brief turnaround mentioned in the problem.

</div>
