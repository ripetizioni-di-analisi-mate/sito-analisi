## Exercise 1

A cart has an initial velocity of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a constant acceleration of $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ along the same axis. Calculate its velocity after $4\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=v_0+at\\[6pt]
&=5\,\mathrm{m/s}+1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot4\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=5\,\mathrm{m/s}+6\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
&=11\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is constant, so the velocity increases by $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second. In four seconds, the increase is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The initial velocity is already $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the final velocity is $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The product of acceleration and time has units of $\mathrm{m/s}$, just like the velocity to which it is added.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle starts with velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and has a constant acceleration of $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Determine how long it takes for its velocity to reach $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v-v_0&=at\\[6pt]
t&=\frac{v-v_0}{a}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{14\,\mathrm{m/s}-2\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}\\[6pt]
&=4\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
v(4\,\mathrm{s})=2\,\mathrm{m/s}+3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot4\,\mathrm{s}=14\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **velocity equation**, subtract $v_0$ from both sides and then divide by $a$, which is nonzero. The required increase is $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the increase each second is $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so four seconds are needed. The time is positive and corresponds to motion after the start. Substituting it into the original equation gives the required velocity.

</div>
