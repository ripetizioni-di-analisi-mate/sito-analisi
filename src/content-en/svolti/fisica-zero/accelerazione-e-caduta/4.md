## Exercise 1

A cart's velocity increases linearly from $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $5\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate its displacement using the area under the velocity–time graph.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=\frac{v_0+v_f}{2}\,t\\[6pt]
&=\frac{4\,\mathrm{m/s}+12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}\cdot5\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=8\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot5\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=40\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4+1.6*x+0*sqrt(x*(5-x))" area="4+1.6*x;0;0;5" punti="0,4,pieno;5,12,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=14 nomi="t,v" legenda="v=4+1.6t" titolo="With time in seconds and velocity in $\mathrm{m/s}$, the area of the trapezoid represents a displacement of $40\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity changes linearly, so its graph is a straight line segment. The region between the segment and the time axis is a trapezoid with heights $4$ and $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and base $5\,\mathrm{s}$. Its area is the average of the heights multiplied by the base. The entire region lies above the axis, so the displacement is positive and equals the distance traveled.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $0\leq t\leq6\,\mathrm{s}$, a particle has velocity $v=6-2t$, with $v$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and $t$ in seconds. Calculate the displacement and the total distance traveled over the interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=6-2t_{\mathrm{inversione}},\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{inversione}}&=3\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
\Delta x_1&=\frac{3\,\mathrm{s}\cdot6\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}=9\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta x_2&=-\frac{3\,\mathrm{s}\cdot6\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}=-9\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta x&=9\,\mathrm{m}-9\,\mathrm{m}=0,\\[6pt]
s&=9\,\mathrm{m}+9\,\mathrm{m}=18\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="6-2*x+0*sqrt(x*(6-x))" area="6-2*x;0;0;3|0;6-2*x;3;6" punti="0,6,pieno;3,0,pieno;6,-6,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-8 ymax=8 nomi="t,v" legenda="v=6-2t" titolo="With time in seconds and velocity in $\mathrm{m/s}$, the two areas have magnitude $9\,\mathrm{m}$ and opposite signs"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity reaches zero at three seconds and then becomes negative, so the particle reverses direction. Before the reversal, the triangle lies above the axis and represents a positive displacement. Afterward, the triangle lies below the axis and represents a negative displacement. The areas have equal magnitudes, so the particle returns to its initial position. The **distance traveled** is the sum of the lengths of the two segments and equals $18\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
