## Exercise 1

A cart's velocity along an axis changes from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $4\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate its average acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{v_f-v_i}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{14\,\mathrm{m/s}-2\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
&=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of **average acceleration**, we subtract the initial velocity from the final velocity. The change is $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over four seconds, so the ratio is $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The positive sign indicates that the velocity component has increased. The result describes the entire interval. The data do not establish whether the acceleration was constant during the motion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle moves along an axis with a chosen positive direction. Its velocity changes from $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $6\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the average acceleration and compare the initial and final speeds.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{v_f-v_i}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{-15\,\mathrm{m/s}-(-3\,\mathrm{m/s})}{6\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{-12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{6\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
&=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
|v_i|=3\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad |v_f|=15\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $-3$ is equivalent to adding $3$, so the change in the velocity component is $-12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The average acceleration is negative because this component decreases. Speed is the magnitude of velocity, so the final speed is greater than the initial speed. The object moves in the negative direction at both instants, and a negative average acceleration is consistent with a greater final speed.

</div>
