## Exercise 1

An object is released from rest at a height of $45\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglect air resistance and use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to calculate the time of fall and the object's speed just before it reaches the ground.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=\frac{1}{2}gt^2,\\[6pt]
t&=\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot45\,\mathrm{m}}{10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}}=3\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
v&=gt=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot3\,\mathrm{s}=30\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the axis to point downward from the release point. The initial velocity is zero and the acceleration is $+g$, so the distance fallen is $\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$. The square of the time is $9\,\mathrm{s^2}$. The duration is positive, so we take the positive root, which equals three seconds. The velocity points downward and refers to the instant just before contact, when the free-fall model still applies.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object is released from rest and falls freely for $2\,\mathrm{s}$ without reaching the ground. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. Calculate the distance traveled and the speed at the end of this interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=\frac{1}{2}gt^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot(2\,\mathrm{s})^2\\[6pt]
&=19.62\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
v&=gt\\[6pt]
&=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot2\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=19.62\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance fallen depends on the square of the time, while the velocity depends on the first power of the time. Here the results have the same numerical value but different units and meanings. Distance is measured in meters and velocity in meters per second. The problem states that the object is still in flight after two seconds, so both formulas apply over the entire interval.

</div>
