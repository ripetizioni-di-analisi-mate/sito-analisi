## Exercise 1

A vehicle travels at $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and brakes with a constant acceleration of $-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ along an axis oriented in the initial direction of motion. Calculate the time and distance from the start of braking until the vehicle stops.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=\frac{72}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s}=20\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{arresto}}&=-\frac{v_0}{a}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{20\,\mathrm{m/s}}{-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}=5\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{frenata}}&=\frac{v_0}{2}\,t_{\mathrm{arresto}}\\[6pt]
&=10\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot5\,\mathrm{s}=50\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial velocity is expressed in meters per second because the acceleration is given in terms of seconds and meters. The velocity is zero when the vehicle stops, so the equation $v=v_0+at$ gives a time of five seconds. Under uniform braking, the average velocity is half the initial velocity. Multiplying it by the time gives the distance traveled. This result covers only the distance traveled after braking begins.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart traveling at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ comes to rest in $3\,\mathrm{s}$ with constant acceleration. Calculate the acceleration and the distance traveled before stopping, taking the initial direction of motion as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{v_f-v_0}{t}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0-15\,\mathrm{m/s}}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
s&=\frac{v_0+v_f}{2}\,t\\[6pt]
&=\frac{15\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}\cdot3\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=22.5\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final velocity is zero, so the change in velocity is negative. Since the acceleration is constant, it equals the average acceleration, $-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The cart continues to move in the positive direction until it stops, so the distance traveled equals the displacement. Multiplying the average velocity, $7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, by three seconds gives that displacement.

</div>
