## Exercise 1

A homogeneous block of density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and volume $0.005\,\mathrm{m^3}$ floats at rest in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Calculate the immersed volume and the percentage of the volume above water. Neglect the buoyant force of the air.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{V_{\mathrm{imm}}}{V}&=\frac{\rho_c}{\rho_f}=\frac{600}{1000}=0.60,\\[6pt]V_{\mathrm{imm}}&=0.60\cdot0.005\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]&=0.003\,\mathrm{m^3},\\[6pt]\text{percentage above water}&=100\%-60\%=40\%.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block is at rest and subject only to its weight and the buoyant force, so the two forces have equal magnitudes. For a homogeneous body, the ratio of immersed volume to total volume equals the ratio of the densities. The value $0.60$ means that $60\%$ is submerged. The remainder is $40\%$ of the total volume. These are percentages of volume. For a block with a constant cross section, they also equal the percentages of its height.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A small homogeneous object has a mass of $0.24\,\mathrm{kg}$ and a volume of $0.00020\,\mathrm{m^3}$. It is fully submerged in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and is unsupported. Calculate its weight, the buoyant force, and the initial net force using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, neglecting drag at the instant of release. Determine whether it begins to rise or sink.

$$
\begin{aligned}\rho_c&=\frac{m}{V}=\frac{0.24}{0.00020}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\\[6pt]&=1200\,\mathrm{kg/m^3},\\[6pt]P&=mg=2.4\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]F_A&=\rho_f gV=2.0\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]F_{\mathrm{ris}}&=P-F_A=0.4\,\mathrm{N}\quad\text{downward}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object is denser than water. Comparing the forces directly confirms that the weight of $2.4\,\mathrm{N}$ exceeds the buoyant force of $2.0\,\mathrm{N}$. The initial net force therefore points downward, and the body **begins to sink**. Water resistance can then increase as the body moves, so this calculation of the net force applies to the specified instant of release.

</div>
