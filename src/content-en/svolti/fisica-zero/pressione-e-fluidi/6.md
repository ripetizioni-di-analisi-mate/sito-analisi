## Exercise 1

An ideal hydraulic press has pistons with areas $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and $80\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ at the same height. The external atmospheric pressures are equal. Neglect the piston weights and friction. A force of $25\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied to the smaller piston. Calculate the force that can be balanced on the larger piston.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{F_1}{A_1}&=\frac{F_2}{A_2},\\[6pt]F_2&=F_1\frac{A_2}{A_1}\\[6pt]&=25\cdot\frac{80}{4}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=500\,\mathrm{N}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By **Pascal's principle**, the pressure increase is the same under both pistons. The ratio of their areas is $80:4=20$, so the force on the larger piston is twenty times the force applied to the smaller one. The areas can be left in square centimeters in the ratio because they are in the same unit and the units cancel.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal hydraulic press must balance $900\,\mathrm{N}$ on a piston of area $120\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The smaller piston has an area of $6\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Calculate the force that must be applied to the smaller piston. The pistons are at the same height, their weights and friction are negligible, and the external pressure is the same on both.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{F_1}{A_1}&=\frac{F_2}{A_2},\\[6pt]F_1&=F_2\frac{A_1}{A_2}\\[6pt]&=900\cdot\frac{6}{120}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=45\,\mathrm{N}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To isolate the unknown force $F_1$, multiply both sides of the pressure equality by $A_1$. The smaller area is one twentieth of the larger area, so a force of one twentieth of $900\,\mathrm{N}$ suffices. The result, $45\,\mathrm{N}$, is less than the load, as the area ratio implies.

</div>
