## Exercise 1

A uniform pressure of $2500\,\mathrm{Pa}$ acts on a flat surface of area $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Calculate the magnitude of the force exerted on that face.

$$
\begin{aligned}p&=\frac{F}{A},\\[6pt]F&=pA\\[6pt]&=2500\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]&=100\,\mathrm{N}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressure is uniform, so $F=pA$ applies. The product of the units, $\mathrm{Pa\cdot m^2}$, is equivalent to a newton, and the result is the force perpendicular to the face. The problem asks for the force on that face. To find the net force, any pressure on the opposite face would have to be considered separately.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The pressure difference across a piston separating two regions is uniform and equal to $20000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The net force due to pressure is $60\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate the area of the piston in square meters and square centimeters.

$$
\begin{aligned}F&=(p_1-p_2)A,\\[6pt]A&=\frac{F}{p_1-p_2}\\[6pt]&=\frac{60}{20000}\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]&=0.0030\,\mathrm{m^2}=30\,\mathrm{cm^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces on the two faces of the piston act in opposite directions, so the net force depends on the **pressure difference**. Dividing the net force by this difference gives the area. One square meter contains $10000$ square centimeters, so $0.0030\,\mathrm{m^2}$ corresponds to $30\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.

</div>
