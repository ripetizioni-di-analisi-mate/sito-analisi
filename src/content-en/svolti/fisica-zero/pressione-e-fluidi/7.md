## Exercise 1

A body completely submerged in water and clear of the bottom has a volume of $2\,\mathrm{L}$. Calculate the buoyant force using $\rho_f=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}V_{\mathrm{imm}}&=0.002\,\mathrm{m^3},\\[6pt]F_A&=\rho_f gV_{\mathrm{imm}}\\[6pt]&=1000\cdot10\cdot0.002\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=20\,\mathrm{N}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body is completely submerged, so the volume of liquid displaced equals the volume of the body. A volume of two liters equals $0.002\,\mathrm{m^3}$. This volume of water has a mass of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and a weight of $20\,\mathrm{N}$, so the **buoyant force** has a magnitude of $20\,\mathrm{N}$ and acts upward.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ and volume $0.001\,\mathrm{m^3}$ hangs at rest from a spring scale, fully submerged in water and clear of the bottom. Calculate the spring scale reading using $\rho_f=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}P&=mg=3\cdot10\,\mathrm{N}=30\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]F_A&=\rho_f gV=1000\cdot10\cdot0.001\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=10\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]T&=P-F_A=30-10=20\,\mathrm{N}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body is at rest, so the net vertical force is zero. Its weight of $30\,\mathrm{N}$ acts downward, while the buoyant force of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ and the tension from the spring scale act upward. For equilibrium, the tension must equal $20\,\mathrm{N}$. The scale measures this tension, called the **apparent weight**. The body's weight remains $30\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>
