## Exercise 1

The water in a tank is at rest and has a density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The surface pressure is $100000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Calculate the gauge and absolute pressures at a depth of $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$, using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}p_{\mathrm{rel}}&=\rho gh\\[6pt]&=1000\cdot9.81\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{Pa}\\[6pt]&=14715\,\mathrm{Pa},\\[6pt]p&=p_0+p_{\mathrm{rel}}\\[6pt]&=114715\,\mathrm{Pa}\approx115\,\mathrm{kPa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The liquid is at rest and has uniform density, so the **hydrostatic pressure law** applies. The increase in pressure relative to the surface is $\rho gh$, which equals the gauge pressure relative to the atmosphere in this problem. Absolute pressure also includes the $100000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ already present at the surface. The final result is rounded to the nearest kilopascal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An open tank contains oil at rest with a density of $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. At a certain depth, the pressure exceeds atmospheric pressure by $16000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Calculate the depth assuming $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}p_{\mathrm{rel}}&=\rho gh,\\[6pt]h&=\frac{p_{\mathrm{rel}}}{\rho g}\\[6pt]&=\frac{16000}{800\cdot10}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]&=2\,\mathrm{m}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given value is the difference between the pressure in the liquid and atmospheric pressure. Since the surface is open to the atmosphere, this difference is $\rho gh$. Dividing by $\rho g=8000\,\mathrm{Pa/m}$ gives $h=2\,\mathrm{m}$. The required depth is measured vertically from the free surface.

</div>
