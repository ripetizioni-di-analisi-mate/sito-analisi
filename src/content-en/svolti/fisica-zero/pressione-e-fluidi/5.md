## Exercise 1

Two open containers hold still water to the same depth of $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. Their horizontal bottoms have areas of $0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $0.060\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Using a water density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, calculate the gauge pressure on the bottoms and the forces in excess of those due to atmospheric pressure.

$$
\begin{aligned}p_{\mathrm{rel}}&=\rho gh=1000\cdot10\cdot0.40\,\mathrm{Pa}\\[6pt]&=4000\,\mathrm{Pa},\\[6pt]F_1&=p_{\mathrm{rel}}A_1=4000\cdot0.020\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=80\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]F_2&=p_{\mathrm{rel}}A_2=4000\cdot0.060\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=240\,\mathrm{N}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two bottoms are at the same depth and in contact with water of the same density, so the gauge pressure is the same. Each bottom is horizontal, so the pressure is uniform over its surface. Multiplying the gauge pressure by the area gives the additional force. The second bottom has three times the area and experiences three times the additional force.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two connected arms contain still water and are open to the same atmosphere. The free surface in the first arm is $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ above the junction. Neglecting surface tension, find the height of the surface in the other arm and the gauge pressure at the junction. Use $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}p_0+\rho gh_1&=p_0+\rho gh_2,\\[6pt]h_2&=h_1=0.60\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]p_{\mathrm{rel}}&=\rho gh_1\\[6pt]&=1000\cdot10\cdot0.60\,\mathrm{Pa}\\[6pt]&=6000\,\mathrm{Pa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the level of the junction the liquid is continuous and in equilibrium, so the pressure must be the same in both arms. The surface pressures are equal and the density is the same, so the hydrostatic pressure law gives $h_1=h_2$. The surface in the second arm is therefore at the same height. The junction is $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ below both surfaces, and the gauge pressure is $6000\,\mathrm{Pa}$.

</div>
