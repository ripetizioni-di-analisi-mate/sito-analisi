## Exercise 1

As $4\,\mathrm{C}$ passes through a load, the electric field transfers $36\,\mathrm{J}$ to the component. Calculate the constant voltage across its terminals using the sign convention for a load.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{W}{q}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{36\,\mathrm{J}}{4\,\mathrm{C}}\\[6pt]
&=9\,\mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Voltage** gives the energy transferred for each coulomb of charge. Since the voltage is constant and the energy corresponds to the passage of the specified $4\,\mathrm{C}$, divide $36\,\mathrm{J}$ by $4\,\mathrm{C}$.

The ratio is $9\,\mathrm{J/C}$, or $9\,\mathrm{V}$. This means that $9\,\mathrm{J}$ is transferred to the load for each coulomb. The time the charge takes to pass is unnecessary because voltage relates energy to charge without dividing by time.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A load has a constant voltage of $12\,\mathrm{V}$ across its terminals. Calculate the energy it receives as $0.50\,\mathrm{C}$ passes through it in the direction of conventional current.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{W}{q}\\[6pt]
W&=qV\\[6pt]
&=0.50\,\mathrm{C}\cdot12\,\mathrm{V}\\[6pt]
&=6\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying both sides of the definition of voltage by the charge gives the energy. A voltage of $12\,\mathrm{V}$ corresponds to $12\,\mathrm{J}$ per coulomb. Since half a coulomb passes through, the energy received is half of $12\,\mathrm{J}$.

The units confirm the calculation. One volt is one joule per coulomb, so multiplying coulombs by volts gives joules. The component **receives energy**, consistent with the sign convention and the positive value used in the problem.

</div>
