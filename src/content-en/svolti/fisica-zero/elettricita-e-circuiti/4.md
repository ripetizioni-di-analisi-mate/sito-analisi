## Exercise 1

An ohmic resistor of $450\,\Omega$ at constant temperature has $9\,\mathrm{V}$ across its terminals. Calculate the current in amperes and milliamperes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=RI\\[6pt]
I&=\frac{V}{R}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{9\,\mathrm{V}}{450\,\Omega}\\[6pt]
&=0.020\,\mathrm{A}=20\,\mathrm{mA}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The component is ohmic and the temperature is constant, so **Ohm's law** applies with the given resistance. To isolate the current, divide both sides by $R$, which is positive.

The ratio $9:450$ equals $0.020$. To express the current in milliamperes, multiply the value in amperes by $1000$ to obtain $20\,\mathrm{mA}$. Substituting the result gives $450\cdot0.020=9$, equal to the original voltage.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ohmic resistor carries $0.15\,\mathrm{A}$ when $12\,\mathrm{V}$ is applied across its terminals. Determine the resistance and the current it would carry at $6\,\mathrm{V}$ if the temperature stayed the same.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=\frac{V}{I}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{0.15\,\mathrm{A}}=80\,\Omega\\[6pt]
I'&=\frac{V'}{R}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{6\,\mathrm{V}}{80\,\Omega}=0.075\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$
::grafico{f="0.08*x" xmin=0 xmax=200 ymin=0 ymax=16 nomi="I,V" legenda=0 punti="75,6,pieno;150,12,pieno" titolo="Current on the horizontal axis is in milliamperes and voltage is in volts. The resistor has a constant resistance of $80\,\Omega$, so the pairs $(0.075\,\mathrm{A},6\,\mathrm{V})$ and $(0.15\,\mathrm{A},12\,\mathrm{V})$ lie on the same straight line"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first pair of values gives the **resistance** by dividing voltage by current. The ratio is $80\,\Omega$. Since the resistor is ohmic and its temperature stays the same, this resistance also applies at the second voltage.

The voltage changes from $12$ to $6$ volts, so it is halved. Since current is directly proportional to voltage by Ohm's law, the current is also halved, from $0.15$ to $0.075$ amperes. The graph shows both pairs on the same straight line through the origin.

</div>
