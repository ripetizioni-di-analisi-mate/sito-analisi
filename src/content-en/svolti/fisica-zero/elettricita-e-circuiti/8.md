## Exercise 1

A load has a constant voltage of $12\,\mathrm{V}$ across it and carries a constant current of $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$. Calculate the power and the energy absorbed in $5$ minutes, giving the energy in joules and kilowatt-hours.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_e&=VI=12\,\mathrm{V}\cdot0.50\,\mathrm{A}=6\,\mathrm{W}\\[6pt]
\Delta t&=5\cdot60\,\mathrm{s}=300\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
E&=P_e\Delta t=6\,\mathrm{W}\cdot300\,\mathrm{s}=1800\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]
E&=\frac{1800}{3\,600\,000}\,\mathrm{kWh}\\[6pt]
&=0.00050\,\mathrm{kWh}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Electrical power** is the product of voltage and current. The result of $6\,\mathrm{W}$ means that the load absorbs $6\,\mathrm{J}$ every second. To obtain the energy in joules, express the time in seconds and multiply it by the constant power.

One kilowatt-hour corresponds to $3\,600\,000\,\mathrm{J}$, so divide by this value to convert the energy. The small energy value in kilowatt-hours is consistent with a power of only a few watts over five minutes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ohmic resistor of $6\,\Omega$ maintains constant resistance while connected across $12\,\mathrm{V}$ for half an hour. Calculate the current, the power dissipated, and the energy received in joules and kilowatt-hours.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&=\frac{V}{R}=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{6\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
P_e&=VI=12\,\mathrm{V}\cdot2\,\mathrm{A}=24\,\mathrm{W}\\[6pt]
\Delta t&=0.50\,\mathrm{h}=1800\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
E&=P_e\Delta t=24\cdot1800\,\mathrm{J}=43\,200\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]
E&=0.024\,\mathrm{kW}\cdot0.50\,\mathrm{h}\\[6pt]
&=0.012\,\mathrm{kWh}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Ohm's law** gives a current of $2\,\mathrm{A}$. The power is therefore $24\,\mathrm{W}$. The energy received by the resistor is converted to internal energy through Joule heating. Since the resistance is assumed constant, the power stays the same throughout the interval.

Half an hour equals $1800\,\mathrm{s}$, so the energy is $43\,200\,\mathrm{J}$. For the calculation in kilowatt-hours, convert watts to kilowatts and keep the time in hours. As a check, $0.012\cdot3\,600\,000=43\,200\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>
