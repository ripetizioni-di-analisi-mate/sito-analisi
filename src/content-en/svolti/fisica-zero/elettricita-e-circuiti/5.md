## Exercise 1

Two ohmic resistors of $2\,\Omega$ and $4\,\Omega$ are connected in series across an ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source. The wires have negligible resistance, and the resistances remain constant. Calculate the equivalent resistance, the current, and the voltage across each resistor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}}&=R_1+R_2=(2+4)\,\Omega=6\,\Omega\\[6pt]
I&=\frac{V}{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{6\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
V_1&=R_1I=2\,\Omega\cdot2\,\mathrm{A}=4\,\mathrm{V}\\[6pt]
V_2&=R_2I=4\,\Omega\cdot2\,\mathrm{A}=8\,\mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When resistors are connected **in series**, the current is the same in both resistors and their resistances add. Replacing both components with the equivalent $6\,\Omega$ resistor and applying Ohm's law gives the total current, which is also the current in each component.

The voltage across each resistor is its resistance multiplied by the common current of $2\,\mathrm{A}$. The resistor with twice the resistance has twice the voltage across it. Adding the two voltages as a check gives $V_1+V_2=4+8=12\,\mathrm{V}$, which matches the source voltage.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two ohmic resistors of $10\,\Omega$ and $20\,\Omega$ are connected in series across an ideal $6\,\mathrm{V}$ source. Assuming ideal wires and constant resistances, determine the current and the two voltages.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}}&=R_1+R_2=(10+20)\,\Omega=30\,\Omega\\[6pt]
I&=\frac{V}{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\frac{6\,\mathrm{V}}{30\,\Omega}=0.20\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
V_1&=R_1I=10\,\Omega\cdot0.20\,\mathrm{A}=2\,\mathrm{V}\\[6pt]
V_2&=R_2I=20\,\Omega\cdot0.20\,\mathrm{A}=4\,\mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both resistors carry the same current because there are no branches between them. The **equivalent resistance** is $30\,\Omega$, so the current is the source voltage of $6\,\mathrm{V}$ divided by that resistance of $30\,\Omega$.

Applying Ohm's law to each resistor gives $2\,\mathrm{V}$ and $4\,\mathrm{V}$. Their sum is $6\,\mathrm{V}$, so it matches the voltage specified for the entire combination. The current remains $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$ before, between, and after the two resistors.

</div>
