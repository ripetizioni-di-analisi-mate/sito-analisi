## Exercise 1

Two ohmic resistors of $6\,\Omega$ and $3\,\Omega$ are connected in parallel across an ideal $6\,\mathrm{V}$ source. Assuming ideal wires and constant resistances, calculate the equivalent resistance, the branch currents, and the total current.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{6\cdot3}{6+3}\,\Omega=2\,\Omega\\[6pt]
I_1&=\frac{V}{R_1}=\frac{6\,\mathrm{V}}{6\,\Omega}=1\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
I_2&=\frac{V}{R_2}=\frac{6\,\mathrm{V}}{3\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
I&=I_1+I_2=3\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When the resistors are connected **in parallel**, each is connected to the same terminals, so each has $6\,\mathrm{V}$ across it. Applying Ohm's law separately to the two branches shows that the smaller resistance carries the larger current.

The currents add in the common section because charge does not accumulate at the node. The total current is $3\,\mathrm{A}$. Checking with the equivalent resistance gives $6\,\mathrm{V}:2\,\Omega=3\,\mathrm{A}$, the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two ohmic resistors of $12\,\Omega$ and $4\,\Omega$ are connected in parallel across an ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source. Assuming constant resistances and ideal wires, find the equivalent resistance and all the currents.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{12\cdot4}{12+4}\,\Omega=3\,\Omega\\[6pt]
I_1&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{12\,\Omega}=1\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
I_2&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{4\,\Omega}=3\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
I&=I_1+I_2=4\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two resistors have the same voltage across them, so each current is found by dividing $12\,\mathrm{V}$ by the resistance of its branch. The $4\,\Omega$ branch has one-third the resistance of the other branch and, at the same voltage, carries three times the current.

Dividing the product of the resistances by their sum gives an **equivalent resistance** of $3\,\Omega$. It is smaller than either resistance, as expected for a parallel connection. The current supplied by the source is the sum of the two currents, or $4\,\mathrm{A}$.

</div>
