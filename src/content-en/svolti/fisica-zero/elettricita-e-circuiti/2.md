## Exercise 1

A net charge of $18\,\mathrm{C}$ passes through a cross section of a wire in the conventional direction in $6\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the average current. If this current remains constant, calculate the charge that passes in $10\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I&=\frac{q}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{18\,\mathrm{C}}{6\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
q'&=I\Delta t'\\[6pt]
&=3\,\mathrm{A}\cdot10\,\mathrm{s}=30\,\mathrm{C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **average current** is the net charge passing through a cross section divided by the time it takes. The ratio $18:6$ equals $3$, so an average of $3\,\mathrm{C}$ passes through that cross section each second.

For the second interval, the current is given as constant. We can therefore multiply the current by this ten-second interval to obtain $30\,\mathrm{C}$. The product of the units $\mathrm{A}\cdot\mathrm{s}$ is equivalent to the coulomb.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A constant current transfers $0.24\,\mathrm{C}$ in $2$ minutes. Calculate the current in amperes and milliamperes, then find the time it takes to transfer $0.60\,\mathrm{C}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t&=2\cdot60\,\mathrm{s}=120\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
I&=\frac{q}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.24\,\mathrm{C}}{120\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
&=0.002\,\mathrm{A}=2\,\mathrm{mA}\\[6pt]
\Delta t'&=\frac{q'}{I}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.60\,\mathrm{C}}{0.002\,\mathrm{A}}\\[6pt]
&=300\,\mathrm{s}=5\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To obtain amperes, express the time in seconds. Two minutes equals $120\,\mathrm{s}$, so dividing charge by time gives $0.002\,\mathrm{A}$. The prefix milli means one thousandth, so this value is equivalent to $2\,\mathrm{mA}$.

For a constant current, $q'=I\Delta t'$ holds. Dividing by the nonzero current gives the time. The new charge is $2.5$ times the original charge, so the time is also $2.5$ times two minutes, or five minutes.

</div>
