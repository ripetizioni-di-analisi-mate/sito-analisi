## Exercise 1

A resistor $R_1=2\,\Omega$ is in series with the parallel combination of $R_2=6\,\Omega$ and $R_3=3\,\Omega$. An ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source powers the circuit. All the resistors are ohmic with constant resistance, and the wires are ideal. Calculate the equivalent resistance and the three currents.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{23}&=\frac{R_2R_3}{R_2+R_3}=\frac{6\cdot3}{6+3}\,\Omega=2\,\Omega\\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}}&=R_1+R_{23}=4\,\Omega\\[6pt]
I_1&=\frac{V}{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{4\,\Omega}=3\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
V_{23}&=I_1R_{23}=3\,\mathrm{A}\cdot2\,\Omega=6\,\mathrm{V}\\[6pt]
I_2&=\frac{V_{23}}{R_2}=1\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
I_3&=\frac{V_{23}}{R_3}=2\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First replace the **parallel combination** with its equivalent resistance $R_{23}$. This resistance is in series with $R_1$, so the total resistance is their sum. The total current flows through $R_1$ and equals $3\,\mathrm{A}$.

To find the branch currents, we need the voltage across the parallel combination alone. It equals $I_1R_{23}=6\,\mathrm{V}$, so each current is found by dividing this voltage of $6\,\mathrm{V}$ by the branch resistance. Checking the sum of the branch currents gives $I_2+I_3=1+2=3\,\mathrm{A}$, which matches the current in the common section.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One branch contains two resistors in series, $R_1=4\,\Omega$ and $R_2=8\,\Omega$. This branch is in parallel with $R_3=6\,\Omega$, and an ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source powers both branches. Assuming constant ohmic resistances and ideal wires, calculate the equivalent resistance, the currents, and the voltages across $R_1$ and $R_2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{12}&=R_1+R_2=12\,\Omega\\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{R_{12}R_3}{R_{12}+R_3}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{12\cdot6}{12+6}\,\Omega=4\,\Omega\\[6pt]
I_{12}&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{12\,\Omega}=1\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
I_3&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{V}}{6\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
I&=I_{12}+I_3=3\,\mathrm{A}\\[6pt]
V_1&=R_1I_{12}=4\,\mathrm{V}\\[6pt]
V_2&=R_2I_{12}=8\,\mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Resistors $R_1$ and $R_2$ form one branch with no junctions between them, so their resistances add to give $R_{12}=12\,\Omega$. The equivalent branch is **in parallel** with $R_3$, so both branches have the source voltage across them.

The branch with two resistors carries $1\,\mathrm{A}$, the same current in $R_1$ and $R_2$. The $6\,\Omega$ branch carries $2\,\mathrm{A}$. The total current is $3\,\mathrm{A}$. The voltages of $4\,\mathrm{V}$ and $8\,\mathrm{V}$ across the resistors in the series branch add up to the specified $12\,\mathrm{V}$.

</div>
