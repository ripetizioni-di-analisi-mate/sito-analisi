## Exercise 1

Two bodies that exchange no charge with their surroundings initially have charges $q_{A,i}=+3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ and $q_{B,i}=-5\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. After they exchange charge with each other, body $A$ has charge $q_{A,f}=+1\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. Determine the final charge of $B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{\mathrm{tot}}&=q_{A,i}+q_{B,i}\\[6pt]
&=(3-5)\,\mathrm{\mu C}=-2\,\mathrm{\mu C}\\[6pt]
q_{A,f}+q_{B,f}&=q_{\mathrm{tot}}\\[6pt]
q_{B,f}&=q_{\mathrm{tot}}-q_{A,f}\\[6pt]
&=(-2-1)\,\mathrm{\mu C}=-3\,\mathrm{\mu C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two bodies exchange charge only with each other, so **conservation of charge** applies to the system formed by both. Adding the initial charges with their signs gives $3-5=-2$, so the total charge is negative.

The final sum must still equal $-2\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. Subtract the final charge of $A$ to find the final charge of $B$, which is $-3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. Checking the final charges gives $+1-3=-2$, which matches the initial sum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two bodies have initial charges of $+8\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. After a transfer of charge involving only these two bodies, the first has charge $+1\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. Calculate the final charge of the second and the change in charge of each body.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(8-2)\,\mathrm{\mu C}=6\,\mathrm{\mu C}\\[6pt]
q_{2,f}&=q_{\mathrm{tot}}-q_{1,f}\\[6pt]
&=(6-1)\,\mathrm{\mu C}=5\,\mathrm{\mu C}\\[6pt]
\Delta q_1&=(1-8)\,\mathrm{\mu C}=-7\,\mathrm{\mu C}\\[6pt]
\Delta q_2&=[5-(-2)]\,\mathrm{\mu C}=+7\,\mathrm{\mu C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial sum is $6\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ and must be conserved. Since the first body is left with $1\,\mathrm{\mu C}$, the second must have $5\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. The **change in charge** is the final value minus the initial value.

For the second body we subtract a negative charge, so $5-(-2)=7$. The changes are equal and opposite, and their sum is zero, as required for a system that exchanges no charge with its surroundings. The sign of the change tells us whether the body's charge increased or decreased, regardless of the sign of its final charge.

</div>
