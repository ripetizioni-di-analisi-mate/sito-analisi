## Exercise 1

A spring with spring constant $k=150\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is stretched by $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate the magnitude of the spring force. Hooke's law holds for this extension.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta\ell&=0.04\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]F_e&=k\Delta\ell\\[6pt]&=150\,\mathrm{N/m}\cdot0.04\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]&=6\,\mathrm{N}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring constant gives the force per meter of extension, so $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ is converted to $0.04\,\mathrm{m}$. **Hooke's law** gives the product $150\cdot0.04=6$. The spring force has magnitude $6\,\mathrm{N}$ and acts in the direction that reduces the extension.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A spring fixed at its left end has a natural length of $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its right end is moved until the total length is $23\,\mathrm{cm}$. The spring constant is $80\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Calculate the spring force on the end, assuming Hooke's law holds and taking right as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta\ell&=\ell-\ell_0\\[6pt]&=23\,\mathrm{cm}-18\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]&=5\,\mathrm{cm}=0.05\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]F_e&=k\Delta\ell\\[6pt]&=80\,\mathrm{N/m}\cdot0.05\,\mathrm{m}=4\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]F_{e,x}&=-4\,\mathrm{N}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **deformation** is the difference between the final length and the natural length, so it equals $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. This difference, expressed in meters, is the length that appears in Hooke's law. The magnitude of the force is $4\,\mathrm{N}$. The spring is stretched to the right and tends to return to its natural length, so the force on its right end points left and has a negative component.

</div>
