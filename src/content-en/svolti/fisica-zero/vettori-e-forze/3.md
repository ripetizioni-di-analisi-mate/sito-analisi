## Exercise 1

A body moves $5\,\mathrm{m}$ east and then $12\,\mathrm{m}$ north. Calculate the magnitude of the overall displacement.

$$
\begin{aligned}R&=\sqrt{A^2+B^2}\\[6pt]&=\sqrt{(5\,\mathrm{m})^2+(12\,\mathrm{m})^2}\\[6pt]&=\sqrt{25+144}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]&=13\,\mathrm{m}\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,5,0,A;5,0,5,12,B;0,0,5,12,R" uguale=1 xmin=-2 xmax=9 ymin=-2 ymax=15 nomi="est,nord" titolo="Displacements of $5\,\mathrm{m}$ east and $12\,\mathrm{m}$ north. Their sum has magnitude $13\,\mathrm{m}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two displacements form a right angle. The **tip-to-tail rule** gives a right triangle in which the overall displacement is the hypotenuse. By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the square of its magnitude is $25+144=169\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The positive square root of $169$ is $13$, so the magnitude is $13\,\mathrm{m}$. The vector points northeast.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two perpendicular forces act on the same particle. One is $8\,\mathrm{N}$ to the left and the other is $15\,\mathrm{N}$ upward. Calculate the magnitude of the resultant.

$$
\begin{aligned}R&=\sqrt{F_1^2+F_2^2}\\[6pt]&=\sqrt{8^2+15^2}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=\sqrt{289}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&=17\,\mathrm{N}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces are perpendicular, so the Pythagorean theorem gives the magnitude of their sum. The squares of their magnitudes are $64\,\mathrm{N^2}$ and $225\,\mathrm{N^2}$, and their sum is $289\,\mathrm{N^2}$. Since $17\cdot17=289$, the resultant has magnitude $17\,\mathrm{N}$. It points up and to the left.

</div>
