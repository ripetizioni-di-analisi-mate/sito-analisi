## Exercise 1

An object has a mass of $750\,\mathrm{g}$. Calculate the magnitude of its weight using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. Round to three significant figures.

$$
\begin{aligned}m&=0.750\,\mathrm{kg}\\[6pt]P&=mg\\[6pt]&=0.750\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot9.81\,\mathrm{N/kg}\\[6pt]&=7.3575\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]&\approx7.36\,\mathrm{N}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value of $g$ is expressed in newtons per kilogram, so the mass is first converted to kilograms by dividing $750$ by $1000$. The relation between **weight and mass** gives a weight of $7.3575\,\mathrm{N}$. To three significant figures this is $7.36\,\mathrm{N}$. The weight acts vertically downward.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At a location where $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, a body has a weight of $24\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate its mass and the weight it would have at another location where $g=4\,\mathrm{N/kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}P_1&=mg_1\\[6pt]m&=\frac{P_1}{g_1}\\[6pt]&=\frac{24\,\mathrm{N}}{10\,\mathrm{N/kg}}=2.4\,\mathrm{kg}\\[6pt]P_2&=mg_2\\[6pt]&=2.4\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4\,\mathrm{N/kg}=9.6\,\mathrm{N}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relation between weight and mass gives $m$ after division by $g_1$, which is positive. The mass found in this way is a property of the body itself and remains $2.4\,\mathrm{kg}$ at the second location. Multiplying it by $g_2$ gives the new weight of $9.6\,\mathrm{N}$. The weight changes because the local gravitational field changes.

</div>
