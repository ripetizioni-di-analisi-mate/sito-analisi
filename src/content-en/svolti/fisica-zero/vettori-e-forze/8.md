## Exercise 1

A spring with a spring constant of $240\,\mathrm{N/m}$ exerts a spring force of magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate the extension in meters and centimeters. The spring obeys Hooke's law.

$$
\begin{aligned}F_e&=k\Delta\ell\\[6pt]\Delta\ell&=\frac{F_e}{k}\\[6pt]&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{N}}{240\,\mathrm{N/m}}\\[6pt]&=0.05\,\mathrm{m}=5\,\mathrm{cm}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown quantity is the extension. Since $k$ multiplies $\Delta\ell$, dividing both sides of Hooke's law by $k$ gives $\Delta\ell=\dfrac{F_e}{k}$. Dividing $12$ by $240$ gives $0.05$, and the resulting unit is meters. Multiplying by $100$ gives an extension of $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A spring with a natural length of $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ reaches a length of $12.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ when the spring force has magnitude $7.5\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate the spring constant and the extension corresponding to $9\,\mathrm{N}$. Hooke's law holds in both cases.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta\ell_1&=12.5\,\mathrm{cm}-10\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]&=0.025\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]k&=\frac{F_{e,1}}{\Delta\ell_1}\\[6pt]&=\frac{7.5\,\mathrm{N}}{0.025\,\mathrm{m}}=300\,\mathrm{N/m}\\[6pt]\Delta\ell_2&=\frac{F_{e,2}}{k}\\[6pt]&=\frac{9\,\mathrm{N}}{300\,\mathrm{N/m}}\\[6pt]&=0.03\,\mathrm{m}=3\,\mathrm{cm}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first extension is $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, or $0.025\,\mathrm{m}$. Dividing the force by this value gives a **spring constant** of $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$. The constant is the same in the second case because the same spring is operating within the range where the law holds. Dividing $9\,\mathrm{N}$ by $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ gives $0.03\,\mathrm{m}$, or $3\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
