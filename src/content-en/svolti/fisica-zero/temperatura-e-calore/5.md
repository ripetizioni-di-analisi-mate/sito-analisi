## Exercise 1

Mix $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $70\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ with $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The container is insulated and has negligible heat capacity. Taking $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ as constant, calculate the final temperature and the heat absorbed by the cold water.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_e&=\frac{m_1t_1+m_2t_2}{m_1+m_2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.30\cdot70+0.30\cdot10}{0.30+0.30}\\[6pt]
&=40.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
Q_{\mathrm{fredda}}=0.30\cdot4180\cdot(40-10)\,\mathrm{J}=37\,620\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The final temperature is $40\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The container is insulated, so the cold water absorbs all the heat released by the hot water. The two specific heat capacities are equal and cancel in the formula for the **equilibrium temperature**.

The masses are equal, so the final temperature is the mean of the initial temperatures. The cold water warms by $30\,\mathrm{K}$. Multiplying this change by $0.30\cdot4180=1254\,\mathrm{J/K}$ gives $37\,620\,\mathrm{J}$. The temperature of the hot water also changes by $30\,\mathrm{K}$ in magnitude, with the opposite sign, so the net heat transfer is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Mix $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $75\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ with $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in an insulated container of negligible heat capacity. Assume $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ is constant. Determine the final temperature and the heat released by the initially hot water.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_e&=\frac{m_1t_1+m_2t_2}{m_1+m_2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.40\cdot75+0.60\cdot25}{0.40+0.60}\\[6pt]
&=45.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
Q_{\mathrm{calda}}=0.40\cdot4180\cdot(45-75)\,\mathrm{J}=-50\,160\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
The final temperature is $45\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, and the hot water releases $50\,160\,\mathrm{J}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The **energy balance** applies because the system is isolated and both samples of water remain liquid. The masses differ, so each initial temperature is multiplied by its corresponding mass to find the final temperature. The products are $30$ and $15$. Dividing their sum by the total mass of one kilogram gives $45$.

The initially hot water cools by $30\,\mathrm{K}$, so its heat transfer is negative. The cold water warms by $20\,\mathrm{K}$ and absorbs $0.60\cdot4180\cdot20=50\,160\,\mathrm{J}$, the same amount in magnitude.

</div>
