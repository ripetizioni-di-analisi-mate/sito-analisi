## Exercise 1

Calculate the heat absorbed by $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water as its temperature rises from $15\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $35\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Assume $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ is constant and there is no change of state.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T&=(35-15)\,\mathrm{K}=20\,\mathrm{K}\\[6pt]
Q&=mc_a\Delta T\\[6pt]
&=0.20\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot20\,\mathrm{K}\\[6pt]
&=16\,720\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water remains liquid and its specific heat capacity is assumed constant, so the **specific heat equation** applies. The temperature change is $20\,\mathrm{K}$ because the final temperature is $20$ degrees higher than the initial temperature.

The product $0.20\cdot4180$ equals $836$. Multiplying by $20$ gives $16\,720\,\mathrm{J}$. The positive sign indicates that the water **absorbs heat**. Any energy absorbed by the container is excluded from the quantity requested.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An aluminum block of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ cools from $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. With $c=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ constant and no change of state, calculate the heat transferred to or from the block.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T&=(25-80)\,\mathrm{K}=-55\,\mathrm{K}\\[6pt]
Q&=mc\Delta T\\[6pt]
&=0.50\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot(-55\,\mathrm{K})\\[6pt]
&=-24\,750\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The block releases $24\,750\,\mathrm{J}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The temperature change is the final temperature minus the initial temperature, so it is $-55\,\mathrm{K}$. The mass and specific heat capacity are positive, so the product in the **specific heat equation** is negative.

The product of the mass and specific heat capacity is $450\,\mathrm{J/K}$. For a temperature drop of $55\,\mathrm{K}$, the heat transfer for the block is $450\cdot(-55)=-24\,750\,\mathrm{J}$. The sign refers to the block, which **releases energy** to its surroundings.

</div>
