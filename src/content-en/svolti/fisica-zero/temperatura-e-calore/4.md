## Exercise 1

An object absorbs $7200\,\mathrm{J}$ and its temperature rises by $12\,\mathrm{K}$. Assuming no change of state and constant heat capacity, find its heat capacity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=C\Delta T\\[6pt]
C&=\frac{Q}{\Delta T}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{7200\,\mathrm{J}}{12\,\mathrm{K}}\\[6pt]
&=600\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Heat capacity** tells us how much energy is needed to change the temperature of the entire object by one kelvin. Since the specified change is nonzero, divide the heat absorbed by that change.

The ratio $7200:12$ equals $600$. The result means that under the same conditions a temperature rise of $1\,\mathrm{K}$ would require $600\,\mathrm{J}$. The mass is unnecessary because the heat capacity already refers to the whole object.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous object of mass $0.75\,\mathrm{kg}$ has a specific heat capacity of $800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. Calculate its heat capacity and the heat needed to raise its temperature by $18\,\mathrm{K}$ without a change of state.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=mc\\[6pt]
&=0.75\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot800\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\\[6pt]
&=600\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
Q&=C\Delta T\\[6pt]
&=600\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot18\,\mathrm{K}\\[6pt]
&=10\,800\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a homogeneous object, the heat capacity is the product of its mass and specific heat capacity. The product $0.75\cdot800$ equals $600$, so each rise of one kelvin requires $600\,\mathrm{J}$.

The required increase is $18\,\mathrm{K}$, so multiplying $600$ by $18$ gives $10\,800\,\mathrm{J}$. The specific heat capacity refers to one kilogram of the substance, while the calculated heat capacity applies to the entire $0.75\,\mathrm{kg}$ object.

</div>
