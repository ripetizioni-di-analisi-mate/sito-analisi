## Exercise 1

A sample of $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water initially at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ absorbs $16\,720\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate the final temperature, assuming $c_a=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ is constant and there is no change of state.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=mc_a\Delta T\\[6pt]
\Delta T&=\frac{Q}{mc_a}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{16\,720\,\mathrm{J}}{0.40\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}}\\[6pt]
&=10\,\mathrm{K}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The temperature rises by $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, so the final temperature is $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The temperature change is a factor in the specific heat equation. Since the mass and specific heat capacity are positive, dividing by their product gives the **rearranged formula**.

The denominator is $1672\,\mathrm{J/K}$, and the ratio $16\,720:1672$ equals $10$. The water absorbs heat, so the change is positive. Adding the rise of $10$ degrees to the initial $20$ degrees gives the final temperature.

</div>

## Exercise 2

To rise in temperature from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, an aluminum block absorbs $13\,500\,\mathrm{J}$. Determine its mass, assuming $c=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ is constant and there is no change of state.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T&=(50-20)\,\mathrm{K}=30\,\mathrm{K}\\[6pt]
Q&=mc\Delta T\\[6pt]
m&=\frac{Q}{c\Delta T}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{13\,500\,\mathrm{J}}{900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}\cdot30\,\mathrm{K}}\\[6pt]
&=0.50\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The temperature difference is $30\,\mathrm{K}$. To find the mass, divide the heat by the product of the specific heat capacity and the temperature change, which is nonzero in this problem.

The denominator is $27\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, so dividing $13\,500$ by $27\,000$ gives $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$. Substitution into the original relation gives $0.50\cdot900\cdot30=13\,500$, so the calculated mass reproduces the given amount of heat.

</div>
