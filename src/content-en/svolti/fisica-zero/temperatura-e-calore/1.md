## Exercise 1

Express the temperatures $23\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $-12\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ in kelvins.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_K&=t_C+273.15\\[6pt]
T_{K,1}&=23+273.15=296.15\\[6pt]
T_{K,2}&=-12+273.15=261.15.
\end{aligned}
$$
The temperatures are $296.15\,\mathrm{K}$ and $261.15\,\mathrm{K}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Intervals have the same size on the Celsius and Kelvin scales, but the scales have different zero points. To **convert to kelvins**, add $273.15$ to the numerical value in degrees Celsius. For the first temperature, therefore, add $23$ to this offset. For the second, add $-12$, that is, subtract $12$.

The negative Celsius temperature still corresponds to a positive value in kelvins. The two values describe the same state of the object on different scales. The conversion does not heat the object.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object's temperature changes from $310.15\,\mathrm{K}$ to $295.15\,\mathrm{K}$. Express both temperatures in degrees Celsius and calculate the temperature change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_{C,i}&=310.15-273.15=37\\[6pt]
t_{C,f}&=295.15-273.15=22\\[6pt]
\Delta T&=T_f-T_i\\[6pt]
&=(295.15-310.15)\,\mathrm{K}\\[6pt]
&=-15\,\mathrm{K}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The object's temperature changes from $37\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $22\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, with $\Delta T=-15\,\mathrm{K}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

To convert from kelvins to degrees Celsius, subtract $273.15$ from each value. The **temperature change**, however, is found by subtracting the initial temperature directly from the final temperature.

The difference is negative, so the object has cooled. Checking in degrees Celsius gives $22-37=-15$, the same numerical difference as in kelvins, because intervals on the two scales have the same size.

</div>
