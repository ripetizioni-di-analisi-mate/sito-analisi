## Exercise 1

Calculate the heat needed to melt $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice completely when it is already at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and at standard atmospheric pressure. Use $L_f=334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and neglect energy exchange with the surroundings.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=mL_f\\[6pt]
&=0.15\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}\\[6pt]
&=50\,100\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$
When melting is complete, the water is at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The ice is already at its melting point, so all the heat required goes into the **change of state**. Multiplying the mass by the specific latent heat of fusion gives $50\,100\,\mathrm{J}$.

During melting, the temperature stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ under the stated conditions. The heat transfer is positive because the ice absorbs energy. Raising the temperature of the water after it melts would require additional energy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample of ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ absorbs $66\,800\,\mathrm{J}$ solely for melting at standard atmospheric pressure. Given $L_f=334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, determine the mass that melts.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=mL_f\\[6pt]
m&=\frac{Q}{L_f}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{66\,800\,\mathrm{J}}{334\,000\,\mathrm{J/kg}}\\[6pt]
&=0.20\,\mathrm{kg}=200\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the specified heat goes into melting the ice, so the relation $Q=mL_f$ applies. Since the latent heat is positive, dividing by $L_f$ gives the mass that melts.

The ratio $66\,800:334\,000$ equals $0.20$. The units simplify to kilograms because an energy is divided by an energy per kilogram. Multiplying by $1000$ gives the same mass in grams. The given information does not determine how much of the sample, if any, remains solid.

</div>
