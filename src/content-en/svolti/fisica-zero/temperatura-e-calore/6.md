## Exercise 1

An aluminum block of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is brought into contact with an iron block of mass $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The system is isolated and the supports absorb negligible heat. Assume $c_{\mathrm{Al}}=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ and $c_{\mathrm{Fe}}=450\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ are constant. Calculate the final temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{Al}}&=0.20\cdot900\,\mathrm{J/K}=180\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
C_{\mathrm{Fe}}&=0.40\cdot450\,\mathrm{J/K}=180\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
t_e&=\frac{C_{\mathrm{Al}}t_{\mathrm{Al}}+C_{\mathrm{Fe}}t_{\mathrm{Fe}}}{C_{\mathrm{Al}}+C_{\mathrm{Fe}}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{180\cdot80+180\cdot20}{180+180}=50.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final temperature is $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The objects are made of different materials, so their specific heat capacities remain distinct in the **energy balance**. First calculate the heat capacities. The aluminum has half the mass of the iron and twice its specific heat capacity, so the two products are equal.

When the heat capacities are equal, the temperature changes are equal in magnitude. The final temperature is therefore the mean of $80$ and $20$ degrees. Checking the energy balance gives $180\cdot(50-80)=-5400\,\mathrm{J}$ for the aluminum and $180\cdot(50-20)=5400\,\mathrm{J}$ for the iron. Their sum is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An aluminum block of mass $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ exchanges heat with $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ of iron at $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The system is isolated, there are no changes of state, and the supports have negligible heat capacity. Use $c_{\mathrm{Al}}=900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ and $c_{\mathrm{Fe}}=450\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. Calculate the final temperature and the heat absorbed by the iron.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{Al}}&=180\,\mathrm{J/K},\qquad C_{\mathrm{Fe}}=360\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
t_e&=\frac{180\cdot100+360\cdot25}{180+360}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{27\,000}{540}=50\\[6pt]
Q_{\mathrm{Fe}}&=C_{\mathrm{Fe}}\Delta T_{\mathrm{Fe}}\\[6pt]
&=360\,\mathrm{J/K}\cdot25\,\mathrm{K}=9000\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final temperature is $50\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The heat capacity of the iron is twice that of the aluminum. In the formula for the **equilibrium temperature**, the initial temperatures of the iron and aluminum are therefore multiplied by $360$ and $180$, respectively.

The final temperature lies between $25$ and $100$ degrees and is closer to the initial temperature of the iron. The iron warms by $25\,\mathrm{K}$, while the aluminum cools by $50\,\mathrm{K}$. Both heat transfers have a magnitude of $9000\,\mathrm{J}$, so the energy released by one block equals the energy received by the other.

</div>
