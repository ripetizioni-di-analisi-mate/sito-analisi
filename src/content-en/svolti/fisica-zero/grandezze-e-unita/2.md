## Exercise 1

Express $0.72\,\mathrm{kg}$ in grams and $350\,\mathrm{mg}$ in grams.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{kg}&=1000\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
0.72\,\mathrm{kg}&=720\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
1\,\mathrm{mg}&=0.001\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
350\,\mathrm{mg}&=350\cdot0.001\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=0.350\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Converting from kilograms to grams multiplies the numerical value by $1000$, so $0.72\cdot1000=720$. In the second conversion, one thousand milligrams make one gram, so $350\,\mathrm{mg}$ equals $\dfrac{350}{1000}\,\mathrm{g}$. The mass is less than one gram, in agreement with the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A package has an empty mass of $180\,\mathrm{g}$ and holds two objects with a mass of $0.45\,\mathrm{kg}$ each. Calculate the combined mass in kilograms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_c&=\frac{180}{1000}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.180\,\mathrm{kg},\\[6pt]
m_{\mathrm{tot}}&=m_c+2m_o\\[6pt]
&=0.180\,\mathrm{kg}+2\cdot0.45\,\mathrm{kg}\\[6pt]
&=1.080\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbol $m_c$ denotes the mass of the package and $m_o$ the mass of one object. Dividing by $1000$ gives the mass of the package in kilograms. The two objects have a total mass of $2\cdot0.45=0.90\,\mathrm{kg}$. Adding $0.180\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives $1.080\,\mathrm{kg}$.

</div>
