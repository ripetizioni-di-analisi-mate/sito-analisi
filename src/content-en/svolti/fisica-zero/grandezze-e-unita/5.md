## Exercise 1

Express $2.4\,\mathrm{L}$ in milliliters and $0.018\,\mathrm{m^3}$ in liters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{L}&=1000\,\mathrm{mL},\\[6pt]
2.4\,\mathrm{L}&=2400\,\mathrm{mL},\\[6pt]
1\,\mathrm{m^3}&=1000\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]
0.018\,\mathrm{m^3}&=18\,\mathrm{L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A liter contains one thousand milliliters, so $2.4$ liters contain $2400$ milliliters. A cubic meter contains one thousand cubic decimeters, each equal to a liter, so multiplying $0.018$ by $1000$ also gives $18$ liters.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular container has internal dimensions of $40\,\mathrm{cm}$, $25\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $30\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate its capacity in liters when filled to the rim.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=a\,b\,h\\[6pt]
&=40\,\mathrm{cm}\cdot25\,\mathrm{cm}\cdot30\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&=30\,000\,\mathrm{cm^3},\\[6pt]
V&=\frac{30\,000}{1000}\,\mathrm{L}\\[6pt]
&=30\,\mathrm{L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The dimensions are internal, so their product gives the space available for the liquid. The base has an area of $40\cdot25=1000\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Multiplying by the height gives $30\,000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Since $1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}=1\,\mathrm{L}$, the **capacity of the container** is $30\,\mathrm{L}$.

</div>
