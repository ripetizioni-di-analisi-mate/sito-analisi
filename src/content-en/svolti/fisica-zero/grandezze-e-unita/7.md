## Exercise 1

A homogeneous liquid has a density of $0.80\,\mathrm{kg/L}$. Calculate the mass of $2.5\,\mathrm{L}$ of this liquid.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m}{V},\\[6pt]
m&=\rho V\\[6pt]
&=0.80\,\mathrm{kg/L}\cdot2.5\,\mathrm{L}\\[6pt]
&=2.0\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The density gives the mass of one liter. The volume is $2.5$ liters, so the mass is found by multiplying the mass per liter by the number of liters. The formula also follows by multiplying both sides of $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$ by $V$. The liters cancel, leaving kilograms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solid, homogeneous block has a mass of $1.62\,\mathrm{kg}$ and a density of $2700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Calculate its volume in cubic meters and in cubic centimeters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m}{V},\\[6pt]
\rho V&=m,\\[6pt]
V&=\frac{m}{\rho}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1.62\,\mathrm{kg}}{2700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}}\\[6pt]
&=0.00060\,\mathrm{m^3},\\[6pt]
V&=0.00060\cdot1\,000\,000\,\mathrm{cm^3}\\[6pt]
&=600\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Starting from the definition of density, multiply both sides by $V$ and then divide by the density, which is positive. This gives the **formula for volume**, $V=\dfrac{m}{\rho}$. The ratio gives $0.00060\,\mathrm{m^3}$. A cubic meter contains one million cubic centimeters, so the result is equivalent to $600\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.

</div>
