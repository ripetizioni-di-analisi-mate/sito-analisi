## Exercise 1

A homogeneous sample has a mass of $96\,\mathrm{g}$ and a volume of $40\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Calculate its density in grams per cubic centimeter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m}{V}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{96\,\mathrm{g}}{40\,\mathrm{cm^3}}\\[6pt]
&=2.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Density** is mass divided by volume. The sample occupies forty cubic centimeters, so dividing $96$ grams by $40$ gives the mass of one cubic centimeter. The result means that each cubic centimeter of the material has a mass of $2.4$ grams under the stated conditions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A material has a density of $1.15\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Express it in kilograms per cubic meter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{g}&=0.001\,\mathrm{kg},\\[6pt]
1\,\mathrm{cm^3}&=0.000001\,\mathrm{m^3},\\[6pt]
\rho&=\frac{1.15\cdot0.001\,\mathrm{kg}}{0.000001\,\mathrm{m^3}}\\[6pt]
&=1150\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density has a unit of mass in the numerator and a unit of volume in the denominator. Both units are converted. A gram is one thousandth of a kilogram, and a cubic centimeter is one millionth of a cubic meter. The ratio of the two factors is $1000$, so $1.15\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ is equivalent to $1150\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

</div>
