## Exercise 1

Express $3.6\,\mathrm{m}$ in centimeters and $45\,\mathrm{mm}$ in meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{m}&=100\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
3.6\,\mathrm{m}&=3.6\cdot100\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&=360\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
1\,\mathrm{mm}&=0.001\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
45\,\mathrm{mm}&=45\cdot0.001\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=0.045\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each meter contains one hundred centimeters, so multiplying $3.6$ by $100$ gives $360$. A millimeter is one thousandth of a meter, so forty-five millimeters is forty-five thousandths of a meter. The length stays the same in both conversions. The numerical value and the unit change.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A route consists of three stretches measuring $0.85\,\mathrm{km}$, $420\,\mathrm{m}$, and $75\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the total length in meters and in kilometers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.85\,\mathrm{km}&=850\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\ell&=\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3\\[6pt]
&=(850+420+75)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=1345\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\ell&=\frac{1345}{1000}\,\mathrm{km}\\[6pt]
&=1.345\,\mathrm{km}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbols $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, and $\ell_3$ denote the lengths of the three stretches. The first length is given in kilometers, so multiply it by $1000$ to express it in meters. Then add the lengths in two steps, $850+420=1270$ and $1270+75=1345$. To convert back to kilometers, divide by $1000$, which gives **the total length** of $1.345\,\mathrm{km}$.

</div>
