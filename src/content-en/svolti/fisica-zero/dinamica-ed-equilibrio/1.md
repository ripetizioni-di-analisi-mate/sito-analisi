## Exercise 1

A cart of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ is subject to a horizontal force of $8\,\mathrm{N}$ to the right and another of $3\,\mathrm{N}$ to the left. The vertical forces balance. Calculate the net force and the acceleration, taking rightward as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x&=F_1-F_2\\[6pt]
&=8\,\mathrm{N}-3\,\mathrm{N}=5\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
R_x&=ma_x,\\[6pt]
a_x&=\frac{R_x}{m}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5\,\mathrm{N}}{2\,\mathrm{kg}}=2.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal forces point in opposite directions, so the horizontal component of the net force is the difference between their magnitudes. The result is positive and indicates a net force to the right. By **Newton's second law of motion**, dividing this force by the mass gives the acceleration. The vertical acceleration is zero because the vertical forces balance. The net force determines the direction of the acceleration regardless of the cart's initial velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body subject to a net force of $6\,\mathrm{N}$ has an acceleration of magnitude $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate its mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=ma,\\[6pt]
m&=\frac{R}{a}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{6\,\mathrm{N}}{1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}\\[6pt]
&=4\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given force is already the net force, so we can apply **Newton's second law** directly. To isolate the mass, divide both sides by the acceleration, which is nonzero. Since one newton equals $1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s^2}$, the ratio is in kilograms. Substituting the calculated mass gives $4\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=6\,\mathrm{N}$, equal to the given net force.

</div>
