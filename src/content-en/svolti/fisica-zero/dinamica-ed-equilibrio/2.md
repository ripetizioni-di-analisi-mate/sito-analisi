## Exercise 1

Two carts of masses $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ are on a horizontal frictionless track. As they push against each other, the first exerts a force of $10\,\mathrm{N}$ to the right on the second. Calculate the force of the second cart on the first and the accelerations of both carts at the same instant. The vertical forces balance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{2\to1,x}&=-F_{1\to2,x}=-10\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
a_{1,x}&=\frac{F_{2\to1,x}}{m_1}
=\frac{-10\,\mathrm{N}}{2\,\mathrm{kg}}=-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
a_{2,x}&=\frac{F_{1\to2,x}}{m_2}
=\frac{10\,\mathrm{N}}{5\,\mathrm{kg}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the same axis for both carts, taking rightward as positive. By **Newton's third law**, the force on the first cart is equal and opposite to the force on the second. The two forces act on different bodies, so each appears in the equation of motion for the body it acts on. The less massive cart has the larger acceleration magnitude because the same force magnitude is divided by a smaller mass.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two skaters of masses $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ push against each other on a horizontal surface. At a particular instant a horizontal force of $120\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on the first skater to the left. Neglecting horizontal friction, calculate the force on the second skater and both accelerations at that instant. The vertical forces balance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{2\to1,x}&=-120\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
F_{1\to2,x}&=-F_{2\to1,x}=120\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
a_{1,x}&=\frac{-120\,\mathrm{N}}{40\,\mathrm{kg}}=-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
a_{2,x}&=\frac{120\,\mathrm{N}}{60\,\mathrm{kg}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Rightward is positive, so the force component on the first skater is negative. By **Newton's third law**, the force on the second skater has the same magnitude and points in the opposite direction. In the absence of other horizontal forces, each force is the net horizontal force on the skater it acts on. The accelerations apply at the given instant. The problem does not specify that the push remains constant throughout contact.

</div>
