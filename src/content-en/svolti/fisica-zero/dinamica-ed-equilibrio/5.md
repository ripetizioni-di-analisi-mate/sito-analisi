## Exercise 1

A mass of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs at rest from a single ideal vertical string. Calculate the tension using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T-mg&=0,\\[6pt]
T&=mg\\[6pt]
&=2\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
&=19.62\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The only forces on the mass are the upward tension and the downward weight. Take the positive axis to point upward. The mass is at rest, so its acceleration is zero and the components of the two forces cancel. The ideal string pulls vertically on the mass with a force of magnitude $19.62\,\mathrm{N}$. This result depends on equilibrium. With vertical acceleration, the tension would generally have a different value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from an ideal vertical string. The string remains taut as the mass accelerates downward at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate the tension. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T-mg&=ma,\qquad a=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
T&=m(g+a)\\[6pt]
&=2\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot(10-2)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
&=16\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Upward is positive, so the given acceleration has a negative component. By **Newton's second law**, the net force must point downward, so the tension is smaller than the weight. The weight is $20\,\mathrm{N}$ and the net force component is $16-20=-4\,\mathrm{N}$. Dividing that component by $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The tension remains positive, so the string can stay taut as stated in the problem.

</div>
