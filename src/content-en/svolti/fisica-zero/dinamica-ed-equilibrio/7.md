## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ is sliding to the right on a horizontal surface. A horizontal force of $18\,\mathrm{N}$ pulls it to the right and the coefficient of kinetic friction is $0.25$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate the acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=mg=40\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
F_d&=\mu_dN=0.25\cdot40\,\mathrm{N}=10\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
F-F_d&=ma,\\[6pt]
a&=\frac{F-F_d}{m}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{18\,\mathrm{N}-10\,\mathrm{N}}{4\,\mathrm{kg}}\\[6pt]
&=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The weight and the normal force balance vertically"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The crate is already sliding, so the **kinetic friction** model applies. There are no additional vertical forces and the vertical acceleration is zero, so $N=mg$. Friction opposes the sliding motion and therefore points to the left. The horizontal net force is the difference between the pull and friction. Its positive sign shows that the crate is speeding up to the right.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A crate of mass $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides at constant velocity on a horizontal surface. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $0.20$. Calculate the magnitude of the horizontal force required to maintain its motion, using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. There are no other horizontal forces.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F-F_d&=0,\\[6pt]
F&=F_d=\mu_dmg\\[6pt]
&=0.20\cdot6\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
&=11.772\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&\approx11.8\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Constant velocity implies zero acceleration, so the pull must exactly balance friction. The weight and the normal force balance vertically because the pull is horizontal. Therefore the frictional force has magnitude $\mu_dmg$. The result is rounded to three significant figures. The pull points in the direction of sliding, while friction points in the opposite direction.

</div>
