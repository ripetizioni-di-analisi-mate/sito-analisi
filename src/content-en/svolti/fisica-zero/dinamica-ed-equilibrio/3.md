## Exercise 1

A crate is pulled across a horizontal surface at a constant velocity of $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to the right. The horizontal resistive force has magnitude $14\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate the horizontal pulling force, assuming that these are the only horizontal forces.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_x&=0,\\[6pt]
R_x&=ma_x=0,\\[6pt]
F-F_r&=0,\\[6pt]
F&=F_r=14\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity remains constant, so the acceleration is zero. By **Newton's second law**, the horizontal net force must therefore be zero. The resistive force acts to the left and the pull to the right, so their magnitudes must be equal. A net force of zero is consistent with the motion of the crate because **Newton's first law** also allows motion in a straight line at constant speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A force of $3\,\mathrm{N}$ to the right and a force of $4\,\mathrm{N}$ upward act on a particle initially at rest. Find the components and the magnitude of a third force that will keep it at rest. The positive $x$ axis points to the right and the positive $y$ axis points upward.

$$
\begin{cases}
3\,\mathrm{N}+F_{3x}=0,\\[4pt]
4\,\mathrm{N}+F_{3y}=0.
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{3x}&=-3\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
F_{3y}&=-4\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
F_3&=\sqrt{F_{3x}^2+F_{3y}^2}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{9+16}\,\mathrm{N}=5\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,3,0,F₁;0,0,0,4,F₂;0,0,-3,-4,F₃" xmin=-4.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-5.2 ymax=5.2 uguale=1 titolo="Force components in newtons. The third force has components $-3\,\mathrm{N}$ and $-4\,\mathrm{N}$ and cancels the sum of the other two forces"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For the particle to remain at rest, the net force must be zero along both axes. The brace therefore groups two conditions that must hold simultaneously. Each component of the third force is the negative of the sum of the components already acting along the same axis. The components are perpendicular, so by the **Pythagorean theorem** the magnitude is the square root of the sum of their squares. The force points to the left and downward.

</div>
