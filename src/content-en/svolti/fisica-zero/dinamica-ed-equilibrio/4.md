## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on a horizontal surface. A hand also exerts a vertical force of $12\,\mathrm{N}$ downward on it. Calculate the normal force using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N-mg-F&=0,\\[6pt]
N&=mg+F\\[6pt]
&=3\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}+12\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=42\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take upward as positive. The normal force has a positive component, while the weight and the push from the hand point downward and have negative components. The crate is at rest, so the three components add to zero. The surface must support both the weight and the additional push, so the normal force exceeds the crate's weight of $30\,\mathrm{N}$. The positive result is consistent with contact between the crate and the surface.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A crate of mass $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on a horizontal surface. A vertical string pulls it upward with tension $15\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate the normal force exerted by the surface and check that contact is possible. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N+T-mg&=0,\\[6pt]
N&=mg-T\\[6pt]
&=4\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}-15\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=25\,\mathrm{N}>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tension and the normal force both point upward, while the weight points downward. **Vertical equilibrium** requires that the tension and the normal force add up to the weight. The tension already supports part of the weight, so the normal force from the surface is smaller. The result is positive, so the surface can exert this force by pushing upward on the crate, and the assumption of contact is consistent.

</div>
