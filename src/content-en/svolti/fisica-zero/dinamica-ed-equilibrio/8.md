## Exercise 1

A cart of mass $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts from rest on a horizontal frictionless track. A constant horizontal force of $12\,\mathrm{N}$ acts for $2\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the acceleration, the final velocity, and the distance traveled. The vertical forces balance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{F}{m}=\frac{12\,\mathrm{N}}{3\,\mathrm{kg}}=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
v&=v_0+at\\[6pt]
&=0+4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot2\,\mathrm{s}=8\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
s&=v_0t+\frac{1}{2}at^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\cdot(2\,\mathrm{s})^2\\[6pt]
&=8\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pull is the only horizontal force, so it is the net horizontal force. By **Newton's second law**, the acceleration is constant because both force and mass are constant. We can therefore apply the equations for motion with constant acceleration introduced in the chapter on acceleration and free fall. The initial velocity is zero, so the terms containing it vanish. Here the velocity and distance have the same numerical value but are measured in different units.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A crate of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ is sliding at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a horizontal surface. Friction is the only horizontal force, with $\mu_d=0.20$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate the acceleration while the crate is sliding and the time and distance needed to stop.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x&=-\mu_dmg,\\[6pt]
a_x&=\frac{R_x}{m}=-\mu_dg\\[6pt]
&=-0.20\cdot10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{arresto}}&=-\frac{v_0}{a_x}=3\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
s&=\frac{v_0}{2}t_{\mathrm{arresto}}\\[6pt]
&=3\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot3\,\mathrm{s}=9\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the initial direction of motion as positive. Friction acts in the opposite direction, so the net force and the acceleration have negative components. The mass cancels when we divide the frictional force by the mass. For constant coefficients, the deceleration is constant, so we find the stopping time by setting the final velocity equal to zero. The equations apply only until the crate stops. It then remains at rest in the absence of other horizontal forces.

</div>
