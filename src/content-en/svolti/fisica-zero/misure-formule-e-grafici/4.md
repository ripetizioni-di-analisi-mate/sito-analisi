## Exercise 1

A length is measured as $\ell=(80\pm2)\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the relative uncertainty and the percentage uncertainty.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_r&=\frac{u}{\ell}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{2\,\mathrm{cm}}{80\,\mathrm{cm}}\\[6pt]
&=0.025,\\[6pt]
u_{\%}&=2.5\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Relative uncertainty compares the absolute uncertainty with the measured value. The ratio is $\dfrac{2}{80}=0.025$, and the centimeter units cancel. To express it as a percentage, multiply the number by $100$ to obtain $2.5\%$. Here, the symbol $u_{\%}$ denotes the same uncertainty expressed as a percentage.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The uncertainties in two measurements, $m_1=(50\pm1)\,\mathrm{g}$ and $m_2=(200\pm3)\,\mathrm{g}$, were estimated using comparable rules. Determine which measurement has the smaller relative uncertainty.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_{r,1}&=\frac{1}{50}=0.02=2\%,\\[6pt]
u_{r,2}&=\frac{3}{200}=0.015=1.5\%,\\[6pt]
1.5\%&<2\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first measurement has the smaller absolute uncertainty, but it is also a measurement of a smaller mass. Dividing each uncertainty by its own measured value gives $2\%$ and $1.5\%$. The **second measurement has the smaller relative uncertainty** because three grams out of two hundred is a smaller fraction than one gram out of fifty.

</div>
