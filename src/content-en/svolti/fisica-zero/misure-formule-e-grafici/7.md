## Exercise 1

A homogeneous sample with a volume of $3\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ has a mass of $12\,\mathrm{g}$. For the same material under the same conditions, calculate the mass corresponding to $8\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m_1}{V_1}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{g}}{3\,\mathrm{cm^3}}\\[6pt]
&=4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3},\\[6pt]
m_2&=\rho V_2\\[6pt]
&=4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}\cdot8\,\mathrm{cm^3}\\[6pt]
&=32\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The material and the conditions are the same, so the density is constant. Mass and volume are **directly proportional**. The first sample gives the constant of proportionality, $4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Multiplying it by the new volume gives $32\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two rectangles have the same area. The first has a base of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a height of $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. The second has a base of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate its height.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=b_1h_1\\[6pt]
&=4\,\mathrm{cm}\cdot9\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&=36\,\mathrm{cm^2},\\[6pt]
h_2&=\frac{A}{b_2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{36\,\mathrm{cm^2}}{6\,\mathrm{cm}}\\[6pt]
&=6\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area is fixed, so the product of the base and the height is constant. The two dimensions are **inversely proportional**. First calculate the product $4\cdot9=36$, then divide by the new base, $6$. The height decreases from $9$ to $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ as the base increases, keeping the area at $36\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.

</div>
