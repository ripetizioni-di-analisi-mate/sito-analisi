## Exercise 1

For a homogeneous material, a table lists the volumes $1$, $2$, and $3\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and the corresponding masses $3$, $6$, and $9\,\mathrm{g}$. Check that the data show direct proportionality and, assuming the relationship holds between data points, calculate the mass at $V=2.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{1}&=\frac{6}{2}=\frac{9}{3}=3,\\[6pt]
\rho&=3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3},\\[6pt]
m&=\rho V\\[6pt]
&=3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}\cdot2.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}\\[6pt]
&=7.5\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio of mass to volume is the same for every pair in the table, so the data are consistent with direct proportionality. The problem assumes that the relationship also holds between the measured points, so the mass at $2.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ can be calculated. The graph shows the mass corresponding to each volume.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An empty container has a mass of $5\,\mathrm{g}$. A liquid with density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ is added to it. Calculate the total mass for $V=0$, $1$, and $3\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, and determine whether total mass and volume are directly proportional.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=m_0+\rho V,\\[6pt]
m(0)&=5\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m(1)&=5\,\mathrm{g}+2\,\mathrm{g}=7\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m(3)&=5\,\mathrm{g}+6\,\mathrm{g}=11\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
\frac{m(1)}{1\,\mathrm{cm^3}}&=7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3},\\[6pt]
\frac{m(3)}{3\,\mathrm{cm^3}}&=\frac{11}{3}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial mass $m_0$ is the mass of the container. The mass of the liquid, which equals $\rho V$, is added to the initial mass. Here, $m(1)$ denotes the mass corresponding to a volume of one cubic centimeter. The ratios of total mass to volume differ, so **there is no direct proportionality**. The graph is a straight line with an initial value of $5\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>
