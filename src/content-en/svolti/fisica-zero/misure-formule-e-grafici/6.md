## Exercise 1

A rectangle has an area of $35\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and a base of $7\,\mathrm{cm}$. Derive a formula for the height and calculate its value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=bh,\\[6pt]
\frac{A}{b}&=\frac{bh}{b}=h,\\[6pt]
h&=\frac{35\,\mathrm{cm^2}}{7\,\mathrm{cm}}\\[6pt]
&=5\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base is positive, so both sides of the formula can be divided by $b$. On the right, the factor $b$ cancels, leaving $h$. Substitution gives $35$ divided by $7$, or $5$. The unit is the centimeter because dividing an area by a length gives a length.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous material has a density of $1.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. A sample has a mass of $120\,\mathrm{g}$. Derive a formula for the volume and calculate its value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m}{V},\\[6pt]
\rho V&=m,\\[6pt]
V&=\frac{m}{\rho}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{120\,\mathrm{g}}{1.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}}\\[6pt]
&=80\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Volume appears in the denominator. First multiply by $V$ to obtain $\rho V=m$, then divide by the positive density. Dividing $120$ by $1.5$ gives $80$. As a check, the product $1.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}\cdot80\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ gives the original mass of $120\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>
