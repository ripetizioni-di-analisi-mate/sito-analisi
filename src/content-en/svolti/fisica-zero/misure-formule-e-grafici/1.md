## Exercise 1

There are ten equal intervals between the $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ marks on a scale. Calculate the value of one scale division.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{\text{difference between the endpoint values}}{\text{number of intervals}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{12-10}{10}\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&=0.2\,\mathrm{cm}=2\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference between the endpoint values is $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. This length is divided into ten equal parts, so each interval represents $\dfrac{2}{10}\,\mathrm{cm}$. To express the result in millimeters, multiply by $10$ to obtain $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Count the **intervals**, the spaces between the marks.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A volume scale has major marks at $40\,\mathrm{mL}$ and $60\,\mathrm{mL}$, with four equal intervals between them. The reading falls on the third mark after $40\,\mathrm{mL}$. Determine the reading.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{60-40}{4}\,\mathrm{mL}\\[6pt]
&=5\,\mathrm{mL},\\[6pt]
V&=40\,\mathrm{mL}+3r\\[6pt]
&=40\,\mathrm{mL}+15\,\mathrm{mL}\\[6pt]
&=55\,\mathrm{mL}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each division represents $5\,\mathrm{mL}$ because the full interval of $20\,\mathrm{mL}$ is divided into four equal parts. The third mark after $40\,\mathrm{mL}$ is three divisions farther along, so add $3\cdot5=15\,\mathrm{mL}$. The reading is $55\,\mathrm{mL}$.

</div>
