## Exercise 1

A ruler has a smallest division of $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. The measured length is $84\,\mathrm{mm}$. Take half the smallest division as the only uncertainty and report the measurement with its associated interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=\frac{2\,\mathrm{mm}}{2}=1\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
\ell&=(84\pm1)\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
\ell_{\min}&=(84-1)\,\mathrm{mm}=83\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
\ell_{\max}&=(84+1)\,\mathrm{mm}=85\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem gives the rule for reading uncertainty, so divide the smallest division by two. This gives $u=1\,\mathrm{mm}$, in the same unit as the length. Subtracting this value from the reading and adding it to the reading gives **the associated interval**, from $83\,\mathrm{mm}$ to $85\,\mathrm{mm}$. The estimate accounts only for the contribution stated in the problem.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass is reported as $m=(320\pm4)\,\mathrm{g}$. Find the endpoints and the width of the associated interval, then express the measurement in kilograms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\min}&=(320-4)\,\mathrm{g}=316\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m_{\max}&=(320+4)\,\mathrm{g}=324\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
\Delta m&=(324-316)\,\mathrm{g}=8\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m&=(0.320\pm0.004)\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbols $m_{\min}$ and $m_{\max}$ denote the endpoints of the associated interval. Its width $\Delta m$ is the difference between the endpoints and is twice the uncertainty. To convert to kilograms, divide both the measured value and the uncertainty by $1000$ because both are expressed in grams.

</div>
