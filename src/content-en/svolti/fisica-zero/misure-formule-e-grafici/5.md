## Exercise 1

Round $7.386\,\mathrm{m}$ to the nearest hundredth of a meter and $12.041\,\mathrm{s}$ to the nearest tenth of a second.

$$
\begin{aligned}
7.386\,\mathrm{m}&\approx7.39\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
12.041\,\mathrm{s}&\approx12.0\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the first number, keep two digits after the decimal point. The next digit is $6$, so the hundredths digit increases from $8$ to $9$. For the second number, keep only the tenths place after the decimal point. The next digit is $4$, so the tenths digit remains $0$. The final zero in $12.0\,\mathrm{s}$ shows the required decimal place.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The calculated mean length is $5.267\,\mathrm{cm}$. The uncertainty is already reported as $0.03\,\mathrm{cm}$. Report the result with consistent decimal places.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{\ell}&=5.267\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&\approx5.27\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\ell&=(5.27\pm0.03)\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The assigned uncertainty is given to the nearest hundredth of a centimeter, so report the mean to the nearest hundredth as well. The third decimal digit of the mean is $7$, so the hundredths digit increases from $6$ to $7$. The measured value and its uncertainty therefore end at the same decimal place.

</div>
