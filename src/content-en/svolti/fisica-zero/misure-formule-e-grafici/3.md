## Exercise 1

Three measurements of the same time interval under the same conditions give $4.6\,\mathrm{s}$, $4.8\,\mathrm{s}$, and $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the mean and the half-range. In this exercise, use the half-range as the uncertainty.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{t}&=\frac{t_1+t_2+t_3}{3}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{4.6+4.8+5.0}{3}\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=4.8\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
s&=\frac{t_{\max}-t_{\min}}{2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5.0-4.6}{2}\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=0.2\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
t&=(4.8\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three durations refer to the same quantity and are expressed in seconds, so the **arithmetic mean** can be calculated. Their sum is $14.4\,\mathrm{s}$, and dividing by three gives $4.8\,\mathrm{s}$. The difference between the maximum and minimum is $0.4\,\mathrm{s}$. Half this difference is $0.2\,\mathrm{s}$. The stated rule uses this last value as the uncertainty.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four measurements of the same mass give $10.2\,\mathrm{g}$, $10.3\,\mathrm{g}$, $10.3\,\mathrm{g}$, and $10.4\,\mathrm{g}$. Find the mean and the half-range. Use the larger of the half-range and $0.2\,\mathrm{g}$ as the uncertainty, as specified in the problem. Report the result.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{m}&=\frac{10.2+10.3+10.3+10.4}{4}\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=10.3\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
s&=\frac{10.4-10.2}{2}\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=0.1\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
u&=0.2\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m&=(10.3\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum of the readings is $41.2\,\mathrm{g}$, so dividing by four gives $10.3\,\mathrm{g}$. The half-range is $0.1\,\mathrm{g}$, while the other assigned uncertainty is $0.2\,\mathrm{g}$. The rule in the problem calls for the larger of the two values, so the uncertainty is $0.2\,\mathrm{g}$. This choice is part of the model specified in the exercise.

</div>
