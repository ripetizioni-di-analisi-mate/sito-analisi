## Exercise 1

An ideal spring with spring constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is compressed by $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ within its elastic limit. Calculate the stored elastic energy, taking it to be zero at the natural length.

$$
\begin{aligned}x&=0.10\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]U_e&=\frac{1}{2}kx^2\\[6pt]&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot200\cdot(0.10)^2\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]&=100\cdot0.01\,\mathrm{J}=1\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring constant is in newtons per meter, so the compression must be expressed in meters. Ten centimeters corresponds to $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. Square the deformation and multiply by half the spring constant. The elastic energy is positive even under compression because it depends on the square of the deformation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$ is initially at rest on a smooth horizontal surface against an ideal spring compressed by $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. The spring constant is $100\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Calculate the speed of the body when the spring returns to its natural length, neglecting the mass of the spring.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{2}kx^2&=\frac{1}{2}mv^2,\\[6pt]v&=x\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}\\[6pt]&=0.20\sqrt{\frac{100}{0.25}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]&=0.20\cdot20\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The surface is smooth, so there is no friction. The weight and the normal force are perpendicular to the motion and do zero work. The gravitational potential energy stays constant, so the initial elastic energy becomes the kinetic energy of the body. At the natural length, $U_e=0$. The ratio $k/m$ equals $400\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$, its square root is $20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, and multiplication by $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>
