## Exercise 1

A cart modeled as a particle has mass $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ and speed $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate its kinetic energy.

$$
\begin{aligned}K&=\frac{1}{2}mv^2\\[6pt]&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot4\cdot3^2\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]&=2\cdot9\,\mathrm{J}=18\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass is in kilograms and the speed is in meters per second, so the **kinetic energy** formula gives the result directly in joules. First calculate $3^2=9$, then multiply by half the mass, which is $2$. The kinetic energy is positive because the cart is moving.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ has kinetic energy $72\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate its speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}K&=\frac{1}{2}mv^2,\\[6pt]v^2&=\frac{2K}{m},\\[6pt]v&=\sqrt{\frac{2K}{m}}\\[6pt]&=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot72}{4}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]&=\sqrt{36}\,\mathrm{m/s}=6\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To isolate $v^2$, multiply both sides by $2$ and divide by the positive mass $m$. This gives $v^2=36\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Speed is nonnegative, so we take the positive root, $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The kinetic energy does not determine the direction of motion.

</div>
