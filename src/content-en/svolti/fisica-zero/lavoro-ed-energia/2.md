## Exercise 1

A crate slides $4\,\mathrm{m}$ across a horizontal floor. A constant force of $30\,\mathrm{N}$ pulls it in the direction of the displacement. Friction has magnitude $8\,\mathrm{N}$ and opposes the motion. The weight and the normal force are vertical. Calculate the work done by each force and the net work.

$$
\begin{aligned}W_F&=Fs=30\cdot4\,\mathrm{J}=120\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]W_a&=-fs=-8\cdot4\,\mathrm{J}=-32\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]W_P&=W_N=0,\\[6pt]W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=120-32=88\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pulling force acts in the direction of the displacement, so it does positive work. Friction acts in the opposite direction and does negative work. The weight and the normal force are perpendicular to the horizontal displacement, so each does zero work. The **algebraic sum** of the four contributions is $88\,\mathrm{J}$. The positive work done by the pulling force is greater in magnitude than the work done by friction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A motor raises a mass of $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ vertically through $2\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant speed. Only gravity and the force exerted by the motor act on the mass. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate the work done by the motor, the work done by gravity, and the net work.

$$
\begin{aligned}F&=mg=3\cdot10\,\mathrm{N}=30\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]W_F&=Fh=30\cdot2\,\mathrm{J}=60\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]W_P&=-mgh=-60\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=60-60=0.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is constant, so the acceleration is zero and the force exerted by the motor balances the weight. The motor pulls upward, in the direction of the displacement, so it does $60\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. Gravity acts downward, so it does $-60\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. The work contributions cancel and the kinetic energy stays constant, while the gravitational potential energy increases.

</div>
