## Exercise 1

A box of mass $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests $2\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor. Set the potential energy to zero at floor level and use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to calculate its gravitational potential energy.

$$
\begin{aligned}U&=mgh\\[6pt]&=3\cdot9.81\cdot2\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]&=58.86\,\mathrm{J}\approx58.9\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The floor is the reference level, so the height of the box is $h=2\,\mathrm{m}$. We apply $U=mgh$, taking the acceleration due to gravity to be constant over this small range of heights. The positive result indicates that the box is above the chosen zero level.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass of $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ descends from height $4\,\mathrm{m}$ to height $1\,\mathrm{m}$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate the change in potential energy and the work done by gravity.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta U&=U_f-U_i=mg(h_f-h_i)\\[6pt]&=5\cdot10\cdot(1-4)\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]&=-150\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]W_P&=-\Delta U=150\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The height decreases by $3\,\mathrm{m}$, so the potential energy decreases. The symbol $\Delta U$ denotes the final value minus the initial value, so it is negative here. The **work done by gravity** is the negative of this change and equals $+150\,\mathrm{J}$. The body moves downward in the direction of gravity, consistent with the positive sign of the work.

</div>
