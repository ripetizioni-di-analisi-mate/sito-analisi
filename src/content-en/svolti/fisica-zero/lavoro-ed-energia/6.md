## Exercise 1

A small ball is dropped from rest at a height of $3.2\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Neglect air resistance and use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to calculate its speed just before it hits the ground.

$$
\begin{aligned}K_i+U_i&=K_f+U_f,\\[6pt]mgh&=\frac{1}{2}mv_f^2,\\[6pt]v_f&=\sqrt{2gh}\\[6pt]&=\sqrt{2\cdot10\cdot3.2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]&=8\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We set the potential energy to zero at ground level. The ball starts from rest, so $K_i=0$. At the final height, $U_f=0$. Only gravity does work during the fall, so **mechanical energy** is conserved. The mass appears on both sides and cancels. The square root of $64$ is $8$, so the final speed is $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the velocity points downward.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A small ball is thrown vertically upward at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate the maximum height it reaches above the release point, neglecting air resistance and using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{2}mv_i^2&=mgh_{\max},\\[6pt]h_{\max}&=\frac{v_i^2}{2g}\\[6pt]&=\frac{6^2}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]&=\frac{36}{20}\,\mathrm{m}=1.8\,\mathrm{m}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the highest point the velocity is momentarily zero, so all the mechanical energy is potential energy. We set the potential energy to zero at the release point. Since only gravity acts, the initial kinetic energy equals the increase in potential energy. Dividing by $mg$ gives the height. This height is measured from the release point, whatever its height above the ground.

</div>
