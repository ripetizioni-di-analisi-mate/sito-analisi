## Exercise 1

A hoist raises a mass of $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ through $3\,\mathrm{m}$ in $6\,\mathrm{s}$. The initial and final speeds are equal and friction is negligible. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate the average useful mechanical power.

$$
\begin{aligned}W_{\mathrm{utile}}&=mgh=10\cdot10\cdot3\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]&=300\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]P_m&=\frac{W_{\mathrm{utile}}}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]&=\frac{300\,\mathrm{J}}{6\,\mathrm{s}}=50\,\mathrm{W}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial and final kinetic energies are equal, so the useful work equals the increase in potential energy. The weight of the mass is $100\,\mathrm{N}$ and it rises by $3\,\mathrm{m}$, so the work is $300\,\mathrm{J}$. Dividing by $6\,\mathrm{s}$ gives the **average useful power**. The speed need not be constant throughout the lift for this calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A motor draws a constant power of $400\,\mathrm{W}$ and has efficiency $75\%$. Calculate its useful power output and the useful energy it delivers in $20\,\mathrm{s}$, assuming that changes in energy stored in the motor are negligible.

$$
\begin{aligned}\eta&=0.75,\\[6pt]P_{\mathrm{utile}}&=\eta P_{\mathrm{ass}}\\[6pt]&=0.75\cdot400\,\mathrm{W}=300\,\mathrm{W},\\[6pt]E_{\mathrm{utile}}&=P_{\mathrm{utile}}\Delta t\\[6pt]&=300\cdot20\,\mathrm{J}=6000\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An efficiency of $75\%$ corresponds to the ratio $0.75$. Multiplying the input power by this ratio gives a useful power output of $300\,\mathrm{W}$. The motor delivers $300\,\mathrm{J}$ of useful energy each second, so in $20\,\mathrm{s}$ it delivers $6000\,\mathrm{J}$. The remaining power, equal to $100\,\mathrm{W}$, is transferred in other forms.

</div>
