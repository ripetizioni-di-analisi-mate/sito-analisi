## Exercise 1

A constant horizontal force of $8\,\mathrm{N}$ pulls a box through $5\,\mathrm{m}$ in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done by this force.

$$
\begin{aligned}W&=Fs\\[6pt]&=8\,\mathrm{N}\cdot5\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]&=40\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force is constant and points in the direction of the displacement, so its work is **positive**. Applying $W=Fs$, we multiply $8\,\mathrm{N}$ by $5\,\mathrm{m}$. The product $\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ is equivalent to one joule. This result is the work done by this force. Finding the net work would also require the work done by the other forces.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A constant friction force of $6\,\mathrm{N}$ opposes a body as it slides through $12\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the work done by friction.

$$
\begin{aligned}W_a&=-fs\\[6pt]&=-6\,\mathrm{N}\cdot12\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]&=-72\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Friction acts opposite to the displacement over the entire distance, so its work is **negative**. The product of the magnitudes is $6\cdot12=72$. Applying the negative sign gives $-72\,\mathrm{J}$. The sign depends on the relative directions of the force and the displacement. The force has a positive magnitude of $6\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>
