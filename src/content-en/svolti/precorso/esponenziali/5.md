## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
4^{x} - 6 \cdot 2^{x} + 8 < 0 .
$$

Setting $t = 2^{x}$, with $t > 0$, since $4^{x} = (2^{x})^{2} = t^{2}$:
$$
t^{2} - 6t + 8 < 0 \iff 2 < t < 4
$$
$$
2^{1} < 2^{x} < 2^{2} \iff 1 < x < 2
$$

Solutions: $1 < x < 2$.

::grafico{f="4^x-6*2^x+8" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=9 asintoti="y=8" area="0;4^x-6*2^x+8;1;2" punti="1,0,vuoto;2,0,vuoto" titolo="$y = 4^{x} - 6 \cdot 2^{x} + 8$ is negative for $1 < x < 2$; the line $y = 8$, in red, is its horizontal asymptote"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The substitution $t = 2^{x}$ transforms the inequality into $t^{2} - 6t + 8 < 0$. The trinomial has roots $t = 2$ and $t = 4$ and is **negative between the roots**, so $2 < t < 4$; the interval is entirely contained in $t > 0$, therefore *no* part of it is discarded. Returning to the variable $x$, the double inequality $2^{1} < 2^{x} < 2^{2}$ has base greater than $1$, so the direction is **preserved** in both comparisons and the solutions are $1 < x < 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
9^{x} - 2 \cdot 3^{x} - 3 \geq 0 .
$$

Setting $t = 3^{x}$, with $t > 0$, since $9^{x} = (3^{x})^{2} = t^{2}$:
$$
t^{2} - 2t - 3 \geq 0 \iff t \leq -1 \ \text{or} \ t \geq 3
$$
The intersection with $t > 0$ gives $t \geq 3$.
$$
3^{x} \geq 3^{1} \iff x \geq 1
$$

Solutions: $x \geq 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

With the substitution $t = 3^{x}$ the inequality becomes $t^{2} - 2t - 3 \geq 0$; the trinomial has roots $t = -1$ and $t = 3$ and is **nonnegative outside the roots**. The condition $t > 0$ excludes the whole half-line $t \leq -1$, because a power with a positive base takes *no* negative values, so only $t \geq 3$ remains. The inequality $3^{x} \geq 3$ has base greater than $1$, therefore the direction is **preserved** and the solutions are $x \geq 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{x} - 5 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} + 4 \leq 0 .
$$

Setting $t = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$, with $t > 0$, since $\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{x} = t^{2}$:
$$
t^{2} - 5t + 4 \leq 0 \iff 1 \leq t \leq 4
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x} \geq 1 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{0} \\[10pt]
\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x} \leq 4 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-2}
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x \leq 0 \\
x \geq -2
\end{cases}
\iff -2 \leq x \leq 0
$$

Solutions: $-2 \leq x \leq 0$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $\dfrac{1}{4} = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}$, the substitution $t = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$ transforms the inequality into $t^{2} - 5t + 4 \leq 0$, which is satisfied between the roots $t = 1$ and $t = 4$; the interval is contained in $t > 0$, so *all* of it is acceptable. The double condition on $t$ must be satisfied simultaneously, therefore it forms a **system** of two exponential inequalities. The base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, so in both of them the direction of the inequality is **reversed** in passing to the exponents: the first gives $x \leq 0$, the second $x \geq -2$, and the intersection gives $-2 \leq x \leq 0$.

</div>
