## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
3^{x+1} + 3^{x} = 36 .
$$

$$
3^{x+1} = 3^{1} \cdot 3^{x} = 3 \cdot 3^{x}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3 \cdot 3^{x} + 3^{x} = 36 &\iff 3^{x} (3 + 1) = 36 \\[6pt]
&\iff 4 \cdot 3^{x} = 36 \\[6pt]
&\iff 3^{x} = 9 = 3^{2} \\[6pt]
&\iff x = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Solution: $x = 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two terms on the left-hand side have the same base $3$ but different exponents, and a sum of powers does not simplify with the laws of exponents. By the **product of powers rule**, however, $3^{x+1} = 3^{1} \cdot 3^{x}$, so both terms contain the factor $3^{x}$.

Factoring out $3^{x}$, the left-hand side becomes $3^{x}(3 + 1) = 4 \cdot 3^{x}$. The number $4$ is nonzero, therefore both sides can be divided by $4$, and we obtain $3^{x} = 9$.

The number $9$ is the power $3^{2}$, so the two sides are powers of the same base. The exponential function with base $3$ is injective, therefore the equality of the powers is equivalent to the **equality of the exponents** and the solution is $x = 2$.

The result is checked by substituting $x = 2$ into the original equation. The left-hand side equals $3^{3} + 3^{2} = 27 + 9 = 36$, which coincides with the right-hand side, so $x = 2$ is a **solution**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
2^{x+1} + 2^{x-1} = 15 .
$$

$$
2^{x+1} = 2^{2} \cdot 2^{x-1} = 4 \cdot 2^{x-1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
4 \cdot 2^{x-1} + 2^{x-1} = 15 &\iff 2^{x-1} (4 + 1) = 15 \\[6pt]
&\iff 5 \cdot 2^{x-1} = 15 \\[6pt]
&\iff 2^{x-1} = 3 \\[6pt]
&\iff x - 1 = \log_2 3 \\[6pt]
&\iff x = 1 + \log_2 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Solution: $x = 1 + \log_2 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The common factor is chosen with the *smallest* exponent, that is, $2^{x-1}$, so that after factoring only numbers remain. Since $x + 1 = (x - 1) + 2$, by the **product of powers rule** we have $2^{x+1} = 2^{2} \cdot 2^{x-1} = 4 \cdot 2^{x-1}$.

Factoring out $2^{x-1}$, the left-hand side becomes $5 \cdot 2^{x-1}$; dividing both sides by $5$ gives $2^{x-1} = 3$.

The number $3$ is not a power of $2$ with integer exponent, so the **exponential equation with a constant right-hand side** applies. The right-hand side is positive, therefore the exponent $x - 1$ equals $\log_2 3$. What remains is the linear equation $x - 1 = \log_2 3$, hence $x = 1 + \log_2 3$.

The solution lies between $2$ and $3$. Indeed $2 = 2^{1} < 3 < 4 = 2^{2}$ and the base $2$ is greater than $1$, so $1 < \log_2 3 < 2$ and, adding $1$, we obtain $2 < 1 + \log_2 3 < 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x-1} + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} < 12 .
$$

$$
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x-1} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-1} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} = 2 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} < 12 &\iff 3 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} < 12 \\[6pt]
&\iff \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} < 4 \\[6pt]
&\iff \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x} < \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-2} \\[6pt]
&\iff x > -2
\end{aligned}
$$

Solutions: $x > -2$.

::grafico{f="0.5^(x-1)+0.5^x;12" xmin=-4 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=20 punti="-2,12,vuoto" legenda="y = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x-1} + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x};y = 12" titolo="The graph of $y = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x - 1} + \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$, decreasing, lies below the line $y = 12$ for $x > -2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $x - 1 = x + (-1)$, by the **product of powers rule** the first term is written $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1} \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$, and $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1} = 2$. Factoring out $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$ the left-hand side becomes $3 \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}$. The number $3$ is positive, so dividing by $3$ preserves the direction of the inequality and we obtain $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x} < 4$.

The number $4$ is written as a power of the base $\dfrac{1}{2}$, since $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-2} = 2^{2} = 4$. The base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, so the exponential function is strictly decreasing and in passing to the exponents the direction **is reversed**. The inequality is therefore equivalent to $x > -2$.

At the boundary value $x = -2$ the left-hand side equals $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-3} + \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-2} = 8 + 4 = 12$, equal to the right-hand side. Because the inequality is strict, the point $x = -2$ is therefore **excluded**, as the graph shows.

</div>
