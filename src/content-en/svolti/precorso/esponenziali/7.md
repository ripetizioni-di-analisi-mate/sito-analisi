## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
5^{x} = 3^{x+1} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{x} = 3^{x+1} &\iff x = (x + 1) \log_5 3 \\[6pt]
&\iff x - x \log_5 3 = \log_5 3 \\[6pt]
&\iff x \left(1 - \log_5 3\right) = \log_5 3 \\[6pt]
&\iff x = \frac{\log_5 3}{1 - \log_5 3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solution: $x = \dfrac{\log_5 3}{1 - \log_5 3} \approx 2.15$.

::grafico{f="5^x;3^(x+1)" xmin=-2 xmax=3 ymin=-4 ymax=45 punti="2.151,31.88,pieno" segmenti="2.151,0,2.151,31.88" legenda="y = 5^x;y = 3^{x+1}" titolo="The graphs of $y = 5^{x}$ and $y = 3^{x + 1}$ meet at a single point, with abscissa $\dfrac{\log_5 3}{1 - \log_5 3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $5$ and $3$ are not powers of the same number with integer exponent, so they cannot be brought to the same base with the laws of exponents. We therefore apply the theorem on **equations with different bases** with $a = 5$ and $b = 3$. Since $3 = 5^{\log_5 3}$, the equation is equivalent to $x = (x + 1) \log_5 3$.

The number $\log_5 3$ is treated as a coefficient. Moving the terms with the unknown to the left-hand side and factoring out $x$ gives $x \left(1 - \log_5 3\right) = \log_5 3$.

To divide, the coefficient $1 - \log_5 3$ must be nonzero. Since $3 < 5 = 5^{1}$ and the base $5$ is greater than $1$, we have $\log_5 3 < 1$, so $1 - \log_5 3 > 0$ and the division is allowed. The solution is therefore $x = \dfrac{\log_5 3}{1 - \log_5 3}$, which with $\log_5 3 \approx 0.683$ is approximately $2.15$.

The same result is obtained by dividing both sides by $3^{x}$, which is positive. We get $\left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right)^{x} = 3$, so $x = \log_{\frac{5}{3}} 3$, another way of writing the same number.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
4^{x} - 3 \cdot 6^{x} + 2 \cdot 9^{x} = 0 .
$$

$$
\frac{4^{x}}{9^{x}} - 3 \cdot \frac{6^{x}}{9^{x}} + 2 = 0
\iff \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2x} - 3 \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{x} + 2 = 0
$$
Setting $t = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{x}$, with $t > 0$:
$$
t^{2} - 3t + 2 = 0 \iff t = 1 \ \text{or} \ t = 2
$$
$$
\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{x} = 1 \iff x = 0, \qquad
\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{x} = 2 \iff x = \log_{\frac{2}{3}} 2
$$

Solutions: $x = 0$ and $x = \log_{\frac{2}{3}} 2 \approx -1.71$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three powers have bases $4 = 2^{2}$, $6 = 2 \cdot 3$ and $9 = 3^{2}$, and each term has total degree $2$ with respect to $2^{x}$ and $3^{x}$; for this reason the equation is called **homogeneous**. The term $9^{x}$ is positive for every $x$, so dividing both sides by $9^{x}$ gives an equivalent equation.

After the division, $\dfrac{4^{x}}{9^{x}} = \left(\dfrac{4}{9}\right)^{x} = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{2x}$ and $\dfrac{6^{x}}{9^{x}} = \left(\dfrac{6}{9}\right)^{x} = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{x}$. Therefore the **substitution** $t = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{x}$, with $t > 0$, turns the equation into $t^{2} - 3t + 2 = 0$, which has solutions $t = 1$ and $t = 2$, both positive and therefore acceptable.

From $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{x} = 1 = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{0}$ it follows that $x = 0$. The number $2$ is not a power of $\dfrac{2}{3}$ with integer exponent, so from $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{x} = 2$ it follows that $x = \log_{\frac{2}{3}} 2$ by definition of logarithm. This solution is **negative**, because the base $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is less than $1$ and the power exceeds $1$ only for negative exponents.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
2^{x+1} \cdot 3^{x} = 5^{x} .
$$

$$
2^{x+1} \cdot 3^{x} = 2 \cdot 2^{x} \cdot 3^{x} = 2 \cdot 6^{x}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot 6^{x} = 5^{x} &\iff \frac{6^{x}}{5^{x}} = \frac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
&\iff \left(\frac{6}{5}\right)^{x} = \frac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
&\iff x = \log_{\frac{6}{5}} \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solution: $x = \log_{\frac{6}{5}} \dfrac{1}{2} \approx -3.80$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the product of powers rule $2^{x+1} = 2 \cdot 2^{x}$. The powers $2^{x}$ and $3^{x}$ have the same exponent, so their product is the power of the product of the bases, $2^{x} \cdot 3^{x} = (2 \cdot 3)^{x} = 6^{x}$. The equation therefore becomes $2 \cdot 6^{x} = 5^{x}$.

The term $5^{x}$ is positive for every $x$, so both sides can be divided by $2 \cdot 5^{x}$ without losing or adding solutions. We obtain $\dfrac{6^{x}}{5^{x}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and the quotient of two powers with the same exponent is the power of the quotient of the bases, $\left(\dfrac{6}{5}\right)^{x}$.

The equation $\left(\dfrac{6}{5}\right)^{x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ has a positive right-hand side, so by definition of logarithm the solution is $x = \log_{\frac{6}{5}} \dfrac{1}{2}$. The solution is **negative**, because the base $\dfrac{6}{5}$ is greater than $1$ and the power is less than $1$ only for negative exponents.

</div>
