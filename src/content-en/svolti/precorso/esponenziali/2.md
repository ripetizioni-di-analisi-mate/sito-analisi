## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
3^{x} = 7 .
$$

$$
3^{x} = 7 \iff x = \log_3 7
$$

Solution: $x = \log_3 7$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $7$ is not a power of $3$ with a recognizable rational exponent, so the equation cannot be reduced to the equality of powers with the same base in elementary form. By definition, the logarithm $\log_3 7$ is the exponent to which $3$ must be raised to obtain $7$, therefore the solution is $x = \log_3 7$. The right-hand side is positive, so the solution exists and is unique by the injectivity of the exponential function.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
2^{x-3} = 5 .
$$

$$
2^{x-3} = 5 \iff x - 3 = \log_2 5 \iff x = 3 + \log_2 5
$$

Solution: $x = 3 + \log_2 5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation has the form $2^{A} = 5$ with $A = x - 3$, so by the definition of logarithm the exponent equals $A = \log_2 5$. What remains is the first-degree equation $x - 3 = \log_2 5$, from which $x = 3 + \log_2 5$; the value $\log_2 5$ is treated as a number, which lies between $2$ and $3$ because $2^{2} = 4 < 5 < 8 = 2^{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
5^{2x+1} = 2 .
$$

$$
5^{2x+1} = 2 \iff 2x + 1 = \log_5 2 \iff x = \frac{\log_5 2 - 1}{2}
$$

Solution: $x = \dfrac{\log_5 2 - 1}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $2$ is not a power of $5$ with a recognizable exponent, so by the definition of logarithm the whole exponent equals $2x + 1 = \log_5 2$. The first-degree equation is then solved: subtracting $1$ and dividing by $2$ gives $x = \dfrac{\log_5 2 - 1}{2}$. The result is negative, since $\log_5 2 < 1$; the check is consistent, because to obtain a value smaller than the base the exponent $2x + 1 = \log_5 2$ is less than $1$.

</div>
