## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
3^{x} = 81 .
$$

$$
3^{x} = 81 \iff 3^{x} = 3^{4} \iff x = 4
$$

Solution: $x = 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $81$ is a power of $3$, since $3^{4} = 81$, so the two sides can be written as powers of the same base. The exponential function with base $3$ is injective, therefore the equality of the powers is equivalent to the equality of the exponents, that is, $x = 4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
2^{x+1} = 8^{x-1} .
$$

$$
8^{x-1} = (2^{3})^{x-1} = 2^{3x-3}
$$
$$
2^{x+1} = 2^{3x-3} \iff x + 1 = 3x - 3 \iff 4 = 2x \iff x = 2
$$

Solution: $x = 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $2$ and $8$ are different, but $8 = 2^{3}$, so the right-hand side is rewritten as a power of $2$ with the power-of-a-power rule, which multiplies the exponents. The two sides are now powers of the same base, therefore the equation is equivalent to the equality of the exponents $x + 1 = 3x - 3$, a first-degree equation with solution $x = 2$. The check confirms the result: for $x = 2$ the left-hand side equals $2^{3} = 8$ and the right-hand side equals $8^{1} = 8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)^{x} = 27^{\,x-1} .
$$

$$
\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)^{x} = (3^{-2})^{x} = 3^{-2x}, \qquad
27^{\,x-1} = (3^{3})^{x-1} = 3^{3x-3}
$$
$$
3^{-2x} = 3^{3x-3} \iff -2x = 3x - 3 \iff 3 = 5x \iff x = \frac{3}{5}
$$

Solution: $x = \dfrac{3}{5}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Both $\dfrac{1}{9}$ and $27$ are powers of $3$, since $\dfrac{1}{9} = 3^{-2}$ and $27 = 3^{3}$, so both sides are rewritten as powers with base $3$ using the power-of-a-power rule. The equality of the powers is then equivalent to the equality of the exponents $-2x = 3x - 3$; moving the terms containing the unknown to the same side gives $5x = 3$, so $x = \dfrac{3}{5}$.

</div>
