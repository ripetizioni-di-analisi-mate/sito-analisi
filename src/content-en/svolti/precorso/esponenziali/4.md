## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
2^{3x-2} > 16 .
$$

$$
2^{3x-2} > 2^{4} \iff 3x - 2 > 4 \iff x > 2
$$

Solutions: $x > 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $16$ is written as $2^{4}$, so the two sides are powers of the same base $2$. The base is greater than $1$, therefore the exponential function is **strictly increasing** and the direction of the inequality is **preserved** in passing to the exponents: the inequality is equivalent to $3x - 2 > 4$, from which $x > 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
3^{x+2} \leq \frac{1}{9} .
$$

$$
3^{x+2} \leq 3^{-2} \iff x + 2 \leq -2 \iff x \leq -4
$$

Solutions: $x \leq -4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $\dfrac{1}{9}$ is a power of $3$ with a negative exponent, since $\dfrac{1}{9} = 3^{-2}$. The base $3$ is greater than $1$, so the direction of the inequality is **preserved** in passing to the exponents and the inequality is equivalent to $x + 2 \leq -2$, that is, $x \leq -4$. A check with one value confirms the result: for $x = -4$ the left-hand side equals $3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{9}$, equal to the right-hand side, and the inequality with $\leq$ is **satisfied**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x-1} < 8 .
$$

$$
8 = 2^{3} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-3}
$$
$$
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x-1} < \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-3} \iff x - 1 > -3 \iff x > -2
$$

Solutions: $x > -2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $8$ is written as a power of the base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ with a negative exponent, since $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-3} = 2^{3} = 8$. The base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ lies between $0$ and $1$, so the exponential function is **strictly decreasing** and the direction of the inequality is **reversed** in passing to the exponents: the inequality is equivalent to $x - 1 > -3$, from which $x > -2$. The check with $x = 0$ confirms the result, because $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1} = 2 < 8$.

</div>
