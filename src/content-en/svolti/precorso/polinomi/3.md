## Exercise 1

Compute, without carrying out the divisions, the remainder of the division of $P(x) = x^4 - 3x^3 + 2x - 1$ by $x - 2$ and by $x + 1$.

$$
P(2) = 2^4 - 3 \cdot 2^3 + 2 \cdot 2 - 1 = 16 - 24 + 4 - 1 = -5
$$

$$
P(-1) = (-1)^4 - 3 \cdot (-1)^3 + 2 \cdot (-1) - 1 = 1 + 3 - 2 - 1 = 1
$$

The remainders are $-5$ and $1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **remainder theorem**, the remainder of the division of $P(x)$ by $x - c$ is the value $P(c)$, so a single evaluation of the polynomial is enough. In the first division the divisor is $x - 2$, therefore $c = 2$ and the remainder is $P(2) = -5$.

The divisor $x + 1$ is written as $x - (-1)$, so $c = -1$ and the remainder is $P(-1) = 1$. Since both remainders are nonzero, by **Ruffini's theorem** *neither* $x - 2$ nor $x + 1$ divides $P$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using Ruffini's rule, find the quotient and the remainder of the division of $P(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 + 4$ by $x - 2$.

$$
2x^3 - 3x^2 + 0x + 4
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 2 & -3 & 0 & 4 \\
2 &  & 4 & 2 & 4 \\ \hline
 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 8
\end{array}
$$

$$
Q(x) = 2x^2 + x + 2, \qquad R = 8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polynomial lacks the term of degree $1$, so in the first row of the scheme the corresponding coefficient is $0$; the number on the left is $c = 2$, because the divisor is $x - 2$.

The first coefficient $2$ comes down unchanged. Then the multiply-and-add step is repeated: $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ and $-3 + 4 = 1$; then $1 \cdot 2 = 2$ and $0 + 2 = 2$; finally $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ and $4 + 4 = 8$. The first three numbers in the row of results are the **coefficients of the quotient**, which has degree $2$, and the last one is the **remainder**.

The **remainder theorem** provides the check: $P(2) = 16 - 12 + 4 = 8$, which equals the remainder in the scheme.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $k$ so that $x + 2$ divides $P(x) = x^3 + kx^2 + x - 6$, and write the division for the value found.

$$
P(-2) = -8 + 4k - 2 - 6 = 4k - 16
$$

$$
4k - 16 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k = 4
$$

For $k = 4$, with Ruffini's rule:

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & 4 & 1 & -6 \\
-2 &  & -2 & -4 & 6 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 2 & -3 & 0
\end{array}
$$

$$
x^3 + 4x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 2)\left( x^2 + 2x - 3 \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By **Ruffini's theorem**, $x + 2$ divides $P$ *if and only if* $P(-2) = 0$. The value $P(-2)$ depends on $k$ and equals $4k - 16$, so the **divisibility condition** becomes the equation $4k - 16 = 0$, whose solution is $k = 4$.

With $k = 4$ Ruffini's scheme with $c = -2$ proceeds as follows: the coefficient $1$ comes down; $1 \cdot (-2) = -2$ and $4 - 2 = 2$; $2 \cdot (-2) = -4$ and $1 - 4 = -3$; $-3 \cdot (-2) = 6$ and $-6 + 6 = 0$. The remainder is $0$, as required, and the quotient is $x^2 + 2x - 3$, therefore the division provides the factorization shown.

</div>
