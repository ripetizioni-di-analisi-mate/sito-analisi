## Exercise 1

Factor $P(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$ completely.

$$
P(1) = 1 - 6 + 11 - 6 = 0
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & -6 & 11 & -6 \\
1 &  & 1 & -5 & 6 \\ \hline
 & 1 & -5 & 6 & 0
\end{array}
$$

$$
P(x) = (x - 1)\left( x^2 - 5x + 6 \right) = (x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3)
$$

::grafico{f="x^3-6*x^2+11*x-6" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 punti="1,0,pieno;2,0,pieno;3,0,pieno" titolo="$y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$ meets the $x$-axis at the three roots $1$, $2$, $3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The leading coefficient is $1$, so any rational roots are integers and divide the constant term $-6$: the candidates are $\pm 1$, $\pm 2$, $\pm 3$, $\pm 6$. The first candidate works, since $P(1) = 0$, therefore by **Ruffini's theorem** $x - 1$ divides $P$.

Ruffini's scheme with $c = 1$ produces the quotient $x^2 - 5x + 6$ with remainder $0$. The quotient is a **special trinomial**, given that $-2 + (-3) = -5$ and $(-2) \cdot (-3) = 6$, so it factors as $(x - 2)(x - 3)$. All the factors have degree $1$, therefore the factorization is **complete**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $P(x) = 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 8x + 3$ completely.

$$
P(1) = 2 + 3 - 8 + 3 = 0
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 2 & 3 & -8 & 3 \\
1 &  & 2 & 5 & -3 \\ \hline
 & 2 & 5 & -3 & 0
\end{array}
$$

For the quotient $P_1(x) = 2x^2 + 5x - 3$ we have $P_1(-3) = 18 - 15 - 3 = 0$:

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
 & 2 & 5 & -3 \\
-3 &  & -6 & 3 \\ \hline
 & 2 & -1 & 0
\end{array}
$$

$$
P(x) = (x - 1)\left( 2x^2 + 5x - 3 \right) = (x - 1)(x + 3)(2x - 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The leading coefficient is $2$, so the **rational candidates** are the ratios of the divisors of $3$ to the divisors of $2$, that is, $\pm 1$, $\pm 3$, $\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\pm\dfrac{3}{2}$. Since $P(1) = 0$, the binomial $x - 1$ divides $P$ and Ruffini's scheme gives the quotient $P_1(x) = 2x^2 + 5x - 3$.

The quotient has leading coefficient $2$, so it is *not* a special trinomial, and the strategy **starts again** with the search for rational roots. The candidates for $P_1$ are the same as for $P$, because the leading coefficient is still $2$ and the constant term $-3$ has the same divisors as $3$. Since $P_1(-3) = 0$, by **Ruffini's theorem** the binomial $x + 3$ divides $P_1$, and the scheme with $c = -3$ gives the quotient $2x - 1$ with remainder $0$. Therefore $P_1(x) = (x + 3)(2x - 1)$, and the check by expansion gives $(x + 3)(2x - 1) = 2x^2 + 5x - 3$.

The three factors have degree $1$, so the factorization is **complete**. The leading coefficient $2$ remains in the factor $2x - 1$, whose root $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is another of the candidates.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $P(x) = x^4 + x^3 - 7x^2 - x + 6$ completely.

$$
P(1) = 1 + 1 - 7 - 1 + 6 = 0
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc|c}
 & 1 & 1 & -7 & -1 & 6 \\
1 &  & 1 & 2 & -5 & -6 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 2 & -5 & -6 & 0
\end{array}
$$

For the quotient $P_1(x) = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6$ we have $P_1(-1) = -1 + 2 + 5 - 6 = 0$:

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & 2 & -5 & -6 \\
-1 &  & -1 & -1 & 6 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 1 & -6 & 0
\end{array}
$$

$$
P(x) = (x - 1)(x + 1)\left( x^2 + x - 6 \right) = (x - 1)(x + 1)(x + 3)(x - 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rational candidates divide the constant term $6$, since the leading coefficient is $1$. The candidate $1$ is a root, so a first Ruffini scheme lowers the degree: $P(x) = (x - 1)\left( x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6 \right)$.

The strategy **starts again** on the quotient $P_1(x) = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6$, whose candidates divide $-6$. Since $P_1(-1) = 0$, a second scheme with $c = -1$ gives $P_1(x) = (x + 1)\left( x^2 + x - 6 \right)$.

The trinomial $x^2 + x - 6$ is **special**, given that $3 + (-2) = 1$ and $3 \cdot (-2) = -6$, so it factors as $(x + 3)(x - 2)$. The four factors have degree $1$, in agreement with the degree $4$ of the original polynomial, therefore the factorization is **complete**.

</div>
