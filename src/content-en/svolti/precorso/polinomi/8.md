## Exercise 1

Simplify the fraction $\dfrac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4}$, stating the domain conditions.

$$
x^2 - 4 \neq 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x \neq -2 \ \text{ and } \ x \neq 2
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4} &= \frac{(x - 2)\left( x^2 + 2x + 4 \right)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x^2 + 2x + 4}{x + 2}, \qquad x \neq -2, \ x \neq 2
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x^2+2*x+4)/(x+2)" xmin=-7 xmax=5 ymin=-12 ymax=10 punti="2,3,vuoto" asintoti="x=-2" titolo="$y = \dfrac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4}$: the graph coincides with that of $\dfrac{x^2 + 2x + 4}{x + 2}$ without the point $(2, 3)$, because $2$ is excluded by the domain conditions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

An algebraic fraction is defined where the denominator is nonzero, therefore the **domain conditions** are written *before* simplifying. The denominator $x^2 - 4$ is a difference of squares, equal to $(x - 2)(x + 2)$, and it vanishes at $x = 2$ and at $x = -2$; these two values are excluded.

The numerator $x^3 - 8$ is the **difference of the cubes** of $A = x$ and $B = 2$, hence it factors as $(x - 2)\left( x^2 + 2x + 4 \right)$. The second factor is a quadratic that is not a perfect square, equal to $(x + 1)^2 + 3$, therefore it does not vanish and cannot be factored further.

Numerator and denominator have the common factor $x - 2$. For $x \neq 2$ this factor is nonzero, hence it cancels and the fraction becomes $\dfrac{x^2 + 2x + 4}{x + 2}$.

The simplified fraction is also defined at $x = 2$, where it equals $\dfrac{12}{4} = 3$, while the original fraction is not defined at $x = 2$. The two expressions therefore coincide only under the condition $x \neq 2$, which must be written next to the result together with $x \neq -2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify the fraction $\dfrac{x^3 - 3x + 2}{x^3 - x^2 - x + 1}$, stating the domain conditions.

$$
x^3 - x^2 - x + 1 = x^2 (x - 1) - (x - 1) = (x - 1)\left( x^2 - 1 \right) = (x - 1)^2 (x + 1)
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & 0 & -3 & 2 \\
1 &  & 1 & 1 & -2 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 1 & -2 & 0
\end{array}
\qquad
x^3 - 3x + 2 = (x - 1)\left( x^2 + x - 2 \right) = (x - 1)^2 (x + 2)
$$

$$
\frac{x^3 - 3x + 2}{x^3 - x^2 - x + 1} = \frac{(x - 1)^2 (x + 2)}{(x - 1)^2 (x + 1)} = \frac{x + 2}{x + 1}, \qquad x \neq -1, \ x \neq 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator has four terms, therefore we try **factoring by grouping**: $x^2$ is factored out of the first two terms and $-1$ out of the last two, so that both groups leave the factor $x - 1$. The remaining factor $x^2 - 1$ is a difference of squares, hence the denominator is $(x - 1)^2 (x + 1)$ and it vanishes at $x = 1$ and at $x = -1$. The **domain conditions** are $x \neq 1$ and $x \neq -1$.

The numerator has no $x^2$ term, therefore its coefficient in the synthetic division scheme is $0$. Since the numerator equals $1 - 3 + 2 = 0$ at $x = 1$, the scheme with $c = 1$ gives remainder $0$ and quotient $x^2 + x - 2$. This quotient is a **monic quadratic trinomial**, equal to $(x - 1)(x + 2)$, therefore the numerator is $(x - 1)^2 (x + 2)$.

The factor $(x - 1)^2$ appears above and below and is nonzero for $x \neq 1$, hence it cancels. The simplified fraction $\dfrac{x + 2}{x + 1}$ makes sense also at $x = 1$, where it equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$, but the original fraction is not defined at $1$; for this reason the result carries *both* conditions $x \neq 1$ and $x \neq -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify the fraction $\dfrac{x^4 + 4}{x^2 + 2x + 2}$, stating the domain conditions.

$$
x^2 + 2x + 2 = (x + 1)^2 + 1 \geq 1
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^4 + 4 &= x^4 + 4x^2 + 4 - 4x^2 \\[6pt]
&= \left( x^2 + 2 \right)^2 - (2x)^2 \\[6pt]
&= \left( x^2 - 2x + 2 \right)\left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x^4 + 4}{x^2 + 2x + 2} = x^2 - 2x + 2 \qquad \text{for every } x \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is rewritten by completing the square: $x^2 + 2x + 2 = x^2 + 2x + 1 + 1 = (x + 1)^2 + 1$. A square is greater than or equal to zero, therefore the denominator is at least $1$ and does not vanish for any real $x$. Hence the fraction is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$ and there are no values to exclude.

The numerator $x^4 + 4$ is positive for every $x$, therefore it has no real roots and synthetic division provides no factors. We add and subtract $4x^2$, twice the product of $x^2$ and $2$; in this way the first three terms form the **square of a binomial** $\left( x^2 + 2 \right)^2$, and the expression becomes a **difference of squares** with $A = x^2 + 2$ and $B = 2x$. The product $(A - B)(A + B)$ gives the two factors $x^2 - 2x + 2$ and $x^2 + 2x + 2$.

The second factor coincides with the denominator, which never vanishes, therefore the simplification holds without conditions. The fraction is equal to the polynomial $x^2 - 2x + 2$ for every real $x$.

</div>
