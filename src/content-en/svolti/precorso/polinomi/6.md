## Exercise 1

Determine $k$ so that the remainder of the division of $P(x) = 2x^3 - kx^2 + 3x - 1$ by $x + 1$ is $-10$. For the value found, write the quotient and the remainder with synthetic division.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-1) &= 2 \cdot (-1)^3 - k \cdot (-1)^2 + 3 \cdot (-1) - 1 \\[6pt]
&= -2 - k - 3 - 1 \\[6pt]
&= -6 - k
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
-6 - k = -10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k = 4
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 2 & -4 & 3 & -1 \\
-1 &  & -2 & 6 & -9 \\ \hline
 & 2 & -6 & 9 & -10
\end{array}
$$

$$
Q(x) = 2x^2 - 6x + 9, \qquad R = -10
$$

$$
2x^3 - 4x^2 + 3x - 1 = (x + 1)\left( 2x^2 - 6x + 9 \right) - 10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisor $x + 1$ is written as $x - (-1)$, therefore by the **remainder theorem** the remainder of the division is $P(-1)$. The computation of $P(-1)$ is carried out with $k$ left as a letter: $(-1)^3 = -1$ and $(-1)^2 = 1$, hence the four terms are $-2$, $-k$, $-3$ and $-1$, and their sum is $-6 - k$.

The requirement on the remainder then becomes a linear equation in the unknown $k$, that is, $-6 - k = -10$. Adding $6$ to both sides gives $-k = -4$, therefore $k = 4$.

With $k = 4$ the polynomial is $2x^3 - 4x^2 + 3x - 1$, and the **synthetic division scheme** with $c = -1$ proceeds as follows. The coefficient $2$ comes down unchanged; then $2 \cdot (-1) = -2$ and $-4 - 2 = -6$; then $-6 \cdot (-1) = 6$ and $3 + 6 = 9$; finally $9 \cdot (-1) = -9$ and $-1 - 9 = -10$. The last number is the remainder, equal to $-10$ as required, and the numbers preceding it are the coefficients of the quotient, which has degree $3 - 1 = 2$.

For $k = 4$ the quotient is $2x^2 - 6x + 9$ and the remainder is $-10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine $a$ and $b$ so that $P(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + bx - 8$ is divisible both by $x - 2$ and by $x + 1$, then factor the resulting polynomial completely.

$$
P(2) = 8 + 4a + 2b - 8 = 4a + 2b, \qquad P(-1) = -1 + a - b - 8 = a - b - 9
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
4a + 2b = 0 \\[2pt]
a - b - 9 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
b = -2a \\[2pt]
3a = 9
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
a = 3 \\[2pt]
b = -6
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & 3 & -6 & -8 \\
2 &  & 2 & 10 & 8 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 5 & 4 & 0
\end{array}
$$

$$
x^3 + 3x^2 - 6x - 8 = (x - 2)\left( x^2 + 5x + 4 \right) = (x - 2)(x + 1)(x + 4)
$$

::grafico{f="x^3+3*x^2-6*x-8" xmin=-5 xmax=3 ymin=-13 ymax=13 punti="-4,0,pieno;-1,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" titolo="$y = x^3 + 3x^2 - 6x - 8$ meets the $x$-axis at the roots $-4$, $-1$ and $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **factor theorem**, $x - 2$ divides $P$ if and only if $P(2) = 0$, and $x + 1$ divides $P$ if and only if $P(-1) = 0$. The two divisibilities must hold together, therefore $a$ and $b$ satisfy the system formed by the two conditions.

From the first equation we get $b = -2a$. Substituting into the second gives $a + 2a - 9 = 0$, that is, $3a = 9$, therefore $a = 3$ and $b = -6$.

With these values the polynomial is $x^3 + 3x^2 - 6x - 8$. Synthetic division with $c = 2$ gives remainder $0$, as expected, and quotient $x^2 + 5x + 4$. The quotient is a **monic quadratic trinomial**, since $1 + 4 = 5$ and $1 \cdot 4 = 4$, hence it factors as $(x + 1)(x + 4)$; the factor $x + 1$ appears, in agreement with the second condition imposed.

For $a = 3$ and $b = -6$ the polynomial is $(x - 2)(x + 1)(x + 4)$, and its roots are $2$, $-1$ and $-4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine $a$ and $b$ so that the division of $P(x) = x^4 + ax^2 + bx + 1$ by $x^2 - 1$ has remainder $2x + 3$.

$$
P(x) = \left( x^2 - 1 \right) Q(x) + 2x + 3
$$

$$
P(1) = 0 \cdot Q(1) + 2 + 3 = 5, \qquad P(-1) = 0 \cdot Q(-1) - 2 + 3 = 1
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
1 + a + b + 1 = 5 \\[2pt]
1 + a - b + 1 = 1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
a + b = 3 \\[2pt]
a - b = -1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
a = 1 \\[2pt]
b = 2
\end{cases}
$$

$$
x^4 + x^2 + 2x + 1 = \left( x^2 - 1 \right)\left( x^2 + 2 \right) + 2x + 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisor $x^2 - 1$ has degree $2$, therefore the remainder theorem in the form for $x - c$ does not apply directly. We start from the **theorem on division with remainder**: there exists a quotient $Q(x)$ such that $P(x) = \left( x^2 - 1 \right) Q(x) + 2x + 3$, and the given remainder has degree $1$, less than the degree of the divisor.

The divisor vanishes at $x = 1$ and at $x = -1$. Evaluating the equality at these two points, the term with $Q$ equals zero, hence $P(1)$ coincides with the value of the remainder at $1$, that is, $5$, and $P(-1)$ coincides with the value of the remainder at $-1$, that is, $1$. In this way two conditions are obtained without knowing $Q$.

The two conditions form a system in the unknowns $a$ and $b$. Adding the equations $a + b = 3$ and $a - b = -1$ gives $2a = 2$, therefore $a = 1$; from the first it follows that $b = 2$.

The check is carried out with the **identity principle**. The product $\left( x^2 - 1 \right)\left( x^2 + 2 \right)$ equals $x^4 + x^2 - 2$, and adding $2x + 3$ gives $x^4 + x^2 + 2x + 1$, which is $P(x)$ with $a = 1$ and $b = 2$.

</div>
