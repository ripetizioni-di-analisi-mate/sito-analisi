## Exercise 1

Factor $x^2 + 7x + 12$ and $x^2 - 3x - 10$.

$$
x^2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)
$$

$$
x^2 - 3x - 10 = (x - 5)(x + 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first trinomial has the form $x^2 + sx + p$ with $s = 7$ and $p = 12$, so we look for two numbers with **sum** $7$ and **product** $12$. The numbers are $3$ and $4$, therefore the trinomial factors as $(x + 3)(x + 4)$.

In the second trinomial the required sum is $-3$ and the product is $-10$. Since the product is negative, the two numbers have **opposite signs**; the pair $-5$ and $2$ satisfies both conditions, given that $-5 + 2 = -3$ and $(-5) \cdot 2 = -10$, so the factorization is $(x - 5)(x + 2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $2x^3 - x^2 + 6x - 3$.

$$
2x^3 - x^2 + 6x - 3 = x^2 (2x - 1) + 3\,(2x - 1) = (2x - 1)\left( x^2 + 3 \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polynomial has four terms with no factor common to all of them, so **factoring by grouping** is tried. From the first two terms $x^2$ is factored out, which leaves $2x - 1$; from the last two $3$ is factored out, which again leaves $2x - 1$. The two groups have the same factor $2x - 1$, therefore factoring by grouping is completed by factoring out $2x - 1$.

The factor $x^2 + 3$ is the sum of a square and a positive number, so $x^2 + 3 \geq 3 > 0$ for every $x$: the factor has **no real roots** and the factorization is **complete**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12$ completely.

$$
x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x + 12 = x^2 (x - 3) - 4\,(x - 3) = (x - 3)\left( x^2 - 4 \right)
$$

$$
(x - 3)\left( x^2 - 4 \right) = (x - 3)(x - 2)(x + 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Factoring by grouping** groups the first two terms, from which $x^2$ is factored out, and the last two, from which $-4$ is factored out: factoring out $-4$ instead of $4$ leaves the same factor $x - 3$ in both groups, since $-4\,(x - 3) = -4x + 12$. Factoring out $x - 3$ gives the product $(x - 3)\left( x^2 - 4 \right)$.

The strategy starts again on the factors obtained: $x^2 - 4$ is a **difference of squares**, so it factors as $(x - 2)(x + 2)$. All the factors have degree $1$, therefore the factorization is **complete**.

</div>
