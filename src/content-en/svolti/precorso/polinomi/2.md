## Exercise 1

Find the quotient and the remainder of the division of $x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 3$ by $x + 1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 3 - x^2 (x + 1) &= x^2 - x + 3 \\[6pt]
x^2 - x + 3 - x\,(x + 1) &= -2x + 3 \\[6pt]
-2x + 3 - (-2)(x + 1) &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
Q(x) = x^2 + x - 2, \qquad R(x) = 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first term of the quotient is the ratio of the highest-degree terms of the dividend and the divisor, that is, $\dfrac{x^3}{x} = x^2$; the divisor is multiplied by $x^2$ and the product is subtracted from the dividend, and the partial remainder is $x^2 - x + 3$. The partial remainder has degree $2$, greater than the degree of the divisor, so the algorithm continues: the next term is $\dfrac{x^2}{x} = x$ and the new partial remainder is $-2x + 3$. The new partial remainder has degree equal to that of the divisor, therefore a last step is carried out with the term $\dfrac{-2x}{x} = -2$, which leaves the remainder $5$. The remainder has degree zero, less than the degree of the divisor, so the algorithm **stops**.

The check uses the **identity principle**: $(x + 1)(x^2 + x - 2) + 5 = x^3 + 2x^2 - x - 2 + 5 = x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 3$, which is the dividend.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the quotient and the remainder of the division of $2x^4 - 3x^3 + x - 1$ by $x^2 - 2$.

$$
2x^4 - 3x^3 + 0x^2 + x - 1
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^4 - 3x^3 + 0x^2 + x - 1 - 2x^2 (x^2 - 2) &= -3x^3 + 4x^2 + x - 1 \\[6pt]
-3x^3 + 4x^2 + x - 1 - (-3x)(x^2 - 2) &= 4x^2 - 5x - 1 \\[6pt]
4x^2 - 5x - 1 - 4\,(x^2 - 2) &= -5x + 7
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
Q(x) = 2x^2 - 3x + 4, \qquad R(x) = -5x + 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The dividend lacks the term of degree $2$, so it is rewritten with the coefficient $0$ in place of the missing degree; in this way *every* column of the algorithm stays aligned.

The first term of the quotient is $\dfrac{2x^4}{x^2} = 2x^2$ and the first subtraction leaves $-3x^3 + 4x^2 + x - 1$. Since the degree of the partial remainder is $3$, greater than the degree of the divisor, the algorithm continues with $\dfrac{-3x^3}{x^2} = -3x$, which leaves $4x^2 - 5x - 1$. The new partial remainder has degree equal to that of the divisor, therefore the last term of the quotient is $\dfrac{4x^2}{x^2} = 4$ and the remainder is $-5x + 7$. The remainder has degree $1$, less than $2$, so the division is **complete**: when the divisor has degree $2$, the remainder can contain both a term in $x$ and a constant term.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Carry out the division of $x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 2$ by $x - 2$ and determine whether the division is exact.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 2 - x^2 (x - 2) &= -x^2 + 3x - 2 \\[6pt]
-x^2 + 3x - 2 - (-x)(x - 2) &= x - 2 \\[6pt]
x - 2 - 1 \cdot (x - 2) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
Q(x) = x^2 - x + 1, \qquad R(x) = 0
$$

The division is **exact** and $x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 2 = (x - 2)(x^2 - x + 1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three steps of the algorithm produce the terms $x^2$, $-x$ and $1$ of the quotient; after the last subtraction the remainder is $0$, so the division is **exact**. By **Ruffini's theorem** this means that $2$ is a root of the dividend, and indeed $P(2) = 8 - 12 + 6 - 2 = 0$. The divisor $x - 2$ is therefore a **factor** of the dividend, and the division provides the factorization $P(x) = (x - 2)(x^2 - x + 1)$.

</div>
