## Exercise 1

Determine the multiplicity of the root $1$ of the polynomial $P(x) = x^4 - x^3 - 3x^2 + 5x - 2$ and factor it completely.

$$
P(1) = 1 - 1 - 3 + 5 - 2 = 0
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc|c}
 & 1 & -1 & -3 & 5 & -2 \\
1 &  & 1 & 0 & -3 & 2 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 0 & -3 & 2 & 0
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & 0 & -3 & 2 \\
1 &  & 1 & 1 & -2 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 1 & -2 & 0
\end{array}
\qquad\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
 & 1 & 1 & -2 \\
1 &  & 1 & 2 \\ \hline
 & 1 & 2 & 0
\end{array}
$$

$$
P(x) = (x - 1)^3 (x + 2)
$$

::grafico{f="x^4-x^3-3*x^2+5*x-2" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2 ymin=-10 ymax=6 punti="-2,0,pieno;1,0,pieno" titolo="$y = (x - 1)^3 (x + 2)$ crosses the $x$-axis at the simple root $-2$ and at the triple root $1$, where the graph flattens"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $P(1) = 0$, by the **factor theorem** the binomial $x - 1$ divides $P$. The first scheme with $c = 1$ gives the quotient $x^3 - 3x + 2$; the coefficient of $x^2$ in this quotient is $0$ and must be written in the next scheme.

To find out whether the root is repeated, we divide again by $x - 1$. The second scheme gives remainder $0$ and quotient $x^2 + x - 2$, the third gives remainder $0$ and quotient $x + 2$. The quotient $x + 2$ equals $3$ at $x = 1$, therefore a fourth division by $x - 1$ would not be exact.

There are three exact divisions, hence by the definition of **multiplicity** the root $1$ is **triple**, that is, $P(x) = (x - 1)^3\, Q(x)$ with $Q(x) = x + 2$ and $Q(1) = 3 \neq 0$. The other root is $-2$, which is simple.

The sum of the multiplicities is $3 + 1 = 4$, equal to the degree of $P$, in agreement with the rule according to which degrees add up in a product. The multiplicity $3$ is odd, therefore the graph also crosses the $x$-axis at $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine $a$ and $b$ so that $2$ is a double root of $P(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + bx + 12$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & a & b & 12 \\
2 &  & 2 & 2a + 4 & 4a + 2b + 8 \\ \hline
 & 1 & a + 2 & 2a + b + 4 & 4a + 2b + 20
\end{array}
$$

$$
Q(x) = x^2 + (a + 2)\,x + 2a + b + 4, \qquad Q(2) = 4a + b + 12
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
4a + 2b + 20 = 0 \\[2pt]
4a + b + 12 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
b = -8 \\[2pt]
a = -1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
x^3 - x^2 - 8x + 12 = (x - 2)\left( x^2 + x - 6 \right) = (x - 2)^2 (x + 3)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root $2$ is double if $(x - 2)^2$ divides $P$, that is, if the division by $x - 2$ is exact and the quotient also vanishes at $2$. Two conditions are therefore needed, one for each of the two unknowns.

The **synthetic division scheme** with $c = 2$ is carried out with letters. The coefficient $1$ comes down; then $1 \cdot 2 = 2$ and the sum with $a$ is $a + 2$; then $(a + 2) \cdot 2 = 2a + 4$ and the sum with $b$ is $2a + b + 4$; finally $(2a + b + 4) \cdot 2 = 4a + 2b + 8$ and the sum with $12$ gives the remainder $4a + 2b + 20$.

The first condition is that the remainder be zero. The second is $Q(2) = 0$, where $Q(2) = 4 + 2a + 4 + 2a + b + 4 = 4a + b + 12$. Subtracting the second equation from the first gives $b + 8 = 0$, therefore $b = -8$; from the second it follows that $4a = -4$, hence $a = -1$.

With these values the quotient is $x^2 + x - 6$, a **monic quadratic trinomial** equal to $(x - 2)(x + 3)$, which again contains the factor $x - 2$. The remaining factor $x + 3$ equals $5$ at $x = 2$, therefore the root $2$ is exactly double and $P(x) = (x - 2)^2 (x + 3)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the polynomial of degree three that has the simple root $-1$, the double root $3$ and takes the value $18$ at $x = 0$.

$$
P(x) = k\,(x + 1)(x - 3)^2, \qquad k \neq 0
$$

$$
P(0) = k \cdot 1 \cdot 9 = 9k = 18 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k = 2
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x) &= 2\,(x + 1)\left( x^2 - 6x + 9 \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2\left( x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + x^2 - 6x + 9 \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2x^3 - 10x^2 + 6x + 18
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x^3-10*x^2+6*x+18" xmin=-1.5 xmax=4.3 ymin=-12 ymax=24 punti="-1,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;0,18,pieno" titolo="$y = 2(x + 1)(x - 3)^2$ crosses the $x$-axis at the simple root $-1$, touches it without crossing at the double root $3$ and passes through $(0, 18)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of **multiplicity**, the polynomial contains the factor $x + 1$ once and the factor $x - 3$ twice, therefore it is divisible by $(x + 1)(x - 3)^2$, which has degree $1 + 2 = 3$. The polynomial sought also has degree $3$, hence the quotient of this division has degree $0$, that is, it is a nonzero constant $k$.

The constant is obtained from the condition on the value at $0$. Substituting $x = 0$ gives $(0 + 1) = 1$ and $(0 - 3)^2 = 9$, therefore $P(0) = 9k$; the equation $9k = 18$ gives $k = 2$.

The expansion is carried out in two steps: first the **square of a binomial** $(x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9$, then the product by $x + 1$, in which the like terms $-6x^2 + x^2 = -5x^2$ and $9x - 6x = 3x$ are combined. Multiplying by $2$ gives $P(x) = 2x^3 - 10x^2 + 6x + 18$, whose constant term is $18$, in agreement with the condition $P(0) = 18$.

</div>
