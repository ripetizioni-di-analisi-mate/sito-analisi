## Exercise 1

Determine which of $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{10}$ is greater, without using approximate values.

$$
\left(\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}\right)^{2} = 2 + 2\sqrt{6} + 3 = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}, \qquad \left(\sqrt{10}\right)^{2} = 10
$$
$$
5 + 2\sqrt{6} \ \ ? \ \ 10 \iff 2\sqrt{6} \ \ ? \ \ 5
$$
$$
\left(2\sqrt{6}\right)^{2} = 24, \qquad 5^{2} = 25, \qquad 24 < 25
$$

Therefore $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} < \sqrt{10}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two numbers are positive. For positive numbers squaring preserves the order, therefore comparing the two numbers is equivalent to comparing their squares. The symbol $?$ denotes the order relation still to be determined.

The square of the sum is computed with the **square of a binomial**: $\left(\sqrt{2}\right)^{2} = 2$, $\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^{2} = 3$ and twice the product is $2\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{6}$. The comparison therefore becomes $5 + 2\sqrt{6}$ against $10$; subtracting $5$ from both sides leaves $2\sqrt{6}$ against $5$.

Also $2\sqrt{6}$ and $5$ are positive, so they can be squared again. We obtain $4 \cdot 6 = 24$ against $25$, and since $24 < 25$ we have $2\sqrt{6} < 5$. Going back up the chain of equivalent comparisons we conclude that $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} < \sqrt{10}$.

The two numbers are very close: $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} \approx 3.146$ and $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.162$. The algebraic comparison gives an **exact** result. With approximations to one decimal place, such as $1.4 + 1.7 = 3.1$ against $3.2$, the comparison would not be justified, because the error of the approximations is of the same order as the difference between the two numbers.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine which of $\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{6}$ and $\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{5}$ is greater.

$$
\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{6} = \frac{\left(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{6}\right)\left(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{6}\right)}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{6}} = \frac{7 - 6}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{6}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{6}}
$$
$$
\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{5} = \frac{6 - 5}{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5}}
$$
$$
\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{6} > \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5} > 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{6}} < \frac{1}{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5}}
$$

Therefore $\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{6} < \sqrt{6} - \sqrt{5}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two differences are positive, because by the **comparison of roots** with the same index $\sqrt{7} > \sqrt{6} > \sqrt{5}$. Squaring them would produce new terms with roots, so we use the reverse procedure of **rationalization**.

Each difference is multiplied and divided by the conjugate binomial. The numerator becomes a difference of squares, that is, $7 - 6 = 1$ in the first case and $6 - 5 = 1$ in the second, therefore the two differences are written as fractions with numerator $1$.

The two fractions have the same positive numerator, so the one with the greater denominator is smaller. The denominators $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{6}$ and $\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5}$ have the term $\sqrt{6}$ in common, and $\sqrt{7} > \sqrt{5}$; the first denominator is therefore greater, and the first fraction is **smaller**.

With the same computation, for every natural number $n$ we have $\sqrt{n + 1} - \sqrt{n} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n + 1} + \sqrt{n}}$, therefore the difference between the square roots of two consecutive integers decreases as $n$ increases. The approximate values $\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{6} \approx 0.196$ and $\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{5} \approx 0.213$ confirm it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine which of $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{-\sqrt{2}}$ and $3\sqrt{3}$ is greater.

$$
\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{-\sqrt{2}} = \left(3^{-1}\right)^{-\sqrt{2}} = 3^{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad 3\sqrt{3} = 3^{1} \cdot 3^{\frac{1}{2}} = 3^{\frac{3}{2}}
$$
$$
\left(\sqrt{2}\right)^{2} = 2 < \frac{9}{4} = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sqrt{2} < \frac{3}{2}
$$
$$
3 > 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3^{\sqrt{2}} < 3^{\frac{3}{2}}
$$

Therefore $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{-\sqrt{2}} < 3\sqrt{3}$.

::grafico{f="3^x" xmin=-1 xmax=2.2 ymin=-1 ymax=9 punti="1.414,4.729,pieno;1.5,5.196,pieno" segmenti="1.414,0,1.414,4.729;1.5,0,1.5,5.196" legenda="y = 3^x" titolo="The function $y = 3^{x}$ is increasing, therefore from $\sqrt{2} < \dfrac{3}{2}$ it follows that $3^{\sqrt{2}} < 3^{\frac{3}{2}} = 3\sqrt{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two numbers have different forms, therefore both are written as powers of the same base $3$. Since $\dfrac{1}{3} = 3^{-1}$, with the **power of a power** we obtain $\left(3^{-1}\right)^{-\sqrt{2}} = 3^{(-1)(-\sqrt{2})} = 3^{\sqrt{2}}$; the base is positive, so the rule also holds with the real exponent $\sqrt{2}$. Moreover $3\sqrt{3} = 3^{1} \cdot 3^{\frac{1}{2}} = 3^{\frac{3}{2}}$.

The two powers have base $3$, greater than $1$. By the **comparison of powers** with the same base, the power increases as the exponent increases, therefore it suffices to compare the exponents $\sqrt{2}$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

The exponents are positive, so we compare their squares: $2$ and $\dfrac{9}{4}$. Since $2 = \dfrac{8}{4} < \dfrac{9}{4}$, we have $\sqrt{2} < \dfrac{3}{2}$, and consequently $3^{\sqrt{2}} < 3^{\frac{3}{2}}$.

The approximate values are $3^{\sqrt{2}} \approx 4.729$ and $3\sqrt{3} \approx 5.196$. The graph shows the two points on the curve $y = 3^{x}$, with x-coordinates $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>
