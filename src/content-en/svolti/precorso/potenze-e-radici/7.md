## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3} - 1} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3} + 1} .
$$

$$
\left(\sqrt{3} - 1\right)\left(\sqrt{3} + 1\right) = 3 - 1 = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3} - 1} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3} + 1}
&= \frac{\left(\sqrt{3} + 1\right) - \left(\sqrt{3} - 1\right)}{\left(\sqrt{3} - 1\right)\left(\sqrt{3} + 1\right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sqrt{3} + 1 - \sqrt{3} + 1}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{2} = 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Result: $1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two denominators $\sqrt{3} - 1$ and $\sqrt{3} + 1$ are **conjugate binomials**: they have the same terms and differ only in the sign of the second. Their product is therefore a sum times a difference, equal to the difference of squares $\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^{2} - 1^{2} = 3 - 1 = 2$, which is a rational number.

For this reason the product of the two denominators is a rational common denominator. With the common denominator $2$, the first fraction is multiplied above and below by $\sqrt{3} + 1$ and the second by $\sqrt{3} - 1$; the numerator becomes $\left(\sqrt{3} + 1\right) - \left(\sqrt{3} - 1\right)$.

Removing the grouping symbols, the minus sign in front of the second binomial changes the sign of both its terms, so the numerator is $\sqrt{3} + 1 - \sqrt{3} + 1 = 2$. The terms with the root cancel, and the result is the integer $1$.

The same result is obtained by rationalizing the two fractions separately. The first equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2}$ and the second equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{2}$; their difference is $\dfrac{2}{2} = 1$. The numerical check gives $\dfrac{1}{0.732} - \dfrac{1}{2.732} \approx 1.366 - 0.366 = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rationalize and simplify
$$
\frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}
&= \frac{\left(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}\right)\left(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}\right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{6 + 2\sqrt{12} + 2}{6 - 2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{8 + 4\sqrt{3}}{4} \\[6pt]
&= 2 + \sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Result: $2 + \sqrt{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is the binomial $\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}$, therefore numerator and denominator are multiplied by the **conjugate binomial** $\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}$. The denominator becomes the difference of squares $6 - 2 = 4$.

The numerator becomes $\left(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}\right)^{2}$, which is expanded with the **square of a binomial**: the square of the first term is $6$, that of the second is $2$ and twice the product is $2\sqrt{6}\,\sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{12}$, by the product property of roots.

The radical $\sqrt{12}$ is simplified by taking out the factor $4$: $\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} = 2\sqrt{3}$. The numerator therefore equals $8 + 4\sqrt{3}$. All its terms are multiples of $4$, so dividing by the denominator $4$ we obtain $2 + \sqrt{3}$.

The result is checked with approximate values. We have $\sqrt{6} \approx 2.449$ and $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$, therefore the given fraction is approximately $\dfrac{3.863}{1.035} \approx 3.732$; also $2 + \sqrt{3} \approx 2 + 1.732 = 3.732$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize
$$
\frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right) + \sqrt{3}}
&= \frac{1 + \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{3}}{\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right)^{2} - 3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1 + \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{3}}{1 + 2\sqrt{2} + 2 - 3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1 + \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1 + \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}}
&= \frac{\left(1 + \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{3}\right)\sqrt{2}}{2\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{2}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sqrt{2} + 2 - \sqrt{6}}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Result: $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6}}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator has three terms, therefore the rationalization is done in two steps. The first two terms are grouped and the denominator is read as the binomial $\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right) + \sqrt{3}$; its conjugate is $\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right) - \sqrt{3}$.

Multiplying above and below by the conjugate, the denominator becomes the difference of squares $\left(1 + \sqrt{2}\right)^{2} - \left(\sqrt{3}\right)^{2}$. The expansion gives $1 + 2\sqrt{2} + 2 - 3 = 2\sqrt{2}$, so the new denominator no longer contains $\sqrt{3}$ and contains only the root $\sqrt{2}$.

The new denominator $2\sqrt{2}$ contains a single root, so in the second step we multiply above and below by $\sqrt{2}$. The denominator becomes $2 \cdot 2 = 4$, and the numerator is obtained by multiplying each term by $\sqrt{2}$: $1 \cdot \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2$ and $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{6}$.

The final denominator $4$ is rational, therefore the fraction is **rationalized**. The numerical check gives $\dfrac{1}{1 + 1.414 + 1.732} \approx 0.241$ and $\dfrac{2 + 1.414 - 2.449}{4} \approx 0.241$.

</div>
