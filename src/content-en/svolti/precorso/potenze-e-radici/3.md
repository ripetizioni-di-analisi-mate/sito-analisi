## Exercise 1

Rationalize
$$
\frac{6}{\sqrt{12}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6}{\sqrt{12}} = \frac{6}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}}
&= \frac{3 \cdot \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{3} = \sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before rationalizing it is convenient to simplify the root: $12 = 4 \cdot 3$, therefore $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$ and the fraction reduces to $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}}$.

The denominator is a **single root**, therefore numerator and denominator are multiplied by $\sqrt{3}$: the product $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}$ equals $3$, hence the denominator becomes **rational**. The factor $3$ cancels between numerator and denominator and $\sqrt{3}$ remains.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rationalize
$$
\frac{4}{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4}{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3}}
&= \frac{4\,(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})}{(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3})(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4\,(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})}{7 - 3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4\,(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})}{4} = \sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is a binomial with square roots, therefore numerator and denominator are multiplied by the **conjugate binomial** $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$. The product of a difference and the corresponding sum is the **difference of the squares**, hence the squares of the roots appear in the denominator: $7 - 3 = 4$, a rational number.

The factor $4$ in the denominator cancels with the $4$ in the numerator, therefore the result is $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize
$$
\frac{10}{\sqrt[3]{25}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{10}{\sqrt[3]{25}} = \frac{10}{\sqrt[3]{5^2}}
&= \frac{10 \cdot \sqrt[3]{5}}{\sqrt[3]{5^2} \cdot \sqrt[3]{5}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{10\,\sqrt[3]{5}}{\sqrt[3]{5^3}} = \frac{10\,\sqrt[3]{5}}{5} = 2\,\sqrt[3]{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand is written as a power: $25 = 5^2$. With a cube root in the denominator, the multiplying factor is the one that **completes the cube** under the root: since $5^2 \cdot 5 = 5^3$, numerator and denominator are multiplied by $\sqrt[3]{5}$.

The product of cube roots is the cube root of the product, therefore the denominator becomes $\sqrt[3]{5^3} = 5$, a **rational number**. The fraction $\dfrac{10}{5}$ simplifies to $2$ and the result is $2\,\sqrt[3]{5}$.

</div>
