## Exercise 1

Compare
$$
\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{5} \ \text{ and } \ \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{8},
\qquad \text{then} \qquad
3^4 \ \text{ and } \ 5^4 .
$$

$$
0 < \frac{2}{3} < 1, \quad 5 < 8
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{5} > \left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{8}
$$

$$
0 < 3 < 5, \quad 4 > 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
3^4 < 5^4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first comparison the two powers have the same base, between $0$ and $1$: with a base of this kind the power **decreases** as the exponent increases, therefore the power with exponent $5$ is the **larger** one. A check on a smaller case confirms the direction, because $\dfrac{2}{3} > \dfrac{4}{9}$.

In the second comparison the powers have the same positive exponent and different positive bases; with the same positive exponent, the larger base corresponds to the **larger power**, therefore $3^4 < 5^4$. The direct computation gives $81 < 625$ and confirms the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine which of $2^{40}$ and $3^{30}$ is larger.

$$
2^{40} = \left(2^{4}\right)^{10} = 16^{10}, \qquad
3^{30} = \left(3^{3}\right)^{10} = 27^{10}
$$

$$
16 < 27 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 16^{10} < 27^{10} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2^{40} < 3^{30}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases and the exponents are different, therefore a direct comparison is impractical and one looks for a **common exponent**. The exponents $40$ and $30$ have the common divisor $10$, hence with the **power of a power** the two expressions are rewritten as powers with exponent $10$: $2^{40} = (2^4)^{10}$ and $3^{30} = (3^3)^{10}$.

The new bases are $2^4 = 16$ and $3^3 = 27$. The powers now have the same positive exponent, therefore the comparison reduces to the **comparison of the bases**: since $16 < 27$, it follows that $2^{40} < 3^{30}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Order from smallest to largest
$$
\sqrt{2}, \qquad \sqrt[3]{3}, \qquad \sqrt[6]{10} .
$$

$$
\sqrt{2} = \sqrt[6]{2^3} = \sqrt[6]{8}, \qquad
\sqrt[3]{3} = \sqrt[6]{3^2} = \sqrt[6]{9}, \qquad
\sqrt[6]{10}
$$

$$
8 < 9 < 10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sqrt{2} < \sqrt[3]{3} < \sqrt[6]{10}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The indices of the three roots are $2$, $3$ and $6$, and their least common multiple is $6$, therefore with the **invariance property** each root is rewritten with index $6$: for $\sqrt{2}$ index and exponent are multiplied by $3$ and the radicand becomes $2^3 = 8$; for $\sqrt[3]{3}$ they are multiplied by $2$ and the radicand becomes $3^2 = 9$; the third root already has index $6$.

The three roots now have the same index, therefore the ordering follows that of the **radicands**: from $8 < 9 < 10$ it follows that $\sqrt{2} < \sqrt[3]{3} < \sqrt[6]{10}$.

</div>
