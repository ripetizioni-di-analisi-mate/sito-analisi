## Exercise 1

Simplify, for $y \geq 0$ and real $x$,
$$
\sqrt{12\,x^{4} y^{3}} .
$$

$$
12\,x^{4} y^{3} = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot \left(x^{2}\right)^{2} \cdot y^{2} \cdot y = \left(2x^{2}y\right)^{2} \cdot 3y
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{12\,x^{4} y^{3}} &= \sqrt{\left(2x^{2}y\right)^{2}} \cdot \sqrt{3y} \\[6pt]
&= \left|2x^{2}y\right| \sqrt{3y} \\[6pt]
&= 2x^{2}y\,\sqrt{3y}
\end{aligned}
$$

Result: $2x^{2}y\,\sqrt{3y}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand is non-negative, because $x^{4} \geq 0$ for every $x$ and $y^{3} \geq 0$ for $y \geq 0$; therefore the root exists. To take factors out, we separate in the radicand the part that is a **perfect square**: $12 = 4 \cdot 3$, $x^{4} = \left(x^{2}\right)^{2}$ and $y^{3} = y^{2} \cdot y$. The radicand thus becomes $\left(2x^{2}y\right)^{2} \cdot 3y$.

The two factors $\left(2x^{2}y\right)^{2}$ and $3y$ are both non-negative, so the **product property** of roots applies, and we obtain $\sqrt{\left(2x^{2}y\right)^{2}} \cdot \sqrt{3y}$.

The square root of a square is the **absolute value** of the base, therefore $\sqrt{\left(2x^{2}y\right)^{2}} = \left|2x^{2}y\right|$. The number $2x^{2}y$ is non-negative, because $x^{2} \geq 0$ for every $x$ and $y \geq 0$ by hypothesis; the absolute value can therefore be removed.

The absolute value is removed *only* for this reason. If the factor taken out were $x$ instead of $x^{2}$, for example in $\sqrt{x^{2} y} = |x|\sqrt{y}$, the absolute value would remain, because $x$ can be negative. The check with $x = 1$ and $y = 3$ gives $\sqrt{12 \cdot 27} = \sqrt{324} = 18$, and also $2 \cdot 1 \cdot 3 \cdot \sqrt{9} = 18$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Bring the outside factor under the radical sign in
$$
x\sqrt{3} ,
$$
for varying real $x$.

- For $x \geq 0$:
$$
x\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{x^{2}}\,\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{3x^{2}} .
$$
- For $x < 0$:
$$
x\sqrt{3} = -|x|\sqrt{3} = -\sqrt{x^{2}}\,\sqrt{3} = -\sqrt{3x^{2}} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A factor goes under a square root by writing it as the root of its square, and this is possible only for a non-negative number, because a square root is never negative. The sign of $x$ is not known, therefore we distinguish **two cases**.

For $x \geq 0$ we have $x = \sqrt{x^{2}}$, because $\sqrt{x^{2}} = |x|$ and $|x| = x$. With the product property of roots we obtain $\sqrt{x^{2}} \cdot \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{3x^{2}}$.

For $x < 0$ we have $|x| = -x$, that is, $x = -|x|$. The minus sign stays outside the root, and only the positive factor $|x| = \sqrt{x^{2}}$ goes under the radical sign; we obtain $x\sqrt{3} = -\sqrt{3x^{2}}$.

The result of the second case is negative, consistent with the fact that $x\sqrt{3}$ is the product of a negative number and a positive number. With $x = -2$ we have $-2\sqrt{3} = -\sqrt{12} \approx -3.46$. The expression $\sqrt{3x^{2}}$ would instead give $\sqrt{12} \approx 3.46$, which is the opposite value; therefore for $x < 0$ the equality $x\sqrt{3} = \sqrt{3x^{2}}$ is **false**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify, for real $x$,
$$
\sqrt{x^{2} - 6x + 9} + x .
$$

$$
x^{2} - 6x + 9 = (x - 3)^{2}
$$
$$
\sqrt{x^{2} - 6x + 9} + x = \sqrt{(x - 3)^{2}} + x = |x - 3| + x
$$

- For $x \geq 3$: $\ |x - 3| + x = x - 3 + x = 2x - 3$.
- For $x < 3$: $\ |x - 3| + x = 3 - x + x = 3$.

::grafico{f="sqrt((x-3)^2)+x" xmin=-2 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=11 punti="3,3,pieno" legenda="y = \sqrt{x^2 - 6x + 9} + x" titolo="The graph of $y = \sqrt{x^{2} - 6x + 9} + x$ is constant and equal to $3$ for $x < 3$ and coincides with the line $y = 2x - 3$ for $x \geq 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand $x^{2} - 6x + 9$ is the **square of the binomial** $x - 3$, because $(x - 3)^{2} = x^{2} - 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x + 9$. A square is never negative, therefore the expression is defined for every real $x$.

The square root of a square is the absolute value of the base, so $\sqrt{(x - 3)^{2}} = |x - 3|$. The absolute value is written without the symbol after distinguishing the sign of $x - 3$, which is non-negative for $x \geq 3$ and negative for $x < 3$.

For $x \geq 3$ we have $|x - 3| = x - 3$, and the expression equals $2x - 3$. For $x < 3$ we have $|x - 3| = 3 - x$; the terms in $x$ cancel and the expression equals the constant $3$.

The expression $\sqrt{(x - 3)^{2}} = x - 3$ would lead to the result $2x - 3$ for every $x$, which is **false** for $x < 3$. For example for $x = 0$ the given expression equals $\sqrt{9} + 0 = 3$, while $2x - 3$ equals $-3$. The graph shows the two pieces, which join at the point $(3, 3)$.

</div>
