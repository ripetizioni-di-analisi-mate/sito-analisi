## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\frac{27^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 9^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{3^{-1}} .
$$

$$
27 = 3^{3}, \qquad 9 = 3^{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{27^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 9^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{3^{-1}}
&= \frac{\left(3^{3}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} \cdot \left(3^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{3^{-1}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3^{2} \cdot 3^{-1}}{3^{-1}} \\[6pt]
&= 3^{2 - 1 - (-1)} \\[6pt]
&= 3^{2} = 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Result: $9$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $27$, $9$ and $3$ are different, therefore the properties of powers with the same base do not apply directly. The three bases are, however, powers of $3$, because $27 = 3^{3}$ and $9 = 3^{2}$.

With the **power of a power** the exponents are multiplied: $\left(3^{3}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 3^{3 \cdot \frac{2}{3}} = 3^{2}$ and $\left(3^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}} = 3^{2 \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)} = 3^{-1}$. The base $3$ is positive, so by the definition of **power with rational exponent** these rules also hold with fractional exponents.

The expression now contains only powers with base $3$. For the product the exponents are added and for the quotient the exponent of the denominator is subtracted, therefore the final exponent is $2 + (-1) - (-1) = 2$, and the result is $3^{2} = 9$.

The same value is obtained by computing the powers one by one. We have $27^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left(\sqrt[3]{27}\right)^{2} = 3^{2} = 9$ and $9^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{9}} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, while dividing by $3^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $3$. So the result is $9 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 3 = 9$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify, for $a > 0$,
$$
\frac{a^{\frac{1}{2}} \cdot a^{\frac{2}{3}}}{a^{-\frac{1}{6}}}
$$
and write the result with roots.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a^{\frac{1}{2}} \cdot a^{\frac{2}{3}}}{a^{-\frac{1}{6}}}
&= a^{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{6}} \\[6pt]
&= a^{\frac{3 + 4 + 1}{6}} \\[6pt]
&= a^{\frac{8}{6}} = a^{\frac{4}{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
a^{\frac{4}{3}} = a^{1 + \frac{1}{3}} = a \cdot a^{\frac{1}{3}} = a\,\sqrt[3]{a}
$$

Result: $a^{\frac{4}{3}} = a\,\sqrt[3]{a}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The base is the same letter $a$, positive by hypothesis, therefore all the **properties of powers** with rational exponents hold. The product in the numerator gives the sum of the exponents $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{2}{3}$, and the division by $a^{-\frac{1}{6}}$ subtracts the exponent $-\dfrac{1}{6}$, that is, it adds $\dfrac{1}{6}$.

To add the three fractions we use the common denominator $6$: $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{6}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{4}{6}$. The sum is $\dfrac{3 + 4 + 1}{6} = \dfrac{8}{6}$, which in lowest terms equals $\dfrac{4}{3}$.

To write the result with roots we separate the integer part of the exponent: $\dfrac{4}{3} = 1 + \dfrac{1}{3}$. By the product rule $a^{\frac{4}{3}} = a^{1} \cdot a^{\frac{1}{3}}$, and by the definition of power with rational exponent $a^{\frac{1}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{a}$; therefore the result is $a\,\sqrt[3]{a}$. In an equivalent form $a^{\frac{4}{3}} = \sqrt[3]{a^{4}}$, and taking the factor $a^{3}$ out of the root we find $a\,\sqrt[3]{a}$ again.

The check with $a = 8$ gives $8^{\frac{4}{3}} = \left(\sqrt[3]{8}\right)^{4} = 2^{4} = 16$, and also $8\,\sqrt[3]{8} = 8 \cdot 2 = 16$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\frac{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt[3]{4}}{\sqrt[6]{2}} .
$$

$$
\sqrt{2} = 2^{\frac{1}{2}}, \qquad \sqrt[3]{4} = \sqrt[3]{2^{2}} = 2^{\frac{2}{3}}, \qquad \sqrt[6]{2} = 2^{\frac{1}{6}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}} \cdot 2^{\frac{2}{3}}}{2^{\frac{1}{6}}}
&= 2^{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{6}} \\[6pt]
&= 2^{\frac{3 + 4 - 1}{6}} \\[6pt]
&= 2^{\frac{6}{6}} = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Result: $2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three roots have different indices, $2$, $3$ and $6$, therefore the properties of roots with the same index do not apply directly. The radicands are, however, powers of $2$, because $4 = 2^{2}$, and each root is written as a **power with rational exponent** with the rule $\sqrt[n]{a^{m}} = a^{\frac{m}{n}}$.

With the common base $2$, the product adds the exponents and the quotient subtracts the exponent of the denominator. The common denominator of the fractions is $6$, and $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{6}$, $\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{4}{6}$; the resulting exponent is therefore $\dfrac{3 + 4 - 1}{6} = 1$.

The value of the expression is therefore the integer $2$, even though none of the three roots is a rational number.

The same result is obtained with the **invariance property**, by bringing the roots to the common index $6$: $\sqrt{2} = \sqrt[6]{2^{3}}$ and $\sqrt[3]{4} = \sqrt[6]{4^{2}} = \sqrt[6]{2^{4}}$. The product divided by $\sqrt[6]{2}$ becomes $\sqrt[6]{\dfrac{2^{3} \cdot 2^{4}}{2}} = \sqrt[6]{2^{6}} = 2$. With rational exponents the computation is shorter, because the operations on the radicands become sums of fractions.

</div>
