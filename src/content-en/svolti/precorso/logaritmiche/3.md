## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\log_2 x + \log_2 (x - 2) = 3 .
$$

Domain conditions:
$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\
x - 2 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff x > 2
$$

$$
\log_2 \bigl( x (x - 2) \bigr) = 3 \iff x (x - 2) = 2^{3} = 8
$$
$$
x^{2} - 2x - 8 = 0 \iff x = 4 \ \text{or} \ x = -2
$$

The value $x = 4$ satisfies the domain conditions; the value $x = -2$ does *not* satisfy them and is **discarded**.

Solution: $x = 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The system of domain conditions requires $x > 0$ and $x > 2$ together, so it is equivalent to $x > 2$. On positive arguments the sum of logarithms is the **logarithm of the product**, therefore the left-hand side becomes $\log_2 \bigl( x(x - 2) \bigr)$; by the definition of logarithm the argument equals $2^{3} = 8$. The quadratic equation $x^{2} - 2x - 8 = 0$ has solutions $x = 4$ and $x = -2$. Since $4 > 2$, the value $x = 4$ is **acceptable**; for $x = -2$ *both* arguments of the given equation are negative, so the solution is **discarded**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\log_3 (x + 6) - \log_3 x = 1 .
$$

Domain conditions:
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 6 > 0 \\
x > 0
\end{cases}
\iff x > 0
$$

$$
\log_3 \frac{x + 6}{x} = 1 \iff \frac{x + 6}{x} = 3^{1} = 3
$$
$$
x + 6 = 3x \iff 6 = 2x \iff x = 3
$$

The value $x = 3$ satisfies the domain conditions.

Solution: $x = 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain conditions form the system with $x + 6 > 0$ and $x > 0$; the second is the more restrictive, so the system is equivalent to $x > 0$. On positive arguments the difference of logarithms is the **logarithm of the quotient**, therefore the left-hand side becomes $\log_3 \dfrac{x + 6}{x}$, and by the definition of logarithm the argument equals $3$. Since $x > 0$, multiplying both sides by $x$ is legitimate and gives the linear equation $x + 6 = 3x$, with solution $x = 3$, which is positive and therefore **acceptable**. The check confirms the result, because $\log_3 9 - \log_3 3 = 2 - 1 = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
(\log_2 x)^{2} - \log_2 x - 6 = 0 .
$$

The domain condition is $x > 0$. Setting $t = \log_2 x$:
$$
t^{2} - t - 6 = 0 \iff t = 3 \ \text{or} \ t = -2
$$
$$
\log_2 x = 3 \iff x = 2^{3} = 8, \qquad
\log_2 x = -2 \iff x = 2^{-2} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

Both values are positive, so they satisfy the domain condition.

Solutions: $x = 8$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains $(\log_2 x)^{2}$ and $\log_2 x$, so the **substitution** $t = \log_2 x$ transforms it into a quadratic equation in $t$. Unlike the exponential substitution, $t$ can take *any* real value, therefore both solutions $t = 3$ and $t = -2$ are **acceptable**. By the **definition of logarithm**, from $\log_2 x = t$ it follows that $x = 2^{t}$: we obtain $x = 8$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{4}$, both positive, so the domain condition is satisfied in both cases.

</div>
