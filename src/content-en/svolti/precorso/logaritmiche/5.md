## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
\log_{1/2} (x - 1) > 2 .
$$

$$
\log_{1/2} (x - 1) > \log_{1/2} \frac{1}{4} \iff
\begin{cases}
x - 1 < \dfrac{1}{4} \\[8pt]
x - 1 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x < \dfrac{5}{4} \\[8pt]
x > 1
\end{cases}
\iff 1 < x < \frac{5}{4}
$$

Solutions: $1 < x < \dfrac{5}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $2$ is written as $\log_{1/2} \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2} = \log_{1/2} \dfrac{1}{4}$, so the inequality compares two logarithms to base $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The base lies between $0$ and $1$, therefore the logarithmic function is strictly decreasing and the direction is reversed when passing to the arguments. Here the domain condition $x - 1 > 0$ must be written in the system, because the inequality $x - 1 < \dfrac{1}{4}$ alone also admits negative arguments. The intersection of the two conditions gives the interval $1 < x < \dfrac{5}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
\log_{1/3} (x + 2) \leq -1 .
$$

$$
\log_{1/3} (x + 2) \leq \log_{1/3} 3 \iff
\begin{cases}
x + 2 \geq 3 \\
x + 2 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
x > -2
\end{cases}
\iff x \geq 1
$$

Solutions: $x \geq 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $-1$ is written as $\log_{1/3} \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{-1} = \log_{1/3} 3$, so the comparison is between two logarithms to base $\dfrac{1}{3}$. The base lies between $0$ and $1$, therefore the direction is reversed when passing to the arguments and the symbol $\leq$ becomes $\geq$. The condition $x + 2 \geq 3$ is more restrictive than the domain condition $x + 2 > 0$, so the solutions are $x \geq 1$. The check with $x = 1$ confirms the result, because $\log_{1/3} 3 = -1$ and the inequality with $\leq$ is satisfied.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\log_{1/2} (2x - 1) < \log_{1/2} (x + 3) .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
2x - 1 > x + 3 \\
x + 3 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x > 4 \\
x > -3
\end{cases}
\iff x > 4
$$

Solutions: $x > 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two sides are logarithms to the same base $\dfrac{1}{2}$, which lies between $0$ and $1$, so the logarithmic function is strictly decreasing and the direction is reversed when passing to the arguments: from the symbol $<$ between the logarithms we pass to the symbol $>$ between the arguments. The system contains the domain condition $x + 3 > 0$, because the positivity of $2x - 1$ follows from $2x - 1 > x + 3 > 0$. The condition $x > 4$ is more restrictive than $x > -3$, so the solutions are $x > 4$.

</div>
