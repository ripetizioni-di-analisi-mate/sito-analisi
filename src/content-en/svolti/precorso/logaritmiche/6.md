## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\log_2 x + \log_4 x = 3 .
$$

$$
x > 0
$$
$$
\log_4 x = \frac{\log_2 x}{\log_2 4} = \frac{1}{2} \log_2 x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2 x + \frac{1}{2} \log_2 x = 3 &\iff \frac{3}{2} \log_2 x = 3 \\[6pt]
&\iff \log_2 x = 2 \\[6pt]
&\iff x = 2^{2} = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Solution: $x = 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Both logarithms have argument $x$, so the domain restriction is $x > 0$.

The two logarithms have different bases, and the properties of logarithms apply only to logarithms with the same base. The base $4$ is the square of the base $2$, therefore the **change of base** formula is used with new base $2$: $\log_4 x = \dfrac{\log_2 x}{\log_2 4}$, and since $\log_2 4 = 2$ one obtains $\log_4 x = \dfrac{1}{2} \log_2 x$.

The equation now contains only the term $\log_2 x$, with total coefficient $1 + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. Multiplying both sides by $\dfrac{2}{3}$ gives $\log_2 x = 2$. By the **definition of logarithm** this equation is equivalent to $x = 2^{2} = 4$, which satisfies the condition $x > 0$.

The check gives $\log_2 4 + \log_4 4 = 2 + 1 = 3$, equal to the right-hand side, so $x = 4$ is **the solution**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\log_3 x = \log_9 (2x + 3) .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\
2x + 3 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff x > 0
$$
$$
\log_9 (2x + 3) = \frac{\log_3 (2x + 3)}{\log_3 9} = \frac{1}{2} \log_3 (2x + 3)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_3 x = \frac{1}{2} \log_3 (2x + 3) &\iff 2 \log_3 x = \log_3 (2x + 3) \\[6pt]
&\iff \log_3 \left(x^{2}\right) = \log_3 (2x + 3) \\[6pt]
&\iff x^{2} = 2x + 3 \\[6pt]
&\iff x^{2} - 2x - 3 = 0 \\[6pt]
&\iff x = 3 \ \vee \ x = -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Solution: $x = 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The arguments $x$ and $2x + 3$ must be positive. For $x > 0$ the argument $2x + 3$ is positive as well, so the **domain restrictions** reduce to $x > 0$.

The base $9$ is the square of the base $3$, therefore by the **change of base** one has $\log_9 (2x + 3) = \dfrac{1}{2} \log_3 (2x + 3)$, since $\log_3 9 = 2$. Multiplying both sides by $2$ gives $2 \log_3 x = \log_3 (2x + 3)$.

On the domain $x > 0$ the property of the logarithm of a power applies, so $2 \log_3 x = \log_3 \left(x^{2}\right)$. The equation is now an equality between logarithms with the same base; the logarithmic function is injective, therefore the equation is equivalent to the equality of the arguments $x^{2} = 2x + 3$.

The roots of $x^{2} - 2x - 3 = 0$ are $x = 3$ and $x = -1$, because $3$ and $-1$ have sum $2$ and product $-3$. The value $x = -1$ does not satisfy the condition $x > 0$ and **is discarded**. For $x = 3$ the left-hand side equals $\log_3 3 = 1$ and the right-hand side equals $\log_9 9 = 1$, so $x = 3$ is the solution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\log_2 x - 3 \log_x 2 = 2 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\
x \neq 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\log_x 2 = \frac{1}{\log_2 x}
$$
$$
t = \log_2 x , \qquad t \neq 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
t - \frac{3}{t} = 2 &\iff t^{2} - 3 = 2t \\[6pt]
&\iff t^{2} - 2t - 3 = 0 \\[6pt]
&\iff t = 3 \ \vee \ t = -1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\log_2 x = 3 \iff x = 8 , \qquad \log_2 x = -1 \iff x = \frac{1}{2}
$$

Solutions: $x = 8 \ \vee \ x = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown also appears in the base of the second logarithm. A base must be positive and different from $1$, and the argument $x$ of the first logarithm must be positive; the **domain restrictions** are therefore $x > 0$ and $x \neq 1$.

By the **change of base** to base $2$ one obtains $\log_x 2 = \dfrac{\log_2 2}{\log_2 x} = \dfrac{1}{\log_2 x}$. With the auxiliary unknown $t = \log_2 x$ the equation becomes $t - \dfrac{3}{t} = 2$. The condition $x \neq 1$ is equivalent to $t \neq 0$, so both sides can be multiplied by $t$, which gives $t^{2} - 2t - 3 = 0$.

The roots are $t = 3$ and $t = -1$, both nonzero. From the definition of logarithm one obtains $x = 2^{3} = 8$ and $x = 2^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$; both values are positive and different from $1$, therefore they are accepted.

The check for $x = 8$ gives $3 - 3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = 2$; for $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ it gives $-1 - 3 \cdot (-1) = 2$. The equation therefore has **two solutions**.

</div>
