## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\sin x = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} .
$$

From the table of special values, an angle with sine $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ is $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The two families of solutions are
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi = \frac{3\pi}{4} + 2n\pi,
\qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$

Solutions: $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is elementary, so one first looks for an angle $\alpha$ with $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and the table of special values gives $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. On the unit circle there are two points with ordinate $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, symmetric with respect to the vertical axis: the one of angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and the one of the supplementary angle $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. Since the sine is periodic with period $2\pi$, one adds $2n\pi$ to each angle and obtains the two families of solutions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
2\cos x + 1 = 0 .
$$

Isolate the cosine:
$$
\cos x = -\frac{1}{2} .
$$
An angle with cosine $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $\alpha = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, since $\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = -\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The solutions are
$$
x = \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x = -\frac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi,
\qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$

Solutions: $x = \pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is not yet in elementary form, so one first isolates the trigonometric function by moving the constant term to the right-hand side and dividing by $2$. The value $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ is obtained with related angles starting from $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, since the supplementary angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ has the opposite cosine. On the unit circle there are two points with abscissa $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, symmetric with respect to the horizontal axis, and they correspond to the angles $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$; adding whole turns gives the two families, which are written compactly with the double sign.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\tan x = -1 .
$$

The equation makes sense for $\cos x \neq 0$, that is for $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$. An angle with tangent $-1$ is $\alpha = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, since
$$
\tan\left(-\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{\sin\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)}{\cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)} = \frac{-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = -1 .
$$
The tangent has period $\pi$, therefore the solutions are
$$
x = -\frac{\pi}{4} + n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z} .
$$

Solutions: $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$; the family can also be written $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + n\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Before solving, one checks the domain, since the tangent is defined only where the cosine does not vanish; the solutions found do not fall at those points, given that $-\dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ is never of the form $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$. The angle $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ has tangent $-1$ because sine and cosine have the same absolute value and opposite signs, therefore their ratio equals $-1$. Unlike sine and cosine, the tangent has period $\pi$, hence the general solution is obtained by adding $n\pi$ and a single family is enough. The form with $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ describes the same set, given that $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \pi$.

</div>
