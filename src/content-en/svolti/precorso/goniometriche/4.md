## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\sin^2 x - 3\sin x \cos x + 2\cos^2 x = 0 .
$$

The equation is homogeneous of second degree with $a = 1 \neq 0$. At the points with $\cos x = 0$ one has $\sin^2 x = 1$, therefore the left-hand side equals $1 \neq 0$ and those points are not solutions. Divide by $\cos^2 x$:
$$
\tan^2 x - 3\tan x + 2 = 0 .
$$
The discriminant is $\Delta = 9 - 8 = 1$ and the roots are
$$
\tan x = \frac{3 \pm 1}{2}, \qquad \tan x = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad \tan x = 2 .
$$
From $\tan x = 1$:
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{4} + n\pi .
$$
From $\tan x = 2$:
$$
x = \arctan 2 + n\pi .
$$

Solutions: $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + n\pi$ or $x = \arctan 2 + n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

All the terms have degree two in sine and cosine, so the equation is homogeneous. Before dividing, one checks that the points with $\cos x = 0$ are not solutions: at those points the Pythagorean identity gives $\sin^2 x = 1$, the left-hand side reduces to the coefficient of $\sin^2 x$ and does not vanish, hence dividing by $\cos^2 x$ does not lose solutions. After the division each term becomes a power of $\tan x$ and the equation is quadratic in the tangent. The value $2$ is not a special value, so the corresponding angle is denoted by $\arctan 2$, the angle between $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ whose tangent equals $2$; the period $\pi$ of the tangent gives a single family for each root.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\sin x + \cos x = 1 .
$$

With the auxiliary angle method: $a = b = 1$, therefore $R = \sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$ and the angle $\varphi$ satisfies the system
$$
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
therefore $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The equation becomes
$$
\sqrt{2}\,\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = 1,
\qquad
\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} .
$$
The alternatives are
$$
x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{4} + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{3\pi}{4} + 2n\pi ,
$$
which give
$$
x = 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi .
$$

Solutions: $x = 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is linear in sine and cosine, so one rewrites the left-hand side as a single sine with the auxiliary angle method. The modulus $R$ equals $\sqrt{2}$, and the angle $\varphi$ has cosine and sine both equal to $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, therefore $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. After dividing by $\sqrt{2}$, where $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, the equation is elementary in the unknown $x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, and the two families are obtained by subtracting $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ from the solutions. A check: for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ one has $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} + \cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1 + 0 = 1$, therefore the equation is satisfied; for $x = 0$ one has $0 + 1 = 1$, also satisfied.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\sqrt{3}\,\sin x - \cos x = \sqrt{3} .
$$

Here $a = \sqrt{3}$ and $b = -1$, therefore $R = \sqrt{3 + 1} = 2$ and the angle $\varphi$ satisfies the system
$$
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = -\dfrac{1}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
therefore $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The equation becomes
$$
2\sin\left( x - \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = \sqrt{3},
\qquad
\sin\left( x - \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} .
$$
The alternatives are
$$
x - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2n\pi ,
$$
which give
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x = \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi .
$$

Solutions: $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $\cos x$ is negative, so $\sin\varphi$ is negative as well and the auxiliary angle lies in the fourth quadrant: from the system it follows that $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Since $|\sqrt{3}| \leq R = 2$, the resulting elementary equation has solutions. The angles with sine $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ are $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and its supplement $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, and one concludes by adding $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to both. A check: for $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ one has $\sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} - \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$, therefore the equation is satisfied.

</div>
