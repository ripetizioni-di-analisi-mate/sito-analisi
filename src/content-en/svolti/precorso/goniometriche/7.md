## Exercise 1

Solve on $[0, 2\pi)$ the inequality
$$
2\cos^2 x - \cos x - 1 > 0 .
$$

$$
t = \cos x: \qquad 2t^2 - t - 1 > 0
$$
$$
\Delta = (-1)^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot (-1) = 1 + 8 = 9,
\qquad
t = \frac{1 \pm 3}{4},
\qquad
t_1 = -\frac{1}{2}, \quad t_2 = 1
$$
$$
2t^2 - t - 1 > 0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
t < -\frac{1}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad t > 1
$$
$$
\cos x > 1: \ \text{no solution}, \qquad \cos x < -\frac{1}{2}: \ \frac{2\pi}{3} < x < \frac{4\pi}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality contains only $\cos x$ and its square, so with the **substitution** $t = \cos x$ it becomes a quadratic algebraic inequality. The trinomial $2t^2 - t - 1$ has discriminant $9$ and roots $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $1$. The coefficient of $t^2$ is positive, therefore the trinomial is positive for the values of $t$ *outside* the interval between the roots.

We then return to the variable $x$. The condition $\cos x > 1$ is never satisfied, because the cosine does not exceed $1$; so this alternative gives no solutions. What remains is $\cos x < -\dfrac{1}{2}$, which is an **elementary inequality**. The associated equation $\cos x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ has on $[0, 2\pi)$ the solutions $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} = 2\pi - \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. The points of the circle with abscissa less than $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the arc containing the point of angle $\pi$, where the cosine equals $-1$.

The inequality is strict, so the endpoints are excluded, and the solutions are $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} < x < \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$. A check at an interior point: for $x = \pi$ we have $2 \cdot 1 - (-1) - 1 = 2 > 0$. A check at an exterior point: for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the left-hand side equals $-1$, which is negative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve on $[0, 2\pi)$ the inequality
$$
2\sin^2 x + 3\sin x - 2 \leq 0 .
$$

$$
t = \sin x: \qquad 2t^2 + 3t - 2 \leq 0
$$
$$
\Delta = 3^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot (-2) = 9 + 16 = 25,
\qquad
t = \frac{-3 \pm 5}{4},
\qquad
t_1 = -2, \quad t_2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
2t^2 + 3t - 2 \leq 0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
-2 \leq t \leq \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\sin x \geq -2 \\[4pt]
\sin x \leq \dfrac{1}{2}
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\sin x \leq \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
0 \leq x \leq \frac{\pi}{6} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{5\pi}{6} \leq x < 2\pi
$$

::grafico{f="sin(x);0.5" xmin=-0.3 xmax=6.6 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 area="0.5;sin(x);0;0.5236|0.5;sin(x);2.618;6.2832" punti="0.5236,0.5,pieno,π/6;2.618,0.5,pieno,5π/6" titolo="On $[0, 2\pi)$ the graph of $y = \sin x$ lies below the line $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$, or touches it, for $0 \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and for $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} \leq x < 2\pi$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

With the **substitution** $t = \sin x$ we obtain a quadratic inequality in $t$. The trinomial has discriminant $25$ and roots $-2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$; the coefficient of $t^2$ is positive, so the trinomial is negative or zero for the values of $t$ *between* the roots, endpoints included.

The double inequality $-2 \leq \sin x \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$ consists of two conditions to be satisfied together, therefore it is written as a system. The first condition, $\sin x \geq -2$, holds for every $x$, because the sine is never less than $-1$. The system is therefore equivalent to the single elementary inequality $\sin x \leq \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The associated equation $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ has on $[0, 2\pi)$ the solutions $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The points of the circle with ordinate greater than $\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the upper arc between these two angles; the solutions are the angles of the remaining arc, the one containing the point of angle $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$. On $[0, 2\pi)$ this arc splits into two intervals, because it contains the point of angle $0$. The inequality is non-strict, so $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ are **included**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve on $[0, 2\pi)$ the inequality
$$
\frac{2\sin x - 1}{\cos x} \geq 0 .
$$

$$
\text{domain restriction: } \cos x \neq 0 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x \neq \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad x \neq \frac{3\pi}{2}
$$
$$
N(x) = 2\sin x - 1 \geq 0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\sin x \geq \frac{1}{2}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\frac{\pi}{6} \leq x \leq \frac{5\pi}{6}
$$
$$
D(x) = \cos x > 0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
0 \leq x < \frac{\pi}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{3\pi}{2} < x < 2\pi
$$

| $x$ | $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$ | $\left[\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ | $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right]$ | $\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right)$ | $\left(\dfrac{3\pi}{2}, 2\pi\right)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $N(x)$ | $-$ | $+$ or $0$ | $+$ or $0$ | $-$ | $-$ |
| $D(x)$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $\dfrac{N(x)}{D(x)}$ | $-$ | $+$ or $0$ | $-$ or $0$ | $+$ | $-$ |

$$
\frac{\pi}{6} \leq x < \frac{\pi}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{5\pi}{6} \leq x < \frac{3\pi}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is defined only where the denominator does not vanish, so we exclude $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, the angles of $[0, 2\pi)$ with zero cosine. Then we study separately the sign of the numerator and that of the denominator, each with an **elementary inequality**.

The numerator is positive or zero when $\sin x \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, on the upper arc between $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, endpoints included because there the fraction equals $0$ and the inequality is non-strict. The denominator is positive when the point of the circle lies to the right of the vertical axis, that is, in the first and fourth quadrants.

By the **rule of signs** the fraction is positive where numerator and denominator have the same sign. In the table this happens in $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, where both are positive, and in $\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right)$, where both are negative. To these intervals we add the zeros of the numerator, $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, where the denominator does not vanish. The endpoints $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ remain excluded by the domain restriction.

</div>
