## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{\sin 2x}{1 - \cos x} = 0 .
$$

$$
\text{domain restriction: } 1 - \cos x \neq 0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\cos x \neq 1
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
x \neq 2n\pi
$$
$$
\sin 2x = 0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
2x = n\pi
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
x = \frac{n\pi}{2}
$$
$$
x = \frac{n\pi}{2}: \qquad \ldots,\ -\frac{\pi}{2},\ \underbrace{0}_{\text{excluded}},\ \frac{\pi}{2},\ \pi,\ \frac{3\pi}{2},\ \underbrace{2\pi}_{\text{excluded}},\ \ldots
$$
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad x = \pi + 2n\pi, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" uguale=1 xmin=-1.4 xmax=1.4 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 punti="1,0,vuoto,0;0,1,pieno,π/2;-1,0,pieno,π;0,-1,pieno,3π/2" titolo="The angles $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$ determine four points of the circle; the point of angle $0$, hollow, is excluded because there $\cos x = 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is **rational**, so first of all we require that the denominator not vanish. The condition $1 - \cos x \neq 0$ is equivalent to $\cos x \neq 1$, and the cosine equals $1$ only at the angles $2n\pi$, therefore these angles are excluded.

A fraction with nonzero denominator is zero if and only if the numerator is zero. The equation $\sin 2x = 0$ is elementary in the unknown $2x$, and the sine vanishes at the angles $n\pi$, so $2x = n\pi$. Dividing by $2$ we obtain $x = \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$. On the unit circle these angles determine the four points of angles $0$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\pi$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, repeated at every turn.

The point of angle $0$ does not satisfy the domain restriction, so the values $x = 2n\pi$ are discarded. What remains are the points of angles $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, which are half a turn apart and form the family $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$, and the point of angle $\pi$, which forms the family $x = \pi + 2n\pi$. For $x = \pi$ the denominator equals $1 - (-1) = 2$, which is nonzero, and the numerator equals $\sin 2\pi = 0$, so $\pi$ is a **solution**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\tan x = 2\sin x .
$$

$$
\text{domain restriction: } \cos x \neq 0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
x \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} &= 2\sin x \\[6pt]
\sin x &= 2\sin x \cos x \\[6pt]
\sin x - 2\sin x \cos x &= 0 \\[6pt]
\sin x\,(1 - 2\cos x) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\sin x = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = n\pi,
\qquad\qquad
\cos x = \frac{1}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \pm\frac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tangent is defined only where the cosine does not vanish, so the **domain restriction** excludes the angles $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$. With the definition $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$ the equation contains only sine and cosine. Under the condition $\cos x \neq 0$ both sides can be multiplied by $\cos x$, and we obtain an equivalent equation without denominator.

Moving everything to the left-hand side we factor out $\sin x$. Dividing by $\sin x$ would lose the angles where the sine vanishes, which are solutions; by factoring instead we apply the **zero product property**. From $\sin x = 0$ it follows that $x = n\pi$. From $1 - 2\cos x = 0$ it follows that $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which has the solutions $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$.

Finally we check the domain restriction. At the angles $n\pi$ the cosine equals $\pm 1$, and at the angles $\pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ it equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$; in both cases it is nonzero, so **all** the solutions found are acceptable. A check: for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ we have $\tan\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \sqrt{3}$ and $2\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = 2 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\log_2\left(2\sin x\right) + \log_2\left(\cos x\right) = -1 .
$$

$$
\text{domain restrictions: }
\begin{cases}
2\sin x > 0 \\[4pt]
\cos x > 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
2n\pi < x < \frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2\left(2\sin x \cos x\right) &= -1 \\[6pt]
\log_2\left(\sin 2x\right) &= -1 \\[6pt]
\sin 2x &= 2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
2x = \frac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad 2x = \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi
$$
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{12} + n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{5\pi}{12} + n\pi
$$
$$
\text{in the first quadrant: } \quad x = \frac{\pi}{12} + 2n\pi \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{5\pi}{12} + 2n\pi
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" uguale=1 xmin=-1.4 xmax=1.4 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 punti="0.9659,0.2588,pieno,π/12;0.2588,0.9659,pieno,5π/12;-0.9659,-0.2588,vuoto,13π/12;-0.2588,-0.9659,vuoto,17π/12" titolo="The solutions of $\sin 2x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ over one turn: only $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$, in the first quadrant, are acceptable; $\dfrac{13\pi}{12}$ and $\dfrac{17\pi}{12}$, hollow, lie in the third"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The logarithm is defined only for positive arguments, so $\sin x > 0$ and $\cos x > 0$ must hold *together*, and the two conditions are written as a system. Sine and cosine are both positive only in the first quadrant, therefore the solutions are sought among the angles $2n\pi < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$.

Under these conditions the sum of two logarithms with the same base is the **logarithm of the product**. The product $2\sin x \cos x$ equals $\sin 2x$ by the **double-angle formula** for the sine. By the definition of logarithm, $\log_2 (\sin 2x) = -1$ is equivalent to $\sin 2x = 2^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The equation $\sin 2x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is elementary in the unknown $2x$. Dividing the whole right-hand side by $2$, including $2n\pi$, we obtain two families with step $\pi$: over one turn the angles $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$, $\dfrac{13\pi}{12}$ and $\dfrac{17\pi}{12}$. The last two exceed $\pi$ and are less than $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, so they lie in the third quadrant, where sine and cosine are negative, and must be **discarded**. What remains is $x = \dfrac{\pi}{12} + 2n\pi$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{12} + 2n\pi$, that is, the families with step $\pi$ restricted to even $n$.

</div>
