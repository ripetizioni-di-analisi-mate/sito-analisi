## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\sin 3x = \sin x .
$$

Two angles have the same sine when they coincide up to full turns or are supplementary up to full turns:
$$
3x = x + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
3x = \pi - x + 2n\pi .
$$
From the first:
$$
2x = 2n\pi, \qquad x = n\pi .
$$
From the second:
$$
4x = \pi + 2n\pi, \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{n\pi}{2} .
$$

Solutions: $x = n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation sets two sines equal, so one applies the **two alternatives** from the theory: the arguments **coincide** up to an integer number of turns, or they are **supplementary** up to full turns. Each alternative is a linear equation in $x$ and is solved by isolating $x$; the final division also involves the term $2n\pi$, hence in the second family the step between two consecutive solutions reduces to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. A check on one value: for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ one has $\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, therefore the equality is satisfied.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\cos\left( 2x - \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = \cos x .
$$

Two angles have the same cosine when they coincide or are opposite, up to full turns:
$$
2x - \frac{\pi}{3} = x + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
2x - \frac{\pi}{3} = -x + 2n\pi .
$$
From the first:
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi .
$$
From the second:
$$
3x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi, \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{9} + \frac{2n\pi}{3} .
$$

Solutions: $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{9} + \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

For the cosine the two alternatives are **equal** or **opposite** arguments, always up to full turns, because on the unit circle the points with the same abscissa are symmetric with respect to the horizontal axis. The first alternative gives $x$ immediately by moving $x$ to the left and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to the right; the second moves $-x$ to the left, so one divides the **whole right-hand side** by $3$, including the term with $n$. A check on the first family: for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ the argument on the left equals $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, equal to the argument on the right, therefore the equality is satisfied.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\sin 2x = \cos x .
$$

With related angles the cosine is written as a sine:
$$
\cos x = \sin\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - x \right) ,
$$
therefore the equation becomes $\sin 2x = \sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} - x \right)$ and the alternatives are
$$
2x = \frac{\pi}{2} - x + 2n\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
2x = \pi - \left( \frac{\pi}{2} - x \right) + 2n\pi .
$$
From the first:
$$
3x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi, \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{2n\pi}{3} .
$$
From the second:
$$
2x = \frac{\pi}{2} + x + 2n\pi, \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi .
$$

Solutions: $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{2n\pi}{3}$ or $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2n\pi$, with $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation compares a sine with a cosine, so it is *not* yet of the form treated in the theory. With the **related angle** $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right) = \cos x$ the right-hand side becomes a sine, and from there one proceeds with the usual two alternatives. In the second alternative the argument $\pi - \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x\right)$ simplifies to $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + x$, and the resulting equation is linear in $x$.

The same result is obtained another way: with the **double-angle formula** $\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x$ the equation becomes $\cos x\,(2\sin x - 1) = 0$, which gives $\cos x = 0$, that is $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$, or $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$ or $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2n\pi$. Over one turn both approaches give the angles $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, therefore the two solution sets **coincide**.

</div>
