## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
|2x - 3| = 5 .
$$

Since $5 > 0$, the equation is equivalent to the union
$$
2x - 3 = 5 \ \vee \ 2x - 3 = -5 .
$$
$$
2x = 8 \ \vee \ 2x = -2
$$
$$
x = 4 \ \vee \ x = -1
$$

The solutions are $x = -1$ and $x = 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is the constant $5$, which is positive, so the **rule** for $|A| = k$ with $k > 0$ applies: the expression $2x - 3$ equals $5$ or $-5$. Each of the two equations is linear and is solved by moving the constant term to the right and dividing by $2$. Checking by substitution confirms the result: for $x = 4$ we have $|8 - 3| = 5$, and for $x = -1$ we have $|-2 - 3| = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equations
$$
|4x - 1| = 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad |3x + 2| = -5 .
$$

First equation: the only number with absolute value zero is $0$, so
$$
4x - 1 = 0
$$
$$
x = \frac{1}{4}
$$

Second equation: for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$
|3x + 2| \geq 0 > -5 ,
$$
so the equation has **no solutions**.

The first equation has solution $x = \dfrac{1}{4}$; the second has **no solutions**.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first equation the right-hand side is $0$, so the absolute value vanishes *only* where its argument vanishes, and the equation becomes $4x - 1 = 0$. In the second the right-hand side is negative, while the left-hand side is an absolute value and is therefore never less than $0$; since no value of $x$ makes the two sides equal, the solution set is **empty**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
|x - 1| = |2x + 3| .
$$

Two numbers have the same absolute value when they are **equal or opposite**, so
$$
x - 1 = 2x + 3 \ \vee \ x - 1 = -(2x + 3) .
$$
$$
-x = 4 \ \vee \ 3x = -2
$$
$$
x = -4 \ \vee \ x = -\frac{2}{3}
$$

The solutions are $x = -4$ and $x = -\dfrac{2}{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Both sides are absolute values, so neither of them can be negative and **no acceptability condition** is needed: the equation is equivalent to the union of the two cases, equal arguments or opposite arguments. In the second case the minus sign must be distributed over the *whole* second argument, therefore $x - 1 = -2x - 3$. Checking by substitution confirms the result: for $x = -4$ both sides equal $|-5| = 5$, and for $x = -\dfrac{2}{3}$ both equal $\dfrac{5}{3}$.

</div>
