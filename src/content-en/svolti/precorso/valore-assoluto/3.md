## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
|x - 3| < 2 .
$$

Since $2 > 0$, the inequality is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x - 3 < 2 \\
x - 3 > -2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < 5 \\
x > 1
\end{cases}
$$

The solution is $1 < x < 5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is the positive constant $2$, so the inequality is equivalent to the **double inequality** $-2 < x - 3 < 2$, written as the system of the two conditions. Adding $3$ to all sides gives the interval $1 < x < 5$. The **geometric reading** confirms the result, since $|x - 3| < 2$ asks for the points at distance less than $2$ from $3$, and these form the **open interval** with endpoints $1$ and $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
|2x + 5| \leq 3 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 5 \leq 3 \\
2x + 5 \geq -3
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
2x \leq -2 \\
2x \geq -8
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \leq -1 \\
x \geq -4
\end{cases}
$$

The solution is $-4 \leq x \leq -1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is non-strict, so the two conditions of the system are also non-strict and the **endpoints of the interval belong to the solution**. In each condition the $5$ is moved to the right and we divide by $2$, which is positive, therefore the direction of the inequalities does *not* change. Checking at the endpoints confirms the result: for $x = -4$ we have $|-3| = 3 \leq 3$, and for $x = -1$ we have $|3| = 3 \leq 3$; for an exterior point such as $x = 0$, instead, we have $|5| = 5 > 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
|x^2 - 3| \leq 1 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 - 3 \leq 1 \\
x^2 - 3 \geq -1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x^2 \leq 4 \\
x^2 \geq 2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
-2 \leq x \leq 2 \\
x \leq -\sqrt{2} \ \vee \ x \geq \sqrt{2}
\end{cases}
$$

The solution is $-2 \leq x \leq -\sqrt{2} \ \vee \ \sqrt{2} \leq x \leq 2$.

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-3);1" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-1 ymax=5.5 area="1;abs(x^2-3);-2;-1.4142|1;abs(x^2-3);1.4142;2" punti="-2,1,pieno;-1.4142,1,pieno;1.4142,1,pieno;2,1,pieno" titolo="$y = |x^2 - 3|$ lies below the line $y = 1$ or meets it in the two symmetric intervals $-2 \leq x \leq -\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{2} \leq x \leq 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument of the absolute value is quadratic, but the rule is the same: the inequality is equivalent to the system of the two conditions $x^2 - 3 \leq 1$ and $x^2 - 3 \geq -1$. The condition $x^2 \leq 4$ is satisfied on the interval $-2 \leq x \leq 2$, while the condition $x^2 \geq 2$ is satisfied **outside** the open interval with endpoints $-\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{2}$. The two conditions must hold together, so we **intersect** the interval with the union of half-lines, and the two symmetric intervals shown remain. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$ we have $x^2 = \dfrac{9}{4}$ and $\left| \dfrac{9}{4} - 3 \right| = \dfrac{3}{4} \leq 1$, while for $x = 0$ we have $|-3| = 3 > 1$.

</div>
