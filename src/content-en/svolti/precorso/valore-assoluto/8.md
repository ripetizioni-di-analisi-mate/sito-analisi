## Exercise 1

Sketch the graph of the function
$$
f(x) = \left|x^{2} - 4x + 3\right| .
$$

$$
x^{2} - 4x + 3 = (x - 1)(x - 3)
$$
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^{2} - 4x + 3 & \text{if } x \leq 1 \ \vee \ x \geq 3 \\
-x^{2} + 4x - 3 & \text{if } 1 < x < 3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
x^{2} - 4x + 3 \ \text{has vertex at } (2, -1) , \qquad f(2) = |-1| = 1
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-4*x+3);x^2-4*x+3" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=6 punti="1,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;2,1,pieno;2,-1,vuoto;0,3,pieno" legenda="y = |x^2 - 4x + 3|;y = x^2 - 4x + 3" titolo="The graph of $y = \left|x^{2} - 4x + 3\right|$ is obtained from the parabola, dashed, by reflecting across the $x$-axis the arc with $1 < x < 3$; the vertex $(2, -1)$ goes to $(2, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **definition** of absolute value, $f(x)$ coincides with the argument where the argument is non-negative and with its opposite where it is negative. We therefore study the sign of $x^{2} - 4x + 3$, which factors as $(x - 1)(x - 3)$; so the trinomial is non-negative for $x \leq 1$ or $x \geq 3$ and negative for $1 < x < 3$.

The graph of the argument is a parabola opening upward. The vertex has x-coordinate $\dfrac{1 + 3}{2} = 2$, the mean of the roots, and y-coordinate $4 - 8 + 3 = -1$. The parabola crosses the $y$-axis at the point $(0, 3)$.

Where the argument is non-negative the graph of $f$ coincides with the parabola. Where the argument is negative, $f(x) = -\left(x^{2} - 4x + 3\right)$, and the graph of $y = -g(x)$ is the reflection of that of $y = g(x)$ across the $x$-axis. The arc of the parabola that lies below the $x$-axis is therefore **reflected** above the axis, and the vertex $(2, -1)$ becomes the point $(2, 1)$.

The graph of $f$ never goes below the $x$-axis, because an absolute value is not negative. At the points $x = 1$ and $x = 3$ the function equals $0$, and at those points the two pieces join, forming corners.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Sketch the graph of the function
$$
g(x) = x^{2} - 4|x| + 3 .
$$

$$
g(-x) = (-x)^{2} - 4|-x| + 3 = x^{2} - 4|x| + 3 = g(x)
$$
$$
x \geq 0 : \quad g(x) = x^{2} - 4x + 3 = (x - 1)(x - 3)
$$
$$
g(x) = 0 \iff |x| = 1 \ \vee \ |x| = 3 \iff x = \pm 1 \ \vee \ x = \pm 3
$$
$$
g(2) = g(-2) = -1 , \qquad g(0) = 3
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-4*abs(x)+3" xmin=-4.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-2 ymax=6 punti="-3,0,pieno;-1,0,pieno;1,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;-2,-1,pieno;2,-1,pieno;0,3,pieno" legenda="y = x^2 - 4|x| + 3" titolo="The graph of $y = x^{2} - 4|x| + 3$ is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis: for $x \geq 0$ it is the parabola $y = x^{2} - 4x + 3$, for $x < 0$ it is its mirror image"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value is applied to the variable $x$ alone, and the expression $x^{2} - 4|x| + 3$ can take negative values. Since $|-x| = |x|$ and $(-x)^{2} = x^{2}$, replacing $x$ with $-x$ does not change the function, therefore $g$ is an **even** function and its graph is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis. It therefore suffices to draw it for $x \geq 0$ and then reflect the piece obtained.

For $x \geq 0$ we have $|x| = x$, therefore $g(x) = x^{2} - 4x + 3$, the same parabola as in the previous exercise, which crosses the $x$-axis at $1$ and at $3$ and has its vertex at $(2, -1)$. The piece with $x \geq 0$ starts from the point $(0, 3)$.

The piece with $x < 0$ is the reflection across the $y$-axis, therefore it passes through $(-1, 0)$, $(-3, 0)$ and has its minimum at $(-2, -1)$. At the point $(0, 3)$ the two pieces join with a corner.

The two exercises show two different transformations. In $\left|x^{2} - 4x + 3\right|$ the absolute value is applied to the values of the function, and the graph is obtained by reflecting the negative part across the $x$-axis; in $x^{2} - 4|x| + 3$ the absolute value is applied to the variable, and the graph is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis. Indeed $g(2) = -1$ is negative, while the function of exercise 1 takes no negative values.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Sketch the graph of the function
$$
h(x) = |x - 1| + |x + 1|
$$
and determine its minimum value.

$$
h(x) =
\begin{cases}
(1 - x) + (-x - 1) = -2x & \text{if } x < -1 \\
(1 - x) + (x + 1) = 2 & \text{if } -1 \leq x < 1 \\
(x - 1) + (x + 1) = 2x & \text{if } x \geq 1
\end{cases}
$$

Minimum value: $2$, attained for $-1 \leq x \leq 1$.

::grafico{f="abs(x-1)+abs(x+1)" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=7 punti="-1,2,pieno;1,2,pieno" legenda="y = |x - 1| + |x + 1|" titolo="The graph of $y = |x - 1| + |x + 1|$ consists of two half-lines and a horizontal segment: the minimum value $2$ is attained for $-1 \leq x \leq 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The arguments $x - 1$ and $x + 1$ vanish at $1$ and at $-1$, therefore we use the **analysis by intervals** with the three intervals $x < -1$, $-1 \leq x < 1$ and $x \geq 1$. In each interval the signs of the arguments are constant and the absolute values are written without bars.

For $x < -1$ both arguments are negative, so $|x - 1| = 1 - x$ and $|x + 1| = -x - 1$, and the sum equals $-2x$. For $-1 \leq x < 1$ the first argument is negative and the second is non-negative; the terms in $x$ cancel and the sum equals the constant $2$. For $x \geq 1$ both arguments are non-negative and the sum equals $2x$.

The graph therefore consists of the half-line $y = -2x$, decreasing, of the horizontal segment $y = 2$ and of the half-line $y = 2x$, increasing. The pieces join at the points $(-1, 2)$ and $(1, 2)$, because $-2 \cdot (-1) = 2$ and $2 \cdot 1 = 2$. The minimum value is therefore $2$, attained at all points of the interval $[-1, 1]$.

The result has an interpretation in terms of **distances**. The number $|x - 1| + |x + 1|$ is the sum of the distances of $x$ from the points $1$ and $-1$. For a point between $-1$ and $1$ the sum equals the distance between $-1$ and $1$, that is, $2$; for a point outside the interval the sum is greater than $2$.

</div>
