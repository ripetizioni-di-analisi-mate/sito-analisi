## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
|3x - 1| > 5 .
$$

Since $5 > 0$, the inequality is equivalent to the union
$$
3x - 1 < -5 \ \vee \ 3x - 1 > 5 .
$$
$$
3x < -4 \ \vee \ 3x > 6
$$
$$
x < -\frac{4}{3} \ \vee \ x > 2
$$

The solution is $x < -\dfrac{4}{3} \ \vee \ x > 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality requires the expression $3x - 1$ to be at distance greater than $5$ from the origin, so the expression is less than $-5$ or greater than $5$: **two alternative conditions**, *not* a system. Each linear inequality is solved by dividing by $3$, which is positive, therefore the direction does not change. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 3$ we have $|8| = 8 > 5$, while for $x = 0$ we have $|-1| = 1 < 5$, and indeed $0$ does not belong to the solution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
|x + 2| < 3x - 2 .
$$

The right-hand side contains the variable, so the inequality is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2 < 3x - 2 \\
x + 2 > -(3x - 2)
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
-2x < -4 \\
4x > 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x > 2 \\
x > 0
\end{cases}
$$

The solution is $x > 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

With an expression on the right-hand side the solving system is the same as in the case with a constant: the argument of the absolute value must lie between $-(3x - 2)$ and $3x - 2$. In the first condition both sides are divided by $-2$, so the direction of the inequality is **reversed** and we obtain $x > 2$; the second condition gives $x > 0$. The two conditions hold together for $x > 2$, which is the **more restrictive** one. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 3$ we have $|5| = 5 < 7$, while for $x = 2$ we have $|4| = 4$, which is not less than $4$, so the endpoint is **excluded**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
|x - 3| > 2x .
$$

$$
x - 3 > 2x \ \vee \ x - 3 < -2x
$$
$$
-x > 3 \ \vee \ 3x < 3
$$
$$
x < -3 \ \vee \ x < 1
$$

The **union** of the two half-lines is the solution $x < 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The form $|A| > B$ is solved with the union of the two conditions $A > B$ and $A < -B$ also when $B$ contains the variable, because at the points where $2x$ is negative one of the two conditions is **true anyway**. The first condition gives $x < -3$ and the second gives $x < 1$; the half-line $x < -3$ is contained in the half-line $x < 1$, so the union **coincides** with $x < 1$. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 0$ we have $|-3| = 3 > 0$; for $x = 1$ we have $|-2| = 2$, which is not greater than $2$; for $x = 2$ we have $|-1| = 1 < 4$, and indeed $2$ does not belong to the solution.

</div>
