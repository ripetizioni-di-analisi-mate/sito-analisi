## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
|x - 1| + |x + 2| = 5 .
$$

The arguments vanish at $x = 1$ and at $x = -2$, so there are three **intervals**.

**First interval.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x < -2 \\
(1 - x) + (-x - 2) = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < -2 \\
-2x - 1 = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < -2 \\
x = -3
\end{cases}
$$
The solution $x = -3$ is **admissible**.

**Second interval.**
$$
\begin{cases}
-2 \leq x < 1 \\
(1 - x) + (x + 2) = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
-2 \leq x < 1 \\
3 = 5
\end{cases}
$$
The equation $3 = 5$ is false, so the interval **gives no solutions**.

**Third interval.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
(x - 1) + (x + 2) = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
2x + 1 = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
x = 2
\end{cases}
$$
The solution $x = 2$ is **admissible**.

The solutions are $x = -3$ and $x = 2$.

::grafico{f="abs(x-1)+abs(x+2);5" xmin=-5 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=8 punti="-3,5,pieno;2,5,pieno" titolo="$y = |x - 1| + |x + 2|$ equals $3$ in the interval $-2 \leq x \leq 1$; the line $y = 5$ meets it at $x = -3$ and at $x = 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The arguments $x - 1$ and $x + 2$ vanish at different points, so their signs must be studied *together* and the line is divided into **three intervals**. In the first interval both arguments are negative, therefore each absolute value is replaced by the **opposite of its argument**; in the second $x + 2$ is nonnegative and $x - 1$ is negative; in the third both are nonnegative. In each interval the rewritten equation is solved together with the interval condition, and the value found is accepted *only if* it satisfies that condition. Checking by substitution confirms the result: for $x = -3$ we have $|-4| + |-1| = 4 + 1 = 5$, and for $x = 2$ we have $|1| + |4| = 1 + 4 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
|x| + |x - 3| \leq 4 .
$$

The arguments vanish at $x = 0$ and at $x = 3$, so there are three **intervals**.

**First interval.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
-x + (3 - x) \leq 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
-2x \leq 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
x \geq -\dfrac{1}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
The interval contributes $-\dfrac{1}{2} \leq x < 0$.

**Second interval.**
$$
\begin{cases}
0 \leq x < 3 \\
x + (3 - x) \leq 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
0 \leq x < 3 \\
3 \leq 4
\end{cases}
$$
The inequality $3 \leq 4$ is true for every $x$, so the interval contributes **in full** with $0 \leq x < 3$.

**Third interval.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
x + (x - 3) \leq 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
2x \leq 7
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
x \leq \dfrac{7}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
The interval contributes $3 \leq x \leq \dfrac{7}{2}$.

The **union** of the three contributions is the solution $-\dfrac{1}{2} \leq x \leq \dfrac{7}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The **analysis by intervals** applies to inequalities as it does to equations: in each interval the left-hand side is rewritten without absolute values and the system with the interval condition is solved. In the first interval dividing by $-2$ **reverses the direction** of the inequality. In the middle interval the left-hand side is *constantly* equal to $3$, which is less than $4$, therefore the **whole interval** belongs to the solution. The three contributions are consecutive intervals and their union is a single closed interval. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = \dfrac{7}{2}$ we have $\dfrac{7}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 4 \leq 4$, while for $x = -1$ we have $1 + 4 = 5 > 4$, and indeed $-1$ does *not* belong to the solution set.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
|2x - 1| > |x + 1| .
$$

Both sides are nonnegative, so the comparison is equivalent to the **comparison between the squares**:
$$
(2x - 1)^2 > (x + 1)^2
$$
$$
4x^2 - 4x + 1 > x^2 + 2x + 1
$$
$$
3x^2 - 6x > 0
$$
$$
3x(x - 2) > 0
$$
$$
x < 0 \ \vee \ x > 2
$$

The solution is $x < 0 \ \vee \ x > 2$.

::grafico{f="abs(2*x-1);abs(x+1)" xmin=-3 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=7 punti="0,1,vuoto;2,3,vuoto" titolo="$y = |2x - 1|$ lies above $y = |x + 1|$ (dashed) for $x < 0$ and for $x > 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

When both sides are absolute values, analysis by intervals is still possible, but the property $|a|^2 = a^2$ offers a shorter route: two *nonnegative* numbers are in the **same order as their squares**, so the inequality is equivalent to $(2x - 1)^2 > (x + 1)^2$. Expanding the squares and moving everything to the left gives the **quadratic inequality** $3x^2 - 6x > 0$, which factors as $3x(x - 2) > 0$; the product is positive when the two factors have the same sign, that is, **outside the interval between the roots** $0$ and $2$. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 3$ we have $|5| = 5 > 4 = |4|$, while for $x = 1$ we have $|1| = 1 < 2 = |2|$, and indeed $1$ does not belong to the solution set.

</div>
