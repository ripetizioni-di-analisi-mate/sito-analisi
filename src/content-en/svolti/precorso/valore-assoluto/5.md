## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
|x - 1| + |x + 2| = 5 .
$$

The arguments vanish at $x = 1$ and at $x = -2$, so there are three regions.

**First region.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x < -2 \\
(1 - x) + (-x - 2) = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < -2 \\
-2x - 1 = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < -2 \\
x = -3
\end{cases}
$$
The solution $x = -3$ is acceptable.

**Second region.**
$$
\begin{cases}
-2 \leq x < 1 \\
(1 - x) + (x + 2) = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
-2 \leq x < 1 \\
3 = 5
\end{cases}
$$
The equation $3 = 5$ is false, so the region gives no solutions.

**Third region.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
(x - 1) + (x + 2) = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
2x + 1 = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
x = 2
\end{cases}
$$
The solution $x = 2$ is acceptable.

The solutions are $x = -3$ and $x = 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The arguments $x - 1$ and $x + 2$ vanish at different points, so their signs must be studied together and the line is divided into three regions. In the first region both arguments are negative, therefore each absolute value is replaced by the opposite of its argument; in the second $x + 2$ is non-negative and $x - 1$ is negative; in the third both are non-negative. In each region the rewritten equation is solved together with the region condition, and the value found is accepted only if it satisfies that condition. Checking by substitution confirms the result: for $x = -3$ we have $|-4| + |-1| = 4 + 1 = 5$, and for $x = 2$ we have $|1| + |4| = 1 + 4 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
|x| + |x - 3| \leq 4 .
$$

The arguments vanish at $x = 0$ and at $x = 3$, so there are three regions.

**First region.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
-x + (3 - x) \leq 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
-2x \leq 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
x \geq -\dfrac{1}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
The region contributes $-\dfrac{1}{2} \leq x < 0$.

**Second region.**
$$
\begin{cases}
0 \leq x < 3 \\
x + (3 - x) \leq 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
0 \leq x < 3 \\
3 \leq 4
\end{cases}
$$
The inequality $3 \leq 4$ is true for every $x$, so the region contributes in full with $0 \leq x < 3$.

**Third region.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
x + (x - 3) \leq 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
2x \leq 7
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
x \leq \dfrac{7}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
The region contributes $3 \leq x \leq \dfrac{7}{2}$.

The union of the three contributions is the solution $-\dfrac{1}{2} \leq x \leq \dfrac{7}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The procedure by regions applies to inequalities as it does to equations: in each region the left-hand side is rewritten without absolute values and the system with the region condition is solved. In the first region dividing by $-2$ reverses the direction of the inequality. In the middle region the left-hand side is constantly equal to $3$, which is less than $4$, therefore the whole region belongs to the solution. The three contributions are consecutive intervals and their union is a single closed interval. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = \dfrac{7}{2}$ we have $\dfrac{7}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 4 \leq 4$, while for $x = -1$ we have $1 + 4 = 5 > 4$, and indeed $-1$ does not belong to the solution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
|2x - 1| > |x + 1| .
$$

Both sides are non-negative, so the comparison is equivalent to the comparison between the squares:
$$
(2x - 1)^2 > (x + 1)^2
$$
$$
4x^2 - 4x + 1 > x^2 + 2x + 1
$$
$$
3x^2 - 6x > 0
$$
$$
3x(x - 2) > 0
$$
$$
x < 0 \ \vee \ x > 2
$$

The solution is $x < 0 \ \vee \ x > 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

When both sides are absolute values, the study by regions is still possible, but the property $|a|^2 = a^2$ offers a shorter route: two non-negative numbers are in the same order as their squares, so the inequality is equivalent to $(2x - 1)^2 > (x + 1)^2$. Expanding the squares and moving everything to the left gives the quadratic inequality $3x^2 - 6x > 0$, which factors as $3x(x - 2) > 0$; the product is positive when the two factors have the same sign, that is, outside the interval between the roots $0$ and $2$. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 3$ we have $|5| = 5 > 4 = |4|$, while for $x = 1$ we have $|1| = 1 < 2 = |2|$, and indeed $1$ does not belong to the solution.

</div>
