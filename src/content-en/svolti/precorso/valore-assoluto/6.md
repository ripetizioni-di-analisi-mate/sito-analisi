## Exercise 1

Solve, as the real parameter $k$ varies, the equation
$$
|2x - 4| = k - 3 .
$$

**Case $k > 3$.** The right-hand side is positive, therefore
$$
2x - 4 = k - 3 \ \vee \ 2x - 4 = -(k - 3)
$$
$$
2x = k + 1 \ \vee \ 2x = 7 - k
$$
$$
x = \frac{k + 1}{2} \ \vee \ x = \frac{7 - k}{2}
$$

**Case $k = 3$.** The right-hand side is zero, therefore
$$
2x - 4 = 0
$$
$$
x = 2
$$

**Case $k < 3$.** The right-hand side is negative, therefore the equation has no solutions.

For $k > 3$ the solutions are $x = \dfrac{k + 1}{2}$ and $x = \dfrac{7 - k}{2}$; for $k = 3$ the solution is $x = 2$; for $k < 3$ the equation **has no solutions**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side $k - 3$ does not contain $x$, therefore for each fixed value of $k$ it is a constant and the rule for $|A| = k$ applies. That rule distinguishes three cases according to the sign of the constant, so the discussion splits into $k - 3 > 0$, $k - 3 = 0$ and $k - 3 < 0$, that is, $k > 3$, $k = 3$ and $k < 3$.

For $k > 3$ the argument $2x - 4$ equals $k - 3$ or its opposite, and each first-degree equation is solved by dividing by $2$. The two solutions coincide only if $k + 1 = 7 - k$, that is, for $k = 3$, which is excluded in this case; therefore for $k > 3$ there are **two distinct** solutions.

For $k = 3$ the absolute value vanishes only where its argument vanishes, so only the solution $x = 2$ remains. For $k < 3$ the left-hand side is non-negative and the right-hand side is negative, therefore the two sides are never equal.

The check with $k = 5$ confirms the result: the formulas give $x = 3$ and $x = 1$, and indeed $|6 - 4| = 2$ and $|2 - 4| = 2$, both equal to $5 - 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine, as the real parameter $k$ varies, the number of solutions of the equation
$$
|x^2 - 4| = k .
$$

**Case $k < 0$.** The right-hand side is negative, therefore the equation has no solutions.

**Case $k = 0$.**
$$
x^2 - 4 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \pm 2
$$

**Case $k > 0$.**
$$
x^2 - 4 = k \ \vee \ x^2 - 4 = -k
$$
$$
x^2 = 4 + k \ \vee \ x^2 = 4 - k
$$

The first equation always has the two solutions $x = \pm\sqrt{4 + k}$. The second has:
- for $0 < k < 4$, the two solutions $x = \pm\sqrt{4 - k}$;
- for $k = 4$, the single solution $x = 0$;
- for $k > 4$, no solution.

The equation has $0$ solutions for $k < 0$, $2$ for $k = 0$, $4$ for $0 < k < 4$, $3$ for $k = 4$, $2$ for $k > 4$.

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-4);2;4" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1 ymax=7 punti="-2.4495,2,pieno;-1.4142,2,pieno;1.4142,2,pieno;2.4495,2,pieno;-2.8284,4,pieno;0,4,pieno;2.8284,4,pieno" etichette="2.9,1.5,y = 2;2.9,4.6,y = 4" titolo="The line $y = 2$ (dashed) meets $y = |x^2 - 4|$ at four points; the line $y = 4$ (red) at three points, one of which is the local maximum $(0, 4)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The solutions are the x-coordinates of the points where the horizontal line $y = k$ meets the graph of $y = |x^2 - 4|$, so the number of solutions can be read from the figure. The graph is obtained from the parabola $y = x^2 - 4$ by reflecting the part between $x = -2$ and $x = 2$, which lies below the $x$-axis; therefore it has two zeros at $\pm 2$ and a local maximum at $(0, 4)$.

For $k < 0$ the equation has no solutions, because an absolute value is never negative. For $k = 0$ the absolute value vanishes only where its argument vanishes, therefore $x^2 = 4$ and the solutions are $x = -2$ and $x = 2$.

For $k > 0$ the rule for $|A| = k$ gives two pure quadratic equations. The equation $x^2 = 4 + k$ always has two solutions, because $4 + k > 0$. The equation $x^2 = 4 - k$ has two solutions if $4 - k > 0$, one if $4 - k = 0$ and none if $4 - k < 0$. The solutions of the two equations are different from each other, since $4 + k \neq 4 - k$ for $k \neq 0$; therefore the counts add up.

The case $k = 4$ corresponds to the line passing through the local maximum: the second equation gives the single solution $x = 0$ and the total is **three**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine for which values of the real parameter $a$ the inequality
$$
|x - a| \leq 3
$$
is satisfied both by $x = 1$ and by $x = 4$.

Condition on $x = 1$:
$$
|1 - a| \leq 3 \iff -3 \leq 1 - a \leq 3 \iff -2 \leq a \leq 4
$$

Condition on $x = 4$:
$$
|4 - a| \leq 3 \iff -3 \leq 4 - a \leq 3 \iff 1 \leq a \leq 7
$$

The two conditions must hold together:
$$
\begin{cases}
-2 \leq a \leq 4 \\
1 \leq a \leq 7
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 1 \leq a \leq 4
$$

The required values are $1 \leq a \leq 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

A number satisfies the inequality when, substituted for $x$, it makes the inequality true; so we write the inequality with $x = 1$ and with $x = 4$, and we obtain two inequalities in the unknown $a$. Each has the form $|A| \leq k$ with $k = 3 > 0$, therefore it is equivalent to the double inequality $-3 \leq A \leq 3$.

In the first we subtract $1$ from all members and obtain $-4 \leq -a \leq 2$; multiplying by $-1$ the inequality signs are reversed and we get $-2 \leq a \leq 4$. The second is solved in the same way and gives $1 \leq a \leq 7$. The two values of $x$ must *both* satisfy the inequality, therefore the conditions form a system and the solution is the intersection $1 \leq a \leq 4$.

The interpretation as **distance** confirms the result. The solution set of $|x - a| \leq 3$ is the interval $a - 3 \leq x \leq a + 3$, with center $a$ and half-width $3$; it contains $1$ and $4$ if and only if the center $a$ is at distance at most $3$ from both. For $a = 1$ the interval is $-2 \leq x \leq 4$, for $a = 4$ it is $1 \leq x \leq 7$, and in both cases one of the two points is an endpoint.

</div>
