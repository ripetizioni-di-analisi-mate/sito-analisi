## Exercise 1

Determine the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the segment with endpoints $A(1, 2)$ and $B(5, 4)$, that is, of the locus of points of the plane equidistant from $A$ and $B$.

$$
\overline{PA}^{\,2} = \overline{PB}^{\,2}
$$
$$
(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = (x - 5)^2 + (y - 4)^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 - 4y + 4 &= x^2 - 10x + 25 + y^2 - 8y + 16 \\[4pt]
-2x - 4y + 5 &= -10x - 8y + 41 \\[4pt]
8x + 4y - 36 &= 0 \\[4pt]
2x + y - 9 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
M(3, 3), \qquad 2 \cdot 3 + 3 - 9 = 0,
\qquad
m_{AB} = \frac{4 - 2}{5 - 1} = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
m_{\text{bisector}} = -2
$$

::grafico{f="9-2*x" legenda="2x + y - 9 = 0" uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=7 punti="1,2,pieno,A;5,4,pieno,B;3,3,pieno,M" segmenti="1,2,5,4" titolo="The perpendicular bisector of the segment $AB$ is the line $2x + y - 9 = 0$: it passes through the midpoint $M(3, 3)$ and is perpendicular to $AB$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A point $P(x, y)$ belongs to the locus if and only if $\overline{PA} = \overline{PB}$. The two distances are positive, so the equality is equivalent to the equality of their squares, which is written without roots using the distance formula.

Expanding the squares, the terms $x^2$ and $y^2$ appear with the same coefficient on both sides and cancel. For this reason the equation of the locus is of the first degree, so the locus is a **line**. Moving all the terms to the left-hand side gives $8x + 4y - 36 = 0$, and dividing by $4$ we arrive at $2x + y - 9 = 0$.

In the last line of the computation the result is compared with the elementary definition of perpendicular bisector, that is, the line perpendicular to the segment at its midpoint. The midpoint $M(3, 3)$ has as coordinates the averages of the coordinates of $A$ and $B$, and its coordinates satisfy the equation found. The line $AB$ has slope $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the bisector, written as $y = -2x + 9$, has slope $-2$; since $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot (-2) = -1$, the two lines are perpendicular.

The perpendicular bisector of the segment $AB$ is the line $2x + y - 9 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the equation of the circle passing through the points $A(1, 1)$, $B(5, 1)$ and $C(1, 5)$, and compute its center and radius.

$$
x^2 + y^2 + ax + by + c = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
1 + 1 + a + b + c = 0 \\
25 + 1 + 5a + b + c = 0 \\
1 + 25 + a + 5b + c = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(26 + 5a + b + c) - (2 + a + b + c) &= 24 + 4a = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; a = -6 \\[4pt]
(26 + a + 5b + c) - (2 + a + b + c) &= 24 + 4b = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; b = -6 \\[4pt]
c &= -2 - a - b = -2 + 6 + 6 = 10
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 6y + 10 = 0
$$
$$
C_0(3, 3), \qquad r = \sqrt{9 + 9 - 10} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}
$$

::grafico{curve="3+2*sqrt(2)*cos(t),3+2*sqrt(2)*sin(t),0,6.29" legenda="x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 6y + 10 = 0" uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=7 punti="1,1,pieno,A;5,1,pieno,B;1,5,pieno,C;3,3,pieno,C₀" segmenti="1,1,5,1;1,1,1,5;5,1,1,5" titolo="The circle through $A$, $B$ and $C$ has center $C_0(3, 3)$, midpoint of the hypotenuse $BC$, and radius $2\sqrt{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The required circle has an equation in general form with three unknown coefficients $a$, $b$ and $c$. Each given point belongs to the circle, so its coordinates satisfy the equation; the three passage conditions must hold together and form a linear system in the unknowns $a$, $b$, $c$.

The three equations have the same term $c$ and the first two also have the same term $b$, therefore subtracting the first from the second eliminates $b$ and $c$ and gives $a = -6$. In the same way subtracting the first from the third eliminates $a$ and $c$ and gives $b = -6$. Substituting into the first equation gives $c = 10$.

From the general form with $a = b = -6$ and $c = 10$ the center is $\left( -\dfrac{a}{2}, -\dfrac{b}{2} \right) = (3, 3)$ and the radius is $\sqrt{\dfrac{a^2}{4} + \dfrac{b^2}{4} - c} = \sqrt{8}$, positive as required.

The result also has a geometric reading. The sides $AB$ and $AC$ are one horizontal and one vertical, so the triangle $ABC$ is right-angled at $A$; the center of the circle circumscribed about a right triangle is the midpoint of the hypotenuse $BC$, which is precisely $(3, 3)$.

The circle is $x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 6y + 10 = 0$, with center $(3, 3)$ and radius $2\sqrt{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the equation of the parabola with vertical axis, vertex $V(1, 3)$ and passing through the point $P(3, -5)$, and its intersection with the $y$-axis.

$$
y = a(x - 1)^2 + 3
$$
$$
-5 = a(3 - 1)^2 + 3 \;\Rightarrow\; 4a = -8 \;\Rightarrow\; a = -2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= -2(x - 1)^2 + 3 \\[4pt]
&= -2(x^2 - 2x + 1) + 3 \\[4pt]
&= -2x^2 + 4x + 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 1
$$

::grafico{f="-2*x^2+4*x+1" legenda="y = -2x^2 + 4x + 1" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-6 ymax=5 punti="1,3,pieno,V;3,-5,pieno,P;0,1,pieno;2,1,pieno" titolo="Parabola $y = -2x^2 + 4x + 1$ with vertex $V(1, 3)$ passing through $P(3, -5)$; the points $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 1)$ are symmetric with respect to the axis $x = 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

With the vertex given we use the form $y = a(x - x_V)^2 + y_V$, which follows from the theorem on the **vertex of the parabola**: every parabola with vertical axis and vertex $(1, 3)$ has equation $y = a(x - 1)^2 + 3$ for a suitable $a \neq 0$. A single unknown remains, so one condition is enough.

The condition is the passage through $P$: substituting $x = 3$ and $y = -5$ gives $-5 = 4a + 3$, hence $a = -2$. The coefficient $a$ is negative, therefore the parabola opens downward and the vertex is the highest point; this is consistent with the data, because $P$ has ordinate $-5$, less than the ordinate $3$ of the vertex.

To write the equation in the form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ we expand the square $(x - 1)^2 = x^2 - 2x + 1$, multiply by $-2$ and add $3$. With the formula $x_V = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = -\dfrac{4}{-4} = 1$ we recover the abscissa of the given vertex.

The intersection with the $y$-axis is obtained by setting $x = 0$ and coincides with the constant term, so it is the point $(0, 1)$. Its reflection in the axis $x = 1$ is $(2, 1)$, which serves as a further point for the drawing.

The parabola is $y = -2x^2 + 4x + 1$ and intersects the $y$-axis at $(0, 1)$.

</div>
