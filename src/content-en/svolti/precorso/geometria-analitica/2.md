## Exercise 1

Study the parabola
$$
y = x^2 - 4x + 3
$$
and draw its graph.

**Concavity.** $a = 1 > 0$, therefore the parabola opens upward.

**Vertex and axis.**
$$
x_V = -\frac{b}{2a} = \frac{4}{2} = 2,
\qquad
y_V = 2^2 - 4 \cdot 2 + 3 = -1 ,
$$
therefore $V(2, -1)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x = 2$.

**Intersections with the axes.** With the $x$-axis:
$$
x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0, \qquad x = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 12}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm 2}{2},
$$
therefore the points $(1, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$. With the $y$-axis: the point $(0, 3)$.

::grafico{f="x^2-4*x+3" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=5 punti="2,-1,pieno,V" titolo="$y = x^2 - 4x + 3$ with vertex $V(2, -1)$"}

Result: vertex $V(2, -1)$, axis $x = 2$, intersections $(1, 0)$, $(3, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The sign of the coefficient of $x^2$ is read first, because it determines the shape of the graph: here $a$ is positive, therefore the vertex is the **lowest point**. The abscissa of the vertex comes from the formula $x_V = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$, and the ordinate is obtained by substituting $x_V$ into the equation of the parabola, with no need for other formulas. The intersections with the $x$-axis are the solutions of the **associated equation**, here two because the discriminant is positive; the intersection with the $y$-axis is read from the constant term. With vertex, concavity and three points the parabola is **determined** and can be drawn.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Study the parabola
$$
y = -2x^2 + 4x + 6
$$
and draw its graph.

**Concavity.** $a = -2 < 0$, therefore the parabola opens downward.

**Vertex and axis.**
$$
x_V = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{4}{-4} = 1,
\qquad
y_V = -2 + 4 + 6 = 8 ,
$$
therefore $V(1, 8)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x = 1$.

**Intersections with the axes.** With the $x$-axis, dividing by $-2$:
$$
x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0, \qquad x = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 12}}{2} = \frac{2 \pm 4}{2},
$$
therefore the points $(-1, 0)$ and $(3, 0)$. With the $y$-axis: the point $(0, 6)$.

::grafico{f="-2*x^2+4*x+6" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=10 punti="1,8,pieno,V" titolo="$y = -2x^2 + 4x + 6$ with vertex $V(1, 8)$"}

Result: vertex $V(1, 8)$, axis $x = 1$, intersections $(-1, 0)$, $(3, 0)$ and $(0, 6)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient $a = -2$ is negative, therefore the parabola **opens downward** and the vertex is the **highest point**. For the intersections with the $x$-axis it is convenient to divide the equation $-2x^2 + 4x + 6 = 0$ by $-2$, since the solutions do *not* change and the computations are done with smaller coefficients. The vertex has abscissa **equidistant** from the two roots, and indeed $1$ is the average of $-1$ and $3$; this observation also provides a check on the computation of $x_V$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Study the parabola
$$
y = x^2 + 2x + 3
$$
and draw its graph.

**Concavity.** $a = 1 > 0$, therefore the parabola opens upward.

**Vertex and axis.**
$$
x_V = -\frac{b}{2a} = -1,
\qquad
y_V = 1 - 2 + 3 = 2 ,
$$
therefore $V(-1, 2)$ and the axis of symmetry is $x = -1$.

**Intersections with the axes.** With the $x$-axis: the discriminant is
$$
\Delta = 4 - 12 = -8 < 0 ,
$$
therefore the parabola does not intersect the $x$-axis. With the $y$-axis: the point $(0, 3)$, and by symmetry with respect to the axis the point $(-2, 3)$ also belongs to the graph.

::grafico{f="x^2+2*x+3" xmin=-4 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=8 punti="-1,2,pieno,V" titolo="$y = x^2 + 2x + 3$ with vertex $V(-1, 2)$"}

Result: vertex $V(-1, 2)$, axis $x = -1$, no intersection with the $x$-axis, intersection $(0, 3)$ with the $y$-axis.

<div class="spiegazione">

The negative discriminant says that the equation $x^2 + 2x + 3 = 0$ has no real solutions, therefore the parabola **does not touch** the $x$-axis; since it opens upward and the vertex has ordinate $2 > 0$, the whole graph **lies above the axis**. To draw it, however, points other than the vertex are needed: the intersection with the $y$-axis is $(0, 3)$, and its **symmetric point** with respect to the line $x = -1$ is $(-2, 3)$, because the two points have the same distance from the axis of symmetry.

</div>
