## Exercise 1

Determine the position of the line $r$ with equation $y = x - 1$ relative to the circle
$$
x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y - 5 = 0
$$
and find the common points, if any.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 1)^2 - 1 + (y - 2)^2 - 4 - 5 &= 0 \\[4pt]
(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(1, 2), \qquad r = \sqrt{10}
$$
$$
x - y - 1 = 0, \qquad d(C, r) = \frac{|1 - 2 - 1|}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2} < \sqrt{10}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + (x - 1)^2 - 2x - 4(x - 1) - 5 &= 0 \\[4pt]
x^2 + x^2 - 2x + 1 - 2x - 4x + 4 - 5 &= 0 \\[4pt]
2x^2 - 8x &= 0 \\[4pt]
2x(x - 4) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -1, \qquad x = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; y = 3
$$

::grafico{curve="1+sqrt(10)*cos(t),2+sqrt(10)*sin(t),0,6.29" f="x-1" legenda="y = x - 1;x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 4y - 5 = 0" uguale=1 xmin=-3 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=6 punti="1,2,pieno,C;0,-1,pieno,A;4,3,pieno,B" titolo="The line $y = x - 1$ is secant to the circle with center $C(1, 2)$ and radius $\sqrt{10}$ at the points $A(0, -1)$ and $B(4, 3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare the distance from the center to the line with the radius we need the center and the radius, so we complete the squares: $x^2 - 2x = (x - 1)^2 - 1$ and $y^2 - 4y = (y - 2)^2 - 4$. Moving the constants to the right gives the standard form, from which we read the center $C(1, 2)$ and the radius $\sqrt{10}$.

The point-line distance formula applies to the line written in general form, therefore $y = x - 1$ is rewritten as $x - y - 1 = 0$. The distance from the center turns out to be $\sqrt{2}$, which is less than $\sqrt{10}$; hence the line is **secant** and has two points in common with the circle.

To find those points we substitute $y = x - 1$ into the equation of the circle. Expanding $(x - 1)^2$ and the product $-4(x - 1)$ leads to $2x^2 - 8x = 0$, which is solved by factoring out $2x$. The solutions $x = 0$ and $x = 4$, substituted into the equation of the line, give the ordinates $-1$ and $3$. The resulting equation has two distinct real solutions, in agreement with the comparison between distance and radius.

The line intersects the circle at the points $A(0, -1)$ and $B(4, 3)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the line tangent to the circle
$$
x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y - 20 = 0
$$
at its point $P(4, 2)$.

$$
4^2 + 2^2 - 2 \cdot 4 + 4 \cdot 2 - 20 = 16 + 4 - 8 + 8 - 20 = 0
$$
$$
C\left( -\frac{-2}{2},\; -\frac{4}{2} \right) = C(1, -2),
\qquad
r = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 20} = 5
$$
$$
m_{CP} = \frac{2 - (-2)}{4 - 1} = \frac{4}{3},
\qquad
m_t = -\frac{3}{4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 2 &= -\frac{3}{4}(x - 4) \\[4pt]
y &= -\frac{3}{4}x + 5
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="1+5*cos(t),-2+5*sin(t),0,6.29" f="-0.75*x+5" legenda="y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x + 5;x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y - 20 = 0" uguale=1 xmin=-5 xmax=9 ymin=-8 ymax=6 punti="1,-2,pieno,C;4,2,pieno,P" segmenti="1,-2,4,2" titolo="The tangent line at $P(4, 2)$ is perpendicular to the radius $CP$, which has slope $\dfrac{4}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coordinates of $P$ make the left-hand side of the equation vanish, so $P$ is a point of the circle and the tangent line at $P$ is defined.

The center and the radius are read from the general form $x^2 + y^2 + ax + by + c = 0$ with $a = -2$, $b = 4$ and $c = -20$. The center is $C\left( -\dfrac{a}{2}, -\dfrac{b}{2} \right) = (1, -2)$ and the radius is $\sqrt{\dfrac{a^2}{4} + \dfrac{b^2}{4} - c} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 20} = 5$.

The tangent line at a point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius ending at that point, because the point of tangency is the point of the line closest to the center. The radius $CP$ has slope $\dfrac{4}{3}$, so by the **perpendicularity condition** the tangent line has slope $-\dfrac{3}{4}$. The formula of the line through a point with $P(4, 2)$ gives $y - 2 = -\dfrac{3}{4}(x - 4)$, and expanding we obtain $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x + 3 + 2$.

In general form the line is $3x + 4y - 20 = 0$, and its distance from the center is $\dfrac{|3 - 8 - 20|}{5} = 5$, equal to the radius, which confirms that the line is tangent.

The tangent line at $P$ is $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x + 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

As the real parameter $k$ varies, determine the position of the line $y = x + k$ relative to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 8$ and find the points of tangency.

$$
C(0, 0), \qquad r = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
x - y + k = 0, \qquad d = \frac{|k|}{\sqrt{2}}
$$
$$
d < r \iff \frac{|k|}{\sqrt{2}} < 2\sqrt{2} \iff |k| < 4
$$
$$
d = r \iff |k| = 4, \qquad d > r \iff |k| > 4
$$
$$
k = 4: \quad x^2 + (x + 4)^2 = 8 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x^2 + 8x + 8 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (x + 2)^2 = 0
$$
$$
k = -4: \quad x^2 + (x - 4)^2 = 8 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x^2 - 8x + 8 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (x - 2)^2 = 0
$$
$$
T_1(-2, 2), \qquad T_2(2, -2)
$$

::grafico{curve="2*sqrt(2)*cos(t),2*sqrt(2)*sin(t),0,6.29" f="x+4;x-4" legenda="y = x + 4;y = x - 4;x^2 + y^2 = 8" uguale=1 xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-5 ymax=5 punti="-2,2,pieno,T₁;2,-2,pieno,T₂" titolo="The lines $y = x + 4$ and $y = x - 4$ are tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 8$; the lines $y = x + k$ with $-4 < k < 4$ lie between the two and are secant"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The lines $y = x + k$ are all parallel, with slope $1$, and only the intercept $k$ changes; therefore the position depends on the distance of the line from the center $O(0, 0)$, which must be compared with the radius $2\sqrt{2}$.

In general form the line is $x - y + k = 0$, so the distance from the origin is $\dfrac{|k|}{\sqrt{1 + 1}} = \dfrac{|k|}{\sqrt{2}}$. The inequality $\dfrac{|k|}{\sqrt{2}} < 2\sqrt{2}$, multiplied by $\sqrt{2} > 0$, becomes $|k| < 4$. Consequently the line is **secant** for $-4 < k < 4$, **tangent** for $k = \pm 4$ and **external** for $|k| > 4$.

The same result is obtained with the discriminant: substituting $y = x + k$ gives $2x^2 + 2kx + k^2 - 8 = 0$, with $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = k^2 - 2(k^2 - 8) = 16 - k^2$, which is positive for $|k| < 4$ and zero for $k = \pm 4$.

For $k = 4$ the resulting equation becomes $(x + 2)^2 = 0$, so $x = -2$ and $y = -2 + 4 = 2$. For $k = -4$ the resulting equation is $2x^2 - 8x + 8 = 0$, that is, $(x - 2)^2 = 0$, so $x = 2$ and $y = 2 - 4 = -2$. The two points of tangency are symmetric with respect to the origin and lie on the line $y = -x$, perpendicular to the tangent lines and passing through the center.

</div>
