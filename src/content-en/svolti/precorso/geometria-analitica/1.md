## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the line passing through $A(-1, 3)$ and $B(2, -3)$.

The slope is
$$
m = \frac{y_B - y_A}{x_B - x_A} = \frac{-3 - 3}{2 - (-1)} = \frac{-6}{3} = -2 .
$$
The line through $A$ with slope $-2$ is
$$
y - 3 = -2\,(x + 1), \qquad y = -2x + 1 .
$$

Result: $y = -2x + 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The abscissas of the two points are different, so the line is not vertical and has a slope, which is computed as the ratio between the change in $y$ and the change in $x$ in going from $A$ to $B$. Once $m$ is found, one imposes the passage through one of the two points with the point-slope formula; the choice of point does not change the result, because both belong to the line. A check with the other point: for $x = 2$ one obtains $y = -4 + 1 = -3$, which is the ordinate of $B$, therefore the line also passes through $B$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equations of the line through $P(1, 2)$ parallel to the line $y = 3x - 5$ and of the line through $P$ perpendicular to the same line.

The given line has slope $m = 3$. The parallel line through $P$ has the same slope:
$$
y - 2 = 3\,(x - 1), \qquad y = 3x - 1 .
$$
The perpendicular line through $P$ has slope $m' = -\dfrac{1}{3}$, since perpendicularity requires $m \, m' = -1$:
$$
y - 2 = -\frac{1}{3}\,(x - 1), \qquad y = -\frac{1}{3}x + \frac{7}{3} .
$$

Result: parallel $y = 3x - 1$, perpendicular $y = -\dfrac{1}{3}x + \dfrac{7}{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Parallelism and perpendicularity fix the slope of the required line, therefore the passage through $P$ determines everything else. For the parallel line one copies $m = 3$; for the perpendicular line one imposes $m\,m' = -1$, so $m'$ is the negative reciprocal of $3$. In both cases one uses the point-slope formula with the slope found and brings the equation to slope-intercept form. A check: for $x = 1$ the parallel line gives $y = 3 - 1 = 2$ and the perpendicular line gives $y = -\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{7}{3} = 2$, therefore both pass through $P(1, 2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the distance from the point $P(2, 1)$ to the line $r$ of equation
$$
y = \frac{3}{4}x + 1 .
$$

Bring the line to general form by multiplying by $4$ and rearranging:
$$
4y = 3x + 4, \qquad 3x - 4y + 4 = 0 .
$$
The distance formula gives
$$
d(P, r) = \frac{|3 \cdot 2 - 4 \cdot 1 + 4|}{\sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2}} = \frac{|6 - 4 + 4|}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{6}{5} .
$$

Result: $d(P, r) = \dfrac{6}{5}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The point-line distance formula requires the general form $ax + by + c = 0$, so the first operation moves all the terms to the left and eliminates the fraction. In the numerator one substitutes the coordinates of $P$ for $x$ and $y$ and takes the absolute value, because a distance is not negative; the denominator contains the square root of the sum of the squares of the coefficients of $x$ and $y$, which here equals $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$.

</div>
