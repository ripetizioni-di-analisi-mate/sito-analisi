## Exercise 1

Recognize and reduce to standard form the conic
$$
4x^2 + 9y^2 - 8x + 36y + 4 = 0 .
$$

The coefficients of the quadratic terms are $4$ and $9$: same sign and different values, therefore the equation represents an **ellipse**. Factor and complete the squares:
$$
\begin{aligned}
4(x^2 - 2x) + 9(y^2 + 4y) + 4 &= 0 \\[4pt]
4(x - 1)^2 - 4 + 9(y + 2)^2 - 36 + 4 &= 0 \\[4pt]
4(x - 1)^2 + 9(y + 2)^2 &= 36 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Dividing by $36$:
$$
\frac{(x - 1)^2}{9} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{4} = 1 .
$$

Result: ellipse with center $(1, -2)$, semi-axes $3$ along $x$ and $2$ along $y$, vertices $(4, -2)$, $(-2, -2)$, $(1, 0)$ and $(1, -4)$; from $c^2 = 9 - 4 = 5$ the foci are $(1 \pm \sqrt{5}, -2)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The **type of conic** is recognized before *any* computation from the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$. Factoring out $4$ and $9$ prepares the completion: the group $x^2 - 2x$ is completed by adding and subtracting $1$, and the $-1$ multiplied by the factored $4$ produces the outer $-4$; the same happens for the group in $y$, with the inner $-4$ becoming $-36$. The final division by $36$ brings the equation to the **translated standard form**, from which center and semi-axes are read. The major semi-axis is **horizontal**, since $9 > 4$, therefore the foci lie on the line $y = -2$ at distance $\sqrt{5}$ from the center.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Recognize and reduce to standard form the conic
$$
x^2 - 4y^2 - 2x - 8y - 7 = 0 .
$$

The coefficients of the quadratic terms are $1$ and $-4$: opposite signs, therefore the equation represents a **hyperbola**. Factor and complete the squares:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2 - 2x) - 4(y^2 + 2y) - 7 &= 0 \\[4pt]
(x - 1)^2 - 1 - 4(y + 1)^2 + 4 - 7 &= 0 \\[4pt]
(x - 1)^2 - 4(y + 1)^2 &= 4 .
\end{aligned}
$$
Dividing by $4$:
$$
\frac{(x - 1)^2}{4} - (y + 1)^2 = 1 .
$$

Result: hyperbola with center $(1, -1)$ with $a = 2$ and $b = 1$, vertices $(3, -1)$ and $(-1, -1)$, asymptotes $y + 1 = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}(x - 1)$; from $c^2 = 4 + 1 = 5$ the foci are $(1 \pm \sqrt{5}, -1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The **opposite signs** of the quadratic coefficients immediately identify a **hyperbola**. When factoring out $-4$, the term $-8y$ becomes $+2y$ in the factored group, because $-8y = -4 \cdot 2y$; completing $y^2 + 2y$ adds and subtracts $1$, and the inner $-1$ multiplied by $-4$ produces the outer $+4$. After dividing by $4$ the positive term is the one in $x$, therefore the branches open to the right and to the left of the center $(1, -1)$ and the vertices are at distance $a = 2$ from the center in the horizontal direction. The **asymptotes** are the lines through the center with slope $\pm\dfrac{b}{a} = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}$, and they are drawn as the diagonals of the rectangle with center $(1, -1)$ and sides $2a = 4$ and $2b = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Recognize the conic
$$
x^2 + 4x - y + 5 = 0
$$
and draw it.

The term $x^2$ appears but $y^2$ does not, and $y$ is of first degree, therefore the equation represents a **parabola**. Isolate $y$:
$$
y = x^2 + 4x + 5 .
$$
The vertex has coordinates
$$
x_V = -\frac{4}{2} = -2, \qquad y_V = 4 - 8 + 5 = 1 ,
$$
the parabola opens upward since $a = 1 > 0$, the discriminant is $\Delta = 16 - 20 = -4 < 0$, therefore the graph does not intersect the $x$-axis; the intersection with the $y$-axis is $(0, 5)$.

::grafico{f="x^2+4*x+5" xmin=-5 xmax=1 ymin=-1 ymax=10 punti="-2,1" titolo="y = x² + 4x + 5 with vertex V(−2, 1)"}

Result: parabola with vertex $V(-2, 1)$, axis $x = -2$, opening upward, intersection $(0, 5)$ with the $y$-axis.

<div class="spiegazione">

Of the two quadratic terms *only* one appears, and the variable remaining at first degree can be isolated: the conic is a **parabola** with a vertical axis. Once the equation is written in the form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$, the study is the usual one for the parabola: vertex with the formula for the abscissa, concavity from the sign of $a$, intersections with the axes from the discriminant and the constant term. The point $(-4, 5)$, symmetric to $(0, 5)$ with respect to the axis $x = -2$, gives a **further point** for the drawing.

</div>
