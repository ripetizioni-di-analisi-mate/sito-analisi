## Exercise 1

Find the semi-axes, vertices and foci of the ellipse
$$
\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1
$$
and describe its graph.

The semi-axes are
$$
a = \sqrt{25} = 5, \qquad b = \sqrt{9} = 3 ,
$$
therefore the vertices are $(\pm 5, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 3)$. Since $a > b$, the foci lie on the $x$-axis:
$$
c^2 = a^2 - b^2 = 25 - 9 = 16, \qquad c = 4 ,
$$
therefore the foci are $(\pm 4, 0)$.

Result: semi-axes $5$ and $3$, vertices $(\pm 5, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 3)$, foci $(\pm 4, 0)$. The graph is an oval centered at the origin, symmetric with respect to both axes, inscribed in the rectangle $-5 \leq x \leq 5$, $-3 \leq y \leq 3$ and passing through the four vertices.

::grafico{curve="5*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,6.29" uguale=1 xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=4 punti="5,0;-5,0;0,3;0,-3;4,0,vuoto,F₁;-4,0,vuoto,F₂" titolo="Ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$, with foci $F_1$ and $F_2$ at $(\pm 4, 0)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is already in **standard form**, so the squares of the semi-axes are read from the denominators: $a^2$ under $x^2$ and $b^2$ under $y^2$. The **major semi-axis** is the one along $x$, because $25 > 9$, hence the foci lie on the $x$-axis and their distance from the center is computed with $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$, the relation valid for the ellipse. For the drawing, one marks the four vertices and joins them with a symmetric closed curve: the whole graph stays **inside the rectangle** determined by the semi-axes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the semi-axes, vertices and foci of the ellipse
$$
\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{16} = 1 .
$$

The semi-axes are $a = 2$ along $x$ and $b = 4$ along $y$, therefore the vertices are $(\pm 2, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 4)$. Since $b > a$, the foci lie on the $y$-axis:
$$
c^2 = b^2 - a^2 = 16 - 4 = 12, \qquad c = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3} ,
$$
therefore the foci are $(0, \pm 2\sqrt{3})$.

Result: semi-axes $2$ and $4$, vertices $(\pm 2, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 4)$, foci $(0, \pm 2\sqrt{3})$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the larger denominator is under $y^2$, therefore the major semi-axis is **vertical** and the ellipse is elongated along the $y$-axis. The foci always lie on the **axis of the major semi-axis**, hence on the $y$-axis, and in the relation $c^2 = b^2 - a^2$ one subtracts the square of the minor semi-axis from that of the major semi-axis: the difference is **positive**, as it must be for a distance. The root $\sqrt{12}$ simplifies to $2\sqrt{3}$ by factoring out the square $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the vertices, foci and asymptotes of the hyperbola
$$
\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{16} = 1
$$
and describe its graph.

Here $a = 3$ and $b = 4$. The vertices are $(\pm 3, 0)$ and the foci lie on the $x$-axis:
$$
c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 9 + 16 = 25, \qquad c = 5 ,
$$
therefore the foci are $(\pm 5, 0)$. The asymptotes are
$$
y = \frac{4}{3}x, \qquad y = -\frac{4}{3}x .
$$

Result: vertices $(\pm 3, 0)$, foci $(\pm 5, 0)$, asymptotes $y = \pm\dfrac{4}{3}x$. The graph has two branches: one to the right of the vertex $(3, 0)$ and one to the left of $(-3, 0)$; each branch starts from the vertex and approaches the two asymptotes indefinitely, staying outside the strip $-3 < x < 3$.

::grafico{curve="3/cos(t),4*tan(t),0,6.2832" uguale=1 xmin=-8 xmax=8 ymin=-7 ymax=7 segmenti="-5.25,-7,5.25,7;-5.25,7,5.25,-7;3,-4,3,4;-3,-4,-3,4;-3,4,3,4;-3,-4,3,-4" punti="3,0;-3,0;5,0,vuoto,F₁;-5,0,vuoto,F₂" titolo="Hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{9} - \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$: vertices $(\pm 3, 0)$, foci $(\pm 5, 0)$; the asymptotes are the diagonals of the rectangle with sides $x = \pm 3$ and $y = \pm 4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **minus sign** in front of the term in $y^2$ distinguishes the hyperbola from the ellipse, and the plus sign in front of the term in $x^2$ places the vertices and the foci on the $x$-axis. For the hyperbola the relation between the parameters is $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$, with the sum in place of the difference, therefore the foci are **farther away** than the vertices. For the drawing, one traces the rectangle with sides $x = \pm 3$ and $y = \pm 4$: its extended diagonals are the **asymptotes**, and the branches start from the vertices and flatten onto them. A check on the coefficient of the asymptotes: the ratio $\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{4}{3}$ coincides with the slope of the diagonals of the rectangle.

</div>
