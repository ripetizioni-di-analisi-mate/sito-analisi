## Exercise 1

Prove that for every integer $n$ the number $n^2 + n$ is even.

First case: $n$ even, that is, $n = 2k$ with $k$ an integer.
$$
n^2 + n = 4k^2 + 2k = 2\left(2k^2 + k\right)
$$

Second case: $n$ odd, that is, $n = 2k + 1$ with $k$ an integer.
$$
\begin{aligned}
n^2 + n &= (2k + 1)^2 + (2k + 1) \\[6pt]
&= 4k^2 + 4k + 1 + 2k + 1 \\[6pt]
&= 4k^2 + 6k + 2 \\[6pt]
&= 2\left(2k^2 + 3k + 1\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

In both cases $n^2 + n$ is twice an integer, therefore it is **even**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The proposition is universal, hence it requires an argument valid for an arbitrary integer. We use a **direct proof** split into two cases, because every integer is even or odd and the two cases exhaust all possibilities.

If $n$ is even, we write $n = 2k$ with $k$ an integer. Substituting gives $4k^2 + 2k$, and factoring out $2$ gives $2\left(2k^2 + k\right)$; the number $2k^2 + k$ is an integer, therefore $n^2 + n$ is even.

If $n$ is odd, we write $n = 2k + 1$ with $k$ an integer. Expanding the square gives $4k^2 + 4k + 1$, and adding $2k + 1$ gives $4k^2 + 6k + 2$. All the coefficients are even, hence we factor out $2$ and the integer $2k^2 + 3k + 1$ remains; in this case too $n^2 + n$ is even.

The same result is also obtained from the factorization $n^2 + n = n(n + 1)$. The numbers $n$ and $n + 1$ are consecutive integers, therefore one of the two is even, and the product of an even number by an integer is even.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Prove that, for $n$ an integer, if $3n + 2$ is even then $n$ is even.

Contrapositive: if $n$ is odd, then $3n + 2$ is odd.

$$
n = 2k + 1 \quad (k \text{ an integer})
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3n + 2 &= 3(2k + 1) + 2 \\[6pt]
&= 6k + 5 \\[6pt]
&= 2(3k + 2) + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $3n + 2$ is odd, therefore the contrapositive is true and the given implication is **true**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The hypothesis "$3n + 2$ is even" gives information about $3n + 2$ and not directly about $n$, therefore a direct proof would require going back from $3n + 2$ to $n$. The negation of the conclusion, "$n$ is odd", instead provides an explicit expression of $n$, and for this reason we use a **proof by contrapositive**.

The contrapositive of $p \Rightarrow q$ is $\lnot q \Rightarrow \lnot p$. Here $p$ is "$3n + 2$ is even" and $q$ is "$n$ is even"; for an integer, not being even means being odd, hence the contrapositive is "if $n$ is odd, then $3n + 2$ is odd".

By the hypothesis of the contrapositive $n = 2k + 1$ with $k$ an integer. Substituting gives $3n + 2 = 6k + 3 + 2 = 6k + 5$. Since $6k + 5 = 6k + 4 + 1$, factoring $2$ out of the first two terms gives $2(3k + 2) + 1$, which is an odd integer.

The contrapositive is therefore proved. An implication and its contrapositive have the same truth table, hence the implication "if $3n + 2$ is even, then $n$ is even" is also **true**. For example, with $n = 4$ one has $3n + 2 = 14$, and with $n = 5$ one has $3n + 2 = 17$, which is odd.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Prove that for every pair of real numbers $a \geq 0$ and $b \geq 0$
$$
\frac{a + b}{2} \geq \sqrt{ab} .
$$

$$
\left(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}\right)^2 \geq 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}\right)^2 &= \left(\sqrt{a}\right)^2 - 2\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b} + \left(\sqrt{b}\right)^2 \\[6pt]
&= a - 2\sqrt{ab} + b
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
a + b - 2\sqrt{ab} \geq 0 \iff a + b \geq 2\sqrt{ab} \iff \frac{a + b}{2} \geq \sqrt{ab}
$$

::grafico{f="(x+4)/2;sqrt(4*x)" xmin=-1 xmax=10 ymin=-1 ymax=7 punti="4,4,pieno" legenda="y = \frac{x + 4}{2};y = \sqrt{4x}" titolo="With $b = 4$ the line $y = \dfrac{x + 4}{2}$ lies above the graph of $y = \sqrt{4x}$ for every $x \geq 0$ and touches it only at $x = 4$"}

The inequality **holds** for every $a \geq 0$ and $b \geq 0$, with equality if and only if $a = b$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $\dfrac{a + b}{2}$ is the **arithmetic mean** of $a$ and $b$, and $\sqrt{ab}$ is their **geometric mean**. The proof is direct: it starts from an inequality that is true for every pair of nonnegative numbers and reaches the conclusion through equivalent steps.

Since $a \geq 0$ and $b \geq 0$, the roots $\sqrt{a}$ and $\sqrt{b}$ exist. The square of a real number is nonnegative, therefore $\left(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}\right)^2 \geq 0$. Expanding the square of a binomial gives $a - 2\sqrt{ab} + b$, because $\left(\sqrt{a}\right)^2 = a$, $\left(\sqrt{b}\right)^2 = b$ and $\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}$ for nonnegative numbers.

Adding $2\sqrt{ab}$ to both sides of the inequality $a + b - 2\sqrt{ab} \geq 0$ gives $a + b \geq 2\sqrt{ab}$; dividing by the positive number $2$ preserves the direction and gives the conclusion.

Equality holds if and only if $\left(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}\right)^2 = 0$, that is, if $a = b$. In the graph, with $b = 4$, the arithmetic mean $\dfrac{x + 4}{2}$ exceeds the geometric mean $\sqrt{4x}$ at every point $x \geq 0$ other than $4$, and at $x = 4$ both equal $4$.

</div>
