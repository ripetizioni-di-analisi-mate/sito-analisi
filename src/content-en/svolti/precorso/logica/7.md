## Exercise 1

A function $f$ is **bounded** on $A$ if
$$
\exists M > 0 \ \forall x \in A : |f(x)| \leq M .
$$
Write the negation and use it to prove that $f(x) = x^2$ is not bounded on $\mathbb{R}$.

$$
\lnot \bigl( \exists M > 0 \ \forall x \in A : |f(x)| \leq M \bigr)
\equiv \forall M > 0 \ \exists x \in A : |f(x)| > M
$$

$$
M > 0, \quad x = M + 1:
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
|f(M + 1)| &= (M + 1)^2 \\[6pt]
&= M^2 + 2M + 1 \\[6pt]
&> M
\end{aligned}
$$

The function $f(x) = x^2$ **is not bounded** on $\mathbb{R}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The definition contains two quantifiers. The negation proceeds from the outside inward: $\exists M > 0$ becomes $\forall M > 0$, $\forall x \in A$ becomes $\exists x \in A$, and at the end the inequality $|f(x)| \leq M$ becomes $|f(x)| > M$. The condition $M > 0$ stays unchanged, because it indicates the set in which $M$ ranges. In words, the function is not bounded when, however $M$ is chosen, there exists a point at which $|f|$ exceeds $M$.

The negation is a universal proposition in $M$, therefore we fix an arbitrary $M > 0$ and construct an $x$ that depends on $M$. The choice $x = M + 1$ works. Indeed $(M + 1)^2 - M = M^2 + M + 1$, and since $M^2 \geq 0$ and $M + 1 > 0$ this difference is positive, therefore $(M + 1)^2 > M$. The argument holds for every $M > 0$, hence the negation is true and $x^2$ **is not bounded** on $\mathbb{R}$.

The point $x$ changes as $M$ varies; in the negation this is allowed, because $\exists x$ follows $\forall M$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A function $f$ is **strictly increasing** on $A$ if
$$
\forall x_1, x_2 \in A : x_1 < x_2 \ \Longrightarrow\ f(x_1) < f(x_2) .
$$
Write the negation, show that $f(x) = x^2$ is not strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$ and prove that it is strictly increasing on $[0, +\infty)$.

$$
\lnot \bigl( \forall x_1, x_2 \in A : x_1 < x_2 \Rightarrow f(x_1) < f(x_2) \bigr)
\equiv \exists x_1, x_2 \in A : x_1 < x_2 \ \land \ f(x_1) \geq f(x_2)
$$

$$
x_1 = -1, \ x_2 = 0: \qquad -1 < 0, \qquad f(-1) = 1 \geq 0 = f(0)
$$

$$
0 \leq x_1 < x_2: \qquad x_2^2 - x_1^2 = (x_2 - x_1)(x_2 + x_1) > 0
$$

::grafico{f="x^2" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=5 punti="-1,1,pieno;0,0,pieno" segmenti="-1,0,-1,1" etichette="-1.9,1.4,f(−1) = 1;0.2,-0.6,f(0) = 0" titolo="For $x_1 = -1 < x_2 = 0$ one has $f(-1) = 1 \geq f(0) = 0$; for $x \geq 0$ the graph of $y = x^2$ rises"}

The function **is not strictly increasing** on $\mathbb{R}$ and **is strictly increasing** on $[0, +\infty)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The predicate of the definition is an implication, and the **negation of an implication** is the conjunction of the hypothesis with the negation of the conclusion. Together with the swap of $\forall$ with $\exists$ this gives the proposition "there exist $x_1 < x_2$ with $f(x_1) \geq f(x_2)$".

On $\mathbb{R}$ one pair of points is enough. For $x_1 = -1$ and $x_2 = 0$ one has $x_1 < x_2$ and $f(-1) = 1 \geq 0 = f(0)$, therefore the negation is true.

On $[0, +\infty)$ the definition is proved for every pair. We fix $0 \leq x_1 < x_2$ and factor the difference of squares. The factor $x_2 - x_1$ is positive because $x_1 < x_2$; the factor $x_2 + x_1$ is positive because $x_2 > x_1 \geq 0$. The product of two positive numbers is positive, therefore $x_1^2 < x_2^2$ and the function **is strictly increasing** on $[0, +\infty)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A function $f$ **has a maximum** on $A$ if
$$
\exists x_0 \in A \ \forall x \in A : f(x) \leq f(x_0) .
$$
Write the negation and prove that $f(x) = x^2$ has no maximum on $A = [0, 1)$.

$$
\lnot \bigl( \exists x_0 \in A \ \forall x \in A : f(x) \leq f(x_0) \bigr)
\equiv \forall x_0 \in A \ \exists x \in A : f(x) > f(x_0)
$$

$$
x_0 \in [0, 1), \qquad x = \frac{x_0 + 1}{2}
$$

$$
x - x_0 = \frac{1 - x_0}{2} > 0, \qquad 1 - x = \frac{1 - x_0}{2} > 0
$$

$$
0 \leq x_0 < x < 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x) = x^2 > x_0^2 = f(x_0)
$$

The function **has no maximum** on $[0, 1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The negation swaps the two quantifiers and negates the inequality: for *every* point $x_0$ of $A$ there exists a point $x$ of $A$ at which $f$ takes a value greater than $f(x_0)$. We therefore fix an arbitrary $x_0 \in [0, 1)$ and look for a point of $A$ further to the right.

The candidate is the midpoint between $x_0$ and $1$, that is, $x = \dfrac{x_0 + 1}{2}$. The differences $x - x_0$ and $1 - x$ both equal $\dfrac{1 - x_0}{2}$, which is positive because $x_0 < 1$. Therefore $x_0 < x < 1$, and since $x > x_0 \geq 0$ the point $x$ belongs to $A$.

In the previous exercise it was proved that $x^2$ is strictly increasing on $[0, +\infty)$, hence from $x_0 < x$ it follows that $x_0^2 < x^2$. The negation is true for every $x_0$, therefore the function **has no maximum** on $[0, 1)$. The result depends on the fact that the right endpoint $1$ does not belong to $A$; on the closed interval $[0, 1]$ the maximum exists and equals $f(1) = 1$.

</div>
