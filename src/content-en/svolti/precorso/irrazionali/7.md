## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x + 3} \geq \sqrt{5 - x} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 3 \geq 0 \\
5 - x \geq 0 \\
x + 3 \geq 5 - x
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -3 \\
x \leq 5 \\
2x \geq 2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -3 \\
x \leq 5 \\
x \geq 1
\end{cases}
$$

Solutions: $1 \leq x \leq 5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Both roots have even index, so the inequality is meaningful where both radicands are nonnegative; hence the first two conditions of the system, $x \geq -3$ and $x \leq 5$.

At the points where the roots exist, the two sides are nonnegative numbers. By the theorem stating that **squaring preserves order** between nonnegative numbers, the strict inequality between the sides is equivalent to the one between the squares; moreover, two nonnegative numbers are equal if and only if their squares are equal. Therefore $\sqrt{x + 3} \geq \sqrt{5 - x}$ is equivalent to $x + 3 \geq 5 - x$, that is, to $2x \geq 2$ and hence to $x \geq 1$.

The intersection of the three conditions is $1 \leq x \leq 5$. The condition $x \geq -3$ is a consequence of the other two, because from $x + 3 \geq 5 - x \geq 0$ it follows that $x + 3 \geq 0$; writing it does not change the result.

At the endpoints the inequality holds as an equality or strictly. For $x = 1$ the two radicands both equal $4$ and the sides are equal to $2$; for $x = 5$ the left-hand side equals $\sqrt{8}$ and the right-hand side equals $0$. The solutions are therefore **all the numbers** in the closed interval $[1, 5]$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{\sqrt{x + 2} - x}{x - 1} \geq 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2 \geq 0 \\
x - 1 \neq 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -2 \\
x \neq 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\sqrt{x + 2} > x \iff -2 \leq x < 2 ,
\qquad
\sqrt{x + 2} = x \iff x = 2
$$
$$
x - 1 > 0 \iff x > 1
$$

- For $-2 \leq x < 1$ the numerator is positive and the denominator is negative: the fraction is negative.
- For $1 < x < 2$ numerator and denominator are positive: the fraction is positive.
- For $x = 2$ the numerator is zero: the fraction is zero.
- For $x > 2$ the numerator is negative and the denominator is positive: the fraction is negative.

Solutions: $1 < x \leq 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is meaningful where the root exists and the denominator is nonzero, that is, for $x \geq -2$ and $x \neq 1$. The sign of a fraction depends on the signs of the numerator and of the denominator, so they are studied separately.

The sign of the numerator depends on the comparison between $\sqrt{x + 2}$ and $x$. By the **inequality** $\sqrt{A} > B$ with $B = x$, the first system ($x < 0$ and $x + 2 \geq 0$) gives $-2 \leq x < 0$; the second ($x \geq 0$ and $x + 2 > x^2$) gives $0 \leq x < 2$, because $x^2 - x - 2 < 0$ is equivalent to $-1 < x < 2$. The numerator is therefore positive for $-2 \leq x < 2$. The equation $\sqrt{x + 2} = x$ has the single solution $x = 2$, since it requires $x \geq 0$ and $x^2 - x - 2 = 0$; for $x > 2$ the numerator is therefore negative.

The denominator is positive for $x > 1$. Combining the signs on the four intervals shows that the fraction is positive for $1 < x < 2$ and zero for $x = 2$. The inequality asks for **positive or zero** values, so the solutions are $1 < x \leq 2$; the value $x = 1$ is excluded because it makes the denominator zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x - 2} < 3 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 \geq 0 \\
x - 2 \geq 0
\end{cases}
\iff x \geq 2
$$
$$
\sqrt{x + 1} < 3 - \sqrt{x - 2}
$$
$$
3 - \sqrt{x - 2} > 0 \iff \sqrt{x - 2} < 3 \iff 2 \leq x < 11
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 1 < \left(3 - \sqrt{x - 2}\right)^2 &\iff x + 1 < 9 - 6\sqrt{x - 2} + x - 2 \\[6pt]
&\iff 6\sqrt{x - 2} < 6 \\[6pt]
&\iff \sqrt{x - 2} < 1 \\[6pt]
&\iff 2 \leq x < 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Solutions: $2 \leq x < 3$.

::grafico{f="sqrt(x+1)+sqrt(x-2);3" xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-1 ymax=6 punti="2,1.732,pieno;3,3,vuoto" legenda="y = \sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x - 2};y = 3" titolo="The graph of $y = \sqrt{x + 1} + \sqrt{x - 2}$, defined for $x \geq 2$, lies below the line $y = 3$ for $2 \leq x < 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both roots must exist, so $x + 1 \geq 0$ and $x - 2 \geq 0$; the second condition implies the first, therefore the domain is $x \geq 2$.

Isolating $\sqrt{x + 1}$ on the left-hand side gives an inequality of the form $\sqrt{A} < B$ with $B = 3 - \sqrt{x - 2}$. The **solving system** for this form requires $B > 0$, that is, $\sqrt{x - 2} < 3$, which on the domain is equivalent to $x - 2 < 9$ and hence to $2 \leq x < 11$.

With both sides nonnegative, squaring preserves the direction. The square of the right-hand side is $9 - 6\sqrt{x - 2} + (x - 2)$; the terms in $x$ cancel and what remains is $6\sqrt{x - 2} < 6$. Dividing by $6$ gives $\sqrt{x - 2} < 1$, which on the domain is equivalent to $x - 2 < 1$, that is, to $x < 3$.

The intersection with the previous conditions gives $2 \leq x < 3$. The left-hand side is a sum of two increasing functions, so it is increasing; at the point $x = 3$ it equals $\sqrt{4} + \sqrt{1} = 3$, and this confirms that the inequality is satisfied **only to the left** of $x = 3$, as the graph shows.

</div>
