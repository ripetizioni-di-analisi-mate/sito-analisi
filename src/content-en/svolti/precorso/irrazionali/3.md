## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x - 1} < 2 .
$$

The inequality is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x - 1 \geq 0 \\
2 > 0 \\
x - 1 < 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
x < 5
\end{cases}
$$

The solution is $1 \leq x < 5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The solving system contains the domain condition $x - 1 \geq 0$, the positivity of the right-hand side and the comparison between the radicand and the square of the right-hand side. The right-hand side is the constant $2$, which is positive, so the condition $2 > 0$ is satisfied for every $x$ and excludes nothing. The conditions $x \geq 1$ and $x < 5$ remain, and they hold together on the interval indicated. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 2$ we have $\sqrt{1} = 1 < 2$, while for $x = 10$ we have $\sqrt{9} = 3 > 2$, and indeed $10$ does not belong to the solution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x + 6} \leq x .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 6 \geq 0 \\
x \geq 0 \\
x + 6 \leq x^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -6 \\
x \geq 0 \\
x^2 - x - 6 \geq 0
\end{cases}
$$

The second-degree inequality factors:
$$
(x - 3)(x + 2) \geq 0
$$
$$
x \leq -2 \ \vee \ x \geq 3
$$

The intersection with the conditions $x \geq -6$ and $x \geq 0$ gives the solution $x \geq 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is non-strict, so the right-hand side must satisfy $x \geq 0$ and the comparison between radicand and square is non-strict as well. The inequality $x^2 - x - 6 \geq 0$ has roots $-2$ and $3$ and is satisfied outside the interval between the roots. Of the three conditions, $x \geq 0$ eliminates the half-line $x \leq -2$, therefore the half-line $x \geq 3$ remains, which also satisfies $x \geq -6$. Checking at the endpoint and with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 3$ we have $\sqrt{9} = 3 \leq 3$, while for $x = 2$ we have $\sqrt{8} > 2$, since $8 > 4$, and indeed $2$ does not belong to the solution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{2x + 5} < x + 1 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 5 \geq 0 \\
x + 1 > 0 \\
2x + 5 < (x + 1)^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -\dfrac{5}{2} \\
x > -1 \\
2x + 5 < x^2 + 2x + 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -\dfrac{5}{2} \\
x > -1 \\
x^2 > 4
\end{cases}
$$

The condition $x^2 > 4$ is equivalent to $x < -2 \ \vee \ x > 2$; the intersection with $x > -1$ gives the solution $x > 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side contains the variable, so the condition $x + 1 > 0$ enters the system alongside the domain condition. In the expansion of $(x + 1)^2$ the $2x$ terms cancel with those on the left-hand side and the condition $x^2 > 4$ remains, satisfied outside the closed interval with endpoints $-2$ and $2$. Since the condition $x > -1$ excludes the half-line $x < -2$, the solution is the half-line $x > 2$, which also satisfies the domain condition. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 3$ we have $\sqrt{11} < 4$, since $11 < 16$, while for $x = 2$ we have $\sqrt{9} = 3$, which is not less than $3$.

</div>
