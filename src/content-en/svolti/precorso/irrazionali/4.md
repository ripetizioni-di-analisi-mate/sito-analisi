## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x + 4} > 2 .
$$

The right-hand side is $2 \geq 0$ for every $x$, so the first system of the union has **no solutions** and what remains is
$$
\begin{cases}
2 \geq 0 \\
x + 4 > 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
2 \geq 0 \\
x > 0
\end{cases}
$$

The solution is $x > 0$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The general rule for $\sqrt{A} > B$ is the **union of two systems**, one for $B < 0$ and one for $B \geq 0$. The right-hand side is the constant $2$, *never* negative, so the case $B < 0$ **does not occur** and what remains is the comparison between the radicand and the square $4$. The domain condition $x + 4 \geq 0$ follows from $x + 4 > 4$ and need not be added. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = 5$ we have $\sqrt{9} = 3 > 2$, while for $x = 0$ we have $\sqrt{4} = 2$, which is not greater than $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x + 2} > x .
$$

The inequality is equivalent to the union of two systems:
$$
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
x + 2 \geq 0
\end{cases}
\ \cup \
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
x + 2 > x^2
\end{cases}
$$

**First system.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \\
x \geq -2
\end{cases}
$$
The system gives $-2 \leq x < 0$.

**Second system.** The inequality $x^2 - x - 2 < 0$ factors:
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
(x - 2)(x + 1) < 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
-1 < x < 2
\end{cases}
$$
The system gives $0 \leq x < 2$.

The **union** of the two systems is the solution $-2 \leq x < 2$.

::grafico{f="sqrt(x+2);x" xmin=-3 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 area="sqrt(x+2);x;-2;2" punti="-2,0,pieno;2,2,vuoto" titolo="The graph of $y = \sqrt{x + 2}$ lies above the line $y = x$ for $-2 \leq x < 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The sign of the right-hand side is *not* known in advance, so the **two cases** are distinguished. Where $x < 0$ the right-hand side is negative and the root, which is never negative, exceeds it at *every* point where it exists; the domain condition $x \geq -2$ is then enough. Where $x \geq 0$ both sides are nonnegative and the squares are compared: the inequality $x^2 - x - 2 < 0$ is satisfied between the roots $-1$ and $2$. The two contributions are adjacent intervals and their **union** is the interval $-2 \leq x < 2$. Checking with a test value confirms the result: for $x = -2$ we have $\sqrt{0} = 0 > -2$, for $x = 1$ we have $\sqrt{3} > 1$, since $3 > 1$, while for $x = 2$ we have $\sqrt{4} = 2$, which is not greater than $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x^2 - 4} \geq x - 1 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x - 1 < 0 \\
x^2 - 4 \geq 0
\end{cases}
\ \cup \
\begin{cases}
x - 1 \geq 0 \\
x^2 - 4 \geq (x - 1)^2
\end{cases}
$$

**First system.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x < 1 \\
x \leq -2 \ \vee \ x \geq 2
\end{cases}
$$
The system gives $x \leq -2$.

**Second system.**
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
x^2 - 4 \geq x^2 - 2x + 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
2x \geq 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 1 \\
x \geq \dfrac{5}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
The system gives $x \geq \dfrac{5}{2}$.

The **union** of the two systems is the solution $x \leq -2 \ \vee \ x \geq \dfrac{5}{2}$.

::grafico{f="sqrt(x^2-4);x-1" xmin=-6 xmax=7 ymin=-6 ymax=6 area="sqrt(x^2-4);x-1;-6;-2|sqrt(x^2-4);x-1;2.5;7" punti="-2,0,pieno;2.5,1.5,pieno" titolo="The graph of $y = \sqrt{x^2 - 4}$ lies above the line $y = x - 1$, or touches it, for $x \leq -2$ and for $x \geq \dfrac{5}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is **non-strict**, so in the second system the comparison between radicand and square is non-strict as well. In the first system the **domain condition** $x^2 - 4 \geq 0$ is satisfied outside the open interval with endpoints $-2$ and $2$; the intersection with $x < 1$ leaves the half-line $x \leq -2$. In the second system the $x^2$ terms cancel and a **first-degree inequality** remains, hence $x \geq \dfrac{5}{2}$, which also satisfies $x \geq 1$. Checking with test values confirms the result: for $x = -3$ we have $\sqrt{5} \geq -4$, for $x = \dfrac{5}{2}$ both sides equal $\dfrac{3}{2}$, while for $x = 2$ we have $\sqrt{0} = 0 < 1$, and indeed $2$ does *not* belong to the solution.

</div>
