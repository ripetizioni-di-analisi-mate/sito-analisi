## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\sqrt{2x - 1} = 3 .
$$

The equation is equivalent to the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3 \geq 0 \\
2x - 1 = 9
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
3 \geq 0 \\
x = 5
\end{cases}
$$

The solution is $x = 5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is the constant $3$, which is nonnegative, so the condition $B \geq 0$ is satisfied for every $x$ and only the equation between the radicand and the square of the right-hand side remains. From $2x - 1 = 9$ it follows that $x = 5$. Checking by substitution confirms the result, since the radicand equals $2 \cdot 5 - 1 = 9$ and $\sqrt{9} = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\sqrt{x + 3} = x - 3 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x - 3 \geq 0 \\
x + 3 = (x - 3)^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
x + 3 = x^2 - 6x + 9
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
x^2 - 7x + 6 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

The second-degree equation factors:
$$
(x - 1)(x - 6) = 0
$$
$$
x = 1 \ \vee \ x = 6
$$

The value $x = 1$ does not satisfy the condition $x \geq 3$ and is discarded. The solution is $x = 6$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side contains the variable, so the condition $x - 3 \geq 0$ enters the system and decides which roots are acceptable. Squaring gives the equation $x^2 - 7x + 6 = 0$, with roots $1$ and $6$. Since $1 < 3$, the first root does not satisfy the condition and is discarded; indeed, for $x = 1$ the left-hand side equals $\sqrt{4} = 2$ and the right-hand side equals $-2$. Checking by substitution confirms the remaining solution: for $x = 6$ we have $\sqrt{9} = 3 = 6 - 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} = 2x .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
2x \geq 0 \\
x^2 + 3x = 4x^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
3x^2 - 3x = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
3x(x - 1) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

The roots $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ both satisfy the condition $x \geq 0$.

The solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceptability condition is $2x \geq 0$, that is, $x \geq 0$. Squaring, the radicand $x^2 + 3x$ must be set equal to $(2x)^2 = 4x^2$; moving everything to one side gives $3x^2 - 3x = 0$, which factors by factoring out $3x$. Both roots are nonnegative, so neither is discarded. Checking by substitution confirms the result: for $x = 0$ both sides equal $0$, and for $x = 1$ we have $\sqrt{1 + 3} = 2 = 2 \cdot 1$.

</div>
