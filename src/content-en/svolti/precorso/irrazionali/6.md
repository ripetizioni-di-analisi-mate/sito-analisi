## Exercise 1

As the real parameter $k$ varies, determine the number of solutions of the equation
$$
\sqrt{4 - x^2} = k
$$
and compute them.

$$
\begin{cases}
k \geq 0 \\
4 - x^2 = k^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
k \geq 0 \\
x^2 = 4 - k^2
\end{cases}
$$

- For $k < 0$ the equation has no solutions.
- For $0 \leq k < 2$ the equation has two solutions, $x = -\sqrt{4 - k^2}$ and $x = \sqrt{4 - k^2}$.
- For $k = 2$ the equation has the single solution $x = 0$.
- For $k > 2$ the equation has no solutions.

::grafico{f="sqrt(4-x^2);1" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="-1.732,1,pieno;1.732,1,pieno;0,2,pieno" legenda="y = \sqrt{4 - x^2};y = k \text{ with } k = 1" titolo="For $k = 1$ the line $y = 1$ meets the semicircle $y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$ at the points with abscissas $-\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{3}$; for $k = 2$ it touches it only at $(0, 2)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is the parameter $k$, which for each fixed value is a constant. The **solving system** for $\sqrt{A} = B$ therefore applies with $B = k$. The condition $B \geq 0$ becomes $k \geq 0$, and squaring gives $4 - x^2 = k^2$. For $k < 0$ the first condition is false, therefore the equation has no solutions.

For $k \geq 0$ the equation $x^2 = 4 - k^2$ remains, and its number of solutions depends on the sign of $4 - k^2$. If $4 - k^2 > 0$, that is, for $0 \leq k < 2$, the solutions are the two opposite numbers $\pm\sqrt{4 - k^2}$. If $4 - k^2 = 0$, that is, for $k = 2$, the only solution is $x = 0$. If $4 - k^2 < 0$, that is, for $k > 2$, a square would have to be negative, so there are no solutions.

The graph of $y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$ is the upper semicircle with center at the origin and radius $2$, because $y \geq 0$ and $x^2 + y^2 = 4$. The solutions are the abscissas of the points where the line $y = k$ meets the semicircle, and this confirms the **discussion**. The check for $k = 1$ gives $x = \pm\sqrt{3}$, with radicand $4 - 3 = 1$ and $\sqrt{1} = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

As the real parameter $k$ varies, determine the number of solutions of the equation
$$
\sqrt{x + k} = x .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
x + k = x^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
x^2 - x - k = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Delta = 1 + 4k ,
\qquad
x_1 = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 + 4k}}{2} ,
\qquad
x_2 = \frac{1 + \sqrt{1 + 4k}}{2}
$$
$$
x_1 \geq 0 \iff \sqrt{1 + 4k} \leq 1 \iff 1 + 4k \leq 1 \iff k \leq 0
$$

- For $k < -\dfrac{1}{4}$ the equation has no solutions.
- For $k = -\dfrac{1}{4}$ the equation has the single solution $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
- For $-\dfrac{1}{4} < k \leq 0$ the equation has two solutions, $x_1$ and $x_2$.
- For $k > 0$ the equation has the single solution $x_2$.

::grafico{f="x^2-x+0*sqrt(x);-0.125;1" xmin=-1 xmax=2.5 ymin=-1 ymax=2.5 punti="0.5,-0.25,pieno;0.146,-0.125,pieno;0.854,-0.125,pieno;1.618,1,pieno" legenda="y = x^2 - x \text{ with } x \geq 0;y = -\frac{1}{8};y = 1" titolo="The line $y = -\dfrac{1}{8}$ meets the graph of $y = x^2 - x$, with $x \geq 0$, at two points; the line $y = 1$ at a single point"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is $x$, so the condition of the **solving system** is $x \geq 0$. Squaring gives the quadratic equation $x^2 - x - k = 0$, with discriminant $\Delta = 1 + 4k$. For $k < -\dfrac{1}{4}$ the discriminant is negative and there are no solutions; for $k = -\dfrac{1}{4}$ there is the double root $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which is positive and is therefore accepted.

For $k > -\dfrac{1}{4}$ the roots are $x_1 < x_2$. The root $x_2$ is at least $\dfrac{1}{2}$, because $\sqrt{1 + 4k} \geq 0$, therefore it always satisfies the condition $x \geq 0$. The root $x_1$ is nonnegative if and only if $\sqrt{1 + 4k} \leq 1$; since both sides are nonnegative, squaring gives $k \leq 0$. Hence for $-\dfrac{1}{4} < k \leq 0$ there are two solutions, and for $k > 0$ the root $x_1$ is negative and is discarded.

The system is equivalent to $k = x^2 - x$ with $x \geq 0$, therefore the solutions are the abscissas of the points common to the line $y = k$ and to the arc of parabola in the graph. The arc starts at $(0, 0)$ and has its vertex at $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$, and this explains the values $k = -\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $k = 0$ in the discussion. For $k = 0$ the solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 1$, and indeed $\sqrt{0} = 0$ and $\sqrt{1} = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine for which values of the real parameter $a$ the equation
$$
\sqrt{x^2 + a} = x + 1
$$
has solutions, and compute them.

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 \geq 0 \\
x^2 + a = (x + 1)^2
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -1 \\
x^2 + a = x^2 + 2x + 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -1 \\
x = \dfrac{a - 1}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\frac{a - 1}{2} \geq -1 \iff a - 1 \geq -2 \iff a \geq -1
$$

- For $a \geq -1$ the equation has the single solution $x = \dfrac{a - 1}{2}$.
- For $a < -1$ the equation has no solutions.

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side contains the variable, so the **solving system** for $\sqrt{A} = B$ contains the condition $x + 1 \geq 0$. The expansion of $(x + 1)^2$ contains the term $x^2$, which cancels with the one in the radicand; what remains is the linear equation $2x = a - 1$, which for every value of $a$ has the single root $x = \dfrac{a - 1}{2}$.

The root is acceptable if it satisfies the condition $x \geq -1$. Multiplying both sides of $\dfrac{a - 1}{2} \geq -1$ by $2$ gives $a - 1 \geq -2$, that is, $a \geq -1$. For $a < -1$ the root is less than $-1$, the right-hand side is negative and the root **is discarded**. The domain restriction $x^2 + a \geq 0$ need not be imposed separately, because in the system the radicand equals the square $(x + 1)^2$.

Two checks confirm the discussion. For $a = 5$ the solution is $x = 2$, and indeed $\sqrt{4 + 5} = 3 = 2 + 1$. For $a = -3$ the root $x = -2$ makes the radicand equal to $4 - 3 = 1$, but the left-hand side equals $1$ and the right-hand side equals $-1$, so $x = -2$ is not a solution, as predicted by the condition $a \geq -1$.

</div>
