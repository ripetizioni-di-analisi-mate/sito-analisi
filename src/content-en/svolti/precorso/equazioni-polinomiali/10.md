## Exercise 1

A rectangle has perimeter $14\ \text{cm}$ and area $10\ \text{cm}^2$. Determine the lengths of its sides.

$$
\text{base } x, \qquad \text{height } 7 - x, \qquad 0 < x < 7
$$

$$
x\,(7 - x) = 10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 7x - x^2 = 10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0
$$

$$
\Delta = 49 - 40 = 9, \qquad x = \frac{7 \pm 3}{2}, \qquad x_1 = 2, \quad x_2 = 5
$$

$$
x = 2: \ \ \text{height } 5; \qquad x = 5: \ \ \text{height } 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We choose as unknown the base $x$, measured in centimeters. The perimeter is twice the sum of base and height, so the sum of base and height equals $7\ \text{cm}$ and the height is $7 - x$. The two lengths must be positive, therefore the **admissibility conditions** are $x > 0$ and $7 - x > 0$, that is, $0 < x < 7$.

The area is the product of base and height, so the condition on the area is written $x\,(7 - x) = 10$. Moving every term to the right-hand side gives the quadratic equation $x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$, with discriminant $9$ and solutions $2$ and $5$. The same roots are found with **sum and product**, because $2 + 5 = 7$ and $2 \cdot 5 = 10$.

Both values lie between $0$ and $7$, so they are admissible. For $x = 2$ the height is $5$, for $x = 5$ the height is $2$, so the two solutions describe the same rectangle with base and height swapped. The sides of the rectangle therefore measure $2\ \text{cm}$ and $5\ \text{cm}$; indeed $2\,(2 + 5) = 14$ and $2 \cdot 5 = 10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine for which values of $x$ the parabola $y = x^2 - 3x$ lies below the line $y = x + 5$, and find the points where the two curves meet.

$$
x^2 - 3x < x + 5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2 - 4x - 5 < 0
$$

$$
x^2 - 4x - 5 = (x - 5)(x + 1), \qquad \text{roots } -1 \ \text{and} \ 5
$$

$$
-1 < x < 5
$$

$$
x = -1: \ \ y = -1 + 5 = 4; \qquad x = 5: \ \ y = 5 + 5 = 10
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-3*x;x+5" xmin=-3 xmax=7 ymin=-4 ymax=13 area="x+5;x^2-3*x;-1;5" punti="-1,4,vuoto;5,10,vuoto" titolo="The parabola $y = x^2 - 3x$ lies below the line $y = x + 5$ (dashed) for $-1 < x < 5$; the two curves meet at the points $(-1, 4)$ and $(5, 10)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For the same abscissa $x$, the parabola lies below the line when the ordinate of its point is less than that of the point of the line, that is, when $x^2 - 3x < x + 5$. Moving every term to the left-hand side gives the quadratic inequality $x^2 - 4x - 5 < 0$.

The trinomial has roots $-1$ and $5$, which have sum $4$ and product $-5$, and positive leading coefficient. By the **sign of the quadratic trinomial** it is negative between the roots, so the parabola lies below the line for $-1 < x < 5$. The inequality is strict, therefore the endpoints are excluded.

At the roots the trinomial vanishes, so at $x = -1$ and at $x = 5$ the two ordinates coincide and the curves meet. The ordinates are computed with the equation of the line, which is simpler, and we obtain the points $(-1, 4)$ and $(5, 10)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the set of real numbers $x$ for which the expression
$$
\sqrt{\frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 1}}
$$
is defined.

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 \neq 0 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x + 1} \geq 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 1} = \frac{(x + 2)(x - 2)}{x + 1}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 & x < -2 & -2 < x < -1 & -1 < x < 2 & x > 2 \\ \hline
x + 2 & - & + & + & + \\
x + 1 & - & - & + & + \\
x - 2 & - & - & - & + \\ \hline
\text{quotient} & - & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
-2 \leq x < -1 \ \text{ or } \ x \geq 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression contains a fraction with the unknown in the denominator and a square root. The fraction is defined when the denominator is nonzero, and a square root is defined in the real numbers when the radicand is greater than or equal to zero. The two conditions must hold *together*, so they form a system.

The second condition is a **rational inequality** with the sign $\geq$. The numerator is a difference of squares and factors as $(x + 2)(x - 2)$, therefore the sign chart has three rows, with the zeros $-2$, $-1$ and $2$. By the theorem on the **sign of a quotient**, the sign of the fraction in each column is the product of the signs, so the quotient is positive for $-2 < x < -1$ and for $x > 2$.

The sign $\geq$ includes the zeros $-2$ and $2$ of the numerator. The point $-1$ makes the denominator vanish, so it is excluded, as required by the first condition of the system. The expression is therefore defined for $-2 \leq x < -1$ or $x \geq 2$.

</div>
