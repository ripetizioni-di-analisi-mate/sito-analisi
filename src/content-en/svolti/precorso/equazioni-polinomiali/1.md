## Exercise 1

Solve the incomplete equations
$$
2x^2 - 6x = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad 3x^2 - 12 = 0 .
$$

$$
2x^2 - 6x = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2x\,(x - 3) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 0 \ \text{ or } \ x = 3
$$

$$
3x^2 - 12 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^2 = 4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \pm 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation has no constant term, so the common factor $2x$ is factored out. By the zero product property, the product $2x\,(x - 3)$ is zero when $2x = 0$ or $x - 3 = 0$, therefore the solutions are $0$ and $3$.

The second equation has no first-degree term. Isolating $x^2$ gives $x^2 = 4$; since $4 > 0$, the solutions are the two numbers whose square is $4$, that is, $2$ and $-2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
2x^2 + x - 3 = 0 .
$$

$$
\Delta = 1^2 - 4 \cdot 2 \cdot (-3) = 1 + 24 = 25
$$

$$
x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{25}}{2 \cdot 2} = \frac{-1 \pm 5}{4}
$$

$$
x_1 = \frac{-1 - 5}{4} = -\frac{3}{2}, \qquad x_2 = \frac{-1 + 5}{4} = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is complete, with $a = 2$, $b = 1$ and $c = -3$, so the quadratic formula applies. The discriminant equals $25 > 0$, therefore there are two distinct real solutions.

The square root of the discriminant is $5$ and the formula gives the two values $\dfrac{-1 - 5}{4} = -\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $\dfrac{-1 + 5}{4} = 1$. Checking by substitution confirms the result: for $x = 1$ we get $2 + 1 - 3 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equations
$$
4x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0 .
$$

$$
4x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0: \qquad \Delta = 144 - 144 = 0, \qquad x = \frac{12}{8} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

$$
x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0: \qquad \Delta = 4 - 20 = -16 < 0
$$

The first equation has the double solution $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$; the second has no real solutions.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first equation the discriminant is zero, so there is only one real solution, a double one, given by $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = \dfrac{12}{8} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. Indeed, the trinomial is the square of a binomial, since $4x^2 - 12x + 9 = (2x - 3)^2$, and a square vanishes only where its base vanishes.

In the second equation the discriminant is negative, therefore the quadratic formula would require the square root of a negative number, which does not exist in the real numbers. Hence the equation has no real solutions.

</div>
