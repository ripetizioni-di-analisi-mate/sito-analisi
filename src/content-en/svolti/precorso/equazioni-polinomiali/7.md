## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{x + 1}{x - 2} = 3 .
$$

$$
x - 2 \neq 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x \neq 2
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x + 1}{x - 2} - 3 &= 0 \\[6pt]
\frac{x + 1 - 3\,(x - 2)}{x - 2} &= 0 \\[6pt]
\frac{7 - 2x}{x - 2} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
7 - 2x = 0 \\[2pt]
x \neq 2
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \frac{7}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown appears in the denominator, so the equation is **rational** and the first step is the **domain condition**. The denominator $x - 2$ vanishes at $2$, therefore the domain consists of the real numbers different from $2$.

The term $3$ is moved to the left-hand side and everything is reduced to the common denominator $x - 2$; the numerator becomes $x + 1 - 3x + 6 = 7 - 2x$. By the theorem on the **zero of a fraction**, the fraction is zero when the numerator is zero and the denominator is nonzero, that is, when the two conditions of the system hold together.

The numerator vanishes at $x = \dfrac{7}{2}$, which is different from $2$ and therefore belongs to the domain. Hence the equation has the **solution** $x = \dfrac{7}{2}$. The check by substitution gives $\dfrac{\frac{7}{2} + 1}{\frac{7}{2} - 2} = \dfrac{\frac{9}{2}}{\frac{3}{2}} = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{x}{x - 1} + \frac{2}{x + 1} = \frac{2}{x^2 - 1} .
$$

$$
x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1), \qquad
\begin{cases}
x - 1 \neq 0 \\[2pt]
x + 1 \neq 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x \neq 1, \ \ x \neq -1
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x\,(x + 1) + 2\,(x - 1) - 2}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} &= 0 \\[6pt]
\frac{x^2 + 3x - 4}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
x^2 + 3x - 4 = (x + 4)(x - 1) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = -4 \ \text{ or } \ x = 1
$$

The value $x = 1$ does not satisfy the domain condition, so the only solution is $x = -4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $x^2 - 1$ is a **difference of squares** and factors as $(x - 1)(x + 1)$, therefore the common denominator is $(x - 1)(x + 1)$ and the **domain conditions** exclude $1$ and $-1$.

Moving the right-hand side and reducing to the common denominator, the first term is multiplied by $x + 1$, the second by $x - 1$, and the third already has the common denominator. The numerator is $x^2 + x + 2x - 2 - 2 = x^2 + 3x - 4$, a **special trinomial**. Since $4 + (-1) = 3$ and $4 \cdot (-1) = -4$, it factors as $(x + 4)(x - 1)$, with zeros $-4$ and $1$.

The value $x = 1$ makes the numerator vanish but also the denominator, that is, at $1$ the original fractions are not defined, so it is **discarded**. The value $-4$ belongs to the domain, therefore the equation has the **only solution** $x = -4$. The check gives $\dfrac{-4}{-5} + \dfrac{2}{-3} = \dfrac{4}{5} - \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{2}{15}$ on the left-hand side and $\dfrac{2}{16 - 1} = \dfrac{2}{15}$ on the right-hand side.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{x^2 + 2x + 2}{x^2 - x - 6} \leq 0 .
$$

$$
x^2 - x - 6 = (x + 2)(x - 3), \qquad x \neq -2, \ \ x \neq 3
$$

$$
x^2 + 2x + 2: \qquad \Delta = 4 - 8 = -4 < 0
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & x < -2 & -2 < x < 3 & x > 3 \\ \hline
x^2 + 2x + 2 & + & + & + \\
x + 2 & - & + & + \\
x - 3 & - & - & + \\ \hline
\text{quotient} & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
-2 < x < 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is a **special trinomial**, with $(-3) + 2 = -1$ and $(-3) \cdot 2 = -6$, so it factors as $(x + 2)(x - 3)$ and the **domain conditions** exclude $-2$ and $3$.

The numerator has a negative discriminant and a positive leading coefficient, therefore by the sign of the quadratic trinomial it is **positive for every** $x$; therefore in the chart its row carries only plus signs and introduces no new zeros. By the theorem on the **sign of a quotient**, the sign of the fraction on each interval is the product of the signs in the column, so it depends only on the factors $x + 2$ and $x - 3$.

The quotient is negative for $-2 < x < 3$. The direction $\leq$ would include the zeros of the numerator, which however do *not* exist, and the endpoints $-2$ and $3$ are excluded by the domain conditions. The solutions are therefore $-2 < x < 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{x}{x - 2} - \frac{1}{x} \geq \frac{4}{x^2 - 2x} .
$$

$$
x^2 - 2x = x\,(x - 2), \qquad x \neq 0, \ \ x \neq 2
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x \cdot x - (x - 2) - 4}{x\,(x - 2)} &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
\frac{x^2 - x - 2}{x\,(x - 2)} &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
\frac{(x + 1)(x - 2)}{x\,(x - 2)} &\geq 0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x + 1}{x} \geq 0 \quad \text{for } x \neq 2
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & x < -1 & -1 < x < 0 & x > 0 \\ \hline
x + 1 & - & + & + \\
x & - & - & + \\ \hline
\dfrac{x+1}{x} & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
x \leq -1 \ \text{ or } \ 0 < x < 2 \ \text{ or } \ x > 2
$$

::grafico{f="(x+1)/x" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=6 asintoti="x=0;y=1" area="(x+1)/x;0;-5;-1|(x+1)/x;0;0.18;5" punti="-1,0,pieno;2,1.5,vuoto" titolo="The difference between the left-hand and the right-hand side coincides with $y = \dfrac{x + 1}{x}$ except at $x = 2$ (open dot), which is excluded by the domain conditions; the shaded regions correspond to the solutions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $x^2 - 2x$ factors by **factoring out** $x$ as $x\,(x - 2)$, which is also the common denominator. The **domain conditions** exclude $0$ and $2$ and are derived from the original expression, before any simplification.

Moving the right-hand side and reducing to the common denominator, the numerator is $x^2 - x + 2 - 4 = x^2 - x - 2$, a special trinomial that factors as $(x + 1)(x - 2)$. In the domain $x - 2 \neq 0$ holds, so the factor $x - 2$ **cancels** and the inequality is equivalent to $\dfrac{x + 1}{x} \geq 0$ with the condition $x \neq 2$.

The sign chart gives a positive quotient for $x < -1$ and for $x > 0$; the direction $\geq$ includes the zero of the numerator $x = -1$ and excludes $x = 0$. The comparison with the **domain** finally removes the point $x = 2$, which the simplification had hidden: the simplified fraction equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$ at $2$, but the original inequality is not defined there. The solutions are $x \leq -1$ or $0 < x < 2$ or $x > 2$.

</div>
