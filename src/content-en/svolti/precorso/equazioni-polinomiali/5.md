## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{x - 3}{x + 1} \geq 0 .
$$

$$
N(x) = x - 3 \geq 0 \ \text{ for } \ x \geq 3; \qquad D(x) = x + 1 > 0 \ \text{ for } \ x > -1
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & x < -1 & -1 < x < 3 & x > 3 \\ \hline
x - 3 & - & - & + \\
x + 1 & - & + & + \\ \hline
\dfrac{x-3}{x+1} & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
x < -1 \ \text{ or } \ x \geq 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is already in the form of a quotient, so the signs of numerator and denominator are studied: the numerator vanishes at $3$ and the denominator at $-1$, and these two points divide the line into three intervals. The quotient has the same sign as the product of the two factors, therefore it is positive where the **signs agree**.

The direction is $\geq$, so the zero of the numerator $x = 3$ is **included** in the solutions. The point $x = -1$ makes the denominator vanish, therefore it is **excluded** for *every* direction, since the fraction is not defined at that point. The solutions are $x < -1$ or $x \geq 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 1} < 0 .
$$

$$
\frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 1} = \frac{(x + 2)(x - 2)}{x - 1}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 & x < -2 & -2 < x < 1 & 1 < x < 2 & x > 2 \\ \hline
x + 2 & - & + & + & + \\
x - 1 & - & - & + & + \\
x - 2 & - & - & - & + \\ \hline
\text{quotient} & - & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
x < -2 \ \text{ or } \ 1 < x < 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is a **difference of squares** and factors as $(x + 2)(x - 2)$, so there are three factors involved, with zeros $-2$, $1$ and $2$. In the chart the sign of the quotient in each column is the product of the three signs, therefore the quotient is negative where the minus signs appear an **odd number of times**.

The quotient is negative for $x < -2$ and for $1 < x < 2$. The direction is the strict $<$, so the zeros of the numerator are excluded, and the point $x = 1$ is **excluded** in any case because it makes the denominator vanish. The solutions are $x < -2$ or $1 < x < 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{2x}{x - 1} \leq 1 .
$$

$$
\frac{2x}{x - 1} - 1 \leq 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{2x - (x - 1)}{x - 1} \leq 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{x + 1}{x - 1} \leq 0
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & x < -1 & -1 < x < 1 & x > 1 \\ \hline
x + 1 & - & + & + \\
x - 1 & - & - & + \\ \hline
\dfrac{x+1}{x-1} & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
-1 \leq x < 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is nonzero, so the first step moves the term $1$ to the left-hand side and reduces to a **common denominator**; the numerator becomes $2x - (x - 1) = x + 1$. The denominator depends on $x$ and changes sign, therefore the inequality is solved with the **sign chart** of the fraction $\dfrac{x + 1}{x - 1}$, *without* multiplying both sides by $x - 1$.

The quotient is negative where the signs differ, that is, for $-1 < x < 1$. The direction is $\leq$, so the zero of the numerator $x = -1$ is included, while $x = 1$ remains **excluded** because it makes the denominator vanish. The solutions are $-1 \leq x < 1$.

</div>
