## Exercise 1

Solve, as the real parameter $a$ varies, the equation
$$
\left( a^2 - 1 \right) x = a + 1 .
$$

$$
a^2 - 1 = (a - 1)(a + 1)
$$

$$
a \neq \pm 1: \qquad x = \frac{a + 1}{(a - 1)(a + 1)} = \frac{1}{a - 1}
$$

$$
a = 1: \qquad 0 \cdot x = 2 \qquad \text{inconsistent}
$$

$$
a = -1: \qquad 0 \cdot x = 0 \qquad \text{identity}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is linear in $x$, with coefficient $a^2 - 1$ and constant term $a + 1$. To isolate $x$ both sides are divided by the coefficient, and this division is allowed *only if* the coefficient is nonzero. The coefficient factors as a difference of squares into $(a - 1)(a + 1)$, so it vanishes for $a = 1$ and for $a = -1$, and the discussion separates these two values from all the others.

For $a \neq \pm 1$ the division is allowed. The factor $a + 1$ appears in the numerator and in the denominator and is nonzero, therefore it cancels, and the equation has the **unique solution** $x = \dfrac{1}{a - 1}$. For example, for $a = 2$ the solution is $x = 1$, and indeed $3 \cdot 1 = 3$.

For $a = 1$ the left-hand side equals $0 \cdot x = 0$ for every $x$, while the right-hand side equals $2$. The equality $0 = 2$ is false, so the equation is **inconsistent**.

For $a = -1$ both the coefficient and the constant term vanish, and the equation becomes $0 = 0$, true for every $x$. The equation is therefore an **identity** and every real number is a solution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine, as the real parameter $k$ varies, how many real solutions the equation
$$
kx^2 - 4x + k = 0
$$
has, and write them when they exist.

$$
k = 0: \qquad -4x = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 0
$$

$$
k \neq 0: \qquad \frac{\Delta}{4} = (-2)^2 - k \cdot k = 4 - k^2 = (2 - k)(2 + k)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2 < k < 2,\ k \neq 0: &\qquad x = \frac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - k^2}}{k} \\[6pt]
k = 2: &\qquad 2\,(x - 1)^2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 1 \ \text{(double)} \\[6pt]
k = -2: &\qquad -2\,(x + 1)^2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = -1 \ \text{(double)} \\[6pt]
k < -2 \ \text{or} \ k > 2: &\qquad \text{no real solution}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parameter multiplies the term $x^2$, so for $k = 0$ the equation changes degree. In that case it becomes $-4x = 0$, a linear equation, with the only solution $x = 0$.

For $k \neq 0$ the equation is quadratic and the coefficient $b = -4$ is even, therefore the **reduced formula** is used with $\beta = -2$. A quarter of the discriminant is $4 - k^2$, a trinomial in $k$ with roots $\pm 2$ and negative leading coefficient, so it is positive for $-2 < k < 2$, zero for $k = \pm 2$ and negative for $|k| > 2$.

For $-2 < k < 2$ with $k \neq 0$ there are **two distinct** solutions, given by $\dfrac{-\beta \pm \sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{4}}}{a}$. For $k = 2$ the discriminant is zero and the double solution is $x = -\dfrac{\beta}{a} = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1$; for $k = -2$ the double solution is $x = -\dfrac{\beta}{a} = \dfrac{2}{-2} = -1$. For $|k| > 2$ the discriminant is negative, so the equation **has no real solutions**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine for which values of the real parameter $m$ the inequality
$$
x^2 - 2mx + m + 6 > 0
$$
is satisfied by every real number $x$.

$$
\frac{\Delta}{4} = m^2 - (m + 6) = m^2 - m - 6 = (m - 3)(m + 2)
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta}{4} < 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (m - 3)(m + 2) < 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -2 < m < 3
$$

$$
m = 3: \quad x^2 - 6x + 9 = (x - 3)^2, \qquad m = -2: \quad x^2 + 4x + 4 = (x + 2)^2
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-2*x+7;x^2-6*x+9" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=12 punti="3,0,pieno" titolo="For $m = 1$ the trinomial $x^2 - 2x + 7$ is positive for every $x$; for $m = 3$ the trinomial $x^2 - 6x + 9$ (dashed) vanishes at $x = 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The leading coefficient is $1 > 0$, so the parabola $y = x^2 - 2mx + m + 6$ opens upward for every $m$. By the **sign of the quadratic trinomial**, a trinomial with $a > 0$ is positive for *every* $x$ exactly when $\Delta < 0$.

The coefficient $b = -2m$ is even, therefore we compute $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = \beta^2 - ac$ with $\beta = -m$, and we obtain $m^2 - m - 6$. The trinomial in $m$ has roots $-2$ and $3$ and positive leading coefficient, so it is negative between the roots, and the condition is satisfied for $-2 < m < 3$.

The endpoints are excluded. For $m = 3$ the trinomial is $(x - 3)^2$, which vanishes at $x = 3$, so the strict inequality does not hold at that point; the same happens for $m = -2$ at $x = -2$. The required values are therefore $-2 < m < 3$.

</div>
