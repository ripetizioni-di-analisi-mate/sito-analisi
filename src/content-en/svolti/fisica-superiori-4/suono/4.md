## Exercise 1

At a point, the sound intensity is $I=1.6\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. Using $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, find the sound intensity level.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{I}{I_0}&=1.6\cdot10^6, \\[6pt]
\beta&=10\log_{10}(1.6\cdot10^6)\,\mathrm{dB} \\[6pt]
&=\bigl(60+10\log_{10}1.6\bigr)\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq62.04\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both intensities are expressed in the same unit. Dividing by the reference tells how many times the measured intensity exceeds $I_0$. The factor $10^6$ gives $60\,\mathrm{dB}$, and the factor $1.6$ adds just over $2\,\mathrm{dB}$. The result must fall between $60$ and $70\,\mathrm{dB}$ because $1<1.6<10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A microphone measures $I_A=2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ with source A alone. An independent source B produces one quarter of this intensity when it is on alone. Using $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, find the individual levels and the total level.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta_A&=10\log_{10}(2\cdot10^6)\,\mathrm{dB}\simeq63.01\,\mathrm{dB}, \\[6pt]
\beta_B&=\beta_A+10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac14\right)\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq56.99\,\mathrm{dB}, \\[6pt]
I_{\mathrm{tot}}&=2.50\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}, \\[6pt]
\beta_{\mathrm{tot}}&=10\log_{10}(2.50\cdot10^6)\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq63.98\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio $\dfrac{I_B}{I_A}=\dfrac14$ corresponds to a decrease of about $6.02\,\mathrm{dB}$. With both sources on, add the average intensities, since the two signals are independent. The contribution from B is $0.50\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, so the total is $1.25I_A$. Take the logarithm after forming this sum.

The total level exceeds that of A by about $0.97\,\mathrm{dB}$. This increase is smaller than the roughly $3.01\,\mathrm{dB}$ obtained with two equal intensities.

</div>
