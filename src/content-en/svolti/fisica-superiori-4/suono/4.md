## Exercise 1

At a point the sound intensity is $I=1.6\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. With $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, find the sound intensity level.

$$
\beta=10\log_{10}\frac{I}{I_0}\,\mathrm{dB}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{I}{I_0}&=\frac{1.6\cdot10^{-6}}{10^{-12}}=1.6\cdot10^6, \\[6pt]
\log_{10}(1.6\cdot10^6)&=\log_{10}1.6+\log_{10}10^6\simeq0.204+6, \\[6pt]
\beta&=10\cdot6.204\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq62.04\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two intensities expressed in the same unit are compared, so the ratio $\dfrac{I}{I_0}$ is a pure number. Dividing by the reference amounts to asking how many times the measured intensity exceeds $I_0$, and here it exceeds it by a factor of $1.6\cdot10^6$.

The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms. The factor $10^6$ contributes $6$, that is, $60\,\mathrm{dB}$ after multiplying by $10$, and the factor $1.6$ contributes $0.204$, that is, a little over $2\,\mathrm{dB}$. The level is **$62.04\,\mathrm{dB}$**.

The result must lie between $60$ and $70\,\mathrm{dB}$, because $1<1.6<10$ implies $0<\log_{10}1.6<1$ and therefore $6<\log_{10}(1.6\cdot10^6)<7$. The value obtained satisfies this condition. The factor $0.204$ is read with a calculator, since $10^{0.204}\simeq1.6$.

With the same method we can recover the intensity from the level by inverting the formula, $I=I_0\,10^{\frac{\beta}{10\,\mathrm{dB}}}$. For $\beta=62.04\,\mathrm{dB}$ we have $I=10^{-12}\cdot10^{6.204}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}\simeq1.6\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, which coincides with the given value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A microphone measures $I_A=2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ with only source A on. A source B, independent of A, produces on its own one quarter of that intensity. With $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, find the levels of the individual sources and the total level.

$$
\beta=10\log_{10}\frac{I}{I_0}\,\mathrm{dB}
\qquad
I_{\mathrm{tot}}=I_A+I_B
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta_A&=10\log_{10}(2\cdot10^6)\,\mathrm{dB}\simeq63.01\,\mathrm{dB}, \\[6pt]
I_B&=\frac{I_A}{4}=0.50\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}, \\[6pt]
\beta_B&=\beta_A+10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac14\right)\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq56.99\,\mathrm{dB}, \\[6pt]
I_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(2.00+0.50)\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}=2.50\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}, \\[6pt]
\beta_{\mathrm{tot}}&=10\log_{10}(2.50\cdot10^6)\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq63.98\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The level of A follows from the definition of $\beta$ with $\dfrac{I_A}{I_0}=2\cdot10^6$. For B we can avoid recomputing the whole logarithm. The difference between two levels referred to the same $I_0$ depends only on the ratio of the intensities, and $\dfrac{I_B}{I_A}=\dfrac14$ gives $10\log_{10}\dfrac14\simeq-6.02\,\mathrm{dB}$, so $\beta_B\simeq63.01-6.02=56.99\,\mathrm{dB}$.

With both sources on, the average intensities add, since the two signals are independent. The contribution of B is $0.50\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, so the total is $2.50\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}=1.25I_A$. The logarithm is applied only after this sum, because decibel levels do not add: adding $63.01\,\mathrm{dB}$ and $56.99\,\mathrm{dB}$ would give $120\,\mathrm{dB}$, a value that corresponds to an intensity of $1\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.

The total level is **$63.98\,\mathrm{dB}$**. It exceeds that of A by $10\log_{10}1.25\simeq0.97\,\mathrm{dB}$, which is obtained by adding this term to $\beta_A=63.01\,\mathrm{dB}$. This is an increase smaller than the roughly $3.01\,\mathrm{dB}$ we would have if the two intensities were equal.

</div>
