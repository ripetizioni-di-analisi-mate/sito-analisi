## Exercise 1

A sound has frequency $f=400\,\mathrm{Hz}$ in a medium where the speed is $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the wavelength and the period.

$$
u=\lambda f
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lambda=\frac{u}{f}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{340\,\mathrm{m/s}}{400\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} \\[6pt]
&= 0.85\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{400\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}}=2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm s, \\[6pt]
uT &= (340\,\mathrm{m/s})(2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm s)=0.85\,\mathrm m .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(2*pi*(x-0.2)/0.85)" xmin=0 xmax=1.28 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.35 nomi="x,Δp (Pa)" punti="0.2,1,pieno;1.05,1,pieno" segmenti="0.2,0,0.2,1;1.05,0,1.05,1" legenda="\Delta p(x)" titolo="The two pressure maxima are separated by one wavelength, equal to $0.85\,\mathrm m$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Frequency is the number of cycles per second and one cycle lasts $T$, so $f=\dfrac{1}{T}$. In one period the disturbance travels one wavelength, so the speed is the ratio $\dfrac{\lambda}{T}=\lambda f$. Since $u$ and $f$ are given, we solve for $\lambda$ by dividing both sides by $f$.

In the substitution we divide $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by $400\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The numerical quotient is $\dfrac{340}{400}=0.85$. The unit $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ in the denominator, together with the second already in the denominator of $\mathrm{m/s}$, leaves the meter. The wavelength is therefore **$0.85\,\mathrm m$**.

The period is the reciprocal of the frequency and equals $2.5\,\mathrm{ms}$. As a check, the product $uT$ is the distance traveled by the wavefront in one period and coincides with $\lambda$.

The graph is a snapshot of the pressure along the direction of propagation at a single instant, so the horizontal coordinate is a position. The maxima of the curve are the points of maximum compression and two consecutive maxima are one wavelength apart. The two marked maxima at $0.20\,\mathrm m$ and $1.05\,\mathrm m$ are $1.05-0.20=0.85\,\mathrm m$ apart, as in the calculated value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $500\,\mathrm{Hz}$ sound propagates in the same medium with $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the wavelength and the period measured by a stationary microphone. Then compare the wavelength with that of the $400\,\mathrm{Hz}$ sound.

$$
T=\frac{1}{f}
\qquad
\lambda=uT
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac{1}{500\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}}
=0.0020\,\mathrm s, \\[6pt]
\lambda &= uT
=(340\,\mathrm{m/s})(0.0020\,\mathrm s)
=0.68\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
\frac{\lambda_{500}}{\lambda_{400}} &= \frac{0.68\,\mathrm m}{0.85\,\mathrm m}=0.80=\frac{400}{500}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The microphone stays at the same point and measures a time interval between two compressions. The frequency indicates how many compressions arrive in one second, so the interval between two consecutive arrivals is the reciprocal of the frequency and equals **$2.0\,\mathrm{ms}$**. Between the two arrivals the wavefront advances $uT$, which is the spatial separation between compressions.

This gives $\lambda=0.68\,\mathrm m$. The same value follows from $\lambda=\dfrac{u}{f}$, since $uT=\dfrac{u}{f}$.

The medium is the same, so $u$ does not change and $\lambda$ is inversely proportional to $f$. Writing $\lambda_{500}=\dfrac{u}{500}$ and $\lambda_{400}=\dfrac{u}{400}$, $u$ cancels in the ratio and $\dfrac{\lambda_{500}}{\lambda_{400}}=\dfrac{400}{500}=0.80$ remains. The direct calculation with numerical values gives the same number. The higher-pitched sound has the shorter wavelength.

</div>
