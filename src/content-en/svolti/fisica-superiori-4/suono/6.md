## Exercise 1

An ideal pipe of length $L=0.60\,\mathrm m$ is open at both ends. With $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and negligible end corrections, find the fundamental frequency.

$$
L=\frac{\lambda_1}{2}
\qquad
f_1=\frac{u}{\lambda_1}=\frac{u}{2L}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_1&=2L=2\cdot0.60\,\mathrm m=1.20\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
f_1&=\frac{u}{\lambda_1}=\frac{340\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.20\,\mathrm m} \\[6pt]
&\simeq283.3\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,cos(pi*t/0.6),0,0.6" xmin=-0.035 xmax=0.68 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 nomi="x,s/sₘₐₓ" punti="0,1,pieno;0.3,0,pieno;0.6,-1,pieno" legenda="s(x)/s_{\max}" titolo="The fundamental has a displacement node at $x=0.30\,\mathrm m$ and an antinode at each end."}

<div class="spiegazione">

At both openings the air can move longitudinally, so the displacement profile has an antinode at each end. The fundamental mode is the one with the fewest internal nodes, that is, with the largest wavelength compatible with the two antinodes. It has a single node at the midpoint and two antinodes separated by half a wavelength, because the distance from one antinode to the next is half the spatial period of the wave. It follows that $L=\dfrac{\lambda_1}{2}$ and $\lambda_1=2L=1.20\,\mathrm m$.

The relation $u=\lambda f$ holds for every mode, so dividing the speed of sound by this wavelength gives the frequency. In the division the meter cancels and $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ remains, that is, the hertz. The fundamental is **$283.3\,\mathrm{Hz}$**, and the same value follows from the formula $f_1=\dfrac{u}{2L}=\dfrac{340}{1.20}\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

The values $+1$ and $-1$ at the two ends in the graph indicate that the maximum displacement has opposite directions at the same instant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal pipe open at both ends is $0.75\,\mathrm m$ long. Sound propagates at $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglecting end corrections, find the fundamental frequency and the third harmonic. How many internal displacement nodes does the latter have?

$$
f_n=\frac{nu}{2L}
\qquad
\lambda_n=\frac{2L}{n}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{340}{2\cdot0.75}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq226.7\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
\lambda_3&=\frac{2L}{3}=\frac{1.50\,\mathrm m}{3}=0.50\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
f_3&=\frac{340\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.50\,\mathrm m}
=680\,\mathrm{Hz}=3f_1, \\[6pt]
x_k&=(2k-1)\frac{\lambda_3}{4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_1=0.125\,\mathrm m,\ x_2=0.375\,\mathrm m,\ x_3=0.625\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,cos(4*pi*t),0,0.75" xmin=-0.034 xmax=0.81 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 nomi="x,s/sₘₐₓ" punti="0.125,0,pieno;0.375,0,pieno;0.625,0,pieno" legenda="s(x)/s_{\max}" titolo="In the third mode the three internal displacement nodes are marked."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For $n=1$ the formula for open pipes gives the fundamental. For the third mode the wavelength is a third of $2L$, so three half wavelengths fit in the pipe and $\lambda_3=0.50\,\mathrm m$. Dividing $u$ by $\lambda_3$ gives $f_3=680\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

Each half-wave segment between two consecutive antinodes contains a displacement node, at the middle of the segment. Three half waves fit in the pipe, so there are **three internal nodes**. The antinode is at $x=0$, so the first node is at $\dfrac{\lambda_3}{4}=0.125\,\mathrm m$ from the left end. The following nodes are separated by $\dfrac{\lambda_3}{2}=0.25\,\mathrm m$ and are at $0.375\,\mathrm m$ and $0.625\,\mathrm m$, as the formula for $x_k$ indicates with $k=2$ and $k=3$.

The exact frequency of the third harmonic is three times the fundamental, because $f_3=\dfrac{3u}{2L}$. This exact ratio is used, not three times the already rounded value of $f_1$, which would give $680.1\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The next harmonics of this pipe are $f_2=453.3\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $f_4=906.7\,\mathrm{Hz}$, because an open pipe admits all integer multiples of the fundamental.

</div>
