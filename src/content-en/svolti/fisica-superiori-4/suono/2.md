## Exercise 1

A source and a microphone are at the same point. A pulse directed toward a wall returns after $\Delta t=0.28\,\mathrm s$. With $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$, find the distance $d$ of the wall.

$$
\Delta t=\frac{2d}{u}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
d=\frac{u\,\Delta t}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2d&=u\Delta t
=(340\,\mathrm{m/s})(0.28\,\mathrm s)
=95.2\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
d&=\frac{95.2\,\mathrm m}{2}
=47.6\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{out}}&=\frac{d}{u}=\frac{47.6\,\mathrm m}{340\,\mathrm{m/s}}=0.14\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-1.1 xmax=7.4 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.6 assi=0 linee="6,-0.85 6,1.02" punti="0,0,pieno" vettori="0,0.35,5.95,0.35;5.95,-0.35,0,-0.35" etichette="0.3,1.05,S;6,1.39,P;3,0.87,d;3,-0.87,d" unicolore=1 titolo="S is the common position of source and microphone. The pulse reaches the wall P and returns to S."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The timer starts at emission and stops when the pulse returns. The wall is flat and perpendicular to the direction of propagation, so the pulse returns along the same path. The sound first travels $d$ toward the wall and then another $d$ toward the microphone, so the total path is $2d$. Since the air is homogeneous and the speed is constant, the distance traveled is the product $u\Delta t$, and from $2d=u\Delta t$ we obtain $d$ by dividing by two.

In the product $(340\,\mathrm{m/s})(0.28\,\mathrm s)$ the second cancels and the meter remains. The wall is therefore **$47.6\,\mathrm m$** away.

As a check, the outward trip lasts $\dfrac{47.6}{340}\,\mathrm s=0.14\,\mathrm s$. The return covers the same distance at the same speed, so it lasts the same time and the expected delay is $2\cdot0.14\,\mathrm s=0.28\,\mathrm s$, equal to the given value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A reflected pulse returns to the point of emission after $0.35\,\mathrm s$. With $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$, find the whole path traveled and the distance of the wall. Also determine when the pulse reaches the wall.

$$
\ell=u\,\Delta t
\qquad
d=\frac{\ell}{2}
\qquad
t_{\mathrm r}=\frac{d}{u}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell&=u\Delta t=(340\,\mathrm{m/s})(0.35\,\mathrm s)=119\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
d&=\frac{\ell}{2}=\frac{119\,\mathrm m}{2}=59.5\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm r}&=\frac{d}{u}=\frac{59.5\,\mathrm m}{340\,\mathrm{m/s}}
=0.175\,\mathrm s, \\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm r}&=\frac{d}{u}=\frac{u\,\Delta t}{2u}=\frac{\Delta t}{2}=\frac{0.35\,\mathrm s}{2}=0.175\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The path $\ell$ is the distance traveled by the sound in the interval $\Delta t$ at constant speed, so $\ell=u\Delta t=119\,\mathrm m$. This path includes the two equal legs out and back, so the wall is **$59.5\,\mathrm m$** away, that is, half of $\ell$.

The speed has the same value on both legs, so the reflection occurs halfway through the measured interval. The same number is obtained by dividing $d$ by $u$ and, alternatively, by substituting $d=\dfrac{u\Delta t}{2}$ into $\dfrac{d}{u}$, where $u$ cancels and $\dfrac{\Delta t}{2}=0.175\,\mathrm s$ remains.

A microphone placed on the wall would record the one-way wavefront at **$0.175\,\mathrm s$**. The microphone near the source instead records the reflected wavefront at the end of the second leg, at $0.35\,\mathrm s$.

</div>
