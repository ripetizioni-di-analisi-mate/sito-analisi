## Exercise 1

A $496\,\mathrm{Hz}$ source approaches a stationary observer at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In still air sound propagates at $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The motion is along the line joining them. Find the received frequency and the wavelength of the wavefronts ahead of the source.

$$
f'=f\,\frac{u+v_o}{u-v_s}
\qquad
v_o=0,\quad v_s=+18\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
u-v_s&=(340-18)\,\mathrm{m/s}=322\,\mathrm{m/s}, \\[6pt]
f'&=f\frac{u}{u-v_s}=496\,\frac{340}{322}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq523.7\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
\lambda'&=\frac{u-v_s}{f}=\frac{322\,\mathrm{m/s}}{496\,\mathrm{Hz}}\simeq0.649\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Doppler formula applies because the air is still, the motion is along the line joining source and observer, and the speed of the source is less than that of sound. With the observer stationary, the speed at which the wavefronts reach the observer is $u$, so the numerator of the formula remains $u$. In the time between two emissions the source advances toward the observer, so ahead of it the spacing between two wavefronts becomes $\dfrac{u-v_s}{f}$ and equals $0.649\,\mathrm m$, smaller than the emitted wavelength $\dfrac{340}{496}\,\mathrm m=0.685\,\mathrm m$.

According to the sign convention, $v_s=+18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for an approaching source. The denominator is therefore less than $u$ and the received frequency is greater than the emitted one, **$523.7\,\mathrm{Hz}$**.

As a check, the wavefronts move through the air at speed $u$, so the product $f'\lambda'$ must equal $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and indeed $523.7\cdot0.649\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq340\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The ratio $\dfrac{f'}{f}=\dfrac{340}{322}\simeq1.056$ says that the frequency increases by about $5.6\%$, a small value because $v_s$ is much less than $u$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A source emits $620\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and passes a stationary observer. The motion along the line joining them has magnitude $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while the speed of sound in still air is $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the frequencies received during the approach and during the recession.

$$
f'=f\,\frac{u}{u-v_s}
\qquad
v_s=+18\,\mathrm{m/s}\ \text{(approach)},
\quad
v_s=-18\,\mathrm{m/s}\ \text{(recession)}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f'_{\mathrm a}&=620\,\frac{340}{340-18}\,\mathrm{Hz}
=620\,\frac{340}{322}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq654.7\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
f'_{\mathrm r}&=620\,\frac{340}{340+18}\,\mathrm{Hz}
=620\,\frac{340}{358}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq588.8\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{vettori="588.8,0,588.8,1;620,0,620,1;654.7,0,654.7,1" xmin=575 xmax=670 ymin=0 ymax=1.6 nomi="f,A" etichette="588.8,1.2,588.8;620,1.2,620;654.7,1.2,654.7" titolo="Frequencies in Hz: received during recession ($588.8$), emitted ($620$), and received during approach ($654.7$)."}

<div class="spiegazione">

During the approach $v_s$ is positive and the wavefronts ahead of the source are closer together. During the recession $v_s$ is negative and the wavefronts reaching the observer are more widely spaced. In both cases the observer is stationary, so $u$ remains in the numerator, and the denominator contains $u-v_s$, which equals $340-18=322\,\mathrm{m/s}$ during the approach and $340-(-18)=358\,\mathrm{m/s}$ during the recession.

The two received frequencies are **$654.7\,\mathrm{Hz}$** during the approach and **$588.8\,\mathrm{Hz}$** during the recession. In the graph the emitted frequency lies between the two.

The increase relative to $620\,\mathrm{Hz}$ is about $34.7\,\mathrm{Hz}$, while the decrease is about $31.2\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The magnitudes differ because $u\pm18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ appears in the denominator and $\dfrac{1}{322}$ is farther from $\dfrac{1}{340}$ than $\dfrac{1}{358}$ is.

</div>
