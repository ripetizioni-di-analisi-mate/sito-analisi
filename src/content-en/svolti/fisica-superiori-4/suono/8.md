## Exercise 1

A $496\,\mathrm{Hz}$ source approaches a stationary observer at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Sound travels at $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in still air. The motion is along the line joining source and observer. Find the received frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u-v_s&=(340-18)\,\mathrm{m/s}=322\,\mathrm{m/s}, \\[6pt]
f'&=f\frac{u}{u-v_s}=496\frac{340}{322}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq523.7\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With a stationary observer, the speed at which the fronts reach the observer is $u$. Between two emissions the source advances toward the observer, so the spacing ahead of it becomes $\dfrac{u-v_s}{f}$. Under the sign convention, $v_s=+18\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the denominator is smaller than $u$ and the received frequency exceeds the emitted frequency.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A source emits at $620\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and passes a stationary observer. Its speed along the line between them is $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while sound travels at $340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in still air. Find the frequencies received during approach and recession.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'_{\mathrm a}&=620\frac{340}{340-18}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq654.7\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
f'_{\mathrm r}&=620\frac{340}{340+18}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq588.8\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During approach, $v_s$ is positive and the fronts ahead of the source are closer together. During recession, $v_s$ is negative and the wavefronts arriving at the observer are farther apart. In both cases the observer is stationary, so $u$ remains in the numerator.

The increase above $620\,\mathrm{Hz}$ is about $34.7\,\mathrm{Hz}$, while the decrease is about $31.2\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Their magnitudes differ because $u\pm18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ appears in the denominator. This observation helps check the results before rounding.

</div>
