## Exercise 1

An isotropic point source emits sound power $P=0.48\,\mathrm W$. In a free field with no absorption or reflections, find the intensity at $r=3\,\mathrm m$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=4\pi(3\,\mathrm m)^2=36\pi\,\mathrm{m^2}, \\[6pt]
I&=\frac{P}{A}=\frac{0.48}{36\pi}\,\mathrm{W/m^2} \\[6pt]
&\simeq4.24\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="9/x^2" xmin=2.65 xmax=8 ymin=0 ymax=1.35 nomi="r,I/I₃" punti="3,1,pieno;6,0.25,pieno" segmenti="6,0,6,0.25;3,0,3,1" legenda="I(r)/I_3" titolo="$I_3$ is the intensity at $3\,\mathrm m$. At $6\,\mathrm m$ the ratio is $\dfrac14$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

All points $3\,\mathrm m$ from the source lie on a sphere centered on it. Because the source is isotropic, each square meter of the sphere receives the same share of power. Dividing the power by the full area therefore gives the intensity in $\mathrm{W/m^2}$.

The graph uses the calculated value as a reference. At $6\,\mathrm m$ the radius is twice as large and the area is four times as large, so a microphone measures $\dfrac{I(3\,\mathrm m)}4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isotropic point source emits $0.60\,\mathrm W$ in a free field with no absorption or reflections. Find the intensity and level at $3\,\mathrm m$, using $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. How does the level change at $6\,\mathrm m$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3&=\frac{0.60}{36\pi}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}
\simeq 5.31\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}, \\[6pt]
\beta_3&=10\log_{10}(5.31\cdot10^9)\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq 97.25\,\mathrm{dB}, \\[6pt]
\beta_6-\beta_3&=10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac14\right)\,\mathrm{dB}
\simeq-6.02\,\mathrm{dB}, \\[6pt]
\beta_6&\simeq91.23\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the power by the spherical area gives the physical intensity at the point. To find its level, divide that intensity by $I_0$ and take the logarithm of the dimensionless ratio.

For the second microphone, consider the change. The power remains constant and the radius doubles, so the intensity is one quarter of its initial value. The logarithm of one quarter is negative, so the level falls. The unit $\mathrm{dB}$ belongs to the level difference, while the factor of one quarter applies to the intensities.

</div>
