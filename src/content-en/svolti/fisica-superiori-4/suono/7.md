## Exercise 1

An ideal pipe of length $L=0.52\,\mathrm m$ is closed at one end and open at the other. With $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and negligible end correction, find the fundamental frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_1&=4L=4\cdot0.52\,\mathrm m=2.08\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
f_1&=\frac{340}{2.08}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq163.5\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wall prevents the air from moving at the closed end, so that point is a node. The open end is a displacement antinode. In the simplest profile this node and antinode are adjacent. Their spacing is a quarter wavelength, so the fundamental wavelength is four times the pipe length.

A pipe of the same length open at both ends would have $\lambda_1=2L$. Its fundamental frequency would therefore be twice as high at the same sound speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal pipe of length $L=0.65\,\mathrm m$ is closed at one end and open at the other. With $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and negligible end correction, find the fundamental and the next resonance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{u}{4L}=\frac{340}{2.60}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq130.8\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
L&=\frac{3\lambda_2}{4}, \\[6pt]
f_2&=\frac{3u}{4L}=\frac{3\cdot340}{2.60}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq392.3\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,sin(pi*t/1.3),0,0.65;t,sin(3*pi*t/1.3),0,0.65" xmin=-0.04 xmax=0.73 ymin=-1.35 ymax=1.35 nomi="x,s/sₘₐₓ" segmenti="0.65,-1.10,0.65,1.10" legenda="\text{Mode 1};\text{Mode 2}" titolo="Displacement profiles for the first two modes. The closed end is at $x=0$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The fundamental spans one quarter wavelength from the closed-end node to the open-end antinode. The next mode adds half a wavelength, the spacing between two nodes of one profile. Three quarters of a wavelength now fit inside the pipe, so the frequency triples.

The index $2$ counts the second allowed mode. It is the **third harmonic** because its frequency is $3f_1$. In the graph, both curves start at zero displacement at the closed end and reach an extremum at the open end.

</div>
