## Exercise 1

An ideal pipe of length $L=0.52\,\mathrm m$ is closed at one end and open at the other. With $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and negligible end correction, find the fundamental and compare it with that of an open pipe of the same length.

$$
L=\frac{\lambda_1}{4}
\qquad
f_1=\frac{u}{\lambda_1}=\frac{u}{4L}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_1&=4L=4\cdot0.52\,\mathrm m=2.08\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
f_1&=\frac{340\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2.08\,\mathrm m}\simeq163.5\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
f_{1,\mathrm{open}}&=\frac{u}{2L}=\frac{340}{1.04}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq326.9\,\mathrm{Hz}=2f_1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wall prevents displacement of the air at the closed end, so there is a node at that point. At the open end there is instead a displacement antinode. The fundamental mode has the largest wavelength compatible with these two conditions, so the node and the antinode are consecutive. Their distance is a quarter of a wavelength, so $L=\dfrac{\lambda_1}{4}$ and the wavelength of the fundamental is four times the length of the pipe.

Dividing the speed of sound by $\lambda_1=2.08\,\mathrm m$ gives **$163.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$**.

A pipe open at both ends and of the same length has $\lambda_1=2L$, that is, a wavelength half that of the closed pipe. The ratio of the two fundamentals is $\dfrac{f_{1,\mathrm{open}}}{f_1}=\dfrac{4L}{2L}=2$, independent of $L$ and $u$. At the same propagation speed its fundamental is therefore double, $326.9\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal pipe of length $L=0.65\,\mathrm m$ is closed at one end and open at the other. With $u=340\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and negligible end correction, find the fundamental and the next resonance.

$$
f_n=\frac{(2n-1)u}{4L}
\qquad
L=\frac{(2n-1)\lambda_n}{4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{u}{4L}=\frac{340}{2.60}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq130.8\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
L&=\frac{3\lambda_2}{4}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_2=\frac{4L}{3}\simeq0.867\,\mathrm m, \\[6pt]
f_2&=\frac{3u}{4L}=\frac{3\cdot340}{2.60}\,\mathrm{Hz}
\simeq392.3\,\mathrm{Hz}=3f_1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fundamental spans a quarter wavelength from the closed node to the open antinode, so $\lambda_1=4L=2.60\,\mathrm m$ and $f_1\simeq130.8\,\mathrm{Hz}$. For the next mode half a wavelength is added, that is, the distance from one antinode to the next. In this way the open end remains an antinode. Adding only a quarter wavelength instead would put a node at the open end, where the condition requires an antinode. Three quarters of a wavelength then fit in the pipe, and from $L=\dfrac{3\lambda_2}{4}$ it follows that $\lambda_2=\dfrac{4L}{3}\simeq0.867\,\mathrm m$.

Since the speed is the same, the frequency is inversely proportional to the wavelength. The wavelength is a third of that of the fundamental, so the frequency triples and equals **$392.3\,\mathrm{Hz}$**.

The index $2$ counts the second allowed mode. The frequency corresponds to the **third harmonic** because it equals $3f_1$. In the graph, both curves start from zero displacement at the closed end and reach an extreme at the open end.

</div>
