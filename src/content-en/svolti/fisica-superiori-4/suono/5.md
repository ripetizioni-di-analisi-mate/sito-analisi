## Exercise 1

Two pure tones of comparable amplitudes have frequencies $f_1=440\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $f_2=445\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find the beat frequency, the interval between two consecutive maxima, and the frequency of the perceived tone.

$$
f_b=|f_2-f_1|
\qquad
T_b=\frac{1}{f_b}
\qquad
\bar f=\frac{f_1+f_2}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_b&=|445-440|\,\mathrm{Hz}
=5\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
T_b&=\frac{1}{5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}}=0.20\,\mathrm s, \\[6pt]
\bar f&=\frac{440+445}{2}\,\mathrm{Hz}=442.5\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In one second the second tone completes five more cycles than the first. Each time the lead grows by one full cycle, the two tones are in phase again and reinforce each other, so there are five maxima per second and the beat frequency is **$5\,\mathrm{Hz}$**.

Five maxima per second are equally spaced in time, so the interval between two maxima is the reciprocal of this frequency and equals $\dfrac{1}{5}\,\mathrm s=0.20\,\mathrm s$. The absolute value in $f_b=|f_2-f_1|$ makes the beat frequency positive whichever tone is higher. The amplitudes are comparable, so the minima are pronounced and the maxima are easily distinguished.

The tone heard has a frequency close to the mean $442.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The frequency of $5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ instead describes the slow variations of the amplitude, and is too low to be perceived as a sound.

With two tones of equal amplitude $A$ the sum has amplitude $2A\left|\cos(\pi f_b t)\right|$, as the theorem on the envelope of two close tones shows. This amplitude reaches its maximum value $2A$ every $0.20\,\mathrm s$ and vanishes at the intermediate points. The ear follows this variation as a pulsation of intensity, five times per second.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tuning fork of frequency $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and a second tuning fork produce an intensity maximum every $\dfrac16\,\mathrm s$. During a test the frequency of the second fork is increased without crossing that of the first. The beats become faster. Find the frequency of the second fork before the test.

$$
f_b=\frac{1}{T_b}
\qquad
|f_2-f_1|=f_b
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_b&=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{6}\,\mathrm s}=6\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
|f_2-440\,\mathrm{Hz}|&=6\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
f_2&=440\,\mathrm{Hz}\pm6\,\mathrm{Hz}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_2=434\,\mathrm{Hz}\ \text{or}\ f_2=446\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
f_2&=446\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*abs(cos(6*pi*x))" xmin=0 xmax=0.5 ymin=-0.3 ymax=2.5 nomi="t,A" punti="0,2,pieno;0.1667,2,pieno;0.3333,2,pieno;0.5,2,pieno" legenda="2\lvert\cos(6\pi t)\rvert" titolo="Relative amplitude of the sum of the two tones, with $t$ in seconds. The maxima are separated by $\dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm s$, that is, beats at $6\,\mathrm{Hz}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The time between maxima is the beat period, and its reciprocal gives the frequency difference, $6\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Since an absolute value appears, the first datum gives two possibilities, $434\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $446\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

If the starting frequency were $434\,\mathrm{Hz}$, a small increase would bring it closer to $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The difference would decrease and the beats would become slower, for example $f_b=|436-440|\,\mathrm{Hz}=4\,\mathrm{Hz}$ with $f_2=436\,\mathrm{Hz}$. In the test the maxima move closer together in time, so the difference grows. This happens only if the second fork started from the higher value, so the frequency sought is **$446\,\mathrm{Hz}$**. With $f_2=447\,\mathrm{Hz}$ we would have $f_b=7\,\mathrm{Hz}$, that is, maxima closer together, at intervals of $\dfrac{1}{7}\,\mathrm s$.

The graph shows the amplitude of the sum of the two tones, which has the form $2\lvert\cos(\pi f_b t)\rvert$, with $f_b=6\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The maximum value repeats at intervals of $\dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm s$.

</div>
