## Exercise 1

Two pure tones of comparable amplitudes have frequencies $f_1=440\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $f_2=445\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find the beat frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_b&=|f_2-f_1|
=|445-440|\,\mathrm{Hz}
=5\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In one second the second tone completes five more cycles than the first. Whenever the difference grows by one whole cycle, the tones return to phase and reinforce each other. This gives five maxima per second. The perceived pitch is near $442.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$, the mean frequency. The $5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ frequency describes the slow changes in amplitude.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$ tuning fork and a second fork produce an intensity maximum every $\dfrac16\,\mathrm s$. In a test, the frequency of the second fork is increased without crossing the first fork's frequency. The beats become faster. Find the second fork's frequency before the test.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_b&=\frac{1}{T_b}=6\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
|f_2-440\,\mathrm{Hz}|&=6\,\mathrm{Hz}, \\[6pt]
f_2&=440\,\mathrm{Hz}+6\,\mathrm{Hz}=446\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The time between maxima gives the frequency difference, so the first measurement leaves two possibilities, $434\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $446\,\mathrm{Hz}$. In the first case a small increase would bring the second frequency closer to $440\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and slow the beats. In the test, the maxima come closer together in time. The frequency gap grows, so the second fork started at the higher value.

</div>
