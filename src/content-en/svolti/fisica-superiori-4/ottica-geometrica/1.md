## Exercise 1

A transparent medium has index $n=1.2$. Find the phase speed in the medium using $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=\frac{c}{n}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{3.00\cdot10^8}{1.2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
 &=2.5\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,3/t,1,3.2" xmin=0 xmax=3.4 ymin=0 ymax=3.4 nomi="n,u (10⁸ m/s)" punti="1.2,2.5,pieno" legenda="\dfrac{u}{10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}}=\dfrac{3}{n}" titolo="The point shows the speed for $n=1.2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio $n=\dfrac{c}{u}$ compares speeds in the same units. Multiplying by $u$ and dividing by $n$ gives $u=\dfrac{c}{n}$. Since the index is dimensionless, the result is still in meters per second. In the graph, the vertical coordinate $2.5$ represents $2.5\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. An index greater than one requires a speed below $c$, as the calculation confirms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Glass has index $n=1.5$. Light with a wavelength of $500\,\mathrm{nm}$ in vacuum enters the glass. Find the speed and wavelength in the glass using $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=\frac{c}{n}
  =\frac{3.00\cdot10^8}{1.5}\,\mathrm{m/s}
  =2.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_0}&=\frac{u}{c}=\frac{1}{n}\\[6pt]
\lambda&=\frac{500}{1.5}\,\mathrm{nm}
       \simeq333\,\mathrm{nm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the stationary interface, frequency $\nu$ stays the same. Dividing $u=\lambda\nu$ by $c=\lambda_0\nu$ therefore cancels $\nu$. The speed and wavelength both decrease by a factor of $\dfrac{1}{1.5}$. The calculation of $\lambda$ can stay in nanometers because a length is divided by a dimensionless index. The result of about $333\,\mathrm{nm}$ is the spacing between crests in the glass. The frequency associated with $500\,\mathrm{nm}$ in vacuum is unchanged.

</div>
