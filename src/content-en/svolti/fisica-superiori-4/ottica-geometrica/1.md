## Exercise 1

A transparent medium has index $n=1.2$. Calculate the phase speed in the medium using $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=\frac{c}{u}\\[6pt]
u&=\frac{c}{n}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.2}\\[6pt]
 &=2.5\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
\frac{u}{c}&=\frac{1}{1.2}\simeq0.83.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,3/t,1,3.2" xmin=0 xmax=3.4 ymin=0 ymax=3.4 nomi="n,u (10⁸ m/s)" punti="1.2,2.5,pieno" legenda="\dfrac{u}{10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}}=\dfrac{3}{n}" titolo="The point shows the speed for $n=1.2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The refractive index is defined by the ratio $n=\dfrac{c}{u}$ between the speed of light in vacuum and the phase speed in the medium. Since $c$ and $n$ are known, multiply both sides by $u$ and divide by $n$, obtaining $u=\dfrac{c}{n}$. In the substitution $c$ has the unit $\mathrm{m/s}$ and $n$ is dimensionless, so $u$ is also measured in meters per second. The value $n=1.2$ is greater than $1$, so $u$ is less than $c$. The ratio $\dfrac{u}{c}\simeq0.83$ indicates that in the medium light travels at about $83\%$ of its speed in vacuum. In the graph the ordinate $2.5$, in units of $10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, corresponds to the point with abscissa $n=1.2$. The phase speed in the medium is therefore $2.5\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. As a check, the speed found is less than $c$ and greater than $\dfrac{c}{2}=1.5\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which would be obtained with $n=2$. Since $1<n<2$, the phase speed must fall between these two values.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A glass has index $n=1.5$. Light with a vacuum wavelength of $500\,\mathrm{nm}$ enters the glass. Calculate the speed and the wavelength in the glass using $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=\frac{c}{n}
  =\frac{3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.5}
  =2.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
u&=\lambda\nu,\qquad c=\lambda_0\nu\\[6pt]
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_0}&=\frac{u}{c}=\frac{1}{n}\\[6pt]
\lambda&=\frac{\lambda_0}{n}=\frac{500\,\mathrm{nm}}{1.5}
       \simeq333\,\mathrm{nm}\\[6pt]
\nu&=\frac{c}{\lambda_0}
   =\frac{3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}}{500\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m}
   =6.0\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The speed follows from the ratio $u=\dfrac{c}{n}$, as in the previous exercise. For the wavelength, note that at a stationary interface the frequency $\nu$ is the same in the two media, because the oscillations on the two sides of the surface correspond one to one. We therefore write $u=\lambda\nu$ in the glass and $c=\lambda_0\nu$ in vacuum. Dividing the first equality by the second, $\nu$ cancels and $\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_0}=\dfrac{u}{c}=\dfrac{1}{n}$ remains. The speed and the wavelength are therefore both reduced by a factor $\dfrac{1}{1.5}$. Since a length is divided by a pure number, $\lambda$ can stay in nanometers. The frequency is computed in vacuum, where $\lambda_0$ and $c$ are given, and equals $6.0\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$: it is the same in the glass, where $\dfrac{u}{\lambda}$ gives the same value. In the glass the light therefore has speed $2.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a wavelength of about $333\,\mathrm{nm}$.

</div>
