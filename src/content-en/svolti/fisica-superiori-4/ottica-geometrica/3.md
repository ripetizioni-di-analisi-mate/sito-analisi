## Exercise 1

A ray travels from a medium of index $n_1=1.24$ toward air of index $n_2=1.00$. Determine the critical angle and establish whether total reflection occurs at $60^\circ$ from the normal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_1\sin\theta_c&=n_2\sin90^\circ\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_c&=\frac{n_2}{n_1}=\frac{1.00}{1.24}\simeq0.8065\\[6pt]
\theta_c&=\arcsin(0.8065)\simeq53.8^\circ\\[6pt]
60^\circ&>53.8^\circ\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_2&=\frac{n_1}{n_2}\sin60^\circ
             =1.24\cdot0.8660\simeq1.07>1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3.5,0 3.5,0" segmenti="0,-0.65,0,2.7" vettori="-2.598,1.5,0,0;0,0,2.598,1.5" etichette="-2.45,2.16,n₁ = 1.24;-2.4,-0.45,n₂ = 1.00;0.5,2.32,normal" xmin=-3.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-0.85 ymax=2.8 titolo="At $60^\circ$ incidence the two rays stay in the medium of index $1.24$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The index of the starting medium is greater than that of the destination, so there is a limiting incidence at which the refracted ray would graze the surface. Grazing means $\theta_2=90^\circ$, with $\sin90^\circ=1$, and Snell's law becomes $n_1\sin\theta_c=n_2$. Dividing by $n_1$ gives $\sin\theta_c=\dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$ and therefore $\theta_c\simeq53.8^\circ$. The incidence of $60^\circ$ exceeds this value. Applying Snell's law anyway would give $\sin\theta_2\simeq1.07$, which is greater than $1$ and corresponds to no real angle. There is therefore no transmitted ray. In the medium of index $1.24$ there is **total reflection**, with the reflected ray at $60^\circ$ from the normal, as the figure shows. For incidences smaller than $53.8^\circ$ the ray would instead be transmitted into air.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ray travels in a medium of index $1.55$ toward air of index $1.00$. Calculate the critical angle and the speed in the starting medium using $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_c&=\frac{n_2}{n_1}=\frac{1.00}{1.55}\simeq0.6452\\[6pt]
\theta_c&=\arcsin(0.6452)\simeq40.2^\circ\\[6pt]
u&=\frac{c}{n_1}
  =\frac{3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}}{1.55}
  \simeq1.94\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The critical angle depends on both indices, because it is the incidence for which the refracted ray would become grazing in the destination medium. Here $n_1=1.55$ is the index of the starting medium and $n_2=1.00$ that of the destination. The ratio $\dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$ is less than $1$, and this is the condition for the sine to have a real arcsine. From the value $0.6452$ we obtain $\theta_c\simeq40.2^\circ$. The phase speed follows from the index of the starting medium alone with $u=\dfrac{c}{n_1}$, and has the unit of $c$, that is, $\mathrm{m/s}$. With an outer medium of index greater than $1.00$ and less than $1.55$, the ratio $\dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$ would increase and the critical angle would grow, while the speed in the starting medium would stay the same. The critical angle is therefore about $40.2^\circ$ and the speed in the medium is about $1.94\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. An index $n_1=1.55$ greater than $1.24$ gives a smaller critical angle than in the previous exercise, $40.2^\circ$ against $53.8^\circ$: a greater index corresponds to a smaller critical angle.

</div>
