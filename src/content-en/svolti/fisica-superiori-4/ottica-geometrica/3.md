## Exercise 1

A ray travels from a medium of index $n_1=1.24$ toward air of index $n_2=1.00$. Find the critical angle and determine whether total internal reflection occurs at $60^\circ$ from the normal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_c&=\frac{1.00}{1.24}\simeq0.8065\\[6pt]
\theta_c&=\arcsin\left(\frac{1.00}{1.24}\right)
        \simeq53.8^\circ\\[6pt]
60^\circ&>53.8^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3.5,0 3.5,0" segmenti="0,-0.65,0,2.7" vettori="-2.598,1.5,0,0;0,0,2.598,1.5" etichette="-2.45,2.16,n₁ = 1.24;-2.4,-0.45,n₂ = 1.00;0.5,2.32,normal" xmin=-3.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-0.85 ymax=2.8 titolo="At $60^\circ$ incidence both rays stay in the medium of index $1.24$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The incident medium has a higher index than the second medium, so there is an incidence angle at which the refracted ray would run along the surface. Since the sine of $90^\circ$ is $1$, Snell's law at that incidence becomes $n_1\sin\theta_c=n_2$. The actual angle of incidence exceeds $\theta_c$. Indeed, $1.24\sin60^\circ\simeq1.07$ exceeds the largest possible sine in air. Total internal reflection occurs.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ray travels in a medium of index $1.55$ toward air of index $1.00$. Find the critical angle and the speed in the incident medium using $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_c&=\arcsin\left(\frac{1.00}{1.55}\right)
        \simeq40.2^\circ\\[6pt]
u&=\frac{3.00\cdot10^8}{1.55}\,\mathrm{m/s}
  \simeq1.94\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The critical angle requires both indices because reflection depends on the medium the ray attempts to enter. Take the ratio of the second index to the incident index, which is below one here. The speed in the incident medium depends only on its index $1.55$. Replacing the air outside with a higher-index liquid would increase the critical angle as long as $n_2<n_1$. The speed in the incident medium would stay the same.

</div>
