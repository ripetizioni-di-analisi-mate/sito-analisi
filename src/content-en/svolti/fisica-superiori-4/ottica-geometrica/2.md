## Exercise 1

A ray passes from air ($n_1=1.00$) into glass ($n_2=1.50$). The angle of incidence from the normal is $28^\circ$. Calculate the angle of refraction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_1\sin\theta_1&=n_2\sin\theta_2\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_2&=\frac{n_1}{n_2}\sin\theta_1\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1.00}{1.50}\cdot\sin28^\circ\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1.00}{1.50}\cdot0.4695\simeq0.3130\\[6pt]
\theta_2&=\arcsin(0.3130)\simeq18.2^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3,0 3,0" segmenti="0,-3.1,0,3.1" vettori="-1.409,2.649,0,0;0,0,0.939,-2.849" etichette="-2.35,2.15,air;1.8,-2.2,glass;0.45,2.65,normal" xmin=-3.4 xmax=3.4 ymin=-3.3 ymax=3.3 titolo="The rays form $28^\circ$ in air and about $18.2^\circ$ in glass with the dashed normal."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Snell's law relates angles measured from the normal. The given $28^\circ$ is already referred to the normal, so it can be substituted directly. Divide both sides by $n_2$ to isolate $\sin\theta_2$. The factor $\dfrac{1.00}{1.50}$ is less than $1$, so $\sin\theta_2$ is less than $\sin\theta_1$. Between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ the sine is increasing, so $\theta_2<\theta_1$ and the refracted ray bends toward the normal. The calculator, set to degrees, gives $\arcsin(0.3130)\simeq18.2^\circ$. In the glass the ray therefore forms an angle of about $18.2^\circ$ with the normal, less than the $28^\circ$ in air, as the figure shows. Substituting $\theta_2$ in the right-hand side of the law gives back $n_2\sin\theta_2\simeq1.50\cdot0.312\simeq0.47$, equal to $n_1\sin\theta_1\simeq0.47$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ray enters from air ($n_1=1.00$) into a glass with $n_2=1.50$. The incidence from the normal is $35^\circ$. Calculate the refracted angle from the normal and the one from the surface.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_2&=\frac{n_1}{n_2}\sin\theta_1
             =\frac{1.00}{1.50}\cdot0.5736\simeq0.3824\\[6pt]
\theta_2&=\arcsin(0.3824)\simeq22.5^\circ\\[6pt]
\alpha_1&=90^\circ-\theta_1=90^\circ-35^\circ=55^\circ\\[6pt]
\alpha_2&=90^\circ-\theta_2\simeq67.5^\circ\\[6pt]
\theta_1-\theta_2&=35^\circ-22.5^\circ=12.5^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The normal is perpendicular to the surface, so for each ray the angle with the normal and the angle with the surface are complementary. Snell's law requires the angles from the normal, so $\theta_2$ is computed first, with $\sin35^\circ\simeq0.5736$ and the ratio $\dfrac{1.00}{1.50}$. Then $\theta_2$ is subtracted from $90^\circ$ to obtain the inclination from the surface. In air the ray forms $\alpha_1=55^\circ$ with the surface. Since in the glass it bends toward the normal, the angle with the surface increases and becomes $\alpha_2\simeq67.5^\circ$. The refracted ray therefore forms $22.5^\circ$ with the normal and $67.5^\circ$ with the surface. The deviation toward the normal is $\theta_1-\theta_2=35^\circ-22.5^\circ=12.5^\circ$. With the same incidence and an index $n_2$ greater than $1.50$, the sine of $\theta_2$ would be smaller and the deviation larger.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ray in air of index $1.00$ forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with the surface of a glass block. In the glass it forms $30^\circ$ with the *normal*. Determine the index of the glass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_1&=90^\circ-30^\circ=60^\circ,\qquad\theta_2=30^\circ\\[6pt]
n_1\sin\theta_1&=n_2\sin\theta_2\\[6pt]
n_2&=\frac{n_1\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2}
   =\frac{1.00\cdot\sin60^\circ}{\sin30^\circ}\\[6pt]
   &=\frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{3}\simeq1.73.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first datum is referred to the surface, whereas Snell's law uses angles from the normal. Since the normal is perpendicular to the surface, the incidence is $\theta_1=90^\circ-30^\circ=60^\circ$. The second datum is already an angle from the normal, so $\theta_2=30^\circ$. The two $30^\circ$ angles in the statement refer to different references and cannot be compared. To isolate $n_2$, divide by $\sin\theta_2$ and substitute $\sin60^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin30^\circ=\dfrac{1}{2}$. The ratio equals $\sqrt{3}$. The ray goes from $60^\circ$ to $30^\circ$ with respect to the normal, so it bends toward the normal and the index of the second medium must exceed that of air: $1.73>1.00$ satisfies this condition. The glass has index $\sqrt{3}\simeq1.73$.

</div>
