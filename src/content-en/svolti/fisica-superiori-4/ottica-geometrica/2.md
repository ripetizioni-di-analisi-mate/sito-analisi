## Exercise 1

A ray passes from air ($n_1=1.00$) into glass ($n_2=1.50$). Its angle of incidence from the normal is $28^\circ$. Calculate the angle of refraction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_2&=\frac{n_1}{n_2}\sin\theta_1
             =\frac{1.00}{1.50}\sin28^\circ
             \simeq0.3130\\[6pt]
\theta_2&=\arcsin(0.3130)\simeq18.2^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3,0 3,0" segmenti="0,-3.1,0,3.1" vettori="-1.409,2.649,0,0;0,0,0.939,-2.849" etichette="-2.35,2.15,air;1.8,-2.2,glass;0.45,2.65,normal" xmin=-3.4 xmax=3.4 ymin=-3.3 ymax=3.3 titolo="The rays make angles of $28^\circ$ in air and about $18.2^\circ$ in glass with the dashed normal."}

<div class="spiegazione">

To apply Snell's law, measure both angles from the normal. Dividing by $n_2$ gives the *sine* of the refraction angle, so take the inverse sine in degrees. The factor $\dfrac{1.00}{1.50}$ is below one, so the sine decreases. In the range from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$, this means the refracted ray bends toward the normal. The angles $28^\circ$ and $18.2^\circ$ in the diagram satisfy this comparison.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ray travels from air ($n_1=1.00$) into glass with $n_2=1.50$. The angle of incidence from the normal is $35^\circ$. Find the refraction angle from the normal and the angle from the surface.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_2&=\arcsin\left(\frac{1.00}{1.50}\sin35^\circ\right)
          \simeq22.5^\circ\\[6pt]
\alpha_2&=90^\circ-\theta_2\simeq67.5^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The normal is perpendicular to the surface. The requested angles are therefore complementary and describe the same ray. First apply Snell's law to $\theta_2$ because it uses angles from the normal. Then subtract $\theta_2$ from $90^\circ$ to find the angle from the surface. As the ray bends toward the normal, the angle from the surface increases. Here it is $67.5^\circ$, greater than the $55^\circ$ the incident ray makes with the surface.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ray in air of index $1.00$ makes a $30^\circ$ angle with a glass block's surface. In glass it makes a $30^\circ$ angle with the *normal*. Find the refractive index of the glass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_1&=90^\circ-30^\circ=60^\circ\\[6pt]
n_2&=\frac{n_1\sin60^\circ}{\sin30^\circ}
   =\frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{3}\simeq1.73.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle in air is measured from the surface, so convert it before substituting into Snell's law. The angle in glass is already measured from the normal. The two equal numbers $30^\circ$ use different reference lines. Since the ray goes from $60^\circ$ to $30^\circ$ from the normal, the calculated index must exceed that of air. Substituting $n_2=\sqrt{3}$ into the law indeed gives $\sqrt{3}\sin30^\circ=\sin60^\circ$.

</div>
