## Exercise 1

A converging lens has focal length $f=0.12\,\mathrm m$. A real object is at distance $p=0.288\,\mathrm m$. Determine the position of the image.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{f}&=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{q}&=\frac{1}{f}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{p-f}{fp}\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{fp}{p-f}=\frac{0.12\cdot0.288}{0.288-0.12}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.03456}{0.168}\,\mathrm m\simeq0.206\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{q}{p}=-\frac{0.2057}{0.288}\simeq-0.714.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We apply the thin lens equation, valid for rays close to the axis, with $f>0$ because the lens is converging and $p>0$ because the object is real. Isolate the reciprocal of $q$ and bring it to a common denominator, obtaining $q=\dfrac{fp}{p-f}$. The denominator $p-f=0.168\,\mathrm m$ is positive, because the object is beyond the front focus, so $q>0$ and the image is real and forms on the side opposite the object. Since $p>2f=0.24\,\mathrm m$, the image must fall between $f$ and $2f$, and the value $0.206\,\mathrm m$ satisfies this condition. The negative sign of $M=-\dfrac{q}{p}$ indicates an inverted image, while $|M|<1$ indicates that it is reduced. The screen must therefore be placed about $20.6\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens on the exit side, where a real, inverted image forms, reduced to about $0.71$ times the object. With the object at $p=2f=0.24\,\mathrm m$ we would instead obtain $q=0.24\,\mathrm m$ and $M=-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A converging lens has focal length $f=0.12\,\mathrm m$ and an object at distance $p=0.36\,\mathrm m$. Calculate the position of the image and the signed magnification.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=\frac{fp}{p-f}=\frac{0.12\cdot0.36}{0.36-0.12}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.0432}{0.24}\,\mathrm m=0.18\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{q}{p}=-\frac{0.18}{0.36}=-0.50.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-6.8,0 4,0;0,-2 0,2.2" unicolore=1 vettori="-6,0,-6,1.5;-6,1.5,0,1.5;0,1.5,3,-0.75;-6,1.5,3,-0.75;3,0,3,-0.75" punti="-2,0,pieno,F₁;2,0,pieno,F₂" xmin=-7.3 xmax=4.3 ymin=-2.2 ymax=2.55 titolo="The focus is $0.12\,\mathrm m$ from the lens. The central ray and the ray refracted toward the focus meet at $0.18\,\mathrm m$ from the lens."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The object is at $p=3f$. Substituting $p=3f$ in the expression for $q$ gives $q=\dfrac{3f^2}{2f}=\dfrac{3}{2}f$, so $q=1.5\cdot0.12\,\mathrm m=0.18\,\mathrm m$, in agreement with the numerical calculation. The positive sign of $q$ places the real image beyond the lens. In the figure, the ray parallel to the axis is deflected toward the rear focus and the ray through the optical center continues undeviated. Their intersection below the axis is the top of the image. The two triangles formed by the central ray with the axis have bases $p$ and $q$, so the ratio of the heights in absolute value is $\dfrac{q}{p}=\dfrac{1}{2}$. The negative sign of $M$ indicates that the top of the image lies below the axis, that is, the image is inverted. The image forms $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens and is real, inverted, and half the height of the object.

</div>
