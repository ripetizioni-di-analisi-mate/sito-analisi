## Exercise 1

A thin lens has focal length $f=0.20\,\mathrm m$. Calculate the signed optical power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\frac{1}{f}
  =\frac{1}{0.20}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\\[6pt]
 &=+5.0\,\mathrm D.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One diopter is the reciprocal of one meter. The focal length is already in meters, so take its reciprocal directly. Using the number $20$ after expressing the focal length in centimeters would give $0.05\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, which still needs conversion to diopters. In meters, $D$ is positive like $f$, so the lens converges.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A thin lens of focal length $0.25\,\mathrm m$ is placed in contact with a second thin lens of power $-1.5\,\mathrm D$. Find the power of the first lens and then the power and focal length of the combination.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=\frac{1}{0.25}\,\mathrm D=+4.0\,\mathrm D\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{eq}}&=4.0\,\mathrm D-1.5\,\mathrm D=+2.5\,\mathrm D\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{1}{2.5}\,\mathrm m=0.40\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lenses are thin and in contact, so add their signed powers. The negative power of the second lens reduces the convergence of the first. The total power is still positive, so the combination remains converging. To find the focal length, take the reciprocal of the total power. Since the power has decreased, the focal length increases from $0.25\,\mathrm m$ to $0.40\,\mathrm m$.

</div>
