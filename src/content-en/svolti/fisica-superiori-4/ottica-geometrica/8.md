## Exercise 1

A thin lens has focal length $f=0.20\,\mathrm m$. Calculate the signed optical power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\frac{1}{f}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1}{0.20\,\mathrm m}=5.0\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\\[6pt]
&=+5.0\,\mathrm D.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A diopter corresponds to the reciprocal of one meter, so the focal length must be expressed in meters before taking the reciprocal. Here $f=0.20\,\mathrm m$ is already in meters and we can compute directly $\dfrac{1}{0.20}=5.0$. With the focal length in centimeters, $f=20\,\mathrm{cm}$, we would obtain $\dfrac{1}{20}=0.05\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, and to convert to $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ we must multiply by $100$, because $1\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{cm}$. The sign of $D$ is that of $f$, positive, so the lens is converging. The optical power is $+5.0\,\mathrm D$, and the lens makes rays parallel to the axis converge at a point $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ behind it. A lens with the same focal length in magnitude and the opposite sign, $f=-0.20\,\mathrm m$, would have power $-5.0\,\mathrm D$ and would be diverging.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A second thin lens of $-1.5\,\mathrm D$ is placed in contact with a thin lens of focal length $0.25\,\mathrm m$. Calculate the power of the first lens, then the power and the focal length of the system in contact.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=\frac{1}{f_1}=\frac{1}{0.25\,\mathrm m}=+4.0\,\mathrm D\\[6pt]
f_2&=\frac{1}{D_2}=\frac{1}{-1.5\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}}\simeq-0.67\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{eq}}&=D_1+D_2=4.0\,\mathrm D-1.5\,\mathrm D=+2.5\,\mathrm D\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\frac{1}{2.5\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}}=0.40\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lenses are thin and in contact in the same medium, so the powers add with their signs: $D_{\mathrm{eq}}=D_1+D_2$. The first lens has $D_1=+4.0\,\mathrm D$, because the focal length is expressed in meters. The second has negative power, so it is diverging, with focal length $f_2\simeq-0.67\,\mathrm m$. Its power reduces the convergence of the first. The sum $+2.5\,\mathrm D$ is positive, so the combination is still converging. The reciprocal of the total power is then taken to obtain the focal length. Since the power has decreased from $4.0\,\mathrm D$ to $2.5\,\mathrm D$, the focal length increases from $0.25\,\mathrm m$ to $0.40\,\mathrm m$. The system therefore has power $+2.5\,\mathrm D$ and focal length $0.40\,\mathrm m$. The sign of the sum depends on the comparison of the magnitudes: since $|D_1|>|D_2|$, the converging lens prevails.

</div>
