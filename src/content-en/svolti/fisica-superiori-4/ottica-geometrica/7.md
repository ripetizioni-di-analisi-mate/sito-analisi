## Exercise 1

A concave mirror has focal length $f=0.18\,\mathrm m$. A real object is at $p=0.432\,\mathrm m$. In the paraxial model, determine the position of the image.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{f}&=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{fp}{p-f}=\frac{0.18\cdot0.432}{0.432-0.18}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.07776}{0.252}\,\mathrm m\simeq0.309\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{q}{p}=-\frac{0.309}{0.432}\simeq-0.714.
\end{aligned}
$$

Heights are enlarged relative to distances."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For the concave mirror the focal length $f=0.18\,\mathrm m$ is positive and the equation has the same form as that of lenses. Isolate $\dfrac{1}{q}$ and bring it to a common denominator, obtaining $q=\dfrac{fp}{p-f}$. Since $p>f$, the denominator is positive and $q>0$. The mirror reflects light, so an image with $q>0$ is real and forms in front of the surface, on the object side. The object lies beyond the center of curvature, because $p=0.432\,\mathrm m>2f=0.36\,\mathrm m$, and the image must fall between $f$ and $2f$: indeed $0.18\,\mathrm m<0.309\,\mathrm m<0.36\,\mathrm m$. In the figure the two rays meet below the axis. The negative sign of $M\simeq-0.714$ indicates an inverted image and a magnitude less than $1$ indicates that it is reduced. The real, inverted, reduced image forms about $30.9\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A concave mirror has $f=0.18\,\mathrm m$ and an object at $p=0.54\,\mathrm m$. Determine $q$ and the signed magnification.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{q}&=\frac{1}{f}-\frac{1}{p}=\left(\frac{1}{0.18}-\frac{1}{0.54}\right)\mathrm{m^{-1}}\simeq3.70\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{fp}{p-f}=\frac{0.18\cdot0.54}{0.54-0.18}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.0972}{0.36}\,\mathrm m=0.27\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{q}{p}=-\frac{0.27}{0.54}=-0.50.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object is at $p=3f$, because $0.54\,\mathrm m=3\cdot0.18\,\mathrm m$, and the denominator $p-f$ equals $2f$. We obtain $q=\dfrac{3f^2}{2f}=\dfrac{3}{2}f=\dfrac{p}{2}$. The calculation with reciprocals gives $\dfrac{1}{q}\simeq3.70\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, that is, the same value $q=0.27\,\mathrm m$. The positive sign of $q$ places the image in front of the mirror, where the reflected light converges. The magnification is $M=-\dfrac{q}{p}=-\dfrac{1}{2}$. The negative sign indicates that the image is inverted and the absolute value indicates that the height is halved. An object $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall has an image whose top is at $-2\,\mathrm{cm}$ relative to the axis. The image is therefore real, inverted, and reduced to half, and forms $27\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex. With $p=2f=0.36\,\mathrm m$, that is, with the object at the center of curvature, we would have $q=2f$ and $M=-1$: the real image would have the same size as the object.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A concave mirror has radius of curvature $R=36\,\mathrm{cm}$. A real object is at $p=9\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex. Calculate the position and orientation of the image.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{R}{2}=\frac{36\,\mathrm{cm}}{2}=18\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{q}&=\frac{1}{f}-\frac{1}{p}=\left(\frac{1}{18}-\frac{1}{9}\right)\mathrm{cm^{-1}}=-\frac{1}{18}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{fp}{p-f}=\frac{18\cdot9}{9-18}\,\mathrm{cm}=\frac{162}{-9}\,\mathrm{cm}=-18\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{q}{p}=-\frac{-18}{9}=+2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the paraxial model the focal length is half the radius of curvature, so $f=18\,\mathrm{cm}$. The object is at $p=9\,\mathrm{cm}$, less than $f$, so it lies inside the focal length and the denominator $p-f=-9\,\mathrm{cm}$ is negative. It follows that $q<0$, in agreement with $\dfrac{1}{q}=-\dfrac{1}{18}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, which is negative. The reflected rays diverge and only their extensions meet behind the mirror, $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the surface, so the image is virtual. The magnification $M=+2$ is positive and greater than $1$, so the image is upright and enlarged to twice the size. This is the effect of a concave mirror used up close, for example for makeup. In general, for $p=\dfrac{f}{2}$ we have $q=-f$ and $M=+2$: the distance of the image from the mirror equals the focal length.

</div>
