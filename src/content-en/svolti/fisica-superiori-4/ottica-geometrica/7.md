## Exercise 1

A concave mirror has focal length $f=0.18\,\mathrm m$. A real object is at $p=0.432\,\mathrm m$. Find the image position in the paraxial model.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{q}&=\frac{1}{f}-\frac{1}{p}\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{0.18\cdot0.432}{0.432-0.18}\,\mathrm m
  \simeq0.309\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the mirror equation the focal length is positive because the mirror is concave. Since $p$ exceeds $f$, we obtain $q>0$. The mirror reflects light and the rays converge on the side in front of the surface. The image is therefore real on that side, about $30.9\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the mirror vertex. This is between $f$ and $2f=0.36\,\mathrm m$, as expected for an object beyond the center of curvature.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A concave mirror has $f=0.18\,\mathrm m$ and an object at $p=0.54\,\mathrm m$. Find $q$ and the signed magnification.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=\frac{0.18\cdot0.54}{0.54-0.18}\,\mathrm m
  =0.27\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{0.27}{0.54}=-0.50.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object distance is three times the focal length, so the denominator in the expression for $q$ is twice the focal length. This gives $q=\dfrac{p}{2}$. The positive sign puts the image in front of the mirror. The negative sign of $M$ indicates that the image is inverted, while its magnitude gives half the height. An object $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ high would therefore have an image tip at $-2\,\mathrm{cm}$ relative to the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A concave mirror has radius of curvature $R=36\,\mathrm{cm}$. A real object is at $p=9\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex. Find the image position and orientation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{36}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=18\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{18\cdot9}{9-18}\,\mathrm{cm}=-18\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{-18}{9}=+2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radius of curvature is twice the focal length in the paraxial model. Therefore $p=9\,\mathrm{cm}$ lies inside the focal length. The reflected rays diverge, and their backward extensions give a virtual image $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ behind the surface. The magnification is positive, so the image is upright. The value $+2$ explains the doubled height seen in a concave mirror used at close range.

</div>
