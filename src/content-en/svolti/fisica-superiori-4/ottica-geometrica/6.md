## Exercise 1

A diverging lens has focal length $f=-0.15\,\mathrm m$. The real object is at $p=0.24\,\mathrm m$. Calculate $q$ and state whether the image lies between the lens and the front focus.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=\frac{(-0.15)\cdot0.24}{0.24-(-0.15)}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{-0.036}{0.39}\,\mathrm m
  \simeq-0.0923\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sign of $f$ must appear in both the numerator and the denominator. In the denominator, $p-f$ becomes $p+|f|$ and remains positive. The numerator is negative, so the image is virtual on the object side. Its distance from the lens is about $9.23\,\mathrm{cm}$, less than the $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the lens to the front focus. The image therefore lies between the lens and the focus.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object is at $p=0.30\,\mathrm m$ from a diverging lens of focal length $f=-0.15\,\mathrm m$. Find $q$ and $M$, and determine the image height if the object is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ high.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=\frac{(-0.15)\cdot0.30}{0.30-(-0.15)}\,\mathrm m
  =-0.10\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{-0.10}{0.30}=\frac{1}{3}\\[6pt]
h'&=Mh=\frac{1}{3}\cdot3\,\mathrm{cm}=1\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outgoing rays diverge while their backward extensions meet on the entrance side. This gives a negative $q$. The ratio $-\dfrac{q}{p}$ is positive and less than one, so the image is upright and reduced. Since the height ratio is dimensionless, the height in centimeters can be used directly. The factor $\dfrac{1}{3}$ maps a $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ object to a $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ image.

</div>
