## Exercise 1

A closed surface encloses $q_1=+2.4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q_2=-1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. Outside there is $q_3=+5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. Find the total flux. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="1.65*cos(t),1.65*sin(t),0,6.28318530718" punti="-0.52,0.54,pieno,q₁;0.45,-0.51,pieno,q₂;2.46,0.59,pieno,q₃" xmin=-2.5 xmax=3.3 ymin=-2 ymax=2 titolo="The surface encloses two of the three charges. The exterior point contributes to the local field."}

We apply Gauss's law, in which $Q_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the algebraic sum of the enclosed charges only: $\Phi_E=\dfrac{q_1+q_2}{\varepsilon_0}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{int}}&=(2.4-1.0)\,\mathrm{nC}
=1.4\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C},\\[6pt]
\Phi_E&=\frac{1.4\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}}\\[6pt]
&\simeq158\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the balance for the surface we include the charges it encloses and exclude the others. We add $q_1$ and $q_2$ with their signs, so the net enclosed charge is $+1.4\,\mathrm{nC}$. To apply the formula, the number of nanocoulombs is multiplied by $10^{-9}$ and divided by $\varepsilon_0$, giving $158\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$, because $\dfrac{1.4\cdot10^{-9}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\simeq158.2$. The total flux is positive even though one interior charge is negative, since the positive charge prevails. The charge $q_3$ modifies the field at every point of the surface, but its net contribution to the flux through the closed surface is exactly zero, since the field of $q_3$ enters through one part of the surface and exits through the opposite part with the same flux. The flux therefore depends only on the interior charge, so moving $q_3$ along any path that stays outside the surface leaves the value $158\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$ unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A closed surface contains $q_1=+3\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q_2=-1\,\mathrm{nC}$. The charge $q_3=+5\,\mathrm{nC}$ is first outside and then, at the end of a displacement, inside. Find the total flux in the two states. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

Gauss's law applies in each of the two states with the charge enclosed at that moment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{int},1}&=(3-1)\,\mathrm{nC}=2\,\mathrm{nC},\\[6pt]
\Phi_1&=\frac{2\cdot10^{-9}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}\simeq226\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C},\\[10pt]
Q_{\mathrm{int},2}&=(3-1+5)\,\mathrm{nC}=7\,\mathrm{nC},\\[6pt]
\Phi_2&=\frac{7\cdot10^{-9}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}\simeq791\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the start the exterior charge gives zero net flux, so only $q_1$ and $q_2$ appear in the numerator. Dividing $2\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}$ by $\varepsilon_0$ gives the first flux, about $226\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. At the end $q_3$ is also enclosed, so the net charge becomes $7\,\mathrm{nC}$ and the flux about $791\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. As a check, the jump in flux must be the contribution of the charge that crossed the surface, that is, $\dfrac{5\cdot10^{-9}}{\varepsilon_0}\simeq565\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$, and indeed $791-226=565$. During the displacement the field at each point changes continuously, while the flux through the surface changes only at the moment the charge enters the volume. The same procedure holds for any number of charges, interior or exterior: only the interior ones are added with their sign and the sum is divided by $\varepsilon_0$.

</div>
