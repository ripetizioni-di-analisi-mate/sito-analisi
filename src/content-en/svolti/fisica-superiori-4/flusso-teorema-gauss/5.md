## Exercise 1

An insulating sphere of radius $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ carries a uniformly distributed total charge of $0.8\,\mathrm{nC}$. Calculate the field magnitude $0.040\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{int}}&=0.8\left(\frac{0.040}{0.10}\right)^3\,\mathrm{nC}
=0.0512\,\mathrm{nC},\\[6pt]
E&=\frac{0.0512\cdot10^{-9}}{4\pi(8.85\cdot10^{-12})(0.040)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq288\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point is inside the insulator. For uniform density, the fraction of charge enclosed equals the fraction of the volume. The Gaussian sphere has a radius $0.4$ times the outer radius, so the enclosed charge is $0.4^3$ times the total. Only this enclosed charge enters Gauss's law. Divide by $\varepsilon_0$ and the area of the inner sphere, which includes $(0.040)^2$. The magnitude is about $288\,\mathrm{N/C}$, and the positive charge gives an outward field.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An insulating sphere of radius $R=0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ has a uniformly distributed total charge of $1\,\mathrm{nC}$. Calculate the field magnitude at $r=0.040\,\mathrm{m}$ and at the surface. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

::grafico{f="x<=10?89.918*x:89918/x^2" xmin=0 xmax=25 ymin=0 ymax=1000 nomi="r,E (N/C)" punti="4,359.67,pieno;10,899.18,pieno" legenda="E" titolo="Radius $r$ is in centimeters. The inner point at $0.040\,\mathrm{m}$ and the surface at $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ are marked."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(R)&=\frac{10^{-9}}{4\pi(8.85\cdot10^{-12})(0.10)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq899\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
E(r)&=\frac{r}{R}E(R)\simeq360\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the surface the Gaussian sphere encloses all the charge and its area is $4\pi R^2$. The result is about $899\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Moving to an inner sphere, enclosed charge falls as $r^3$ and area as $r^2$. Their ratio therefore falls in proportion to $r$. At $0.040\,\mathrm{m}$ the field is forty percent of the surface value. The result of about $360\,\mathrm{N/C}$ agrees with the rising portion of the graph.

</div>
