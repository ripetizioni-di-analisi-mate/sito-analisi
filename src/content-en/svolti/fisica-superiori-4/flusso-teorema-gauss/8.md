## Exercise 1

Two parallel plates have densities $+\sigma_q$ and $-\sigma_q$, with $\sigma_q=6.4\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$. Neglecting edge effects, find the magnitude and direction of the field between them. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.15,-1.2 -1.15,1.2;1.15,-1.2 1.15,1.2" vettori="-0.85,0.52,0.65,0.52,E₁;-0.85,-0.48,0.65,-0.48,E₂" etichette="-1.57,1.35,+σ;1.07,1.35,−σ" xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-1.62 ymax=1.68 titolo="Between the plates, both contributions point from the positive plate toward the negative one."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\frac{\sigma_q}{2\varepsilon_0}+\frac{\sigma_q}{2\varepsilon_0}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{6.4\cdot10^{-9}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq723\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field of the positive plate points away from it. The field of the negative plate points toward it. Between the plates, both point from positive to negative. The sum of the magnitudes has two contributions, each equal to the density divided by $2\varepsilon_0$, so the two cancels. The resulting magnitude is about $723\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Uniform density and the infinite-plane approximation give the same value throughout the gap.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two parallel plates with opposite densities of magnitude $8\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$ are $0.015\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. Neglecting edge effects, calculate the field magnitude and the magnitude of the potential difference. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\frac{8\cdot10^{-9}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq904\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
|\Delta V|&=Ed\simeq904\cdot0.015\,\mathrm{V}
\simeq13.6\,\mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field within the gap adds the two fields in the same direction and is about $904\,\mathrm{N/C}$. In a uniform field the potential decreases by $E$ per unit distance along the field. The perpendicular distance between the plates is $0.015\,\mathrm{m}$. Multiplying it by the field magnitude gives about $13.6\,\mathrm{V}$. The negative plate has lower potential than the positive plate, since moving from positive to negative follows the field.

</div>
