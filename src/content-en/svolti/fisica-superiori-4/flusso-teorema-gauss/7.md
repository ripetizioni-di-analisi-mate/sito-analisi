## Exercise 1

An infinite insulating plane has uniform density $1.2\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$. Calculate the field magnitude on one side. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2.55,0 2.55,0;-1.15,-0.65 1.15,-0.65 1.15,0.65 -1.15,0.65 -1.15,-0.65" vettori="-0.42,0.68,-0.42,1.48,E;0.42,-0.68,0.42,-1.48,E" etichette="-1.99,0.18,+σ;0.34,0.78,A;-0.5,-0.82,A" xmin=-2.85 xmax=2.85 ymin=-1.65 ymax=1.65 titolo="The field has the same magnitude on both caps. The curved side is parallel to the field arrows."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
2EA&=\frac{\sigma_q A}{\varepsilon_0},\\[6pt]
E&=\frac{1.2\cdot10^{-8}}{2\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq678\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use a short cylinder crossing the plane. The field is parallel to the curved side and perpendicular to the two caps. The density is positive, so flux leaves through both caps and the contributions have the same sign. The enclosed charge is the density times the cylinder cross-section $A$. In Gauss's law the area cancels, while the factor of two from the two contributions remains. On each side the field points away from the plane and has a magnitude of about $678\,\mathrm{N/C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An infinite insulating plane has uniform positive density $1.5\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$. Find the field magnitude for this plane alone and after adding a parallel plane of equal and opposite density. In the second case consider the region between the planes. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1 &=\frac{1.5\cdot10^{-8}}{2\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq847\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
E_2 &=2E_1\simeq1.69\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each isolated plane gives a field of about $847\,\mathrm{N/C}$ on either side. Between the planes the positive plane's field points toward the negative one, and the negative plane's field points toward the negative one too. The contributions therefore point in the same direction and add. Outside they cancel, since the magnitudes remain equal and the directions there are opposite. The field between the planes is about $1.69\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$.

</div>
