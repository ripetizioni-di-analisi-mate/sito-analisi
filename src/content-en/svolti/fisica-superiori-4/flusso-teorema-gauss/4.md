## Exercise 1

A spherically symmetric distribution of radius $R=0.050\,\mathrm{m}$ has total charge $Q=+1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$. Calculate the magnitude of the field at $r=0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="0.55*cos(t),0.55*sin(t),0,6.28318530718;1.9*cos(t),1.9*sin(t),0,6.28318530718" segmenti="0,0,1.9,0" punti="0,0,pieno;1.9,0,pieno,P" etichette="0.35,0.35,R;1.12,-0.16,r" xmin=-2.25 xmax=2.55 ymin=-2.15 ymax=2.15 titolo="The larger Gaussian surface encloses the entire distribution, shown by the small circle."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1.6\cdot10^{-9}}{4\pi(8.85\cdot10^{-12})(0.20)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq360\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The observation point lies farther from the center than the boundary of the distribution. The Gaussian sphere of radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ therefore encloses all $1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$. By symmetry the field has a constant magnitude over the sphere and points radially. Positive charge gives an outward direction. The square of $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ belongs in the denominator because it sets the area crossed by the field, while the radius $R=0.050\,\mathrm{m}$ establishes that the point is outside.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A spherically symmetric distribution of radius $0.050\,\mathrm{m}$ has a total charge of $+2\,\mathrm{nC}$. Calculate the field magnitudes at $0.20$ and $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1&=\frac{2\cdot10^{-9}}{4\pi(8.85\cdot10^{-12})(0.20)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq450\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
E_2&=\frac{E_1}{2^2}\simeq112\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both spheres are outside the distribution, so each encloses $+2\,\mathrm{nC}$. Dividing the flux by the area of the first gives about $450\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The second has double the radius and therefore four times the area. The flux is the same through both surfaces, so the second field is one-quarter as large. The first value before rounding is about $449.6\,\mathrm{N/C}$, which gives about $112\,\mathrm{N/C}$ for the second after rounding.

</div>
