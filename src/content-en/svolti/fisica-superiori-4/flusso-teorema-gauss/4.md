## Exercise 1

A spherically symmetric distribution of radius $R=0.050\,\mathrm{m}$ has total charge $Q=+1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$. Find the magnitude of the field at $r=0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="0.55*cos(t),0.55*sin(t),0,6.28318530718;1.9*cos(t),1.9*sin(t),0,6.28318530718" segmenti="0,0,1.9,0" punti="0,0,pieno;1.9,0,pieno,P" etichette="0.35,0.35,R;1.12,-0.16,r" xmin=-2.25 xmax=2.55 ymin=-2.15 ymax=2.15 titolo="The larger Gaussian surface encloses the whole distribution, represented by the small circle."}

By symmetry the field is radial and has the same magnitude on a concentric Gaussian sphere of radius $r$. Gauss's law, with $r>R$ and therefore $Q_{\mathrm{int}}=Q$, gives
$$
E\cdot4\pi r^2=\frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2&=4\pi\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}\cdot(0.20)^2\,\mathrm{C^2/N}\\[6pt]
&\simeq4.45\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/N},\\[10pt]
E&=\frac{1.6\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}}{4.45\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/N}}\\[6pt]
&\simeq360\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The observation point is farther from the center than the radius of the distribution, so the Gaussian sphere of radius $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ encloses all $1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$. By symmetry the field has the same magnitude at every point of the surface and points in the radial direction, that is, parallel to the outward normal, so the flux is simply $E\cdot4\pi r^2$. With positive charge the direction is outward. The denominator contains the square of $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, which determines the area crossed by the field, while the radius $R=0.050\,\mathrm{m}$ serves only to check that the point is exterior. The unit $\mathrm{C/(C^2/N)}$ reduces to $\mathrm{N/C}$ and the field is about $360\,\mathrm{N/C}$. As a check, the field of a charge of $1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$ at $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ has the order of magnitude $\dfrac{9\cdot10^9\cdot1.6\cdot10^{-9}}{0.04}\simeq360\,\mathrm{N/C}$, computed with Coulomb's law, and coincides with the value found.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A symmetric spherical distribution of radius $0.050\,\mathrm{m}$ has total charge $+2\,\mathrm{nC}$. Find the magnitudes of the field at $0.20$ and $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

Both points are exterior to the distribution, so for both $E=\dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$ holds with $Q=2\,\mathrm{nC}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1&=\frac{2\cdot10^{-9}}{4\pi\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}\cdot(0.20)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq450\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[10pt]
E_2&=\frac{2\cdot10^{-9}}{4\pi\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}\cdot(0.40)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq112\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[10pt]
\frac{E_2}{E_1}&=\left(\frac{0.20}{0.40}\right)^2=\frac{1}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For both Gaussian spheres the enclosed charge is $+2\,\mathrm{nC}$, so the flux through the two surfaces is the same, $\dfrac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}$. Dividing the flux by the area of the first sphere, $4\pi(0.20)^2\,\mathrm{m^2}$, gives a magnitude of about $450\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The second sphere has double the radius and therefore four times the area, so for the same flux the field is one fourth of the first. The precise value of $E_1$ is $449.6\,\mathrm{N/C}$, from which $E_2=112.4\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The ratio $\dfrac{1}{4}$ does not depend on $Q$ or on $\varepsilon_0$ and holds for any pair of exterior points with radii in the ratio $1:2$. As a check, the flux on each sphere is $\dfrac{2\cdot10^{-9}}{8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\simeq226\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$, and dividing it by the areas $0.503\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $2.01\,\mathrm{m^2}$ gives back $450$ and $112\,\mathrm{N/C}$.

</div>
