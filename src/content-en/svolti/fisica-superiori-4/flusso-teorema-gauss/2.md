## Exercise 1

The total outward flux from a closed surface is $+192\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. Calculate the net enclosed charge in nanocoulombs. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{int}}&=\varepsilon_0\Phi_E
=(8.85\cdot10^{-12})(192)\,\mathrm{C}\\[6pt]
&=1.6992\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}\simeq1.70\,\mathrm{nC}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given flux is the net value over the whole surface. Gauss's law therefore gives the enclosed charge by multiplication by $\varepsilon_0$. In the product the newtons, square meters, and one of the two coulomb factors cancel, leaving coulombs. Since a nanocoulomb is $10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}$, the result is $1.70\,\mathrm{nC}$. The positive sign applies to the sum of enclosed charges. The flux alone gives no count of how many charges are inside.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A closed surface has net flux $240\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. Calculate the net enclosed charge. Then calculate the flux if the enclosed charge doubles. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{int}}&=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\cdot240\,\mathrm{C}
=2.124\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C},\\[6pt]
\Phi_2&=\frac{2Q_{\mathrm{int}}}{\varepsilon_0}
=2\cdot240\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}=480\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first situation the positive flux gives a positive net charge of about $2.12\,\mathrm{nC}$. In the second the numerator of Gauss's formula changes while $\varepsilon_0$ is the same constant. The flux increases in the same ratio as the charge, so it doubles to $480\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. The calculation is for the total flux. The same charge can be distributed in different ways and produce different local field values.

</div>
