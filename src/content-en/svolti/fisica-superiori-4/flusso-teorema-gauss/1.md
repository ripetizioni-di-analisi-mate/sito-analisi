## Exercise 1

A uniform field of magnitude $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$ makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the normal to a flat surface of area $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Calculate the signed flux.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2.1,0 2.6,0" vettori="0,0,0,2,n;0,0,2.08,1.2,E" etichette="0.47,0.79,60°;0.76,-0.35,A = 400 cm²" xmin=-2.7 xmax=3 ymin=-0.75 ymax=2.25 titolo="The normal is the reference for the angle. The component perpendicular to the plane is positive."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_n&=E\cos60^\circ=200\cdot\tfrac12\,\mathrm{N/C}=100\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
\Phi_E&=E_n A=100\cdot0.040\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}
=4.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To calculate flux, use the **normal** component of the field. The angle is already measured from the normal, so multiply $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$ by the cosine of $60^\circ$, which is $\dfrac12$. The normal component is therefore $100\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Multiplying by the area of $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $4.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. The result is positive because the field has a component in the direction of the chosen normal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform field of magnitude $250\,\mathrm{N/C}$ makes a $60^\circ$ angle with the chosen normal to a flat surface of area $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find the flux. The surface is then rotated until its normal points in the direction of the field. Find the new flux.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{60}&=250\cdot0.040\cos60^\circ\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}
=5\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C},\\[6pt]
\Phi_0&=250\cdot0.040\cos0^\circ\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}
=10\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field magnitude and area are fixed. In the first position only half the field contributes along the normal because $\cos60^\circ=\dfrac12$. In the second position the entire field points along the area vector and the cosine is one. The flux doubles from $5$ to $10\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. This is the maximum for the given field and area, since the normal component cannot exceed the field magnitude.

</div>
