## Exercise 1

A uniform field of magnitude $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$ makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the normal to a flat surface of area $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find the signed flux.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2.1,0 2.6,0" vettori="0,0,0,2,n;0,0,2.08,1.2,E" etichette="0.47,0.79,60°;0.76,-0.35,A = 400 cm²" xmin=-2.7 xmax=3 ymin=-0.75 ymax=2.25 titolo="The normal is the reference for the angle. The component perpendicular to the plane is positive."}

The field is uniform and the surface is flat, so the definition of flux applies,
$$
\Phi_E=\vec E\cdot\vec A=EA\cos\theta,
$$
with $E=200\,\mathrm{N/C}$, $A=0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and $\theta=60^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_n&=E\cos60^\circ=200\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{N/C}=100\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
\Phi_E&=E_nA=100\,\mathrm{N/C}\cdot0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
&=4.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compute the flux we use the **normal** component of the field, that is, the projection of $\vec E$ on the direction of $\hat n$. The given angle is already referred to the normal, so we multiply $200\,\mathrm{N/C}$ by $\cos60^\circ=\dfrac{1}{2}$ and obtain $E_n=100\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Since the field is uniform, $E_n$ has the same value over the whole surface and the flux is the product of $E_n$ and the area. The units are $\mathrm{N/C}\cdot\mathrm{m^2}=\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$, that of flux. The flux is **positive**, $4.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$, because the cosine of $60^\circ$ is positive and the field has a component along the chosen normal. If we chose the opposite normal the angle would become $120^\circ$ and the flux would be $-4.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. As a check, the result is smaller than $EA=200\cdot0.040=8.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$, the maximum possible flux with these data, and equals exactly half of it, as required by $\cos60^\circ=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform field of magnitude $250\,\mathrm{N/C}$ makes $60^\circ$ with the chosen normal to a flat surface of area $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find the flux. The surface is then rotated until the normal points along the field. Find the new flux.

We again use $\Phi_E=EA\cos\theta$ with $E=250\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and $A=0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ fixed. Only the angle $\theta$ between the field and the normal changes, from $60^\circ$ to $0^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{60}&=250\,\mathrm{N/C}\cdot0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}\cdot\cos60^\circ\\[6pt]
&=10\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}=5.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C},\\[10pt]
\Phi_{0}&=250\,\mathrm{N/C}\cdot0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}\cdot\cos0^\circ\\[6pt]
&=10\cdot1\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}=10\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product $EA$ is $250\cdot0.040=10\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$ in both positions, because the field magnitude and the area do not change. In the first position only the normal component contributes, which is half the field since $\cos60^\circ=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the flux is $5.0\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$. In the second position the field is parallel to $\vec A$ and $\cos0^\circ=1$, so the flux is $10\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}$ and doubles. This is the **maximum value** for given field and area, because the normal component of the field cannot exceed its magnitude. The flux would be zero at $\theta=90^\circ$, that is, with the field parallel to the surface. The ratio of the two fluxes is $\dfrac{\Phi_0}{\Phi_{60}}=\dfrac{1}{\cos60^\circ}=2$, independent of the values of $E$ and $A$, and coincides with the ratio $\dfrac{10}{5}$ found in the calculation.

</div>
