## Exercise 1

An infinite wire has uniform linear density $\lambda_q=0.96\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. Calculate the field magnitude $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ from its axis. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\frac{\lambda_q}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.96\cdot10^{-9}}{2\pi(8.85\cdot10^{-12})(0.080)}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq216\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point lies on the curved side of a coaxial cylinder of radius $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$. The field is tangent to the caps, so all the flux is through the curved side, where its magnitude is constant. The enclosed charge is the linear density times the cylinder length. The same factor occurs in the side area and cancels. Convert the density to coulombs per meter and divide by $2\pi\varepsilon_0r$. The positive density gives an outward field of about $216\,\mathrm{N/C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An infinite wire has uniform density $1.2\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. Calculate the field magnitude at $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and at twice that distance. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1&=\frac{1.2\cdot10^{-9}}{2\pi(8.85\cdot10^{-12})(0.080)}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq270\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
E_2&=\frac{E_1}{2}\simeq135\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare the two points, choose two cylinders of equal length. Both enclose the same section of wire, so their total fluxes are equal. Doubling the radius doubles the circumference and hence the area carrying the flux. Half the initial field, about $135\,\mathrm{N/C}$, maintains the same flux. The factor of two comes from the shape of the curved cylinder side.

</div>
