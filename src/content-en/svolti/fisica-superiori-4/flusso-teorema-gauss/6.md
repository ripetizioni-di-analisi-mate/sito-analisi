## Exercise 1

An infinite wire has uniform linear density $\lambda_q=0.96\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. Find the magnitude of the field at distance $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ from the axis. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2.4,-1.12 2.4,-1.12 2.4,1.12 -2.4,1.12 -2.4,-1.12" segmenti="-3.12,0,3.12,0;0,-1.12,0,0;-2.4,-1.46,2.4,-1.46" vettori="-1.5,1.13,-1.5,1.71,E;1.2,-1.13,1.2,-1.72,E;0.85,0.24,0.85,0.84,E" etichette="-0.32,-0.59,r;-0.04,-1.6,L;2.58,-0.13,wire" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.75 ymin=-1.95 ymax=1.95 titolo="Cross-section along the axis of the Gaussian cylinder of radius $r$ and length $L$. The field crosses the curved side."}

We choose a Gaussian cylinder coaxial with the wire, of radius $r=0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and length $L$. The flux through the end caps is zero and that through the curved side is $E\cdot2\pi rL$, so
$$
E\cdot2\pi rL=\frac{\lambda_qL}{\varepsilon_0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E=\frac{\lambda_q}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\pi\varepsilon_0r&=2\pi\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}\cdot0.080\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m)}\\[6pt]
&\simeq4.45\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m)},\\[10pt]
E&=\frac{0.96\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C/m}}{4.45\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m)}}\\[6pt]
&\simeq216\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point lies on the curved side of a coaxial cylinder of radius $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$. By symmetry the field is radial, so it is tangent to the end caps, which do not contribute to the flux, and has constant magnitude on the curved side. The enclosed charge is the linear density times the length $L$ of the cylinder. Since the same factor $L$ appears in the lateral area $2\pi rL$, it cancels and the result does not depend on the chosen length. The density is converted to $\mathrm{C/m}$ with $\mathrm{nC}=10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C}$ and divided by $2\pi\varepsilon_0r$. The units reduce to $\mathrm{N/C}$. The density is positive, so the field points outward and is about $216\,\mathrm{N/C}$. As a check, $\dfrac{\lambda_q}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}=\dfrac{0.96\cdot10^{-9}}{2\pi\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}}\simeq17.3\,\mathrm{N\,m/C}$ and, divided by $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$, gives $216\,\mathrm{N/C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An infinite wire has uniform density $1.2\,\mathrm{nC/m}$. Find the magnitude of the field at $0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and at double that distance. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}$.

The field magnitude is $E=\dfrac{\lambda_q}{2\pi\varepsilon_0r}$, inversely proportional to $r$. We compute $E_1$ at $r_1=0.080\,\mathrm{m}$ and then $E_2$ at $r_2=2r_1=0.16\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1&=\frac{1.2\cdot10^{-9}}{2\pi\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}\cdot0.080}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq270\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[10pt]
E_2&=\frac{1.2\cdot10^{-9}}{2\pi\cdot8.85\cdot10^{-12}\cdot0.16}\,\mathrm{N/C}
\simeq135\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[10pt]
\frac{E_2}{E_1}&=\frac{r_1}{r_2}=\frac{1}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare the two points we choose two cylinders of the same length. Both enclose the same stretch of wire, so their total flux is equal. Doubling the radius doubles the circumference of the curved side and therefore the area carrying the flux, so the field magnitude is halved and $E_2=\dfrac{E_1}{2}\simeq135\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The factor two comes from the shape of the cylindrical side, whose area grows linearly with $r$. For a point charge, whose Gaussian sphere has area proportional to $r^2$, the same doubling of the distance would give a field four times smaller. As a check, the two values satisfy $E_1r_1=E_2r_2$, that is, $270\cdot0.080=135\cdot0.16\simeq21.6\,\mathrm{N\,m/C}$, and this product equals $\dfrac{\lambda_q}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}$.

</div>
