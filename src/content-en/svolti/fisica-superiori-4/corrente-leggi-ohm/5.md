## Exercise 1

A wire has $R_0=12\,\Omega$ at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $\alpha=4.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Find its resistance at $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ using the linear model.

$$
\Delta T=50\,\mathrm{K}.
$$

$$
R=12(1+0.004\cdot50)\,\Omega=14.4\,\Omega.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The law uses the difference between the final temperature and the one at which $R_0$ was measured. Celsius and kelvin temperature differences have the same numerical value. The product $\alpha\Delta T$ is $0.20$, a $20\%$ relative increase. Add it to one before multiplying by the reference resistance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wire has $R_0=15\,\Omega$ at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $\alpha=4.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. After warming to $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ it is held at $12\,\mathrm{V}$. Find its resistance and current.

::grafico{f="15*(1+0.004*(x-20))" xmin=20 xmax=80 ymin=14 ymax=20 nomi="T,R" punti="20,15,pieno;70,18,pieno" titolo="Temperature in degrees Celsius and resistance in ohms."}

$$
R=15(1+0.004\cdot50)\,\Omega=18\,\Omega.
$$

$$
I=\frac{12}{18}\,\mathrm{A}=\frac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{A}\simeq0.667\,\mathrm{A}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final temperature is $50\,\mathrm{K}$ above the reference, so the hot wire has higher resistance. Use $18\,\Omega$, the resistance at its operating temperature, when finding current. The same voltage across the cold wire would give $0.80\,\mathrm{A}$. This confirms that the current falls at fixed voltage.

</div>
