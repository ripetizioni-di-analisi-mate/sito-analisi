## Exercise 1

A $14.4\,\Omega$ ohmic resistor is held at $9.0\,\mathrm{V}$ and constant temperature. Find its current.

::grafico{f="x/14.4" xmin=0 xmax=12 ymin=0 ymax=0.9 nomi="V,I" punti="9,0.625,pieno" titolo="Voltage in volts and current in amperes. At 9 V the current is 0.625 A."}

$$
I=\frac{V}{R}=\frac{9.0}{14.4}\,\mathrm{A}=0.625\,\mathrm{A}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The component is ohmic and its temperature is constant, so the same $R$ relates voltage to current throughout the operating range. Divide by $R$ to solve for current in amperes. At a given voltage, a larger resistance would give a lower point on the graph of $I$ versus $V$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An $18\,\Omega$ ohmic resistor is held at $9.0\,\mathrm{V}$. Find the current and power absorbed.

$$
I=\frac{9.0}{18}\,\mathrm{A}=0.50\,\mathrm{A}.
$$

$$
P=VI=9.0\cdot0.50\,\mathrm{W}=4.5\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The voltage tells us each coulomb transfers $9.0\,\mathrm{J}$ as it crosses the resistor. Ohm's law gives a current of $0.50$ net coulombs per second. Their product measures energy transferred per second. An equivalent check is to divide the square of $9.0$ by $18$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An ohmic characteristic passes through $(V,I)=(2\,\mathrm{V},0.50\,\mathrm{A})$ and $(6\,\mathrm{V},1.50\,\mathrm{A})$. Find the slope on the graph of $I$ versus $V$ and use it to find $R$.

$$
p=\frac{1.50-0.50}{6-2}\,\mathrm{A/V}=0.25\,\mathrm{A/V}.
$$

$$
R=\frac{1}{p}=4\,\Omega.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In these coordinates, current is on the vertical axis, so slope measures amperes per volt. The law $I=\dfrac{V}{R}$ identifies the coefficient of $V$ as the reciprocal of resistance. Swapping the axes gives a slope of $4\,\mathrm{V/A}$ instead. Units identify which ratio was calculated.

</div>
