## Exercise 1

An ohmic resistor of $14.4\,\Omega$ is kept at $9.0\,\mathrm{V}$ at constant temperature. Calculate the current.

::grafico{f="x/14.4" xmin=0 xmax=12 ymin=0 ymax=0.9 nomi="V,I" punti="9,0.625,pieno" titolo="Voltage in volts and current in amperes. At 9 V we read 0.625 A."}

Ohm's law for the ohmic resistor at constant temperature:

$$
V=R\,I\quad\Longrightarrow\quad I=\frac{V}{R}.
$$

Substituting the data gives

$$
I=\frac{9.0\,\mathrm{V}}{14.4\,\Omega}=0.625\,\mathrm{A}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The component is ohmic and its temperature is constant, so the same $R$ relates voltage and current over the whole working range and the law $V=RI$ applies. Since $V$ and $R$ are known, we solve for $I$ by dividing by $R$.

The ratio of volts to ohms is expressed in amperes, because $1\,\Omega=1\,\mathrm{V/A}$. The numerical quotient $\dfrac{9.0}{14.4}$ equals $0.625$.

In the graph of $I$ versus $V$ the characteristic is a line through the origin of slope $\dfrac{1}{14.4}\,\mathrm{A/V}$, and at $9.0\,\mathrm{V}$ the point has ordinate $0.625\,\mathrm{A}$. At a given voltage, a larger resistance would produce a lower point. The current in the resistor is **$0.625\,\mathrm{A}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ohmic resistor of $18\,\Omega$ is kept at $9.0\,\mathrm{V}$. Calculate the current and the power absorbed.

We apply Ohm's law and the power absorbed by a resistor, which can also be written with the voltage and the resistance alone:

$$
I=\frac{V}{R},\qquad P=VI=\frac{V^2}{R}.
$$

Substituting the data gives

$$
I=\frac{9.0\,\mathrm{V}}{18\,\Omega}=0.50\,\mathrm{A}.
$$

$$
P=VI=9.0\,\mathrm{V}\cdot0.50\,\mathrm{A}=4.5\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

Check with the formula that uses only the data:

$$
P=\frac{V^2}{R}=\frac{9.0^2}{18}\,\mathrm{W}=\frac{81}{18}\,\mathrm{W}=4.5\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The resistor is ohmic, so the current follows from Ohm's law and equals $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$. This means that every second a net $0.50\,\mathrm{C}$ crosses the resistor.

The voltage of $9.0\,\mathrm{V}$ indicates that each coulomb crossing the resistor transfers $9.0\,\mathrm{J}$. The product $VI$ is therefore the energy transferred each second, that is, the power, and $\mathrm{V\cdot A}=\mathrm{W}$.

The second expression is obtained by substituting $I=\dfrac{V}{R}$ into $P=VI$ and gives the same value, because the data are the same. The resistor absorbs **$4.5\,\mathrm{W}$** with a current of **$0.50\,\mathrm{A}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The ohmic characteristic of a resistor passes through $(V,I)=(2\,\mathrm{V},0.50\,\mathrm{A})$ and $(6\,\mathrm{V},1.50\,\mathrm{A})$. Calculate the slope in the graph of $I$ versus $V$ and find $R$.

::grafico{f="0.25*x" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=0 ymax=2 nomi="V,I" punti="2,0.5,pieno;6,1.5,pieno" titolo="Voltage in volts and current in amperes. The slope of the line is $0.25\,\mathrm{A/V}$."}

In the graph with $I$ on the vertical axis the characteristic is $I=\dfrac{1}{R}V$, so the slope is $p=\dfrac{1}{R}$. Given two points,

$$
p=\frac{\Delta I}{\Delta V}=\frac{I_2-I_1}{V_2-V_1},\qquad R=\frac{1}{p}.
$$

Substituting the data gives

$$
p=\frac{1.50\,\mathrm{A}-0.50\,\mathrm{A}}{6\,\mathrm{V}-2\,\mathrm{V}}=\frac{1.00\,\mathrm{A}}{4\,\mathrm{V}}=0.25\,\mathrm{A/V}.
$$

$$
R=\frac{1}{0.25\,\mathrm{A/V}}=4\,\Omega.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The resistor is ohmic, so $I=\dfrac{V}{R}$ is a line through the origin. In the chosen coordinates the coefficient of $V$ is the slope, and by Ohm's law it coincides with $\dfrac{1}{R}$.

The slope is calculated by dividing the change in current by the change in voltage between the two points. The differences are $1.00\,\mathrm{A}$ and $4\,\mathrm{V}$, so the slope is $0.25\,\mathrm{A/V}$. The line through the origin with this slope passes through the first point, because $0.25\cdot2=0.50$.

The reciprocal of the slope gives the resistance, $4\,\Omega$. If the axes were swapped the slope would be $4\,\mathrm{V/A}$. The unit makes it possible to identify which ratio has been calculated. The resistance of the component is **$4\,\Omega$**.

</div>
