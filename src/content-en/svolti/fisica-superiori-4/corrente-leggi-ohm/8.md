## Exercise 1

A $1200\,\mathrm{W}$ heater transfers $80\%$ of the energy it draws to water. It heats $0.72\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water by $25\,\mathrm{K}$ with $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Find the time.

$$
Q=mc\Delta T=75240\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
P_{\mathrm{useful}}=0.80\cdot1200\,\mathrm{W}=960\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

$$
\Delta t=\frac{75240}{960}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq78.4\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required heat raises the temperature of the stated mass of water. Only four fifths of the energy drawn from the circuit reaches the water, so useful power is $960\,\mathrm{J/s}$. Divide the required heat by that power. This is greater than the roughly $63\,\mathrm{s}$ needed at perfect efficiency.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $1200\,\mathrm{W}$ heater transfers a fraction $\eta=0.80$ of its energy to water. It heats $0.90\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water by $25\,\mathrm{K}$ with $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Find the time and electrical energy drawn.

$$
Q=0.90\cdot4180\cdot25\,\mathrm{J}=94050\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{94050}{0.80}\,\mathrm{J}=117562.5\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
\Delta t=\frac{117562.5}{1200}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq98.0\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The heat gained by the water is the useful energy. Since it equals $80\%$ of the electrical input, divide by $0.80$ to find the input energy. That amount is greater than the useful heat. Time follows from input energy and the full electrical power. Dividing useful heat by $960\,\mathrm{W}$ gives the same result.

</div>
