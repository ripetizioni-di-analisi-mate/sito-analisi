## Exercise 1

A $1200\,\mathrm{W}$ heater transfers $80\%$ of the energy it draws to the water. It heats $0.72\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water by $25\,\mathrm{K}$ with $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Calculate the time.

::grafico{f="0.319*x" xmin=0 xmax=90 ymin=0 ymax=30 nomi="t,ΔT" punti="78.4,25,pieno" titolo="Time in seconds and temperature rise in kelvins. The rise of $25\,\mathrm{K}$ is reached after about $78\,\mathrm{s}$."}

We apply the heat needed to warm the water, the useful power with the efficiency $\eta$, and the time as the ratio of heat to useful power:

$$
Q=m\,c\,\Delta T,\qquad P_{\mathrm{useful}}=\eta\,P,\qquad \Delta t=\frac{Q}{P_{\mathrm{useful}}}=\frac{m\,c\,\Delta T}{\eta\,P}.
$$

Substituting the data gives

$$
Q=0.72\cdot4180\cdot25\,\mathrm{J}=75\,240\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
P_{\mathrm{useful}}=0.80\cdot1200\,\mathrm{W}=960\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

$$
\Delta t=\frac{75\,240\,\mathrm{J}}{960\,\mathrm{J/s}}\simeq78.4\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The heat required is the one that raises the temperature of the indicated mass of water, so it is calculated with $mc\Delta T$ and equals $75\,240\,\mathrm{J}$.

Of the energy drawn by the circuit only $80\%$ reaches the water, so the useful power is $0.80\cdot1200=960\,\mathrm{W}$, that is, $960\,\mathrm{J}$ every second. Dividing the heat by this power gives the seconds needed.

With unit efficiency the denominator would be $1200\,\mathrm{W}$ and the time about $63\,\mathrm{s}$. The value obtained is larger, because losses lengthen the time for the same heat required. The heating takes about **$78.4\,\mathrm{s}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $1200\,\mathrm{W}$ heater transfers the fraction $\eta=0.80$ to the water. It heats $0.90\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water by $25\,\mathrm{K}$ with $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Calculate the time and the electrical energy drawn.

We apply the useful heat, the electrical energy drawn related to the efficiency, and the time with the full electrical power:

$$
Q=m\,c\,\Delta T,\qquad Q=\eta\,E_{\mathrm{el}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{Q}{\eta},\qquad \Delta t=\frac{E_{\mathrm{el}}}{P}.
$$

Substituting the data gives

$$
Q=0.90\cdot4180\cdot25\,\mathrm{J}=94\,050\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{94\,050\,\mathrm{J}}{0.80}=117\,562.5\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
\Delta t=\frac{117\,562.5\,\mathrm{J}}{1200\,\mathrm{W}}\simeq98.0\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The heat the water needs is the useful energy and is calculated with $mc\Delta T$. Since this energy is $80\%$ of the electrical energy, we divide by $0.80$ to find the energy drawn by the circuit. The latter is larger than the useful heat.

The time is obtained by dividing the electrical energy by the full electrical power of the heater, because every second the circuit draws $1200\,\mathrm{J}$. The energy lost is $117\,562.5-94\,050=23\,512.5\,\mathrm{J}$.

The same time is obtained by dividing the useful heat by the useful power $960\,\mathrm{W}$, because $\dfrac{Q}{\eta P}=\dfrac{E_{\mathrm{el}}}{P}$. It takes **$98.0\,\mathrm{s}$** and the circuit draws **$1.18\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{J}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $1500\,\mathrm{W}$ heater transfers $75\%$ of the electrical energy it draws to the water. It heats $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water from $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $65\,{}^\circ\mathrm{C}$, with $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Calculate the time, the electrical energy drawn in kilowatt-hours, and the energy lost.

We apply the useful heat, the electrical energy with the efficiency, the time, and the energy lost:

$$
Q=m\,c\,\Delta T,\quad E_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{Q}{\eta},\quad \Delta t=\frac{E_{\mathrm{el}}}{P},\quad E_{\mathrm{lost}}=E_{\mathrm{el}}-Q.
$$

Temperature difference and substitution of the data:

$$
\Delta T=(65-20)\,\mathrm{K}=45\,\mathrm{K}.
$$

$$
Q=1.5\cdot4180\cdot45\,\mathrm{J}=282\,150\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{282\,150}{0.75}\,\mathrm{J}=376\,200\,\mathrm{J},\qquad \Delta t=\frac{376\,200}{1500}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq251\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
E_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{376\,200}{3.6\cdot10^6}\,\mathrm{kWh}\simeq0.105\,\mathrm{kWh},\qquad E_{\mathrm{lost}}=(376\,200-282\,150)\,\mathrm{J}=94\,050\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The temperature difference in Celsius and in kelvins has the same numerical value, so $\Delta T=45\,\mathrm{K}$ and the useful heat is $282\,150\,\mathrm{J}$.

This heat is $75\%$ of the electrical energy drawn, so we divide by $0.75$ and obtain $376\,200\,\mathrm{J}$. Dividing by the electrical power of $1500\,\mathrm{W}$ gives the time, about $251\,\mathrm{s}$.

To convert to kilowatt-hours we divide by $3.6\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{J/kWh}$, because a kilowatt-hour contains this number of joules. The result is $0.105\,\mathrm{kWh}$.

The energy lost is the difference between the energy drawn and the useful energy, that is, $25\%$ of $E_{\mathrm{el}}$. The heating takes about **$251\,\mathrm{s}$**, draws **$0.105\,\mathrm{kWh}$**, and loses **$9.41\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{J}$**.

</div>
