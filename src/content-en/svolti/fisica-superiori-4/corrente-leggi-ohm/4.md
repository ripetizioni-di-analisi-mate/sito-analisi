## Exercise 1

A uniform wire has $\rho=1.7\cdot10^{-8}\,\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$, length $9.6\,\mathrm{m}$, and cross-sectional area $1.5\,\mathrm{mm^2}$. Find its resistance at fixed temperature.

$$
A=1.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$

$$
R=\rho\frac{L}{A}=\frac{1.7\cdot10^{-8}\cdot9.6}{1.5\cdot10^{-6}}\,\Omega=0.1088\,\Omega.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross section is an area, so square the $10^{-3}$ factor from millimeters to meters. Then apply the law for a uniform wire. The meters in resistivity times those in length cancel the square meters of cross-sectional area, leaving resistance in ohms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wire with $\rho=1.7\cdot10^{-8}\,\Omega\,\mathrm{m}$ is $12\,\mathrm{m}$ long and has area $1.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find its resistance and the power dissipated at $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$.

$$
R=\frac{1.7\cdot10^{-8}\cdot12}{1.5\cdot10^{-6}}\,\Omega=0.136\,\Omega.
$$

$$
P=RI^2=0.136\cdot2.0^2\,\mathrm{W}=0.544\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Geometry and material determine the resistance. Because current is known, power follows directly from $RI^2$ using the wire's resistance. The voltage drop along the wire is $IR=0.272\,\mathrm{V}$, and multiplying it by $2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ gives the same power.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $0.24\,\Omega$ wire is stretched to one and a half times its original length while volume and resistivity stay constant. Find the new resistance.

$$
L_2=1.5L_1,\qquad A_2=\frac{A_1}{1.5}.
$$

$$
R_2=R_1\frac{L_2}{L_1}\frac{A_1}{A_2}=0.24\cdot1.5^2\,\Omega=0.54\,\Omega.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire's volume equals its length times its cross-sectional area. At constant volume, increasing length by a factor of $1.5$ divides area by the same factor. Resistance contains length in the numerator and area in the denominator, so both changes increase the ratio.

</div>
