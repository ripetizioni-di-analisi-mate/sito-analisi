## Exercise 1

A $20\,\Omega$ ohmic resistor carries $0.40\,\mathrm{A}$. Find the power dissipated.

$$
P=RI^2=20\cdot0.40^2\,\mathrm{W}=3.2\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Power is $VI$, but resistance and current are the known quantities. Since the drop across the resistor is $RI$, the product has two factors of current. The drop is $8\,\mathrm{V}$, so $8\,\mathrm{V}$ times $0.40\,\mathrm{A}$ also gives $3.2\,\mathrm{J/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $25\,\Omega$ resistor carries a constant current of $0.40\,\mathrm{A}$. Find its power and the energy dissipated in $90\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
P=25\cdot0.40^2\,\mathrm{W}=4\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

$$
E=P\Delta t=4\cdot90\,\mathrm{J}=360\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At constant current and resistance, the resistor dissipates $4\,\mathrm{J}$ each second. Multiply by time for the total energy. Power remains $4\,\mathrm{W}$ if the duration doubles; the total energy dissipated then doubles.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $20\,\Omega$ resistor operates at $8\,\mathrm{V}$ and $0.40\,\mathrm{A}$. It is replaced with a $40\,\Omega$ resistor. Find the new power first at fixed $8\,\mathrm{V}$, then at fixed $0.40\,\mathrm{A}$.

$$
P_V=\frac{8^2}{40}\,\mathrm{W}=1.6\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

$$
P_I=40\cdot0.40^2\,\mathrm{W}=6.4\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first case voltage is fixed and current falls to $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$. In the second, current is fixed and voltage rises to $16\,\mathrm{V}$. Thus $VI$ halves in the first case and doubles in the second relative to the original $3.2\,\mathrm{W}$. The two setups hold different quantities fixed.

</div>
