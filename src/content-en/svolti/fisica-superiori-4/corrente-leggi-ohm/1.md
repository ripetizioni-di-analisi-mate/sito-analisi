## Exercise 1

A constant current of $0.28\,\mathrm{A}$ crosses a wire section for $150\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the net charge transferred.

::grafico{f="0.28" xmin=0 xmax=150 ymin=0 ymax=0.5 nomi="t,I" area="0.28;0;0;150" punti="150,0.28,pieno" titolo="Time in seconds and current in amperes. The shaded area measures charge."}

$$
\Delta q=I\Delta t=0.28\cdot150\,\mathrm{C}=42\,\mathrm{C}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A net $0.28\,\mathrm{C}$ passes through the section every second. Since the current is constant, multiply this amount by $150$. On the graph, charge is the rectangular area between the line for $I$ and the time axis. Amperes times seconds gives coulombs.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A constant current of $0.35\,\mathrm{A}$ flows in the wire for $150\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the charge and corresponding net number of electrons with $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

$$
\Delta q=0.35\cdot150\,\mathrm{C}=52.5\,\mathrm{C}.
$$

$$
N=\frac{52.5}{1.60\cdot10^{-19}}\simeq3.28\cdot10^{20}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation gives net charge through a cross section, in coulombs. The second calculation divides that magnitude by the elementary charge, giving a dimensionless count. Negatively charged electrons drift opposite to the conventional current. Use the positive magnitude $e$ to find their number.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Current through a wire is $+0.60\,\mathrm{A}$ for $20\,\mathrm{s}$ and then $-0.20\,\mathrm{A}$ for $30\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the net charge and the average current.

$$
\Delta q=(0.60\cdot20-0.20\cdot30)\,\mathrm{C}=+6\,\mathrm{C}.
$$

$$
I_{\mathrm{avg}}=\frac{6}{50}\,\mathrm{A}=+0.12\,\mathrm{A}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first interval transfers $12\,\mathrm{C}$ in the positive direction. The second transfers $6\,\mathrm{C}$ in the opposite direction, leaving $+6\,\mathrm{C}$ net. The average uses the full $50\,\mathrm{s}$. The intervals have different durations, so the simple arithmetic mean of the two currents would give a different value.

</div>
