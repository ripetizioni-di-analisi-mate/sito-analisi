## Exercise 1

A charge of magnitude $0.384\,\mathrm{C}$ passes through a wire cross section. Find the net electron count with $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

::grafico{f="0.16*x" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=0.7 nomi="N,q" punti="2.4,0.384,pieno" titolo="The count N is in units of 10¹⁸ and the charge q is in coulombs."}

$$
N=\frac{|\Delta q|}{e}=\frac{0.384}{1.60\cdot10^{-19}}=2.40\cdot10^{18}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each electron contributes the same elementary amount of charge. Divide the charge transfer magnitude by $e$. Coulombs cancel between numerator and denominator. The result is the net count corresponding to the charge transfer.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Charge of magnitude $0.48\,\mathrm{C}$ passes through a wire at a constant current of $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$. Find the net electron count and the time, using $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

$$
N=\frac{0.48}{1.60\cdot10^{-19}}=3.00\cdot10^{18}.
$$

$$
\Delta t=\frac{0.48}{0.20}\,\mathrm{s}=2.4\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find how many electrons correspond to the net transfer, divide by the charge carried per electron. To find how long it takes, divide the same charge by the amount transferred in one second. The denominators have different units. As a check, $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$ for $2.4\,\mathrm{s}$ gives $0.48\,\mathrm{C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wire of cross section $2.0\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ contains $8.0\cdot10^{28}$ conduction electrons per cubic meter. Find the drift speed with current $2.56\,\mathrm{A}$ and $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$.

$$
A=2.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$

$$
v_d=\frac{I}{neA}=\frac{2.56}{8.0\cdot10^{28}\cdot1.60\cdot10^{-19}\cdot2.0\cdot10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=1.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First express the area in square meters because carrier density is given per cubic meter. Divide current by the charge contained in one meter of wire, $neA$. The result describes average electron motion. Doubling the area at fixed current and density would mean the electrons need only half the drift speed.

</div>
