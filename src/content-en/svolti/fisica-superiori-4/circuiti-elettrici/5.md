## Exercise 1

A current of $5\,\mathrm{mA}$ enters a node and a current of $9\,\mathrm{mA}$ leaves it. For the third branch the outgoing direction is chosen as positive. Find the signed current and its actual direction.

::schema{riferimento=0 punti="0,0,A,pieno" linee="-3,0 3,0;0,0 0,-2" frecce="-2.8,0.65,-0.9,0.65,5 mA;0.85,0.65,2.7,0.65,9 mA;0.55,-0.2,0.55,-1.8,I_3" titolo="The arrows show the directions used in the balance at A"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum I_{\mathrm{in}} &= \sum I_{\mathrm{out}}, \\[6pt]
5\,\mathrm{mA} &= 9\,\mathrm{mA}+I_3, \\[6pt]
I_3 &= 5\,\mathrm{mA}-9\,\mathrm{mA}=-4\,\mathrm{mA}, \\[6pt]
5\,\mathrm{mA}+4\,\mathrm{mA} &= 9\,\mathrm{mA}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Kirchhoff's current law** expresses conservation of charge, so at a node with no accumulation the sum of the entering currents equals the sum of the leaving ones. We write $I_3$ in the side of the leaving currents, because the arrow chosen for the third branch points away from the node. Solving the equation gives a negative value, so the current in the third branch has the direction opposite to the arrow, that is, it enters the node. Its magnitude is $4\,\mathrm{mA}$ and it adds to the $5\,\mathrm{mA}$ already entering. The last line checks the balance, because the total entering $5\,\mathrm{mA}+4\,\mathrm{mA}$ equals the $9\,\mathrm{mA}$ leaving. In the third branch, therefore, $4\,\mathrm{mA}$ flows toward the node.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A loop contains two opposing ideal sources of $15\,\mathrm V$ and $9\,\mathrm V$, in series with resistances of $2\,\Omega$ and $4\,\Omega$. The positive direction crosses the $9\,\mathrm V$ source from the negative terminal to the positive one. Calculate the signed current and the power absorbed by the smaller source.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_2-\mathcal E_1-(R_1+R_2)I &= 0, \\[6pt]
9\,\mathrm V-15\,\mathrm V-(6\,\Omega)I &= 0, \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{-6\,\mathrm V}{6\,\Omega}=-1\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{abs},9} &= -\mathcal E_2 I=-(9\,\mathrm V)(-1\,\mathrm A)=9\,\mathrm W, \\[6pt]
P_R &= (R_1+R_2)I^2=(6\,\Omega)(1\,\mathrm A)^2=6\,\mathrm W, \\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{sup},15} &= \mathcal E_1\,|I|=(15\,\mathrm V)(1\,\mathrm A)=15\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the reference direction the $9\,\mathrm V$ source produces a rise in potential and the $15\,\mathrm V$ source a drop. By **Kirchhoff's voltage law** the algebraic sum of the potential changes around the loop is zero, and the drop across the resistances, traversed in the positive direction, is $-(R_1+R_2)I$. The value $I=-1\,\mathrm A$ is negative, so the current has the direction opposite to the reference one and the $15\,\mathrm V$ source prevails. In this direction the current enters the positive terminal of the smaller source, which therefore absorbs power $\mathcal E_2|I|=9\,\mathrm W$, while the larger source supplies it. The resistances dissipate $6\,\mathrm W$. The power balance is satisfied, because the $15\,\mathrm W$ supplied is the sum of the $9\,\mathrm W$ absorbed by the smaller source and the $6\,\mathrm W$ dissipated. The current is $1\,\mathrm A$ in the direction opposite to the one chosen.

</div>
