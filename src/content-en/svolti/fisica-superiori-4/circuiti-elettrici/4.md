## Exercise 1

An unloaded voltage divider is supplied with $15\,\mathrm V$ and uses $R_1=2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $R_2=3\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ in series. Find the voltage across $R_2$ and the supply current.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_1+R_2 &= 5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}, \\[6pt]
I &=\frac{15\,\mathrm V}{5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}=3\,\mathrm{mA}, \\[6pt]
V_2 &=(3\,\mathrm{mA})(3\,\mathrm{k\Omega})=9\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divider is **unloaded**, so no current is drawn from the node between $R_1$ and $R_2$. The two resistors share the same current, and $R_2$ represents three-fifths of the total resistance. The requested voltage is therefore three-fifths of $15\,\mathrm V$. In the Ohm's law product, the milliamps factor $10^{-3}$ cancels the kilohms factor $10^3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor $R_1$ and a $4\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor $R_2$ are connected in series across an ideal $12\,\mathrm V$ battery. The output is across $R_2$. When a $4\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ load is added at the output, find the new voltage and load current.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{p}} &= \frac{4\cdot4}{4+4}\,\mathrm{k\Omega}
               =2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}, \\[6pt]
V_2 &= (12\,\mathrm V)\frac{2}{2+2}=6\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
I_{\mathrm{L}} &= \frac{6\,\mathrm V}{4\,\mathrm{k\Omega}}
               =1.5\,\mathrm{mA}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load and $R_2$ share the two output nodes, so they form a **parallel combination**. Since they are equal, the equivalent resistance is half the value of either. The $2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ equivalent and $R_1$ split the $12\,\mathrm V$ equally. The $6\,\mathrm V$ across the equivalent is also across the load, so divide by $4\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ to find its current. The unloaded output was $8\,\mathrm V$ because $R_2$ received two-thirds of the voltage.

</div>
