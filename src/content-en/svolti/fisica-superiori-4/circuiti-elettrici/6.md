## Exercise 1

A source with electromotive force $12\,\mathrm V$ and internal resistance $1\,\Omega$ powers a $3\,\Omega$ load. Find the current, terminal voltage, and power dissipated internally.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{(3+1)\,\Omega}=3\,\mathrm A,\\[6pt]
V &= 12\,\mathrm V-(1\,\Omega)(3\,\mathrm A)=9\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
P_r &=(3\,\mathrm A)^2(1\,\Omega)=9\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The internal resistance and the load are in series in the model, so the electromotive force is shared across their combined $4\,\Omega$. Of the ideal source's $12\,\mathrm V$, $3\,\mathrm V$ drops across the internal resistance. The remaining $9\,\mathrm V$ is measured at the **terminals** and also equals $IR$ across the load. Each second, the source supplies $36\,\mathrm J$. Of this, $27\,\mathrm J$ is dissipated in the load and $9\,\mathrm J$ inside the source.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A battery supplies $1\,\mathrm A$ at a measured terminal voltage of $14\,\mathrm V$, and $3\,\mathrm A$ at $10\,\mathrm V$. Assuming constant $\mathcal E$ and $r$, find both parameters and the current through a new $6\,\Omega$ load.

::grafico{f="16-2*x" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=18 nomi="I,V" punti="1,14,pieno;3,10,pieno" titolo="Voltage in volts and current in amperes. The voltage falls in proportion to the increase in current."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \frac{14\,\mathrm V-10\,\mathrm V}{3\,\mathrm A-1\,\mathrm A}
   =2\,\Omega,\\[6pt]
\mathcal E &= 14\,\mathrm V+(2\,\Omega)(1\,\mathrm A)
           =16\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
I &= \frac{16\,\mathrm V}{(6+2)\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the relation $V=\mathcal E-rI$, subtracting the two values of $V$ eliminates $\mathcal E$. The extra $4\,\mathrm V$ drop for $2\,\mathrm A$ more current therefore identifies $r$. Then extrapolate from one measurement to zero current to find $\mathcal E$. The new load is in series with $r$, so its current is determined by the combined $8\,\Omega$. At $2\,\mathrm A$, the model gives $12\,\mathrm V$ at the terminals, halfway between the two measurements.

</div>
