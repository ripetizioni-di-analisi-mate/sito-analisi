## Exercise 1

A source of electromotive force $12\,\mathrm V$ and internal resistance $1\,\Omega$ powers a $3\,\Omega$ load. Calculate the current, the terminal voltage, and the power dissipated internally.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{\mathcal E}{R+r}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{(3+1)\,\Omega}=3\,\mathrm A,\\[6pt]
V_{\mathrm{term}} &= \mathcal E-rI=12\,\mathrm V-(1\,\Omega)(3\,\mathrm A)=9\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
V_{\mathrm{term}} &= IR=(3\,\mathrm A)(3\,\Omega)=9\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
P_r &= I^2r=(3\,\mathrm A)^2(1\,\Omega)=9\,\mathrm W,\\[6pt]
P_R &= I^2R=(3\,\mathrm A)^2(3\,\Omega)=27\,\mathrm W,\\[6pt]
\mathcal E I &= (12\,\mathrm V)(3\,\mathrm A)=36\,\mathrm W=P_R+P_r.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The internal resistance and the load are in series in the model of the real source, so the same current flows in the same circuit and the electromotive force is distributed over their total of $4\,\Omega$. Of the $12\,\mathrm V$ of the ideal part, $3\,\mathrm V$ drops across the internal resistance. At the **terminals** we measure the remaining $9\,\mathrm V$, also equal to the product $IR$ across the load, and the two expressions coincide. The power dissipated in the internal resistance is $I^2r$. The power supplied by the ideal part, $\mathcal E I=36\,\mathrm W$, is divided between the load, which absorbs $27\,\mathrm W$, and the internal resistance. In the source $9\,\mathrm W$ is therefore dissipated, one quarter of the power supplied.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A battery supplies $1\,\mathrm A$ when $14\,\mathrm V$ is measured at the terminals, and supplies $3\,\mathrm A$ when $10\,\mathrm V$ is measured. Assuming $\mathcal E$ and $r$ constant, find both parameters and the current in a new $6\,\Omega$ load.

::grafico{f="16-2*x" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=18 nomi="I,V" punti="1,14,pieno;3,10,pieno" titolo="Voltage in volts and current in amperes. The voltage decreases in proportion to the increase in current."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_1 &= \mathcal E-rI_1, \qquad V_2=\mathcal E-rI_2, \\[6pt]
r &= \frac{V_1-V_2}{I_2-I_1}=\frac{14\,\mathrm V-10\,\mathrm V}{3\,\mathrm A-1\,\mathrm A}
   =2\,\Omega,\\[6pt]
\mathcal E &= V_1+rI_1=14\,\mathrm V+(2\,\Omega)(1\,\mathrm A)
           =16\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
I &= \frac{\mathcal E}{R+r}=\frac{16\,\mathrm V}{(6+2)\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A,\\[6pt]
V &= IR=(2\,\mathrm A)(6\,\Omega)=12\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each measurement the relation $V=\mathcal E-rI$ of the real source holds. Subtracting the two equations side by side eliminates $\mathcal E$, and $V_1-V_2=r(I_2-I_1)$ remains. The additional drop of $4\,\mathrm V$ for $2\,\mathrm A$ more therefore identifies $r$. Then $\mathcal E$ is found from one of the measurements, going back to the voltage at zero current, which on the graph is the intercept with the voltage axis. The new load is in series with $r$, so its current is fixed by the total of $8\,\Omega$. At the terminals the model gives $12\,\mathrm V$, a value between the two measured voltages, as expected for a current of $2\,\mathrm A$ lying between $1\,\mathrm A$ and $3\,\mathrm A$. The source has $\mathcal E=16\,\mathrm V$ and $r=2\,\Omega$, and $2\,\mathrm A$ flows in the new load.

</div>
