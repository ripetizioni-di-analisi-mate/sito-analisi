## Exercise 1

An initially uncharged $200\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor is connected in series with $5\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and a $12\,\mathrm V$ source. Find the voltage, charge, and current $1\,\mathrm s$ after closing.

::schema{riferimento=0 linee="0,1.48 0,3 2.2,3;4.2,3 7,3 7,1.64;7,1.34 7,0 0,0 0,1.16;-0.44,1.48 0.44,1.48;-0.27,1.16 0.27,1.16;2.2,2.72 4.2,2.72 4.2,3.28 2.2,3.28 2.2,2.72;6.55,1.64 7.45,1.64;6.55,1.34 7.45,1.34" frecce="3.8,3.9,5.6,3.9,I" etichette="1.0,1.32,12 V;3.2,2.2,5 kΩ;5.65,1.48,200 µF" titolo="Circuit in the charging position"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &= RC=(5000\,\Omega)(2\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm F)=1\,\mathrm s,\\[6pt]
e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} &= e^{-1}\simeq0.368,\\[6pt]
V_C &= \mathcal E\left(1-e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}\right)\simeq(12\,\mathrm V)(0.632)\simeq7.59\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
q &= CV_C=(2\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm F)(7.59\,\mathrm V)\simeq1.52\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm C,\\[6pt]
I &= \frac{\mathcal E}{R}e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}=(2.4\,\mathrm{mA})(0.368)\simeq0.883\,\mathrm{mA},\\[6pt]
RI+V_C &\simeq4.41\,\mathrm V+7.59\,\mathrm V=12\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To recognize the point in the transient we express $R$ and $C$ in ohms and farads. The product is $1\,\mathrm s$, so exactly one **time constant** has elapsed. The capacitor was uncharged, so we apply the charging law with $V_C(0)=0$, and after $\tau$ the voltage has reached $63.2\%$ of the final value $\mathcal E$. The charge follows from the relation $q=CV_C$ between charge and voltage of a capacitor. The initial current is $\dfrac{\mathcal E}{R}=2.4\,\mathrm{mA}$, because at $t=0$ the full source voltage drops across the resistor, and it falls to $36.8\%$ after one time constant. As a check, the loop law requires $RI+V_C$ to equal $\mathcal E$, and the sum gives $12\,\mathrm V$. After $1\,\mathrm s$ the capacitor has voltage $7.59\,\mathrm V$ and charge $1.52\,\mathrm{mC}$, and $0.883\,\mathrm{mA}$ flows in the circuit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an ideal charging circuit, with an initially uncharged capacitor and a $20\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ resistor, the voltage reaches half of its final value after $(2\ln 2)\,\mathrm s$. Determine the capacitance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{V_C}{\mathcal E}=1-e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}&=\frac{1}{2},\\[6pt]
e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}&=\frac{1}{2},\\[6pt]
\frac{t}{\tau}&=\ln 2,\\[6pt]
\tau&=\frac{t}{\ln 2}=\frac{(2\ln 2)\,\mathrm s}{\ln 2}=2\,\mathrm s,\\[6pt]
C&=\frac{\tau}{R}=\frac{2\,\mathrm s}{20\,000\,\Omega}
 =10^{-4}\,\mathrm F=100\,\mathrm{\mu F}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The known voltage is expressed as a fraction of the final one, so $\mathcal E$ cancels in the charging law and need not be known. At half charge the exponential term equals one half. The natural logarithm turns the equation $e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}=\dfrac{1}{2}$ into the equality between $\dfrac{t}{\tau}$ and $\ln 2$, since $\ln\dfrac{1}{2}=-\ln 2$. We thus find $\tau=2\,\mathrm s$ from the measured time. By the definition $\tau=RC$ the capacitance is the ratio of $\tau$ to $R$, and the ratio of seconds to ohms gives farads. The given time coincides with $\tau\ln 2$, that is, with the time in which the distance from the final value is halved. The capacitance is $100\,\mathrm{\mu F}$.

</div>
