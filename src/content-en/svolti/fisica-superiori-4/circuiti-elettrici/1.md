## Exercise 1

An ideal $18\,\mathrm V$ source powers two resistors of $3\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$ in series. Calculate the current and the voltage across each resistor.

::schema{riferimento=0 linee="0,1.48 0,3 1.4,3;2.8,3 4.2,3;5.6,3 7,3 7,0 0,0 0,1.16;-0.44,1.48 0.44,1.48;-0.27,1.16 0.27,1.16;1.4,2.72 2.8,2.72 2.8,3.28 1.4,3.28 1.4,2.72;4.2,2.72 5.6,2.72 5.6,3.28 4.2,3.28 4.2,2.72" frecce="0.8,3.85,2.2,3.85,I" etichette="0.9,1.32,18 V;2.1,2.25,3 Ω;4.9,2.25,6 Ω" titolo="A single loop passes through both resistors"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= R_1+R_2=3\,\Omega+6\,\Omega=9\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{\mathcal E}{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\frac{18\,\mathrm V}{9\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_1 &= IR_1=(2\,\mathrm A)(3\,\Omega)=6\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
V_2 &= IR_2=(2\,\mathrm A)(6\,\Omega)=12\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
V_1+V_2 &= 6\,\mathrm V+12\,\mathrm V=18\,\mathrm V=\mathcal E.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the point joining the two resistors there is no branching, so the current leaving the first enters the second entirely and the resistors are in **series**. Since they are in series, the equivalent resistance is the sum $R_1+R_2=9\,\Omega$. **Ohm's law** applied to the whole series, with the voltage $\mathcal E=18\,\mathrm V$ maintained by the ideal source, gives the common current $2\,\mathrm A$. We then apply Ohm's law to each resistor with this same current, because the voltage across a resistor is the product of its resistance and the current through it. The second resistor has twice the resistance of the first, so it receives twice the voltage. The sum of the two drops equals $\mathcal E$, as the loop law requires. A current of $2\,\mathrm A$ flows in the series and the drops across the two resistors are $6\,\mathrm V$ and $12\,\mathrm V$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal $14\,\mathrm V$ battery powers an $8\,\Omega$ resistor and an unknown resistor in series. The current is $0.50\,\mathrm A$. Determine the unknown resistance and its voltage drop.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= \frac{\mathcal E}{I}=\frac{14\,\mathrm V}{0.50\,\mathrm A}=28\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
R_2 &= R_{\mathrm{eq}}-R_1=28\,\Omega-8\,\Omega=20\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
V_1 &= IR_1=(0.50\,\mathrm A)(8\,\Omega)=4\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
V_2 &= IR_2=(0.50\,\mathrm A)(20\,\Omega)=10\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
V_1+V_2 &= 4\,\mathrm V+10\,\mathrm V=14\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The voltage of $14\,\mathrm V$ and the current of $0.50\,\mathrm A$ concern the whole series, so their ratio, by **Ohm's law**, is the equivalent resistance. Since in a series the equivalent resistance is the sum of the resistances, the value $28\,\Omega$ already includes the $8\,\Omega$ of the first resistor, and subtracting it gives $R_2$. The same current passes through both resistors, because there is no branching between them, so the drop across each is calculated with $V=IR$. The sum of the two drops is $14\,\mathrm V$, equal to the voltage of the battery, as the loop law requires. The unknown resistance is $20\,\Omega$ and $10\,\mathrm V$ drops across it.

</div>
