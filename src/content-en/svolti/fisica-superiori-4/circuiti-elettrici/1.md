## Exercise 1

An ideal $18\,\mathrm V$ source powers $3\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$ resistors in series. Find the current and the voltage across each resistor.

::schema{riferimento=0 linee="0,1.48 0,3 1.4,3;2.8,3 4.2,3;5.6,3 7,3 7,0 0,0 0,1.16;-0.44,1.48 0.44,1.48;-0.27,1.16 0.27,1.16;1.4,2.72 2.8,2.72 2.8,3.28 1.4,3.28 1.4,2.72;4.2,2.72 5.6,2.72 5.6,3.28 4.2,3.28 4.2,2.72" frecce="0.8,3.85,2.2,3.85,I" etichette="0.9,1.32,18 V;2.1,2.25,3 Ω;4.9,2.25,6 Ω" titolo="One loop passes through both resistors"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= 3\,\Omega+6\,\Omega=9\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{18\,\mathrm V}{9\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_1 &= (2\,\mathrm A)(3\,\Omega)=6\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
V_2 &= (2\,\mathrm A)(6\,\Omega)=12\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There is no branch at the junction between the two resistors, so the current leaving the first also passes through the second. For **Ohm's law** applied to the full series combination, divide the source voltage by $9\,\Omega$, the sum of the resistances. Then use the $2\,\mathrm A$ current to find each drop. The second resistor has twice the resistance and therefore twice the voltage. The sum $6\,\mathrm V+12\,\mathrm V$ gives the source voltage.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal $14\,\mathrm V$ battery powers an $8\,\Omega$ resistor in series with an unknown resistor. The current is $0.50\,\mathrm A$. Find the unknown resistance and its voltage drop.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= \frac{14\,\mathrm V}{0.50\,\mathrm A}=28\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
R_2 &= 28\,\Omega-8\,\Omega=20\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
V_2 &= (0.50\,\mathrm A)(20\,\Omega)=10\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The known voltage and current apply to the entire series combination, so their ratio gives the **equivalent resistance**. The $28\,\Omega$ already includes the first resistor's $8\,\Omega$. Subtract that part to find $R_2$. The same current also passes through $R_1$, which has a $4\,\mathrm V$ drop. The two drops add to $14\,\mathrm V$, as required by Kirchhoff's voltage law.

</div>
