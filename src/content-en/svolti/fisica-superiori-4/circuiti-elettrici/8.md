## Exercise 1

A capacitor at $12\,\mathrm V$ discharges through a resistor. The time constant is $3\,\mathrm s$. Find the voltage and the fraction of the initial energy still stored at $t=(3\ln 3)\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{t}{\tau} &= \ln 3,\\[6pt]
V_C &=(12\,\mathrm V)e^{-\ln 3}=4\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
\frac{U}{U_0} &=\left(\frac{4\,\mathrm V}{12\,\mathrm V}\right)^2
              =\frac{1}{9}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During discharge the source is disconnected, so the voltage decays exponentially from its initial $12\,\mathrm V$. Since $\dfrac{t}{\tau}=\ln 3$, the voltage factor is one-third. For energy use $U=\dfrac{1}{2}CV_C^2$. The capacitance is the same at both times, so the requested fraction is one-third squared. The resistor has already dissipated eight-ninths of the initial energy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $250\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor initially at $16\,\mathrm V$ discharges through $8\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. How long does it take for its stored energy to fall to one-sixteenth of its initial energy?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &=(8000\,\Omega)(2.50\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm F)=2\,\mathrm s,\\[6pt]
\frac{V_C}{V_0} &=\sqrt{\frac{1}{16}}=\frac{1}{4},\\[6pt]
\frac{t}{\tau} &= \ln 4,\\[6pt]
t &=(2\,\mathrm s)\ln 4\simeq2.77\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The energy ratio is the square of the voltage ratio. During discharge $V_C$ remains positive, so take the positive square root and find when the voltage is $16\,\mathrm V\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}=4\,\mathrm V$. The exponential law gives $\dfrac{t}{\tau}=\ln 4$. The same result represents two consecutive halvings of the voltage, each taking $\tau\ln 2$.

</div>
