## Exercise 1

A capacitor at $12\,\mathrm V$ discharges through a resistor. The time constant is $3\,\mathrm s$. Find the voltage and the fraction of the initial energy still stored at $t=(3\ln 3)\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{t}{\tau} &= \frac{(3\ln 3)\,\mathrm s}{3\,\mathrm s}=\ln 3,\\[6pt]
V_C &= V_0e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}=(12\,\mathrm V)e^{-\ln 3}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{3}=4\,\mathrm V,\\[6pt]
\frac{U}{U_0} &=\frac{\frac{1}{2}CV_C^2}{\frac{1}{2}CV_0^2}=\left(\frac{V_C}{V_0}\right)^2=\left(\frac{4\,\mathrm V}{12\,\mathrm V}\right)^2
              =\frac{1}{9},\\[6pt]
1-\frac{U}{U_0} &= 1-\frac{1}{9}=\frac{8}{9}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During discharge the source is disconnected, so the voltage follows the decreasing exponential starting from the initial $12\,\mathrm V$. Since $\dfrac{t}{\tau}=\ln 3$ and $e^{-\ln 3}=\dfrac{1}{3}$, the voltage is reduced to one third. To go to the energy we use the expression $U=\dfrac{1}{2}CV_C^2$ for the energy of a capacitor. The capacitance is the same at the two instants and cancels in the ratio, so the fraction of remaining energy is the square of the ratio of the voltages. The part of the initial energy that is no longer stored has been dissipated by the resistor, because the source is absent and no other element absorbs power. At that instant the voltage is $4\,\mathrm V$ and one ninth of the initial energy remains, while the resistor has dissipated eight ninths.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $250\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor initially at $16\,\mathrm V$ discharges through $8\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. After what time is the stored energy one sixteenth of the initial one?

::grafico{f="exp(-x);exp(-2*x)" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=0 ymax=1.2 nomi="t/τ," legenda="\dfrac{V_C}{V_0};\dfrac{U}{U_0}" punti="1.386294,0.25,pieno;1.386294,0.0625,pieno" titolo="Voltage and energy relative to the initial values. At $\dfrac{t}{\tau}=\ln 4$ the voltage is one quarter and the energy one sixteenth"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &=RC=(8000\,\Omega)(2.50\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm F)=2\,\mathrm s,\\[6pt]
U_0 &=\frac{1}{2}CV_0^2=\frac{1}{2}(2.50\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm F)(16\,\mathrm V)^2=32.0\,\mathrm{mJ},\\[6pt]
\frac{V_C}{V_0} &=\sqrt{\frac{U}{U_0}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{16}}=\frac{1}{4},\\[6pt]
e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} &=\frac{1}{4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{t}{\tau}=\ln 4,\\[6pt]
t &=\tau\ln 4=(2\,\mathrm s)\ln 4\simeq2.77\,\mathrm s,\\[6pt]
U &=\frac{U_0}{16}=2.00\,\mathrm{mJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The time constant follows from $\tau=RC$ with the data of the circuit. The ratio of the energies is the square of the ratio of the voltages, because $U=\dfrac{1}{2}CV_C^2$ with $C$ constant. During discharge $V_C$ stays positive, so we take the positive root and look for the instant when the voltage is $16\,\mathrm V\cdot\dfrac{1}{4}=4\,\mathrm V$. The exponential law $V_C=V_0e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$ then gives $\dfrac{t}{\tau}=\ln 4$. The same result can be read as two consecutive halvings of the voltage, each of which takes $\tau\ln 2$, because $\ln 4=2\ln 2$. In the graph the two marked points have the same abscissa $\ln 4$, and the ordinates $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $\dfrac{1}{16}$ coincide with those found. The energy falls from $32.0\,\mathrm{mJ}$ to $2.00\,\mathrm{mJ}$ after $2.77\,\mathrm s$.

</div>
