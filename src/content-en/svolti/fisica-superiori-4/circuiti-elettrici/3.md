## Exercise 1

A $3\,\Omega$ resistor is in series with the parallel combination of $12\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$. The ideal source supplies $21\,\mathrm V$. Find the total current and the currents of the two branches.

::schema{riferimento=0 linee="0,1.48 0,3 1.4,3;2.6,3 7,3 7,2.13;7,0.87 7,0 0,0 0,1.16;4.2,3 4.2,2.13;4.2,0.87 4.2,0;-0.44,1.48 0.44,1.48;-0.27,1.16 0.27,1.16;1.4,2.72 2.6,2.72 2.6,3.28 1.4,3.28 1.4,2.72;3.93,0.87 4.47,0.87 4.47,2.13 3.93,2.13 3.93,0.87;6.73,0.87 7.27,0.87 7.27,2.13 6.73,2.13 6.73,0.87" punti="4.2,3,A,pieno;4.2,0,B,pieno" etichette="0.98,1.32,21 V;2,3.76,3 Ω;3.05,1.51,12 Ω;6.12,1.51,6 Ω" titolo="The current that divides at A flows through the resistor at the top"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{AB} &= \frac{(12\,\Omega)(6\,\Omega)}{12\,\Omega+6\,\Omega}=4\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= 3\,\Omega+R_{AB}=7\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{\mathcal E}{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\frac{21\,\mathrm V}{7\,\Omega}=3\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_{AB} &= IR_{AB}=(3\,\mathrm A)(4\,\Omega)=12\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
I_1 &= \frac{V_{AB}}{12\,\Omega}=1\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_2 &= \frac{V_{AB}}{6\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_1+I_2 &= 1\,\mathrm A+2\,\mathrm A=3\,\mathrm A=I.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $12\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$ resistors have the same two nodes $A$ and $B$ across them, so they are in parallel and can be replaced with their **equivalent** of $4\,\Omega$. The $3\,\Omega$ resistor carries all the current before it divides at $A$, so it is in series with the equivalent and the two resistances add. Ohm's law applied to the whole circuit gives the total current of $3\,\mathrm A$. Of the $21\,\mathrm V$ available, $9\,\mathrm V$ drops across the series resistor and $12\,\mathrm V$ between $A$ and $B$. We return to the original diagram and apply Ohm's law in the two branches with those $12\,\mathrm V$. **Kirchhoff's current law** at $A$ is verified, because $1\,\mathrm A+2\,\mathrm A$ equals the $3\,\mathrm A$ arriving from the series resistor.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Between $A$ and $B$ one branch contains $5\,\Omega$ and $10\,\Omega$ in series and the other contains $6\,\Omega$. The group is in series with a $4\,\Omega$ resistor and an ideal $29\,\mathrm V$ source. Calculate the voltage drop across the $10\,\Omega$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{branch}} &= 5\,\Omega+10\,\Omega=15\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
R_{AB} &= \frac{(15\,\Omega)(6\,\Omega)}{15\,\Omega+6\,\Omega}
        = \frac{30}{7}\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= \left(4+\frac{30}{7}\right)\,\Omega
                 =\frac{58}{7}\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{\mathcal E}{R_{\mathrm{eq}}}
   =\frac{29\,\mathrm V}{\frac{58}{7}\,\Omega}
   =3.5\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_4 &= (3.5\,\mathrm A)(4\,\Omega)=14\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
V_{AB} &=29\,\mathrm V-14\,\mathrm V=15\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
I_{\mathrm{branch}} &= \frac{V_{AB}}{R_{\mathrm{branch}}}=\frac{15\,\mathrm V}{15\,\Omega}=1\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_{10} &=(1\,\mathrm A)(10\,\Omega)=10\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
I_6 &= \frac{15\,\mathrm V}{6\,\Omega}=2.5\,\mathrm A,
\quad I_{\mathrm{branch}}+I_6=3.5\,\mathrm A=I.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We start from the branch containing two resistors in series, because at their point of contact there is no tap. The branch is therefore $15\,\Omega$ and is in parallel with the $6\,\Omega$ branch, because both connect $A$ to $B$. The equivalent $\dfrac{30}{7}\,\Omega$ is in series with the $4\,\Omega$ resistor, so they add. Ohm's law gives the total current. All this current passes through the $4\,\Omega$ resistor and $14\,\mathrm V$ drops across it, so $15\,\mathrm V$ remains between $A$ and $B$. This voltage is applied to the branch **as a whole**, so $1\,\mathrm A$ passes through it, and the same current flows in the two series resistors. $10\,\mathrm V$ drops across the $10\,\Omega$ resistor and $5\,\mathrm V$ across the $5\,\Omega$ resistor. The $6\,\Omega$ branch carries $2.5\,\mathrm A$, and the sum of the currents of the two branches equals the total one, as Kirchhoff's current law requires.

</div>
