## Exercise 1

A $3\,\Omega$ resistor is in series with a parallel combination of $12\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$. The ideal source supplies $21\,\mathrm V$. Find the total current and both branch currents.

::schema{riferimento=0 linee="0,1.48 0,3 1.4,3;2.6,3 7,3 7,2.13;7,0.87 7,0 0,0 0,1.16;4.2,3 4.2,2.13;4.2,0.87 4.2,0;-0.44,1.48 0.44,1.48;-0.27,1.16 0.27,1.16;1.4,2.72 2.6,2.72 2.6,3.28 1.4,3.28 1.4,2.72;3.93,0.87 4.47,0.87 4.47,2.13 3.93,2.13 3.93,0.87;6.73,0.87 7.27,0.87 7.27,2.13 6.73,2.13 6.73,0.87" punti="4.2,3,A,pieno;4.2,0,B,pieno" etichette="0.98,1.32,21 V;2,3.76,3 Ω;3.05,1.51,12 Ω;6.12,1.51,6 Ω" titolo="The current through the top resistor splits at A"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{AB} &= \frac{12\cdot6}{12+6}\,\Omega=4\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{21\,\mathrm V}{(3+4)\,\Omega}=3\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_A-V_B &= (3\,\mathrm A)(4\,\Omega)=12\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
I_1 &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{12\,\Omega}=1\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_2 &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{6\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $12\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$ resistors both connect between $A$ and $B$, so they can be replaced by their $4\,\Omega$ **equivalent**. The $3\,\Omega$ resistor carries all the current before it splits at $A$, so its resistance adds to the equivalent. Of the available $21\,\mathrm V$, $9\,\mathrm V$ drops across the series resistor and $12\,\mathrm V$ across $A$ and $B$. Return to the original diagram and apply Ohm's law in both branches using that $12\,\mathrm V$. The currents $1\,\mathrm A$ and $2\,\mathrm A$ add to $3\,\mathrm A$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Between $A$ and $B$, one branch contains $5\,\Omega$ and $10\,\Omega$ in series and the other contains $6\,\Omega$. The combination is in series with a $4\,\Omega$ resistor and an ideal $29\,\mathrm V$ source. Find the drop across the $10\,\Omega$ resistor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{AB} &= \frac{(5+10)\cdot6}{5+10+6}\,\Omega
        = \frac{30}{7}\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= \left(4+\frac{30}{7}\right)\,\Omega
                 =\frac{58}{7}\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{29\,\mathrm V}{\frac{58}{7}\,\Omega}
   =3.5\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_A-V_B &=29\,\mathrm V-(3.5\,\mathrm A)(4\,\Omega)
         =15\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
I_{\mathrm{ramo}} &= \frac{15\,\mathrm V}{(5+10)\,\Omega}=1\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
V_{10} &=(1\,\mathrm A)(10\,\Omega)=10\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Start with the branch containing two series resistors, because there is no junction to another branch where they meet. That branch has resistance $15\,\Omega$. The parallel combination of $15\,\Omega$ and $6\,\Omega$ is the equivalent between $A$ and $B$. Once the total current is known, subtract the $14\,\mathrm V$ drop across the $4\,\Omega$ resistor from $29\,\mathrm V$. The remaining $15\,\mathrm V$ is applied to the **entire branch**, so it carries $1\,\mathrm A$. The requested resistor drops $10\,\mathrm V$, and the other $5\,\Omega$ resistor drops $5\,\mathrm V$.

</div>
