## Exercise 1

An $8\,\Omega$ resistor and a $24\,\Omega$ resistor are connected in parallel across an ideal $12\,\mathrm V$ source. Find the branch currents and equivalent resistance.

::schema{riferimento=0 linee="0,1.48 0,3 6,3 6,2.12;6,0.88 6,0 0,0 0,1.16;3,3 3,2.12;3,0.88 3,0;-0.44,1.48 0.44,1.48;-0.27,1.16 0.27,1.16;2.73,0.88 3.27,0.88 3.27,2.12 2.73,2.12 2.73,0.88;5.73,0.88 6.27,0.88 6.27,2.12 5.73,2.12 5.73,0.88" punti="3,3,A,pieno;3,0,B,pieno" frecce="3.92,2.16,3.92,0.83,I_1;6.95,2.16,6.95,0.83,I_2" etichette="0.9,1.32,12 V;2.13,1.51,8 Ω;5.06,1.51,24 Ω" titolo="Both resistors have the same voltage across them"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{8\,\Omega}=1.5\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_2 &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{24\,\Omega}=0.5\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_{\mathrm{tot}} &= 1.5\,\mathrm A+0.5\,\mathrm A=2\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{2\,\mathrm A}=6\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both branches start at $A$ and end at $B$. In each branch, divide the **same voltage** by that branch's resistance. Since the first resistor has one-third the resistance of the second, it carries three times the current. The source supplies both branches, so its total is $2\,\mathrm A$. A $6\,\Omega$ resistor would draw the same $2\,\mathrm A$ at the same voltage. This is below the $8\,\Omega$ of the less resistive branch.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal $12\,\mathrm V$ source supplies $3\,\mathrm A$ to two resistors in parallel. The first resistance is $6\,\Omega$. Find the second resistance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{6\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_2 &= 3\,\mathrm A-2\,\mathrm A=1\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
R_2 &= \frac{12\,\mathrm V}{1\,\mathrm A}=12\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Kirchhoff's current law** gives the missing current from the known branch. The first resistor has $12\,\mathrm V$ across it, so it carries $2\,\mathrm A$. Of the battery's $3\,\mathrm A$, this leaves $1\,\mathrm A$ for the other branch. Since that branch also has $12\,\mathrm V$ across it, the unknown resistance is $12\,\Omega$. As a check, the equivalent resistance from the battery data is $4\,\Omega$, which agrees with $\dfrac{6\cdot12}{6+12}\,\Omega$.

</div>
