## Exercise 1

Two resistors of $8\,\Omega$ and $24\,\Omega$ are connected in parallel across the terminals of an ideal $12\,\mathrm V$ source. Calculate the branch currents and the equivalent resistance.

::schema{riferimento=0 linee="0,1.48 0,3 6,3 6,2.12;6,0.88 6,0 0,0 0,1.16;3,3 3,2.12;3,0.88 3,0;-0.44,1.48 0.44,1.48;-0.27,1.16 0.27,1.16;2.73,0.88 3.27,0.88 3.27,2.12 2.73,2.12 2.73,0.88;5.73,0.88 6.27,0.88 6.27,2.12 5.73,2.12 5.73,0.88" punti="3,3,A,pieno;3,0,B,pieno" frecce="3.92,2.16,3.92,0.83,I_1;6.95,2.16,6.95,0.83,I_2" etichette="0.9,1.32,12 V;2.13,1.51,8 Ω;5.06,1.51,24 Ω" titolo="The two resistors have the same voltage across them"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= \frac{\Delta V}{R_1}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{8\,\Omega}=1.5\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_2 &= \frac{\Delta V}{R_2}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{24\,\Omega}=0.5\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_{\mathrm{tot}} &= I_1+I_2=1.5\,\mathrm A+0.5\,\mathrm A=2\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= \frac{\Delta V}{I_{\mathrm{tot}}}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{2\,\mathrm A}=6\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
\frac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2} &= \frac{(8\,\Omega)(24\,\Omega)}{32\,\Omega}=6\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both resistors have one terminal at $A$ and the other at $B$, so they are in **parallel** and the same voltage $\Delta V=12\,\mathrm V$, maintained by the ideal source, is across them. By Ohm's law the current in each branch is this voltage divided by the resistance of the branch. The first resistor has one third of the resistance of the second, so it carries three times the current. **Kirchhoff's current law** at $A$ ensures that the current supplied by the source is the sum of the currents of the two branches, that is, $2\,\mathrm A$. The equivalent resistance is the ratio of the voltage to this total current and coincides with the value of the formula $\dfrac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}$. Since $6\,\Omega$ is smaller than $8\,\Omega$, the smaller of the two resistances, the result is consistent with the fact that adding a branch increases the total current.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal $12\,\mathrm V$ source supplies $3\,\mathrm A$ to two resistors in parallel. The resistance of the first is $6\,\Omega$. Find the resistance of the second.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= \frac{\Delta V}{R_1}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{6\,\Omega}=2\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
I_2 &= I_{\mathrm{tot}}-I_1=3\,\mathrm A-2\,\mathrm A=1\,\mathrm A, \\[6pt]
R_2 &= \frac{\Delta V}{I_2}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{1\,\mathrm A}=12\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}} &= \frac{\Delta V}{I_{\mathrm{tot}}}=\frac{12\,\mathrm V}{3\,\mathrm A}=4\,\Omega, \\[6pt]
\frac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2} &= \frac{(6\,\Omega)(12\,\Omega)}{18\,\Omega}=4\,\Omega.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two resistors are in parallel across the terminals of the source, so the same voltage $\Delta V=12\,\mathrm V$ drops across each. We first apply Ohm's law to the known branch, which carries $2\,\mathrm A$. **Kirchhoff's current law** ensures that the $3\,\mathrm A$ supplied by the source is the sum of the currents of the two branches, so in the unknown branch $3\,\mathrm A-2\,\mathrm A=1\,\mathrm A$ flows. Since $12\,\mathrm V$ is also applied to that branch, its resistance is the ratio of the voltage to this current. As a check, the equivalent resistance obtained from the data of the source is $4\,\Omega$ and coincides with the one given by the parallel formula. The resistance of the second resistor is $12\,\Omega$, twice the first, and the branch carries half of its current.

</div>
