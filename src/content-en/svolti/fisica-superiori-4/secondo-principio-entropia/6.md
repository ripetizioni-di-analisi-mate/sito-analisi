## Exercise 1

An ideal thermal reservoir at $360\,\mathrm{K}$ gives up $900\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate the entropy change of the reservoir.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{sorg}} &=-900\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\Delta S&=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{sorg}}}{T}
=\frac{-900\,\mathrm{J}}{360\,\mathrm{K}}
=-2.5\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system under consideration is the reservoir that gives up heat. For this reservoir heat has a negative sign. Its temperature is constant by the definition of an ideal reservoir. Its entropy change between the two states is therefore the signed heat divided by $360\,\mathrm{K}$. The result $-2.5\,\mathrm{J/K}$ means that the entropy of the reservoir alone decreases. The total change would also require data for the body that receives the heat.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal reservoir at $450\,\mathrm{K}$ gives up $900\,\mathrm{J}$ to an ideal reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the hot reservoir's entropy change and the total change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S_h&=-\frac{900}{450}\,\mathrm{J/K}=-2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_c&=\frac{900}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}=3\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(-2+3)\,\mathrm{J/K}=1\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The amounts of heat have the same magnitude, since the energy leaving the hot reservoir enters the cold one. Their signs are opposite, however, and each contribution is evaluated at the temperature of its own reservoir. At $450\,\mathrm{K}$ the loss is $2\,\mathrm{J/K}$. At $300\,\mathrm{K}$ the increase reaches $3\,\mathrm{J/K}$ because the denominator is smaller. The positive total is consistent with spontaneous transfer in isolation and shows that thermal contact across a finite temperature difference is **irreversible**.

</div>
