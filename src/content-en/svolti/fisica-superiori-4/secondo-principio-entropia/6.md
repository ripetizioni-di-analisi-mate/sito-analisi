## Exercise 1

An ideal thermal reservoir at $360\,\mathrm{K}$ gives off $900\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate the change in entropy of the reservoir.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{res}} &=-900\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\Delta S&=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{res}}}{T}
=\frac{-900\,\mathrm{J}}{360\,\mathrm{K}}
=-2.5\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system considered is the reservoir that gives off heat. For this reservoir the heat is negative, because heat is positive when absorbed by the system. The temperature is constant by the definition of an ideal reservoir, so the exchange is reversible and the relation $\Delta S=\dfrac{Q}{T}$ holds. The change in entropy between its states is therefore obtained by dividing the signed heat by $360\,\mathrm{K}$. The result $-2.5\,\mathrm{J/K}$ indicates that the entropy of the reservoir alone decreases. The unit is joules per kelvin, because joules are divided by kelvin. The change obtained refers only to the reservoir that gives off heat. To obtain the total change, the data of the body that receives the heat would also be needed. For the same heat exchanged, the magnitude of the change is greater at a lower temperature. For example, at $300\,\mathrm{K}$ the same exchange of $900\,\mathrm{J}$ would give $3\,\mathrm{J/K}$ in magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal reservoir at $450\,\mathrm{K}$ gives $900\,\mathrm{J}$ to an ideal reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the change in entropy of the hot reservoir and the total change.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3,-0.65 -1,-0.65 -1,0.65 -3,0.65 -3,-0.65;1,-0.65 3,-0.65 3,0.65 1,0.65 1,-0.65" vettori="-0.95,0,0.95,0,900 J" etichette="-2.73,0,450 K;1.3,0,300 K" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1.35 ymax=1.45 titolo="The arrow shows which reservoir loses heat and which receives it."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S_h&=-\frac{900}{450}\,\mathrm{J/K}=-2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_c&=\frac{900}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}=3\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\Delta S_h+\Delta S_c=(-2+3)\,\mathrm{J/K}=1\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=Q\,\frac{T_h-T_c}{T_hT_c}=900\cdot\frac{150}{135000}\,\mathrm{J/K}=1\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two heats have the same magnitude, because the energy leaving the hot reservoir enters the cold one. The signs are opposite, however, and each contribution is calculated at the temperature of the reservoir to which it belongs. At $450\,\mathrm{K}$ the loss is $2\,\mathrm{J/K}$. At $300\,\mathrm{K}$ the increase reaches $3\,\mathrm{J/K}$, because the denominator is smaller. The positive sum is consistent with a spontaneous exchange in isolation and shows that thermal contact across a finite temperature difference is **irreversible**. The general formula $Q\,\dfrac{T_h-T_c}{T_hT_c}$ gives the same value, since $450\cdot300=135000$ and $450-300=150$. By the theorem on direct transfer, the sign is positive whenever heat passes from the hotter reservoir to the colder one. The total entropy increases by $1\,\mathrm{J/K}$, while that of the hot reservoir alone decreases by $2\,\mathrm{J/K}$. A decrease of entropy in one part of the whole is therefore compatible with the second law, as long as the sum remains positive.

</div>
