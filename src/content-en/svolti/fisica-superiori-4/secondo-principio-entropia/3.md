## Exercise 1

A heat engine absorbs $Q_h=1600\,\mathrm{J}$ and delivers $W=620\,\mathrm{J}$ of work per cycle. Calculate the magnitude of the heat rejected to the cold reservoir.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,2 2,2 2,3 -2,3 -2,2;-1,-0.65 1,-0.65 1,0.65 -1,0.65 -1,-0.65;-2,-3 2,-3 2,-2 -2,-2 -2,-3" vettori="0,1.95,0,0.7,Q_h = 1600 J;0,-0.7,0,-1.95,Q_c;1.05,0,3.6,0,W = 620 J" etichette="-1.37,2.5,Hot reservoir;-0.61,0.03,Engine;-1.38,-2.5,Cold reservoir" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="The heat rejected follows from the data on the other two arrows."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_h&=W+Q_c,\\[6pt]
Q_c&=Q_h-W=(1600-620)\,\mathrm{J}=980\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

It is the engine that receives the $1600\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir. Over the cycle $620\,\mathrm{J}$ leaves as work. Since the engine returns to its initial state, the remaining $980\,\mathrm{J}$ must leave as heat. This number is positive because it is the **magnitude** of the heat rejected. The cold reservoir receives $980\,\mathrm{J}$, while the working fluid loses the same amount during that part of the cycle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A heat engine absorbs $2000\,\mathrm{J}$ and produces $620\,\mathrm{J}$ of work per cycle. Calculate the magnitude of the heat rejected and its efficiency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_c&=(2000-620)\,\mathrm{J}=1380\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\eta&=\frac{620\,\mathrm{J}}{2000\,\mathrm{J}}=0.31.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The work per cycle is the same as in the previous exercise, while the heat absorbed is $400\,\mathrm{J}$ greater. The balance requires all the extra $400\,\mathrm{J}$ to be rejected to the cold reservoir, so $Q_c$ rises to $1380\,\mathrm{J}$. For the same work, increasing the heat absorbed reduces the ratio $\dfrac{W}{Q_h}$. The result means that **31 percent** of the energy absorbed leaves as work. The rejected fraction is $\dfrac{1380}{2000}=0.69$, so the two fractions add to one.

</div>
