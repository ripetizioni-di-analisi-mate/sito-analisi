## Exercise 1

A heat engine absorbs $Q_h=1600\,\mathrm{J}$ and produces $W=620\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle. Calculate the magnitude of the heat rejected to the cold reservoir.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,2 2,2 2,3 -2,3 -2,2;-1,-0.65 1,-0.65 1,0.65 -1,0.65 -1,-0.65;-2,-3 2,-3 2,-2 -2,-2 -2,-3" vettori="0,1.95,0,0.7,Q_h = 1600 J;0,-0.7,0,-1.95,Q_c;1.05,0,3.6,0,W = 620 J" etichette="-1.37,2.5,Hot reservoir;-0.61,0.03,Engine;-1.38,-2.5,Cold reservoir" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="The heat rejected is found by comparing the data on the other two arrows."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&=0=Q_h-Q_c-W,\\[6pt]
Q_h&=W+Q_c,\\[6pt]
Q_c&=Q_h-W=(1600-620)\,\mathrm{J}=980\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

It is the engine that receives the $1600\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir. In the cycle, $620\,\mathrm{J}$ leaves as work. Since the engine returns to its initial state, its internal energy does not change and the first law gives $Q_h-Q_c-W=0$. The remaining $980\,\mathrm{J}$ must therefore leave as heat. In the sign convention referred to the fluid, the heat rejected would be $-980\,\mathrm{J}$. This number is positive because it represents the **magnitude** of the heat rejected. The cold reservoir receives $980\,\mathrm{J}$, while the fluid loses the same amount during that part of the cycle. The efficiency of the engine is $\eta=\dfrac{620}{1600}=0.3875$ and the heat rejected is the fraction $1-\eta=0.6125$ of the heat absorbed, because $0.6125\cdot1600\,\mathrm{J}=980\,\mathrm{J}$. The value found is less than $Q_h$ and greater than zero, as required by the fact that the engine absorbs energy and returns only part of it as work. $980\,\mathrm{J}$ is rejected to the cold reservoir per cycle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A heat engine absorbs $2000\,\mathrm{J}$ and produces $620\,\mathrm{J}$ of work per cycle. Calculate the magnitude of the heat rejected and the efficiency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_c&=Q_h-W=(2000-620)\,\mathrm{J}=1380\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\eta&=\frac{W}{Q_h}=\frac{620\,\mathrm{J}}{2000\,\mathrm{J}}=0.31,\\[6pt]
\frac{Q_c}{Q_h}&=\frac{1380}{2000}=0.69=1-\eta.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The work per cycle is the same as in the previous exercise, while the heat absorbed is greater by $400\,\mathrm{J}$. The balance requires all the extra $400\,\mathrm{J}$ to be rejected to the cold reservoir, so $Q_c$ grows from $980\,\mathrm{J}$ to $1380\,\mathrm{J}$. For the same work, increasing the heat absorbed reduces the ratio $\dfrac{W}{Q_h}$, which goes from $0.3875$ to $0.31$. The new figure means that **31 percent** of the energy absorbed leaves as work. The fraction rejected is $\dfrac{1380}{2000}=0.69$, so the two fractions add up to one. This happens because $W$ and $Q_c$ are the only two outputs of energy and their total is $Q_h$. The efficiency is calculated by dividing the work by the heat absorbed and not by the heat rejected, because the efficiency compares the work with the energy that enters the engine. The heat rejected is $1380\,\mathrm{J}$ and the efficiency is $0.31$.

</div>
