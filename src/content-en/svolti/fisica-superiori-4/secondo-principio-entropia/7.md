## Exercise 1

A body with constant heat capacity $C=128\,\mathrm{J/K}$ goes from $300$ to $450\,\mathrm{K}$ with no phase change. Calculate its entropy change.

::grafico{f="128*log(x/300)" punti="300,0,pieno;450,51.8995338,pieno" xmin=275 xmax=500 ymin=-15 ymax=75 nomi="T,ΔS (J/K)" titolo="Entropy change relative to the state at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. The slope decreases as the temperature rises."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S&=C\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i}\\[6pt]
&=128\ln\frac{450}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}
\simeq51.9\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body's temperature increases during the transfer. To calculate the entropy change, consider reversible heating between the same endpoints and add the contributions $\dfrac{C\,\mathrm{d}T}{T}$. With constant $C$, the sum is $C$ times the logarithm of the ratio of the absolute temperatures. The ratio is $\dfrac32$, so its logarithm is positive. Its value, about $0.4055$, multiplied by $128\,\mathrm{J/K}$ gives $51.9\,\mathrm{J/K}$. A single temperature in the denominator would lose the dependence on $T$ at different points during the transfer.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body with constant heat capacity $160\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is heated at fixed volume from $300$ to $450\,\mathrm{K}$ without a phase change. Calculate the energy absorbed and the entropy change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=C(T_f-T_i)
=160\cdot(450-300)\,\mathrm{J}=24000\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\Delta S&=C\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i}\\[6pt]
&=160\ln\frac{450}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}
\simeq64.9\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For energy, each kelvin of increase requires $160\,\mathrm{J}$, so over the full increase of $150\,\mathrm{K}$ the body absorbs $24000\,\mathrm{J}$. For entropy, divide each small heat contribution by the absolute temperature at which the reversible transfer occurs. This gives the logarithm of the ratio $\dfrac{450}{300}$. The result must be between $\dfrac{24000}{450}\simeq53.3\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and $\dfrac{24000}{300}=80\,\mathrm{J/K}$ because all temperatures lie between $300$ and $450\,\mathrm{K}$. The value $64.9\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is within that interval.

</div>
