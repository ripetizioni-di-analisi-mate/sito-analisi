## Exercise 1

A body with constant heat capacity $C=128\,\mathrm{J/K}$ goes from $300$ to $450\,\mathrm{K}$ without changes of state. Calculate its change in entropy.

::grafico{f="128*log(x/300)" punti="300,0,pieno;450,51.8995338,pieno" xmin=275 xmax=500 ymin=-15 ymax=75 nomi="T,ΔS (J/K)" titolo="Change in entropy relative to the state at $300\,\mathrm{K}$, with $T$ in kelvin. The slope decreases as the temperature rises."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S&=C\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i}\\[6pt]
&=128\ln\frac{450}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
&=128\ln\frac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
&\simeq128\cdot0.4055\,\mathrm{J/K}\simeq51.9\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The temperature of the body rises during the exchange. To calculate the change in entropy one can consider a reversible heating with the same end points and add up the contributions $\dfrac{C\,\mathrm{d}T}{T}$. With constant $C$ the sum is $C$ times the logarithm of the ratio of the absolute temperatures. The ratio is $\dfrac{450}{300}=\dfrac32$, so the logarithm is positive. Its value, about $0.4055$, multiplied by $128\,\mathrm{J/K}$ gives $51.9\,\mathrm{J/K}$. A single temperature value in the denominator would lose the dependence on $T$ at the different instants of the exchange. The logarithm has a dimensionless argument, so the unit of the result is that of $C$, namely $\mathrm{J/K}$. The positive sign corresponds to the fact that the body absorbs heat. In the figure the point at $450\,\mathrm{K}$ lies above the axis, and the curve becomes flatter as $T$ increases, because the same heat, divided by a higher temperature, produces less entropy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body with constant heat capacity $160\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is heated at constant volume from $300$ to $450\,\mathrm{K}$ without phase transitions. Calculate the energy absorbed and the change in entropy.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=C(T_f-T_i)
=160\cdot(450-300)\,\mathrm{J}=24000\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\Delta S&=C\ln\frac{T_f}{T_i}\\[6pt]
&=160\ln\frac{450}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
&\simeq160\cdot0.4055\,\mathrm{J/K}\simeq64.9\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\frac{Q}{T_f}&=\frac{24000}{450}\,\mathrm{J/K}\simeq53.3\,\mathrm{J/K}<\Delta S<\frac{Q}{T_i}=80\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the energy, each kelvin of increase requires $160\,\mathrm{J}$, so over the whole $150\,\mathrm{K}$ a total of $24000\,\mathrm{J}$ enters. For the entropy, each small contribution of heat is divided by the absolute temperature at which the reversible transfer occurs. This is why the logarithm of the ratio $\dfrac{450}{300}$ appears. The result must lie between $\dfrac{24000}{450}\simeq53.3\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and $\dfrac{24000}{300}=80\,\mathrm{J/K}$, because all the temperatures crossed are between $300$ and $450\,\mathrm{K}$. The value $64.9\,\mathrm{J/K}$ falls within the interval. Dividing the whole heat by the mean temperature $375\,\mathrm{K}$ would give $64\,\mathrm{J/K}$, close to but not equal to the exact value, because the mean does not account for how the heat is distributed along the heating. If the temperatures were given in degrees Celsius, the ratio $\dfrac{T_f}{T_i}$ would have to be calculated after conversion to kelvin, and with the values in degrees Celsius, $\dfrac{176.85}{26.85}$, the logarithm would give a very different number. The energy absorbed is $24000\,\mathrm{J}$ and the entropy of the body increases by about $64.9\,\mathrm{J/K}$.

</div>
