## Exercise 1

$960\,\mathrm{J}$ is transferred directly from a reservoir at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ to one at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the total entropy change.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3,-0.65 -1,-0.65 -1,0.65 -3,0.65 -3,-0.65;1,-0.65 3,-0.65 3,0.65 1,0.65 1,-0.65" vettori="-0.95,0,0.95,0,960 J" etichette="-2.73,0,600 K;1.3,0,300 K" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1.35 ymax=1.45 titolo="The arrow shows which reservoir loses heat and which receives it."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S_h&=-\frac{960}{600}\,\mathrm{J/K}=-1.6\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_c&=\frac{960}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}=3.2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(-1.6+3.2)\,\mathrm{J/K}=1.6\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the balance for the two reservoirs, count the heat once as energy lost by the hot reservoir and once as energy received by the cold one. Use the respective absolute temperatures in the two ratios. The cold temperature is half the hot one, so the cold reservoir's entropy increase is twice the magnitude of the hot reservoir's entropy decrease. The net increase is $1.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Its positive sign is consistent with the spontaneous direction of the transfer.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Between the same reservoirs at $600$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$, $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ is transferred from hot to cold. Calculate the separate changes and determine whether transfer in the opposite direction with the same energy and no other effects would be consistent with the second law.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S_h&=-\frac{1200}{600}\,\mathrm{J/K}=-2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_c&=\frac{1200}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}=4\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot,inverso}}&=(2-4)\,\mathrm{J/K}=-2\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the stated transfer the reservoir at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ has negative heat and the one at $300\,\mathrm{K}$ has positive heat. The changes are $-2$ and $+4\,\mathrm{J/K}$, so the entropy of the combined system increases by $2\,\mathrm{J/K}$. With the transfer reversed the temperatures stay the same and only the signs of heat change. The sum would be negative, so this transfer is impossible as the sole effect. A refrigerator transfers heat from cold to hot by consuming work and rejecting more than the $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ removed to the hot side.

</div>
