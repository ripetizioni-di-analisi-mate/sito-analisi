## Exercise 1

$960\,\mathrm{J}$ pass directly from a reservoir at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ to a reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the total change in entropy.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3,-0.65 -1,-0.65 -1,0.65 -3,0.65 -3,-0.65;1,-0.65 3,-0.65 3,0.65 1,0.65 1,-0.65" vettori="-0.95,0,0.95,0,960 J" etichette="-2.73,0,600 K;1.3,0,300 K" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1.35 ymax=1.45 titolo="The arrow shows which reservoir loses heat and which receives it."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S_h&=-\frac{960}{600}\,\mathrm{J/K}=-1.6\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_c&=\frac{960}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}=3.2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\Delta S_h+\Delta S_c=(-1.6+3.2)\,\mathrm{J/K}=1.6\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the balance of the two reservoirs, the heat is counted once as energy lost by the hot one and once as energy received by the cold one. In the two ratios the respective absolute temperatures are used. The cold temperature is half the hot one, so the entropy increase of the cold reservoir is twice the decrease of the hot one in magnitude, and indeed $3.2=2\cdot1.6$. A net increase of $1.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$ remains. The positive sign is consistent with the spontaneous direction of the exchange. The two reservoirs are ideal, so each keeps its own temperature and the heat exchanged does not change its value, a condition that allows $\Delta S=\dfrac{Q}{T}$ to be used without integrals. The process is irreversible, since the total entropy increases, and the value $1.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$ measures how far the pair of reservoirs departs from the reversible situation, for which the change would be zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Between the same reservoirs at $600$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$, $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ is transferred from the hot one to the cold one. Calculate the separate changes and determine whether the opposite transfer would be compatible with the second law for the same energy, with no other effects.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S_h&=-\frac{1200}{600}\,\mathrm{J/K}=-2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_c&=\frac{1200}{300}\,\mathrm{J/K}=4\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(-2+4)\,\mathrm{J/K}=2\,\mathrm{J/K},\\[6pt]
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot,reversed}}&=\left(+\frac{1200}{600}-\frac{1200}{300}\right)\mathrm{J/K}=(2-4)\,\mathrm{J/K}=-2\,\mathrm{J/K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the transfer described, the reservoir at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ has negative heat and the one at $300\,\mathrm{K}$ has positive heat. The values found are $-2$ and $+4\,\mathrm{J/K}$, so the entropy of the whole increases by $2\,\mathrm{J/K}$. In the opposite transfer the temperatures remain the same and only the signs of the heats change, so the hot reservoir receives $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ and the cold one gives it off. The sum would become negative, so this transfer is impossible as the only effect, because in an isolated system the total entropy cannot decrease. The result agrees with the Clausius statement, which excludes the passage of heat from cold to hot as the only result. A refrigerator achieves the flow from the cold reservoir to the hot one by consuming work and delivering to the hot side more than the $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ removed. In that case the total change in entropy of the surroundings is greater than or equal to zero, because the extra heat delivered compensates for the decrease.

</div>
