## Exercise 1

A cyclic engine absorbs $Q_h=1920\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir and rejects $Q_c=1500\,\mathrm{J}$ to the cold one. Calculate its efficiency as a number between zero and one.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,2 2,2 2,3 -2,3 -2,2;-1,-0.65 1,-0.65 1,0.65 -1,0.65 -1,-0.65;-2,-3 2,-3 2,-2 -2,-2 -2,-3" vettori="0,1.95,0,0.7,Q_h = 1920 J;0,-0.7,0,-1.95,Q_c = 1500 J;1.05,0,3.6,0,W" etichette="-1.37,2.5,Hot reservoir;-0.61,0.03,Engine;-1.38,-2.5,Cold reservoir" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="The two amounts of heat are shown as magnitudes. The arrows indicate their directions."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= Q_h-Q_c\\[6pt]
  &= (1920-1500)\,\mathrm{J}=420\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\eta &= \frac{W}{Q_h}=\frac{420\,\mathrm{J}}{1920\,\mathrm{J}}
=\frac{7}{32}\simeq 0.219.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the end of the cycle, the internal energy has returned to its initial value. The $1920\,\mathrm{J}$ of incoming heat is therefore split between heat rejected to the cold reservoir and output work. Subtracting the $1500\,\mathrm{J}$ rejected leaves $420\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. The **efficiency** compares the useful $420\,\mathrm{J}$ with all $1920\,\mathrm{J}$ absorbed. The units cancel and the result is about $0.219$, or $21.9\%$. The remaining fraction, about $0.781$, is rejected as heat.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cyclic engine absorbs $2400\,\mathrm{J}$ and rejects $1500\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle. It runs at $4$ cycles per second. Calculate the work per cycle, efficiency, and average mechanical power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &=(2400-1500)\,\mathrm{J}=900\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\eta &=\frac{900\,\mathrm{J}}{2400\,\mathrm{J}}=0.375,\\[6pt]
P&=fW=4\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\cdot900\,\mathrm{J}
=3600\,\mathrm{W}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The energy balance first applies to one cycle. Since the working fluid returns to its initial state, the difference between heat absorbed and heat rejected is the work of $900\,\mathrm{J}$. Dividing this by the heat absorbed gives an efficiency of $37.5\%$. Four cycles occur in one second, yielding $4\cdot900\,\mathrm{J}$ in that second. The **average power** is $3600\,\mathrm{W}$. The result can also be checked in terms of power. The engine receives $9600\,\mathrm{W}$ as heat and rejects $6000\,\mathrm{W}$ to the cold reservoir, so the difference is again $3600\,\mathrm{W}$.

</div>
