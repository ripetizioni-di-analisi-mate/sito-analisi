## Exercise 1

A cyclic engine absorbs $Q_h=1920\,\mathrm{J}$ from the hot reservoir and rejects $Q_c=1500\,\mathrm{J}$ to the cold one. Calculate the efficiency as a number between zero and one.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,2 2,2 2,3 -2,3 -2,2;-1,-0.65 1,-0.65 1,0.65 -1,0.65 -1,-0.65;-2,-3 2,-3 2,-2 -2,-2 -2,-3" vettori="0,1.95,0,0.7,Q_h = 1920 J;0,-0.7,0,-1.95,Q_c = 1500 J;1.05,0,3.6,0,W" etichette="-1.37,2.5,Hot reservoir;-0.61,0.03,Engine;-1.38,-2.5,Cold reservoir" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="The two heats are given as magnitudes. The arrows show their direction."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U &= 0=Q_h-Q_c-W,\\[6pt]
W &= Q_h-Q_c\\[6pt]
  &= (1920-1500)\,\mathrm{J}=420\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\eta &= \frac{W}{Q_h}=\frac{420\,\mathrm{J}}{1920\,\mathrm{J}}
=\frac{7}{32}\simeq 0.219,\\[6pt]
1-\frac{Q_c}{Q_h}&=1-\frac{25}{32}=\frac{7}{32}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The engine operates between just two reservoirs and the working fluid goes through a cycle. At the end of the cycle the internal energy has the same value as at the start. The incoming heat of $1920\,\mathrm{J}$ is therefore divided between the heat rejected to the cold reservoir and the work output. Subtracting the $1500\,\mathrm{J}$ rejected leaves $420\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. The **efficiency** compares the useful $420\,\mathrm{J}$ with all $1920\,\mathrm{J}$ absorbed. The units cancel and the result is about $0.219$, that is, $21.9\%$. The remaining fraction, about $0.781$, is the part rejected as heat. The ratio $\dfrac{1500}{1920}$ simplifies by $60$ and equals $\dfrac{25}{32}$, so the formula $\eta=1-\dfrac{Q_c}{Q_h}$ also gives $\dfrac{7}{32}$, as the direct division does. The result is a pure number between zero and one, as it must be for an engine that rejects heat to the cold reservoir. If $Q_c$ were zero, $\eta=1$ would result, a case ruled out by the Kelvin–Planck statement. The work of $420\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle is therefore a little more than a fifth of the energy absorbed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cyclic engine absorbs $2400\,\mathrm{J}$ and rejects $1500\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle. It completes $4$ cycles per second. Calculate the work per cycle, the efficiency, and the average mechanical power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &=Q_h-Q_c=(2400-1500)\,\mathrm{J}=900\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\eta &=\frac{W}{Q_h}=\frac{900\,\mathrm{J}}{2400\,\mathrm{J}}=0.375,\\[6pt]
P&=fW=4\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\cdot900\,\mathrm{J}
=3600\,\mathrm{W},\\[6pt]
P_h&=fQ_h=9600\,\mathrm{W},\qquad P_c=fQ_c=6000\,\mathrm{W}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The efficiency and the work are calculated first for a single cycle. By the first law, the energy balance of a cycle is written $\Delta U=0=Q_h-Q_c-W$. Since the fluid returns to its initial state, the difference between the heat absorbed and the heat rejected is the work of $900\,\mathrm{J}$. Dividing it by the heat absorbed gives an efficiency of $37.5\%$. Four cycles repeat in one second, so $4\cdot900\,\mathrm{J}$ is produced in that second. The **average power** is $3600\,\mathrm{W}$. It can also be checked in terms of power. The engine receives $9600\,\mathrm{W}$ in thermal form, because $4\cdot2400=9600$, and rejects $6000\,\mathrm{W}$ to the cold reservoir, because $4\cdot1500=6000$. The difference is again $3600\,\mathrm{W}$, so the average power delivered is $3.6\,\mathrm{kW}$. The efficiency coincides with the ratio of the powers, $\dfrac{3600}{9600}=0.375$, since the frequency appears in both terms and cancels.

</div>
