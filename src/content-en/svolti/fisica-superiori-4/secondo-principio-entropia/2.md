## Exercise 1

A heat engine absorbs $Q_h=1440\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle and has efficiency $\eta=0.28$. Calculate the work produced in one cycle.

::grafico{f="0.28*x" punti="1440,403.2,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=1900 ymin=0 ymax=600 nomi="Qₕ,W (J)" titolo="Work per cycle at fixed efficiency. The marked point corresponds to $Q_h=1440\,\mathrm{J}$."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta&=\frac{W}{Q_h},\\[6pt]
W&=\eta Q_h=0.28\cdot1440\,\mathrm{J}=403.2\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
Q_c&=Q_h-W=(1440-403.2)\,\mathrm{J}=1036.8\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The efficiency is defined as the ratio of work produced to heat absorbed. It therefore expresses how much work is obtained for each joule absorbed. Here $0.28\,\mathrm{J}$ of work is obtained for each joule that enters. So, given the total energy absorbed, $1440\,\mathrm{J}$ is multiplied by the fraction $0.28$. The product is $403.2\,\mathrm{J}$ and is less than $1440\,\mathrm{J}$, as expected for an efficiency below one. The point in the figure lies on the line of slope $0.28$, which represents $W=\eta Q_h$ as the heat absorbed varies. Since the fluid returns to its initial state, by the first law the remaining part of the absorbed energy is rejected to the cold reservoir, and it equals $1036.8\,\mathrm{J}$. A check on the units confirms the result, since the efficiency is a pure number and the product $\eta Q_h$ therefore has the same units as $Q_h$, namely joules. The work produced in one cycle is therefore about $403\,\mathrm{J}$, and $1036.8\,\mathrm{J}$ is rejected to the cold reservoir.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A heat engine absorbs $Q_h=1800\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle and has efficiency $\eta=0.28$. Calculate the work produced and the heat rejected to the cold reservoir.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &=\eta Q_h=0.28\cdot1800\,\mathrm{J}=504\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
Q_c &=Q_h-W=(1800-504)\,\mathrm{J}=1296\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
Q_c &=(1-\eta)Q_h=0.72\cdot1800\,\mathrm{J}=1296\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the energy absorbed the engine obtains work in the proportion set by $\eta$. This gives $504\,\mathrm{J}$ of work per cycle. By the first law, the part of the absorbed heat that remains is rejected to the cold reservoir, because the fluid does not accumulate energy from one cycle to the next. The difference $1800\,\mathrm{J}-504\,\mathrm{J}$ is therefore $1296\,\mathrm{J}$. The fraction rejected is $1-\eta=0.72$ and $0.72\cdot1800\,\mathrm{J}=1296\,\mathrm{J}$, in agreement with the subtraction. The signed heat, referred to the fluid during the rejection, is $-1296\,\mathrm{J}$. The heat absorbed is greater than in the previous exercise and the efficiency is the same, so the work is greater and goes from $403.2\,\mathrm{J}$ to $504\,\mathrm{J}$, in the same ratio $\dfrac{1800}{1440}=1.25$ as the heats absorbed. The two routes to $Q_c$, the subtraction and the product by $1-\eta$, give the same value because $W=\eta Q_h$. The engine produces $504\,\mathrm{J}$ of work and rejects $1296\,\mathrm{J}$ to the cold reservoir, that is, about $1.3\,\mathrm{kJ}$.

</div>
