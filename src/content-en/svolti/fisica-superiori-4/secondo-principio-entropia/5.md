## Exercise 1

A refrigerator removes $Q_c=576\,\mathrm{J}$ from the cold reservoir and receives work $W_{\mathrm{in}}=240\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate the refrigerator coefficient of performance.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,2 2,2 2,3 -2,3 -2,2;-1.55,-0.65 1.55,-0.65 1.55,0.65 -1.55,0.65 -1.55,-0.65;-2,-3 2,-3 2,-2 -2,-2 -2,-3" vettori="0,0.7,0,1.95,Q_h;0,-1.95,0,-0.7,Q_c = 576 J;3.6,0,1.6,0,W_in = 240 J" etichette="-1.37,2.5,Hot reservoir;-1.03,0.03,Refrigerator;-1.38,-2.5,Cold reservoir" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="The heat removed from the cold reservoir and the work input both enter the refrigerator."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{COP}_f&=\frac{Q_c}{W_{\mathrm{in}}}
=\frac{576\,\mathrm{J}}{240\,\mathrm{J}}=2.4,\\[6pt]
Q_h&=Q_c+W_{\mathrm{in}}=(576+240)\,\mathrm{J}=816\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The useful effect of the refrigerator is the heat removed from the cold side. The cost of the transfer is the work received by the compressor. The coefficient of performance is the ratio of the useful effect to the cost, so the two data are divided. The units cancel and the result is a pure number. For each joule of work, $2.4\,\mathrm{J}$ is removed from the cold reservoir. The value exceeds one because the work received makes it possible to transfer to the hot reservoir energy that comes from the cold compartment. The balance holds because, by the first law applied to the cycle, the machine rejects both contributions to the hot reservoir, a total of $816\,\mathrm{J}$. The refrigerator coefficient of performance is $2.4$, and a higher value would indicate a refrigerator that removes more heat for the same work consumed. The heat rejected to the hot reservoir, $816\,\mathrm{J}$, is about $42\%$ more than the heat removed from the compartment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A refrigerator removes $720\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle from the cold reservoir, with a work input of $240\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate the heat rejected and the coefficients of performance as a refrigerator and as a heat pump.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_h&=Q_c+W_{\mathrm{in}}=(720+240)\,\mathrm{J}=960\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\mathrm{COP}_f&=\frac{Q_c}{W_{\mathrm{in}}}=\frac{720\,\mathrm{J}}{240\,\mathrm{J}}=3,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{COP}_p&=\frac{Q_h}{W_{\mathrm{in}}}=\frac{960\,\mathrm{J}}{240\,\mathrm{J}}=4,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{COP}_p&=\mathrm{COP}_f+1=3+1=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the cycle the fluid receives $720\,\mathrm{J}$ as heat and $240\,\mathrm{J}$ as work. Since it does not accumulate energy, it delivers $960\,\mathrm{J}$ to the hot environment. In the calculation of $\mathrm{COP}_f$ the numerator is the heat removed from the cold compartment. In the calculation of $\mathrm{COP}_p$ the effect of interest is heating, so the numerator is the heat delivered to the hot side. The denominator is the same work input. The difference between the coefficients is one, as the balance requires, because $Q_h=Q_c+W_{\mathrm{in}}$ and therefore $\dfrac{Q_h}{W_{\mathrm{in}}}=\dfrac{Q_c}{W_{\mathrm{in}}}+1$. The same machine, used for heating, therefore delivers $4\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat per joule of work, against the $3\,\mathrm{J}$ removed when it is used for cooling. The heat rejected is $960\,\mathrm{J}$ and the coefficients are $3$ and $4$, both pure numbers. The heat rejected exceeds the heat removed by the whole work input, $240\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>
