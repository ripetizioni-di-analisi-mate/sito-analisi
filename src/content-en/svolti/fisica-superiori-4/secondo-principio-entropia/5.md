## Exercise 1

A refrigerator removes $Q_c=576\,\mathrm{J}$ from the cold reservoir and receives work $W_{\mathrm{in}}=240\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate the refrigerator's coefficient of performance.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,2 2,2 2,3 -2,3 -2,2;-1.55,-0.65 1.55,-0.65 1.55,0.65 -1.55,0.65 -1.55,-0.65;-2,-3 2,-3 2,-2 -2,-2 -2,-3" vettori="0,0.7,0,1.95,Q_h;0,-1.95,0,-0.7,Q_c = 576 J;3.6,0,1.6,0,W_in = 240 J" etichette="-1.37,2.5,Hot reservoir;-1.03,0.03,Refrigerator;-1.38,-2.5,Cold reservoir" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="Heat removed from the cold reservoir and work input both enter the refrigerator."}

$$
\mathrm{COP}_f=\frac{Q_c}{W_{\mathrm{in}}}
=\frac{576\,\mathrm{J}}{240\,\mathrm{J}}=2.4.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The useful effect of the refrigerator is the heat removed from the cold side. The cost of the transfer is the work received by the compressor. Dividing the two values gives a dimensionless number. For each joule of work, $2.4\,\mathrm{J}$ is removed from the cold reservoir. The energy balance holds because the machine rejects both contributions, a total of $816\,\mathrm{J}$, to the hot reservoir.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A refrigerator removes $720\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle from the cold reservoir and receives $240\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. Calculate the heat rejected and the coefficients of performance for refrigeration and heating.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_h&=Q_c+W_{\mathrm{in}}=(720+240)\,\mathrm{J}=960\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\mathrm{COP}_f&=\frac{720\,\mathrm{J}}{240\,\mathrm{J}}=3,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{COP}_p&=\frac{960\,\mathrm{J}}{240\,\mathrm{J}}=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Over a cycle the fluid receives $720\,\mathrm{J}$ as heat and $240\,\mathrm{J}$ as work. Since it accumulates no energy, it supplies $960\,\mathrm{J}$ to the hot surroundings. For $\mathrm{COP}_f$ the numerator is the heat removed from the refrigerated compartment. For $\mathrm{COP}_p$ the effect of interest is heating, so the numerator is the heat delivered to the hot side. The denominator is the same work input in both cases. The coefficients differ by one, as predicted by the balance.

</div>
