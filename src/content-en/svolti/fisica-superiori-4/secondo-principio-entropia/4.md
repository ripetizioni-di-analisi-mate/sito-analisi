## Exercise 1

An engine operates only between two reservoirs at $T_h=520\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_c=300\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the maximum possible efficiency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta&\leq\eta_C=1-\frac{T_c}{T_h}\\[6pt]
&=1-\frac{300\,\mathrm{K}}{520\,\mathrm{K}}\\[6pt]
&=1-\frac{15}{26}=\frac{11}{26}\simeq0.423.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Carnot's theorem applies because the engine is cyclic and exchanges heat only with two reservoirs at constant temperature. The two temperatures fix the maximum attainable by a reversible cycle. The data are already in kelvin, so the cold temperature $300\,\mathrm{K}$ is divided directly by the hot one $520\,\mathrm{K}$. The ratio simplifies by $20$ and equals $\dfrac{15}{26}$, that is, about $0.577$. This is the minimum fraction of the absorbed heat that an engine between those reservoirs must reject to the cold one. Subtracting it from one gives the **limit** of $42.3\%$. With temperatures given in degrees Celsius, the conversion to kelvin is obtained by adding $273$ to each value, because Carnot's ratio requires the absolute scale. The actual efficiency depends on the engine and stays below the limit if its cycle is irreversible. For $Q_h=1000\,\mathrm{J}$ the work therefore cannot exceed about $423\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle and the heat rejected cannot be less than about $577\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An engine operates only between two reservoirs at $650\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and absorbs $1800\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle. Calculate the maximum efficiency and work.

::grafico{f="1-300/x" punti="520,0.423076923,pieno;650,0.538461538,pieno" xmin=300 xmax=800 ymin=0 ymax=0.7 nomi="Tₕ,η_C" titolo="With the same cold reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$, the higher hot temperature gives a higher efficiency limit."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_C&=1-\frac{300}{650}=1-\frac{6}{13}=\frac{7}{13}\simeq0.538,\\[6pt]
W_{\max}&=\eta_C Q_h
=\frac{7}{13}\cdot 1800\,\mathrm{J}
\simeq969\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
Q_{c,\min}&=Q_h-W_{\max}=\frac{6}{13}\cdot1800\,\mathrm{J}\simeq831\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Carnot's theorem gives the limit $\dfrac{7}{13}$ using the two absolute temperatures, already expressed in kelvin. To obtain the energy, the exact fraction is multiplied by the heat absorbed, $1800\,\mathrm{J}$. Using the fraction before rounding avoids an error in the estimate of the work. About $969\,\mathrm{J}$ is obtained as the maximum per cycle. In the reversible cycle the remaining heat rejected is about $831\,\mathrm{J}$, and it is obtained in the same way by multiplying $\dfrac{6}{13}$ by $1800\,\mathrm{J}$. By comparison, in a real cycle between the same reservoirs, for the same heat absorbed, the work is smaller and the heat rejected is greater. In the figure the two points show that, for the same cold reservoir, the limit rises from $0.423$ to $0.538$ as the hot reservoir goes from $520\,\mathrm{K}$ to $650\,\mathrm{K}$. The maximum efficiency is about $0.538$ and the maximum work per cycle is about $969\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>
