## Exercise 1

An engine operates only between two reservoirs at $T_h=520\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_c=300\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the maximum possible efficiency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_C&=1-\frac{T_c}{T_h}\\[6pt]
&=1-\frac{300\,\mathrm{K}}{520\,\mathrm{K}}
=\frac{11}{26}\simeq0.423.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two temperatures set the maximum attainable by a reversible cycle. They are already in kelvin, so divide the cold temperature of $300\,\mathrm{K}$ by the hot temperature of $520\,\mathrm{K}$. The ratio is about $0.577$ and is the minimum fraction of the absorbed heat that an engine between those reservoirs must reject to the cold one. Subtracting it from one gives the **limit** of $42.3\%$. Actual efficiency depends on the engine and remains below this limit if the cycle is irreversible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An engine operates only between two reservoirs at $650\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and absorbs $1800\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle. Calculate the maximum efficiency and work.

::grafico{f="1-300/x" punti="520,0.423076923,pieno;650,0.538461538,pieno" xmin=300 xmax=800 ymin=0 ymax=0.7 nomi="Tₕ,η_C" titolo="With the same cold reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$, the higher hot temperature gives a higher efficiency limit."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_C&=1-\frac{300}{650}=\frac{7}{13}\simeq0.538,\\[6pt]
W_{\max}&=\eta_C Q_h
=\frac{7}{13}\cdot 1800\,\mathrm{J}
\simeq969\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Carnot's theorem gives the limit $\dfrac{7}{13}$ from the two absolute temperatures. To calculate the amount of energy, multiply the exact fraction by the absorbed heat of $1800\,\mathrm{J}$. Keeping the fraction until the last step avoids a rounding error in the work estimate. The maximum is about $969\,\mathrm{J}$ per cycle. In the reversible cycle the remaining heat rejected is about $831\,\mathrm{J}$. For comparison, an actual irreversible cycle between the same reservoirs, with the same heat input, delivers less work and rejects more heat.

</div>
