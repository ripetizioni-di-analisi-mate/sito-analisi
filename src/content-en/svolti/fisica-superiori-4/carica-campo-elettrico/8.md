## Exercise 1

A dipole consists of two charges of magnitude $1.2\,\mathrm{nC}$ separated by $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The dipole moment is perpendicular to a uniform field of magnitude $800\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Find the magnitude of the torque.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=qd\\[6pt]
&=(1.2\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm C)(0.020\,\mathrm m)\\[6pt]
&=2.4\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\\[10pt]
M&=pE\sin\theta,\qquad\theta=90^\circ\\[6pt]
&=(2.4\cdot10^{-11})(800)(1)\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
&=1.92\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,-1,pieno,−q;0,1,pieno,+q" linee="0,-1 0,1" vettori="0,-1,-1.6,-1,F−;0,1,1.6,1,F+;1.4,-0.2,3.0,-0.2,E" xmin=-2.3 xmax=3.7 ymin=-2.0 ymax=2.2 titolo="The opposite forces form a couple with an arm of 2 cm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive charge experiences a force along the field, while the negative one experiences an equal and opposite force. The two forces cancel in the sum, but their lines of action are distinct, so they exert a moment on the dipole. The dipole moment has magnitude $p=qd$ and the torque is $M=pE\sin\theta$. Since the dipole is perpendicular to the field, $\theta=90^\circ$ and $\sin\theta=1$, so the distance between the lines of action coincides with $d$. The moment of the couple then has magnitude $qEd=pE$. The separation is converted from centimeters to meters before substituting, to obtain the result in newton meters. The torque is $1.92\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. If the dipole were parallel to the field, the lines of action would coincide and the moment would be zero. The value obtained is the maximum possible for this dipole and this field. The moment is proportional to both the field and the separation, so with a doubled field the moment would double.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A dipole with charges of magnitude $1.5\,\mathrm{nC}$ separated by $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with a uniform field of $800\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Find the magnitude of the torque and state what fraction of the maximum it is.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=qd=(1.5\cdot10^{-9})(0.020)\,\mathrm{C\,m}=3.0\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\\[6pt]
M_{\max}&=pE=(3.0\cdot10^{-11})(800)\,\mathrm{N\,m}=2.4\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
M&=M_{\max}\sin30^\circ\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1}{2}\,(2.4\cdot10^{-8})\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
&=1.2\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}=\frac{M_{\max}}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lines of action of the forces are parallel to the field. Their distance is the component of the separation perpendicular to the field, that is, $d\sin30^\circ=1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. The magnitude of each force remains $qE=1.2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$ at every orientation, because the field is uniform. The moment of the couple is the product $qE\cdot d\sin30^\circ=1.2\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, which coincides with $pE\sin30^\circ$. The ratio to the maximum moment $pE$, obtained at $90^\circ$, is therefore $\sin30^\circ=\dfrac12$. Only the arm of the couple changes, while the magnitude of the forces remains the same. The moment is therefore half the value it would have for a dipole perpendicular to the field. The dipole tends to rotate, reducing the angle with the field to $0^\circ$, where the moment vanishes and there is stable equilibrium. The moment is maximum for $\theta=90^\circ$ and zero for $\theta=0^\circ$ and for $\theta=180^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A dipole with charges of magnitude $2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ separated by $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ is in a uniform field of $600\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The measured magnitude of the torque is $9.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Find the angle between the dipole moment and the field.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=qd=(2.0\cdot10^{-9})(0.015)\,\mathrm{C\,m}=3.0\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\\[6pt]
M_{\max}&=pE=(3.0\cdot10^{-11})(600)\,\mathrm{N\,m}=1.8\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
\sin\theta&=\frac{M}{pE}=\frac{9.0\cdot10^{-9}}{1.8\cdot10^{-8}}=\frac12\\[6pt]
\theta&=30^\circ\quad\text{or}\quad\theta=150^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque on a dipole in a uniform field is $M=pE\sin\theta$, so the sine of the angle is found by dividing the measured moment by the maximum value $pE$. The dipole moment is $p=qd$ and the separation is converted from centimeters to meters, $0.015\,\mathrm m$. The ratio of $9.0\cdot10^{-9}$ to $1.8\cdot10^{-8}$ is $\dfrac12$. In the interval from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$ the sine equals $\dfrac12$ for two angles, $30^\circ$ and $150^\circ$, which have the same arm of the couple. The magnitude of the moment does not distinguish the two cases, because in both the dipole makes the same acute angle with the line of the field. To choose one we need to know whether the dipole is closer to the parallel or to the antiparallel orientation with respect to the field. With $\theta=90^\circ$ the moment would be the maximum, $1.8\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, that is, twice the measured value. In both cases the couple tends to bring the dipole toward alignment with the field.

</div>
