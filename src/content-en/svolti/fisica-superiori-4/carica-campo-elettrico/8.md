## Exercise 1

A dipole consists of two charges of magnitude $1.2\,\mathrm{nC}$ separated by $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its dipole moment is perpendicular to a uniform field of magnitude $800\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Calculate the torque magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=qd\\
&=(1.2\cdot10^{-9})\cdot0.020\,\mathrm{C\,m}\\
&=2.4\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\\[5pt]
M&=pE\sin(90^\circ)\\
&=(2.4\cdot10^{-11})\cdot800\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\
&=1.92\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,-1,pieno,−q;0,1,pieno,+q" linee="0,-1 0,1" vettori="0,-1,-1.6,-1,F−;0,1,1.6,1,F+;1.4,-0.2,3.0,-0.2,E" xmin=-2.3 xmax=3.7 ymin=-2.0 ymax=2.2 titolo="The opposite forces form a couple with a 2 cm moment arm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive charge experiences a force along the field, while the negative charge experiences an equal and opposite force. The forces cancel in the sum, but their lines of action are distinct. Because the dipole is perpendicular to the field, the distance between these lines equals $d$. The couple therefore has torque magnitude $qEd=pE$. Convert centimeters to meters to express the product in newton meters. If the dipole were parallel to the field, the lines of action would coincide and the torque would be zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A dipole with charges of magnitude $1.5\,\mathrm{nC}$ separated by $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with a uniform field of $800\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Calculate the torque magnitude and state what fraction of its maximum it is.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=(1.5\cdot10^{-9})\cdot0.020\,\mathrm{C\,m}\\
&=3.0\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\\[5pt]
M_{\max}&=pE=2.4\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\
M&=M_{\max}\sin(30^\circ)\\
&=1.2\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{N\,m}=\frac{M_{\max}}2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force lines of action are parallel to the field. Their distance is the component of the separation perpendicular to the field, namely $d\sin(30^\circ)$. Its ratio to the maximum moment arm $d$ is therefore $\sin(30^\circ)=\dfrac12$. Each force retains the magnitude $qE$ at every orientation because the field is uniform. Only the couple's moment arm changes. The torque is therefore half what it would be for a perpendicular dipole.

</div>
