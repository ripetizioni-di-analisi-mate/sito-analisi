## Exercise 1

Two positive charges $Q_1=2.4\,\mathrm{nC}$ on the left and $Q_2=1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ on the right are $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. With $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$, calculate the field component at the midpoint, with right taken as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=0.10\,\mathrm m\\[4pt]
E_x&=\frac{k_eQ_1}{r^2}-\frac{k_eQ_2}{r^2}\\
&=\frac{8.99\cdot10^9(2.4-1.0)\cdot10^{-9}}{(0.10)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}\\
&\simeq+1.26\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="-3,0,pieno,+Q1;0,0,pieno,P;3,0,pieno,+Q2" vettori="0,-0.4,2.4,-0.4,E1;0,-0.4,-1.0,-0.4,E2" xmin=-4.0 xmax=4.0 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.2 titolo="Field contributions at P are opposite. Each source is 10 cm away"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each positive charge produces a field pointing away from itself. At the midpoint, the left source's field points right and the right source's field points left. The algebraic sum therefore subtracts the magnitudes. The denominators are equal because each source is half the full separation from the midpoint. The positive result means that the larger charge on the left contributes the stronger field. Using $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ as the distance of each source from $P$ would incorrectly reduce each contribution by a factor of four.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two identical sources $Q=+5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ are at $(-0.30,0)\,\mathrm m$ and $(0.30,0)\,\mathrm m$. A test charge $q_0=-5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at $P=(0,0.40)\,\mathrm m$. Calculate the field at $P$ and the force on $q_0$, using $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\sqrt{(0.30)^2+(0.40)^2}\,\mathrm m=0.50\,\mathrm m\\
E_1=E_2&=k_e\frac Q{r^2}=179.8\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[5pt]
E_x&=0\\
E_y&=2E_1\frac{0.40}{0.50}=287.68\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[5pt]
F_x&=0,\qquad F_y=q_0 E_y\simeq-1.44\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="-3,0,pieno,+Q;3,0,pieno,+Q;0,4,pieno" etichette="-0.50,4.12,P" segmenti="-3,0,0,4;3,0,0,4" vettori="0,4,0.9,5.2,E1;0,4,-0.9,5.2,E2;0,4,0,6.4,E" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-0.9 ymax=7.5 titolo="Symmetry cancels the horizontal field components at P"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Point $P$ lies on the pair's symmetry axis and is $0.50\,\mathrm m$ from each source. The two contributions therefore have equal magnitudes. The horizontal components point in opposite directions and cancel. Both vertical components are positive because the field of each positive source goes from the charge toward $P$. Each vertical projection equals its magnitude times $0.40/0.50$, the ratio of the vertical leg to the hypotenuse. The net field points up. Multiplying by $q_0<0$ gives a downward force.

</div>
