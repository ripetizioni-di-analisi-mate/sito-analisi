## Exercise 1

Two positive charges $Q_1=2.4\,\mathrm{nC}$ on the left and $Q_2=1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ on the right are $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart. With $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$, find the component of the field at the midpoint, with the positive direction to the right.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{0.20}{2}\,\mathrm m=0.10\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
E_{1,x}&=+\frac{k_eQ_1}{r^2}=\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(2.4\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.10)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}\simeq+2158\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
E_{2,x}&=-\frac{k_eQ_2}{r^2}=-\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(1.0\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.10)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}=-899\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
E_x&=E_{1,x}+E_{2,x}\\[6pt]
&\simeq+1.26\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="-3,0,pieno,+Q1;0,0,pieno,P;3,0,pieno,+Q2" vettori="0,-0.4,2.4,-0.4,E1;0,-0.4,-1.0,-0.4,E2" xmin=-4.0 xmax=4.0 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.2 titolo="The contributions to the field at P are opposite. The distance from each source is 10 cm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each positive charge produces a field that points away from itself. At the midpoint the field of the source on the left points right and that of the source on the right points left, so the two components have opposite signs. By the superposition principle the total field is the sum of the two components, which is equivalent to the difference of the magnitudes. The midpoint is at $r=0.10\,\mathrm m$ from each source, half the separation, so the two contributions have the same denominator $r^2=1.0\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The contribution of $Q_1$ is larger than that of $Q_2$, because $Q_1>Q_2$. The field at the midpoint therefore has a positive component of about $1.26\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and points to the right. If the two charges were equal, the two contributions would have the same magnitude and the field at the midpoint would be zero. The resultant field lies along the line joining the two charges, because both contributions are parallel to it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two equal sources $Q=+5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ are at $(-0.30,0)\,\mathrm m$ and $(0.30,0)\,\mathrm m$. A test charge $q_0=-5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at $P=(0,0.40)\,\mathrm m$. Find the field at $P$ and the force on $q_0$, using $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\sqrt{(0.30)^2+(0.40)^2}\,\mathrm m=0.50\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
E_1=E_2&=k_e\frac Q{r^2}=\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(5.0\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.50)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}=179.8\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
E_x&=E_1\frac{0.30}{0.50}-E_2\frac{0.30}{0.50}=0\\[6pt]
E_y&=2E_1\frac{0.40}{0.50}=287.68\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
F_x&=0,\qquad F_y=q_0E_y\simeq-1.44\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="-3,0,pieno,+Q;3,0,pieno,+Q;0,4,pieno" etichette="-0.50,4.12,P" segmenti="-3,0,0,4;3,0,0,4" vettori="0,4,0.9,5.2,E1;0,4,-0.9,5.2,E2;0,4,0,6.4,E" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-0.9 ymax=7.5 titolo="Symmetry cancels the horizontal components of the field at P"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $P$ lies on the symmetry axis of the pair and is at $0.50\,\mathrm m$ from each source, so the two contributions have the same magnitude $E_1=E_2$. The horizontal components have opposite directions and magnitude $E_1\dfrac{0.30}{0.50}$, so they cancel. The vertical components are both positive, because the field of each positive source points from the charge toward $P$, and each equals $E_1$ times the ratio of the vertical leg to the hypotenuse, $\dfrac{0.40}{0.50}$. The resultant field points upward and has magnitude $287.68\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The same value is given by the formula for the field on the axis of a pair, $\dfrac{2k_eQy}{(y^2+a^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$, with $a=0.30\,\mathrm m$ and $y=0.40\,\mathrm m$. Since $q_0$ is negative, the force $q_0\vec E$ is opposite to the field and its magnitude is about $1.44\,\mathrm{\mu N}$ downward. If $q_0$ were positive, the force would have the same magnitude and would point upward, along the field. The same holds at every point of the symmetry axis, where the horizontal components cancel.

</div>
