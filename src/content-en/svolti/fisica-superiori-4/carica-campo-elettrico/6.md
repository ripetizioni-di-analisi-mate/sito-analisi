## Exercise 1

Two positive charges $Q$ and $4Q$ are at $x=0$ and $x=0.36\,\mathrm m$ respectively. Find the coordinate of the point where the total field is zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{k_eQ}{x^2}&=\frac{4k_eQ}{(d-x)^2},\qquad 0<x<d\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{x}&=\frac{2}{d-x}\\[6pt]
d-x&=2x\\[6pt]
x&=\frac d3=\frac{0.36}{3}\,\mathrm m=0.12\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{(0.12)^2}&=\frac{4}{(0.24)^2}=69.4\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,0,pieno,+Q;1,0,pieno,P;3,0,pieno,+4Q" vettori="1,-0.5,1.7,-0.5,E1;1,-0.5,0.3,-0.5,E2" xmin=-0.7 xmax=3.8 ymin=-1.25 ymax=1.1 titolo="At P the distances from the sources are 12 cm and 24 cm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Between the two positive sources the fields point in opposite directions, whereas outside, on either side, they point the same way and their sum is not zero. We therefore look for the interior point where the magnitudes coincide. The common factor $k_eQ$ is cancelled and the square root is taken, choosing the positive root because $x$ and $d-x$ are distances. This gives $d-x=2x$, that is, $x=\dfrac d3$. The charge four times as strong must therefore be at double the distance to give the same field. The point found is $0.12\,\mathrm m$ from $Q$ and $0.24\,\mathrm m$ from $4Q$. The magnitudes coincide, because $\dfrac{1}{(0.12)^2}$ and $\dfrac{4}{(0.24)^2}$ both equal $69.4\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two charges $+Q$ and $-4Q$ are at $x=0$ and $x=0.45\,\mathrm m$. Find the coordinate of the point where the field is zero and justify the region chosen.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=-x>0,\qquad d=0.45\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
\frac{k_eQ}{s^2}&=\frac{4k_eQ}{(s+d)^2}\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{s}&=\frac{2}{s+d}\\[6pt]
s+d&=2s\\[6pt]
s&=d=0.45\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
x&=-0.45\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Between the two charges the fields have the same direction, both to the right, so their sum is not zero. To the right of $-4Q$ the fields are opposite, but that charge has the larger magnitude and is also closer, so its field always prevails. The region to the left of $+Q$ remains, where the field of $+Q$ points left and that of $-4Q$ points right. If $s$ is the distance from $+Q$, the distance from $-4Q$ is $s+d$, so the sum $s+d$ appears in the equality of the magnitudes. Taking the square root gives $s+d=2s$ and hence $s=d$. The point is $0.45\,\mathrm m$ from $+Q$ and $0.90\,\mathrm m$ from $-4Q$, and has coordinate $x=-0.45\,\mathrm m$. The magnitudes coincide, because $\dfrac{1}{(0.45)^2}=\dfrac{4}{(0.90)^2}=4.94\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$. The point is closer to $+Q$, the charge of smaller magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A charge $Q_1=+1.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at $x=0$ and a charge $Q_2=-9.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at $x=0.20\,\mathrm m$. Find the coordinate of the point where the field is zero and the magnitude of the field of each charge at that point. Use $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\frac{d\sqrt{|Q_1|}}{\sqrt{|Q_2|}-\sqrt{|Q_1|}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.20\cdot\sqrt{1.0}}{\sqrt{9.0}-\sqrt{1.0}}\,\mathrm m=\frac{0.20}{3-1}\,\mathrm m=0.10\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
x&=-s=-0.10\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
E_1&=k_e\frac{|Q_1|}{s^2}=\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(1.0\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.10)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}=899\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
E_2&=k_e\frac{|Q_2|}{(s+d)^2}=\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(9.0\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.30)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}=899\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The charges have opposite signs and $|Q_1|<|Q_2|$, so by the theorem on the zero-field point the field vanishes at a single point outside the segment, on the side of the charge of smaller magnitude, that is, to the left of $Q_1$. In that region the distance from $Q_1$ is $s$ and that from $Q_2$ is $s+d=0.30\,\mathrm m$. The formula gives $s=0.10\,\mathrm m$, so the point has coordinate $x=-0.10\,\mathrm m$. The field of the positive $Q_1$ points to the left, away from $Q_1$, and that of the negative $Q_2$ points toward $Q_2$, that is, to the right. The two directions are opposite. The magnitudes computed are both $899\,\mathrm{N/C}$, so the total field is zero. With $|Q_2|=|Q_1|$ the point would not exist, whereas with $|Q_2|=4|Q_1|$ we would have $s=d$, as in the previous exercise.

</div>
