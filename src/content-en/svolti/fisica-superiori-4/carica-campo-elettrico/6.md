## Exercise 1

Positive charges $Q$ and $4Q$ are at $x=0$ and $x=0.36\,\mathrm m$, respectively. Find the coordinate of the point where the total field is zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{k_eQ}{x^2}&=\frac{4k_eQ}{(d-x)^2},\qquad 0<x<d\\[5pt]
d-x&=2x\\
x&=\frac d3\\
&=\frac{0.36}{3}\,\mathrm m=0.12\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,0,pieno,+Q;1,0,pieno,P;3,0,pieno,+4Q" vettori="1,-0.5,1.7,-0.5,E1;1,-0.5,0.3,-0.5,E2" xmin=-0.7 xmax=3.8 ymin=-1.25 ymax=1.1 titolo="At P the distances from the sources are 12 cm and 24 cm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Between the sources the fields point in opposite directions. On each side outside them, the fields point in the same direction. Look for the interior point where the magnitudes match. Cancel the common factor $k_eQ$ and take the positive square root because the distances $x$ and $d-x$ are positive. The charge four times as strong must be twice as far away to give the same field. The point is $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $Q$ and $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $4Q$, as expected.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Charges $+Q$ and $-4Q$ are at $x=0$ and $x=0.45\,\mathrm m$. Find the coordinate where the total field vanishes and justify the region selected.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=-x>0,\qquad d=0.45\,\mathrm m\\
\frac{k_eQ}{s^2}&=\frac{4k_eQ}{(s+d)^2}\\[5pt]
s+d&=2s\\
s&=d\\
x&=-0.45\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Between the charges both fields point right, so they cannot cancel. To the right of $-4Q$ the fields point in opposite directions, but this charge has the larger magnitude and is closer. This leaves the region to the left of $+Q$. There, the field of $+Q$ points left and that of $-4Q$ points right. The distances are $s$ and $s+d$, so the equation for the magnitudes uses the sum $s+d$. The point is $0.45\,\mathrm m$ from the first charge and $0.90\,\mathrm m$ from the second. The distance ratio compensates for the factor of four in the charge magnitudes.

</div>
