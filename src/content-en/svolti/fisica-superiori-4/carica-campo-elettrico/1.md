## Exercise 1

An object has $N=3.2\cdot10^{12}$ excess electrons relative to neutrality. With $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$, calculate the net charge and express it in microcoulombs as well.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=-Ne\\
&=-(3.2\cdot10^{12})(1.60\cdot10^{-19})\,\mathrm C\\
&=-5.12\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm C\\
&=-0.512\,\mathrm{\mu C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-160*x" punti="3.2,-512,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=4.6 ymin=-900 ymax=100 nomi="N,q (nC)" titolo="The N axis counts excess electrons in trillions. Charge is in nanocoulombs"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the neutral object the charge of the original electrons balanced the positive charge. Each added electron contributes $-e$, so multiply the number added by the charge of one electron. The powers of ten give $10^{12-19}=10^{-7}$. Since one microcoulomb equals $10^{-6}\,\mathrm C$, rewrite $5.12\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm C$ as $0.512\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm C$. The negative sign stays when changing units. The graph shows how the signed charge depends on the number of added electrons.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object $A$ has $4.0\cdot10^{12}$ excess electrons and an object $B$ is neutral. Half the excess electrons are transferred from $A$ to $B$ while the pair is isolated. With $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$, calculate both final charges.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{A,i}&=-(4.0\cdot10^{12})(1.60\cdot10^{-19})\,\mathrm C\\
&=-6.40\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm C\\[5pt]
q_{B,f}&=-(2.0\cdot10^{12})(1.60\cdot10^{-19})\,\mathrm C\\
&=-3.20\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm C\\[5pt]
q_{A,f}&=q_{A,i}-q_{B,f}\\
&=-3.20\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm C.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The transfer involves half the excess electrons, so $B$ receives $2.0\cdot10^{12}$ electrons. Since it was neutral, its final charge equals the negative charge it receives. The total charge stays equal to the initial charge of $A$ because no charge crosses the system boundary. Subtract the final charge of $B$ from the total to find that of $A$. The result agrees with direct counting. The electrons left over on $A$ are as numerous as those received by $B$.

</div>
