## Exercise 1

In vacuum, $q_1=+1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$ lies $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the left of $q_2=-3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. Use $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$ and calculate the horizontal component of the force on $q_2$, taking right as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{1\to2,x}&=k_e\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}\\
&=\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(1.6\cdot10^{-9})(-3.0\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.12)^2}\,\mathrm N\\
&\simeq-3.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,0,pieno,q1 (+);4,0,pieno,q2 (−)" vettori="0.4,0,1.8,0,F on q1;3.6,0,2.2,0,F on q2" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.4 titolo="Charges 12 cm apart. The forces point toward each other"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The force acts along the horizontal line joining the sources. From $q_1$ to $q_2$ is to the right, so the unit vector in the formula points along the positive axis. The product of the charges is negative, giving a negative force component on $q_2$. That sign means the force points left, toward the positive charge attracting it. Convert nanocoulombs to coulombs and centimeters to meters before substituting. The force on $q_1$ instead points to the right, acting on the other body.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two positive charges $q_1=2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q_2=3.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ are $0.12\,\mathrm m$ apart in vacuum. The second is to the right of the first. Find the field of $q_1$ at $q_2$ and the force on $q_2$. Use $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_x&=k_e\frac{q_1}{r^2}\\
&=\frac{8.99\cdot10^9\cdot2.0\cdot10^{-9}}{(0.12)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}\\
&\simeq1.25\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
F_x&=q_2E_x\simeq3.75\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To distinguish field from force, start with the effect of the source $q_1$ alone. To the right of a positive source, the field points outward to the right. Its magnitude follows from the squared distance $r^2$. Find the force on $q_2$ by multiplying the field by the second charge. The positive sign of $q_2$ preserves the rightward direction. The magnitude agrees with applying Coulomb's law directly to the two charges.

</div>
