## Exercise 1

A particle of charge $q=-1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$ is in a uniform field of $450\,\mathrm{N/C}$ pointing right. Calculate the horizontal force component, taking right as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_x&=+450\,\mathrm{N/C}\\
F_x&=qE_x\\
&=(-1.6\cdot10^{-9})\cdot450\,\mathrm N\\
&=-7.2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm N=-0.72\,\mathrm{\mu N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,0,pieno,q < 0" vettori="0,0.45,2.2,0.45,E;0,-0.45,-2.2,-0.45,F" xmin=-3.0 xmax=3.0 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.45 titolo="The field and force point in opposite directions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The field component is positive because the axis points along the field. The force is found by multiplying the field vector by the charge. Here the factor is negative, so it reverses the vector direction. The minus sign shows that the force points left. Its magnitude is $0.72\,\mathrm{\mu N}$. For a unit check, coulombs multiplied by newtons per coulomb give newtons.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle of charge $-2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and mass $1.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{kg}$ is in a uniform field with components $(E_x,E_y)=(450,600)\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Neglecting other forces, calculate the acceleration components and magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac qm&=-2.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{C/kg}\\
a_x&=\frac qm E_x=-0.90\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\
a_y&=\frac qm E_y=-1.20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[5pt]
|\vec a|&=\sqrt{(-0.90)^2+(-1.20)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\
&=1.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since only the electric force acts, Newton's second law gives $\vec a=\dfrac qm\vec E$. The ratio $q/m$ is negative and multiplies both components. A field pointing right and upward therefore gives an acceleration pointing left and downward. The components are perpendicular, so the magnitude is the square root of the sum of their squares. As a check, the field magnitude is $750\,\mathrm{N/C}$, and multiplying it by the magnitude of $q/m$ gives the same $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

</div>
