## Exercise 1

A particle of charge $q=-1.6\,\mathrm{nC}$ is in a uniform field of $450\,\mathrm{N/C}$ pointing to the right. Find the horizontal component of the force, with the right as the positive direction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F&=q\vec E\\[6pt]
E_x&=+450\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
F_x&=qE_x\\[6pt]
&=(-1.6\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm C)(450\,\mathrm{N/C})\\[6pt]
&=-7.2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
&=-0.72\,\mathrm{\mu N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,0,pieno,q < 0" vettori="0,0.45,2.2,0.45,E;0,-0.45,-2.2,-0.45,F" xmin=-3.0 xmax=3.0 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.45 titolo="The field and the force have opposite directions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The field has a positive component because the axis was oriented along it. The force on a charge in a field is obtained by multiplying the field vector by the charge, $\vec F=q\vec E$, and this relation holds for the components along the axis. The factor $q$ is negative, so it reverses the direction of the vector and the component $F_x$ is negative. The force therefore points to the left, opposite to the field, and has magnitude $0.72\,\mathrm{\mu N}$. The product of the coefficients is $1.6\cdot450=720$, which with the power $10^{-9}$ gives $7.2\cdot10^{-7}$. The units are $\mathrm{C}\cdot\mathrm{N/C}=\mathrm N$. With a positive charge of the same magnitude the field would be unchanged and the force would point right, along the field.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle of charge $-2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and mass $1.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{kg}$ is in a uniform field with components $(E_x,E_y)=(450,600)\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Neglecting other forces, find the components and the magnitude of the acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec a&=\frac{q}{m}\vec E\\[6pt]
\frac qm&=\frac{-2.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm C}{1.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{kg}}=-2.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{C/kg}\\[6pt]
a_x&=\frac qm E_x=-0.90\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
a_y&=\frac qm E_y=-1.20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
|\vec a|&=\sqrt{(-0.90)^2+(-1.20)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{2.25}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since only the electric force acts, Newton's second law $m\vec a=q\vec E$ gives $\vec a=\dfrac qm\vec E$. The ratio $q/m$ is negative and multiplies both components of the field, so a field pointing right and upward produces an acceleration pointing left and downward. The two components are orthogonal, so the magnitude is found with the square root of the sum of the squares, and $0.81+1.44=2.25$. The acceleration therefore has magnitude $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. As a check, the magnitude of the field is $\sqrt{450^2+600^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}=750\,\mathrm{N/C}$ and the product with $|q|/m=2.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{C/kg}$ gives the same value, $1.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The acceleration makes with the $x$ axis an angle whose tangent is $\dfrac{a_y}{a_x}=\dfrac{4}{3}$, the same ratio as the components of the field, because $\vec a$ and $\vec E$ are proportional.

</div>
