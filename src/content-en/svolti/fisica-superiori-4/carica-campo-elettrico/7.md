## Exercise 1

An electron is in a uniform field of $96\,\mathrm{N/C}$ pointing to the right. Neglect other forces. With $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$, calculate the acceleration along the axis pointing right.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_x&=(-e)E_x\\
&=-(1.60\cdot10^{-19})\cdot96\,\mathrm N\\
&=-1.536\cdot10^{-17}\,\mathrm N\\[5pt]
a_x&=\frac{F_x}{m_e}\\
&\simeq-1.69\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="0,0,pieno,e⁻" vettori="0,0.45,2,0.45,E;0,-0.45,-2,-0.45,a" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.35 titolo="The electron accelerates opposite to the field"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The force points left because the electron's charge is negative and the field points right. Acceleration has the same direction as force because mass is positive. Its magnitude is large because the mass in the denominator is very small. For the units, newtons divided by kilograms gives $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. If released from rest, the electron would begin moving to the left. With an initial velocity to the right, it would first slow down.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron starts at the origin with $v_{0x}=4.0\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_{0y}=0$. A uniform field of $120\,\mathrm{N/C}$ points upward. Neglecting other forces, calculate $x$ and $y$ after $1.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm s$, with $y$ pointing up. Use $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_x&=0,\qquad a_y=\frac{-eE_y}{m_e}\simeq-2.11\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[5pt]
x&=v_{0x}t=4.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm m\\[5pt]
y&=\frac12 a_yt^2\\
&\simeq-\frac{2.11\cdot10^{13}\cdot(10^{-9})^2}{2}\,\mathrm m\\
&\simeq-1.05\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The electric force points downward. Its horizontal component is zero and the velocity along $x$ stays constant. Vertically, the electron starts with zero velocity and gains downward velocity. The vertical displacement varies with time squared, so $10^{-9}\,\mathrm s$ is squared. The negative sign of $y$ means the final position is below the origin. The magnitude of the vertical velocity after the given time is about $2.11\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Both velocity components are much smaller than the speed of light.

</div>
