## Exercise 1

A point source $Q=+3.2\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at the origin. In vacuum, observe point $A$ at $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ on the right and point $B$ at $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ on the left. With $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$, calculate both horizontal field components with right as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=k_e\frac{Q}{r^2}\\
&=\frac{8.99\cdot10^9\cdot3.2\cdot10^{-9}}{(0.15)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}\\
&\simeq1.28\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[5pt]
E_{A,x}&=+E,\qquad E_{B,x}=-E.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="-2,0,pieno,B;0,0,pieno,+Q;2,0,pieno,A" vettori="-2,-0.2,-3.4,-0.2,E_B;2,-0.2,3.4,-0.2,E_A" xmin=-4.2 xmax=4.2 ymin=-1.0 ymax=1.1 titolo="A and B are 15 cm from the source. The fields point in opposite directions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The points are equally far from the source, so they have equal field magnitudes. The source's sign makes the field point radially outward. At $A$, outward points right and the component is positive. At $B$, outward points left and the component is negative. Calculate the magnitude once and assign the two signs according to the unit vectors. The components are opposite even though the source is the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A charge $Q=-4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at the origin. A test electron is $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ above it. Calculate the vertical field and force components, taking upward as positive. Use $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$ and $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_y&=k_e\frac{Q}{r^2}\\
&=\frac{8.99\cdot10^9\cdot(-4.0\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.15)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}\\
&\simeq-1.60\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[5pt]
F_y&=(-e)E_y\simeq+2.56\cdot10^{-16}\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unit vector from the source to the point goes upward. The source is negative, so the field goes downward and its component is negative. To find the force, multiply this value by the electron charge, which is also negative. The two signs cancel, so the force points upward. The electron is repelled by the like-charged source. If a positive charge of equal magnitude were put in its place, the field would stay the same and the force would point down.

</div>
