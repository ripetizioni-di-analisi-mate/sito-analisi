## Exercise 1

A point source $Q=+3.2\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at the origin. In vacuum, the points $A$ at $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the right and $B$ at $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the left are observed. With $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$, find the two horizontal components of the field with the right taken as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=k_e\frac{Q}{r^2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(3.2\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.15)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{28.77}{2.25\cdot10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
&\simeq1.28\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[10pt]
E_{A,x}&=+E=+1.28\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C},\\[6pt]
E_{B,x}&=-E=-1.28\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 punti="-2,0,pieno,B;0,0,pieno,+Q;2,0,pieno,A" vettori="-2,-0.2,-3.4,-0.2,E_B;2,-0.2,3.4,-0.2,E_A" xmin=-4.2 xmax=4.2 ymin=-1.0 ymax=1.1 titolo="A and B are 15 cm from the source. The field directions are opposite"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The field of a point source has magnitude $k_e\dfrac{|Q|}{r^2}$, and the two points are at the same distance $r=0.15\,\mathrm m$ from the source, so they have the same field magnitude. The magnitude is therefore computed only once, converting centimeters to meters, and then the sign is assigned to each point. The source is positive, so the field is radially outward. At $A$ the outward direction is to the right and the component is positive. At $B$ the outward direction is to the left and the component is negative. The two components are therefore opposite, even though the source is the same and the distance is equal. The field at $A$ is about $1.28\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$ to the right and the one at $B$ has the same magnitude to the left. At double the distance, $30\,\mathrm{cm}$, the field would drop to one fourth, about $3.2\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}$. The sign of the component is determined by the side on which the point lies relative to the source.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A charge $Q=-4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is at the origin. A test electron is $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ above the origin. Find the vertical components of the field and of the electric force, with the positive direction upward. Use $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$ and $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_y&=k_e\frac{Q}{r^2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{(8.99\cdot10^9)(-4.0\cdot10^{-9})}{(0.15)^2}\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[6pt]
&\simeq-1.60\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}\\[10pt]
F_y&=qE_y=(-e)E_y\\[6pt]
&=(-1.60\cdot10^{-19})(-1.60\cdot10^3)\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
&\simeq+2.56\cdot10^{-16}\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unit vector from the source to the point points upward, so the vertical component of the field is $k_e\dfrac{Q}{r^2}$ with the sign of $Q$. The source is negative, so the field points downward and its component is negative. The force on the electron is obtained from the relation $\vec F=q\vec E$, multiplying the field by the charge of the electron, $-e$. This charge is also negative, so the two signs cancel and the component of the force is positive. The force therefore points upward and has magnitude about $2.56\cdot10^{-16}\,\mathrm N$. The electron is repelled by the source because the two charges have the same sign. If a positive charge of equal magnitude were put in its place, the field at the point would be the same and the force would point the opposite way, that is, downward. The magnitude of the force can also be found as the product $eE=(1.60\cdot10^{-19})(1.60\cdot10^3)\,\mathrm N$ and coincides with the value found. The value of $E_y$ is the same for any charge placed at that point, because the field is determined by the source alone.

</div>
