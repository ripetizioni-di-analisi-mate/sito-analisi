## Exercise 1

A grating has spacing $d=2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ and is illuminated at normal incidence with $\lambda=400\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find the angle of the first positive maximum.

$$
d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sin\theta_1=\frac{\lambda}{d}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_1&=\frac{0.400\,\mathrm{\mu m}}{2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}}
             =0.20\\[6pt]
\theta_1&=\arcsin(0.20)\simeq11.5^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a direction of maximum, two adjacent slits contribute in phase. For the first order their path difference $d\sin\theta$ is one wavelength, so $d\sin\theta_1=\lambda$. Both lengths are expressed in micrometers, so $400\,\mathrm{nm}=0.400\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ and the ratio is dimensionless. The ratio found is the sine of the angle. The value $0.20$ is less than one, so the first order exists, and the arcsine gives **about $11.5^\circ$**. If $0.20$ were the angle in degrees, we would obtain $0.20^\circ$, about sixty times smaller. For the second order we would have $\sin\theta_2=0.40$ and $\theta_2\simeq23.6^\circ$. The allowed positive orders satisfy $m<\dfrac{d}{\lambda}=5$, so they go up to $m=4$. With $\lambda=700\,\mathrm{nm}$ we would have $\sin\theta_1=0.35$ and $\theta_1\simeq20.5^\circ$, so light of longer wavelength is deflected more.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A grating of spacing $2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ receives light with $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$ at normal incidence. Find the positive angles of the first two orders.

$$
\sin\theta_m=\frac{m\lambda}{d},\qquad m=1,2
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_1&=\frac{0.500}{2.0}=0.25
              \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\theta_1\simeq14.5^\circ\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_2&=\frac{2\cdot0.500}{2.0}=0.50
              \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\theta_2=30^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sine of the angle of the maximum of order $m$ is $\dfrac{m\lambda}{d}$, so the second order requires twice the path difference of the first and doubles the sine. The angle does not double, because the arcsine function is not linear: going from $\sin\theta=0.25$ to $\sin\theta=0.50$ we pass from about $14.5^\circ$ to $30^\circ$. Both maxima have an allowed direction of propagation because the two sines are less than one. In the graph the maxima are equally spaced in the variable $s=\sin\theta$ and not in the angle. The fourth order would have $\sin\theta=1$, so it is grazing and only the orders up to the third reach the plane screen. The first two angles are **$14.5^\circ$ and $30^\circ$**. The third order has $\sin\theta_3=0.75$, so $\theta_3\simeq48.6^\circ$, and the angles between successive orders grow larger and larger.

</div>
