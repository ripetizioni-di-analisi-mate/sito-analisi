## Exercise 1

A grating has spacing $d=2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ and is illuminated at normal incidence with $\lambda=400\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find the angle of the first positive maximum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_1&=\frac{\lambda}{d}=\frac{0.400}{2.0}
             =0.20\\[6pt]
\theta_1&=\arcsin(0.20)\simeq11.5^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a maximum direction, adjacent slits contribute in phase. At first order their path difference is one wavelength. Compute the ratio of wavelength to grating spacing after expressing both in micrometers. The ratio is the sine, so the angle is about $11.5^\circ$, not $0.20^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A grating of spacing $2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ receives light of wavelength $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$ at normal incidence. Find the positive angles of the first two orders.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_1&=\frac{0.500}{2.0}=0.25
              \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\theta_1\simeq14.5^\circ\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_2&=\frac{2\cdot0.500}{2.0}=0.50
              \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\theta_2=30^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Second order requires twice the path difference of first order, so the sine of the angle doubles. The inverse sine is nonlinear, so doubling the first angle does not give the second. Here moving from sine $0.25$ to $0.50$ takes the angle from about $14.5^\circ$ to $30^\circ$. Both maxima have allowed propagation directions because the two sines are less than one.

</div>
