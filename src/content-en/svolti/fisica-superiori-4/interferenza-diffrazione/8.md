## Exercise 1

A grating contains $320$ lines per millimeter. Find the spacing in meters.

$$
d=\frac{1}{n},\qquad n=320\ \text{lines/mm}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{1\,\mathrm{mm}}{320}=\frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}{320}=\frac{1000\,\mathrm{\mu m}}{320}\\[6pt]
 &=3.125\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m=3.125\,\mathrm{\mu m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of lines per millimeter is a linear density, that is, the number of lines per unit length. The distance between two consecutive lines is the reciprocal of the density, because $320$ equal intervals fit in $1\,\mathrm{mm}$. So the spacing is $\dfrac{1}{320}\,\mathrm{mm}$. It is then converted to meters by multiplying by $10^{-3}$, because $1\,\mathrm{mm}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$. The same result is obtained by first expressing the density as $320\,000$ lines per meter and taking its reciprocal. The spacing is **$3.125\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$**, that is, just over three micrometers, about six wavelengths of $500\,\mathrm{nm}$ light. A grating with $500$ lines per millimeter has $d=2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, smaller than $3.125\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, because it has more lines in the same millimeter. As a check of the reciprocal, $320\cdot3.125\,\mathrm{\mu m}=1000\,\mathrm{\mu m}=1\,\mathrm{mm}$, that is, $320$ spacings cover one millimeter.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A grating contains $400$ lines per millimeter and is illuminated at normal incidence with $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find the spacing and the angle of the first positive maximum.

$$
d=\frac{1}{n},\qquad \sin\theta_1=\frac{\lambda}{d}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{10^{-3}}{400}\,\mathrm m
   =2.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_1&=\frac{600\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m}{2.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m}
             =0.24\\[6pt]
\theta_1&=\arcsin(0.24)\simeq13.9^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,1000/t,200,600" xmin=0 xmax=640 ymin=0 ymax=6 nomi="n,d" punti="320,3.125,pieno;400,2.5,pieno" legenda="d=\dfrac{1}{n}" titolo="The number of lines $n$ is in lines per millimeter and the spacing $d$ in micrometers. The points correspond to $320$ and $400$ lines per millimeter."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The line density determines the spacing to use in the grating equation $d\sin\theta_1=\lambda$. After conversion to meters the ratio $\dfrac{\lambda}{d}=0.24$ is less than one, so the first order exists. The ratio is the sine of the angle and the arcsine gives **about $13.9^\circ$**. The graph shows that more lines per millimeter correspond to a smaller spacing. At fixed wavelength a smaller spacing makes the sine of the first angle larger, as long as the first order remains allowed, that is, as long as $d>\lambda$. The ratio $\dfrac{d}{\lambda}=4.17$ indicates that the positive maxima go up to order $m=4$, because $\sin\theta_4=0.96<1$. With $320$ lines per millimeter the same grating would give $\sin\theta_1=\dfrac{0.600}{3.125}=0.192$, that is, a smaller angle.

</div>
