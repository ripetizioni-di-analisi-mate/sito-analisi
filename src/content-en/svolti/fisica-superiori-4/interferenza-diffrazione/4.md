## Exercise 1

In a Young experiment $\lambda=550\,\mathrm{nm}$, $L=1.44\,\mathrm m$, and $d=0.25\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find the position of the maximum $m=+2$ for small angles.

$$
d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda,\qquad y_m\simeq L\sin\theta_m\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y_m=\frac{m\lambda L}{d}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_2&=\frac{2\lambda L}{d}
    =\frac{2\cdot(550\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m)\cdot(1.44\,\mathrm m)}{0.25\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}\\[6pt]
    &=\frac{1.584\cdot10^{-6}}{0.25\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
    =6.336\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m\simeq6.3\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(pi*x/3.168)^2" xmin=-10 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=1.15 nomi="y,I/Imax" punti="0,1,pieno;3.168,1,pieno;6.336,1,pieno" legenda="I/I_{\max}" titolo="The coordinates $y$ are in millimeters. The second positive maximum is $6.3\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the center, that is, two fringe spacings away."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The center corresponds to order zero, where the two paths coincide. At the maximum of order $m=2$ the path difference is $2\lambda$, so $d\sin\theta_2=2\lambda$. For small angles the coordinate on the screen is $y\simeq L\sin\theta$, and substituting gives $y_2=\dfrac{2\lambda L}{d}$. All lengths are converted to meters, the numerator equals $1.584\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and dividing by $d$ leaves a length in meters. The positive sign places the maximum on the side of increasing $y$. The ratio $\dfrac{y_2}{L}=4.4\cdot10^{-3}$ is much smaller than one, so the relation $y\simeq L\sin\theta$ is consistent with the data. The second positive maximum is **about $6.3\,\mathrm{mm}$** from the center. The maximum $m=+1$ is at half of this distance, that is, at $3.2\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the first minimum at $1.6\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a Young experiment with $\lambda=550\,\mathrm{nm}$, $L=1.8\,\mathrm m$, and $d=0.25\,\mathrm{mm}$, find the position of the maximum $m=+2$ and the distance between two adjacent maxima for small angles.

$$
\Delta y=y_{m+1}-y_m=\frac{\lambda L}{d},\qquad y_2=2\Delta y
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y&=\frac{(550\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m)\cdot(1.8\,\mathrm m)}{0.25\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}
          =3.96\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m
          \simeq4.0\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
y_2&=2\cdot3.96\,\mathrm{mm}=7.92\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq7.9\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To obtain both quantities we first find the spacing between orders, because in the small-angle model the maxima are equally spaced: the coordinate of order $m$ is $m\Delta y$. The calculation, before rounding, gives $\Delta y=3.96\,\mathrm{mm}$. The maximum of order $2$ is two such intervals from the center and equals $7.92\,\mathrm{mm}$. The unrounded value is used in the second step, because $2\cdot4.0\,\mathrm{mm}=8.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ would differ in the second digit. The fringe spacing is a distance and is therefore positive, whereas the maximum $m=-2$ on the other side has coordinate $y_{-2}=-y_2=-7.9\,\mathrm{mm}$. Compared with the previous exercise, $L$ is larger by a factor $\dfrac{1.8}{1.44}=1.25$, so the fringes are farther apart by the same factor: $6.336\,\mathrm{mm}\cdot1.25=7.92\,\mathrm{mm}$. Moreover $\dfrac{y_2}{L}=\dfrac{7.92\cdot10^{-3}}{1.8}=4.4\cdot10^{-3}$ confirms that the angle is small. The maximum $m=3$ would be at $3\cdot3.96\,\mathrm{mm}=11.9\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>
