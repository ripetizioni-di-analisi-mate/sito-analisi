## Exercise 1

In Young's experiment, $\lambda=550\,\mathrm{nm}$, $L=1.44\,\mathrm m$, and $d=0.25\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find the position of maximum $m=+2$ for small angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_2&=\frac{2\lambda L}{d}
    =\frac{2(550\cdot10^{-9})\cdot1.44}{0.25\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
    \simeq6.3\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center is the zeroth order. At the second positive maximum the path difference is $2\lambda$, so the position is twice the fringe spacing. The positive sign places the maximum on the side of increasing $y$. The magnitude is small compared with $1.44\,\mathrm m$, so the relation $y\simeq L\sin\theta$ agrees with the data.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In Young's experiment with $\lambda=550\,\mathrm{nm}$, $L=1.8\,\mathrm m$, and $d=0.25\,\mathrm{mm}$, calculate the position of maximum $m=+2$ and the spacing between adjacent maxima for small angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y&=\frac{(550\cdot10^{-9})\cdot1.8}{0.25\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
          \simeq4.0\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
y_2&=2\Delta y\simeq7.9\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find both quantities, first calculate the spacing between orders. Before rounding, the calculation gives $\Delta y=3.96\,\mathrm{mm}$. Two of these intervals give $y_2=7.92\,\mathrm{mm}$. Fringe spacing is always a positive distance, whereas the coordinate of the second maximum on the other side would be $y_{-2}=-y_2$.

</div>
