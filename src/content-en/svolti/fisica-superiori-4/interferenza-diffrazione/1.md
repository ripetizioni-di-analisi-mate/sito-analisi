## Exercise 1

Light in vacuum has wavelength $\lambda_0=4.4\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm m$. Find the frequency using $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{c}{\lambda_0}=\frac{3.00\cdot10^8}{4.4\cdot10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{Hz}\\[6pt]
  &=\frac{3.00}{4.4}\cdot10^{15}\,\mathrm{Hz}
  \simeq6.8\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,3000/t,380,780" xmin=0 xmax=810 ymin=0 ymax=9 nomi="λ₀,f" punti="440,6.818,pieno" legenda="f=\dfrac{c}{\lambda_0}" titolo="Wavelength in nanometers and frequency in units of $10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

One oscillation occupies a distance $\lambda_0$ in the direction of travel, so $f$ counts how many of these distances are covered each second. In the numerical expression, dividing by $10^{-7}$ increases the exponent by seven. Dividing $\mathrm{m/s}$ by $\mathrm m$ gives $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, or hertz. The point on the graph represents the pair $440\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $6.8\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Light of vacuum wavelength $\lambda_0=5.5\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm m$ enters a stationary medium with $n=1.50$. Find its frequency and wavelength in the medium. Use $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{3.00\cdot10^8}{5.5\cdot10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{Hz}
 \simeq5.5\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}\\[6pt]
\lambda&=\frac{\lambda_0}{n}=\frac{550}{1.50}\,\mathrm{nm}
 \simeq3.7\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{nm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find the frequency from the vacuum data. It is unchanged at the interface. In the medium the speed drops to $\dfrac cn$, and with the same number of oscillations per second, the distance between successive crests decreases. This is why the wavelength is divided by $n$, while the frequency remains the one calculated from $\lambda_0$. The conversion $5.5\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm m=550\,\mathrm{nm}$ makes the two wavelengths easy to compare.

</div>
