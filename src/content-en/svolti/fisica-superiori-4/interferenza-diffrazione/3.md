## Exercise 1

Two thin slits are separated by $d=0.24\,\mathrm{mm}$ and are illuminated in phase with $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is at $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$. Find the fringe spacing in the small-angle model.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y&=\frac{\lambda L}{d}
          =\frac{(600\cdot10^{-9})\cdot2.0}{0.24\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
          &=5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m=5.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(pi*x/5)^2" xmin=-11 xmax=11 ymin=0 ymax=1.15 nomi="y,I/Imax" punti="-5,1;0,1;5,1" legenda="I/I_{\max}" titolo="Coordinates $y$ are in millimeters. The maxima near the center are $5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Adjacent maxima differ by one in order, so their path difference changes by one wavelength. At small angles, the interval on the screen is $\dfrac{\lambda L}{d}$. Convert nanometers and millimeters to meters before forming the ratio. For near-center fringes, $\dfrac{\Delta y}{L}=2.5\cdot10^{-3}$ confirms that the angle is small.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Young's setup uses $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$, $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$, and $d=0.30\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find the fringe spacing and the position of the first positive minimum for equal slit amplitudes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y&=\frac{(600\cdot10^{-9})\cdot2.0}{0.30\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
          =4.0\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
y_{\mathrm{min},1}&=\frac{\Delta y}{2}=2.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the central maximum the two paths have equal length. At the first positive maximum the path difference is $\lambda$, so the fringe spacing is $4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The first minimum instead requires $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$. In the small-angle model the path difference is proportional to $y$, so this minimum lies halfway between them. The equal amplitudes make the resulting field zero there.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A double slit with $d=0.40\,\mathrm{mm}$ produces fringes spaced $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart. With the same screen position and wavelength, a new plate has $d=0.60\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the individual openings have the same width as before. Find the new fringe spacing.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta y_{\mathrm{new}}}{\Delta y_{\mathrm{old}}}
 &=\frac{0.40}{0.60}=\frac{2}{3}\\[6pt]
\Delta y_{\mathrm{new}}&=\frac{2}{3}\cdot3.0\,\mathrm{mm}=2.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $\lambda$ and $L$ stay fixed, compare only the separation of the two slit pairs. Fringe spacing varies with the reciprocal of $d$, so increasing the separation by a factor of $\dfrac{3}{2}$ reduces the spacing to two thirds. The diffraction envelope keeps its width because the individual openings have the same width.

</div>
