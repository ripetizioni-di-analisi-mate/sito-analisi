## Exercise 1

Two thin slits are separated by $d=0.24\,\mathrm{mm}$ and are illuminated in phase with $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is at $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$. Find the fringe spacing in the small-angle model.

$$
\Delta y=\frac{\lambda L}{d}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y&=\frac{(600\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m)\cdot(2.0\,\mathrm m)}{0.24\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}\\[6pt]
          &=\frac{1.2\cdot10^{-6}}{0.24\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
          =5.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m=5.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(pi*x/5)^2" xmin=-11 xmax=11 ymin=0 ymax=1.15 nomi="y,I/Imax" punti="-5,1;0,1;5,1" legenda="I/I_{\max}" titolo="The coordinates $y$ are in millimeters. The maxima near the center are $5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two adjacent maxima differ by one unit in order, so their path difference changes by one wavelength. Since $d\sin\theta=m\lambda$ and for small angles $y\simeq L\sin\theta$, the coordinate of the maximum of order $m$ is $\dfrac{m\lambda L}{d}$ and the distance between two consecutive maxima is $\dfrac{\lambda L}{d}$. Nanometers and millimeters are converted to meters before forming the ratio, so the result comes out in meters. The fringe spacing is **$5.0\,\mathrm{mm}$**. The ratio $\dfrac{\Delta y}{L}=2.5\cdot10^{-3}$ is much smaller than one, so for the fringes near the center the angle is small and the approximation $\tan\theta\simeq\sin\theta$ is consistent with the data. Since $\Delta y$ is inversely proportional to $d$, halving the spacing of the slits would double the fringe spacing to $10\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In Young's setup, $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$, $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$, and $d=0.30\,\mathrm{mm}$ are used. Find the fringe spacing and the position of the first positive minimum with slits of equal amplitude.

$$
\Delta y=\frac{\lambda L}{d},\qquad d\sin\theta=\frac{\lambda}{2}\ \Longrightarrow\ y_{\mathrm{min},1}\simeq\frac{\lambda L}{2d}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y&=\frac{(600\cdot10^{-9})\cdot2.0}{0.30\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
          =4.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m=4.0\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
y_{\mathrm{min},1}&=\frac{\Delta y}{2}=2.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the central maximum the two paths coincide. At the first positive maximum the path difference is $\lambda$, so the fringe spacing is $4.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The first minimum instead requires a path difference $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$, that is, the phase shift $\pi$. In the small-angle approximation the path difference is proportional to $y$, so a halved path difference corresponds to a halved coordinate and the minimum falls halfway between the central maximum and the first off-center maximum. Since the two slits have equal amplitude, at that point the fields have equal magnitude and opposite direction and the resultant field is zero. The first positive minimum is at **$2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$** from the center. In general the positive minima are at $y=\left(m+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\Delta y$, so the second is at $\dfrac{3}{2}\cdot4.0\,\mathrm{mm}=6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A double slit with $d=0.40\,\mathrm{mm}$ gives fringes spaced $3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart. With the same screen and the same wavelength, a new plate has $d=0.60\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the individual openings have the same width as before. Find the new fringe spacing.

$$
\Delta y=\frac{\lambda L}{d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta y\,d=\lambda L=\text{constant}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y_{\mathrm{old}}\,d_{\mathrm{old}}&=\Delta y_{\mathrm{new}}\,d_{\mathrm{new}}\\[6pt]
\Delta y_{\mathrm{new}}&=\Delta y_{\mathrm{old}}\,\frac{d_{\mathrm{old}}}{d_{\mathrm{new}}}
=3.0\,\mathrm{mm}\cdot\frac{0.40}{0.60}\\[6pt]
&=3.0\,\mathrm{mm}\cdot\frac{2}{3}=2.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $\lambda$ and $L$ stay fixed, the product $\Delta y\,d$ is the same for the two plates and their values need not be known. Equating the products gives $\Delta y_{\mathrm{new}}$. The fringe spacing is inversely proportional to the slit spacing, so increasing the spacing by a factor of $\dfrac{3}{2}$ reduces the fringe spacing to two thirds, that is, to **$2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$**. The diffraction envelope keeps its own width, because it depends on the width $a$ of the individual openings, which has not changed. More fringes than before therefore fall within the same envelope. As a check, $3.0\cdot0.40=2.0\cdot0.60=1.2$ in $\mathrm{mm^2}$, and this product equals $\lambda L=1.2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, for example $600\,\mathrm{nm}$ times $2.0\,\mathrm m$. In a $12\,\mathrm{mm}$ stretch of the screen there now fall $\dfrac{12}{2.0}=6$ fringe spacings, against $\dfrac{12}{3.0}=4$ for the previous plate.

</div>
