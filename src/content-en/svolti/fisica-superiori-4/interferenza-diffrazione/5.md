## Exercise 1

A single slit of width $a=0.16\,\mathrm{mm}$ is illuminated with $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is at $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$. Find the width between the first minima in the small-angle model.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_1&=\frac{\lambda L}{a}
    =\frac{(600\cdot10^{-9})\cdot2.0}{0.16\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
    =7.5\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
w&=y_1-(-y_1)=15\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(sin(pi*x/7.5)/(pi*x/7.5))^2" xmin=-16 xmax=16 ymin=0 ymax=1.25 nomi="y,I/Imax" punti="-7.5,0;0,1;7.5,0" legenda="I/I_{\max}" titolo="Coordinate $y$ is given in millimeters. The first minima bound a width of $15\,\mathrm{mm}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

There is one opening, so the first minimum satisfies $a\sin\theta_1=\lambda$. For a distant screen and small angle, estimate $y_1$ by $\dfrac{\lambda L}{a}$. This is the distance from the center to one minimum only. The two boundaries of the central maximum have opposite coordinates, so the width includes both intervals and is $15\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a slit of width $a=0.20\,\mathrm{mm}$, use $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$. Find the central maximum width and the distance to the first positive minimum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
w&=\frac{2\lambda L}{a}
   =\frac{2(600\cdot10^{-9})\cdot2.0}{0.20\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
   =12\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
y_1&=\frac{w}{2}=6.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The full width can be obtained directly using the factor of two in the formula. Symmetry about the normal puts the first positive minimum at half the measured width. Compared with a slit $0.16\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide at the same $\lambda$ and $L$, the wider opening reduces the angle needed for the same phase difference. The central maximum therefore narrows.

</div>
