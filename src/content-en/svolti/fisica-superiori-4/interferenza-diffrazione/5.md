## Exercise 1

A single slit of width $a=0.16\,\mathrm{mm}$ is illuminated with $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The screen is at $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$. Find the width between the first minima in the small-angle model.

$$
a\sin\theta_1=\lambda,\qquad y_1\simeq L\sin\theta_1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y_1=\frac{\lambda L}{a}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_1&=\frac{(600\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m)\cdot(2.0\,\mathrm m)}{0.16\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}
    =7.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m=7.5\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
w&=y_1-(-y_1)=2y_1=15\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(sin(pi*x/7.5)/(pi*x/7.5))^2" xmin=-16 xmax=16 ymin=0 ymax=1.25 nomi="y,I/Imax" punti="-7.5,0;0,1;7.5,0" legenda="I/I_{\max}" titolo="The coordinate $y$ is expressed in millimeters. The two first minima bound a width of $15\,\mathrm{mm}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

There is a single opening, so the first minimum satisfies $a\sin\theta_1=\lambda$: the waves emitted from points of the slit a distance $\dfrac{a}{2}$ apart have path difference $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ and cancel in pairs. In the far-screen geometry the small angle allows us to write $y_1\simeq L\sin\theta_1=\dfrac{\lambda L}{a}$. The data are converted to meters and the ratio gives $7.5\,\mathrm{mm}$. This value is the distance from the center to a single minimum. The two edges of the central maximum have opposite coordinates, $-y_1$ and $+y_1$, so the width includes both intervals. The width of the central maximum is **$15\,\mathrm{mm}$**. The second minimum is at $2y_1=15\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the center and the side maximum between $7.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $15\,\mathrm{mm}$ has width $7.5\,\mathrm{mm}$, that is, half the central maximum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a slit of width $a=0.20\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $L=2.0\,\mathrm m$ are used. Find the width of the central maximum and the distance of the first positive minimum.

$$
w=\frac{2\lambda L}{a},\qquad y_1=\frac{w}{2}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
w&=\frac{2\cdot(600\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m)\cdot(2.0\,\mathrm m)}{0.20\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{2.4\cdot10^{-6}}{0.20\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m=12\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m=12\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
y_1&=\frac{12\,\mathrm{mm}}{2}=6.0\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The width $w$ is the distance between the first negative minimum and the first positive minimum, so the factor two appears in the formula. Symmetry about the normal places the first positive minimum at half the width and its distance from the center is $6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The previous exercise had $a=0.16\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $w=15\,\mathrm{mm}$. Here the opening is larger, so the angle needed to obtain the path difference $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$ between the two halves of the slit is smaller and the central maximum narrows. The product $w\,a=2\lambda L=2.4\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is the same in the two cases, as shown by $15\cdot0.16=12\cdot0.20=2.4$ in $\mathrm{mm^2}$. The central maximum is **$12\,\mathrm{mm}$** wide. With $a=0.10\,\mathrm{mm}$, half the value, the width would double and equal $24\,\mathrm{mm}$. The first minimum has $\sin\theta_1=\dfrac{\lambda}{a}=3.0\cdot10^{-3}$, so $y_1=L\sin\theta_1=6.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ coincides with half the width found.

</div>
