## Exercise 1

A diffraction pattern shows a central maximum $9.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide on a screen at $1.5\,\mathrm m$. The light has $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find the width of the slit in the small-angle model.

$$
w=\frac{2\lambda L}{a}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=\frac{2\lambda L}{w}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{2\cdot(500\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m)\cdot(1.5\,\mathrm m)}{9.6\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{1.5\cdot10^{-6}}{9.6\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
 =1.5625\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m\simeq0.16\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(sin(pi*x/4.8)/(pi*x/4.8))^2" xmin=-15 xmax=15 ymin=0 ymax=1.25 nomi="y,I/Imax" punti="-4.8,0;0,1;4.8,0" legenda="I/I_{\max}" titolo="The coordinate $y$ is in millimeters. The central maximum, $9.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide, lies between the minima at $y=\pm4.8\,\mathrm{mm}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The given measurement covers the whole bright region between the first minima, so it is the width $w$, that is, twice the distance $y_1$ of a minimum from the center. From the relation $w=\dfrac{2\lambda L}{a}$, multiplying by $a$ and dividing by $w$, we isolate the width of the opening. The data are substituted in meters and the numerator equals $1.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Using $9.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ as the distance of the first minimum from the center, that is, $y_1$, would give a halved width, because $y_1=\dfrac{w}{2}=4.8\,\mathrm{mm}$. The width of the slit is **about $0.16\,\mathrm{mm}$**, that is, about a tenth of a millimeter, whereas the pattern on the screen measures millimeters. As a check, for $a=0.156\,\mathrm{mm}$ we have $\sin\theta_1=\dfrac{500\,\mathrm{nm}}{156\,\mathrm{\mu m}}=3.2\cdot10^{-3}$ and $y_1=L\sin\theta_1=1.5\,\mathrm m\cdot3.2\cdot10^{-3}=4.8\,\mathrm{mm}$, half the measured width.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A slit produces a central maximum $12\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide on a screen at $1.5\,\mathrm m$ with $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$. Estimate $a$ in the small-angle model. Then find the angle of the first minimum with the sine condition.

$$
a=\frac{2\lambda L}{w},\qquad \sin\theta_1=\frac{\lambda}{a}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{2\cdot(500\cdot10^{-9})\cdot1.5}{12\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
   =1.25\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_1&=\frac{500\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm m}{1.25\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m}=0.0040\\[6pt]
\theta_1&=\arcsin(0.0040)\simeq0.23^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The measurement of the pattern first gives the width of the slit, with the same formula as in the previous exercise. The first minimum satisfies $a\sin\theta_1=\lambda$, so we compute the ratio of the wavelength to the slit width, both expressed in meters. The result is a sine, so to obtain the angle we apply the arcsine. The value $0.0040$ is much smaller than one and the angle, in degrees, is small. The result can be checked from the screen geometry, because $\tan\theta_1=\dfrac{6.0\,\mathrm{mm}}{1.5\,\mathrm m}=0.0040$ with $y_1=\dfrac{12\,\mathrm{mm}}{2}$. The two ratios coincide to the precision of the data, as expected from the equality $\tan\theta\simeq\sin\theta$ for small angles. The first minimum is at **about $0.23^\circ$** from the normal. The slit is $\dfrac{a}{\lambda}=\dfrac{125\,\mathrm{\mu m}}{0.500\,\mathrm{\mu m}}=250$ wavelengths wide, so the light is deflected by less than one degree and the pattern is treated with small angles.

</div>
