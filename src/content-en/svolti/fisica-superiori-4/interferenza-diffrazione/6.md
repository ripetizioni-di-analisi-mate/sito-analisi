## Exercise 1

A diffraction pattern has a central maximum $9.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide on a screen $1.5\,\mathrm m$ away. The light has $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find the slit width in the small-angle model.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{2\lambda L}{w}
   =\frac{2(500\cdot10^{-9})\cdot1.5}{9.6\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
 &=1.5625\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m\simeq0.16\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The measured width covers the entire bright region between the first minima. Solving for the opening width gives $\dfrac{2\lambda L}{w}$. Using $9.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ as the distance from the center to one minimum would halve the inferred width. The result on the scale of tenths of a millimeter describes the slit, while the millimeters measured on the screen describe the pattern.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A slit produces a central maximum $12\,\mathrm{mm}$ wide on a screen $1.5\,\mathrm m$ away with $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$. Estimate $a$ in the small-angle model. Then calculate the first minimum angle using the sine condition.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{2(500\cdot10^{-9})\cdot1.5}{12\cdot10^{-3}}\,\mathrm m
   =1.25\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
\sin\theta_1&=\frac{500\cdot10^{-9}}{1.25\cdot10^{-4}}=0.0040\\[6pt]
\theta_1&=\arcsin(0.0040)\simeq0.23^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First use the measured pattern to find the slit width. Then calculate the angle using the ratio of wavelength to slit width in the same units. The result is a sine, so take the inverse sine. Geometry provides a check because $\tan\theta_1=\dfrac{6\,\mathrm{mm}}{1.5\,\mathrm m}=0.0040$. The two ratios agree to the precision of the data.

</div>
