## Exercise 1

Two coherent waves start in phase and have $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. In their common medium their paths differ by $\delta=320\,\mathrm{nm}$. Calculate the phase difference in radians, without reducing it modulo $2\pi$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\varphi&=2\pi\frac{\delta}{\lambda}
              =2\pi\frac{320}{600}
              =\frac{16\pi}{15}\,\mathrm{rad}
              \simeq3.35\,\mathrm{rad}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio of the path difference to the wavelength counts the extra fraction of a cycle. Both data are in nanometers, so the units cancel. Multiplying by $2\pi$ converts cycles to radians. Since $320\,\mathrm{nm}$ is slightly more than half a wavelength, the phase difference is slightly above $\pi$ and the superposition is close to a minimum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two waves initially in phase with $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ travel paths that differ by $400\,\mathrm{nm}$ in the same medium. Find their phase difference and the least increase in $\delta$ that produces constructive interference.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\varphi&=2\pi\frac{400}{600}
              =\frac{4\pi}{3}\,\mathrm{rad}\\[6pt]
\delta_{\mathrm{max}}&=600\,\mathrm{nm}\\[6pt]
\delta_{\mathrm{max}}-\delta&=(600-400)\,\mathrm{nm}=200\,\mathrm{nm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With a phase shift of $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, the wave has traveled two thirds of an extra cycle. Constructive interference requires an integer number of cycles, so the path difference must reach the first multiple of $\lambda$ greater than the current value. That multiple is $600\,\mathrm{nm}$. An increase of $200\,\mathrm{nm}$ completes the final third of the cycle.

</div>
