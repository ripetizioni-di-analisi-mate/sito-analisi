## Exercise 1

Two coherent waves start in phase and have $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. In the common medium the paths differ by $\delta=320\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find the phase difference in radians, without reducing it modulo $2\pi$.

$$
\Delta\varphi=\frac{2\pi\delta}{\lambda}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\varphi&=2\pi\cdot\frac{320\,\mathrm{nm}}{600\,\mathrm{nm}}
              =2\pi\cdot\frac{8}{15}\\[6pt]
              &=\frac{16\pi}{15}\,\mathrm{rad}
              \simeq3.35\,\mathrm{rad}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(2*pi*x);cos(2*pi*x+16*pi/15)" xmin=0 xmax=2 ymin=-1.35 ymax=1.35 nomi="t/T,E" legenda="E_1/E_0;E_2/E_0" titolo="For equal amplitudes, a phase shift of $\dfrac{16\pi}{15}$ brings the two waves almost into antiphase."}

<div class="spiegazione">

An extra path equal to $\lambda$ adds one complete cycle, that is, $2\pi$ radians, so a path $\delta$ adds the fraction $\dfrac{\delta}{\lambda}$ of a cycle. Both data are in nanometers, so the ratio is dimensionless and equals $\dfrac{8}{15}$. Multiplying by $2\pi$ converts from cycles to radians and gives $\dfrac{16\pi}{15}$. Since $\dfrac{8}{15}>\dfrac{1}{2}$, the phase shift exceeds $\pi$ by $\dfrac{\pi}{15}$ and the two waves are almost in antiphase. At a point where they arrive with these phases and with equal amplitudes, the intensity is much smaller than the maximum, but it is not zero, because $\cos\Delta\varphi\simeq-0.98$. The ratio of the intensity to the maximum is $\dfrac{1+\cos\Delta\varphi}{2}\simeq0.011$, that is, about $1\%$ of the maximum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two waves initially in phase with $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ travel along paths that differ by $400\,\mathrm{nm}$ in the same medium. Find the phase shift and the smallest increase of $\delta$ that produces constructive interference.

$$
\delta_{\mathrm{max}}=m\lambda,\qquad m=1,2,\ldots
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\varphi&=2\pi\frac{400}{600}
              =\frac{4\pi}{3}\,\mathrm{rad}\\[6pt]
\frac{\delta}{\lambda}&=\frac{400}{600}=\frac{2}{3}<1\\[6pt]
\delta_{\mathrm{max}}&=1\cdot600\,\mathrm{nm}=600\,\mathrm{nm}\\[6pt]
\delta_{\mathrm{max}}-\delta&=(600-400)\,\mathrm{nm}=200\,\mathrm{nm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $\dfrac{\delta}{\lambda}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ the waves differ by two thirds of a cycle, that is, by $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ radians. For the condition of constructive interference the path difference must equal an integer number of wavelengths. The order $m=0$ corresponds to $\delta=0$ and would require decreasing the path difference. For $\delta$ to increase, the first allowed value is therefore $m=1$, that is, $\delta=600\,\mathrm{nm}$. The required increment is the difference between this value and the current one and equals **$200\,\mathrm{nm}$**, that is, the last third of a cycle that completes the phase shift to $2\pi$. In terms of phase, $2\pi-\dfrac{4\pi}{3}=\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is missing, and this fraction of a cycle corresponds to $\dfrac{2\pi/3}{2\pi}\cdot600\,\mathrm{nm}=200\,\mathrm{nm}$. Also $\delta=2\lambda$ and $\delta=3\lambda$ give constructive interference, so the allowed increases are $200\,\mathrm{nm}$, $800\,\mathrm{nm}$, $1400\,\mathrm{nm}$, …

</div>
