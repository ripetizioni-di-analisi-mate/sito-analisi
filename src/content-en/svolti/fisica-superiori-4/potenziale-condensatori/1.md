## Exercise 1

A point charge $Q=+2.4\,\mathrm{nC}$ is in vacuum. Find the potential $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the charge, with zero at infinity and $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

::grafico{linee="1,2 5,2" punti="1,2,pieno,Q;5,2,pieno,P" etichette="3,2.7,20 cm" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=4 assi=0 titolo="Calculate the potential due to source Q at point P."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=k_e\frac{Q}{r} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{8.99\cdot10^9\cdot2.4\cdot10^{-9}}{0.20}\,\mathrm V \\[6pt]
&\simeq108\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The source sets the potential, so the calculation requires only the distance from the point to $Q$. To use $k_e$ in the given units, convert nanocoulombs to coulombs and centimeters to meters. The factors $10^9$ and $10^{-9}$ cancel, leaving a numerator of about $21.6\,\mathrm{V\,m}$. The source is positive and the reference is at infinity. The resulting potential is therefore positive and decreases with distance from $Q$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two fixed point sources $Q_1=+3\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $Q_2=-6\,\mathrm{nC}$ are at respective distances from $P$ of $20$ and $60\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find $V_P$ and the energy of a test charge $q=-2\,\mathrm{nC}$ at $P$. Use $k_e\simeq9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$ and zero at infinity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_P&=k_e\left(\frac{Q_1}{r_1}+\frac{Q_2}{r_2}\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq9\left(\frac3{0.20}-\frac6{0.60}\right)\mathrm V=45\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
U&=qV_P\simeq(-2\cdot10^{-9})\cdot45\,\mathrm J \\[6pt]
&=-90\,\mathrm{nJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Add the potentials with their signs because potential is a scalar. The contribution from $Q_1$ is about $135\,\mathrm V$ and that from $Q_2$ is about $-90\,\mathrm V$. Although $Q_2$ has twice the charge magnitude, it is three times as far away, so its contribution is smaller in magnitude. Only after finding $45\,\mathrm V$ do we multiply by the test charge. That charge is negative, so its energy at the point with positive potential is negative relative to the chosen zero.

</div>
