## Exercise 1

A charge $q=+2\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ moves from $V_A=120\,\mathrm V$ to $V_B=40\,\mathrm V$. Find the electric work.

::grafico{linee="1,1 1,5;5,1 5,5" punti="1,2,pieno,A;5,4,pieno,B" etichette="1,5.35,120 V;5,5.35,40 V" segmenti="1,2,5,4" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=6 assi=0 titolo="The charge starts on the 120-volt surface and reaches the 40-volt surface."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=(40-120)\,\mathrm V=-80\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
W_{A\to B}&=-q\Delta V=-2\cdot(-80)\,\mathrm{\mu J} \\[6pt]
&=160\,\mathrm{\mu J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential difference is the final value minus the initial one. Because the charge is positive, potential energy also decreases on going from $A$ to $B$. Electric work is the negative of that change, so it is positive. The calculation can keep microcoulombs and volts because their product is microjoules. Any path between the two points gives the same work.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a uniform field of $E=500\,\mathrm{V/m}$ directed to the right, a charge $q=-3\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ moves $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ right and $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ up. Find $\Delta V$, $\Delta U$, and the electric work.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=-E\Delta x=-500\cdot0.04\,\mathrm V=-20\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=q\Delta V=(-3)(-20)\,\mathrm{\mu J}=60\,\mathrm{\mu J}, \\[6pt]
W_{A\to B}&=-\Delta U=-60\,\mathrm{\mu J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Project the displacement onto the field direction because the vertical component is perpendicular to the field and contributes no work. Over the $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ horizontal displacement the potential drops by $20\,\mathrm V$. The charge is negative, so $q\Delta V$ is positive and potential energy increases. The force on an electron or any negative charge is opposite to the field. Here it points left, while the relevant displacement component points right, in agreement with negative work.

</div>
