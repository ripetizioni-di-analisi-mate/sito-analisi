## Exercise 1

An ideal capacitor of capacitance $64\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ is charged to $18\,\mathrm V$. Find the stored energy.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_C&=\frac12 C(\Delta V)^2 \\[6pt]
&=\frac12\cdot64\cdot18^2\,\mathrm{\mu J} \\[6pt]
&=10368\,\mathrm{\mu J}=10.368\,\mathrm{mJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final voltage is known and the capacitance is constant, so use the form with voltage squared. The factor of one half accounts for the rise in voltage from zero to its final value during charging. Using capacitance in microfarads gives the result in microjoules because farads times volts squared equals coulombs times volts. Divide by one thousand to convert from microjoules to millijoules.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has a capacitance of $4\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ and remains connected to an ideal $10\,\mathrm V$ source. A linear dielectric with $\varepsilon_r=3$ fills the entire gap between the plates. Find the change in stored energy and the work supplied by the source.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C'&=12\,\mathrm{\mu F},\qquad \Delta C=8\,\mathrm{\mu F}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U_C&=\frac12\Delta C(\Delta V)^2=400\,\mathrm{\mu J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta Q&=\Delta C\Delta V=80\,\mathrm{\mu C}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{gen}}&=\Delta V\Delta Q=800\,\mathrm{\mu J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The source fixes the voltage, so the increase in capacitance brings in additional charge. The capacitor's energy change is found by subtracting its initial energy from its final energy at the same voltage. The source moves each additional coulomb through all $10\,\mathrm V$, so its work is greater than the increase in stored energy. The difference of $400\,\mathrm{\mu J}$ can appear as mechanical work or dissipation, depending on the insertion conditions.

</div>
