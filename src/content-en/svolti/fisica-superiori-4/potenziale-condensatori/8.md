## Exercise 1

An ideal capacitor of capacitance $64\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ is charged to $18\,\mathrm V$. Calculate the stored energy.

::grafico{f="x/64" area="x/64;0;0;1152" punti="1152,18,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=1200 ymin=0 ymax=20 nomi="Q,ΔV" titolo="Charge in microcoulombs and voltage in volts. The area of the triangle is the stored energy."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_C&=\frac12 C\,(\Delta V)^2=\frac12\cdot64\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm F\cdot(18\,\mathrm V)^2 \\[6pt]
&=\frac12\cdot64\cdot324\,\mathrm{\mu J}=10368\,\mathrm{\mu J}\simeq10.4\,\mathrm{mJ}, \\[6pt]
Q&=C\,\Delta V=64\cdot18\,\mathrm{\mu C}=1152\,\mathrm{\mu C},\qquad
U_C=\frac12 Q\,\Delta V=\frac12\cdot1152\cdot18\,\mathrm{\mu J}=10368\,\mathrm{\mu J}, \\[6pt]
U_C&=\frac{Q^2}{2C}=\frac{(1152\,\mathrm{\mu C})^2}{2\cdot64\,\mathrm{\mu F}}=10368\,\mathrm{\mu J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Capacitance and voltage are known, so we use the form $U_C=\dfrac{1}{2}C(\Delta V)^2$. The factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ appears because during charging the voltage grows from zero to $18\,\mathrm V$, and the energy is the area of the triangle under the line $\Delta V=\dfrac{Q}{C}$ in the graph.

With the capacitance in microfarads and the voltage in volts the result comes out in microjoules, because a microfarad times a volt squared is a microjoule. With the charge $Q=1152\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ we obtain the same value: the base of the triangle is $1152\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ and the height $18\,\mathrm V$, so the area is the same. The stored energy is about $10.4\,\mathrm{mJ}$.

The three expressions $\dfrac{1}{2}C(\Delta V)^2$, $\dfrac{1}{2}Q\Delta V$, and $\dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$ are equivalent, because $Q=C\Delta V$, and give the same value. We choose the one that uses the known data.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has capacitance $4\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ and stays connected to an ideal $10\,\mathrm V$ source. A linear dielectric with $\varepsilon_r=3$ fills all the space between the plates. Calculate the change in stored energy and the work supplied by the source.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C'&=\varepsilon_rC=12\,\mathrm{\mu F},\qquad U_i=\frac12\cdot4\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{\mu J}=200\,\mathrm{\mu J}, \\[6pt]
U_f&=\frac12\cdot12\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{\mu J}=600\,\mathrm{\mu J},\qquad \Delta U_C=U_f-U_i=400\,\mathrm{\mu J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta Q&=(C'-C)\,\Delta V=8\cdot10\,\mathrm{\mu C}=80\,\mathrm{\mu C}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{gen}}&=\Delta V\,\Delta Q=10\cdot80\,\mathrm{\mu J}=800\,\mathrm{\mu J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The source is ideal and stays connected, so the voltage remains $10\,\mathrm V$. The dielectric multiplies the capacitance by $\varepsilon_r$, so the capacitor can hold more charge at the same voltage, and the source must supply it. The charge goes from $40\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ to $120\,\mathrm{\mu C}$, an increase of $80\,\mathrm{\mu C}$.

The energy of the capacitor goes from $200\,\mathrm{\mu J}$ to $600\,\mathrm{\mu J}$, that is, it increases by $400\,\mathrm{\mu J}$. The source moves each additional coulomb through all $10\,\mathrm V$, so its work is $800\,\mathrm{\mu J}$, twice the increase in stored energy. The difference of $400\,\mathrm{\mu J}$ can appear as mechanical work on the dielectric or as dissipated energy, depending on how the dielectric is inserted.

</div>
