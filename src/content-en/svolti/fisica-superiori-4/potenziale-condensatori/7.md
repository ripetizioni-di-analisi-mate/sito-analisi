## Exercise 1

Two initially uncharged capacitors of $3$ and $6\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ are connected in series to a $12\,\mathrm V$ source. Calculate the equivalent capacitance, the magnitude of the charge, and the voltages.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{eq}}}&=\frac{1}{C_1}+\frac{1}{C_2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{C_1C_2}{C_1+C_2}, \\[6pt]
C_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{3\cdot6}{3+6}\,\mathrm{\mu F}=2\,\mathrm{\mu F}, \\[6pt]
Q&=C_{\mathrm{eq}}\,\Delta V=2\cdot12\,\mathrm{\mu C}=24\,\mathrm{\mu C}, \\[6pt]
\Delta V_1&=\frac{Q}{C_1}=\frac{24}{3}\,\mathrm V=8\,\mathrm V,\qquad \Delta V_2=\frac{Q}{C_2}=\frac{24}{6}\,\mathrm V=4\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The node common to the two capacitors is isolated from the rest of the circuit and initially neutral, so its two plates have charges equal in magnitude and opposite in sign. It follows that the two capacitors have the same charge magnitude $Q$, and the voltages add. We therefore apply the formula for the equivalent capacitance in series, which gives $2\,\mathrm{\mu F}$.

The common charge is calculated with the equivalent capacitance and the voltage of the source, and equals $24\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. Dividing by the capacitance of each capacitor gives the voltages. The $3\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor has the larger drop. As a check, $8\,\mathrm V+4\,\mathrm V=12\,\mathrm V$, and the equivalent capacitance is smaller than both capacitances.

For comparison, the same capacitors in parallel would have $C_{\mathrm{eq}}=9\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ and, at $12\,\mathrm V$, the total charge would be $108\,\mathrm{\mu C}$, with the same voltage across both. In series the equivalent capacitance is smaller, because the source must sustain the sum of the voltages at equal charge.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $2\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor is in series with a group made of $3$ and $5\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ in parallel. The circuit is initially uncharged and is connected to $20\,\mathrm V$. Calculate the voltage of the group and the magnitude of the charge on the $5\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor.

::grafico{linee="0.5,2 1.2,2;1.2,1.4 1.2,2.6;1.6,1.4 1.6,2.6;1.6,2 2.4,2;2.4,1 2.4,3;2.4,3 3.3,3;2.4,1 3.3,1;3.3,2.55 3.3,3.45;3.7,2.55 3.7,3.45;3.3,0.55 3.3,1.45;3.7,0.55 3.7,1.45;3.7,3 4.6,3;3.7,1 4.6,1;4.6,1 4.6,3;4.6,2 5.5,2" etichette="1.4,3,2 μF;3.5,3.9,3 μF;3.5,0.18,5 μF" punti="2.4,2,pieno,A;4.6,2,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=4.5 assi=0 titolo="The two elements of 3 and 5 microfarads are on the branches between A and B."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{par}}&=C_3+C_5=(3+5)\,\mathrm{\mu F}=8\,\mathrm{\mu F}, \\[6pt]
C_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{2\cdot8}{2+8}\,\mathrm{\mu F}=1.6\,\mathrm{\mu F}, \\[6pt]
Q_{\mathrm{ser}}&=C_{\mathrm{eq}}\,\Delta V=1.6\cdot20\,\mathrm{\mu C}=32\,\mathrm{\mu C}, \\[6pt]
\Delta V_{\mathrm{par}}&=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{ser}}}{C_{\mathrm{par}}}=\frac{32}{8}\,\mathrm V=4\,\mathrm V, \\[6pt]
Q_5&=C_5\,\Delta V_{\mathrm{par}}=5\cdot4\,\mathrm{\mu C}=20\,\mathrm{\mu C},\qquad Q_3=3\cdot4\,\mathrm{\mu C}=12\,\mathrm{\mu C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $3$ and $5\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitors are connected to the same two nodes, so they are in parallel and their capacitances add, $8\,\mathrm{\mu F}$. The group behaves as a single capacitor, in series with the $2\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ one. In series the charge magnitude is common, so the charge of the group is the one calculated with the equivalent capacitance, $32\,\mathrm{\mu C}$.

The voltage across the group is obtained by dividing that charge by $8\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ and equals $4\,\mathrm V$. Inside the group the two capacitors have the same voltage and the charges are proportional to the capacitances. The $5\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor carries $20\,\mathrm{\mu C}$. As a check, $12+20=32\,\mathrm{\mu C}$, and the $2\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ capacitor has voltage $\dfrac{32}{2}\,\mathrm V=16\,\mathrm V$, with $16+4=20\,\mathrm V$.

</div>
