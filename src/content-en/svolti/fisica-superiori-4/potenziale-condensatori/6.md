## Exercise 1

A capacitor with capacitance $2.8\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ is held at $12\,\mathrm V$. Find the charge on each plate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=C\Delta V=2.8\cdot12\,\mathrm{\mu C}=33.6\,\mathrm{\mu C}, \\[6pt]
Q_+&=+33.6\,\mathrm{\mu C},\qquad Q_-=-33.6\,\mathrm{\mu C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Capacitance is defined using the charge magnitude on one of the two plates. Multiplying it by the voltage therefore gives $33.6\,\mathrm{\mu C}$ because microfarads times volts gives microcoulombs. The plate at higher potential has positive charge and the other has negative charge. The sum of the two charges is zero for the ideal capacitor, while the charge magnitude on each plate is nonzero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ parallel-plate capacitor initially at $12\,\mathrm V$ is disconnected. The plates are then moved to twice their original separation. Find the charge magnitude on one plate and the new voltage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=3\cdot12\,\mathrm{\mu C}=36\,\mathrm{\mu C}, \\[6pt]
C'&=\frac{C}{2}=1.5\,\mathrm{\mu F}, \\[6pt]
\Delta V'&=\frac{36\,\mathrm{\mu C}}{1.5\,\mathrm{\mu F}}=24\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When the source is disconnected, the charge stays on the plates. The initial capacitance and voltage therefore determine that charge. Doubling the separation then halves the capacitance because $d$ appears in the denominator for a parallel-plate capacitor. To carry the same charge magnitude with half the capacitance, the potential difference must double. In this model the field stays the same because $Q$ and the area are fixed. Twice the distance over which that field acts gives twice the voltage.

</div>
