## Exercise 1

Two point charges $Q=+3.2\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q=-2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ are $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. Calculate the potential energy with zero at infinity and $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

::grafico{linee="1,2 5,2" punti="1,2,pieno,+Q;5,2,pieno,q" etichette="3,2.65,10 cm" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=4 assi=0 titolo="The distance between the charges is 10 centimeters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
U&=k_e\frac{Qq}{r}, \\[6pt]
Qq&=(3.2\cdot10^{-9})(-2.0\cdot10^{-9})\,\mathrm{C^2}=-6.4\cdot10^{-18}\,\mathrm{C^2}, \\[6pt]
k_eQq&=8.99\cdot10^9\cdot(-6.4\cdot10^{-18})\,\mathrm{J\,m}=-5.75\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J\,m}, \\[6pt]
U&=\frac{-5.75\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J\,m}}{0.10\,\mathrm m}\simeq-5.75\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm J, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{est}}&=0-U\simeq+5.75\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential energy of two point charges with zero at infinity is $U=k_e\dfrac{Qq}{r}$, and in the product $Qq$ the sign of each charge is kept. The two charges are expressed in nanocoulombs, so the product contains $10^{-9}\cdot10^{-9}=10^{-18}$. The opposite signs make the product negative, so $U$ is negative.

The negative value indicates that the pair, at $0.10\,\mathrm m$, has lower energy than the charges separated at infinity. To move them apart slowly, the surroundings must therefore do positive external work, equal to $5.75\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$, that is, to the difference between the final energy, which is zero, and the initial one.

The units are consistent, since $\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}\cdot\mathrm{C^2}=\mathrm{N\,m^2}$ and dividing by meters leaves $\mathrm{N\,m}=\mathrm J$. If the two charges had the same sign, the energy would be $+5.75\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$. In that case the system could return this energy as kinetic energy, letting the charges move apart. The energy of the pair in question is about $-5.75\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm J$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two charges $+4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{nC}$ are slowly separated from $0.10$ to $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the external work and the electric work using $k_e\simeq9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k_eQq&=9\cdot10^9\cdot(4\cdot10^{-9})(-2\cdot10^{-9})\,\mathrm{J\,m}=-7.2\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J\,m}, \\[6pt]
U_i&=\frac{-7.2\cdot10^{-8}}{0.10}\,\mathrm J=-720\,\mathrm{nJ},\qquad
U_f=\frac{-7.2\cdot10^{-8}}{0.20}\,\mathrm J=-360\,\mathrm{nJ}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=U_f-U_i=[-360-(-720)]\,\mathrm{nJ}=360\,\mathrm{nJ}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{est}}&=\Delta U=360\,\mathrm{nJ},\qquad W_{\mathrm{el}}=-\Delta U=-360\,\mathrm{nJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product $k_eQq$ is the same at the two instants, so it is enough to divide it by the two distances. The initial energy is $-720\,\mathrm{nJ}$ and the final energy is $-360\,\mathrm{nJ}$. As they move apart, the pair goes to a less negative value, that is, $U$ increases by $360\,\mathrm{nJ}$.

The charges separate slowly, so the change in kinetic energy is negligible. By the work-energy theorem the sum of the works is then zero, and the external work is the opposite of the electric work. The electric force is attractive and the relative displacement has the opposite direction, so $W_{\mathrm{el}}$ is negative. The external work is $+360\,\mathrm{nJ}$ and the electric work is $-360\,\mathrm{nJ}$.

The electric work is also $W_{\mathrm{el}}=U_i-U_f=[-720-(-360)]\,\mathrm{nJ}$, which gives the same value. With charges of the same sign the force would be repulsive, so moving them apart the electric work would be positive and the external work negative.

</div>
