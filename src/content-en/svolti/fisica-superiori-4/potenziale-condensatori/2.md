## Exercise 1

Two point charges $Q=+3.2\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q=-2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ are $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. Find the potential energy with zero at infinity and $k_e=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

::grafico{linee="1,2 5,2" punti="1,2,pieno,+Q;5,2,pieno,q" etichette="3,2.65,10 cm" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=4 assi=0 titolo="The separation of the charges is 10 centimeters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
U&=k_e\frac{Qq}{r} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{8.99\cdot3.2\cdot(-2.0)}{0.10}\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm J \\[6pt]
&\simeq-5.75\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both charges in the product are measured in nanocoulombs, so together they give a factor of $10^{-18}$ before multiplication by $k_e$. The opposite signs make the energy negative. This means the pair at the stated distance has less potential energy than when the charges are infinitely far apart. To separate them slowly, the surroundings must therefore increase the system's energy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two charges $+4\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{nC}$ are slowly moved apart from $0.10$ to $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the external and electric work using $k_e\simeq9\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_i&\simeq-720\,\mathrm{nJ},\qquad U_f\simeq-360\,\mathrm{nJ}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=U_f-U_i\simeq[-360-(-720)]\,\mathrm{nJ}=360\,\mathrm{nJ}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{est}}&\simeq360\,\mathrm{nJ},\qquad W_{\mathrm{el}}\simeq-360\,\mathrm{nJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product $k_eQq$ is about $-72\,\mathrm{nJ\,m}$. Dividing first by $0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and then by $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ gives the energies at the endpoints. As the charges move apart, the pair goes from a more negative value to a less negative one. The electric force is attractive, so its work is negative as the charges separate. Since motion is slow and the kinetic energy change is negligible, the external work has the same magnitude and positive sign.

</div>
