## Exercise 1

An electron starts at rest and moves under the electric force alone from $0$ to $64\,\mathrm V$. Find the classical speed using $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_f&=(-e)(0-64\,\mathrm V)=64\,\mathrm{eV} \\[6pt]
&=1.024\cdot10^{-17}\,\mathrm J, \\[6pt]
v&=\sqrt{\frac{2K_f}{m_e}}\simeq4.74\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the electron $q=-e$, while the potential increase is positive. Its potential energy decreases. Since it starts at rest and only the electric force acts, all of that decrease becomes kinetic energy. To find speed, convert the $64\,\mathrm{eV}$ to joules and then divide twice the energy by the mass. The resulting speed is about $1.6\%$ of the speed of light, so the classical approximation is appropriate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron and a proton, both initially at rest, gain energy in two accelerations through voltages of magnitude $80\,\mathrm V$. Find the ratio $\dfrac{v_e}{v_p}$ using $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_p=1.67\cdot10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_e&=K_p=80\,\mathrm{eV}, \\[6pt]
\frac{v_e}{v_p}&=\frac{\sqrt{\dfrac{2K_e}{m_e}}}{\sqrt{\dfrac{2K_p}{m_p}}} \\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{\frac{1.67\cdot10^{-27}}{9.11\cdot10^{-31}}}\simeq42.8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two particles have equal charge magnitudes and opposite signs. To accelerate from rest, they cross potential differences of the same magnitude, each in the direction in which the electric work is positive. They therefore gain the same kinetic energy. In the speed ratio the energy cancels, leaving the square root of the mass ratio. The electron is much lighter and its speed is about forty-three times the proton's.

</div>
