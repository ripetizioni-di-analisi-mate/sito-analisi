## Exercise 1

An electron starts from rest and moves under the electric force alone from $0$ to $64\,\mathrm V$. Calculate the classical speed with $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="1,0.8 1,4.2;5,0.8 5,4.2" vettori="4.6,3.5,1.4,3.5,E;2.2,2.2,3.8,2.2,F" punti="2.2,2.2,pieno,e⁻" etichette="1,4.6,0 V;5,4.6,64 V" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=5.2 titolo="The electron goes from the plate at $0\,\mathrm V$ to the one at $64\,\mathrm V$."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_f&=q\,(V_i-V_f)=(-e)(0-64\,\mathrm V)=64\,\mathrm{eV}, \\[6pt]
K_f&=64\cdot1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm J=1.024\cdot10^{-17}\,\mathrm J, \\[6pt]
v&=\sqrt{\frac{2K_f}{m_e}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot1.024\cdot10^{-17}}{9.11\cdot10^{-31}}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{2.25\cdot10^{13}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq4.74\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Only the electric force acts and the electron starts from rest, so the change in kinetic energy is $K_f=q(V_i-V_f)$. The electron has $q=-e$ and the potential increases by $64\,\mathrm V$, so the product is positive and the final kinetic energy is $64\,\mathrm{eV}$. The electric force on the electron points toward the higher potential, that is, in the direction of the displacement, as the figure shows.

With $K_f=\dfrac{1}{2}m_ev^2$ we find the speed, after converting electronvolts to joules. The result is about $1.6\%$ of the speed of light, so the condition $v\ll c$ is satisfied and the classical formula applies. The final speed of the electron is about $4.74\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The electronvolt is the work on a charge of magnitude $e$ through $1\,\mathrm V$, that is, $1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm J$, so $64\,\mathrm{eV}$ is $64$ times that value. The ratio $\dfrac{2K_f}{m_e}$ equals $2.25\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ and has the dimensions of the square of a speed, so its square root has the right dimensions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron and a proton, both initially at rest, gain energy in two accelerations through voltages of magnitude $80\,\mathrm V$. Calculate the ratio $\dfrac{v_e}{v_p}$ with $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_p=1.67\cdot10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_e&=K_p=80\,\mathrm{eV}=1.28\cdot10^{-17}\,\mathrm J, \\[6pt]
v_e&=\sqrt{\frac{2K}{m_e}}\simeq5.30\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
v_p=\sqrt{\frac{2K}{m_p}}\simeq1.24\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}, \\[6pt]
\frac{v_e}{v_p}&=\frac{\sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m_e}}}{\sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m_p}}}=\sqrt{\frac{m_p}{m_e}}=\sqrt{\frac{1.67\cdot10^{-27}}{9.11\cdot10^{-31}}}\simeq42.8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two particles have charges of magnitude $e$ and opposite sign. For each we choose the direction of displacement in which the electric work is positive, so both cross a potential difference of $80\,\mathrm V$ in magnitude. The work done is $e\cdot80\,\mathrm V$ for both, and the final kinetic energies are equal, $80\,\mathrm{eV}$.

The speeds follow from $K=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. In the ratio the energy $K$ cancels and the square root of the ratio of the masses remains. The electron has a much smaller mass and its speed is about $43$ times that of the proton. Both speeds are much smaller than $c$, so the classical formula is adequate.

The numerical values are $v_e\simeq5.30\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, about $1.8\%$ of $c$, and $v_p\simeq1.24\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, about $0.04\%$ of $c$. Their ratio coincides with $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}}$, confirming that the energy does not appear in the result.

</div>
