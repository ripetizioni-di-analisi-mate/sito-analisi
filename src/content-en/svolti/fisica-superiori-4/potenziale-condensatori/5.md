## Exercise 1

A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has a plate area of $120\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and a separation of $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find its capacitance neglecting edge effects, using $\varepsilon_0=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F/m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=120\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}=0.012\,\mathrm{m^2}, \\[6pt]
C&=\varepsilon_0\frac Ad=8.85\cdot10^{-12}\frac{0.012}{0.0010}\,\mathrm F \\[6pt]
&=106.2\,\mathrm{pF}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A square centimeter is the square of one hundredth of a meter, so the area conversion factor is $10^{-4}$. The plate separation is one thousandth of a meter. The area-to-distance ratio has units of length, canceling the meter in the denominator of the permittivity's unit. This gives capacitance in farads. The result is of the order of a hundred picofarads for the stated geometry.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A parallel-plate capacitor has capacitance $C_0=90\,\mathrm{pF}$ in vacuum. The plate separation is halved and the gap is completely filled with a linear dielectric of $\varepsilon_r=4$. Find the new capacitance and the charge magnitude on one plate at $100\,\mathrm V$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C'&=\frac{4\varepsilon_0 A}{\dfrac d2}=8C_0=720\,\mathrm{pF}, \\[6pt]
Q&=C'\Delta V=720\cdot100\,\mathrm{pC}=72\,\mathrm{nC}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Capacitance increases for two reasons. Halving the separation doubles the area-to-distance ratio, while the dielectric quadruples the permittivity. Since both factors appear in the same product, they multiply and the capacitance becomes eight times its initial value. The voltage in the second part is the final voltage. Multiplying the new capacitance by $100\,\mathrm V$ gives the magnitude of the charge on a single plate.

</div>
