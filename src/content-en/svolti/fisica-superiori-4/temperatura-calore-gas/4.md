## Exercise 1

A mass of ice $m=0.096\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at its melting point. Use $\ell_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$ to calculate the energy needed to melt it completely at atmospheric pressure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{fus}}&=m\ell_f\\
&=0.096\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}\\
&=32.064\,\mathrm{kJ}\simeq32.1\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 96,32.064" punti="96,32.064,pieno,A" xmin=-8 xmax=110 ymin=-3 ymax=40 nomi="m,Q (kJ)" titolo="Heat versus the mass of ice that has melted, in grams"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The ice is already at its melting point, so calculate the phase-change contribution directly. The specific latent heat is the energy needed to melt one kilogram. The mass here is slightly less than one tenth of a kilogram, so the energy should be slightly less than $33.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The temperature stays constant until melting is complete. On the graph the slope gives the heat required to melt one gram. The marked point corresponds to turning all $96\,\mathrm g$ into water.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A heater delivers a constant $250\,\mathrm W$ to $0.12\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Neglect heat loss and use $\ell_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. Calculate the time required for complete melting and the mass still solid after $80\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{fus}}&=0.12\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=40.08\,\mathrm{kJ}\\
\Delta t_{\mathrm{fus}}&=\frac{40\,080}{250}\,\mathrm s=160.32\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
Q_{80}&=250\cdot80\,\mathrm J=20\,000\,\mathrm J\\
m_{\mathrm{fusa}}&=\frac{20\,000}{334\,000}\,\mathrm{kg}\simeq0.0599\,\mathrm{kg}\\
m_{\mathrm{sol}}&=0.12\,\mathrm{kg}-m_{\mathrm{fusa}}\simeq0.0601\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ice receives $250\,\mathrm J$ each second. Divide the energy needed for complete melting by the power to find the time. After $80\,\mathrm s$, the heater has supplied less than the energy needed to melt the entire sample, so ice and water coexist at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Divide the energy supplied up to that point by the specific latent heat. The quotient is the mass that melted. The remaining mass is slightly more than half of the original mass, consistent with the elapsed time being slightly less than half the total melting time.

</div>
