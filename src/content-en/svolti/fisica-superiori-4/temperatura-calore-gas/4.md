## Exercise 1

A mass of ice $m=0.096\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at the melting temperature. Use $\ell_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$ to calculate the energy needed to melt it completely at atmospheric pressure.

Apply $Q_{\mathrm{fus}}=m\ell_f$, which is valid for the mass $m$ that melts at constant temperature and constant pressure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{fus}}&=m\ell_f\\
&=0.096\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}\\
&=32.064\,\mathrm{kJ}\simeq32.1\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 96,32.064" punti="96,32.064,pieno,A" xmin=-12 xmax=108 ymin=-5 ymax=40 nomi="m,Q (kJ)" titolo="Heat as a function of the mass of ice melted, in grams"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The ice is already at the melting temperature, so the phase-change contribution can be calculated directly. The specific latent heat is the energy needed to melt one kilogram. In this case the mass is less than a tenth of a kilogram, so the energy must be slightly less than $33.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$. Until melting is complete, the temperature stays constant. On the graph the slope is the heat needed to melt one gram. The marked point corresponds to the conversion of all $96\,\mathrm g$ into water. Each gram requires $334\,\mathrm J$, so $96\,\mathrm g$ require $96\cdot334\,\mathrm J=32\,064\,\mathrm J$, the same value as the calculation in kilograms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A heater delivers a constant $250\,\mathrm W$ to $0.12\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Neglect losses and use $\ell_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. Calculate the time needed for complete melting and the mass still solid after $80\,\mathrm s$.

Apply $Q_{\mathrm{fus}}=m\ell_f$, $\Delta t=\dfrac QP$, and $m_{\mathrm{fusa}}=\dfrac{Q}{\ell_f}$, where $m_{\mathrm{sol}}=m-m_{\mathrm{fusa}}$ is the mass still solid.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\mathrm{fus}}&=0.12\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=40.08\,\mathrm{kJ}\\
\Delta t_{\mathrm{fus}}&=\frac{40\,080}{250}\,\mathrm s=160.32\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
Q_{80}&=250\cdot80\,\mathrm J=20\,000\,\mathrm J\\
m_{\mathrm{fusa}}&=\frac{20\,000}{334\,000}\,\mathrm{kg}\simeq0.0599\,\mathrm{kg}\\
m_{\mathrm{sol}}&=0.12\,\mathrm{kg}-m_{\mathrm{fusa}}\simeq0.0601\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ice receives $250\,\mathrm J$ every second. The energy needed for complete melting is therefore divided by the power to obtain the time. After $80\,\mathrm s$ the heater has supplied less than the energy needed to melt the whole sample, so ice and water coexist at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The energy supplied up to that instant is divided by the specific latent heat. The quotient is the mass melted. The residual mass is a little more than half the initial one, consistent with an elapsed time slightly less than half the melting time. Complete melting takes $160.32\,\mathrm s$, about $2$ minutes and $40$ seconds. The temperature stays at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ throughout this interval, because the energy supplied is absorbed by the phase change.

</div>
