## Exercise 1

A mass of water $m=0.28\,\mathrm{kg}$ is heated by $\Delta T=24\,\mathrm K$. Use $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and assume that the water stays liquid. Calculate the heat absorbed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=mc\\
&=0.28\cdot4180\,\mathrm{J/K}\\
&=1170.4\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]
Q&=C\Delta T\\
&=1170.4\cdot24\,\mathrm J\\
&=28\,089.6\,\mathrm J\simeq28.1\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="1.1704*x" punti="24,28.0896,pieno,A" xmin=-3 xmax=31 ymin=-3 ymax=40 nomi="ΔT,Q (kJ)" titolo="Heat absorbed by the water sample versus temperature rise in kelvin"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass stays in the same phase, so its specific heat capacity relates the temperature change directly to the energy absorbed. Multiplying $4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ by $0.28\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives the heat capacity of the entire sample. This number is the energy required to raise the temperature of that amount of water by one kelvin. The required temperature rise is $24\,\mathrm K$, so multiply the result by $24$. The product is in joules and is positive because the water receives energy. On the graph the heat capacity is the slope of the line. The marked point corresponds to about $28.1\,\mathrm{kJ}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass of water $m=0.35\,\mathrm{kg}$ warms by $24\,\mathrm K$ without changing phase. A heater delivers a constant $600\,\mathrm W$ to the water. With $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, calculate the heat absorbed and the time required.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=mc\Delta T\\
&=0.35\cdot4180\cdot24\,\mathrm J\\
&=35\,112\,\mathrm J\simeq35.1\,\mathrm{kJ}\\[6pt]
\Delta t&=\frac QP\\
&=\frac{35\,112}{600}\,\mathrm s\\
&=58.52\,\mathrm s\simeq58.5\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The temperature rise and $c$ determine how much energy the water must receive. Power determines how long supplying that energy takes. Since $600\,\mathrm W$ is the power actually delivered to the water, $600\,\mathrm J$ enters each second. Divide the heat absorbed by the power. The unit $\mathrm{J/W}$ reduces to seconds. One minute at that rate would supply $36\,000\,\mathrm J$, slightly more than required. The calculated time is indeed slightly less than a minute.

</div>
