## Exercise 1

A rod free to expand starts at $L_0=2.0\,\mathrm m$. The linear coefficient of the material is $1.2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Calculate the elongation for a temperature rise of $40\,\mathrm K$.

Apply $\Delta L=L_0\alpha\Delta T$ for the elongation and $L=L_0+\Delta L$ for the final length.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha\Delta T&=(1.2\cdot10^{-5})\cdot40\\
&=4.8\cdot10^{-4}\\[6pt]
\Delta L&=L_0\alpha\Delta T\\
&=2.0\cdot4.8\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m\\
&=9.6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m\\
&=0.96\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
L&=L_0+\Delta L\\
&=2.00096\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.024*x" punti="40,0.96,pieno,A" xmin=-6 xmax=54 ymin=-0.2 ymax=1.45 nomi="ΔT,ΔL (mm)" titolo="Elongation of the free rod for a temperature rise of 40 K"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The product of the coefficient and the temperature change is the fraction of its length that the rod gains. This fraction is multiplied by the initial length. The result in meters is $9.6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$, equivalent to $0.96\,\mathrm{mm}$. The positive sign agrees with the heating and with $\alpha>0$. The linear model is suitable because the relative change $4.8\cdot10^{-4}$ is small. The final length is therefore $2.00096\,\mathrm m$, greater than the initial one by less than one thousandth. On the graph the point at $40\,\mathrm K$ lies just below $1\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rod $2.5\,\mathrm m$ long has one end fixed and the other free, with a gap of $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ to an obstacle. Its linear coefficient is $1.2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. After uniform heating by $40\,\mathrm K$, calculate the length of the rod and the remaining gap.

Apply $\Delta L=L_0\alpha\Delta T$ and compare $\Delta L$ with the available gap $g=2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. If $\Delta L<g$, the expansion is free and the remaining gap is $g-\Delta L$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L&=L_0\alpha\Delta T\\
&=2.5\cdot(1.2\cdot10^{-5})\cdot40\,\mathrm m\\
&=1.2\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
L&=2.5\,\mathrm m+0.0012\,\mathrm m\\
&=2.5012\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
s&=(2.0-1.2)\,\mathrm{mm}\\
&=0.8\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The free end can advance as long as the elongation is less than the available gap. One therefore calculates how much the rod would lengthen if there were no contact. The value of $1.2\,\mathrm{mm}$ is less than the $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ available, so the hypothesis of free expansion is satisfied throughout the heating. To obtain the final length, the elongation is added to $L_0$ after expressing both in meters. The remaining gap is found instead by subtracting the advance from the initial gap, with both expressed in millimeters. The result is $0.8\,\mathrm{mm}$. If the heating were $70\,\mathrm K$, the calculated elongation would be $2.1\,\mathrm{mm}$, greater than the available gap, and the free-rod model would no longer apply because the obstacle would block the end.

</div>
