## Exercise 1

A fixed amount of ideal gas goes from the state $p_1=112\,\mathrm{kPa}$, $V_1=12\,\mathrm L$, $T_1=300\,\mathrm K$ to a state with $V_2=18\,\mathrm L$ and $T_2=360\,\mathrm K$. Calculate the final pressure.

For a fixed amount of ideal gas, $nR=\dfrac{pV}{T}$ is constant, so $\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\dfrac{p_2V_2}{T_2}$ with absolute temperatures.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1}&=\frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}\\[6pt]
p_2&=p_1\frac{V_1}{V_2}\frac{T_2}{T_1}\\
&=112\cdot\frac{12}{18}\cdot\frac{360}{300}\,\mathrm{kPa}\\
&=112\cdot\frac45\,\mathrm{kPa}\\
&=89.6\,\mathrm{kPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{punti="12,112,pieno,A;18,89.6,pieno,B" xmin=8 xmax=22 ymin=72 ymax=132 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="Initial state A and final state B with V in liters"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The amount of gas stays fixed, so in both states the ratio of $pV$ to $T$ equals $nR$. In this ratio it is enough for the two volumes to have the same units, so the liters cancel. The pressure is affected by two opposite changes. The expansion from $12$ to $18\,\mathrm L$ would reduce the pressure to two thirds at fixed temperature. The change from $300$ to $360\,\mathrm K$ instead multiplies it by $1.2$. Their product is $0.80$, so the final pressure is lower. The two states are marked on the graph. From the data the final pressure can be found without specifying the path. As a check, $\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\dfrac{112\cdot12}{300}=4.48\,\mathrm{kPa\,L/K}$ and $\dfrac{89.6\cdot18}{360}=4.48\,\mathrm{kPa\,L/K}$, with the same value in the two states.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A closed ideal gas occupies $12\,\mathrm L$ at $300\,\mathrm K$ with absolute pressure $140\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The volume becomes $18\,\mathrm L$. Determine the final temperature required for the absolute pressure to be the same at the beginning and at the end.

Apply $\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\dfrac{p_2V_2}{T_2}$, with the condition $p_2=p_1$ imposed by the problem.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1}&=\frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}\\
p_2&=p_1\\[4pt]
T_2&=T_1\frac{V_2}{V_1}\\
&=300\cdot\frac{18}{12}\,\mathrm K\\
&=450\,\mathrm K.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same amount of gas is present in the two states, so the values of $pV$ divided by $T$ can be equated. Since the problem imposes equal absolute pressures at the ends, the pressure cancels. This leaves the ratio of volume to absolute temperature. To have the same value in the two states, when the volume grows by $50\%$ the temperature in kelvin must also grow by $50\%$. The temperature rise is therefore $150\,\mathrm K$. The final state at $18\,\mathrm L$ and $450\,\mathrm K$ has $pV=140\cdot18\,\mathrm J=2520\,\mathrm J$, whereas the initial state has $pV=140\cdot12\,\mathrm J=1680\,\mathrm J$, and the ratio $\dfrac{2520}{1680}=\dfrac32$ coincides with $\dfrac{450}{300}$.

</div>
