## Exercise 1

A fixed amount of ideal gas goes from the state $p_1=112\,\mathrm{kPa}$, $V_1=12\,\mathrm L$, and $T_1=300\,\mathrm K$ to a state with $V_2=18\,\mathrm L$ and $T_2=360\,\mathrm K$. Calculate the final pressure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1}&=\frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}\\[6pt]
p_2&=p_1\frac{V_1}{V_2}\frac{T_2}{T_1}\\
&=112\cdot\frac{12}{18}\cdot\frac{360}{300}\,\mathrm{kPa}\\
&=112\cdot\frac45\,\mathrm{kPa}\\
&=89.6\,\mathrm{kPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{punti="12,112,pieno,A;18,89.6,pieno,B" xmin=7 xmax=22 ymin=68 ymax=130 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="Initial state A and final state B with V in liters"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The amount of gas stays fixed, so in both states the ratio of $pV$ to $T$ equals $nR$. The two volumes can use any common unit in their ratio, so liters cancel. Two changes affect pressure in opposite directions. Expanding from $12$ to $18\,\mathrm L$ would reduce pressure to two thirds at constant temperature. Warming from $300$ to $360\,\mathrm K$ instead multiplies it by $1.2$. Their product is $0.80$, so the final pressure is lower. The graph marks the two states. The data determine the final pressure without specifying a path.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sealed ideal gas occupies $12\,\mathrm L$ at $300\,\mathrm K$ with absolute pressure $140\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Its volume changes to $18\,\mathrm L$. Find the final temperature required for the final absolute pressure to equal its initial value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{p_1V_1}{T_1}&=\frac{p_2V_2}{T_2}\\
p_2&=p_1\\[4pt]
T_2&=T_1\frac{V_2}{V_1}\\
&=300\cdot\frac{18}{12}\,\mathrm K\\
&=450\,\mathrm K.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both states contain the same amount of gas, so the values of $pV$ divided by $T$ can be equated. Since the problem specifies equal absolute pressures at the endpoints, the pressure cancels. This leaves the ratio of volume to absolute temperature. For the ratio to stay the same, a $50\%$ increase in volume requires a $50\%$ increase in kelvin temperature. The final value of $450\,\mathrm K$ supplies the required $150\,\mathrm K$ rise. The data require only that pressures match at the endpoints.

</div>
