## Exercise 1

An ideal gas in equilibrium contains $n=0.48\,\mathrm{mol}$ and occupies $18\,\mathrm L$ at $310\,\mathrm K$. Use $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$ to calculate the absolute pressure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=18\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\
&=0.018\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
p&=\frac{nRT}{V}\\
&=\frac{0.48\cdot8.314\cdot310}{0.018}\,\mathrm{Pa}\\
&\simeq68\,729\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq68.7\,\mathrm{kPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.2217066667*x" punti="310,68.7290667,pieno,A" xmin=230 xmax=390 ymin=40 ymax=95 nomi="T,p (kPa)" titolo="Absolute pressure versus temperature T in kelvin"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas is in equilibrium, so the equation of state applies with the given data. The temperature is already absolute. Convert liters to cubic meters because $R$ is given in joules per mole per kelvin. The product $nRT$ is an energy, so the ratio of joules to cubic meters has units of pascals. The graph shows the behavior for the same rigid container and the same amount of gas. At fixed volume, pressure is proportional to absolute temperature. The marked point associates about $68.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$ with $310\,\mathrm K$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.60\,\mathrm{mol}$ sample of a monatomic ideal gas is in equilibrium in an $18\,\mathrm L$ container at $310\,\mathrm K$. The outside atmospheric pressure is $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$, calculate the absolute pressure, gauge pressure, and internal energy in the classical model.

$$
\begin{aligned}
nRT&=0.60\cdot8.314\cdot310\,\mathrm J\\
&=1546.404\,\mathrm J\\[6pt]
p&=\frac{1546.404}{0.018}\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq85.9\,\mathrm{kPa}\\
p_{\mathrm{man}}&=p-p_{\mathrm{atm}}\simeq-14.1\,\mathrm{kPa}\\[6pt]
U&=\frac32 nRT=2319.606\,\mathrm J\\
&\simeq2.32\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation of state uses absolute pressure. First calculate $nRT$ and divide it by volume in cubic meters. Gauge pressure is a difference relative to the atmosphere, so it can be negative even though absolute pressure is positive. Here atmospheric pressure exceeds the gas pressure. For internal energy use $nRT$ again because in the monatomic model the total translational energy is three halves of that product. The value of $U$ can also be checked with $\dfrac32pV$, using absolute pressure.

</div>
