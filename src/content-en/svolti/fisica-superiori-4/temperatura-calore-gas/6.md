## Exercise 1

An ideal gas in equilibrium contains $n=0.48\,\mathrm{mol}$ and occupies $18\,\mathrm L$ at $310\,\mathrm K$. Use $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$ to calculate the absolute pressure.

Apply the equation of state $pV=nRT$, which gives $p=\dfrac{nRT}{V}$, with $T$ absolute and $V$ in cubic meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=18\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\
&=0.018\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
p&=\frac{nRT}{V}\\
&=\frac{0.48\cdot8.314\cdot310}{0.018}\,\mathrm{Pa}\\
&=\frac{1237.1}{0.018}\,\mathrm{Pa}\\
&\simeq68\,729\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq68.7\,\mathrm{kPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.2217066667*x" punti="310,68.7290667,pieno,A" xmin=200 xmax=400 ymin=40 ymax=90 nomi="T,p (kPa)" titolo="Absolute pressure as the temperature T in kelvin varies"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas is in equilibrium, so the equation of state can be used with the given data. The temperature is already absolute. The liters, however, must be converted to cubic meters, because the value of $R$ is expressed in joules per mole and kelvin. The product $nRT$ is an energy, so the ratio of joules to cubic meters gives exactly pascals. The graph shows the behavior for the same rigid container and the same amount of gas. At constant volume, the pressure is proportional to the absolute temperature. The marked point associates about $68.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$ with $310\,\mathrm K$. The numerator is $nRT=1237.1\,\mathrm J$, so the absolute pressure of the gas is about $0.68$ times the standard atmospheric pressure of $101.3\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A monatomic ideal gas of $0.60\,\mathrm{mol}$ is in equilibrium in a container of $18\,\mathrm L$ at $310\,\mathrm K$. The external atmospheric pressure is $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. With $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$, calculate the absolute pressure, the gauge pressure, and the internal energy in the classical model.

Apply $p=\dfrac{nRT}{V}$, $p_{\mathrm{man}}=p-p_{\mathrm{atm}}$, and, for the monatomic gas, $U=\dfrac32nRT$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
nRT&=0.60\cdot8.314\cdot310\,\mathrm J\\
&=1546.404\,\mathrm J\\[6pt]
p&=\frac{1546.404}{0.018}\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq85.9\,\mathrm{kPa}\\
p_{\mathrm{man}}&=p-p_{\mathrm{atm}}\simeq-14.1\,\mathrm{kPa}\\[6pt]
U&=\frac32 nRT=2319.606\,\mathrm J\\
&\simeq2.32\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation of state contains the absolute pressure. The product $nRT$ is calculated first and divided by the volume in cubic meters. The gauge pressure is a difference relative to the atmosphere, so it can be negative even though the absolute pressure is positive. Here the atmosphere has a greater pressure than the gas. For the internal energy, $nRT$ is reused, because in the monatomic model the total translational energy equals three halves of that product. The value of $U$ can also be checked with $\dfrac32pV$, using the absolute pressure: $\dfrac32\cdot85\,911\cdot0.018\,\mathrm J\simeq2320\,\mathrm J$. The two expressions coincide because the equation of state gives $pV=nRT$. The negative sign of $p_{\mathrm{man}}$ indicates that the absolute pressure of the gas is lower than $p_{\mathrm{atm}}$.

</div>
