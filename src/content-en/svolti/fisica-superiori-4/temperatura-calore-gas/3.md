## Exercise 1

In an insulated container of negligible heat capacity, $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ are mixed. The specific heat capacity $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ is constant. Calculate the final temperature.

Apply the calorimetric balance $m_1c(t_f-t_1)+m_2c(t_f-t_2)=0$, which is valid because the container is insulated and no portion changes phase.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.20c(t_f-70)+0.40c(t_f-20)&=0\\
0.20(70-t_f)&=0.40(t_f-20)\\
70-t_f&=2(t_f-20)\\
3t_f&=110\\
t_f&\simeq36.7\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\\[6pt]
Q_{\mathrm{ced}}&=0.20\cdot4180\cdot(70-36.7)\,\mathrm J\simeq27.9\,\mathrm{kJ}\\
Q_{\mathrm{ass}}&=0.40\cdot4180\cdot(36.7-20)\,\mathrm J\simeq27.9\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.836*(70-x);1.672*(x-20)" legenda="Released;Absorbed" punti="36.6667,27.8667,pieno" xmin=20 xmax=70 ymin=-10 ymax=90 nomi="t_f,Q (kJ)" titolo="At equilibrium the energies coincide. The temperature t_f is in degrees Celsius"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The final temperature is common because the portions mix until they reach equilibrium. The hot water releases energy and the cold water absorbs it. The insulated container cannot exchange heat with the outside, so the quantities released and absorbed have the same magnitude. The specific heat capacity is the same for the two portions and cancels. The cold mass is double, so its temperature rise is half the cooling of the hot mass. On the graph the balance holds at the intersection point. The equilibrium temperature is closer to $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ than to $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, because the initially cold mass prevails. The two heats calculated with the final temperature coincide, about $27.9\,\mathrm{kJ}$, and this confirms the equality between the energy released and absorbed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an insulated calorimeter, $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ are mixed. The calorimeter starts at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and has heat capacity $C_r=418\,\mathrm{J/K}$. With $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, calculate the final temperature and how much heat the container absorbs.

Apply the balance $C_h(t_f-t_h)+(C_c+C_r)(t_f-t_c)=0$, where the calorimeter and the cold water start at the same temperature $t_c=20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_h&=0.25\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot c=1045\,\mathrm{J/K}\\
C_c+C_r&=0.40\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot c+418\,\mathrm{J/K}\\
&=2090\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[5pt]
1045(70-t_f)&=2090(t_f-20)\\
t_f&=\frac{110}{3}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\simeq36.7\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\\[5pt]
Q_r&=418\left(\frac{110}{3}-20\right)\,\mathrm J\\
&\simeq6.97\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cold water and the calorimeter start at the same temperature and reach the same equilibrium temperature. Both absorb heat, so their heat capacities are added. The heat capacity of the cold water is $0.40\cdot4180=1672\,\mathrm{J/K}$ and, added to $418\,\mathrm{J/K}$, gives $2090\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Together they have a heat capacity double that of the hot water. For this reason, in the balance the cooling of the hot water is double the warming of the cold assembly. Once the common temperature is found, the heat absorbed by the container alone is obtained using its capacity of $418\,\mathrm{J/K}$. As a check, of the roughly $34.8\,\mathrm{kJ}$ released by the hot water, about $27.9\,\mathrm{kJ}$ warms the cold water and $6.97\,\mathrm{kJ}$ the container.

</div>
