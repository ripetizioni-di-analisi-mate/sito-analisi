## Exercise 1

In an insulated container of negligible heat capacity, $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is mixed with $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The specific heat capacity $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ is constant. Calculate the final temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.20c(t_f-70)+0.40c(t_f-20)&=0\\
0.20(70-t_f)&=0.40(t_f-20)\\
70-t_f&=2(t_f-20)\\
3t_f&=110\\
t_f&\simeq36.7\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.836*(70-x);1.672*(x-20)" legenda="Released;Absorbed" punti="36.6667,27.8667,pieno" xmin=20 xmax=70 ymin=-5 ymax=90 nomi="t_f,Q (kJ)" titolo="The energies are equal at equilibrium. The temperature t_f is in degrees Celsius"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The final temperature is shared because the samples mix to reach thermal equilibrium. The warm water releases energy and the cool water absorbs it. The insulated container cannot exchange heat with its surroundings, so the amounts released and absorbed have the same magnitude. Both portions have the same specific heat capacity, which cancels. There is twice as much cool water, so its temperature rise is half the warm water's temperature drop. The balance holds at the intersection of the curves. The equilibrium temperature is closer to $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ than to $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ because there is more initially cool water.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An insulated calorimeter mixes $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The calorimeter starts at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and has heat capacity $C_r=418\,\mathrm{J/K}$. With $c=4180\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, calculate the final temperature and the heat the vessel absorbs.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_h&=0.25\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot c=1045\,\mathrm{J/K}\\
C_c+C_r&=0.40\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot c+418\,\mathrm{J/K}\\
&=2090\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[5pt]
1045(70-t_f)&=2090(t_f-20)\\
t_f&=\frac{110}{3}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\simeq36.7\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\\[5pt]
Q_r&=418\left(\frac{110}{3}-20\right)\,\mathrm J\\
&\simeq6.97\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cool water and the calorimeter start at the same temperature and reach the same equilibrium temperature. Both absorb heat, so their heat capacities add. Together their heat capacity is twice that of the warm water. In the balance, the warm water therefore cools by twice as much as the cooler components warm. Once the common temperature is known, use the vessel's own heat capacity of $418\,\mathrm{J/K}$ to find the heat it absorbs. As a check, of the roughly $34.8\,\mathrm{kJ}$ released by the warm water, about $27.9\,\mathrm{kJ}$ warms the cool water and $6.97\,\mathrm{kJ}$ warms the vessel.

</div>
