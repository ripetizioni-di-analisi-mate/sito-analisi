## Exercise 1

A uniform flat wall of area $6.4\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and thickness $0.12\,\mathrm m$ has conductivity $\kappa=0.050\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$. Its faces are at $30$ and $6\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Neglect lateral losses and calculate the steady rate of heat transfer by conduction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T &= (30-6)\,\mathrm K=24\,\mathrm K\\[4pt]
\frac{\Delta T}{d}&=\frac{24}{0.12}\,\mathrm{K/m}=200\,\mathrm{K/m}\\[4pt]
P&=\kappa A\frac{\Delta T}{d}\\
&=0.050\cdot6.4\cdot200\,\mathrm W=64\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,30 12,6" punti="0,30,pieno;12,6,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=14 ymin=0 ymax=35 nomi="x,t (°C)" titolo="Temperature in the wall. Distance x from the warm face is in centimeters"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In steady state the wall does not accumulate energy, so heat enters the warm face and leaves the cool face at the same rate. In uniform material with no internal heat sources, the temperature profile is linear. A drop of $24\,\mathrm K$ across $0.12\,\mathrm m$ gives a gradient magnitude of $200\,\mathrm{K/m}$. Multiply by conductivity to obtain the heat transfer rate per square meter, which is $10\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. Multiplying again by area gives $64\,\mathrm W$. Net transfer is from the face at $30\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to the one at $6\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wall consists of two flat layers of area $8\,\mathrm{m^2}$ in perfect thermal contact. The warm-side layer is $0.12\,\mathrm m$ thick with conductivity $0.050\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$. The cool-side layer is $0.08\,\mathrm m$ thick with conductivity $0.10\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$. The outer faces are at $30$ and $6\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Neglect lateral losses and calculate the interface temperature and the energy transferred in $600\,\mathrm s$ in steady state.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_1&=\frac{0.12}{0.050\cdot8}\,\mathrm{K/W}=0.30\,\mathrm{K/W}\\
R_2&=\frac{0.08}{0.10\cdot8}\,\mathrm{K/W}=0.10\,\mathrm{K/W}\\[5pt]
P&=\frac{24}{0.30+0.10}\,\mathrm W=60\,\mathrm W\\
\Delta T_1&=PR_1=18\,\mathrm K\\
t_{\mathrm{int}}&=(30-18)\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=12\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\\[5pt]
Q&=60\cdot600\,\mathrm J=36\,\mathrm{kJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,30 12,12 20,6" punti="12,12,pieno,A" xmin=-2 xmax=23 ymin=0 ymax=35 nomi="x,t (°C)" titolo="Point A separates the layers. Distance x is in centimeters"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The heat transfer rate is the same through both layers because the interface does not accumulate energy in steady state. Each layer produces a temperature drop equal to that rate times its thermal resistance. The resistances in series add, giving $60\,\mathrm W$ by dividing the total drop of $24\,\mathrm K$ by $0.40\,\mathrm{K/W}$. The warm-side layer accounts for three quarters of the total resistance, so its drop is $18\,\mathrm K$. From the interface temperature there is a remaining drop of $6\,\mathrm K$ through the second layer, consistent with $PR_2$. During $600\,\mathrm s$, $60\,\mathrm{J}$ passes through the wall each second, for a total of $36\,\mathrm{kJ}$.

</div>
