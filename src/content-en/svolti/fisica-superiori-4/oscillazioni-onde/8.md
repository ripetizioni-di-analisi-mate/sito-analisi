## Exercise 1

A string of length $L=0.96\,\mathrm{m}$, fixed at both ends, has wave speed $u=120\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the frequency of the third harmonic.

$$
\lambda_n=\frac{2L}{n}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lambda_3=\frac{2\cdot0.96}{3}\,\mathrm{m}=0.64\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

$$
f_3=\frac{u}{\lambda_3}=\frac{120\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.64\,\mathrm{m}}=187.5\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

$$
f_1=\frac{u}{2L}=\frac{120}{1.92}\,\mathrm{Hz}=62.5\,\mathrm{Hz},\qquad f_3=3f_1=187.5\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is ideal and fixed at the ends, which are therefore nodes. In the third harmonic three intervals between consecutive nodes fill the string, and each is $\dfrac{0.96}{3}\,\mathrm{m}=0.32\,\mathrm{m}$ long. Each interval is half a wavelength, so $\lambda_3=0.64\,\mathrm{m}$, and the frequency is $\dfrac{u}{\lambda_3}$. The third line compares the result with the fundamental: $f_1=62.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $3f_1=187.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The internal nodes are at $x=0.32\,\mathrm{m}$ and $x=0.64\,\mathrm{m}$ and the antinodes are at the midpoint of each interval. The period of the third harmonic is $\dfrac{1}{187.5}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq5.3\,\mathrm{ms}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A string of length $L=1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, fixed at both ends, has wave speed $u=120\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the fundamental frequency, the frequency of the third harmonic, and the wavelength of the third harmonic.

$$
f_1=\frac{u}{2L}=\frac{120}{2\cdot1.20}\,\mathrm{Hz}=50\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

$$
f_3=3f_1=3\cdot50\,\mathrm{Hz}=150\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

$$
\lambda_3=\frac{u}{f_3}=\frac{120}{150}\,\mathrm{m}=0.80\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

$$
3\cdot\frac{\lambda_3}{2}=3\cdot0.40\,\mathrm{m}=1.20\,\mathrm{m}=L.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same assumptions apply and the speed $u$ is the same for every mode. In the fundamental the string contains a single interval between nodes, that is, half a wavelength, so $\lambda_1=2L=2.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and $f_1=\dfrac{u}{2L}$. At fixed speed the third harmonic has triple the frequency and the wavelength reduced to a third. The last line checks the result: three intervals of $\dfrac{0.80}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ fill the length of the string. The frequencies are integer multiples of the fundamental because an integer number of half wavelengths must fit in $L$, so the second harmonic, at $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and the fourth, at $200\,\mathrm{Hz}$, are also allowed. The period of the fundamental, $\dfrac{1}{50}\,\mathrm{s}=20\,\mathrm{ms}$, is the round-trip time of a pulse, $\dfrac{2L}{u}=\dfrac{2.40}{120}\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On a string fixed at both ends, $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ long with $u=120\,\mathrm{m/s}$, three internal nodes are seen in a single standing mode. Find the frequency of the mode and the positions of the three nodes.

::grafico{f="sin(4*pi*x/120)" xmin=0 xmax=120 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.3 nomi="x,y/A" punti="0,0,pieno;30,0,pieno;60,0,pieno;90,0,pieno;120,0,pieno" titolo="Five nodes, including the two ends, bound four intervals. Horizontal coordinates in centimeters."}

$$
n=3+1=4,\qquad \frac{\lambda_4}{2}=\frac{L}{4}=\frac{1.20}{4}\,\mathrm{m}=0.30\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

$$
\lambda_4=0.60\,\mathrm{m},\qquad f_4=\frac{u}{\lambda_4}=\frac{120}{0.60}\,\mathrm{Hz}=200\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

$$
x_1=0.30\,\mathrm{m},\quad x_2=2\cdot0.30\,\mathrm{m}=0.60\,\mathrm{m},\quad x_3=3\cdot0.30\,\mathrm{m}=0.90\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Counting also the two nodes at the ends gives five nodes, which bound four equal intervals of length $\dfrac{1.20}{4}\,\mathrm{m}=0.30\,\mathrm{m}$, so the mode is the fourth, $n=4$. An interval between consecutive nodes is half a wavelength, so $\lambda_4=0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and the frequency is $\dfrac{u}{\lambda_4}$. The three internal nodes are the first three multiples of $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ starting from $x=0$, and the fourth multiple, $1.20\,\mathrm{m}$, coincides with the right end. As a check, the fundamental of this string is $\dfrac{120}{2\cdot1.20}\,\mathrm{Hz}=50\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $4f_1=200\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The mode has four antinodes, at $x=0.15\,\mathrm{m}$, $0.45\,\mathrm{m}$, $0.75\,\mathrm{m}$, and $1.05\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
