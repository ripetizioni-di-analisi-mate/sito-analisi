## Exercise 1

A harmonic oscillator has amplitude $A=0.028\,\mathrm{m}$ and frequency $f=2.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find the maximum magnitude of the velocity.

::grafico{f="2.8*cos(pi*x/200)" xmin=0 xmax=400 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3.5 nomi="t,x (cm)" punti="0,2.8,pieno;100,0,pieno;200,-2.8,pieno;300,0,pieno" titolo="For zero initial phase, the slope of the graph has maximum magnitude at the passages through equilibrium. Time in milliseconds."}

$$
\omega=2\pi f=2\pi\cdot2.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}=5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{\max}&=\omega A\\[6pt]
        &=5\pi\cdot0.028\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
        &=0.14\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq0.44\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is harmonic, so $x=A\cos(\omega t+\varphi)$ and $v=-\omega A\sin(\omega t+\varphi)$. The magnitude of $v$ is maximum when the sine equals $\pm1$, that is, when the cosine of the same phase vanishes and the position is the equilibrium position. This happens at $x=0$ both on the way out and on the way back. Multiplying the cycles per second by $2\pi$ gives $\omega$ in radians per second. The product $\omega A$ has units $\mathrm{rad/s}\cdot\mathrm{m}$, which is read as $\mathrm{m/s}$ because the radian is dimensionless. For comparison, the period is $T=\dfrac{1}{2.5}\,\mathrm{s}=0.40\,\mathrm{s}$ and in one period the point travels $4A=0.112\,\mathrm{m}$, so the average speed is $\dfrac{0.112}{0.40}\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.28\,\mathrm{m/s}$, smaller than the maximum because the point slows down until it stops at the turning points.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A harmonic oscillator has amplitude $A=0.035\,\mathrm{m}$ and frequency $f=2.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find the maximum magnitudes of the velocity and of the acceleration.

$$
\omega=2\pi f=5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}\simeq15.7\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

$$
v_{\max}=\omega A=5\pi\cdot0.035\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq0.55\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{\max}&=\omega^2A\\[6pt]
        &=(5\pi)^2\cdot0.035\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
        &\simeq246.7\cdot0.035\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq8.64\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is harmonic with constant amplitude. The velocity has maximum magnitude $\omega A$ at $x=0$, while the acceleration $a=-\omega^2x$ has maximum magnitude $\omega^2A$ for $|x|=A$. The two values are therefore reached at different instants: where the velocity is maximum the acceleration is zero, and vice versa. With $\omega=5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ we have $\omega^2=25\pi^2\simeq246.7\,\mathrm{rad^2/s^2}$. Compared with the previous exercise, the frequency is the same and the amplitude is larger in the ratio $\dfrac{0.035}{0.028}=\dfrac{5}{4}$, so the maximum velocity also increases by that factor: $0.44\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot\dfrac{5}{4}=0.55\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The maximum acceleration is about $\dfrac{8.64}{9.81}\simeq0.88$ times $g$. As a check, $\omega v_{\max}$ gives the same value as $\omega^2A$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A harmonic oscillator with amplitude $0.035\,\mathrm{m}$ and frequency $2.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ passes through $x=+0.021\,\mathrm{m}$ moving toward equilibrium. Find the velocity and the acceleration, with their signs.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|v|&=\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}\\[6pt]
   &=5\pi\sqrt{0.035^2-0.021^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
   &=5\pi\sqrt{7.84\cdot10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
   &=5\pi\cdot0.028\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.14\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
v\simeq-0.440\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

$$
a=-\omega^2x=-(5\pi)^2\cdot0.021\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq-5.18\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is harmonic and the mass passes through $x=+0.021\,\mathrm{m}$ with velocity directed toward $x=0$. From the theorem on velocity as a function of position, $|v|=\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}$. Under the root, $0.035^2-0.021^2=0.001225-0.000441=0.000784$, whose root is $0.028$. The point is on the positive side and approaches $x=0$, so $x$ decreases and $v$ is negative. The acceleration $a=-\omega^2x$ is opposite to the positive displacement, so it is negative and has the same direction as the velocity: the magnitude of the velocity increases. Since $x$ is three fifths of $A$, the magnitude of the velocity is $\sqrt{1-\dfrac{9}{25}}=\dfrac{4}{5}$ of the maximum, that is, $0.8\cdot0.55\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.44\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in agreement with the calculation.

</div>
