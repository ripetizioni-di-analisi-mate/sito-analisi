## Exercise 1

A harmonic oscillator has amplitude $A=0.028\,\mathrm{m}$ and frequency $f=2.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find its maximum speed.

::grafico{f="2.8*cos(5*pi*x)" xmin=0 xmax=0.42 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3.5 nomi="t,x (cm)" punti="0,2.8,pieno;0.1,0,pieno;0.2,-2.8,pieno;0.3,0,pieno" titolo="With zero initial phase, the graph is steepest in magnitude at the equilibrium crossings. Time in seconds."}

$$
\omega=2\pi f=5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

$$
v_{\max}=\omega A=5\pi\cdot0.028\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq0.44\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the velocity equation $v=-\omega A\sin(\omega t+\varphi)$, the sine can reach either $+1$ or $-1$. In each case the body is at equilibrium, because the cosine of the same phase is zero. To convert $2.5$ cycles per second to phase advanced per second, multiply by $2\pi$. The result is a speed and is reached in both directions of motion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A harmonic oscillator has amplitude $A=0.035\,\mathrm{m}$ and frequency $f=2.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find the maximum speed and acceleration magnitude.

$$
\omega=5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

$$
v_{\max}=0.035\cdot5\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq0.55\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

$$
a_{\max}=0.035\cdot(5\pi)^2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq8.64\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Speed is greatest at $x=0$, while acceleration magnitude is greatest at $|x|=A$, since $a=-\omega^2 x$. The two results therefore describe different instants. At the same angular frequency, this oscillator travels a greater distance over the same period as in the previous exercise. Both magnitudes increase in the ratio of the amplitudes, which is $\dfrac{0.035}{0.028}=\dfrac{5}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A harmonic oscillator with amplitude $0.035\,\mathrm{m}$ and frequency $2.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$ passes through $x=+0.021\,\mathrm{m}$ moving toward equilibrium. Find its signed velocity and acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|v|&=\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}\\[6pt]
   &=5\pi\sqrt{0.035^2-0.021^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
   &=0.14\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
v\simeq-0.440\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

$$
a=-(5\pi)^2\cdot0.021\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq-5.18\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Harmonic motion gives $|v|=\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}$ because the square of the sine of a phase equals one minus the square of its cosine. The point is on the positive side and moves toward $x=0$, so $x$ decreases and $v$ is negative. The acceleration $a$ is also negative because it opposes the positive displacement. The two vectors point in the same direction here and the speed is increasing.

</div>
