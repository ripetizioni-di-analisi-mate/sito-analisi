## Exercise 1

An ideal mass-spring system oscillates without friction with $k=128\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and amplitude $A=0.045\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the total mechanical energy.

$$
E=\frac{1}{2}mv^2+\frac{1}{2}kx^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E=\frac{1}{2}kA^2\quad(v=0,\ |x|=A).
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot128\,\mathrm{N/m}\cdot(0.045\,\mathrm{m})^2\\[6pt]
 &=64\cdot2.025\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]
 &=0.1296\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We assume an ideal spring and no dissipative forces, so the mechanical energy is conserved because the spring force is conservative. The energy is evaluated at a turning point, where the velocity is zero and $|x|=A$: only the elastic term remains and the mass does not appear. The unit is $\mathrm{N/m\cdot m^2}=\mathrm{N\,m}=\mathrm{J}$. At equilibrium the elastic term vanishes and the same energy is entirely kinetic. With double the amplitude, $A=0.090\,\mathrm{m}$, the energy would be $0.5184\,\mathrm{J}$, four times larger, because $A$ appears squared. At $x=\dfrac{A}{2}$ the energy divides into $\dfrac{E}{4}=0.0324\,\mathrm{J}$ elastic and $\dfrac{3E}{4}=0.0972\,\mathrm{J}$ kinetic.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A frictionless mass-spring system has $k=160\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $A=0.045\,\mathrm{m}$, and $m=0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$. Find the total energy and the maximum magnitude of the velocity.

$$
E=\frac{1}{2}kA^2=\frac{160}{2}\cdot0.045^2\,\mathrm{J}=80\cdot2.025\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}=0.162\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2=E\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}.
$$

$$
v_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot0.162}{0.40}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\sqrt{0.81}\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The assumptions of the previous exercise hold. At the turning points the kinetic term vanishes and $E=\dfrac{1}{2}kA^2$. At equilibrium the elastic term vanishes, so $E=\dfrac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2$ and, solving for the velocity, $v_{\max}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2E}{m}}$, taken positive because a magnitude is requested. As a check, $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{160}{0.40}}\,\mathrm{rad/s}=20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\omega A=20\cdot0.045\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$, equal to the value found from the energy. In a single formula, $v_{\max}=A\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$, which depends on $k$ and $m$ only through their ratio.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The same system has $k=160\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $A=0.045\,\mathrm{m}$, and $m=0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$. At $x=0.027\,\mathrm{m}$ the mass is moving away from equilibrium. Find its kinetic energy and its velocity, with sign.

::grafico{f="8*x^2;162-8*x^2" xmin=-4.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=0 ymax=200 nomi="x,E (mJ)" punti="2.7,58.32,pieno;2.7,103.68,pieno" legenda="\text{elastic energy};\text{kinetic energy}" titolo="At x = 2.7 cm the two energies add up to 162 mJ. Horizontal coordinates in centimeters, energies in millijoules."}

$$
U=\frac{1}{2}kx^2=80\cdot0.027^2\,\mathrm{J}=80\cdot7.29\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{J}=0.05832\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=E-U=\frac{1}{2}k(A^2-x^2)\\[6pt]
 &=80(0.045^2-0.027^2)\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]
 &=(0.162-0.05832)\,\mathrm{J}=0.10368\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
v=+\sqrt{\frac{2K}{m}}=+\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot0.10368}{0.40}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=+\sqrt{0.5184}\,\mathrm{m/s}=+0.72\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Without dissipative forces the mechanical energy remains $0.162\,\mathrm{J}$ at every position. At $x=0.027\,\mathrm{m}$ the elastic energy $U=\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ is computed and subtracted from the total to obtain the kinetic energy. The relation $K=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ gives the magnitude of the velocity, smaller than $0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is reached at the center. The mass is to the right of equilibrium and moving away from it, so its coordinate increases and the velocity is positive. Since $\dfrac{x}{A}=\dfrac{0.027}{0.045}=0.60$, the elastic part is $36\%$ of the energy and the kinetic part is $64\%$. Indeed, $0.36\cdot0.162\,\mathrm{J}=0.0583\,\mathrm{J}$ and $0.64\cdot0.162\,\mathrm{J}=0.1037\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>
