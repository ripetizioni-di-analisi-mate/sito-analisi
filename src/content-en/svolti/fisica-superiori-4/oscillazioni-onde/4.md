## Exercise 1

An ideal mass–spring system oscillates without friction with $k=128\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and amplitude $A=0.045\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the total mechanical energy.

$$
E=\frac{1}{2}kA^2=\frac{128}{2}\cdot0.045^2\,\mathrm{J}=0.1296\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mechanical energy is conserved because the spring force is conservative and there is no friction. Evaluate the energy at a turning point, where the velocity is zero and the displacement has magnitude $A$. Only spring potential energy remains. The spring constant multiplies a distance squared, so the unit is $\mathrm{N\,m}=\mathrm{J}$. The same value is entirely kinetic as the mass passes through equilibrium.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A frictionless mass–spring system has $k=160\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $A=0.045\,\mathrm{m}$, and $m=0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$. Find the total energy and maximum speed.

$$
E=\frac{160}{2}\cdot0.045^2\,\mathrm{J}=0.162\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

$$
v_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot0.162}{0.40}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the turning points the kinetic term is zero, so the total energy follows from $\dfrac{1}{2}kA^2$. At equilibrium the spring potential term is zero, so all the energy determines the speed through $E=\dfrac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2$. Take the positive square root because we want a magnitude. A check follows from $\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}=20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, which gives $\omega A=0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The same system has $k=160\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $A=0.045\,\mathrm{m}$, and $m=0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$. At $x=0.027\,\mathrm{m}$ the mass moves away from equilibrium. Find its kinetic energy and signed velocity.

::grafico{f="0.008*x^2;0.162-0.008*x^2" xmin=-4.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=0 ymax=0.20 nomi="x,E (J)" punti="2.7,0.05832,pieno;2.7,0.10368,pieno" legenda="\text{spring potential energy};\text{kinetic energy}" titolo="At x = 2.7 cm the two energies add to 0.162 J. Horizontal coordinates in centimeters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=\frac{1}{2}k(A^2-x^2)\\[6pt]
 &=80(0.045^2-0.027^2)\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]
 &=0.10368\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
v=+\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot0.10368}{0.40}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=+0.72\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At $x=0.027\,\mathrm{m}$ the energy is partly kinetic and partly spring potential energy. Subtract the potential term at that position from the total. The result is less than $0.162\,\mathrm{J}$, as expected. Since the mass is to the right of equilibrium and moving away from it, its coordinate increases and its velocity is positive. The speed is less than $0.90\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is reached at the center.

</div>
