## Exercise 1

An oscillator completes $29$ oscillations in $12\,\mathrm{s}$. Find its frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{N}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{29}{12}\,\mathrm{Hz}\\[6pt]
 &\simeq 2.42\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $29$ counts returns to the initial state, meaning the same position and the same velocity. Frequency is the number of cycles per second, so divide the $29$ cycles by the measured $12\,\mathrm{s}$. The quotient has units of $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, equivalent to $\mathrm{Hz}$. The result is a little over two cycles per second, consistent with counting more than $24$ cycles in $12\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An oscillator completes $36$ oscillations in $12\,\mathrm{s}$. Find its frequency and period.

$$
f=\frac{36}{12}\,\mathrm{Hz}=3\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

$$
T=\frac{\Delta t}{N}=\frac{12}{36}\,\mathrm{s}=\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three cycles repeat each second, so the frequency is $3\,\mathrm{Hz}$. The period is the time taken by one cycle. Divide the same time interval by the cycle count, reversing the numerator and denominator from the calculation of $f$. In this case three intervals of $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{s}$ fill one second. The product $fT$ is $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The first displacement maximum is recorded at $t=1.2\,\mathrm{s}$ and the fifth at $t=3.6\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the period and frequency.

::grafico{f="cos(2*pi*(x-1.2)/0.6)" xmin=1.1 xmax=3.8 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 nomi="t,x/A" punti="1.2,1,pieno;1.8,1,pieno;2.4,1,pieno;3,1,pieno;3.6,1,pieno" titolo="Five maxima enclose four complete intervals. Time in seconds."}

$$
\Delta t=(3.6-1.2)\,\mathrm{s}=2.4\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
T=\frac{\Delta t}{5-1}=\frac{2.4}{4}\,\mathrm{s}=0.60\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
f=\frac{1}{T}=\frac{5}{3}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq1.67\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first maximum sets the start of the measurement. The first cycle ends at the second maximum, the second at the third, and the fourth at the fifth. There are therefore four periods between the first and fifth maxima. Dividing the time difference by four gives the duration of one cycle. Its reciprocal is measured in hertz and shows that more than one and fewer than two cycles occur in one second.

</div>
