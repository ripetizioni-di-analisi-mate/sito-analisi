## Exercise 1

An oscillator completes $29$ oscillations in $12\,\mathrm{s}$. Find its frequency and period.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{N}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{29}{12}\,\frac{1}{\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{29}{12}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq 2.42\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=\frac{1}{f}=\frac{\Delta t}{N}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{12}{29}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq0.414\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $29$ counts complete oscillations, that is, returns to the same position with the same velocity, so the frequency, which is the number of cycles per second, is obtained by dividing $N$ by $\Delta t$. The quotient has units of $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, that is, hertz. The period is the duration of one cycle and is obtained by dividing $\Delta t$ by $N$, that is, as the reciprocal of the frequency. The result is consistent with the count: $29$ cycles in $12\,\mathrm{s}$ are more than two cycles per second and each cycle lasts a little over four tenths of a second.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An oscillator completes $36$ oscillations in $12\,\mathrm{s}$. Find its frequency, period, and angular frequency.

$$
f=\frac{N}{\Delta t}=\frac{36}{12}\,\mathrm{Hz}=3\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

$$
T=\frac{\Delta t}{N}=\frac{12}{36}\,\mathrm{s}=\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq0.333\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
\omega=2\pi f=2\pi\cdot3\,\mathrm{rad/s}=6\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}\simeq18.8\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We assume that the $36$ cycles all have the same duration. Three cycles repeat in one second, so $f=3\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and one cycle lasts one third of a second. The phase advances by $2\pi$ radians in each cycle, so in one second it advances by $2\pi$ times the number of cycles per second, that is, $\omega=2\pi f$. The angular frequency is measured in radians per second and has a different value from the frequency: $6\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$ per second is three complete turns, and in $12\,\mathrm{s}$ the $36$ measured cycles accumulate. The angular frequency is needed to write the displacement law $x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\varphi)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The first displacement maximum is recorded at $t=1.2\,\mathrm{s}$ and the fifth at $t=3.6\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the period and the frequency.

::grafico{f="cos(2*pi*(x-1.2)/0.6)" xmin=1.1 xmax=3.8 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 nomi="t,x/A" punti="1.2,1,pieno;1.8,1,pieno;2.4,1,pieno;3,1,pieno;3.6,1,pieno" titolo="Five maxima enclose four complete intervals. Times in seconds."}

$$
\Delta t=(3.6-1.2)\,\mathrm{s}=2.4\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
N=5-1=4,\qquad T=\frac{\Delta t}{N}=\frac{2.4}{4}\,\mathrm{s}=0.60\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
f=\frac{1}{T}=\frac{1}{0.60}\,\mathrm{Hz}=\frac{5}{3}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq1.67\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We assume a constant period and no missing maxima in the record. The first cycle ends at the second maximum, the second at the third, and the fourth at the fifth, so between the first and the fifth maximum there are four periods, as the four intervals between the five points of the figure show. In general, the number of periods between two maxima is the difference of their ordinal numbers. Dividing $\Delta t=2.4\,\mathrm{s}$ by $4$ gives $T=0.60\,\mathrm{s}$, whereas a division by $5$ would give $0.48\,\mathrm{s}$, which contradicts the figure. The frequency is the reciprocal of the period and is a little under two cycles per second, in agreement with the four cycles completed in $2.4\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
