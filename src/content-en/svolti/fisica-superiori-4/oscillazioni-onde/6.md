## Exercise 1

A mechanical wave travels at $u=19.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with frequency $f=6\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find its wavelength.

$$
T=\frac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
\lambda=uT=\frac{u}{f}=\frac{19.2}{6}\,\mathrm{m}=3.2\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Six crests pass a fixed point in the medium every second, so the time between successive passages is $\dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{s}$. During that interval the first crest advances by one wavelength. Multiplying wave speed by period gives a distance in meters. A snapshot also lets us measure that distance between successive crests.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wave travels at $u=24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with $f=6\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find $\lambda$ and the wavenumber $k$.

::grafico{f="cos(pi*x/2)" xmin=0 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.3 nomi="x,y/A" punti="0,1,pieno;2,-1,pieno;4,1,pieno" titolo="The next crest is 4 m from the first. The trough lies halfway between them. Horizontal coordinates in meters."}

$$
\lambda=\frac{24}{6}\,\mathrm{m}=4\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

$$
k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}=\frac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad/m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A crest travels four meters in one period. Two positions that far apart differ by $2\pi$ radians in wave phase. The phase therefore changes by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ radians per meter. At a fixed time, moving two meters along the profile adds $\pi$ to the phase and takes you from a crest to a trough.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $y(x,t)=A\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}x+12\pi t\right)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds, follow the crest at $x=4\,\mathrm{m}$ when $t=0$. Find its position at $t=\dfrac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{s}$.

::grafico{f="cos(pi*x/2);cos(pi*x/2+pi/2)" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.3 nomi="x,y/A" punti="4,1,pieno;3,1,pieno" legenda="t = 0;t = 1/24 s" titolo="The two snapshots show the same crest first at x = 4 m and then at x = 3 m. Horizontal coordinates in meters."}

$$
\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot4=2\pi.
$$

$$
\frac{\pi}{2}x+12\pi\cdot\frac{1}{24}=2\pi.
$$

$$
\frac{\pi}{2}x=\frac{3\pi}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=3\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Track the crest by keeping its phase constant at the initial value $2\pi$. Since the contribution from $t$ increases by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the contribution from $x$ must decrease by the same amount. The profile therefore shifts one meter to the left. Also, $\omega=12\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, so the elapsed time is one quarter of the period and the crest travels one quarter of the $4\,\mathrm{m}$ wavelength.

</div>
