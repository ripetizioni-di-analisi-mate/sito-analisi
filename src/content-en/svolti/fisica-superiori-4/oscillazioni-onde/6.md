## Exercise 1

A mechanical wave propagates at $u=19.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with frequency $f=6\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find the wavelength.

$$
T=\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
\lambda=uT=\frac{u}{f}=\frac{19.2\,\mathrm{m/s}}{6\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}}=3.2\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wave is periodic and the medium is uniform, so $u$ is constant. Six crests per second pass through a fixed point, and between two consecutive passages $\dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{s}$ elapses. In that interval the first crest advances by one wavelength, because the next one reaches the point when the previous one has covered the distance that separates them. Therefore $\lambda=uT=\dfrac{u}{f}$. The same distance is measured in an instantaneous snapshot between two consecutive crests. With $\lambda=3.2\,\mathrm{m}$ the crest travels six wavelengths in one second, that is, $19.2\,\mathrm{m}$. Since $u$ is fixed by the medium, at $12\,\mathrm{Hz}$ the wavelength would be $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$ and at $3\,\mathrm{Hz}$ it would be $6.4\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wave propagates at $u=24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with $f=6\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find $\lambda$ and the wavenumber $k$.

::grafico{f="cos(pi*x/2)" xmin=0 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.3 nomi="x,y/A" punti="0,1,pieno;2,-1,pieno;4,1,pieno" titolo="The next maximum is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from the first. The minimum lies halfway between. Horizontal coordinates in meters."}

$$
\lambda=\frac{u}{f}=\frac{24}{6}\,\mathrm{m}=4\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

$$
k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}=\frac{2\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad/m}=\frac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad/m}\simeq1.57\,\mathrm{rad/m}.
$$

$$
k\,\Delta x=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot2\,\mathrm{rad}=\pi\,\mathrm{rad}\qquad(\Delta x=2\,\mathrm{m}).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wave is harmonic and the medium uniform, so $\lambda$ is constant. A crest travels four meters in one period, and two positions at that distance differ by $2\pi$ radians in phase, so $k=\dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}$. The phase changes by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ radians per meter. At a fixed time, moving two meters along the profile accumulates $\pi$ and takes us from a maximum to a minimum, as the figure shows. The wavenumber has units $\mathrm{rad/m}$ and must be distinguished from the spring constant, which has the same symbol $k$ but units $\mathrm{N/m}$. As a check, $\omega=2\pi f=12\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $\dfrac{\omega}{k}=\dfrac{12\pi}{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=24\,\mathrm{m/s}$ reproduces the given speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $y(x,t)=A\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}x+12\pi t\right)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds, follow the crest that is at $x=4\,\mathrm{m}$ at $t=0$. Find its position at $t=\dfrac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{s}$.

::grafico{f="cos(pi*x/2);cos(pi*x/2+pi/2)" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.3 nomi="x,y/A" punti="4,1,pieno;3,1,pieno" legenda="t = 0;t = 1/24 s" titolo="The two snapshots show the same crest first at x = 4 m and then at x = 3 m. Horizontal coordinates in meters."}

$$
\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot4+12\pi\cdot0=2\pi.
$$

$$
\frac{\pi}{2}x+12\pi\cdot\frac{1}{24}=2\pi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{\pi}{2}x+\frac{\pi}{2}=2\pi.
$$

$$
\frac{\pi}{2}x=\frac{3\pi}{2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=3\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

$$
u=\frac{\omega}{k}=\frac{12\pi}{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,\mathrm{m/s}=24\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad \Delta x=-u\,t=-24\cdot\frac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{m}=-1\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wave is harmonic and propagates without damping, so each crest keeps its phase. At the initial position the phase of the crest is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\cdot4=2\pi$. After $\dfrac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{s}$ the term in $t$ equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the term in $x$ must decrease by the same amount and the crest is at $x=3\,\mathrm{m}$. The sign $+$ in front of $12\pi t$ indicates propagation toward decreasing $x$, with speed of magnitude $\dfrac{\omega}{k}=24\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and in $\dfrac{1}{24}\,\mathrm{s}$ the crest travels $1\,\mathrm{m}$. The period is $\dfrac{1}{6}\,\mathrm{s}$, so the elapsed time is a quarter of the period and the displacement is a quarter of $\lambda=4\,\mathrm{m}$. The second snapshot shows the maximum at $x=3\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
