## Exercise 1

A mass $m=0.36\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to an ideal spring with $k=180\,\mathrm{N/m}$ on a frictionless surface. Find the period.

::grafico{f="-1.8*x" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-8 ymax=8 nomi="x,F (N)" punti="-2,3.6,pieno;0,0,pieno;2,-3.6,pieno" titolo="The spring force has the opposite sign to the displacement. Horizontal coordinates in centimeters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}\\[6pt]
 &=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.36}{180}}\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
 &\simeq0.281\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring force acts along the spring axis and opposes displacement from equilibrium. Newton's second law gives $a=-\dfrac{k}{m}x$. The ratio $\dfrac{k}{m}$ sets the square of the angular frequency, and the period is the time to advance through $2\pi$ radians. In the calculation, $\dfrac{m}{k}$ has units of $\mathrm{s^2}$, so its square root gives seconds. The period found applies at any amplitude within the range where the force remains linear.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass $m=0.45\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to an ideal spring with $k=180\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Find the period and frequency in the absence of friction.

$$
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{180}{0.45}}\,\mathrm{rad/s}=20\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}=\frac{\pi}{10}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq0.314\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
f=\frac{10}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq3.18\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the spring force is the only force along the axis, $\omega^2$ is the ratio of the spring constant to the mass. Its square root is $20\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. A cycle covers $2\pi$ radians, so dividing $2\pi$ by $20$ gives the period. Take the reciprocal of the exact value $\dfrac{\pi}{10}\,\mathrm{s}$ before rounding, so the period and frequency come from the same calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $0.45\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass on a $180\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring has a period of $\dfrac{\pi}{10}\,\mathrm{s}$. A $360\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring is used instead. What mass gives twice the period?

$$
\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^2=\frac{m_2 k_1}{m_1 k_2}=4.
$$

$$
m_2=4\,m_1\,\frac{k_2}{k_1}=4\cdot0.45\cdot2\,\mathrm{kg}=3.60\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both periods contain the same factor $2\pi$, which cancels in the ratio. To double the period, the ratio $\dfrac{m}{k}$ must be four times as large. The new spring has twice the spring constant and would halve $\dfrac{m}{k}$ at fixed mass. The new mass must therefore be eight times the original mass. Check by substituting $\dfrac{3.60}{360}=0.010$ for $\dfrac{0.45}{180}=0.0025$ in the ratio under the square root.

</div>
