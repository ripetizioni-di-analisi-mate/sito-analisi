## Exercise 1

A simple pendulum of length $\ell=0.64\,\mathrm{m}$ oscillates with small amplitude and negligible friction. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the period.

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.64}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq1.60\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At small angles the tangential component of the weight is approximately proportional to displacement along the arc. Dividing this force by mass gives an angular frequency set by $g$ and $\ell$, so the mass drops out of the calculation. Under the square root, length divided by acceleration gives $\mathrm{s^2}$. The result, about $1.60\,\mathrm{s}$, is the time to return to the same position with the same direction of motion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A simple pendulum of length $\ell=0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ oscillates with small amplitude. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find its period and frequency.

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.80}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq1.79\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{9.81}{0.80}}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq0.557\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The length under the square root is greater than for the first pendulum and $g$ is the same, so we expect a period longer than $1.60\,\mathrm{s}$. Find frequency by taking the reciprocal of the period before rounding. One cycle takes nearly two seconds, so the number of cycles per second is slightly greater than half a hertz.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pendulum with $\ell=0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is moved to a location where $g=1.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The oscillation angle remains small. Find the new period and its ratio to the period at $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
T_2=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.80}{1.62}}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq4.42\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\sqrt{\frac{g_1}{g_2}}=\sqrt{\frac{9.81}{1.62}}\simeq2.46.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A smaller value of $g$ reduces the component of the weight that brings the mass back toward equilibrium. At fixed length the motion therefore proceeds more slowly. In the ratio of periods, $2\pi$ and $\sqrt{\ell}$ both cancel. The accelerations appear in reverse order because $T$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$. The ratio is dimensionless and is consistent with the change from about $1.79$ to $4.42\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
