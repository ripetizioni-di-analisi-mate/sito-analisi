## Exercise 1

A simple pendulum of length $\ell=0.64\,\mathrm{m}$ oscillates with small amplitude and negligible friction. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the period.

$$
\frac{\ell}{g}=\frac{0.64\,\mathrm{m}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}=0.0652\,\mathrm{s^2}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}}\\[6pt]
 &=2\pi\sqrt{0.0652}\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
 &=2\pi\cdot0.2554\,\mathrm{s}\simeq1.60\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible and of negligible mass, the mass is a point, and the amplitude is small, so that $\sin\theta\simeq\theta$. Under these conditions the tangential component of the weight is proportional to the displacement along the arc, the mass cancels, and the angular frequency depends only on $g$ and $\ell$. Under the root we have $\dfrac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{m/s^2}}=\mathrm{s^2}$, so the result is in seconds. In one minute the pendulum completes about $\dfrac{60}{1.60}\simeq37$ full oscillations. A pendulum four times as long, $\ell=2.56\,\mathrm{m}$, would have double the period, about $3.21\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A simple pendulum of length $\ell=0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ oscillates with small amplitude. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the period and the frequency.

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.80}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s}=2\pi\cdot0.2856\,\mathrm{s}\simeq1.79\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
f=\frac{1}{T}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{9.81}{0.80}}\,\mathrm{Hz}=\frac{3.502}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq0.557\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same assumptions apply: inextensible string, point mass, negligible friction, and small oscillations. At the same $g$ the length is greater than in the previous exercise, so the period is longer than $1.60\,\mathrm{s}$, and the calculation gives $1.79\,\mathrm{s}$. The frequency is computed as the reciprocal of the period before rounding. The ratio of the two periods, $\dfrac{1.79}{1.60}\simeq1.12$, coincides with $\sqrt{\dfrac{0.80}{0.64}}=\sqrt{1.25}\simeq1.12$, because at the same $g$ the period is proportional to the square root of the length. The period is the round-trip time, whereas a half-oscillation, from one extreme to the other, lasts $\dfrac{T}{2}\simeq0.90\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The pendulum with $\ell=0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ is taken to a place where $g=1.62\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The oscillation angle remains small. Find the new period and its ratio to the period at $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
T_2=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.80}{1.62}}\,\mathrm{s}=2\pi\cdot0.7027\,\mathrm{s}\simeq4.42\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{2\pi\sqrt{\ell/g_2}}{2\pi\sqrt{\ell/g_1}}=\sqrt{\frac{g_1}{g_2}}=\sqrt{\frac{9.81}{1.62}}\simeq2.46.
$$

$$
T_1\cdot\frac{T_2}{T_1}=1.794\,\mathrm{s}\cdot2.461\simeq4.42\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same assumptions apply and the angular amplitude remains small in the new place as well. A smaller value of $g$ reduces the component of the weight that returns the mass toward equilibrium, so the motion is slower. In the ratio of the periods, $2\pi$ and $\sqrt{\ell}$ cancel, and the accelerations appear in inverse order because $T$ is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{g}}$. The ratio $\sqrt{\dfrac{9.81}{1.62}}=\sqrt{6.06}\simeq2.46$ has no units, and the third line recovers $T_2$ by multiplying $T_1=1.794\,\mathrm{s}$ by this ratio. The period does not depend on the suspended mass, so the value holds for any body attached to the string.

</div>
