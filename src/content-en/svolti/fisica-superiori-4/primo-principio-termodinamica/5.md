## Exercise 1

A rigid container holds $0.96\,\mathrm{mol}$ of a monatomic ideal gas. Its temperature increases by $35\,\mathrm{K}$. Volume work is the only possible form of work. Calculate the heat absorbed, using $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=0, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T \\[6pt]
&=0.96\cdot\frac32\cdot8.314\cdot35\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq419\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The container is rigid, so the pressure can rise while the boundary displacement remains zero. Since only volume work is possible, the total work is zero. The heat entering the gas accounts for the entire increase in internal energy. Substitute the amount of gas in moles, the monatomic value of $C_V$, and the $35\,\mathrm{K}$ into the formula. The heat is positive, as required by an increase in temperature at constant volume.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The temperature is increased by $35\,\mathrm{K}$ for two identical $1.2\,\mathrm{mol}$ samples of a monatomic ideal gas, one at constant volume and the other quasistatically at constant pressure. Calculate both amounts of heat and their difference. Use $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_V&=1.2\cdot\frac32\cdot8.314\cdot35\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq524\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q_p&=1.2\cdot\frac52\cdot8.314\cdot35\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq873\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q_p-Q_V&=nR\Delta T\simeq349\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the constant-volume sample, $Q_V=\Delta U$. In the other sample, the piston moves during heating and the gas does work $nR\Delta T$. Both samples have the same temperature increase and the same $\Delta U$. Therefore the difference between the amounts of heat **is the work of the isobaric process**. Subtracting the two products $nC_p\Delta T$ and $nC_V\Delta T$ gives the same result because $C_p-C_V=R$.

</div>
