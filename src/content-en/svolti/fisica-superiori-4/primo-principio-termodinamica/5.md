## Exercise 1

A rigid container holds $0.96\,\mathrm{mol}$ of monatomic ideal gas. The temperature increases by $35\,\mathrm{K}$. The only possible work is volume work. Calculate the heat absorbed with $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

::grafico{linee="2,1 5,1 5,4 2,4 2,1" vettori="0.5,2.5,1.95,2.5,Q" etichette="3.5,2.55,V constant" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=5 assi=0 titolo="The walls of the container are fixed. Heat enters and the volume work is zero."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=0, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U+W=\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T, \\[6pt]
nC_V&=0.96\cdot\frac32\cdot8.314\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{K}}\simeq11.97\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{K}}, \\[6pt]
Q&=11.97\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{K}}\cdot35\,\mathrm{K} \\[6pt]
&\simeq419\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The container is rigid, so the boundary of the system does not move and the pressure can increase without the gas doing work. Since only volume work is allowed, the total work is zero. The first law then gives $Q=\Delta U$, that is, all the heat that enters increases the internal energy.

For a monatomic ideal gas $C_V=\dfrac32R$, so $nC_V$ is about $11.97\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Multiplying by the temperature rise of $35\,\mathrm{K}$ gives a heat of about **$419\,\mathrm{J}$**. The heat is positive, as required for heating at constant volume. With double the moles and the same temperature rise, the heat would double and be about $838\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two identical samples of $1.2\,\mathrm{mol}$ of monatomic ideal gas are heated by $35\,\mathrm{K}$, one at constant volume and the other quasistatically at constant pressure. Calculate the two heats and their difference. Use $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
nC_V&=1.2\cdot\frac32\cdot8.314\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{K}}\simeq14.97\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{K}}, \\[6pt]
Q_V&=nC_V\Delta T=14.97\cdot35\,\mathrm{J}\simeq524\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
nC_p&=1.2\cdot\frac52\cdot8.314\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{K}}\simeq24.94\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{K}}, \\[6pt]
Q_p&=nC_p\Delta T=24.94\cdot35\,\mathrm{J}\simeq873\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q_p-Q_V&=nR\Delta T=1.2\cdot8.314\cdot35\,\mathrm{J}\simeq349\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the constant-volume sample the work is zero, so $Q_V=\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T$. In the constant-pressure sample the piston moves during the heating and the gas does the work $W=nR\Delta T$. For an ideal gas the internal energy depends only on temperature, so the two samples, with the same $\Delta T$, have the same change $\Delta U$.

In the second sample the heat must cover both $\Delta U$ and the work, so $Q_p=\Delta U+W$ is greater than $Q_V$. The difference between the heats **is the work of the isobaric process**, about $349\,\mathrm{J}$. The same number is obtained by subtracting $nC_V\Delta T$ from $nC_p\Delta T$, because by Mayer's relation $C_p-C_V=R$. The ratio $\dfrac{Q_p}{Q_V}=\dfrac{C_p}{C_V}$ is $\dfrac{5}{3}$ for a monatomic gas, in agreement with $\dfrac{873}{524}\simeq1.67$.

</div>
