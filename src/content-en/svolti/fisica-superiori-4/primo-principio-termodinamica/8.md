## Exercise 1

A gas traverses a rectangular cycle clockwise between pressures of $100$ and $208\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and volumes of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{L}$. Calculate the net work.

::grafico{linee="2,100 2,208 6,208 6,100 2,100" area="208;100;2;6" vettori="2,125,2,180;3,208,5,208;6,180,6,125;5,100,3,100" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=300 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="The expansion takes place on the upper side. The compression takes place on the lower side. Volume is in liters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=(6-2)\,\mathrm{L}=4\,\mathrm{L}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{esp}}&=p_{\max}\,\Delta V=208\cdot4\,\mathrm{J}=832\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{comp}}&=p_{\min}(-\Delta V)=100\cdot(-4)\,\mathrm{J}=-400\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(832-400)\,\mathrm{J}=432\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(208-100)\,\mathrm{kPa}\cdot4\,\mathrm{L}=432\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the clockwise direction the gas traverses the upper side from left to right, expanding at $208\,\mathrm{kPa}$, and the lower side from right to left, compressing at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The two vertical sides are at constant volume, so they contribute zero work.

The work of the expansion is positive and equals $208\cdot4=832\,\mathrm{J}$. The work of the compression is negative and equals $100\cdot(-4)=-400\,\mathrm{J}$. The higher pressure acts during the expansion, so the positive work exceeds the negative work in magnitude. The net work is the sum of the two, that is, **$432\,\mathrm{J}$**. The same value is obtained by multiplying the height of the rectangle, $108\,\mathrm{kPa}$, by the base, $4\,\mathrm{L}$, because in a cycle the net work is the enclosed area. Since the cycle returns the gas to its initial state, the net heat exchanged equals $432\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular cycle traversed counterclockwise has extreme pressures of $100$ and $260\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and extreme volumes of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{L}$. In each cycle, $2440\,\mathrm{J}$ enters as heat, in total, along the absorption segments. Calculate the net work, the net heat, and the heat released in absolute value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{100}&=100\cdot(6-2)\,\mathrm{J}=400\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W_{260}&=260\cdot(2-6)\,\mathrm{J}=-1040\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(400-1040)\,\mathrm{J}=-640\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U_{\mathrm{ciclo}}&=0,\qquad Q_{\mathrm{tot}}=W_{\mathrm{tot}}=-640\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
|Q_{\mathrm{ced}}|&=Q_{\mathrm{ass}}-Q_{\mathrm{tot}} \\[6pt]
&=[2440-(-640)]\,\mathrm{J}=3080\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the counterclockwise direction the gas expands at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$, doing $400\,\mathrm{J}$ of work, and is compressed at $260\,\mathrm{kPa}$, receiving $1040\,\mathrm{J}$. The net work is the sum of the two, that is, $-640\,\mathrm{J}$. Its magnitude is the area enclosed by the rectangle, $160\,\mathrm{kPa}\cdot4\,\mathrm{L}$, and the negative sign indicates that in the cycle the surroundings do more work on the gas than the gas does on the surroundings.

Since the gas returns to its initial state, the change in internal energy is zero and the net heat equals the net work: $Q_{\mathrm{tot}}=-640\,\mathrm{J}$. The $2440\,\mathrm{J}$ given in the statement is the heat entering, denoted $Q_{\mathrm{ass}}$. The net heat is the sum of the heat entering and the heat leaving, which has a negative sign, so the magnitude of the heat released is $Q_{\mathrm{ass}}-Q_{\mathrm{tot}}=2440+640$. From the cycle, **$3080\,\mathrm{J}$ leaves as heat**.

</div>
