## Exercise 1

A gas follows a clockwise rectangular cycle between pressures of $100$ and $208\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and volumes of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{L}$. Calculate the net work.

::grafico{linee="2,100 2,208 6,208 6,100 2,100" area="208;100;2;6" vettori="2,125,2,180;3,208,5,208;6,180,6,125;5,100,3,100" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=270 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="Expansion takes place along the upper side. Compression takes place along the lower side. Volume is in liters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm{esp}}&=208(6-2)\,\mathrm{J}=832\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{comp}}&=100(2-6)\,\mathrm{J}=-400\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(832-400)\,\mathrm{J}=432\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving clockwise, the gas follows the upper segment to the right and the lower segment to the left. The two vertical segments are at constant volume and contribute zero work. The higher pressure acts during expansion, so the positive work of $832\,\mathrm{J}$ exceeds the magnitude of the negative compression work. The difference of $432\,\mathrm{J}$ is also obtained by multiplying the rectangle's height, $108\,\mathrm{kPa}$, by its width, $4\,\mathrm{L}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A counterclockwise rectangular cycle has extreme pressures of $100$ and $260\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and extreme volumes of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{L}$. In each cycle, a total of $2440\,\mathrm{J}$ enters as heat along the heat absorption segments. Calculate the net work, the net heat, and the magnitude of the heat released.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=-(260-100)(6-2)\,\mathrm{J}=-640\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U_{\mathrm{ciclo}}&=0,\qquad Q_{\mathrm{tot}}=-640\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
|Q_{\mathrm{ced}}|&=Q_{\mathrm{ass}}-Q_{\mathrm{tot}} \\[6pt]
&=[2440-(-640)]\,\mathrm{J}=3080\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving counterclockwise, the gas expands at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and is compressed at $260\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The net work is negative and its magnitude is the area enclosed by the rectangle. Since the gas returns to its initial state, the change in internal energy is zero and net heat equals net work. The $2440\,\mathrm{J}$ given is the heat entering the gas. For a heat balance of $-640\,\mathrm{J}$, $2440+640=3080$ joules must leave as heat.

</div>
