## Exercise 1

An ideal gas of $0.52\,\mathrm{mol}$ expands reversibly at $320\,\mathrm{K}$ from $10$ to $25\,\mathrm{L}$. Calculate the work with $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

::grafico{f="1383.4496/x" area="1383.4496/x;0;10;25" punti="10,138.34496,pieno,A;25,55.337984,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=30 ymin=0 ymax=200 nomi="V,p (kPa)" legenda="p=\dfrac{nRT}{V}" titolo="During the expansion the pressure decreases from A to B. The shaded area measures the work. Volume is in liters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
nRT&=0.52\cdot8.314\cdot320\,\mathrm{J}\simeq1383\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
p_A&=\frac{nRT}{V_i}=\frac{1383\,\mathrm{J}}{10\,\mathrm{L}}\simeq138\,\mathrm{kPa}, \qquad p_B=\frac{1383\,\mathrm{J}}{25\,\mathrm{L}}\simeq55\,\mathrm{kPa}, \\[6pt]
W&=nRT\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} \\[6pt]
&=1383\,\mathrm{J}\cdot\ln\frac{25}{10}=1383\,\mathrm{J}\cdot0.916 \\[6pt]
&\simeq1.27\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expansion is reversible and the temperature is constant, so at every instant the pressure of the gas is $p=\dfrac{nRT}{V}$ and decreases as the volume grows. The values at the endpoints, about $138\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at A and $55\,\mathrm{kPa}$ at B, are those on the graph. The work is the area under this hyperbola between $V_i$ and $V_f$, and its value is given by the formula with the natural logarithm.

The volume ratio is $\dfrac{25}{10}=2.5$ and has no units, because the two volumes are expressed in liters. Since it is greater than one, its logarithm, about $0.916$, is positive. The work done by the gas is therefore positive and equals about $1.27\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}$. Since in an ideal gas isothermal process $\Delta U=0$, the gas **absorbs the same amount as heat** and transfers it to the surroundings as work.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal gas of $0.65\,\mathrm{mol}$ is compressed reversibly at $320\,\mathrm{K}$ from $25$ to $10\,\mathrm{L}$. With $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$, calculate $\Delta U$, $W$, and $Q$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&=0, \\[6pt]
nRT&=0.65\cdot8.314\cdot320\,\mathrm{J}\simeq1729\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W&=nRT\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}=1729\,\mathrm{J}\cdot\ln\frac{10}{25} \\[6pt]
&=1729\,\mathrm{J}\cdot(-0.916) \\[6pt]
&\simeq-1.58\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U+W\simeq-1.58\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas is ideal and the temperature stays constant, so the internal energy does not change and $\Delta U=0$. The final volume is the smaller one, so the argument of the logarithm, $\dfrac{10}{25}=0.4$, is less than one and its logarithm is negative. It follows that the work done by the gas is negative: the surroundings transfer energy to the gas by compressing it.

From the first law $Q=\Delta U+W$ and from $\Delta U=0$ one obtains $Q=W$. The energy received as work must therefore leave entirely as heat, toward the reservoir that keeps the temperature at $320\,\mathrm{K}$. The gas **releases about $1.58\,\mathrm{kJ}$** and its internal energy stays constant. The magnitude is greater than in the previous exercise because here the amount is $0.65$ mol instead of $0.52$ mol, while the volume ratio is the inverse, so only the sign of the logarithm changes.

</div>
