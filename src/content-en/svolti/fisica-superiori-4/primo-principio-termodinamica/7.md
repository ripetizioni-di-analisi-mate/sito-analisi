## Exercise 1

An ideal gas containing $0.52\,\mathrm{mol}$ expands reversibly at $320\,\mathrm{K}$ from $10$ to $25\,\mathrm{L}$. Calculate the work using $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

::grafico{f="1383.4496/x" area="1383.4496/x;0;10;25" punti="10,138.34496,pieno,A;25,55.337984,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=30 ymin=0 ymax=200 nomi="V,p (kPa)" legenda="p=\dfrac{nRT}{V}" titolo="During expansion the pressure falls from A to B. Volume is in liters. The shaded area measures the work. Volume is in liters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=nRT\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} \\[6pt]
&=0.52\cdot8.314\cdot320\ln\frac{25}{10}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq1.27\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expansion is reversible and the temperature is constant. The pressure at each volume is $\dfrac{nRT}{V}$ and falls as the gas expands. The area under the curve gives the expression with the natural logarithm. The volume ratio is $\dfrac{25}{10}$ and is dimensionless because both volumes are expressed in liters. It is greater than one, so its logarithm is positive. The result represents the energy that the gas transfers to the surroundings as work.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal gas containing $0.65\,\mathrm{mol}$ is compressed reversibly at $320\,\mathrm{K}$ from $25$ to $10\,\mathrm{L}$. Using $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$, calculate $\Delta U$, $W$, and $Q$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&=0, \\[6pt]
W&=0.65\cdot8.314\cdot320\ln\frac{10}{25}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq-1.58\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U+W\simeq-1.58\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final volume is the smaller one, so the argument of the logarithm is less than one and the work done by the gas is negative. The surroundings thus transfer energy to the gas by compressing it. Because the gas is ideal and its temperature stays constant, all that energy must leave as heat, and the balance requires $Q=W$. The gas **releases** about $1.58\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and its internal energy remains constant.

</div>
