## Exercise 1

A gas expands quasistatically from $2$ to $5\,\mathrm{L}$ at the constant pressure of $104\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate the work done by the gas.

::grafico{linee="2,104 5,104" area="104;0;2;5" punti="2,104,pieno,A;5,104,pieno,B" vettori="2.8,104,4.25,104" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=150 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="The volume increases from A to B at the same pressure. Volume is in liters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=V_f-V_i=(5-2)\,\mathrm{L}=3\,\mathrm{L}=3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}, \\[6pt]
p&=104\,\mathrm{kPa}=1.04\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}, \\[6pt]
W&=p\,\Delta V \\[6pt]
&=1.04\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} \\[6pt]
&=312\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During the expansion the pressure stays equal to $104\,\mathrm{kPa}$, so the path on the $p$-$V$ diagram is a horizontal segment. The work is the signed area of the rectangle between the path and the volume axis, so it is calculated as $p\,\Delta V$.

The base of the rectangle is the **change** in volume, $5-2=3$ liters, because the piston moves only during that segment. To obtain joules the data are converted to SI units: $1\,\mathrm{kPa}=10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $1\,\mathrm{L}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Since $1\,\mathrm{Pa\cdot m^3}=1\,\mathrm{J}$, the product is $312\,\mathrm{J}$. The factors $10^3$ and $10^{-3}$ cancel, so the product $\mathrm{kPa}\cdot\mathrm{L}$ is directly equivalent to joules. The volume increases, so the work done by the gas is positive: the gas **does $312\,\mathrm{J}$** on the surroundings. For the same change in volume, a doubled pressure would give double the work, that is, $624\,\mathrm{J}$, because the work is proportional to the pressure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A gas is compressed quasistatically from $5$ to $2\,\mathrm{L}$ at $130\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The compression lasts $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the work done by the gas and the average mechanical power supplied by the piston.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=(2-5)\,\mathrm{L}=-3\,\mathrm{L}=-3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}, \\[6pt]
W&=p\,\Delta V=1.30\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot(-3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}) \\[6pt]
&=-390\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{pistone}}&=\frac{-W}{\Delta t} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{390\,\mathrm{J}}{2.0\,\mathrm{s}}=195\,\mathrm{W}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The segment is traversed toward smaller volumes, so the change in volume is negative: $\Delta V=-3\,\mathrm{L}$. The pressure is constant, so $W=p\,\Delta V$ still applies and gives $W=-390\,\mathrm{J}$ in the gas convention. The negative sign indicates that energy passes from the piston to the gas.

The piston **supplies** energy to the gas and the work it does is $-W=390\,\mathrm{J}$. The average power is the energy transferred divided by the duration of the compression, so $390\,\mathrm{J}$ is divided by $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. The quotient is positive and is expressed in watts, that is, in joules per second. The average mechanical power supplied by the piston is **$195\,\mathrm{W}$**. For the same energy transferred, a compression of $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ would require an average power of $390\,\mathrm{W}$, because the power is inversely proportional to the duration.

</div>
