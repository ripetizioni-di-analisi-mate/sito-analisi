## Exercise 1

A gas expands quasistatically from $2$ to $5\,\mathrm{L}$ at a constant pressure of $104\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate the work done by the gas.

::grafico{linee="2,104 5,104" area="104;0;2;5" punti="2,104,pieno,A;5,104,pieno,B" vettori="2.8,104,4.25,104" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=150 nomi="V,p (kPa)" titolo="The volume increases from A to B at the same pressure. Volume is in liters."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=(5-2)\,\mathrm{L}=3\,\mathrm{L}, \\[6pt]
W&=p\Delta V=104\cdot3\,\mathrm{kPa}\cdot\mathrm{L} \\[6pt]
&=312\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The path is horizontal because the pressure stays at $104\,\mathrm{kPa}$ during expansion. The work is therefore the area of the rectangle between the path and the volume axis. The width is the **change** in volume, $5-2=3$ liters, because the piston moves only along that segment. The product $\mathrm{kPa}\cdot\mathrm{L}$ equals a joule because the factors $10^3$ and $10^{-3}$ cancel. The volume increases, so the work done by the gas is positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A gas is compressed quasistatically from $5$ to $2\,\mathrm{L}$ at $130\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Compression takes $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the work done by the gas and the average mechanical power supplied by the piston.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=p(V_f-V_i)=130(2-5)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=-390\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{pistone}}&=\frac{-W}{\Delta t} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{390\,\mathrm{J}}{2.0\,\mathrm{s}}=195\,\mathrm{W}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The path is traversed toward smaller volumes, so the volume change is negative. Multiplying by the constant pressure gives $W=-390\,\mathrm{J}$ in the gas convention. The piston **supplies** energy to the gas, and the work it does equals $-W$. The average power of the piston is that energy divided by the duration of compression. The result is positive and measured in watts, or joules per second.

</div>
