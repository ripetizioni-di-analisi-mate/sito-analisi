## Exercise 1

A monatomic ideal gas of $0.60\,\mathrm{mol}$ is heated quasistatically at constant pressure by $60\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the heat absorbed with $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

::grafico{linee="2,1 2,4.6;5,1 5,4.6;2,1 5,1;2,3 5,3" vettori="3.5,0.1,3.5,0.9,Q;3.5,3.1,3.5,4.4,W" etichette="3.5,2,Gas" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=5.5 assi=0 titolo="The gas is heated at constant pressure. Heat enters and the piston rises, so the gas does work."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_p&=C_V+R=\frac32R+R=\frac52R\simeq20.79\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{mol\,K}}, \\[6pt]
Q&=nC_p\Delta T \\[6pt]
&=0.60\cdot\frac52\cdot8.314\cdot60\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq748\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=nC_V\Delta T=0.60\cdot\frac32\cdot8.314\cdot60\,\mathrm{J}\simeq449\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W&=nR\Delta T=0.60\cdot8.314\cdot60\,\mathrm{J}\simeq299\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At constant pressure the temperature rise is accompanied by an increase in volume, so the gas does positive work. By the first law, the energy supplied as heat must increase the internal energy and cover the work. Per mole and per kelvin the first share is $\dfrac32R$ and the work is $R$, so their sum is the **molar heat capacity at constant pressure** $C_p=\dfrac52R$, in agreement with Mayer's relation.

$C_p$ is multiplied by the number of moles and by the temperature rise, which gives $Q\simeq748\,\mathrm{J}$. The last line splits this heat into the two shares: $449\,\mathrm{J}$ increases the internal energy and $299\,\mathrm{J}$ leaves as work. Their sum, $748\,\mathrm{J}$, coincides with the heat absorbed. The ratio of work to heat is $\dfrac{W}{Q}=\dfrac{R}{C_p}=\dfrac{2}{5}$, independent of $n$ and $\Delta T$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A monatomic ideal gas of $0.75\,\mathrm{mol}$ undergoes a quasistatic isobaric cooling of $60\,\mathrm{K}$. Calculate the heat, the work, and the change in internal energy with $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T&=-60\,\mathrm{K}, \\[6pt]
nR\Delta T&=0.75\cdot8.314\cdot(-60)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq-374\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W&=p\,\Delta V=nR\Delta T\simeq-374\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=\frac32nR\Delta T=\frac32\cdot(-374)\,\mathrm{J}\simeq-561\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U+W\simeq(-561-374)\,\mathrm{J}=-935\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the end of the cooling the temperature is lower, so $\Delta T=-60\,\mathrm{K}$. At constant pressure the equation of state gives $p\,\Delta V=nR\Delta T$, so the product $nR\Delta T$ is negative. It follows that the volume decreases and that the work done by the gas, $W=p\,\Delta V$, is about $-374\,\mathrm{J}$.

For a monatomic ideal gas $\Delta U=\dfrac32nR\Delta T$, so the internal energy also decreases, by about $561\,\mathrm{J}$. The first law is then applied in the form $Q=\Delta U+W$. During the compression energy reaches the gas as work, so the heat released has a greater magnitude than the decrease in internal energy alone. The gas **releases about $935\,\mathrm{J}$**. The same cooling at constant volume would remove only $561\,\mathrm{J}$, because without compression no energy would arrive as work. The additional $374\,\mathrm{J}$ is the work received, which leaves the gas as heat.

</div>
