## Exercise 1

A monatomic ideal gas containing $0.60\,\mathrm{mol}$ undergoes quasistatic heating by $60\,\mathrm{K}$ at constant pressure. Calculate the heat absorbed, using $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_p&=C_V+R=\frac52R, \\[6pt]
Q&=0.60\cdot\frac52\cdot8.314\cdot60\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq748\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At constant pressure, the increase in temperature is accompanied by an increase in volume. The energy supplied as heat must therefore increase the internal energy and allow the gas to do work. The first contribution per mole per kelvin is $\dfrac32R$, and the work per mole per kelvin is $R$. Their sum is the **molar heat capacity at constant pressure**. Multiply $C_p$ by the amount in moles and the temperature increase.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A monatomic ideal gas containing $0.75\,\mathrm{mol}$ is cooled quasistatically by $60\,\mathrm{K}$ at constant pressure. Calculate heat, work, and the change in internal energy using $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
nR\Delta T&=0.75\cdot8.314\cdot(-60)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq-374\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W&=nR\Delta T\simeq-374\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=\frac32nR\Delta T\simeq-561\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U+W\simeq-935\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the end of cooling the temperature is lower, so $\Delta T=-60\,\mathrm{K}$. At constant pressure, the equation of state gives $p\Delta V=nR\Delta T$. Thus the volume decreases and the gas does negative work. The internal energy also decreases. The first law gives negative heat whose magnitude is greater than the decrease in internal energy alone, because some energy also enters the gas as work during compression. The rounded values satisfy $-935-(-374)=-561$ in joules.

</div>
