## Exercise 1

The temperature of $0.64\,\mathrm{mol}$ of a monatomic ideal gas increases by $45\,\mathrm{K}$. Using $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}$, calculate $\Delta U$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&=nC_V\Delta T \\[6pt]
&=0.64\cdot\frac32\cdot8.314\cdot45\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&\simeq359\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The internal energy of an ideal gas depends on temperature, so the information given determines $\Delta U$ even without knowing how the gas was heated. For a monatomic ideal gas the molar heat capacity at constant volume is $C_V=\dfrac32R$. The product $nC_V$ measures the increase in internal energy per kelvin for the whole amount of gas. Multiplying it by $45\,\mathrm{K}$ gives an increase of about $359\,\mathrm{J}$. Finding the heat exchanged would also require the work.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A monatomic ideal gas goes from a state with $p_i=100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $V_i=2\,\mathrm{L}$ to one with $p_f=180\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and $V_f=3\,\mathrm{L}$. Calculate $\Delta U$ without knowing the amount of gas in moles. Also find the heat if the gas does $140\,\mathrm{J}$ of work during the process.

$$
\begin{aligned}
nR\Delta T&=p_fV_f-p_iV_i \\[6pt]
&=(180\cdot3-100\cdot2)\,\mathrm{J}=340\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=\frac32nR\Delta T=510\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U+W=(510+140)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=650\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation of state holds at each endpoint of the process. Subtracting $p_iV_i=nRT_i$ from $p_fV_f=nRT_f$ directly gives the product $nR\Delta T$. The change in internal energy requires that product multiplied by $\dfrac32$. Products $pV$ measured in $\mathrm{kPa}\cdot\mathrm{L}$ give joules. The gas gains $510\,\mathrm{J}$ of internal energy and supplies $140\,\mathrm{J}$ to the surroundings through work. The heat entering the gas must cover both amounts.

</div>
