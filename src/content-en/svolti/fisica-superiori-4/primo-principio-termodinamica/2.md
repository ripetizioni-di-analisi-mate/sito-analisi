## Exercise 1

A gas increases its internal energy by $336\,\mathrm{J}$ and does $180\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. Calculate the heat exchanged.

::grafico{linee="2,1 5,1 5,4 2,4 2,1" vettori="0.25,2.15,1.95,2.15,Q;5.05,2.15,6.75,2.15,W" etichette="3.5,2.35,ΔU" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=5 assi=0 titolo="The balance divides the energy entering as heat between the increase in internal energy and the work."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&=336\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad W=+180\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=Q-W \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q=\Delta U+W \\[6pt]
&=(336+180)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=516\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first law $\Delta U=Q-W$ contains three quantities and two are known, so the third can be found. The gas does work on the surroundings, so $W$ is positive and equals $+180\,\mathrm{J}$. The internal energy increases, so $\Delta U$ is positive and equals $+336\,\mathrm{J}$. To isolate $Q$, $W$ is added to both sides of the equation, which gives $Q=\Delta U+W$.

Adding **the increase in internal energy** and **the work done by the gas** gives $516\,\mathrm{J}$. The positive sign indicates that heat enters the gas. Of the $516\,\mathrm{J}$ that entered, $180\,\mathrm{J}$ passes to the surroundings as work and the remaining $336\,\mathrm{J}$ stays in the gas. The gas **absorbs $516\,\mathrm{J}$ of heat**. With no work done, the same increase in internal energy would require only $336\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

During a compression the surroundings do $640\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on the gas. The internal energy increases by $420\,\mathrm{J}$. Calculate the heat exchanged and the final internal energy if the initial one is $500\,\mathrm{J}$ in the chosen reference.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=-640\,\mathrm{J},\qquad \Delta U=+420\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
Q&=\Delta U+W=(420-640)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=-220\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
U_f&=U_i+\Delta U=(500+420)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=920\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The work is done by the surroundings on the gas, so the energy carried by the work enters the system and the work done by the gas is negative: $W=-640\,\mathrm{J}$. The form $Q=\Delta U+W$ of the first law is used. The gas receives $640\,\mathrm{J}$ as work and retains $420\,\mathrm{J}$ as an increase in internal energy.

The difference, $220\,\mathrm{J}$, leaves the gas. Since the energy leaving the system as heat has a negative sign, $Q=-220\,\mathrm{J}$ and the gas **releases** $220\,\mathrm{J}$ to the wall. To find the final internal energy, the change $\Delta U$ is added to $U_i$, because the change in internal energy already includes the effects of both exchanges. This gives $U_f=920\,\mathrm{J}$. With $Q=0$ the same compression would give $\Delta U=640\,\mathrm{J}$. The difference $640-420=220$ is the heat released.

</div>
