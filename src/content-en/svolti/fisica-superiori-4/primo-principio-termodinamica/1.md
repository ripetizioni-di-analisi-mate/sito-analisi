## Exercise 1

A gas in a closed system absorbs $720\,\mathrm{J}$ and does $250\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on its surroundings. Calculate the change in internal energy, neglecting macroscopic changes in kinetic and potential energy.

::grafico{linee="2,1 5,1 5,4 2,4 2,1" vettori="0.5,2.5,1.95,2.5,Q;5.05,2.5,6.5,2.5,W" etichette="3.5,2.55,Gas" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=5 assi=0 titolo="Heat enters the gas. Work transfers energy to the surroundings."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&=Q-W \\[6pt]
&=(720-250)\,\mathrm{J}=470\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas receives $720\,\mathrm{J}$ as heat, so $Q$ is positive. When it moves the system boundary by exerting a force on the surroundings, it transfers $250\,\mathrm{J}$ out of the system, and work done **by the gas** is positive. In the first law, $W$ is subtracted precisely because that part of the energy has left. The difference of $470\,\mathrm{J}$ increases the internal energy. To check the balance, add what remained in the gas to what left as work. The result is $470+250=720$ joules, the energy that entered as heat.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A gas releases $180\,\mathrm{J}$ as heat while the piston does $700\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on the gas. Calculate $\Delta U$ and the work the piston would have had to do for the increase in internal energy to double with the same heat loss.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=-180\,\mathrm{J},\qquad W=-700\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=[-180-(-700)]\,\mathrm{J}=520\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W'&=Q-2\Delta U=(-180-1040)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=-1220\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $700\,\mathrm{J}$ is attributed to the piston, which does work **on the gas**, so the value enters as $W=-700\,\mathrm{J}$. Substitution in the balance shows that the energy received as work exceeds the energy released as heat by $520\,\mathrm{J}$. In the second process, the gas must gain $1040\,\mathrm{J}$ of internal energy and still releases $180\,\mathrm{J}$ to the wall. The piston must supply both amounts, so it does **$1220\,\mathrm{J}$** of work on the gas.

</div>
