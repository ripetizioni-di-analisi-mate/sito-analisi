## Exercise 1

A gas in a closed system absorbs $720\,\mathrm{J}$ and does $250\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on the surroundings. Calculate the change in internal energy, neglecting macroscopic changes in kinetic and potential energy.

::grafico{linee="2,1 5,1 5,4 2,4 2,1" vettori="0.5,2.5,1.95,2.5,Q;5.05,2.5,6.5,2.5,W" etichette="3.5,2.55,Gas" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=5 assi=0 titolo="Heat enters the gas. Work transfers energy to the surroundings."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=+720\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad W=+250\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=Q-W \\[6pt]
&=(720-250)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=470\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is the gas, which is closed, and the macroscopic changes in kinetic and potential energy are negligible, so the **first law** $\Delta U=Q-W$ applies. The gas receives energy as heat, so $Q$ is positive and equals $+720\,\mathrm{J}$. It moves the boundary of the system by exerting a force on the surroundings, so the work **done by the gas** is positive and equals $+250\,\mathrm{J}$. In the balance the energy $Q$ enters and the energy $W$ leaves, so the work is subtracted from the heat.

The difference is $470\,\mathrm{J}$ and it stays in the gas, so **the internal energy increases by $470\,\mathrm{J}$**. If the gas is ideal, an increase in internal energy corresponds to an increase in temperature. Without the heat absorbed, the same expansion would make the internal energy decrease by $250\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A gas releases $180\,\mathrm{J}$ as heat while the piston does $700\,\mathrm{J}$ of work on the gas. Calculate $\Delta U$ and the work the piston would have had to do for the increase in internal energy to be double, with the same heat release.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=-180\,\mathrm{J},\qquad W=-700\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U&=Q-W=[-180-(-700)]\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=520\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
\Delta U'&=2\Delta U=1040\,\mathrm{J}, \\[6pt]
W'&=Q-\Delta U'=(-180-1040)\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&=-1220\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas releases heat, so $Q=-180\,\mathrm{J}$. The $700\,\mathrm{J}$ is done by the piston **on the gas**, so the work done by the gas is negative and the datum enters as $W=-700\,\mathrm{J}$. Substituting into the first law, the term $-W$ becomes $+700\,\mathrm{J}$ and one obtains $\Delta U=520\,\mathrm{J}$: the energy entering as work exceeds the energy leaving as heat by $520\,\mathrm{J}$.

In the second process the heat is still $-180\,\mathrm{J}$ and the required increase is $\Delta U'=2\cdot520\,\mathrm{J}=1040\,\mathrm{J}$. From the first law $\Delta U'=Q-W'$ one obtains $W'=Q-\Delta U'$. The result is negative, so in this case too the work is done on the gas. The piston must supply both the $1040\,\mathrm{J}$ that stays in the gas and the $180\,\mathrm{J}$ that leaves as heat, for a work on the gas of **$1220\,\mathrm{J}$**. Compared with the first process, the work on the gas increases by $520\,\mathrm{J}$, equal to the additional increase in internal energy, because the heat released is the same.

</div>
