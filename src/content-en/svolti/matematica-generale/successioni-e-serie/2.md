## Exercise 1

In an arithmetic progression we have $a_3 = 11$ and $a_8 = 26$. Find the common difference, the first term, the twentieth term and the sum of the first twenty terms.

$$
a_8 - a_3 = 5d
\iff
26 - 11 = 5d
\iff
d = 3
$$
$$
a_1 = a_3 - 2d = 11 - 6 = 5
$$
$$
a_{20} = a_1 + 19d = 5 + 57 = 62
$$
$$
S_{20} = \frac{20\,(a_1 + a_{20})}{2} = 10 \cdot 67 = 670
$$

::grafico{f="3*x+2" punti="1,5,pieno;2,8,pieno;3,11,pieno,a₃;4,14,pieno;5,17,pieno;6,20,pieno;7,23,pieno;8,26,pieno,a₈" segmenti="3,11,8,11;8,11,8,26" etichette="5.5,9.2,5 steps;8.3,18.5,15" xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-4 ymax=30 nomi="n,aₙ" titolo="From $a_3 = 11$ to $a_8 = 26$ there are $5$ steps and the change is $15$, so the common difference is $d = \dfrac{15}{5} = 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In an **arithmetic progression**, to go from $a_3$ to $a_8$ one adds the common difference five times, so the difference $a_8 - a_3$ equals $5d$, and $d = 3$. The first term is obtained by going back two positions from $a_3$, that is, by subtracting the common difference twice. The twentieth term is found with the formula $a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)\,d$. For the **sum of the terms**, one multiplies the number of terms by the mean of the first and the last, and obtains $670$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Consider the sum $7 + 11 + 15 + 19 + \dots$. Find how many terms are needed for the sum to exceed $1000$.

$$
a_1 = 7,
\qquad
d = 4,
\qquad
a_n = 7 + 4\,(n - 1) = 4n + 3
$$
$$
S_n = \frac{n\,(7 + 4n + 3)}{2} = \frac{n\,(4n + 10)}{2} = n\,(2n + 5)
$$
$$
2n^2 + 5n > 1000
\iff
n > \frac{-5 + \sqrt{25 + 8000}}{4} \approx 21.15
$$
$$
S_{21} = 21 \cdot 47 = 987,
\qquad
S_{22} = 22 \cdot 49 = 1078
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The terms increase by $4$ each time, so they form an arithmetic progression with first term $7$ and common difference $4$. With the formula for the **sum of the terms**, the sum of the first $n$ terms becomes the polynomial $n\,(2n + 5)$. The condition $S_n > 1000$ is a quadratic inequality in $n$; the parabola $2n^2 + 5n - 1000$ opens upward, so it is positive beyond the positive root, about $21.15$, and the negative root is irrelevant because $n$ is a natural number. The first natural number beyond $21.15$ is $22$, therefore **22 terms** are needed, as confirmed by $S_{21} = 987$ and $S_{22} = 1078$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find three numbers in increasing arithmetic progression whose sum is $21$ and whose product is $231$.

$$
x - d,
\qquad
x,
\qquad
x + d
$$
$$
(x - d) + x + (x + d) = 3x = 21
\iff
x = 7
$$
$$
(7 - d) \cdot 7 \cdot (7 + d) = 231
\iff
49 - d^2 = 33
\iff
d^2 = 16
$$
$$
d > 0
\implies
d = 4:
\qquad
3,\ 7,\ 11
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three numbers in **arithmetic progression** are written as the middle term $x$ preceded and followed by the common difference $d$, that is, $x - d$, $x$, $x + d$. With this choice the common difference cancels in the sum, and the first condition immediately gives $x = 7$. The product contains the difference of squares $(7 - d)(7 + d) = 49 - d^2$, so dividing by $7$ gives $d^2 = 16$. Since the progression is increasing the common difference is positive, therefore $d = 4$ and the three numbers **sought** are $3$, $7$ and $11$. As a check, $3 + 7 + 11 = 21$ and $3 \cdot 7 \cdot 11 = 231$.

</div>
