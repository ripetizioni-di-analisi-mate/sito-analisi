## Exercise 1

A foundation receives $1200$ euros a year forever. At the annual rate of $4\%$, compute the present value of the perpetuity if the first payment is made one year from now, and if it is made today.

$$
V = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1200}{1{,}04^{k}} = \frac{\dfrac{1200}{1{,}04}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{1{,}04}} = \frac{1200}{0{,}04} = 30\,000 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
V' = 1200 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1200}{1{,}04^{k}} = 1200 + 30\,000 = 31\,200 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The present value of an amount due in $k$ years is that amount divided by $1{,}04^{k}$. The present value of the perpetuity is therefore the sum of a **geometric series** with first term $\dfrac{1200}{1{,}04}$ and common ratio $\dfrac{1}{1{,}04}$, which is less than $1$. Multiplying the numerator and denominator by $1{,}04$ reduces the sum to $\dfrac{1200}{0{,}04}$, or $30\,000$ euros. An investment of this amount earning $4\%$ yields exactly $1200$ euros of interest each year. If the first payment is immediate, its full value is added to the series without discounting. The value then rises to $31\,200$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An initial investment of $5000$ euros earns a nominal annual interest rate of $6\%$ for one year. Find the future value under annual, quarterly, monthly, and continuous compounding.

$$
\text{annual:}\qquad 5000 \cdot 1{,}06 = 5300{,}00 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
\text{quarterly:}\qquad 5000 \left( 1 + \frac{0{,}06}{4} \right)^{4} = 5000 \cdot 1{,}015^{4} \approx 5306{,}82 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
\text{monthly:}\qquad 5000 \left( 1 + \frac{0{,}06}{12} \right)^{12} = 5000 \cdot 1{,}005^{12} \approx 5308{,}39 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
\text{continuous:}\qquad 5000 \lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{0{,}06}{n} \right)^{n} = 5000\,e^{0{,}06} \approx 5309{,}18 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When the nominal rate of $6\%$ is compounded $n$ times a year, the rate for each period is $\dfrac{0{,}06}{n}$. The principal is multiplied $n$ times by $1 + \dfrac{0{,}06}{n}$, so the balances at the ends of the periods form a **geometric progression**. More frequent compounding gives a higher future value because interest earned in each period earns interest in later periods. The increase diminishes, and as $n$ grows, the future value approaches $5000\,e^{0{,}06}$, as in the theorem on $e$ with rate $0{,}12$ replaced by $0{,}06$. The difference between annual and continuous compounding is just over $9$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The goal is to save $50\,000$ euros in $15$ years by making equal end-of-year deposits into an account earning $3\%$ compound interest. Find the annual deposit.

$$
R + R \cdot 1{,}03 + \dots + R \cdot 1{,}03^{14} = R\,\frac{1{,}03^{15} - 1}{0{,}03} = 50\,000
$$
$$
R = \frac{50\,000 \cdot 0{,}03}{1{,}03^{15} - 1} \approx \frac{1500}{0{,}557967} \approx 2688{,}33 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first deposit stays in the account for $14$ years and the last earns no interest. At the end of the fifteenth year the balance is therefore the sum of $15$ terms of a geometric progression with first term $R$ and common ratio $1{,}03$. The formula for the **sum of the terms of a geometric progression** gives the balance as $R$ times a factor of about $18{,}599$. Setting the balance equal to $50\,000$ euros gives an annual deposit of about $2688{,}33$ euros. The deposits total about $40\,325$ euros, and interest accounts for the remaining $9675$ euros.

</div>
