## Exercise 1

A foundation receives $1200$ euros a year forever. At the annual rate of $4\%$, compute the present value of the perpetuity if the first payment is made one year from now, and if it is made today.

$$
V = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1200}{1.04^{k}} = \frac{\dfrac{1200}{1.04}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{1.04}} = \frac{1200}{0.04} = 30\,000 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
V' = 1200 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1200}{1.04^{k}} = 1200 + 30\,000 = 31\,200 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An amount available in $k$ years is worth today that amount divided by $1.04^{k}$, so the present value of the perpetuity is the sum of a **geometric series** with first term $\dfrac{1200}{1.04}$ and common ratio $\dfrac{1}{1.04}$, which is less than $1$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $1.04$, the sum reduces to $\dfrac{1200}{0.04}$, that is, $30\,000$ euros, which is the capital that, invested at $4\%$, produces exactly $1200$ euros of interest every year. If the first payment is immediate, a payment that is not discounted is added to the same series, and the value rises to $31\,200$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A capital of $5000$ euros is invested for one year at the nominal annual rate of $6\%$. Compute the accumulated amount with annual, quarterly, monthly and continuous compounding.

$$
\text{annual:}\qquad 5000 \cdot 1.06 = 5300.00 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
\text{quarterly:}\qquad 5000 \left( 1 + \frac{0.06}{4} \right)^{4} = 5000 \cdot 1.015^{4} \approx 5306.82 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
\text{monthly:}\qquad 5000 \left( 1 + \frac{0.06}{12} \right)^{12} = 5000 \cdot 1.005^{12} \approx 5308.39 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
\text{continuous:}\qquad 5000 \lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{0.06}{n} \right)^{n} = 5000\,e^{0.06} \approx 5309.18 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the nominal rate of $6\%$ compounded $n$ times a year, the rate $\dfrac{0.06}{n}$ is applied in each period, and the capital is multiplied $n$ times by $1 + \dfrac{0.06}{n}$, therefore the values at the end of each period form a **geometric progression**. The more often interest is compounded, the higher the accumulated amount, because the interest of each period in turn earns interest in the following periods. The increase, however, diminishes, and as $n$ grows the accumulated amount tends to $5000\,e^{0.06}$, as in the theorem on the number $e$ with the rate $0.12$ replaced by $0.06$. Between annual and continuous compounding the difference is just over $9$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A capital of $50\,000$ euros is to be accumulated in $15$ years with equal deposits at the end of every year into an account at $3\%$ compound interest. Compute the annual deposit.

$$
R + R \cdot 1.03 + \dots + R \cdot 1.03^{14} = R\,\frac{1.03^{15} - 1}{0.03} = 50\,000
$$
$$
R = \frac{50\,000 \cdot 0.03}{1.03^{15} - 1} \approx \frac{1500}{0.557967} \approx 2688.33 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first deposit stays in the account $14$ years, the last one none, so at the end of the fifteenth year the balance is the sum of $15$ terms of a geometric progression with first term $R$ and common ratio $1.03$. By the **sum of the terms of a geometric progression**, the balance equals $R$ times a numerical factor, about $18.599$. Setting it equal to $50\,000$ euros gives the annual deposit, about $2688.33$ euros. The deposits total about $40\,325$ euros; the rest, about $9675$ euros, is interest.

</div>
