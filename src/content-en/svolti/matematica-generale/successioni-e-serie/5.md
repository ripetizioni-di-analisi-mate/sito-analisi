## Exercise 1

Determine whether they converge and compute the sums of the series
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} 5 \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{k}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 3 \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right)^{k} .
$$

$$
a = 5,
\quad
q = \frac{2}{3}:
\qquad
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} 5 \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{k} = \frac{5}{1 - \dfrac{2}{3}} = 15
$$
$$
a = 3 \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right) = -\frac{3}{2},
\quad
q = -\frac{1}{2}:
\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 3 \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right)^{k} = \frac{-\dfrac{3}{2}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{2}} = -1
$$

::grafico{punti="1,-1.5000,pieno;2,-0.7500,pieno;3,-1.1250,pieno;4,-0.9375,pieno;5,-1.0312,pieno;6,-0.9844,pieno;7,-1.0078,pieno;8,-0.9961,pieno;9,-1.0020,pieno;10,-0.9990,pieno" asintoti="y=-1" xmin=-2 xmax=12 ymin=-2 ymax=1 nomi="n,Sₙ" titolo="The partial sums of $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 3 \left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{k}$ lie alternately below and above $-1$ and approach $-1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both are **geometric series**, because each term is obtained from the previous one by multiplying by the same common ratio, and they converge because the common ratio lies between $-1$ and $1$. The sum is the first term divided by $1 - q$. In the first series the index starts from $0$, so the first term is $5$ and the sum is $15$. In the second the index starts from $1$, so the first term is the one with $k = 1$, that is, $-\dfrac{3}{2}$, and this value is used in the formula in place of the coefficient $3$. The sum is $-1$; the terms change sign, and the partial sums oscillate around $-1$ as they approach it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the repeating decimal $1.\overline{27} = 1.272727\ldots$ as a fraction.

$$
1.\overline{27} = 1 + \frac{27}{100} + \frac{27}{100^2} + \frac{27}{100^3} + \dots
$$
$$
\frac{27}{100} + \frac{27}{100^2} + \dots = \frac{\dfrac{27}{100}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{100}} = \frac{27}{99} = \frac{3}{11}
$$
$$
1.\overline{27} = 1 + \frac{3}{11} = \frac{14}{11}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The repeating block $27$ recurs every two decimal places, so the decimal part is the sum of $\dfrac{27}{100}$, $\dfrac{27}{10\,000}$, and so on, that is, a **geometric series** with first term $\dfrac{27}{100}$ and common ratio $\dfrac{1}{100}$. The series converges and has sum $\dfrac{27}{99}$, which simplifies to $\dfrac{3}{11}$. Adding the integer part gives $\dfrac{14}{11}$. As a check, the division $14 : 11$ gives $1.2727\ldots$

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the values of $x$ for which the series $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (x - 1)^{k}$ converges and compute its sum.

$$
q = x - 1:
\qquad
-1 < x - 1 < 1
\iff
0 < x < 2
$$
$$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (x - 1)^{k} = \frac{1}{1 - (x - 1)} = \frac{1}{2 - x},
\qquad
0 < x < 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each fixed $x$ the series is geometric with first term $1$ and common ratio $x - 1$. By the theorem on the **geometric series**, it converges if and only if the common ratio lies strictly between $-1$ and $1$, that is, for $0 < x < 2$. In this interval the sum is $\dfrac{1}{2 - x}$. For $x \geq 2$ the common ratio is at least $1$ and the series diverges to $+\infty$; for $x \leq 0$ the common ratio is at most $-1$ and the series is oscillating. For example, for $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ the series is $1 - \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4} - \dots$ and has sum $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

</div>
