## Exercise 1

A geometric progression has positive terms with $a_2 = 6$ and $a_5 = 162$. Find its common ratio, its first and eighth terms, and the sum of its first eight terms.

$$
\frac{a_5}{a_2} = q^3
\iff
q^3 = \frac{162}{6} = 27
\iff
q = 3
$$
$$
a_1 = \frac{a_2}{q} = 2,
\qquad
a_8 = a_1\,q^{7} = 2 \cdot 2187 = 4374
$$
$$
S_8 = a_1\,\frac{1 - q^{8}}{1 - q} = 2 \cdot \frac{1 - 6561}{1 - 3} = 6560
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a **geometric progression**, getting from $a_2$ to $a_5$ requires multiplying by the common ratio three times, so $\dfrac{a_5}{a_2}$ equals $q^3$. The equation $q^3 = 27$ gives $q = 3$, the only real cube root. Dividing $a_2$ by the common ratio gives the first term, and $a_n = a_1\,q^{n-1}$ gives the eighth. The geometric sum formula gives the **sum of the terms**. As a check, $2 + 6 + 18 + 54 + 162 + 486 + 1458 + 4374 = 6560$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A deposit of $200$ euros is made at the end of each month for $24$ months into an account earning $0{,}5\%$ per month, compounded monthly. Find the balance immediately after the last deposit.

$$
S = 200 + 200 \cdot 1{,}005 + 200 \cdot 1{,}005^{2} + \dots + 200 \cdot 1{,}005^{23}
$$
$$
S = 200 \cdot \frac{1{,}005^{24} - 1}{1{,}005 - 1} \approx 200 \cdot 25{,}4320 \approx 5086{,}39 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each deposit stays in the account for a different length of time. The last earns no interest, the second-to-last earns one month of interest, and the first earns $23$ months of interest because it is deposited at the end of the first month. A deposit left for $k$ months is multiplied by $1{,}005^{k}$, so the balance is the sum of $24$ terms of a **geometric progression** with first term $200$ and common ratio $1{,}005$. The formula for the **sum of the terms**, written as $a_1\,\dfrac{q^n - 1}{q - 1}$ so that the numerator and denominator are positive, gives a balance of about $5086{,}39$ euros. The deposits total $4800$ euros, so the interest is about $286$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A machine purchased for $20\,000$ euros loses $15\%$ of its value each year. Find how many years it takes for its value to fall below $5000$ euros.

$$
V_n = 20\,000 \cdot 0{,}85^{\,n}
$$
$$
20\,000 \cdot 0{,}85^{\,n} < 5000
\iff
0{,}85^{\,n} < 0{,}25
\iff
n > \frac{\ln 0{,}25}{\ln 0{,}85} \approx 8{,}53
$$
$$
V_8 \approx 5449{,}81,
\qquad
V_9 \approx 4632{,}34
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A loss of $15\%$ leaves $85\%$ of the value. Thus each year the value is multiplied by $0{,}85$, so the values form a **geometric progression** with common ratio $0{,}85 < 1$. The progression tends to $0$ by the limit law for geometric sequences. Take the logarithm of each side of the inequality. Dividing by $\ln 0{,}85$ reverses the inequality because this logarithm is negative. The resulting condition is $n > 8{,}53$, and the first natural number that satisfies it is $9$. The value therefore falls below $5000$ euros **after 9 years**, as $V_8$ and $V_9$ confirm.

</div>
