## Exercise 1

Find the level curves of $f(x, y) = 2x - y$ and the level curve that passes through the point $(1, 3)$.

$$
2x - y = k
\iff
y = 2x - k,
\qquad
k \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
f(1, 3) = 2 - 3 = -1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
L_{-1} : \ y = 2x + 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **level curve** $k$ consists of the points where $f$ equals $k$, so one solves the equation $2x - y = k$ for $y$. For each $k$ one obtains the line $y = 2x - k$, with slope $2$ and intercept $-k$. The level curves are therefore **parallel lines**, one for each real value of $k$, and none is empty. As $k$ increases the intercept decreases, so the lines of higher levels lie lower.

The point $(1, 3)$ lies on the level curve equal to the value of $f$ at that point, that is, $k = -1$, which is the line $y = 2x + 1$. As a check, $2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3$. The graph of $f$ is the plane $z = 2x - y$, and its horizontal sections are lines, as found.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the level curves of $f(x, y) = y - x^2$ and draw those that pass through the points $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 3)$.

$$
y - x^2 = k
\iff
y = x^2 + k,
\qquad
k \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
f(0, 0) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
L_0 : \ y = x^2
$$
$$
f(1, 3) = 3 - 1 = 2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
L_2 : \ y = x^2 + 2
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;x^2+2" punti="0,0,pieno;1,3,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=6 legenda="y = x^2;y = x^2 + 2" titolo="The level curves $0$ and $2$ of $f(x, y) = y - x^2$ are the parabola $y = x^2$ and the same parabola shifted upward by $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Solving the equation $y - x^2 = k$ for $y$ gives the parabola $y = x^2 + k$, with vertex at $(0, k)$ and opening upward. The level curves are therefore identical **parabolas**, shifted vertically with respect to one another; for every $k$ the curve exists, because the parabola $y = x^2 + k$ is defined for every $x$. The level increases going up, because, for fixed $x$, $f$ increases as $y$ increases.

The value of $f$ at the point $(0, 0)$ is $0$, so the curve through the origin is the parabola $y = x^2$. At the point $(1, 3)$ the function equals $2$, so the curve through $(1, 3)$ is $y = x^2 + 2$; indeed $1^2 + 2 = 3$. The two curves have no points in common, as happens for every pair of different levels.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the level curves of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4x$, the level curve that passes through $(3, 2)$, and the minimum value of $f$.

$$
x^2 - 4x + y^2 = k
\iff
(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 4 + k
$$

- $k < -4$: no points, $L_k = \emptyset$;
- $k = -4$: the single point $(2, 0)$;
- $k > -4$: the circle with center $(2, 0)$ and radius $\sqrt{4 + k}$.

$$
f(3, 2) = 9 + 4 - 12 = 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
L_1 : \ (x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 5
$$
$$
f(x, y) = (x - 2)^2 + y^2 - 4 \geq -4,
\qquad
\min f = f(2, 0) = -4
$$

::grafico{curve="2+cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29;2+2.2361*cos(t),2.2361*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="2,0,pieno;3,2,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda="(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 1;(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 5" titolo="The level curves $k = -3$ and $k = 1$ of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4x$ are circles with center $(2, 0)$; the second passes through $(3, 2)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

To recognize the curve, one **completes the square** in the terms in $x$, that is, one adds and subtracts $4$, and obtains $x^2 - 4x = (x - 2)^2 - 4$. The equation of the level curve becomes $(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = 4 + k$. The left-hand side is the square of the distance of the point $(x, y)$ from $(2, 0)$, which is never negative. Hence for $4 + k < 0$ the curve is empty, for $4 + k = 0$ it consists of the center alone, and for $4 + k > 0$ it is the circle with center $(2, 0)$ and radius $\sqrt{4 + k}$.

The point $(3, 2)$ belongs to the level curve $k = f(3, 2) = 1$, which is the circle of radius $\sqrt{5}$. The level curves are empty for $k < -4$, so $f$ takes no values less than $-4$; the value $-4$ is taken at the point $(2, 0)$. Therefore the **minimum value** of $f$ is $-4$, as confirmed by writing $f(x, y) = (x - 2)^2 + y^2 - 4$, the sum of two squares and $-4$.

</div>
