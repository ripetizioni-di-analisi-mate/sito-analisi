## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the tangent plane to the graph of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 3xy - y^2$ at the point with coordinates $x = 1$, $y = 2$.

$$
f(1, 2) = 1 + 6 - 4 = 3
$$
$$
f_x = 2x + 3y,
\qquad
f_y = 3x - 2y
$$
$$
f_x(1, 2) = 2 + 6 = 8,
\qquad
f_y(1, 2) = 3 - 4 = -1
$$
$$
z = 3 + 8\,(x - 1) - (y - 2) = 8x - y - 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so its partial derivatives are continuous on the whole plane and, by the **total differential theorem**, $f$ is differentiable at every point, so the tangent plane exists. Writing it requires three numbers: the value $f(1, 2) = 3$, which gives the height of the point of tangency, and the two partial derivatives at $(1, 2)$, which give the slopes of the plane in the directions of the axes.

Substituting into the formula of the **tangent plane** gives $z = 3 + 8\,(x - 1) - (y - 2)$, and expanding, $z = 8x - y - 3$. As a check, the plane passes through the point of the graph, because at $(1, 2)$ it gives $8 - 2 - 3 = 3 = f(1, 2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the tangent plane to the graph of $f(x, y) = \ln(x + 2y)$ at the point with coordinates $x = 1$, $y = 0$, and use it to approximate $f(1.1;\ 0.05)$.

$$
f(1, 0) = \ln 1 = 0
$$
$$
f_x = \frac{1}{x + 2y},
\qquad
f_y = \frac{2}{x + 2y},
\qquad
f_x(1, 0) = 1,
\qquad
f_y(1, 0) = 2
$$
$$
z = (x - 1) + 2y
$$
$$
f(1.1;\ 0.05) \approx 0.1 + 2 \cdot 0.05 = 0.2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined where $x + 2y > 0$, and the point $(1, 0)$ is interior to the domain, which is an open half-plane. The partial derivatives are computed with the rule for composite functions and are continuous on the domain, so $f$ is **differentiable** at $(1, 0)$. The tangent plane is $z = 0 + 1 \cdot (x - 1) + 2 \cdot (y - 0)$.

The point $(1.1;\ 0.05)$ is obtained from $(1, 0)$ with the increments $dx = 0.1$ and $dy = 0.05$, which are small. By the **linear approximation** the value of $f$ is approximately the height of the tangent plane, that is, $0.1 + 0.1 = 0.2$. The exact value is $\ln 1.2 = 0.1823\ldots$, so the error is about $0.018$, small compared with the increments.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute an approximate value of $1.02^{2} \cdot 0.97^{3}$ using the differential of a suitable function.

$$
f(x, y) = x^2 y^3,
\qquad
(x_0, y_0) = (1, 1),
\qquad
dx = 0.02,
\qquad
dy = -0.03
$$
$$
f(1, 1) = 1,
\qquad
f_x = 2x\,y^3,
\qquad
f_y = 3x^2 y^2,
\qquad
f_x(1, 1) = 2,
\qquad
f_y(1, 1) = 3
$$
$$
df = 2 \cdot 0.02 + 3 \cdot (-0.03) = 0.04 - 0.09 = -0.05
$$
$$
1.02^{2} \cdot 0.97^{3} \approx 1 - 0.05 = 0.95
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number to compute is the value of the function $f(x, y) = x^2 y^3$ at the point $(1.02;\ 0.97)$, which is close to $(1, 1)$, where $f$ and its derivatives are computed immediately. The polynomial $f$ is differentiable at every point, so one can use the **differential** $df = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy$, with the increments $dx = 0.02$ and $dy = -0.03$.

Each variable contributes its partial derivative times its increment. The increase in $x$ raises the value by $0.04$, and the decrease in $y$ lowers it by $0.09$. The approximation is therefore $1 - 0.05 = 0.95$. The exact value is $0.94954\ldots$, so the error is less than $0.0005$.

</div>
