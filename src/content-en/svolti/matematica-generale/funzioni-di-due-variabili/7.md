## Exercise 1

A firm has production function $Q(K, L) = 3\,K^{\frac{1}{3}} L^{\frac{2}{3}}$. With $K = 8$ and $L = 27$, compute the production, the marginal productivities and the marginal rate of substitution, and interpret the results.

$$
Q(8, 27) = 3 \cdot 8^{\frac{1}{3}} \cdot 27^{\frac{2}{3}} = 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 9 = 54
$$
$$
Q_K = K^{-\frac{2}{3}} L^{\frac{2}{3}},
\qquad
Q_K(8, 27) = \frac{9}{4}
$$
$$
Q_L = 2\,K^{\frac{1}{3}} L^{-\frac{1}{3}},
\qquad
Q_L(8, 27) = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{3}
$$
$$
\mathrm{MRS} = \frac{Q_L}{Q_K} = \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{9} = \frac{16}{27}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The roots are computed immediately, because $8^{\frac{1}{3}} = 2$ and $27^{\frac{1}{3}} = 3$, so $27^{\frac{2}{3}} = 9$. The **marginal productivities** are the partial derivatives. Differentiating with respect to $K$, the exponent $\dfrac{1}{3}$ comes down as a coefficient and becomes $\dfrac{1}{3} - 1 = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, and the coefficient $3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3}$ equals $1$. At $(8, 27)$ we have $8^{-\frac{2}{3}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, so $Q_K = \dfrac{9}{4}$; in the same way $27^{-\frac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ gives $Q_L = \dfrac{4}{3}$. As a check, $Q_K = \dfrac{a\,Q}{K} = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{54}{8} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.

By the linear approximation, one more unit of capital increases production by about $2.25$ units and one more unit of labor by about $1.33$. The **marginal rate of substitution** is $\dfrac{16}{27} \approx 0.59$. Hence, adding one unit of labor, about $0.59$ units of capital can be removed while leaving production unchanged. The same value comes from the formula $\dfrac{b}{a} \cdot \dfrac{K}{L} = 2 \cdot \dfrac{8}{27}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the production function $Q(K, L) = 2\,K^{\frac{1}{4}} L^{\frac{3}{4}}$, write the isoquant $Q = 16$ in the form $K = K(L)$, and compute the marginal rate of substitution at the point $K = 8$, $L = 8$ and the tangent line to the isoquant at that point.

$$
2\,K^{\frac{1}{4}} L^{\frac{3}{4}} = 16
\iff
K^{\frac{1}{4}} L^{\frac{3}{4}} = 8
\iff
K L^{3} = 8^{4} = 4096
\iff
K = \frac{4096}{L^{3}}
$$
$$
\mathrm{MRS} = \frac{b}{a} \cdot \frac{K}{L} = \frac{\frac{3}{4}}{\frac{1}{4}} \cdot \frac{8}{8} = 3
$$
$$
K'(L) = -\frac{3 \cdot 4096}{L^{4}},
\qquad
K'(8) = -\frac{12288}{4096} = -3
$$
$$
K = 8 - 3\,(L - 8) = 32 - 3L
$$

::grafico{f="4096/x^3+0*sqrt(x);32-3*x" punti="8,8,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=18 ymin=-2 ymax=18 nomi="L,K" legenda="K = \dfrac{4096}{L^3};K = 32 - 3L" titolo="The isoquant $Q = 16$ of $Q(K, L) = 2\,K^{\frac{1}{4}} L^{\frac{3}{4}}$ and its tangent at the point $(L, K) = (8, 8)$, with slope $-3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **isoquant** $Q = 16$ is the level curve $16$ of the production function. Dividing by $2$ and raising both sides, which are positive, to the fourth power, one obtains $K L^{3} = 4096$, and hence $K$ as a function of $L$. The point $(L, K) = (8, 8)$ belongs to the isoquant, because $\dfrac{4096}{512} = 8$.

For the Cobb-Douglas the **marginal rate of substitution** is $\dfrac{b}{a} \cdot \dfrac{K}{L}$, with $a = \dfrac{1}{4}$ and $b = \dfrac{3}{4}$, and at the given point it equals $3$. The theorem on the marginal rate of substitution says that the slope of the isoquant is $-\mathrm{MRS} = -3$. This is confirmed by the derivative of $K(L) = 4096\,L^{-3}$, which at $L = 8$ equals $-3$. The tangent line passes through $(8, 8)$ with slope $-3$. At the point considered, one more unit of labor therefore makes it possible to save about $3$ units of capital at constant production.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A consumer has utility function $U(x, y) = x^2 y$, with $x > 0$ and $y > 0$. Write the indifference curve that passes through the bundle $(2, 3)$ and compute the marginal rate of substitution at that point.

$$
U(2, 3) = 4 \cdot 3 = 12,
\qquad
x^2 y = 12
\iff
y = \frac{12}{x^2}
$$
$$
U_x = 2xy,
\qquad
U_y = x^2,
\qquad
\mathrm{MRS} = \frac{U_x}{U_y} = \frac{2xy}{x^2} = \frac{2y}{x}
$$
$$
\mathrm{MRS}(2, 3) = \frac{6}{2} = 3
$$
$$
y'(x) = -\frac{24}{x^3},
\qquad
y'(2) = -3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **indifference curve** through $(2, 3)$ is the level curve of $U$ that passes through that bundle, that is, $x^2 y = 12$, and it is written as $y = \dfrac{12}{x^2}$. The **marginal utilities** are the partial derivatives $U_x = 2xy$ and $U_y = x^2$, both positive, so utility increases if the quantity of either of the two goods increases.

The **marginal rate of substitution** between the two goods is the ratio of the marginal utilities, and at the bundle $(2, 3)$ it equals $3$. Hence the consumer is willing to give up about $3$ units of the second good in exchange for one unit of the first, staying on the same indifference curve. As a check, the derivative of $y = 12\,x^{-2}$ at $x = 2$ is $-3$, the opposite of the marginal rate of substitution.

</div>
