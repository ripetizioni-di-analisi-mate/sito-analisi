## Exercise 1

Compute the second derivatives of $f(x, y) = x^3 y^2 - 4xy + y^3$, verify Schwarz's theorem and write the Hessian matrix at the point $(1, 1)$.

$$
f_x = 3x^2 y^2 - 4y,
\qquad
f_y = 2x^3 y - 4x + 3y^2
$$
$$
f_{xx} = 6xy^2,
\qquad
f_{xy} = 6x^2 y - 4,
\qquad
f_{yx} = 6x^2 y - 4,
\qquad
f_{yy} = 2x^3 + 6y
$$
$$
H(1, 1) =
\begin{pmatrix}
6 & 2 \\
2 & 8
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(1, 1) = 48 - 4 = 44
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First one computes the **first derivatives**, each time treating the other variable as a constant. The second derivatives are obtained by differentiating again; $f_{xx}$ and $f_{xy}$ come from $f_x$, differentiated with respect to $x$ and with respect to $y$; $f_{yx}$ and $f_{yy}$ come from $f_y$. For example, differentiating $f_x = 3x^2 y^2 - 4y$ with respect to $y$ gives $6x^2 y - 4$.

The two mixed derivatives are equal, as **Schwarz's theorem** predicts. Its hypotheses are satisfied, because $f$ is a polynomial, defined on the whole plane, which is open, and the mixed derivatives are polynomials, hence continuous. At the point $(1, 1)$ we have $f_{xx} = 6$, $f_{xy} = f_{yx} = 6 - 4 = 2$ and $f_{yy} = 2 + 6 = 8$, and the Hessian matrix is symmetric with determinant $44$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the second derivatives of $f(x, y) = e^{xy}$ and the Hessian matrix at the point $(1, 0)$.

$$
f_x = y\,e^{xy},
\qquad
f_y = x\,e^{xy}
$$
$$
f_{xx} = y^2 e^{xy},
\qquad
f_{xy} = e^{xy} + xy\,e^{xy} = (1 + xy)\,e^{xy},
\qquad
f_{yy} = x^2 e^{xy}
$$
$$
H(1, 0) =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
1 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **rule for composite functions** the derivative of $e^{xy}$ with respect to $x$ is $e^{xy}$ multiplied by the derivative of $xy$ with respect to $x$, that is, $y$. Differentiating again with respect to $x$, the factor $y$ stays constant, and one obtains $f_{xx} = y^2 e^{xy}$.

The mixed derivative $f_{xy}$ is obtained by differentiating $f_x = y\,e^{xy}$ with respect to $y$, and it is the product of two functions of $y$, so one uses the **product rule**, with the derivative of $y$ equal to $1$ and the derivative of $e^{xy}$ with respect to $y$ equal to $x\,e^{xy}$. Differentiating $f_y = x\,e^{xy}$ with respect to $x$ gives the same expression, in agreement with **Schwarz's theorem**, whose hypotheses are satisfied because $e^{xy}$ and its derivatives are continuous on the whole plane. At $(1, 0)$ we have $e^{0} = 1$, so $f_{xx} = 0$, $f_{xy} = 1$ and $f_{yy} = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the second derivatives of $f(x, y) = \ln(x^2 + y^2)$, verify that $f_{xx} + f_{yy} = 0$ and write the Hessian matrix at the point $(1, 1)$.

$$
f_x = \frac{2x}{x^2 + y^2},
\qquad
f_y = \frac{2y}{x^2 + y^2}
$$
$$
f_{xx} = \frac{2\,(x^2 + y^2) - 2x \cdot 2x}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} = \frac{2\,(y^2 - x^2)}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}
$$
$$
f_{yy} = \frac{2\,(x^2 + y^2) - 2y \cdot 2y}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} = \frac{2\,(x^2 - y^2)}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}
$$
$$
f_{xy} = f_{yx} = -\frac{4xy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}
$$
$$
f_{xx} + f_{yy} = \frac{2y^2 - 2x^2 + 2x^2 - 2y^2}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} = 0
$$
$$
H(1, 1) =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -1 \\
-1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined where $x^2 + y^2 > 0$, that is, outside the origin. The first derivatives come from the rule for composite functions, with the derivative of $\ln u$ equal to $\dfrac{u'}{u}$. For $f_{xx}$ one differentiates the quotient $\dfrac{2x}{x^2 + y^2}$ with respect to $x$ using the **quotient rule**; the computation for $f_{yy}$ is the same with the roles of $x$ and $y$ swapped.

The mixed derivative is obtained by differentiating $f_x = 2x\,(x^2 + y^2)^{-1}$ with respect to $y$, with $2x$ constant, and it equals $2x \cdot (-1)(x^2 + y^2)^{-2} \cdot 2y = -\dfrac{4xy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}$; differentiating $f_y$ with respect to $x$ gives the same expression. Adding $f_{xx}$ and $f_{yy}$, the numerators cancel, so the sum is **zero** at every point of the domain. At the point $(1, 1)$ we have $y^2 - x^2 = 0$ and $(x^2 + y^2)^2 = 4$, and the Hessian matrix has zeros on the diagonal and $-1$ off it.

</div>
