## Exercise 1

Discuss as the real parameter $k$ varies the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + (k - 1)\,y = 1 \\[4pt]
k x + 2y = 2
\end{cases}
$$
and solve it when it is consistent.

$$
\det A = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & k - 1 \\ k & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 2 - k^{2} + k = -(k - 2)(k + 1)
$$
$$
k \neq 2, \ k \neq -1: \qquad
x = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 1 & k - 1 \\ 2 & 2 \end{vmatrix}}{-(k - 2)(k + 1)} = \frac{-2\,(k - 2)}{-(k - 2)(k + 1)} = \frac{2}{k + 1}
$$
$$
y = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ k & 2 \end{vmatrix}}{-(k - 2)(k + 1)} = \frac{-(k - 2)}{-(k - 2)(k + 1)} = \frac{1}{k + 1}
$$
$$
k = 2: \quad
\begin{cases}
x + y = 1 \\[4pt]
2x + 2y = 2
\end{cases}
\implies
(x, y) = (1 - t,\ t), \ t \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
k = -1: \quad
\begin{cases}
x - 2y = 1 \\[4pt]
-x + 2y = 2
\end{cases}
\implies
0 = 3, \ \text{inconsistent}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-1)/2;(x+2)/2" etichette="2.6,-0.6,x − 2y = 1;-3.8,0.6,−x + 2y = 2" xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-3 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="For $k = -1$ the lines $x - 2y = 1$ and $-x + 2y = 2$ are parallel and distinct, so the system is inconsistent"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is square, so we start from the determinant of the coefficient matrix, $2 - k\,(k - 1) = -k^{2} + k + 2$. The roots of the polynomial are $2$ and $-1$, hence the determinant factors as $-(k - 2)(k + 1)$.

If $k \neq 2$ and $k \neq -1$ the determinant is nonzero, and by **Cramer's rule** the system is determinate. The two determinants in the numerators equal $2 - 2\,(k - 1) = 4 - 2k = -2\,(k - 2)$ and $2 - k = -(k - 2)$; canceling the factor $k - 2 \neq 0$ we obtain $x = \dfrac{2}{k + 1}$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{k + 1}$.

For $k = 2$ the second equation is twice the first, so the two ranks equal $1$ and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** there are $\infty^{1}$ solutions; we set $y = t$ and obtain $x = 1 - t$. For $k = -1$ adding the two equations gives $0 = 3$, so the system is **inconsistent**; in the plane the two equations are the parallel lines of the figure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Discuss as the real parameter $k$ varies the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + k z = 2 \\[4pt]
x + k y + 2z = 2
\end{cases}
$$
and solve it when it is consistent.

$$
\det A = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & k \\ 1 & k & 2 \end{vmatrix} = (4 - k^{2}) - (2 - k) + (k - 2) = -k\,(k - 2)
$$
$$
\det A_1 = -(k - 2)^{2},
\qquad
\det A_2 = 2 - k,
\qquad
\det A_3 = 2 - k
$$
$$
k \neq 0, \ k \neq 2: \qquad
x = \frac{k - 2}{k},
\qquad
y = \frac{1}{k},
\qquad
z = \frac{1}{k}
$$
$$
k = 0: \qquad
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 1 \neq 0,
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 4 - 0 - 2 = 2 \neq 0
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = 2, \quad \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 3 \implies \text{inconsistent}
$$
$$
k = 2: \qquad
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + 2z = 2
\end{cases}
\implies
(x, y, z) = (0,\ 1 - t,\ t), \ t \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant, expanded along the first row, is $-k^{2} + 2k = -k\,(k - 2)$, and it vanishes for $k = 0$ and for $k = 2$. For the other values the system is determinate, and **Cramer's rule** gives the three unknowns. The numerators are computed by replacing one column at a time with the constant terms $(1, 2, 2)$; for example $\det A_2 = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & k \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = (4 - 2k) - (2 - k) + 0 = 2 - k$. Dividing by $-k\,(k - 2)$ and canceling the factor $k - 2$ we obtain the solution shown.

For $k = 0$ the coefficient matrix has rank $2$, because its determinant is zero and the minor of the first two rows and columns equals $1$. In the augmented matrix the bordered minor of this minor with the third row and the column of the constant terms is nonzero, so the augmented matrix has rank $3$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is **inconsistent**.

For $k = 2$ the second and third equations coincide, so the two ranks equal $2$ and there are $\infty^{1}$ solutions. Subtracting the first equation from the second we obtain $y + z = 1$, and substituting into the first we get $x = 0$; setting $z = t$ we have $y = 1 - t$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Discuss as the real parameter $k$ varies the system of three equations in two unknowns
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 2 \\[4pt]
x - y = k \\[4pt]
k x + y = 2
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -2 \neq 0 \implies \operatorname{rk} A = 2 \ \text{ for every } k
$$
$$
\det(A \mid b) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & -1 & k \\ k & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = (-2 - k) - (2 - k^{2}) + 2\,(1 + k) = k^{2} + k - 2 = (k - 1)(k + 2)
$$
$$
k \neq 1, \ k \neq -2: \qquad \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 3 \neq \operatorname{rk} A \implies \text{inconsistent}
$$
$$
k = 1: \quad (x, y) = \left( \frac{3}{2},\ \frac{1}{2} \right),
\qquad
k = -2: \quad (x, y) = (0,\ 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is not square, so Cramer's rule does not apply directly and we compare the ranks. The coefficient matrix is $3 \times 2$ and contains the minor $-2 \neq 0$ of the first two rows, hence it has rank $2$ for every value of $k$. The augmented matrix is square of order $3$; its determinant, expanded along the first row, is $(k - 1)(k + 2)$.

If $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -2$ the augmented matrix has rank $3$, different from the rank of $A$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is **inconsistent**. If $k = 1$ or $k = -2$ the two ranks equal $2$, equal to the number of unknowns, so the solution is unique, and it is found with the first two equations, which correspond to the nonzero minor. For $k = 1$ we have $x + y = 2$ and $x - y = 1$, from which $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$; the third equation, $x + y = 2$, is satisfied. For $k = -2$ we have $x + y = 2$ and $x - y = -2$, from which $x = 0$ and $y = 2$, and the third equation, $-2x + y = 2$, is satisfied.

</div>
