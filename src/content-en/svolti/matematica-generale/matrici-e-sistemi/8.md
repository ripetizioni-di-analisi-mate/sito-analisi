## Exercise 1

The demand and supply functions of two goods are
$$
q_{d1} = 42 - 2p_1 - p_2, \quad q_{s1} = 3p_1 - 4,
\qquad
q_{d2} = 30 - p_1 - 2p_2, \quad q_{s2} = 2p_2 - 2 .
$$
Find the equilibrium prices and quantities, and state whether the goods are substitutes or complements.

$$
\begin{cases}
42 - 2p_1 - p_2 = 3p_1 - 4 \\[4pt]
30 - p_1 - 2p_2 = 2p_2 - 2
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
5p_1 + p_2 = 46 \\[4pt]
p_1 + 4p_2 = 32
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\det A = \begin{vmatrix} 5 & 1 \\ 1 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 19
$$
$$
p_1 = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 46 & 1 \\ 32 & 4 \end{vmatrix}}{19} = \frac{184 - 32}{19} = 8,
\qquad
p_2 = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 5 & 46 \\ 1 & 32 \end{vmatrix}}{19} = \frac{160 - 46}{19} = 6
$$
$$
q_1 = 3 \cdot 8 - 4 = 20,
\qquad
q_2 = 2 \cdot 6 - 2 = 10
$$

::grafico{f="46-5*x;(32-x)/4" punti="8,6,pieno" etichette="9.4,3,market 1;0.4,9.8,market 2" xmin=0 xmax=12 ymin=0 ymax=12 nomi="p₁,p₂" titolo="The price pairs that balance the first market ($5p_1 + p_2 = 46$) and the second ($p_1 + 4p_2 = 32$) meet at $(8, 6)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Equilibrium** requires demand to equal supply in *both* markets, so the two conditions form a system. Moving the terms with the prices to the left-hand side and the constant ones to the right-hand side we obtain a linear system of two equations in the unknowns $p_1$ and $p_2$. The determinant of the coefficient matrix is $19 \neq 0$, hence by **Cramer's rule** the equilibrium exists and is unique, with prices $p_1 = 8$ and $p_2 = 6$.

The equilibrium quantities are obtained from the supplies; as a check, the demands give $42 - 16 - 6 = 20$ and $30 - 8 - 12 = 10$. In the demand for each good the price of the other good appears with a negative coefficient, so an increase in the price of one good reduces the demand for the other; hence the two goods are **complements**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An economy with two sectors has technology matrix and final demand
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0.1 & 0.4 \\ 0.3 & 0.2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
d = \begin{pmatrix} 24 \\ 36 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Find the production levels of the two sectors that meet the final demand.

$$
I_2 - A = \begin{pmatrix} 0.9 & -0.4 \\ -0.3 & 0.8 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(I_2 - A) = 0.72 - 0.12 = 0.6
$$
$$
(I_2 - A)^{-1} = \frac{1}{0.6} \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 0.4 \\ 0.3 & 0.9 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{1}{0.6} \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 \cdot 24 + 0.4 \cdot 36 \\ 0.3 \cdot 24 + 0.9 \cdot 36 \end{pmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{0.6} \begin{pmatrix} 33.6 \\ 39.6 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} 56 \\ 66 \end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **Leontief model** the production $x$ must cover the intermediate uses $Ax$ and the final demand $d$, so it satisfies $(I_2 - A)\,x = d$. The matrix $I_2 - A$ has determinant $0.6 \neq 0$, hence it is invertible and $x = (I_2 - A)^{-1} d$. The inverse is obtained with the formula for order $2$, swapping the entries of the main diagonal, changing the sign of the other two and dividing by the determinant.

The first sector must produce $56$ units and the second $66$. As a check, the intermediate uses of the product of the first sector are $0.1 \cdot 56 + 0.4 \cdot 66 = 32$, and $56 - 32 = 24$ is exactly the final demand. The columns of $A$ have sums $0.4$ and $0.6$, less than $1$, so it was predictable that the production levels would turn out positive.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An economy with two sectors has technology matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 & 0.2 \\ 0.25 & 0.4 \end{pmatrix}
$$
and produces the quantities $x = (40, 50)$. Find the final demand that is met. Then find by how much the two production levels must increase if the final demand of the first sector increases by $5$ units and that of the second remains unchanged.

$$
d = (I_2 - A)\,x = \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 & -0.2 \\ -0.25 & 0.6 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 40 \\ 50 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} 20 - 10 \\ -10 + 30 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} 10 \\ 20 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\det(I_2 - A) = 0.3 - 0.05 = 0.25,
\qquad
(I_2 - A)^{-1} = \frac{1}{0.25} \begin{pmatrix} 0.6 & 0.2 \\ 0.25 & 0.5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2.4 & 0.8 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\Delta x = (I_2 - A)^{-1}\, \Delta d = \begin{pmatrix} 2.4 & 0.8 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 12 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When the production is known, the final demand is obtained directly from the equation of the model, $d = x - Ax = (I_2 - A)\,x$, with a row-by-column product. The quantities $40$ and $50$ therefore meet a final demand of $10$ and $20$ units.

If the final demand changes by $\Delta d$, the new production $x + \Delta x$ must satisfy $(I_2 - A)(x + \Delta x) = d + \Delta d$; subtracting $(I_2 - A)\,x = d$ there remains $(I_2 - A)\,\Delta x = \Delta d$, and since $\det(I_2 - A) = 0.25 \neq 0$ we obtain $\Delta x = (I_2 - A)^{-1} \Delta d$. The entries of the first column of the inverse, $2.4$ and $1$, are the increases of the two production levels for each additional unit of final demand of the first sector. With $\Delta d = (5, 0)$ the production levels must increase by $12$ and $5$ units. The second sector must also produce more, because the first sector uses part of its product as input. As a check, $(I_2 - A)(52, 55) = (26 - 11,\ -13 + 33) = (15, 20)$.

</div>
