## Exercise 1

Solve with Cramer's rule the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3x - 2y = 4 \\[4pt]
x + 4y = 6
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\det A = \begin{vmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ 1 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 12 + 2 = 14 \neq 0
$$
$$
x = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ 6 & 4 \end{vmatrix}}{14} = \frac{16 + 12}{14} = 2,
\qquad
y = \frac{\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 6 \end{vmatrix}}{14} = \frac{18 - 4}{14} = 1
$$

::grafico{f="(3*x-4)/2;(6-x)/4" punti="2,1,pieno" etichette="4,2.4,3x − 2y = 4;-1.8,2.4,x + 4y = 6" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=5 uguale=1 titolo="The lines $3x - 2y = 4$ and $x + 4y = 6$ meet at the point $(2, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has two equations and two unknowns, and the determinant of the coefficient matrix is $14 \neq 0$; therefore **Cramer's rule** applies and the system has one and only one solution. For $x$ we replace the first column, that of the coefficients of $x$, with the column of the constant terms $(4, 6)$, and divide the resulting determinant by $14$. For $y$ we replace the second column. We obtain the solution $(2, 1)$. As a check, $3 \cdot 2 - 2 \cdot 1 = 4$ and $2 + 4 \cdot 1 = 6$. In the plane the solution is the point where the two lines meet.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve with Cramer's rule the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 6 \\[4pt]
2x - y + z = 3 \\[4pt]
x + 2y - z = 2
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 \end{vmatrix}
&= (1 + 1 + 4) - (-1 + 2 - 2) \\[6pt]
&= 6 + 1 = 7
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\det A_1 = \begin{vmatrix} 6 & 1 & 1 \\ 3 & -1 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = (6 + 2 + 6) - (-2 + 12 - 3) = 7
$$
$$
\det A_2 = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 6 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = (-3 + 6 + 4) - (3 + 2 - 12) = 14
$$
$$
\det A_3 = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = (-2 + 3 + 24) - (-6 + 6 + 4) = 21
$$
$$
x = \frac{7}{7} = 1,
\qquad
y = \frac{14}{7} = 2,
\qquad
z = \frac{21}{7} = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is computed with **Sarrus's rule**. The products along the descending diagonals are $1 \cdot (-1) \cdot (-1) = 1$, $1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 1$ and $1 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 4$; those along the ascending diagonals are $1 \cdot (-1) \cdot 1 = -1$, $2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 2$ and $(-1) \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = -2$. Therefore $\det A = 6 - (-1) = 7 \neq 0$, and by **Cramer's rule** the system has only one solution.

The matrix $A_j$ is obtained from $A$ by replacing column $j$ with the column of the constant terms $(6, 3, 2)$. The three determinants, again computed with Sarrus, equal $7$, $14$ and $21$, and dividing each by $\det A = 7$ we obtain the solution $(1, 2, 3)$. Substituting into the equations we verify that $1 + 2 + 3 = 6$, $2 - 2 + 3 = 3$ and $1 + 4 - 3 = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A firm produces three items, which pass through three departments. One unit of the first item requires $1$ hour of cutting, $2$ of assembly and $1$ of finishing; one unit of the second requires $2$, $1$ and $1$ hours; one unit of the third requires $1$, $1$ and $2$ hours. The hours available each week are $80$ for cutting, $70$ for assembly and $90$ for finishing. Find the quantities of the three items that use up all the hours of every department.

$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3 = 80 \\[4pt]
2x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 70 \\[4pt]
x_1 + x_2 + 2x_3 = 90
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\det A = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = (2 + 2 + 2) - (1 + 1 + 8) = -4
$$
$$
\det A_1 = \begin{vmatrix} 80 & 2 & 1 \\ 70 & 1 & 1 \\ 90 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = (160 + 180 + 70) - (90 + 80 + 280) = -40
$$
$$
\det A_2 = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 80 & 1 \\ 2 & 70 & 1 \\ 1 & 90 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = (140 + 80 + 180) - (70 + 90 + 320) = -80
$$
$$
\det A_3 = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & 80 \\ 2 & 1 & 70 \\ 1 & 1 & 90 \end{vmatrix} = (90 + 140 + 160) - (80 + 70 + 360) = -120
$$
$$
x_1 = \frac{-40}{-4} = 10,
\qquad
x_2 = \frac{-80}{-4} = 20,
\qquad
x_3 = \frac{-120}{-4} = 30
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Denote by $x_1$, $x_2$, $x_3$ the weekly quantities of the three items. The cutting hours used are $x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3$, and they must equal the $80$ available; in the same way we write the equations for assembly and finishing. We obtain a linear system of three equations in three unknowns, in which each row of the coefficient matrix corresponds to a department and each column to an item.

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is $-4 \neq 0$, so by **Cramer's rule** the production plan that uses all the hours exists and is unique. The determinants $\det A_1$, $\det A_2$, $\det A_3$ equal $-40$, $-80$ and $-120$, hence the firm must produce $10$, $20$ and $30$ units of the three items. The quantities are positive, so the solution makes economic sense. As a check, the cutting hours are $10 + 40 + 30 = 80$.

</div>
