## Exercise 1

Solve with Gauss's method the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y - z = 1 \\[4pt]
2x + 3y + z = 9 \\[4pt]
x - y + 2z = 5
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 & 9 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 & 5 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 - 2R_1 \\ R_3 - R_1}} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & -2 & 3 & 4 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & -2 & 3 & 4 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{R_3 + 2R_2} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 & 18 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
9z = 18 \implies z = 2,
\qquad
y = 7 - 3z = 1,
\qquad
x = 1 - y + z = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Gauss's method** works on the augmented matrix, which has the coefficients to the left of the bar and the constant terms to the right. First we make zero the entries of the first column below the pivot $1$, subtracting twice the first row from the second and the first row from the third; then we make zero the $-2$ of the second column by adding twice the second row to the third. Elementary operations give an equivalent system, so the solutions do not change.

The resulting matrix is in echelon form with three pivots, hence the coefficient matrix and the augmented matrix both have rank $3$, equal to the number of unknowns, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is determinate. We work upward from the last equation, $9z = 18$, which gives $z = 2$; the second, $y + 3z = 7$, gives $y = 1$; the first, $x + y - z = 1$, gives $x = 2$. As a check, $2 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 1 + 2 = 9$ and $2 - 1 + 4 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Discuss and solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y - z = 3 \\[4pt]
2x + 5y + z = 8 \\[4pt]
x + 3y + 2z = 5
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 & 1 & 8 \\ 1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 - 2R_1 \\ R_3 - R_1}} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 2 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{R_3 - R_2} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2 < 3
$$
$$
z = t,
\qquad
y = 2 - 3t,
\qquad
x = 3 - 2y + z = -1 + 7t
$$
$$
S = \{ (-1 + 7t,\ 2 - 3t,\ t) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After the reduction the third row of the augmented matrix is entirely zero, so the coefficient matrix and the augmented matrix have the same rank, $2$. By the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is consistent, and since the rank is less than the number of unknowns the solutions depend on $3 - 2 = 1$ parameter, that is, there are $\infty^{1}$ of them.

The unknowns with a pivot are $x$ and $y$, while $z$ does not correspond to a pivot and can be chosen freely; we set $z = t$. The second equation, $y + 3z = 2$, gives $y = 2 - 3t$, and the first gives $x = 3 - 2\,(2 - 3t) + t = -1 + 7t$. For example, with $t = 0$ we obtain the solution $(-1, 2, 0)$, and indeed $-1 + 4 - 0 = 3$, $-2 + 10 + 0 = 8$, $-1 + 6 + 0 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x - y + 2z = 3 \\[4pt]
2x + 3z = 5
\end{cases}
$$
has solutions.

$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 & 5 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 - R_1 \\ R_3 - 2R_1}} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & 3 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{R_3 - R_2} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = 2,
\qquad
\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After the reduction to echelon form the last row of the augmented matrix is $(0 \ \ 0 \ \ 0 \mid 1)$, which corresponds to the equation $0x + 0y + 0z = 1$, that is, $0 = 1$, which has no solutions. In the coefficient matrix the nonzero rows are two, in the augmented matrix they are three; therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2 \neq 3 = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b)$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is **inconsistent**. The reason can also be seen from the original equations, because the sum of the first two gives $2x + 3z = 4$, in contradiction with the third equation.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether the system of three equations in two unknowns
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x - y = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 3y = 7
\end{cases}
$$
is consistent and, if so, solve it.

$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -3 \neq 0 \implies \operatorname{rk} A = 2
$$
$$
\det(A \mid b) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 7 \end{vmatrix} = 1\,(-7 - 0) - 1\,(14 - 0) + 3\,(6 + 1) = 0
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2 = \operatorname{rk} A = n
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x - y = 0
\end{cases}
\implies
3x = 3, \quad x = 1, \quad y = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient matrix is $3 \times 2$, so its rank is at most $2$, and the minor of the first two rows equals $-3 \neq 0$; hence $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. The augmented matrix is square of order $3$, and its determinant is zero; since it contains the same nonzero minor of order $2$, its rank is also $2$. By the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is consistent, and the common rank equals the number of unknowns, so the solution is **unique**.

The first two equations correspond to the nonzero minor and suffice to find it; the third is a consequence of the other two. Adding the first two equations we obtain $3x = 3$, so $x = 1$ and $y = 2$. As a check, the third equation gives $1 + 6 = 7$.

</div>
