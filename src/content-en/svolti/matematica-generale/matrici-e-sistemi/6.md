## Exercise 1

Use Gaussian elimination to solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y - z = 1 \\[4pt]
2x + 3y + z = 9 \\[4pt]
x - y + 2z = 5
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 & 9 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 & 5 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 - 2R_1 \\ R_3 - R_1}} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & -2 & 3 & 4 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & -2 & 3 & 4 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{R_3 + 2R_2} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 7 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 & 18 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
9z = 18 \implies z = 2,
\qquad
y = 7 - 3z = 1,
\qquad
x = 1 - y + z = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Gaussian elimination** uses the augmented matrix, with the coefficients to the left of the vertical bar and the constant terms to the right. First clear the entries below the pivot $1$ in the first column by subtracting twice the first row from the second and the first row from the third. Then clear $-2$ in the second column by adding twice the second row to the third. These elementary operations give an equivalent system with the same solutions.

The resulting matrix is in row echelon form with three pivots, hence the coefficient matrix and the augmented matrix both have rank $3$, equal to the number of unknowns, and by the **Rouché–Capelli theorem** the system has a unique solution. We work upward from the last equation, $9z = 18$, which gives $z = 2$; the second, $y + 3z = 7$, gives $y = 1$; the first, $x + y - z = 1$, gives $x = 2$. As a check, $2 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 1 + 2 = 9$ and $2 - 1 + 4 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify and solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y - z = 3 \\[4pt]
2x + 5y + z = 8 \\[4pt]
x + 3y + 2z = 5
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 & 1 & 8 \\ 1 & 3 & 2 & 5 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 - 2R_1 \\ R_3 - R_1}} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 2 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{R_3 - R_2} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2 < 3
$$
$$
z = t,
\qquad
y = 2 - 3t,
\qquad
x = 3 - 2y + z = -1 + 7t
$$
$$
S = \{ (-1 + 7t,\ 2 - 3t,\ t) : t \in \mathbb{R} \}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After row reduction, the third row of the augmented matrix is zero, so the coefficient matrix and the augmented matrix both have rank $2$. By the **Rouché–Capelli theorem**, the system is consistent. Since the rank is below the number of unknowns, the solutions depend on $3 - 2 = 1$ free parameter. This is denoted by $\infty^{1}$.

The variables corresponding to pivot columns are $x$ and $y$. The variable $z$ is free, so set $z = t$. The second equation $y + 3z = 2$ gives $y = 2 - 3t$, and the first gives $x = 3 - 2\,(2 - 3t) + t = -1 + 7t$. For example, $t = 0$ gives the solution $(-1, 2, 0)$, since $-1 + 4 - 0 = 3$, $-2 + 10 + 0 = 8$, and $-1 + 6 + 0 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
x - y + 2z = 3 \\[4pt]
2x + 3z = 5
\end{cases}
$$
has solutions.

$$
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 & 5 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 - R_1 \\ R_3 - 2R_1}} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & 3 \end{array} \right)
\ \xrightarrow{R_3 - R_2} \
\left( \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array} \right)
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = 2,
\qquad
\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After row reduction, the last row of the augmented matrix is $(0 \ \ 0 \ \ 0 \mid 1)$. It represents the equation $0x + 0y + 0z = 1$, or $0 = 1$, which has no solution. The coefficient matrix has two nonzero rows, and the augmented matrix has three. Therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2 \neq 3 = \operatorname{rk}(A \mid b)$, so the system is **inconsistent** by the **Rouché–Capelli theorem**. The original equations show the same contradiction because adding the first two gives $2x + 3z = 4$, which conflicts with the third equation.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether the system of three equations in two unknowns
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x - y = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 3y = 7
\end{cases}
$$
is consistent and, if so, solve it.

$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -3 \neq 0 \implies \operatorname{rk} A = 2
$$
$$
\det(A \mid b) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 7 \end{vmatrix} = 1\,(-7 - 0) - 1\,(14 - 0) + 3\,(6 + 1) = 0
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk}(A \mid b) = 2 = \operatorname{rk} A = n
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 3 \\[4pt]
2x - y = 0
\end{cases}
\implies
3x = 3, \quad x = 1, \quad y = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient matrix is $3 \times 2$, so its rank is at most $2$, and the minor of the first two rows equals $-3 \neq 0$; hence $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. The augmented matrix is square of order $3$, and its determinant is zero; since it contains the same nonzero minor of order $2$, its rank is also $2$. By the **Rouché–Capelli theorem** the system is consistent, and the common rank equals the number of unknowns, so the solution is **unique**.

The first two equations correspond to the nonzero minor and are enough to find the solution. The third equation follows from the other two. Adding the first two equations gives $3x = 3$, so $x = 1$ and $y = 2$. As a check, the third equation gives $1 + 6 = 7$.

</div>
