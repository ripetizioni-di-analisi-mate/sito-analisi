## Exercise 1

A debt of $5000$ euros is due in $2$ years. At the rate of $4\%$ per year compound, determine the equivalent amount to be paid in $5$ years and the equivalent amount to be paid today.

$$
X_5 = 5000 \cdot 1.04^{3} = 5000 \cdot 1.124864 = 5624.32 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
X_0 = 5000 \cdot 1.04^{-2} \approx 5000 \cdot 0.924556 \approx 4622.78 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two amounts are **financially equivalent** if they have the same value at the same date. Postponing the payment from time $2$ to time $5$ means accumulating it for $3$ years, so it is multiplied by $1.04^{3}$; bringing it forward to time $0$ means discounting it for $2$ years, so it is multiplied by $1.04^{-2}$.

As a check, the two amounts found must be equivalent to each other, and indeed $4622.78 \cdot 1.04^{5} \approx 5624.32$. The result does not depend on the date chosen for the comparison, because under compound interest the independence of the valuation date holds.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A debtor must pay $1000$ euros in $1$ year, $2000$ euros in $2$ years and $3000$ euros in $4$ years. They agree to replace the three payments with two equal payments $X$, in $2$ and in $3$ years. At the rate of $5\%$ per year compound, compute $X$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{debts at time } 4 &= 1000 \cdot 1.05^{3} + 2000 \cdot 1.05^{2} + 3000 \\[6pt]
&= 1157.625 + 2205 + 3000 \\[6pt]
&= 6362.625
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{payments at time } 4 = X \cdot 1.05^{2} + X \cdot 1.05 = 2.1525\,X
$$
$$
2.1525\,X = 6362.625
\iff
X \approx 2955.92 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two payment plans are equivalent if they have the same value at any date, because under compound interest equivalence **does not depend on the valuation date**. It is convenient to choose the date of the last debt, time $4$, so that all the exponents are non-negative. At time $4$ the debt of $1000$ euros is accumulated for $3$ years, that of $2000$ euros for $2$ years, and that of $3000$ euros falls due exactly at that date.

The new payments, made at times $2$ and $3$, are worth $X \cdot 1.05^{2}$ and $X \cdot 1.05$ at time $4$. Equating the two values gives a linear equation in $X$, and the two payments are about $2955.92$ euros each. As a check with the present value, $1000 \cdot 1.05^{-1} + 2000 \cdot 1.05^{-2} + 3000 \cdot 1.05^{-4} \approx 5234.55$ euros and $2955.92 \cdot \left( 1.05^{-2} + 1.05^{-3} \right) \approx 5234.55$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three debts of $1500$, $2500$ and $4000$ euros are due in $6$, $18$ and $30$ months. Determine the average due date at the rate of $3\%$ per year compound and compare it with the arithmetic average due date.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 1500 \cdot 1.03^{-0.5} + 2500 \cdot 1.03^{-1.5} + 4000 \cdot 1.03^{-2.5} \\[6pt]
&\approx 1477.99 + 2391.58 + 3715.07 \\[6pt]
&\approx 7584.64 \text{ euros}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
8000 \cdot 1.03^{-t^*} = 7584.64
\iff
t^* = \frac{\ln 8000 - \ln 7584.64}{\ln 1.03} \approx \frac{0.053316}{0.029559} \approx 1.804 \text{ years}
$$
$$
1.804 \cdot 12 \approx 21.6 \text{ months},
\qquad
\frac{1500 \cdot 6 + 2500 \cdot 18 + 4000 \cdot 30}{8000} = \frac{174\,000}{8000} = 21.75 \text{ months}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **average due date** is the date at which the single payment of the sum of the debts, $8000$ euros, is equivalent to the three payments. One computes the present value of the three debts, with the times in years ($0.5$, $1.5$ and $2.5$), and requires it to equal the present value of $8000$ euros paid at time $t^*$. The unknown is in the exponent, so one takes the logarithm, and obtains $t^* \approx 1.804$ years, that is, about $21.6$ months.

The **arithmetic average due date** is the mean of the times weighted by the amounts, and it is exact when commercial discount is used. Here it equals $21.75$ months, a value only slightly different from the average due date under compound interest. The due date lies between the first and the last time, and it is closer to $30$ months than to $6$ months, because the largest debt is the last one.

</div>
