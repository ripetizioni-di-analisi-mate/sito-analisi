## Exercise 1

Two investments both cost $5000$ euros today. Investment A returns $2000$ euros at the end of each of the following three years, investment B returns $6000$ euros at the end of the third year. Compare them with the NPV criterion at the rate of $4\%$ and compute the IRR of B.

$$
\operatorname{NPV}_A(0.04) = -5000 + 2000\,a_{\overline{3}|0.04} \approx -5000 + 5550.18 = 550.18 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
\operatorname{NPV}_B(0.04) = -5000 + 6000 \cdot 1.04^{-3} \approx -5000 + 5333.98 = 333.98 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
-5000 + 6000\,(1 + i^*)^{-3} = 0
\iff
(1 + i^*)^{3} = 1.2
\iff
i^* = 1.2^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1 \approx 6.27\%
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inflows of A are three equal annual payments in arrears, so their present value is $2000\,a_{\overline{3}|0.04}$; the inflow of B is a single amount, which is discounted for $3$ years. Both NPVs are positive, therefore at $4\%$ both investments are worthwhile; by the **NPV criterion** A is preferred, since it has the larger net present value. The two total inflows are equal, $6000$ euros, but A distributes them earlier, and an amount available earlier has a larger present value.

The IRR of B makes the NPV zero, and the equation is solved with the cube root, because it contains a single power of $1 + i^*$. The IRR of about $6.27\%$ is greater than $4\%$, in agreement with the fact that $\operatorname{NPV}_B(0.04) > 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An investment requires $2000$ euros today and returns $1100$ euros in one year and $1210$ euros in two years. Verify that the IRR exists and is unique, compute it, and compute the NPV at $6\%$.

$$
a_0 = -2000 < 0,
\qquad
a_1, a_2 > 0,
\qquad
a_0 + a_1 + a_2 = 310 > 0
$$
$$
-2000 + 1100\,v + 1210\,v^{2} = 0,
\qquad
v = (1 + i^*)^{-1}
$$
$$
v = \frac{-1100 \pm \sqrt{1100^{2} + 4 \cdot 1210 \cdot 2000}}{2 \cdot 1210} = \frac{-1100 \pm 3300}{2420}
$$
$$
v = \frac{2200}{2420} = \frac{10}{11}
\iff
1 + i^* = \frac{11}{10}
\iff
i^* = 10\%
$$
$$
\operatorname{NPV}(0.06) = -2000 + \frac{1100}{1.06} + \frac{1210}{1.06^{2}} \approx -2000 + 1037.74 + 1076.90 \approx 114.63 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The investment has an initial outflow followed only by inflows, and the sum of the cash flows is positive, so the hypotheses of the theorem on the **existence and uniqueness of the IRR** are satisfied, and the IRR is unique and positive.

With the substitution $v = (1 + i^*)^{-1}$ the equation $\operatorname{NPV}(i^*) = 0$ becomes quadratic in $v$. The discriminant is $1\,210\,000 + 9\,680\,000 = 10\,890\,000 = 3300^{2}$. The negative root, $v = -\dfrac{20}{11}$, is not acceptable, because $v = \dfrac{1}{1 + i^*}$ is positive for $i^* > -1$; what remains is $v = \dfrac{10}{11}$, so the IRR is $10\%$.

The NPV at $6\%$ is positive, in agreement with the theorem, which gives $\operatorname{NPV}(i) > 0$ for every rate $i$ less than the IRR. As a check of the IRR, $\dfrac{1100}{1.1} + \dfrac{1210}{1.21} = 1000 + 1000 = 2000$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An investment requires $1000$ euros today and returns $300$, $400$ and $500$ euros at the end of the following three years. Prove that the IRR belongs to the interval $(0;\ 0.2)$ and approximate it with five steps of the bisection method.

$$
\operatorname{NPV}(i) = -1000 + \frac{300}{1 + i} + \frac{400}{(1 + i)^{2}} + \frac{500}{(1 + i)^{3}}
$$
$$
\operatorname{NPV}(0) = 200 > 0,
\qquad
\operatorname{NPV}(0.2) = -1000 + 250 + 277.78 + 289.35 \approx -182.87 < 0
$$

| interval | midpoint | NPV at the midpoint | half kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $[0;\ 0.2]$ | $0.1$ | $-21.04$ | left |
| $[0;\ 0.1]$ | $0.05$ | $80.44$ | right |
| $[0.05;\ 0.1]$ | $0.075$ | $27.68$ | right |
| $[0.075;\ 0.1]$ | $0.0875$ | $2.84$ | right |
| $[0.0875;\ 0.1]$ | $0.09375$ | $-9.21$ | left |

$$
i^* \in [0.0875;\ 0.09375],
\qquad
i^* \approx 8.90\%
$$

::grafico{f="-1000+300/(1+x/100)+400/(1+x/100)^2+500/(1+x/100)^3+0*sqrt(x)" punti="0,200,pieno;10,-21.04,pieno;5,80.44,pieno;8.896,0,vuoto" xmin=-4 xmax=36 ymin=-500 ymax=500 nomi="i,NPV" legenda="\operatorname{NPV}(i)" titolo="The NPV of the investment $(-1000, 300, 400, 500)$, with the rate as a percentage on the horizontal axis, vanishes at $i^* \approx 8.90\%$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $\operatorname{NPV}$ is continuous for $i > -1$, because it is a sum of continuous functions, and at the endpoints of the interval $[0;\ 0.2]$ it has opposite signs. By the **intermediate zero theorem** there therefore exists a rate $i^* \in (0;\ 0.2)$ that makes the NPV zero. The hypotheses of the theorem on the existence and uniqueness of the IRR are satisfied, because there is an initial outflow followed only by inflows and the sum of the cash flows is $200 > 0$; therefore this zero is the unique IRR.

At each step of the **bisection method** one computes the NPV at the midpoint of the interval and keeps the half at whose endpoints the NPV has opposite signs. For example at the first step $\operatorname{NPV}(0.1) < 0$ and $\operatorname{NPV}(0) > 0$, so the zero lies in the left half $[0;\ 0.1]$. After five steps the IRR lies in an interval of width $0.00625$, between $8.75\%$ and $9.375\%$; continuing one obtains $i^* \approx 8.90\%$.

</div>
