## Exercise 1

Compute the right-hand and left-hand limits of $f(x) = \dfrac{x + 1}{x - 2}$ as $x \to 2$.

$$
x \to 2:
\qquad
x + 1 \to 3,
\qquad
x - 2 \to 0
$$
$$
x \to 2^+ \implies x - 2 \to 0^+ \implies \frac{x + 1}{x - 2} \to +\infty
$$
$$
x \to 2^- \implies x - 2 \to 0^- \implies \frac{x + 1}{x - 2} \to -\infty
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator tends to $3$ and the denominator to $0$, so the quotient becomes large in absolute value. It is not an indeterminate form, because $\dfrac{3}{0}$ with the sign of the denominator gives an infinite limit. The sign depends on the side. For $x$ just greater than $2$ the denominator is positive and small, and the quotient tends to $+\infty$; for $x$ just less than $2$ the denominator is negative, and the quotient tends to $-\infty$. The two **right-hand and left-hand limits** are different, therefore the limit as $x \to 2$ does not exist, and the line $x = 2$ is a vertical asymptote.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the right-hand and left-hand limits of $f(x) = e^{\frac{1}{x}}$ as $x \to 0$.

$$
x \to 0^+ \implies \frac{1}{x} \to +\infty \implies e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to +\infty
$$
$$
x \to 0^- \implies \frac{1}{x} \to -\infty \implies e^{\frac{1}{x}} \to 0
$$

::grafico{f="exp(1/x)" punti="0,0,vuoto" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=7 legenda="y = e^{\frac{1}{x}}" titolo="$e^{\frac{1}{x}}$ tends to $0$ as $x \to 0^-$ and to $+\infty$ as $x \to 0^+$, so $x = 0$ is a vertical asymptote only from the right"}

<div class="spiegazione">

We first compute the limit of the exponent, then that of the exponential. From the right $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $+\infty$ and the exponential with base $e > 1$ diverges; from the left the exponent tends to $-\infty$ and the exponential tends to $0$, remaining positive. The two **right-hand and left-hand limits** are different, so the limit at $0$ does not exist. The left-hand limit is finite and the right-hand one infinite, therefore the line $x = 0$ is a vertical asymptote only from the right.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^2 + 1 & \text{if } x < 1 , \\[4pt]
3 - x & \text{if } x \geq 1 .
\end{cases}
$$
Determine whether $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$ exists. Then determine it after replacing $3 - x$ with $3x$.

$$
\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^-} (x^2 + 1) = 2,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^+} (3 - x) = 2
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = 2
$$
$$
\text{with } 3x: \qquad \lim_{x \to 1^+} 3x = 3 \neq 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined by two different formulas to the left and to the right of $1$, so we compute separately the **left-hand limit**, with the first formula, and the **right-hand limit**, with the second. Both formulas are polynomials, and the limits are computed by substitution. In the first case both equal $2$, therefore the limit exists and equals $2$. With the formula $3x$ the right-hand limit becomes $3$, different from the left-hand one, so the limit **does not exist** and the graph has a jump at $x = 1$.

</div>
