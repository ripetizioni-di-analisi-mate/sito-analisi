## Exercise 1

Find the value of the parameter $k$ for which the following function is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$:
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
k x + 1 & \text{if } x \leq 2 , \\[4pt]
x^2 - 1 & \text{if } x > 2 .
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 2^-} f(x) = f(2) = 2k + 1,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to 2^+} f(x) = 4 - 1 = 3
$$
$$
2k + 1 = 3
\iff
k = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two formulas are polynomials, so the function is continuous at every point other than $2$, where a single formula holds in a whole neighborhood. At the junction point $x = 2$ **continuity** requires that the limit exist and be equal to $f(2)$. The value $f(2)$ and the left-hand limit are computed with the first formula and equal $2k + 1$; the right-hand limit is computed with the second and equals $3$. Imposing equality gives $k = 1$, and for this value the two pieces of the graph join at the point $(2, 3)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify the points of discontinuity of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - x}{x^2 - 1}$.

$$
x^2 - 1 = 0
\iff
x = 1 \ \text{ or } \ x = -1
$$
$$
\frac{x^2 - x}{x^2 - 1} = \frac{x\,(x - 1)}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} = \frac{x}{x + 1},
\qquad
x \neq \pm 1
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x}{x + 1} = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
\lim_{x \to -1^{\pm}} \frac{x}{x + 1} = \mp\infty
$$

::grafico{f="x/(x+1)" punti="1,0.5,vuoto" asintoti="x=-1" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 legenda="y = \dfrac{x^2 - x}{x^2 - 1}" titolo="The graph coincides with that of $\dfrac{x}{x + 1}$ except at the open point $\left( 1, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$, where the discontinuity is removable; at $x = -1$ there is a vertical asymptote"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a quotient of polynomials, continuous where the denominator does not vanish, so the only points to examine are $1$ and $-1$, excluded from the domain. Factoring numerator and denominator, the common factor $x - 1$ appears, which cancels for $x \neq 1$. At $x = 1$ the limit exists, is finite and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the **discontinuity** is **removable**, and by setting $f(1) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ the function becomes continuous at $1$. At $x = -1$ the numerator tends to $-1$ and the denominator to $0$, so the function tends to $-\infty$ from the right and to $+\infty$ from the left, hence the discontinuity is **of the second kind**, with a vertical asymptote.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A supplier charges a price of $10$ euros per piece for orders of up to $100$ pieces; beyond $100$ pieces the cost of the order is $9q + c$ euros, where $q$ is the number of pieces. Find $c$ so that the cost of the order is a continuous function of $q$, and compute the cost of an order of $150$ pieces.

$$
C(q) =
\begin{cases}
10\,q & \text{if } 0 \leq q \leq 100 , \\[4pt]
9\,q + c & \text{if } q > 100
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\lim_{q \to 100^-} C(q) = C(100) = 1000,
\qquad
\lim_{q \to 100^+} C(q) = 900 + c
$$
$$
900 + c = 1000
\iff
c = 100,
\qquad
C(150) = 9 \cdot 150 + 100 = 1450 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cost is defined piecewise, and the two pieces are continuous; **continuity** has to be imposed only at $q = 100$, where the formula changes. The left-hand limit and the value at $100$ are computed with the first formula, the right-hand limit with the second, and equating them gives $c = 100$. With this value there is no jump, and indeed a customer who orders $101$ pieces spends $1009$ euros, just more than the $1000$ of an order of $100$, and each piece beyond the hundredth costs $9$ euros. With a value of $c$ less than $100$ there would be a downward jump, and for $c < 91$ ordering $101$ pieces would cost less than ordering $100$. The order of $150$ pieces costs $1450$ euros.

</div>
