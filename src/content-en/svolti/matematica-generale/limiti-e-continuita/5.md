## Exercise 1

Find the asymptotes of $f(x) = \dfrac{2x + 3}{x - 1}$.

$$
D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\},
\qquad
\lim_{x \to 1^{\pm}} \frac{2x + 3}{x - 1} = \pm\infty
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x + 3}{x - 1} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2 + \dfrac{3}{x}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{x}} = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical asymptote is sought at the point excluded from the domain, $x = 1$. As $x \to 1$ the numerator tends to $5$ and the denominator to $0$, so the function tends to $+\infty$ from the right and to $-\infty$ from the left, and $x = 1$ is a **vertical asymptote**. As $x \to \pm\infty$ numerator and denominator have the same degree, and the quotient tends to the ratio of the coefficients, $2$; therefore $y = 2$ is a **horizontal asymptote** both on the right and on the left. A function with a horizontal asymptote on one side has no oblique asymptote on that side, so there is no need to look for it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the asymptotes of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - x}{x + 2}$.

$$
\lim_{x \to -2^{\pm}} \frac{x^2 - x}{x + 2} = \pm\infty
$$
$$
m = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x^2 - x}{x\,(x + 2)} = 1
$$
$$
q = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \left( \frac{x^2 - x}{x + 2} - x \right) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x^2 - x - x^2 - 2x}{x + 2} = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{-3x}{x + 2} = -3
$$

::grafico{f="(x^2-x)/(x+2)" asintoti="x=-2" segmenti="-10,-13,8,5" xmin=-10 xmax=8 ymin=-20 ymax=15 titolo="The graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - x}{x + 2}$ with the vertical asymptote $x = -2$ and the oblique asymptote $y = x - 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain excludes $x = -2$, where the numerator equals $6$ and the denominator vanishes, so the function tends to $+\infty$ from the right and to $-\infty$ from the left and $x = -2$ is a **vertical asymptote**. As $x \to \pm\infty$ the numerator has degree one greater than the denominator, so the function diverges and we look for an **oblique asymptote**. The slope $m$ is the limit of $\dfrac{f(x)}{x}$, which equals $1$; the intercept $q$ is the limit of $f(x) - x$, which is computed by bringing to a common denominator and equals $-3$. The oblique asymptote is the line $y = x - 3$, on the right and on the left.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A firm has total cost $C(q) = 400 + 0.1\,q^2$. Find the average cost and its asymptotes for $q > 0$, and interpret them.

$$
\bar{C}(q) = \frac{400 + 0.1\,q^2}{q} = \frac{400}{q} + 0.1\,q
$$
$$
\lim_{q \to 0^+} \bar{C}(q) = +\infty
$$
$$
m = \lim_{q \to +\infty} \frac{\bar{C}(q)}{q} = \lim_{q \to +\infty} \left( \frac{400}{q^2} + 0.1 \right) = 0.1,
\qquad
q_0 = \lim_{q \to +\infty} \left( \bar{C}(q) - 0.1\,q \right) = \lim_{q \to +\infty} \frac{400}{q} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The average cost splits into the fixed cost per unit, $\dfrac{400}{q}$, and the variable part per unit, $0.1\,q$. As $q \to 0^+$ the first term diverges, so the line $q = 0$ is a **vertical asymptote**, because with very small output the fixed cost is divided among very few units. As $q \to +\infty$ the average cost diverges, and the limits for the **oblique asymptote** give slope $0.1$ and intercept $0$; here the intercept is denoted by $q_0$ because the letter $q$ is already the quantity. The asymptote is the line $y = 0.1\,q$, and therefore for large output the fixed cost per unit becomes negligible and the average cost is almost equal to the variable part alone.

</div>
