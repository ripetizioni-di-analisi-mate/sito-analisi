## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{3x^3 - x}{1 - 2x^3} .
$$

$$
\frac{3x^3 - x}{1 - 2x^3} = \frac{x^3 \left( 3 - \dfrac{1}{x^2} \right)}{x^3 \left( \dfrac{1}{x^3} - 2 \right)} = \frac{3 - \dfrac{1}{x^2}}{\dfrac{1}{x^3} - 2} \to \frac{3}{-2} = -\frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator tends to $+\infty$ and the denominator to $-\infty$, so the form is $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. In both we **factor out** the highest power, $x^3$, and cancel it. The terms $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^3}$ tend to $0$, therefore the quotient tends to the ratio of the coefficients of $x^3$, that is $-\dfrac{3}{2}$. The negative sign is consistent with the fact that, for large $x$, the numerator is positive and the denominator negative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{x^2 + 5}{x + 1}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{x + 1}{x^2 + 5} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^2 + 5}{x + 1} = \frac{x^2 \left( 1 + \dfrac{5}{x^2} \right)}{x \left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{x} \right)} = x \cdot \frac{1 + \dfrac{5}{x^2}}{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} \to -\infty
$$
$$
\frac{x + 1}{x^2 + 5} = \frac{1}{x} \cdot \frac{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}}{1 + \dfrac{5}{x^2}} \to 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We factor out the highest powers as for $x \to +\infty$, paying attention to the signs. In the first quotient the factor $x$ remains, which tends to $-\infty$, multiplied by a factor that tends to $1$; the limit is therefore $-\infty$. In the second, which is the reciprocal of the first, the factor $\dfrac{1}{x}$ remains, which tends to $0$, and the limit is $0$. The result is consistent with the sign, because for very negative $x$ the numerator $x^2 + 5$ is positive and the denominator $x + 1$ is negative, so the first quotient is negative, like its limit.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x \right) .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} - x
&= \frac{x^2 + 4x - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4x}{x \left( \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}} + 1 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{x}} + 1}
\to \frac{4}{2} = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root and $x$ both tend to $+\infty$, and the difference is in the form $+\infty - \infty$. We **rationalize** by multiplying and dividing by $\sqrt{x^2 + 4x} + x$, and so the numerator becomes $4x$. In the denominator we bring the factor $x^2$ out of the root, which comes out as $x$ because $x > 0$. After cancelling, the root tends to $1$ and the limit is $2$. The result means that, for large $x$, $\sqrt{x^2 + 4x}$ is approximately $x + 2$.

</div>
