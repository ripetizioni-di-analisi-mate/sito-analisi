## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1 + 3x)}{x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{2x} - 1}{5x} .
$$

$$
\frac{\ln(1 + 3x)}{x} = 3 \cdot \frac{\ln(1 + 3x)}{3x},
\qquad
t = 3x \to 0:
\qquad
3 \cdot \frac{\ln(1 + t)}{t} \to 3
$$
$$
\frac{e^{2x} - 1}{5x} = \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{e^{2x} - 1}{2x},
\qquad
t = 2x \to 0:
\qquad
\frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{e^{t} - 1}{t} \to \frac{2}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both limits are in the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$ and resemble the **standard limits** of the logarithm and of the exponential, but the argument is $3x$ or $2x$ instead of $x$. We multiply and divide by the missing number, so that the same argument appears in the denominator, that is $3x$ in the first case, $2x$ in the second. With the substitution $t = 3x$, or $t = 2x$, the variable $t$ tends to $0$ when $x$ tends to $0$, and the fraction becomes exactly a standard limit, which equals $1$. The numerical factors remain, so the limits are $3$ and $\dfrac{2}{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{x} \right)^{x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2x} \right)^{x} .
$$

$$
t = \frac{x}{2}:
\qquad
\left( 1 + \frac{2}{x} \right)^{x} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{2} \to e^{2}
$$
$$
t = 2x:
\qquad
\left( 1 + \frac{1}{2x} \right)^{x} = \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \right]^{\frac{1}{2}} \to e^{\frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{e}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, so the form is $1^{\infty}$, which is indeterminate. We try to reduce it to the **standard limit** $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{t} \right)^{t} \to e$. In the first case we set $t = \dfrac{x}{2}$, so that $\dfrac{2}{x} = \dfrac{1}{t}$ and the exponent $x$ becomes $2t$; since $t \to +\infty$, the power $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{t} \right)^{t}$ tends to $e$ and the limit is $e^{2}$. In the second case we set $t = 2x$ and the exponent $x$ becomes $\dfrac{t}{2}$, so the limit is $\sqrt{e}$. The same procedure, with $\dfrac{r}{x}$ in place of $\dfrac{2}{x}$, gives the limit $e^{r}$ of continuous compounding.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^5 + e^{x}}{2e^{x} - x^2}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\ln x}{\sqrt{x}} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^5 + e^{x}}{2e^{x} - x^2} = \frac{e^{x} \left( \dfrac{x^5}{e^{x}} + 1 \right)}{e^{x} \left( 2 - \dfrac{x^2}{e^{x}} \right)} = \frac{\dfrac{x^5}{e^{x}} + 1}{2 - \dfrac{x^2}{e^{x}}} \to \frac{0 + 1}{2 - 0} = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{\ln x}{\sqrt{x}} = \frac{\ln x}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}} \to 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first quotient powers and exponentials appear. By the **comparison of infinities** the exponential $e^{x}$ grows faster than any power, so we factor out $e^{x}$ in the numerator and in the denominator. The ratios $\dfrac{x^5}{e^{x}}$ and $\dfrac{x^2}{e^{x}}$ tend to $0$, and the limit is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, because for large $x$ the powers are negligible compared with the exponentials. In the second quotient the logarithm is compared with the power $x^{\frac{1}{2}}$, which grows faster than $\ln x$ even though the exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is small, therefore the limit is $0$.

</div>
