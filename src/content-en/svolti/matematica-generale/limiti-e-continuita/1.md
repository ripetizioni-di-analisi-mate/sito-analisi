## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^3 - 8}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{(x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} = \frac{x^2 + 2x + 4}{x + 2}, \qquad x \neq 2
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 + 2x + 4}{x + 2} = \frac{4 + 4 + 4}{4} = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting $x = 2$, numerator and denominator vanish, so the limit is in the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. A polynomial that vanishes at $2$ is divisible by $x - 2$, therefore both are **factored**, writing the numerator as a difference of cubes and the denominator as a difference of squares. The factor $x - 2$ cancels, because in the limit only $x \neq 2$ matters. The remaining function is continuous at $2$, so the limit is computed by substitution and equals $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 + 2x - 3}{2x^2 - x - 1} .
$$

$$
x^2 + 2x - 3 = (x - 1)(x + 3),
\qquad
2x^2 - x - 1 = (x - 1)(2x + 1)
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(x - 1)(x + 3)}{(x - 1)(2x + 1)} = \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x + 3}{2x + 1} = \frac{4}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $x = 1$ both trinomials equal $0$, so the form is $\dfrac{0}{0}$ and $x = 1$ is a root of both. Each trinomial factors as the product of $x - 1$ and a first-degree factor, which is found from the other root, that is $-3$ for the numerator and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ for the denominator, therefore $2x^2 - x - 1 = 2\,(x - 1)\left( x + \dfrac{1}{2} \right) = (x - 1)(2x + 1)$. After cancelling, we substitute $x = 1$ and obtain $\dfrac{4}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} .
$$

$$
\frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} = \frac{(\sqrt{x} - 2)(\sqrt{x} + 2)}{(x - 4)(\sqrt{x} + 2)} = \frac{x - 4}{(x - 4)(\sqrt{x} + 2)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here too substitution gives $\dfrac{0}{0}$, but the numerator contains a root and does not factor like a polynomial. We **rationalize** by multiplying numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{x} + 2$, and so the numerator becomes the difference of squares $x - 4$, which cancels with the denominator. Alternatively, one can observe that $x - 4 = (\sqrt{x} - 2)(\sqrt{x} + 2)$ for $x \geq 0$. The remaining function is continuous at $4$ and the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>
