## Exercise 1

Let $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$ be defined on the interval $[0, 5]$. Find the range of $f$, determine whether $f$ is injective and find the largest interval containing $5$ on which $f$ is injective.

$$
x_V = -\frac{-6}{2} = 3,
\qquad
f(3) = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4
$$
$$
f(0) = 5,
\qquad
f(5) = 25 - 30 + 5 = 0
$$
$$
f([0, 5]) = [-4, 5]
$$
$$
f(1) = 1 - 6 + 5 = 0 = f(5)
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-6*x+5+0*sqrt(x*(5-x))" punti="0,5,pieno;3,-4,pieno;1,0,pieno;5,0,pieno" segmenti="3,-4,3,0;0,-4,3,-4" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-6 ymax=6 titolo="On $[0, 5]$ the parabola falls from $5$ to the minimum $-4$ at $x = 3$ and rises back to $0$: the range is $[-4, 5]$, and the value $0$ is taken twice"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is quadratic with $a = 1 > 0$, so by the theorem on the **vertex of the parabola** its minimum value is attained at $x_V = 3$ and equals $-4$. The vertex lies in the interval $[0, 5]$; to the left of the vertex the function decreases and to the right it increases, therefore the maximum value on the interval is the larger of the values at the endpoints, $f(0) = 5$ and $f(5) = 0$. The graph on $[0, 5]$ is an unbroken arc of a parabola, so the function takes all the values between the minimum and the maximum, and the **range** is $[-4, 5]$; this property will be justified by the intermediate value theorem in the topic on limits and continuity.

The function is **not injective** on $[0, 5]$, because the two different values $1$ and $5$ have the same image $0$. It is instead increasing, and hence injective, on the interval $[3, 5]$, which lies to the right of the vertex. No larger interval containing $5$ works, because adding points to the left of $3$ gives values already taken to the right of the vertex.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f(x) = 3 - \dfrac{2}{x}$ for $x > 0$. Prove that $f$ is increasing and find its range.

$$
0 < x_1 < x_2
\implies
\frac{1}{x_1} > \frac{1}{x_2}
\implies
-\frac{2}{x_1} < -\frac{2}{x_2}
\implies
f(x_1) < f(x_2)
$$
$$
f(x) = y
\iff
\frac{2}{x} = 3 - y
\iff
x = \frac{2}{3 - y},
\qquad
x > 0 \iff y < 3
$$
$$
f\big((0, +\infty)\big) = (-\infty, 3)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of **increasing function**, we compare the images of any two values $x_1 < x_2$ of the domain. For positive numbers the reciprocal reverses inequalities, so $\dfrac{1}{x_1} > \dfrac{1}{x_2}$; multiplying by $-2$, which is negative, the inequality is reversed again, and adding $3$ to both sides gives $f(x_1) < f(x_2)$. Therefore $f$ is **increasing** on $(0, +\infty)$.

To find the range, we look for the values $y$ for which the equation $f(x) = y$ has a solution in the domain. Isolating $x$ gives $x = \dfrac{2}{3 - y}$, which makes sense for $y \neq 3$ and is positive exactly when $3 - y > 0$. Hence a value $y$ is taken if and only if $y < 3$, and the range is $(-\infty, 3)$. The value $3$ is never reached, because $\dfrac{2}{x}$ is always positive.

</div>
