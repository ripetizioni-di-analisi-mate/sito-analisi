## Exercise 1

A firm has fixed costs of $4500$ euros and a variable cost of $25$ euros per unit, and sells each unit at $40$ euros. Find the break-even point and the profit with $500$ units sold.

$$
C(q) = 4500 + 25q,
\qquad
R(q) = 40q,
\qquad
\pi(q) = 15q - 4500
$$
$$
q_0 = \frac{F}{p - c} = \frac{4500}{40 - 25} = 300
$$
$$
\pi(500) = 15 \cdot 500 - 4500 = 3000 \text{ euros}
$$

::grafico{f="40*x+0*sqrt(x);4500+25*x+0*sqrt(x)" punti="300,12000,pieno" segmenti="300,0,300,12000" xmin=-60 xmax=540 ymin=-2500 ymax=22500 nomi="q,€" legenda="R = 40q;C = 4500 + 25q" titolo="Revenue and cost meet at the break-even point $q_0 = 300$, where both are equal to $12000$ euros"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each unit sold brings a revenue of $40$ euros and costs $25$ euros, so it leaves a margin of $15$ euros. The **break-even point** is the quantity at which the sum of the margins covers the fixed cost, that is, $\dfrac{4500}{15} = 300$ units; at that point revenue and cost are both equal to $12000$ euros. Below $300$ units profit is negative, above it is positive. With $500$ units the firm exceeds break-even by $200$ units, and the profit is the margin on these units, $200 \cdot 15 = 3000$ euros, as the formula gives.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The cost of producing $q$ units is $C(q) = 200 + 12q$ euros. Find the average cost and the minimum quantity to produce for the average cost to be less than $16$ euros.

$$
\bar{C}(q) = \frac{200 + 12q}{q} = \frac{200}{q} + 12,
\qquad
q > 0
$$
$$
\frac{200}{q} + 12 < 16
\iff
\frac{200}{q} < 4
\iff
q > 50
$$

::grafico{f="200/x+12+0*sqrt(x)" asintoti="y=12" segmenti="50,0,50,16;0,16,50,16" punti="50,16,vuoto" xmin=-20 xmax=180 ymin=-5 ymax=45 nomi="q,C̄" legenda=0 titolo="The average cost $\bar{C}(q) = \dfrac{200}{q} + 12$ decreases as $q$ increases and approaches $12$ euros; it falls below $16$ euros for $q > 50$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **average cost** is the total cost divided by the quantity, and for a linear cost it splits into the fixed cost per unit, $\dfrac{200}{q}$, plus the variable unit cost $12$. In the inequality, subtract $12$ from both sides; then, since $q > 0$, we can multiply by $q$ without changing the direction, and we obtain $200 < 4q$, that is, $q > 50$. With exactly $50$ units the average cost is $16$ euros, so the condition requires at least $51$ units. As production increases, the fixed cost is spread over more units, and the average cost falls toward $12$ euros without ever reaching it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A firm faces the inverse demand $p = 60 - 3q$ and has cost $C(q) = 144 + 12q$. Find the break-even points, the quantity that maximizes profit and the maximum profit.

$$
\pi(q) = (60 - 3q)\,q - 144 - 12q = -3q^2 + 48q - 144
$$
$$
\pi(q) = 0
\iff
q^2 - 16q + 48 = 0
\iff
(q - 4)(q - 12) = 0
\iff
q = 4 \ \text{ or } \ q = 12
$$
$$
q_V = -\frac{48}{2 \cdot (-3)} = 8,
\qquad
\pi(8) = -192 + 384 - 144 = 48
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With a price that depends on the quantity, revenue is quadratic, and the profit $\pi = R - C$ is a downward-opening parabola. The **break-even points** are the zeros of the profit. Dividing by $-3$ gives a trinomial that factors as $(q - 4)(q - 12)$, because $4 + 12 = 16$ and $4 \cdot 12 = 48$. There are therefore two break-even points, and profit is positive between $4$ and $12$ units. The vertex lies halfway between the two zeros, at $q = 8$, and the **maximum profit** is $48$ euros, at a price of $60 - 24 = 36$ euros. Unlike the constant-price case, beyond $12$ units profit becomes negative again, because to sell more the price must be lowered too much.

</div>
