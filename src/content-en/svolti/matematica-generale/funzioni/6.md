## Exercise 1

Among all rectangles with perimeter $40\,\mathrm{cm}$, find the one with maximum area.

$$
x + y = 20,
\qquad
A(x) = x\,(20 - x) = -x^2 + 20x,
\qquad
0 < x < 20
$$
$$
x_V = -\frac{20}{2 \cdot (-1)} = 10,
\qquad
A(10) = 100\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If $x$ and $y$ are the lengths of the sides, the perimeter is $2x + 2y = 40$, so $y = 20 - x$, and the area becomes a function of $x$ alone. The function $A(x) = -x^2 + 20x$ is quadratic with a negative coefficient of $x^2$, therefore by the theorem on the **vertex of the parabola** its maximum value is attained at the vertex, $x = 10$, which belongs to the interval $(0, 20)$. Then also $y = 10$, and the rectangle of maximum area is the **square** with side $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, with area $100\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A firm has cost $C(q) = 400 + 40q$ and faces the inverse demand $p = 120 - 2q$. Find the quantity that maximizes profit, the corresponding price, the maximum profit and the interval of quantities for which profit is positive.

$$
\pi(q) = (120 - 2q)\,q - (400 + 40q) = -2q^2 + 80q - 400
$$
$$
q_V = -\frac{80}{2 \cdot (-2)} = 20,
\qquad
p = 120 - 40 = 80,
\qquad
\pi(20) = -800 + 1600 - 400 = 400
$$
$$
\pi(q) = 0
\iff
q^2 - 40q + 200 = 0
\iff
q = 20 \pm \sqrt{200} = 20 \pm 10\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\pi(q) > 0
\iff
20 - 10\sqrt{2} < q < 20 + 10\sqrt{2}
\iff
5.86 < q < 34.14
$$

::grafico{f="-2*x^2+80*x-400+0*sqrt(x*(42-x))" punti="20,400,pieno;5.858,0,pieno;34.142,0,pieno" segmenti="20,0,20,400" xmin=-5 xmax=45 ymin=-500 ymax=500 nomi="q,π" titolo="The profit $\pi(q) = -2q^2 + 80q - 400$ is maximum for $q = 20$ and is positive between the two break-even points $20 \pm 10\sqrt{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Profit** is revenue minus cost; revenue is obtained by multiplying the inverse demand by $q$, and subtracting the cost gives a quadratic function with coefficient of $q^2$ equal to $-2$. The parabola opens downward, so the vertex $q = 20$ gives the **maximum profit**, $400$ euros, at a price of $80$ euros.

The zeros of the profit are the **break-even points**. Dividing the equation $\pi(q) = 0$ by $-2$ gives $q^2 - 40q + 200 = 0$, with discriminant $1600 - 800 = 800 > 0$ and roots $20 \pm 10\sqrt{2}$. Since the coefficient of $q^2$ in $\pi$ is negative, profit is positive *only* between the two roots, that is, for quantities roughly between $5.86$ and $34.14$. The roots are symmetric with respect to the vertex, like all pairs of points of the parabola with the same $y$-coordinate.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The demand for a good as a function of price is $q = 600 - 20p$, with $0 \leq p \leq 30$. Find the price that maximizes revenue. Then find it in the case where the firm cannot produce more than $200$ units.

$$
R(p) = p\,q = p\,(600 - 20p) = -20p^2 + 600p
$$
$$
p_V = -\frac{600}{2 \cdot (-20)} = 15,
\qquad
q = 600 - 300 = 300,
\qquad
R(15) = 4500
$$
$$
q \leq 200
\iff
600 - 20p \leq 200
\iff
p \geq 20
$$
$$
R(20) = 20 \cdot 200 = 4000
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here revenue is written as a function of price, and it is a quadratic function of $p$ opening downward. Without other constraints, revenue is maximum at the vertex, at a price of $15$ euros, with $300$ units sold and a revenue of $4500$ euros.

With a production capacity of $200$ units, the quantity sold cannot exceed $200$, and this is equivalent to a price of at least $20$ euros. The vertex $p = 15$ is outside the admissible interval $[20, 30]$. On that interval, which lies entirely to the right of the vertex, the parabola is **decreasing**, therefore maximum revenue occurs at the left endpoint, $p = 20$, and equals $4000$ euros. The vertex gives the maximum *only* when it belongs to the domain of the problem; otherwise the maximum is at an endpoint.

</div>
