## Exercise 1

Find the natural domain of
$$
f(x) = \frac{x + 2}{x^2 - 9} .
$$

$$
x^2 - 9 \neq 0
\iff
(x - 3)(x + 3) \neq 0
\iff
x \neq 3 \ \text{ and } \ x \neq -3
$$
$$
D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-3, 3\} = (-\infty, -3) \cup (-3, 3) \cup (3, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a quotient of polynomials, so the only condition is that the **denominator be different from zero**. The denominator is a difference of squares and factors as $(x - 3)(x + 3)$; a product is zero when at least one of the factors is, therefore the denominator vanishes *only* at $x = 3$ and at $x = -3$. The numerator imposes no conditions, so at $x = -2$ the function is defined and equals $0$. The domain is therefore $\mathbb{R}$ without the two points $-3$ and $3$, which written with intervals is the union of three open intervals.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the natural domain of
$$
f(x) = \sqrt{\frac{x - 1}{x + 4}} .
$$

$$
\frac{x - 1}{x + 4} \geq 0
$$

| | $x < -4$ | $-4 < x < 1$ | $x > 1$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x - 1$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $x + 4$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| quotient | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
D = (-\infty, -4) \cup [1, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root has even index, so its argument must be **greater than or equal to zero**; moreover the argument is a fraction, and its denominator must be different from zero. The two conditions are satisfied together by studying the sign of the quotient. The numerator is positive for $x > 1$, the denominator for $x > -4$, and the quotient is positive where the two factors have the same sign, that is, for $x < -4$ and for $x > 1$. The quotient equals zero at $x = 1$, which therefore belongs to the domain, since $\sqrt{0} = 0$. The point $x = -4$ makes the denominator zero and is excluded. The domain is therefore $(-\infty, -4) \cup [1, +\infty)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the natural domain of
$$
f(x) = \ln\left(x^2 - 4x\right) + \frac{1}{\sqrt{6 - x}} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 - 4x > 0 \\[4pt]
6 - x > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x < 0 \ \text{ or } \ x > 4 \\[4pt]
x < 6
\end{cases}
$$
$$
D = (-\infty, 0) \cup (4, 6)
$$

::grafico{segmenti="0,-0.6,0,0.6;4,-0.6,4,0.6;6,-0.6,6,0.6" area="0.35;0.15;-4;0|0.35;0.15;4;8|-0.15;-0.35;-4;6" etichette="-3.4,0.75,logarithm;-3.4,-0.85,root;4.6,0.75,logarithm" xmin=-4 xmax=8 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.2 legenda=0 titolo="Above the axis the solutions of $x^2 - 4x > 0$, below it those of $6 - x > 0$: the domain is where the two strips overlap, $(-\infty, 0) \cup (4, 6)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is the sum of two terms, and each imposes its own conditions, which must hold **together**. The argument of the logarithm must be strictly positive. The polynomial $x^2 - 4x = x(x - 4)$ has as its graph an upward-opening parabola that vanishes at $0$ and at $4$, so it is positive outside the interval $[0, 4]$. In the second term the root is in the denominator, therefore its argument must be strictly positive, and not just greater than or equal to zero, because $\sqrt{0} = 0$ would make the denominator zero; this gives $x < 6$. The domain is the **intersection** of the solutions of the two conditions, that is, $(-\infty, 0) \cup (4, 6)$, as the diagram shows.

</div>
