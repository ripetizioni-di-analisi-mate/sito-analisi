## Exercise 1

The demand and supply of a good are $q_d(p) = 120 - 3p$ and $q_s(p) = 2p - 10$, with $p$ in euros. Find the equilibrium price and quantity and the state of the market at a price of $20$ euros.

$$
q_d(p) = q_s(p)
\iff
120 - 3p = 2p - 10
\iff
5p = 130
\iff
p^* = 26
$$
$$
q^* = 120 - 3 \cdot 26 = 42
$$
$$
q_d(20) = 60,
\qquad
q_s(20) = 30,
\qquad
q_d(20) - q_s(20) = 30
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At **market equilibrium** the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, so we set the two functions equal and solve the first-degree equation in the price. The equilibrium price is $26$ euros and the quantity traded is $42$; as a check, $q_s(26) = 52 - 10 = 42$. Supply is positive only for $p > 5$, and the price found satisfies this condition. At a price of $20$ euros, lower than the equilibrium price, consumers demand $60$ units and producers supply $30$, therefore there is an **excess demand** of $30$ units.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The inverse demand of a good is $p = 100 - q^2$ and the inverse supply is $p = 20 + 2q$, with $q \geq 0$. Find the equilibrium.

$$
100 - q^2 = 20 + 2q
\iff
q^2 + 2q - 80 = 0
\iff
q = -1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 80} = -1 \pm 9
$$
$$
q^* = 8,
\qquad
p^* = 20 + 2 \cdot 8 = 36
$$

::grafico{f="100-x^2+0*sqrt(x*(10-x));20+2*x+0*sqrt(x)" punti="8,36,pieno" segmenti="8,0,8,36;0,36,8,36" xmin=-2 xmax=12 ymin=-15 ymax=135 nomi="q,p" legenda="p = 100 - q^2;p = 20 + 2q" titolo="The demand $p = 100 - q^2$ and the supply $p = 20 + 2q$ intersect at the equilibrium point $(8, 36)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two functions give the price as a function of the quantity, so at equilibrium the prices are set equal. This gives a second-degree equation with roots $8$ and $-10$; the negative root has no economic meaning, because a quantity cannot be negative, therefore the equilibrium quantity is $q^* = 8$. The price is obtained from either curve, and it equals $36$ euros; as a check, on the demand curve $100 - 64 = 36$. The graph confirms that the curves meet only once in the first quadrant, because demand is decreasing and supply is increasing.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a market with inverse demand $p = 50 - 2q$ and inverse supply $p = 10 + 3q$, the government introduces a tax of $5$ euros on each unit sold, paid by the producers. Find the new equilibrium, the price paid by consumers, the price received by producers net of the tax and the tax revenue.

$$
p = (10 + 3q) + 5 = 15 + 3q
$$
$$
50 - 2q = 15 + 3q
\iff
q^* = 7,
\qquad
p^* = 50 - 14 = 36
$$
$$
p^* - 5 = 31,
\qquad
G = 5 \cdot 7 = 35 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Without the tax the equilibrium is $q = 8$, $p = 34$, as in the example in the theory on **market equilibrium**. The inverse supply gives the price producers ask in order to sell $q$ units; with the tax they must receive the same amount *after* paying it, so they ask $5$ euros more for each quantity, and the supply curve shifts up by $5$. Setting the new supply price equal to demand gives $q^* = 7$, with a price of $36$ euros paid by consumers. Producers keep $36 - 5 = 31$ euros. The **tax revenue** is the tax times the units sold, $35$ euros. Of the $5$ euros of tax, $2$ fall on consumers, who pay $36$ instead of $34$, and $3$ on producers, who receive $31$ instead of $34$.

</div>
