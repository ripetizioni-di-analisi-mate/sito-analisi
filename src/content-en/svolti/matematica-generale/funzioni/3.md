## Exercise 1

Find the inverse of $f(x) = 2e^{x} + 1$, with its domain, and verify that $f^{-1}(f(x)) = x$.

$$
y = 2e^{x} + 1
\iff
e^{x} = \frac{y - 1}{2}
\iff
x = \ln\frac{y - 1}{2},
\qquad
y > 1
$$
$$
f^{-1}(y) = \ln\frac{y - 1}{2},
\qquad
f^{-1} : (1, +\infty) \to \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
f^{-1}(f(x)) = \ln\frac{2e^{x} + 1 - 1}{2} = \ln e^{x} = x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $e^{x}$ is increasing, so $2e^{x} + 1$ is too, and it is therefore **injective** on $\mathbb{R}$. To find the inverse, solve the equation $y = f(x)$ for $x$: isolate the exponential and then take the natural logarithm, which is the inverse of $e^{x}$. The logarithm requires a positive argument, therefore $\dfrac{y - 1}{2} > 0$, that is, $y > 1$. The domain of the inverse is the range of $f$, and indeed $f(x) = 2e^{x} + 1$ takes exactly the values greater than $1$, since $e^{x}$ takes all positive values. The check confirms that $f^{-1}$ undoes what $f$ does.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ and $g(x) = x - 4$. Write the composite functions $g \circ f$ and $f \circ g$ and find their domains.

$$
(g \circ f)(x) = g(\sqrt{x}) = \sqrt{x} - 4,
\qquad
D_{g \circ f} = [0, +\infty)
$$
$$
(f \circ g)(x) = f(x - 4) = \sqrt{x - 4},
\qquad
x - 4 \geq 0
\iff
x \geq 4,
\qquad
D_{f \circ g} = [4, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **composite function** $g \circ f$, first $f$ is applied and then $g$. For $g \circ f$ we first compute $\sqrt{x}$, which requires $x \geq 0$, and then subtract $4$, an operation that imposes no conditions; the domain is therefore $[0, +\infty)$. For $f \circ g$ we first compute $x - 4$ and then take its square root, which requires $x - 4 \geq 0$; the domain is $[4, +\infty)$. The two compositions are **different**, both in the formula and in the domain; for example, for $x = 9$ the first equals $3 - 4 = -1$ and the second $\sqrt{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the inverse of the function $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 1$ restricted to the interval $[2, +\infty)$, with its domain.

$$
f(x) = (x - 2)^2 - 3,
\qquad
x \geq 2
$$
$$
y = (x - 2)^2 - 3
\iff
(x - 2)^2 = y + 3
\iff
x - 2 = \sqrt{y + 3}
$$
$$
f^{-1}(y) = 2 + \sqrt{y + 3},
\qquad
f^{-1} : [-3, +\infty) \to [2, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On all of $\mathbb{R}$ the function is not injective, because the parabola takes every value greater than the minimum twice. Completing the square shows that the vertex is $(2, -3)$; to the right of the vertex the function is increasing, so its restriction to $[2, +\infty)$ is **injective** and has range $[-3, +\infty)$. To find the inverse, solve $y = (x - 2)^2 - 3$ for $x$. When taking the square root, choose the positive sign, because $x \geq 2$ implies $x - 2 \geq 0$. The inverse is $f^{-1}(y) = 2 + \sqrt{y + 3}$, defined for $y \geq -3$; as a check, $f(3) = -2$ and $f^{-1}(-2) = 2 + 1 = 3$.

</div>
