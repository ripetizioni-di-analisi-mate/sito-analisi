## Exercise 1

A firm has cost $C(q) = q^3 - 12q^2 + 72q + 100$ and the inverse demand for the good is $p = 120 - 3q$, with $q \geq 0$. Find the quantity that maximizes profit, the corresponding price and the maximum profit.

$$
R(q) = (120 - 3q)\,q = 120q - 3q^2,
\qquad
R'(q) = 120 - 6q,
\qquad
C'(q) = 3q^2 - 24q + 72
$$
$$
R'(q) = C'(q)
\iff
3q^2 - 18q - 48 = 0
\iff
q^2 - 6q - 16 = 0
\iff
q = 8 \ \text{ or } \ q = -2
$$
$$
R''(8) = -6 < 24 = C''(8)
$$
$$
\pi'(q) = R'(q) - C'(q) = -3\,(q - 8)(q + 2)
$$
$$
p = 120 - 24 = 96,
\qquad
\pi(8) = R(8) - C(8) = 768 - 420 = 348
$$

Profit is maximum with $8$ units, at the price of $96$ euros, and equals $348$ euros.

::grafico{f="-x^3+9*x^2+48*x-100+0*sqrt(x*(14-x))" punti="8,348,pieno;0,-100,pieno" segmenti="8,0,8,348" xmin=-2 xmax=18 ymin=-100 ymax=400 nomi="q,π" titolo="The profit $\pi(q) = -q^3 + 9q^2 + 48q - 100$ increases up to $q = 8$, where it equals $348$, and then decreases"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Revenue is price times quantity, and the **first-order condition** requires marginal revenue to equal marginal cost. The equation $120 - 6q = 3q^2 - 24q + 72$, with everything brought to the right-hand side and divided by $3$, becomes $q^2 - 6q - 16 = 0$, with solutions $8$ and $-2$; the second is not admissible, because the quantity is not negative.

The **second-order condition** is satisfied, because $R''(q) = -6$ and $C''(q) = 6q - 24$ equals $24$ at $q = 8$; so $q = 8$ is a relative maximum of the profit. The maximum is also absolute on $[0, +\infty)$, because the marginal profit $\pi'(q) = -3\,(q - 8)(q + 2)$ is positive for $0 \leq q < 8$ and negative for $q > 8$, and the theorem on functions with a single stationary point applies. The price is read from the inverse demand, and the profit is the difference between $R(8) = 960 - 192 = 768$ and $C(8) = 512 - 768 + 576 + 100 = 420$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The production cost of a good is $C(q) = q^3 - 10q^2 + 50q + 72$, with $q > 0$. Find the quantity that minimizes average cost and verify that at that point average cost equals marginal cost.

$$
\bar{C}(q) = \frac{C(q)}{q} = q^2 - 10q + 50 + \frac{72}{q}
$$
$$
\bar{C}'(q) = 2q - 10 - \frac{72}{q^2} = \frac{2q^3 - 10q^2 - 72}{q^2} = \frac{2\,(q - 6)(q^2 + q + 6)}{q^2}
$$
$$
\bar{C}'(q) > 0 \iff q > 6
$$
$$
\bar{C}(6) = 36 - 60 + 50 + 12 = 38,
\qquad
C'(6) = 3 \cdot 36 - 20 \cdot 6 + 50 = 38
$$

Average cost is minimum with $6$ units and equals $38$ euros, equal to marginal cost $C'(6)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Average cost is obtained by dividing each term of the cost by $q$. In the derivative, brought to the common denominator $q^2$, the numerator $2q^3 - 10q^2 - 72$ vanishes at $q = 6$, because $432 - 360 - 72 = 0$; dividing the polynomial by $q - 6$ with Ruffini's rule one obtains the factor $q^2 + q + 6$. This factor has discriminant $1 - 24 < 0$, so it is always positive.

The sign of $\bar{C}'$ is therefore that of $q - 6$, negative before $6$ and positive after. By the theorem on **functions with a single stationary point**, $q = 6$ is the absolute minimum point of average cost on $(0, +\infty)$. Marginal cost $C'(q) = 3q^2 - 20q + 50$ equals $38$ at $q = 6$, as predicted by the theorem on **minimum average cost**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The demand for a good is $q(p) = 147 - p^2$, with $0 \leq p \leq \sqrt{147}$. Find the price that maximizes revenue, the quantity sold and the maximum revenue, and compute the elasticity of demand at that price.

$$
R(p) = p\,(147 - p^2) = 147p - p^3
$$
$$
R'(p) = 147 - 3p^2 = 3\,(7 - p)(7 + p),
\qquad
R'(p) = 0 \iff p = 7
$$
$$
R(0) = 0,
\qquad
R(7) = 7 \cdot 98 = 686,
\qquad
R\left( \sqrt{147} \right) = 0
$$
$$
\varepsilon(7) = \frac{p}{q(p)}\,q'(p) = \frac{7}{98} \cdot (-14) = -1
$$

Revenue is maximum at the price of $7$ euros, with $98$ units sold, and equals $686$ euros; at that price the elasticity of demand is $-1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Revenue is price times the quantity demanded, written as a function of price. The interval of admissible prices $\left[ 0, \sqrt{147} \right]$ is closed and bounded, and revenue is a polynomial, so it is continuous; hence the closed-interval method applies. The derivative vanishes in the interval only at $p = 7$, and the comparison with the values at the endpoints gives the **maximum revenue** $686$ euros.

The elasticity of demand is $\dfrac{p}{q}\,q'(p)$ with $q'(p) = -2p$, which at $p = 7$ equals $-14$. The result $\varepsilon = -1$ confirms the theorem on elasticity and revenue, according to which revenue is stationary where demand has **unit elasticity**. For prices less than $7$ demand is inelastic and a price increase makes revenue grow; for higher prices it is elastic and revenue falls.

</div>
