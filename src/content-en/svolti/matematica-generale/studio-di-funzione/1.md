## Exercise 1

Find the relative maximum and minimum points of $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1$.

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12x + 9 = 3\,(x^2 - 4x + 3) = 3\,(x - 1)(x - 3)
$$

| | $x < 1$ | $1 < x < 3$ | $x > 3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x - 1$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x - 3$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | increasing | decreasing | increasing |

$$
f(1) = 1 - 6 + 9 + 1 = 5,
\qquad
f(3) = 27 - 54 + 27 + 1 = 1
$$

The point $x = 1$ is a relative maximum point, with $f(1) = 5$; the point $x = 3$ is a relative minimum point, with $f(3) = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is differentiable on all of $\mathbb{R}$, and by Fermat's theorem the relative extrema must be looked for among the stationary points. The derivative is a polynomial of degree two; taking out $3$ and factoring the trinomial $x^2 - 4x + 3$, which has roots $1$ and $3$, one obtains the product $3\,(x - 1)(x - 3)$.

The sign of a product is obtained from the sign of the factors, as in the table. The derivative is positive before $1$ and negative between $1$ and $3$, so by the **first derivative test** the point $x = 1$ is a relative maximum point. The derivative changes from negative to positive at $3$, hence $x = 3$ is a relative minimum point. The values of the function are computed by substituting into the original polynomial. Neither of the two extrema is absolute, because a polynomial of degree three tends to $+\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$ and to $-\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the relative maximum and minimum points of $f(x) = x^4 - 8x^2 + 3$.

$$
f'(x) = 4x^3 - 16x = 4x\,(x^2 - 4) = 4x\,(x - 2)(x + 2)
$$

| | $x < -2$ | $-2 < x < 0$ | $0 < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $4x$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x^2 - 4$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f'(x)$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
f(-2) = 16 - 32 + 3 = -13,
\qquad
f(0) = 3,
\qquad
f(2) = -13
$$

The points $x = \pm 2$ are relative minimum points, with value $-13$; the point $x = 0$ is a relative maximum point, with value $3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is differentiable everywhere and the only candidates are the stationary points. Taking out $4x$ in the derivative leaves the difference of squares $x^2 - 4$, hence the derivative vanishes at $-2$, $0$ and $2$.

The factor $4x$ is positive for $x > 0$, whereas $x^2 - 4$ is positive outside the interval $[-2, 2]$. In the table the derivative changes sign from $-$ to $+$ at $-2$ and at $2$, which by the **first derivative test** are relative minimum points, and from $+$ to $-$ at $0$, which is a relative maximum point. The function contains only even powers of $x$, so it is even, and indeed the two minima are symmetric and have the same value $-13$. Since $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, the value $-13$ is also the absolute minimum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the relative maximum and minimum points of $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x^2}\,(x - 5)$.

$$
f(x) = x^{\frac{5}{3}} - 5\,x^{\frac{2}{3}},
\qquad
x \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x)
&= \frac{5}{3}\,x^{\frac{2}{3}} - \frac{10}{3}\,x^{-\frac{1}{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{5x - 10}{3\,\sqrt[3]{x}}, \qquad x \neq 0
\end{aligned}
$$

| | $x < 0$ | $0 < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $5x - 10$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $3\sqrt[3]{x}$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
f(0) = 0,
\qquad
f(2) = \sqrt[3]{4}\,(2 - 5) = -3\sqrt[3]{4} \approx -4.76
$$

The point $x = 0$ is a relative maximum point, with $f(0) = 0$; the point $x = 2$ is a relative minimum point, with $f(2) = -3\sqrt[3]{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The cube root is defined for every real number, so the domain is $\mathbb{R}$ and the function is continuous. To differentiate, one writes the function as a sum of powers, multiplying $x^{\frac{2}{3}}$ by $x$ and by $-5$. Bringing the derivative to the common denominator $3\,x^{\frac{1}{3}}$ one obtains a quotient that is *not* defined at $x = 0$; hence at $0$ the function is not differentiable, and $0$ must be added to the candidates together with the stationary point $x = 2$.

The denominator $3\sqrt[3]{x}$ has the sign of $x$, and the numerator is positive for $x > 2$. The derivative is positive to the left of $0$ and negative to the right, so, since $f$ is continuous at $0$, the **first derivative test** applies and $x = 0$ is a **relative maximum point**, even though at $0$ the function is not differentiable. At $x = 2$ the derivative changes from negative to positive, hence $x = 2$ is a relative minimum point. At the point $0$ the graph has a cusp, because $f'(x) \to +\infty$ from the left and $f'(x) \to -\infty$ from the right.

</div>
