## Exercise 1

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5$ on the interval $[-2, 4]$.

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 3\,(x + 1)(x - 3)
$$
$$
f'(x) = 0 \iff x = -1 \ \text{ or } \ x = 3,
\qquad
-1, 3 \in (-2, 4)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-2) &= -8 - 12 + 18 + 5 = 3 \\[4pt]
f(-1) &= -1 - 3 + 9 + 5 = 10 \\[4pt]
f(3) &= 27 - 27 - 27 + 5 = -22 \\[4pt]
f(4) &= 64 - 48 - 36 + 5 = -15
\end{aligned}
$$

The absolute maximum is $10$, attained at $x = -1$; the absolute minimum is $-22$, attained at $x = 3$.

::grafico{f="x^3-3*x^2-9*x+5+0*sqrt((x+2)*(4-x))" punti="-2,3,pieno;-1,10,pieno;3,-22,pieno;4,-15,pieno" segmenti="-1,0,-1,10;3,0,3,-22" xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-30 ymax=20 titolo="On $[-2, 4]$ the function $x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5$ has maximum $10$ at $x = -1$ and minimum $-22$ at $x = 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is continuous on the closed and bounded interval $[-2, 4]$, and by Weierstrass's theorem it has a maximum and a minimum. Since it is also differentiable everywhere, the method for the **absolute maximum and minimum on a closed interval** requires comparing the values at the endpoints $-2$ and $4$ and at the interior stationary points.

The derivative vanishes at $-1$ and at $3$, both interior to the interval. Among the four computed values the largest is $f(-1) = 10$ and the smallest is $f(3) = -22$. There is no need to establish with the sign of $f'$ whether the stationary points are relative maximum or minimum points, because the comparison of the values gives the absolute extrema directly.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x) = |x^2 - 4|$ on the interval $[-3, 1]$.

$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^2 - 4 & \text{if } x \leq -2 \ \text{ or } \ x \geq 2 \\[4pt]
4 - x^2 & \text{if } -2 < x < 2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f'(x) =
\begin{cases}
2x & \text{if } -3 < x < -2 \\[4pt]
-2x & \text{if } -2 < x < 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -2^{-}} f'(x) = -4,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to -2^{+}} f'(x) = 4
$$
$$
f(-3) = 5,
\qquad
f(-2) = 0,
\qquad
f(0) = 4,
\qquad
f(1) = 3
$$

The absolute maximum is $5$, attained at $x = -3$; the absolute minimum is $0$, attained at $x = -2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is continuous, because it is the absolute value of a polynomial, so Weierstrass's theorem applies on the interval $[-3, 1]$. To differentiate it one removes the absolute value, studying the sign of $x^2 - 4$, which is negative *only* between $-2$ and $2$.

On the interval $(-3, -2)$ the derivative is $2x$, which does not vanish; on the interval $(-2, 1)$ the derivative is $-2x$, which vanishes at $x = 0$. At the point $x = -2$ the left and right derivatives equal $-4$ and $4$, so the function **is not differentiable** and $x = -2$ is a corner point, to be added to the candidates. The candidates are therefore the endpoints $-3$ and $1$, the stationary point $0$ and the corner point $-2$.

Comparing the values, the largest is $f(-3) = 5$ and the smallest is $f(-2) = 0$. The minimum could also have been predicted, because an absolute value is never negative and equals $0$ at $x = -2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x) = x - 2\ln x$ on the interval $\left[ 1, e^2 \right]$.

$$
f'(x) = 1 - \frac{2}{x} = \frac{x - 2}{x},
\qquad
f'(x) = 0 \iff x = 2 \in \left( 1, e^2 \right)
$$
$$
f(1) = 1,
\qquad
f(2) = 2 - 2\ln 2 \approx 0.61,
\qquad
f\left( e^2 \right) = e^2 - 4 \approx 3.39
$$

The absolute minimum is $2 - 2\ln 2$, attained at $x = 2$; the absolute maximum is $e^2 - 4$, attained at $x = e^2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined and differentiable for $x > 0$, so it is continuous on the closed interval $\left[ 1, e^2 \right]$ and **Weierstrass's theorem** guarantees the existence of the maximum and the minimum. The derivative vanishes only at $x = 2$, which is interior to the interval because $e^2 \approx 7.39$.

One compares the values at $1$, at $2$ and at $e^2$. To compute $f\left( e^2 \right)$ one uses $\ln e^2 = 2$, so $f\left( e^2 \right) = e^2 - 4$. With $\ln 2 \approx 0.69$ one obtains $f(2) \approx 0.61$, which is the smallest value, whereas the largest is $e^2 - 4 \approx 3.39$. As a check, the derivative is negative for $1 < x < 2$ and positive for $x > 2$, so the function decreases on $[1, 2]$ and increases on $\left[ 2, e^2 \right]$, in agreement with the result.

</div>
