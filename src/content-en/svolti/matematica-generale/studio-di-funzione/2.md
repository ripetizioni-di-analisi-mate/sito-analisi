## Exercise 1

Find the intervals of convexity and concavity and the inflection points of $f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2$, and write the equation of the tangent at the inflection with positive abscissa.

$$
f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x,
\qquad
f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12 = 12\,(x - 1)(x + 1)
$$

| | $x < -1$ | $-1 < x < 1$ | $x > 1$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f''(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | convex | concave | convex |

$$
f(\pm 1) = 1 - 6 = -5,
\qquad
f'(1) = 4 - 12 = -8
$$
$$
y = -5 - 8\,(x - 1) = -8x + 3
$$

The function is convex on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[1, +\infty)$, concave on $[-1, 1]$; the inflections are $(-1, -5)$ and $(1, -5)$, and the tangent at the second one is $y = -8x + 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is twice differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ and concavity is obtained from the sign of the second derivative. The second derivative $12\,(x^2 - 1)$ is a trinomial with roots $\pm 1$ and positive leading coefficient, hence it is positive outside the interval $[-1, 1]$ and negative inside. By the test on the **sign of the second derivative** the function is convex where $f'' > 0$ and concave where $f'' < 0$.

The second derivative changes sign at $-1$ and at $1$, so by the sufficient condition these two points are **inflections**. The inflection tangent is written with the formula of the tangent line, using the value $f(1) = -5$ and the slope $f'(1) = -8$. Since $f'(1) \neq 0$, the inflection is oblique.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the intervals of convexity and concavity and the inflection points of $f(x) = x\,e^{x}$.

$$
f'(x) = e^{x} + x\,e^{x} = (x + 1)\,e^{x}
$$
$$
f''(x) = e^{x} + (x + 1)\,e^{x} = (x + 2)\,e^{x}
$$
$$
f''(x) > 0
\iff
x + 2 > 0
\iff
x > -2
$$
$$
f(-2) = -2\,e^{-2} = -\frac{2}{e^2} \approx -0.27
$$

The function is concave on $(-\infty, -2]$ and convex on $[-2, +\infty)$, with an inflection at the point $\left( -2, -\dfrac{2}{e^2} \right)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined and infinitely differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$, because it is the product of a polynomial and an exponential. The first derivative is computed with the **product rule** and $e^{x}$ is taken out; the second derivative is obtained by differentiating the product $(x + 1)\,e^{x}$ again.

The factor $e^{x}$ is always positive, so the sign of $f''$ is that of $x + 2$. The second derivative is negative for $x < -2$ and positive for $x > -2$, hence the function is concave to the left of $-2$ and convex to the right. The change of sign of $f''$ at $-2$ indicates that $x = -2$ is an **inflection point**, and its value is obtained by substituting into the function.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the intervals of convexity and concavity and the inflection points of $f(x) = \ln\left( 1 + x^2 \right)$, and write the equation of the tangent at the inflection with positive abscissa.

$$
f'(x) = \frac{2x}{1 + x^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f''(x)
&= \frac{2\,(1 + x^2) - 2x \cdot 2x}{(1 + x^2)^2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2\,(1 - x^2)}{(1 + x^2)^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f''(x) > 0
\iff
1 - x^2 > 0
\iff
-1 < x < 1
$$
$$
f(\pm 1) = \ln 2,
\qquad
f'(1) = 1,
\qquad
y = \ln 2 + (x - 1)
$$

The function is convex on $[-1, 1]$ and concave on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[1, +\infty)$; the inflections are $(\pm 1, \ln 2)$, and the tangent at the inflection with abscissa $1$ is $y = x - 1 + \ln 2$.

::grafico{f="log(1+x^2);x-1+log(2)" punti="1,0.693,pieno;-1,0.693,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=4 legenda="y = \ln(1 + x^2);y = x - 1 + \ln 2" titolo="The function $\ln(1 + x^2)$ is convex between the inflections $(\pm 1, \ln 2)$ and concave outside; at the inflection $(1, \ln 2)$ the graph crosses the tangent"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument of the logarithm $1 + x^2$ is always positive, so the domain is $\mathbb{R}$. The first derivative is obtained with the **chain rule**, and the second derivative with the quotient rule; in the numerator the terms $2x^2$ and $-4x^2$ add up to $-2x^2$, and $2$ is taken out.

The denominator $(1 + x^2)^2$ is positive, hence the sign of $f''$ is that of $1 - x^2$, positive for $-1 < x < 1$ and negative outside. The function is therefore convex on the interval $[-1, 1]$ and concave outside, and the points $\pm 1$, where $f''$ changes sign, are **inflections**. The function is even, so the two inflections are symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis. The tangent at the inflection with abscissa $1$ has slope $f'(1) = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1$.

</div>
