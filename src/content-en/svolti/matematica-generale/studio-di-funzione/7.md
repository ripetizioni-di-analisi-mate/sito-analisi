## Exercise 1

Among the rectangles with one side on the $x$-axis and the two upper vertices on the parabola $y = 12 - x^2$, find the one of maximum area.

$$
\text{vertices } (\pm x, 0), \ (\pm x, 12 - x^2),
\qquad
0 \leq x \leq 2\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
A(x) = 2x\,(12 - x^2) = 24x - 2x^3
$$
$$
A'(x) = 24 - 6x^2 = 6\,(2 - x)(2 + x),
\qquad
A'(x) = 0 \iff x = 2
$$
$$
A(0) = 0,
\qquad
A(2) = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 8 = 32,
\qquad
A\left( 2\sqrt{3} \right) = 0
$$

The rectangle of maximum area has base $4$, height $8$ and area $32$.

::grafico{f="12-x^2" area="8;0;-2;2" punti="2,8,pieno;-2,8,pieno" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=14 titolo="The rectangle of maximum area inscribed under the parabola $y = 12 - x^2$ has vertices $(\pm 2, 0)$ and $(\pm 2, 8)$, with area $32$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis, so the rectangle has its vertices at $\pm x$, and the variable $x$ is half of the base. The base measures $2x$ and the height is the ordinate of the vertex on the parabola, $12 - x^2$. The height must be nonnegative, so $x^2 \leq 12$, that is, $0 \leq x \leq 2\sqrt{3}$; the endpoints give degenerate rectangles of zero area.

The area is a continuous function on the closed interval $\left[ 0, 2\sqrt{3} \right]$, hence the **closed-interval method** applies. The derivative vanishes in the interval only at $x = 2$, and the comparison with the values at the endpoints gives the maximum $A(2) = 32$. The base is then $2 \cdot 2 = 4$ and the height is $12 - 4 = 8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A box without a lid, with a square base, must have volume $32\,\mathrm{dm^3}$. Find the dimensions that minimize the surface area of cardboard used.

$$
x^2 h = 32
\implies
h = \frac{32}{x^2},
\qquad
x > 0
$$
$$
S(x) = x^2 + 4xh = x^2 + \frac{128}{x}
$$
$$
S'(x) = 2x - \frac{128}{x^2} = \frac{2\,(x^3 - 64)}{x^2},
\qquad
S'(x) > 0 \iff x^3 > 64 \iff x > 4
$$
$$
h = \frac{32}{16} = 2,
\qquad
S(4) = 16 + 32 = 48
$$

The surface area is minimum with a base of side $4\,\mathrm{dm}$ and height $2\,\mathrm{dm}$, and equals $48\,\mathrm{dm^2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

There are two unknown magnitudes, the side $x$ of the base and the height $h$, linked by the volume constraint, $x^2 h = 32$. Solving the constraint for $h$, the surface area, formed by the base $x^2$ and by the four lateral faces of area $xh$, becomes a function of $x$ alone.

The admissible interval is $(0, +\infty)$, which is not closed, so Weierstrass's theorem does not apply. The sign of $S'$ is that of $x^3 - 64$, negative for $x < 4$ and positive for $x > 4$; by the theorem on **functions with a single stationary point** the point $x = 4$ is an absolute minimum point. The corresponding height is half the side of the base, and the minimum surface area is $48\,\mathrm{dm^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the points of the parabola $y = x^2$ at minimum distance from the point $P = (0, 3)$, and compute the minimum distance.

$$
d^2(x) = (x - 0)^2 + (x^2 - 3)^2 = x^4 - 5x^2 + 9,
\qquad
x \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
\left( d^2 \right)'(x) = 4x^3 - 10x = 2x\,(2x^2 - 5)
$$

| | $x < -\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$ | $-\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}} < x < 0$ | $0 < x < \sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$ | $x > \sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\left( d^2 \right)'$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
d^2\left( \pm\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}} \right) = \frac{25}{4} - \frac{25}{2} + 9 = \frac{11}{4},
\qquad
d^2(0) = 9
$$

The points closest to $P$ are $\left( \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}, \dfrac{5}{2} \right)$, and the minimum distance is $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}{2} \approx 1.66$.

::grafico{f="x^2" curve="1.658*cos(t),3+1.658*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,3,pieno,P;1.581,2.5,pieno;-1.581,2.5,pieno" segmenti="0,3,1.581,2.5" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=6 uguale=1 titolo="The circle with center $P = (0, 3)$ and radius $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}{2}$ touches the parabola $y = x^2$ at the two points of minimum distance"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The generic point of the parabola is $(x, x^2)$, and its distance from $P$ is the root of $x^2 + (x^2 - 3)^2$. The square root is increasing, so the distance is minimum exactly where its square $d^2(x)$ is minimum, which is a polynomial and is easier to differentiate.

The derivative of $d^2$ vanishes at $0$ and at $\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$. From the sign table, $d^2$ decreases up to $-\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$, increases up to $0$, decreases up to $\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$ and then increases; the relative minima are therefore at the two points $\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$, where $d^2$ equals $\dfrac{11}{4}$, whereas $x = 0$ is a relative maximum. Since $d^2 \to +\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, the value $\dfrac{11}{4}$ is the **absolute minimum**.

One has $\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}$, and the ordinate of the two points is $x^2 = \dfrac{5}{2}$. The minimum distance is the root of $\dfrac{11}{4}$, that is, $\dfrac{\sqrt{11}}{2}$. The vertex $(0, 0)$ of the parabola, which would seem to be the closest point, has instead distance $3$ from $P$.

</div>
