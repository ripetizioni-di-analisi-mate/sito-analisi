## Exercise 1

Compute the derivative of $f(x) = (3x^2 + 1)^5$ and of $g(x) = \ln(x^2 + 4)$.

$$
f'(x) = 5\,(3x^2 + 1)^4 \cdot 6x = 30x\,(3x^2 + 1)^4
$$
$$
g'(x) = \frac{1}{x^2 + 4} \cdot 2x = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both are **composite functions**: in $f$ the inner function is $3x^2 + 1$ and the outer one is the fifth power; in $g$ the inner function is $x^2 + 4$ and the outer one is the logarithm. By the **composite function rule** one differentiates the outer function leaving the inner one unchanged, and multiplies by the derivative of the inner function. For $f$ the power gives $5\,(3x^2 + 1)^4$ and the inner function has derivative $6x$. For $g$ the logarithm gives $\dfrac{1}{x^2 + 4}$ and the inner function has derivative $2x$. Expanding $(3x^2 + 1)^5$ before differentiating would give the same result with much more computation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the derivative of $f(x) = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$, together with its domain of differentiability.

$$
f(x) = (1 - x^2)^{\frac{1}{2}},
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{1}{2}\,(1 - x^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \cdot (-2x) = -\frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}
$$
$$
-1 < x < 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root is written as a power with exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the outer function is a power and the inner function is $1 - x^2$. By the **composite function rule** the derivative of the power, $\dfrac{1}{2}\,(1 - x^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, is multiplied by $-2x$, and canceling the $2$ gives $-\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}$. The function is defined for $-1 \leq x \leq 1$, but the derivative has the root in the denominator, so it requires $1 - x^2 > 0$; hence at the endpoints $\pm 1$ the function is not differentiable, and the graph, a semicircle, has a vertical tangent there.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the derivative of $f(x) = 2^{3x}$ and of $g(x) = x^2 e^{-x}$.

$$
f'(x) = 2^{3x} \ln 2 \cdot 3 = 3 \ln 2 \cdot 2^{3x}
$$
$$
g'(x) = 2x\,e^{-x} + x^2 \cdot \left( -e^{-x} \right) = x\,(2 - x)\,e^{-x}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In $f$ the outer function is the exponential with base $2$, which in its variable $u$ has derivative $2^{u} \ln 2$, and the inner function is $3x$, with derivative $3$. In $g$ the **product rule** and the **composite function rule** appear together; in particular the factor $e^{-x}$ is differentiated as a composite function and gives $e^{-x} \cdot (-1)$. Factoring out $x\,e^{-x}$ gives the form $x\,(2 - x)\,e^{-x}$, from which one reads that the derivative vanishes at $0$ and at $2$.

</div>
