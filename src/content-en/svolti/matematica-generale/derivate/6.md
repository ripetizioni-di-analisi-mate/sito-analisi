## Exercise 1

Verify that $f(x) = x^2 - 4x$ satisfies the hypotheses of Lagrange's theorem on the interval $[1, 4]$ and find the point or points $c$ guaranteed by the theorem.

$$
\frac{f(4) - f(1)}{4 - 1} = \frac{0 - (-3)}{3} = 1
$$
$$
f'(c) = 2c - 4 = 1
\iff
c = \frac{5}{2} \in (1, 4)
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-4*x;x-6.25" segmenti="1,-3,4,0" punti="1,-3,pieno;4,0,pieno;2.5,-3.75,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=1 legenda="y = x^2 - 4x;y = x - \dfrac{25}{4}" titolo="The dashed secant through $(1, -3)$ and $(4, 0)$ and the tangent at $c = \dfrac{5}{2}$ have the same slope $1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is continuous on $[1, 4]$ and differentiable on $(1, 4)$, and the hypotheses of **Lagrange's theorem** are satisfied. The average slope over the interval, that is, the slope of the secant through the points $(1, -3)$ and $(4, 0)$, equals $1$. The theorem guarantees an interior point where the derivative equals this slope, and solving $2c - 4 = 1$ gives $c = \dfrac{5}{2}$, which belongs to the interval. At that point the tangent to the graph is parallel to the secant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ satisfies the hypotheses of Rolle's theorem on the interval $\left[ -\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3} \right]$ and find the points where the derivative vanishes.

$$
f\left( -\sqrt{3} \right) = -3\sqrt{3} + 3\sqrt{3} = 0,
\qquad
f\left( \sqrt{3} \right) = 3\sqrt{3} - 3\sqrt{3} = 0
$$
$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 0
\iff
x = -1 \ \text{ or } \ x = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, continuous and differentiable everywhere, and it takes the same value $0$ at the endpoints of the interval, since $\left( \sqrt{3} \right)^3 = 3\sqrt{3}$. The hypotheses of **Rolle's theorem** are therefore satisfied, and the theorem guarantees *at least* one interior point with zero derivative. Solving $f'(x) = 0$ one finds two, $-1$ and $1$, both interior to the interval, because $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.73$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the intervals on which $f(x) = x^3 - 12x + 1$ is increasing and those on which it is decreasing.

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 12 = 3\,(x - 2)(x + 2)
$$
$$
f'(x) > 0
\iff
x < -2 \ \text{ or } \ x > 2,
\qquad
f'(x) < 0
\iff
-2 < x < 2
$$

::grafico{f="x^3-12*x+1" punti="-2,17,pieno;2,-15,pieno" segmenti="-2,0,-2,17;2,0,2,-15" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-20 ymax=20 titolo="The function $x^3 - 12x + 1$ increases up to $x = -2$, decreases on $[-2, 2]$ and increases from $x = 2$ onward"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the criterion on the **sign of the derivative and monotonicity**, on every interval where the derivative is positive the function increases and on every interval where it is negative the function decreases. The derivative is a quadratic trinomial with roots $-2$ and $2$ and positive leading coefficient, so it is positive outside the interval $[-2, 2]$ and negative inside. Hence $f$ is **increasing** on $(-\infty, -2]$ and on $[2, +\infty)$, and **decreasing** on $[-2, 2]$. At the stationary points $-2$ and $2$ the monotonicity changes, and there the function has, respectively, a local maximum, $f(-2) = 17$, and a local minimum, $f(2) = -15$.

</div>
