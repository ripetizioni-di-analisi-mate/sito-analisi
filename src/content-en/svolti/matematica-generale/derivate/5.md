## Exercise 1

The demand for a good is $q(p) = 300 - 5p$. Compute the elasticity of demand at the price $p = 20$, estimate the effect of a $2\%$ price increase on the quantity, and determine whether the increase raises revenue.

$$
\varepsilon(p) = \frac{p}{q(p)}\,q'(p) = \frac{p}{300 - 5p} \cdot (-5) = -\frac{5p}{300 - 5p}
$$
$$
\varepsilon(20) = -\frac{100}{200} = -0.5
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta q}{q} \approx \varepsilon \cdot \frac{\Delta p}{p} = -0.5 \cdot 2\% = -1\%
$$
$$
R'(20) = q(20)\,\big( 1 + \varepsilon(20) \big) = 200 \cdot 0.5 = 100 > 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **elasticity** is the derivative of demand multiplied by the ratio of price to quantity. At the price $20$, $200$ units are sold, and the elasticity equals $-0.5$, so demand is **inelastic**, because $|\varepsilon| < 1$. A $2\%$ price increase lowers the quantity by about $1\%$, so the price rises in percentage terms by more than the quantity falls, and revenue increases. The formula linking **elasticity and revenue** confirms this, because the derivative of revenue with respect to price is positive. As a check, $R(20) = 4000$ and $R(20.4) = 20.4 \cdot 198 = 4039.2$, an increase of about $1\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Show that the demand $q(p) = 1000\,p^{-2}$, for $p > 0$, has constant elasticity, and state what happens to revenue if the price increases.

$$
q'(p) = -2000\,p^{-3}
$$
$$
\varepsilon(p) = \frac{p}{1000\,p^{-2}} \cdot \left( -2000\,p^{-3} \right) = -2
$$
$$
R(p) = p \cdot 1000\,p^{-2} = \frac{1000}{p}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivative of a power is computed with the formula $\left( p^{a} \right)' = a\,p^{a-1}$. In the computation of the **elasticity** the powers of $p$ cancel, and what remains is the number $-2$, the same for every price. In general a demand of the form $k\,p^{a}$ has constant elasticity equal to the exponent $a$. Here $|\varepsilon| = 2 > 1$, so demand is **elastic** at every price, and a price increase always lowers revenue; indeed $R(p) = \dfrac{1000}{p}$ is decreasing. A $1\%$ price increase reduces the quantity by about $2\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The demand for a good is $q(p) = 50\,e^{-0.04\,p}$. Find the elasticity, the price at which it is unitary and the price that maximizes revenue.

$$
q'(p) = 50\,e^{-0.04\,p} \cdot (-0.04) = -0.04\,q(p)
$$
$$
\varepsilon(p) = \frac{p}{q(p)} \cdot \big( -0.04\,q(p) \big) = -0.04\,p
$$
$$
\varepsilon(p) = -1
\iff
p = 25
$$
$$
R'(p) = q(p)\,(1 - 0.04\,p) > 0 \iff p < 25
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivative of demand is computed with the composite function rule and is $-0.04$ times the demand itself, so in the **elasticity** the factor $q(p)$ cancels and what remains is $-0.04\,p$. The elasticity grows in absolute value with the price, hence demand is inelastic for $p < 25$, unitary for $p = 25$ and elastic for $p > 25$. By the relation between **elasticity and revenue**, revenue increases as long as demand is inelastic and decreases when it becomes elastic. The price that maximizes revenue is therefore $p = 25$ euros, at which $50\,e^{-1} \approx 18.4$ units are sold.

</div>
