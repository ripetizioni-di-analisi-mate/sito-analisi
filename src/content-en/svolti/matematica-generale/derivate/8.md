## Exercise 1

Approximate $\sqrt[3]{8.3}$ with the linear approximation of $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ at $x_0 = 8$.

$$
f(x) = x^{\frac{1}{3}},
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{1}{3}\,x^{-\frac{2}{3}},
\qquad
f'(8) = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12}
$$
$$
\sqrt[3]{8.3} \approx f(8) + f'(8) \cdot 0.3 = 2 + \frac{0.3}{12} = 2.025
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $x_0 = 8$ is close to $8.3$ and is a perfect cube, so $f(8) = 2$ and the derivative at $8$ can be computed without a calculator, because $8^{-\frac{2}{3}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. By the **linear approximation** the required value is approximately the value at $8$ plus the derivative times the increment $0.3$. This gives $2.025$, against the value $2.0247\ldots$, with an error of about three ten-thousandths.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The revenue of a firm is $R(q) = 100q - 0.5\,q^2$. Current output is $q = 80$. Estimate with the differential the revenue for $q = 82$ and compare the estimate with the exact value.

$$
R(80) = 8000 - 3200 = 4800,
\qquad
R'(q) = 100 - q,
\qquad
R'(80) = 20
$$
$$
R(82) \approx R(80) + R'(80) \cdot 2 = 4800 + 40 = 4840
$$
$$
R(82) = 8200 - 3362 = 4838
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **differential** estimates the change in revenue by the marginal revenue multiplied by the change in quantity; two more units, at about $20$ euros each, bring in about $40$ euros. The estimate is $4840$ euros, and the exact value is $4838$ euros. The error, $2$ euros, is the term $0.5 \cdot 2^2$ that the linear approximation neglects; it is small because the increment is small compared with the quantity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Approximate $\ln(1.05)$ with the linear approximation of $f(x) = \ln x$ at $x_0 = 1$, and determine whether the estimate is an overestimate or an underestimate.

$$
f(1) = 0,
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{1}{x},
\qquad
f'(1) = 1
$$
$$
\ln(1.05) \approx f(1) + f'(1) \cdot 0.05 = 0.05
$$
$$
g(x) = (x - 1) - \ln x,
\qquad
g'(x) = 1 - \frac{1}{x} = \frac{x - 1}{x},
\qquad
x > 0
$$
$$
\ln(1.05) = 0.04879\ldots < 0.05
$$

::grafico{f="log(x);x-1" punti="1,0,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 legenda="y = \ln x;y = x - 1" titolo="The tangent $y = x - 1$ to the graph of $\ln x$ at the point $(1, 0)$ lies above the graph, so the linear approximation gives overestimates"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $x_0 = 1$ is close to $1.05$, and there the logarithm and its derivative equal $0$ and $1$. By the **linear approximation** the required value is approximately $0 + 1 \cdot 0.05 = 0.05$. To determine the direction of the error one studies the difference $g(x)$ between the tangent $y = x - 1$ and the logarithm. The derivative $g'(x)$ is negative for $0 < x < 1$ and positive for $x > 1$, so by the criterion on the **sign of the derivative and monotonicity** the function $g$ decreases on $(0, 1]$ and increases on $[1, +\infty)$. Hence its smallest value is $g(1) = 0$, and $g(x) \geq 0$ for every $x > 0$. The tangent therefore lies above the graph, and the estimate $0.05$ is **an overestimate**; indeed the true value is $0.04879\ldots$, with an error of about $0.0012$.

</div>
