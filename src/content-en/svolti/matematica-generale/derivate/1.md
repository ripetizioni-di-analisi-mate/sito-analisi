## Exercise 1

Compute from the definition the derivative of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$ at the point $x_0 = 2$ and write the tangent line to the graph at that point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{f(2 + h) - f(2)}{h}
&= \frac{1}{h} \left( \frac{1}{2 + h} - \frac{1}{2} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{h} \cdot \frac{2 - (2 + h)}{2\,(2 + h)} \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{1}{2\,(2 + h)}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f'(2) = \lim_{h \to 0} \left( -\frac{1}{2\,(2 + h)} \right) = -\frac{1}{4}
$$
$$
y = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}\,(x - 2) = -\frac{x}{4} + 1
$$

::grafico{f="1/x+0*sqrt(x);1-x/4" punti="2,0.5,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=3 legenda="y = \dfrac{1}{x};y = -\dfrac{x}{4} + 1" titolo="The tangent to the graph of $\dfrac{1}{x}$ at the point $\left( 2, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ has slope $-\dfrac{1}{4}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **difference quotient** compares the value of $f$ at $2 + h$ with the value at $2$, divided by $h$. Bringing the difference of the two fractions to a common denominator, the numerator that remains is $-h$, which cancels with the $\dfrac{1}{h}$ in front. Before the simplification the limit was of the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$; after it, the limit is computed by substituting $h = 0$ and gives $-\dfrac{1}{4}$. The **tangent line** passes through the point $\left( 2, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ with slope $-\dfrac{1}{4}$, and is $y = -\dfrac{x}{4} + 1$. The slope is negative, consistent with the fact that $\dfrac{1}{x}$ decreases for $x > 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute from the definition the derivative of $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ at the point $x_0 = 9$ and write the tangent line. Use it to approximate $\sqrt{9.6}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{9 + h} - 3}{h}
&= \frac{(\sqrt{9 + h} - 3)(\sqrt{9 + h} + 3)}{h\,(\sqrt{9 + h} + 3)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{9 + h - 9}{h\,(\sqrt{9 + h} + 3)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{9 + h} + 3}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f'(9) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{1}{\sqrt{9 + h} + 3} = \frac{1}{6}
$$
$$
y = 3 + \frac{1}{6}\,(x - 9),
\qquad
\sqrt{9.6} \approx 3 + \frac{0.6}{6} = 3.1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference quotient contains a difference between a root and a number, and as $h \to 0$ it is of the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. It is rationalized by multiplying numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{9 + h} + 3$; in this way the numerator becomes $h$ and cancels. The limit equals $\dfrac{1}{6}$, in agreement with the formula $\left( \sqrt{x} \right)' = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$. The **tangent line** at $(9, 3)$ has slope $\dfrac{1}{6}$. Near $9$ the graph and the tangent almost overlap, so the value of the tangent at $9.6$, that is, $3.1$, approximates $\sqrt{9.6} = 3.098\ldots$

</div>
