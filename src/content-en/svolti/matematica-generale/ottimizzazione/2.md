## Exercise 1

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2x$ on the rectangle $R = [0, 3] \times [-1, 1]$.

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x - 2 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
(x, y) = (1, 0),
\qquad
f(1, 0) = -1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y = \pm 1,\ x \in [0, 3]: &\quad f = x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2 \in [0, 4] \\[6pt]
x = 0,\ y \in [-1, 1]: &\quad f = y^2 \in [0, 1] \\[6pt]
x = 3,\ y \in [-1, 1]: &\quad f = 3 + y^2 \in [3, 4]
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\max_R f = f(3, \pm 1) = 4,
\qquad
\min_R f = f(1, 0) = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rectangle is closed and bounded and $f$ is a polynomial, hence continuous; by the **Weierstrass theorem** the absolute maximum and minimum exist. First we look for the interior stationary points. The system gives the single point $(1, 0)$, which is interior to $R$ because $0 < 1 < 3$ and $-1 < 0 < 1$.

The boundary consists of four sides. On the horizontal sides $y = \pm 1$ the function becomes $(x - 1)^2$ with $x \in [0, 3]$, which goes from $0$, at $x = 1$, to $4$, at $x = 3$. On the side $x = 0$ we have $y^2$, between $0$ and $1$; on the side $x = 3$ we have $3 + y^2$, between $3$ and $4$. Comparing all the values found, the largest is $4$, at the vertices $(3, 1)$ and $(3, -1)$, and the smallest is $-1$, at the interior point $(1, 0)$. Hence the **absolute maximum** is $4$ and the **absolute minimum** is $-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 - xy + y^2 - x$ on the triangle $T$ with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$.

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x - y - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = -x + 2y = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x = 2y \\[4pt]
3y - 1 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
(x, y) = \left( \frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3} \right),
\qquad
f\left( \frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3} \right) = -\frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y = 0,\ x \in [0, 2]: &\quad \varphi_1(x) = x^2 - x \\[6pt]
x = 0,\ y \in [0, 2]: &\quad \varphi_2(y) = y^2 \\[6pt]
y = 2 - x,\ x \in [0, 2]: &\quad \varphi_3(x) = 3x^2 - 7x + 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\varphi_1'(x) = 2x - 1 = 0 \iff x = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
f\left( \frac{1}{2}, 0 \right) = -\frac{1}{4}
$$
$$
\varphi_3'(x) = 6x - 7 = 0 \iff x = \frac{7}{6},
\qquad
f\left( \frac{7}{6}, \frac{5}{6} \right) = -\frac{1}{12}
$$
$$
f(0, 0) = 0, \qquad f(2, 0) = 2, \qquad f(0, 2) = 4
$$
$$
\max_T f = f(0, 2) = 4,
\qquad
\min_T f = f\left( \frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3} \right) = -\frac{1}{3}
$$

::grafico{poligoni="0,0 2,0 0,2" punti="0.667,0.333,pieno,minimum;0,2,pieno,maximum;0.5,0,vuoto;1.167,0.833,vuoto;2,0,vuoto" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The triangle $T$ with the interior stationary point $\left( \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$, the candidates on the sides and the vertex $(0, 2)$, where $f$ is maximum"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangle is the set $x \geq 0$, $y \geq 0$, $x + y \leq 2$, closed and bounded, and $f$ is continuous, so the absolute extrema exist by the **Weierstrass theorem**. The first-order system gives the point $\left( \dfrac{2}{3}, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$, which is interior because the coordinates are positive and their sum is $1 < 2$.

On each side the equation of the side is substituted into $f$. On the side $y = 0$ one obtains the parabola $x^2 - x$, with vertex at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. On the side $x = 0$ one obtains $y^2$, increasing on $[0, 2]$, which gives no new points besides the vertices. On the hypotenuse one substitutes $y = 2 - x$:
$$
x^2 - x\,(2 - x) + (2 - x)^2 - x = x^2 - 2x + x^2 + 4 - 4x + x^2 - x = 3x^2 - 7x + 4,
$$
with vertex at $x = \dfrac{7}{6}$, that is, at the point $\left( \dfrac{7}{6}, \dfrac{5}{6} \right)$. The three vertices are added to the candidates. The smallest value is $-\dfrac{1}{3}$, at the interior point, and the largest is $4$, at the vertex $(0, 2)$; therefore the **absolute minimum** is $-\dfrac{1}{3}$ and the **absolute maximum** is $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 + 2y^2 - x$ on the disk $D = \{(x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1\}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 4y = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
(x, y) = \left( \frac{1}{2}, 0 \right),
\qquad
f\left( \frac{1}{2}, 0 \right) = -\frac{1}{4}
$$
$$
x^2 + y^2 = 1 \implies y^2 = 1 - x^2,
\qquad
\varphi(x) = x^2 + 2\,(1 - x^2) - x = 2 - x^2 - x,
\qquad
x \in [-1, 1]
$$
$$
\varphi'(x) = -2x - 1 = 0 \iff x = -\frac{1}{2},
\qquad
\varphi\left( -\frac{1}{2} \right) = \frac{9}{4},
\qquad
\varphi(1) = 0,
\qquad
\varphi(-1) = 2
$$
$$
\max_D f = f\left( -\frac{1}{2}, \pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = \frac{9}{4},
\qquad
\min_D f = f\left( \frac{1}{2}, 0 \right) = -\frac{1}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The closed disk is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so the absolute maximum and minimum exist. The only stationary point is $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0 \right)$, interior to the disk because $\dfrac{1}{4} < 1$.

The boundary is the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. Only $y^2$ appears in the function, therefore it is convenient to substitute $y^2 = 1 - x^2$, and on the circle $f$ becomes the function of one variable $\varphi(x) = 2 - x^2 - x$, with $x \in [-1, 1]$. Its derivative vanishes at $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, which corresponds to the two points $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)$ of the circle; the endpoints $x = \pm 1$ correspond to the points $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$. Comparing the values $-\dfrac{1}{4}$, $\dfrac{9}{4}$, $0$ and $2$ gives the **absolute maximum** $\dfrac{9}{4}$ and the **absolute minimum** $-\dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>
