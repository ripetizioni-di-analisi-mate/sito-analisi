## Exercise 1

A consumer has utility $U(x, y) = x^2 y$ and an income of $60$ euros; the prices of the two goods are $p_1 = 4$ and $p_2 = 2$ euros. Find the optimal bundle, the maximum utility and the Lagrange multiplier.

$$
\mathcal{L}(x, y, \lambda) = x^2 y - \lambda\,(4x + 2y - 60), \qquad x, y > 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
2xy = 4\lambda \\[4pt]
x^2 = 2\lambda \\[4pt]
4x + 2y = 60
\end{cases}
\implies
\frac{xy}{2} = \frac{x^2}{2}
\implies
y = x
$$
$$
4x + 2x = 60 \iff x = 10,
\qquad
y = 10,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{x^2}{2} = 50,
\qquad
U(10, 10) = 1000
$$

::grafico{f="30-2*x+0*sqrt(x*(15-x));1000/x^2+0*sqrt(x)" punti="10,10,pieno" segmenti="10,0,10,10;0,10,10,10" xmin=-2 xmax=18 ymin=-4 ymax=36 legenda="4x + 2y = 60;x^2 y = 1000" titolo="The indifference curve $x^2 y = 1000$ is tangent to the budget line $4x + 2y = 60$ at the optimal bundle $(10, 10)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem is to maximize $U$ on the **budget constraint** $4x + 2y = 60$, with positive quantities. From the first two equations of the Lagrange system one obtains $\lambda$ in two ways, $\lambda = \dfrac{xy}{2}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$; setting them equal and dividing by $\dfrac{x}{2} > 0$ gives $y = x$. This is the condition $\dfrac{U_x}{U_y} = \dfrac{2xy}{x^2} = \dfrac{2y}{x} = \dfrac{p_1}{p_2} = 2$ of the **consumer's problem**. Substituting into the constraint one finds the bundle $(10, 10)$.

On the constraint $y = 30 - 2x$, with $0 < x < 15$, the utility equals $x^2\,(30 - 2x)$, which is positive in the interior and zero at the endpoints; its derivative $60x - 6x^2$ vanishes only at $x = 10$, so the bundle found is the **maximum**. The maximum utility is $1000$ and the multiplier $\lambda = 50$ is the marginal utility of income, so with one additional euro of income the maximum utility increases by about $50$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A consumer has utility $U(x, y) = \ln x + 2 \ln y$ and an income of $30$ euros, with prices $p_1 = 1$ and $p_2 = 4$ euros. Find the optimal bundle and the maximum utility.

$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{x} = \lambda \\[8pt]
\dfrac{2}{y} = 4\lambda \\[8pt]
x + 4y = 30
\end{cases}
\implies
\frac{2}{y} = \frac{4}{x}
\implies
x = 2y
$$
$$
2y + 4y = 30 \iff y = 5,
\qquad
x = 10,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{1}{10}
$$
$$
U(10, 5) = \ln 10 + 2 \ln 5 = \ln (10 \cdot 25) = \ln 250
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation gives $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{x}$; substituting into the second gives $\dfrac{2}{y} = \dfrac{4}{x}$, that is, $x = 2y$. The constraint becomes $6y = 30$, so the candidate bundle is $(10, 5)$.

On the constraint $x = 30 - 4y$, with $0 < y < 7.5$, the utility equals $\ln(30 - 4y) + 2 \ln y$, which tends to $-\infty$ as $y \to 0^+$ and as $y \to 7.5^-$; the stationary point found is the only one, so it is the **maximum**. By the properties of logarithms the maximum utility is $\ln 250$. The consumer spends $10$ euros on the first good and $20$ euros on the second, that is, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of income, in the same proportions as the coefficients $1$ and $2$ of the logarithms.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A firm has production function $Q = L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{\frac{2}{3}}$ and pays $w = 4$ euros for labor and $r = 1$ euro for capital per unit. Find the combination of factors that produces $Q = 20$ units at minimum cost and the minimum cost.

$$
\min\, C = 4L + K
\qquad \text{subject to} \qquad
L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{\frac{2}{3}} = 20, \quad L, K > 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
4 = \dfrac{\lambda}{3}\,L^{-\frac{2}{3}} K^{\frac{2}{3}} \\[8pt]
1 = \dfrac{2\lambda}{3}\,L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{-\frac{1}{3}} \\[8pt]
L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{\frac{2}{3}} = 20
\end{cases}
\implies
4 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{K}{L}
\implies
K = 8L
$$
$$
L^{\frac{1}{3}}\,(8L)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 4L = 20 \iff L = 5,
\qquad
K = 40
$$
$$
C = 4 \cdot 5 + 40 = 60
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem is to minimize the cost on the **isoquant** $Q = 20$. Dividing the first equation by the second, $\lambda$ cancels and the powers give
$$
\frac{L^{-\frac{2}{3}} K^{\frac{2}{3}}}{2\,L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{-\frac{1}{3}}} = \frac{K}{2L} ,
$$
so $4 = \dfrac{K}{2L}$, that is, $K = 8L$. This is the condition of **minimum cost**, by which the ratio of the marginal products, $\dfrac{Q_L}{Q_K} = \dfrac{K}{2L}$, equals the ratio of the factor prices, $\dfrac{w}{r} = 4$. Substituting into the constraint, $(8L)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 4\,L^{\frac{2}{3}}$, therefore $Q = 4L = 20$ and $L = 5$, $K = 40$.

On the isoquant the capital is $K = 20^{\frac{3}{2}}\,L^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, so the cost $4L + 20^{\frac{3}{2}}\,L^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ tends to $+\infty$ as $L \to 0^+$ and as $L \to +\infty$, and the only stationary point is the **minimum**. The minimum cost is $60$ euros.

</div>
