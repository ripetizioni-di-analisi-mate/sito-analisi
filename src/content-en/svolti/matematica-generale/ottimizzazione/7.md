## Exercise 1

A firm in perfect competition produces two goods in quantities $q_1$ and $q_2$, sold at prices of $22$ and $25$ euros, with cost
$$
C(q_1, q_2) = q_1^2 + q_1 q_2 + 2q_2^2 + 50 .
$$
Find the quantities that maximize profit and the maximum profit.

$$
\pi(q_1, q_2) = 22\,q_1 + 25\,q_2 - q_1^2 - q_1 q_2 - 2q_2^2 - 50
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\pi_{q_1} = 22 - 2q_1 - q_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
\pi_{q_2} = 25 - q_1 - 4q_2 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
q_1 = 25 - 4q_2 \\[4pt]
22 - 50 + 8q_2 - q_2 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
q_1 = 9 \\[4pt]
q_2 = 4
\end{cases}
$$
$$
H = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & -4 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H = 8 - 1 = 7 > 0,
\qquad
\pi_{q_1 q_1} = -2 < 0
$$
$$
\pi(9, 4) = 198 + 100 - 81 - 36 - 32 - 50 = 99
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **profit** is the revenue $22\,q_1 + 25\,q_2$ minus the cost. The first-order conditions say that the price of each good equals its marginal cost, that is, $22 = C_{q_1} = 2q_1 + q_2$ and $25 = C_{q_2} = q_1 + 4q_2$. The system is linear; obtaining $q_1$ from the second equation and substituting into the first gives $7q_2 = 28$, so $q_2 = 4$ and $q_1 = 9$.

The Hessian is constant, with positive determinant and $\pi_{q_1 q_1} < 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the point $(9, 4)$ is a relative maximum point. Since $\pi$ is a second-degree polynomial with a single stationary point and the Hessian is the same at every point, the maximum is also absolute. The **maximum profit** is $99$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A monopolist sells two goods with inverse demands $p_1 = 56 - 2q_1$ and $p_2 = 36 - q_2$, and has cost
$$
C(q_1, q_2) = 100 + 4q_1 + 2q_2 + q_1 q_2 .
$$
Find the quantities and prices that maximize profit and the maximum profit.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi(q_1, q_2)
&= (56 - 2q_1)\,q_1 + (36 - q_2)\,q_2 - (100 + 4q_1 + 2q_2 + q_1 q_2) \\[6pt]
&= -2q_1^2 - q_2^2 - q_1 q_2 + 52\,q_1 + 34\,q_2 - 100
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\pi_{q_1} = -4q_1 - q_2 + 52 = 0 \\[4pt]
\pi_{q_2} = -2q_2 - q_1 + 34 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
q_1 = 34 - 2q_2 \\[4pt]
-136 + 8q_2 - q_2 + 52 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
q_1 = 10 \\[4pt]
q_2 = 12
\end{cases}
$$
$$
H = \begin{pmatrix} -4 & -1 \\ -1 & -2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H = 7 > 0,
\qquad
\pi_{q_1 q_1} = -4 < 0
$$
$$
p_1 = 56 - 20 = 36,
\qquad
p_2 = 36 - 12 = 24,
\qquad
\pi(10, 12) = 360 + 288 - 284 = 364
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a monopolist the price depends on the quantity sold, so the revenue from each good is the inverse demand multiplied by the quantity. Subtracting the cost gives a second-degree polynomial in $q_1$ and $q_2$. From the second first-order condition one obtains $q_1 = 34 - 2q_2$; substituting into the first gives $7q_2 - 84 = 0$, so $q_2 = 12$ and $q_1 = 10$.

The Hessian has positive determinant and $\pi_{q_1 q_1} < 0$, therefore the point is a **maximum** point. The prices are read from the inverse demands, $36$ and $24$ euros. The revenue is $36 \cdot 10 + 24 \cdot 12 = 648$ euros and the cost is $100 + 40 + 24 + 120 = 284$ euros, so the **maximum profit** is $364$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A firm produces the quantity $Q = 2\,L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{\frac{1}{3}}$ with labor $L$ and capital $K$, sells the product at $3$ euros per unit and pays $1$ euro for each unit of labor and of capital. Find the quantities of the factors that maximize profit.

$$
\pi(L, K) = 6\,L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{\frac{1}{3}} - L - K, \qquad L, K > 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\pi_L = 2\,L^{-\frac{2}{3}} K^{\frac{1}{3}} - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
\pi_K = 2\,L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{-\frac{2}{3}} - 1 = 0
\end{cases}
\implies
\frac{L^{-\frac{2}{3}} K^{\frac{1}{3}}}{L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{-\frac{2}{3}}} = \frac{K}{L} = 1
$$
$$
K = L: \quad 2\,L^{-\frac{1}{3}} = 1 \iff L^{\frac{1}{3}} = 2 \iff L = K = 8
$$
$$
\pi_{LL} = -\frac{4}{3}\,L^{-\frac{5}{3}} K^{\frac{1}{3}},
\qquad
\pi_{LK} = \frac{2}{3}\,L^{-\frac{2}{3}} K^{-\frac{2}{3}},
\qquad
\pi_{KK} = -\frac{4}{3}\,L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{-\frac{5}{3}}
$$
$$
H(8, 8) = \begin{pmatrix} -\dfrac{1}{12} & \dfrac{1}{24} \\[8pt] \dfrac{1}{24} & -\dfrac{1}{12} \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(8, 8) = \frac{1}{144} - \frac{1}{576} = \frac{1}{192} > 0
$$
$$
Q = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 8,
\qquad
\pi(8, 8) = 24 - 16 = 8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The profit is the revenue $3Q$ minus the cost of the factors $L + K$. In the first-order conditions the value of the marginal product of each factor equals its price. Dividing side by side the two equalities $2\,L^{-\frac{2}{3}} K^{\frac{1}{3}} = 1$ and $2\,L^{\frac{1}{3}} K^{-\frac{2}{3}} = 1$ gives $\dfrac{K}{L} = 1$, so $K = L$, and the first equation becomes $2\,L^{-\frac{1}{3}} = 1$, that is, $L = 8$.

At $(8, 8)$ we have $L^{-\frac{5}{3}} = \dfrac{1}{32}$, $L^{\frac{1}{3}} = 2$ and $L^{-\frac{2}{3}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, from which the entries of the Hessian follow. The determinant is positive and $\pi_{LL} < 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the point is a **relative maximum** point. The firm employs $8$ units of labor and $8$ of capital, produces $8$ units and earns a profit of $8$ euros. The sum of the exponents is $\dfrac{2}{3} < 1$, that is, the returns to scale are decreasing, and this makes a finite maximum profit possible.

</div>
