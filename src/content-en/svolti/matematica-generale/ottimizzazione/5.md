## Exercise 1

Classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = (x + y)^2 + x^4 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2\,(x + y) + 4x^3 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2\,(x + y) = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x + y = 0 \\[4pt]
4x^3 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
(x, y) = (0, 0)
$$
$$
H(0, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(0, 0) = 0
$$
$$
f(x, y) - f(0, 0) = (x + y)^2 + x^4 \geq 0,
\qquad
f(x, y) = 0 \iff
\begin{cases}
x + y = 0 \\[4pt]
x = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
(x, y) = (0, 0)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the second equation $x + y = 0$; substituting into the first leaves $4x^3 = 0$, so $x = 0$ and $y = 0$. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2 + 12x^2$, $f_{xy} = 2$ and $f_{yy} = 2$, and at the origin the determinant of the Hessian is $4 - 4 = 0$, therefore the **Hessian test** does not allow a conclusion.

We then study the sign of the difference $f(x, y) - f(0, 0)$. It is a sum of two nonnegative terms, so it is $\geq 0$ at every point; moreover it vanishes only when both terms are zero, that is, only at the origin. Therefore the origin is a **strict absolute minimum** point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify the stationary point $(0, 0)$ of
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^3 .
$$

$$
f_x = 2x, \qquad f_y = 3y^2, \qquad \nabla f(0, 0) = (0, 0)
$$
$$
H(0, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(0, 0) = 0
$$
$$
f(0, y) = y^3
\qquad
\begin{cases}
> 0 & \text{for } y > 0 \\[4pt]
< 0 & \text{for } y < 0
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gradient vanishes only at the origin. The second derivatives are $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{xy} = 0$ and $f_{yy} = 6y$, which equals $0$ at the origin; the determinant of the Hessian is therefore zero and the test does not allow a conclusion.

Along the $y$-axis the function equals $y^3$, which in every disk centered at the origin takes positive values, for $y > 0$, and negative values, for $y < 0$, while $f(0, 0) = 0$. Hence the origin is a **saddle point**. Along the $x$-axis the function equals $x^2 \geq 0$, in agreement with $f_{xx} > 0$; the sign of $f_{xx}$ alone is therefore not enough to rule out a saddle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = x^4 + y^4 - (x + y)^2 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 4x^3 - 2\,(x + y) = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 4y^3 - 2\,(x + y) = 0
\end{cases}
\implies
4x^3 = 4y^3 \iff x = y
$$
$$
4x^3 - 4x = 4x\,(x^2 - 1) = 0 \iff x = 0, \ \pm 1
$$
$$
f_{xx} = 12x^2 - 2, \qquad f_{xy} = -2, \qquad f_{yy} = 12y^2 - 2
$$
$$
H(1, 1) = H(-1, -1) = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & -2 \\ -2 & 10 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H = 96 > 0,
\qquad
f(1, 1) = f(-1, -1) = -2
$$
$$
H(0, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(0, 0) = 0
$$
$$
f(t, -t) = 2t^4 > 0,
\qquad
f(t, t) = 2t^4 - 4t^2 = 2t^2\,(t^2 - 2) < 0 \ \text{ for } 0 < |t| < \sqrt{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the two equations gives $4x^3 = 4y^3$, and since the cube function is increasing, hence injective, this is equivalent to $x = y$. With $y = x$ the first equation becomes $4x^3 - 4x = 0$, with solutions $x = 0$ and $x = \pm 1$. The stationary points are $(0, 0)$, $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, -1)$.

At the points $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, -1)$ the determinant is positive and $f_{xx} = 10 > 0$, so they are two **relative minima**, with value $-2$. At the origin the determinant is zero and the test does not allow a conclusion. We then look at the function along the two bisector lines. Along $y = -x$ the term $(x + y)^2$ vanishes and what remains is $2t^4$, positive for $t \neq 0$. Along $y = x$ we have $2t^4 - 4t^2$, negative for $t$ near $0$ and different from $0$. In every disk centered at the origin $f$ therefore takes values greater and values smaller than $f(0, 0) = 0$, hence the origin is a **saddle point**.

</div>
