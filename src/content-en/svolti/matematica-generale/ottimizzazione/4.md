## Exercise 1

Find and classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = (x^2 + y^2)\,e^{-x} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = e^{-x}\,(2x - x^2 - y^2) = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y\,e^{-x} = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
y = 0 \\[4pt]
x\,(2 - x) = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
(x, y) = (0, 0) \ \text{ or } \ (2, 0)
$$
$$
f_{xx} = e^{-x}\,(x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 2),
\qquad
f_{xy} = -2y\,e^{-x},
\qquad
f_{yy} = 2e^{-x}
$$
$$
H(0, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(0, 0) = 4 > 0,
\qquad
f_{xx}(0, 0) = 2 > 0
$$
$$
H(2, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} -2e^{-2} & 0 \\ 0 & 2e^{-2} \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(2, 0) = -4e^{-4} < 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponential $e^{-x}$ is always positive, so the two partial derivatives vanish when the other factors vanish. The second equation gives $y = 0$; with $y = 0$ the first becomes $2x - x^2 = x\,(2 - x) = 0$, which gives $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.

To compute $f_{xx}$ one differentiates $f_x$ with respect to $x$ with the product rule:
$$
f_{xx} = -e^{-x}\,(2x - x^2 - y^2) + e^{-x}\,(2 - 2x) = e^{-x}\,(x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 2) .
$$
At $(0, 0)$ the determinant is positive and $f_{xx} > 0$, so by the **Hessian test** the origin is a **relative minimum**; the minimum is also absolute, because $f \geq 0$ everywhere and $f(0, 0) = 0$. At $(2, 0)$ we have $f_{xx} = e^{-2}\,(4 - 8 + 2) = -2e^{-2}$ and $f_{yy} = 2e^{-2}$, so the determinant is negative and the point is a **saddle** point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find and classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - \ln x - \ln y, \qquad x > 0, \ y > 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x + y - \dfrac{1}{x} = 0 \\[8pt]
f_y = x + 2y - \dfrac{1}{y} = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f_x - f_y = (x - y) + \frac{x - y}{xy} = (x - y) \left( 1 + \frac{1}{xy} \right) = 0
\iff
x = y
$$
$$
3x - \frac{1}{x} = 0 \iff x^2 = \frac{1}{3} \iff x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},
\qquad
P_0 = \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right)
$$
$$
f_{xx} = 2 + \frac{1}{x^2}, \qquad f_{xy} = 1, \qquad f_{yy} = 2 + \frac{1}{y^2}
$$
$$
H(P_0) = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 1 \\ 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(P_0) = 24 > 0,
\qquad
f_{xx}(P_0) = 5 > 0
$$
$$
f(P_0) = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} - 2 \ln \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = 1 + \ln 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain is the open first quadrant, where the logarithms are defined, and each of its points is interior. The two equations have the same structure with the roles of $x$ and $y$ swapped, therefore it is convenient to subtract them. In the difference one factors out $x - y$, and the second factor $1 + \dfrac{1}{xy}$ is positive in the domain, so the difference is zero *only* for $x = y$.

Substituting $y = x$ into the first equation gives $3x = \dfrac{1}{x}$, that is, $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{3}$, and only the positive root is kept. At $P_0$ we have $\dfrac{1}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}{y^2} = 3$, so $f_{xx} = f_{yy} = 5$, the determinant is positive and by the **Hessian test** the point is a **relative minimum** point. For the minimum value one uses $\ln \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = -\dfrac{1}{2} \ln 3$, therefore $-2 \ln \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \ln 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = (x - y)\,e^{-x^2 - y^2} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = e^{-x^2 - y^2} \big( 1 - 2x\,(x - y) \big) = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = e^{-x^2 - y^2} \big( -1 - 2y\,(x - y) \big) = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
1 - 2x\,(x - y) = 0 \\[4pt]
-1 - 2y\,(x - y) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\text{adding:} \quad -2\,(x - y)(x + y) = 0
$$
$$
x = y: \quad 1 = 0 \ \text{ impossible};
\qquad
y = -x: \quad 1 - 4x^2 = 0 \iff x = \pm\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
P_1 = \left( \frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2} \right),
\qquad
P_2 = \left( -\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)
$$
$$
H(P_1) = e^{-\frac{1}{2}} \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 1 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H(P_2) = e^{-\frac{1}{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H = 8e^{-1} > 0
$$
$$
f(P_1) = e^{-\frac{1}{2}} \ \text{ relative maximum},
\qquad
f(P_2) = -e^{-\frac{1}{2}} \ \text{ relative minimum}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponential is always positive, so the factors in parentheses must vanish. Adding the two equations gives $-2x^2 + 2xy - 2xy + 2y^2 = -2\,(x^2 - y^2)$, that is, $-2\,(x - y)(x + y) = 0$. The case $x = y$ makes $x - y$ zero, and the first equation becomes $1 = 0$, so it is impossible. In the case $y = -x$ we have $x - y = 2x$, and the first equation becomes $1 - 4x^2 = 0$. The stationary points are therefore $P_1$ and $P_2$.

For the second derivatives one writes $f_x = e^{-x^2 - y^2}\,A(x, y)$, with $A = 1 - 2x^2 + 2xy$. Differentiating with respect to $x$ gives $f_{xx} = e^{-x^2 - y^2} \big( -2x\,A + (-4x + 2y) \big)$; at the stationary points $A = 0$, so what remains is $f_{xx} = e^{-x^2 - y^2}\,(-4x + 2y)$, which at $P_1$ equals $-3e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$. With the same reasoning $f_{xy} = 2x\,e^{-x^2 - y^2}$ and $f_{yy} = e^{-x^2 - y^2}\,(-2x + 4y)$ at the stationary points. At both points the determinant equals $(9 - 1)\,e^{-1} > 0$. At $P_1$ we have $f_{xx} < 0$, so $P_1$ is a **relative maximum**; at $P_2$ we have $f_{xx} > 0$, so $P_2$ is a **relative minimum**. The values are opposite, because $f(-x, -y) = -f(x, y)$ and $P_2 = -P_1$.

</div>
