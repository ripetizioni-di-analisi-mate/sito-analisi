## Exercise 1

Find the maximum and minimum of $f(x, y) = 3x + 4y$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.

$$
\mathcal{L}(x, y, \lambda) = 3x + 4y - \lambda\,(x^2 + y^2 - 25)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
3 = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
4 = 2\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 = 25
\end{cases}
\implies
x = \frac{3}{2\lambda}, \quad y = \frac{2}{\lambda},
\qquad
\frac{9}{4\lambda^2} + \frac{4}{\lambda^2} = \frac{25}{4\lambda^2} = 25
$$
$$
\lambda^2 = \frac{1}{4}
\implies
\begin{cases}
\lambda = \dfrac{1}{2}: & (x, y) = (3, 4), \quad f = 25 \\[8pt]
\lambda = -\dfrac{1}{2}: & (x, y) = (-3, -4), \quad f = -25
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\max f = f(3, 4) = 25,
\qquad
\min f = f(-3, -4) = -25
$$

::grafico{f="(25-3*x)/4" curve="5*cos(t),5*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="3,4,pieno;-3,-4,pieno" xmin=-6 xmax=10 ymin=-6 ymax=10 uguale=1 legenda="3x + 4y = 25;x^2 + y^2 = 25" titolo="The level line $3x + 4y = 25$ is tangent to the circle at the maximum point $(3, 4)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The functions $f$ and $g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 25$ are polynomials, and $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$ vanishes only at the origin, which does not lie on the constraint; so at every constrained extremum the condition of the **Lagrange multiplier theorem** holds. In the first equation $\lambda$ cannot be $0$, because that would give $3 = 0$; therefore $x$ and $y$ are obtained as functions of $\lambda$ and substituted into the constraint, which gives $\lambda = \pm\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The candidates are $(3, 4)$ and $(-3, -4)$. The circle is closed and bounded and $f$ is continuous, so by the Weierstrass theorem $f$ has a maximum and a minimum on the constraint, which must be among the candidates. The **maximum** is $25$ at $(3, 4)$ and the **minimum** is $-25$ at $(-3, -4)$. At the maximum point the gradient $\nabla f = (3, 4)$ points in the direction of the radius, so the level line $3x + 4y = 25$ is tangent to the circle, as the figure shows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the maximum and minimum of $f(x, y) = x^2 y$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 3$.

$$
\begin{cases}
2xy = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
x^2 = 2\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 = 3
\end{cases}
\qquad
2x\,(y - \lambda) = 0 \iff x = 0 \ \text{ or } \ y = \lambda
$$
$$
x = 0: \quad y = \pm\sqrt{3}, \qquad f\left( 0, \pm\sqrt{3} \right) = 0
$$
$$
y = \lambda: \quad x^2 = 2y^2, \quad 3y^2 = 3, \quad y = \pm 1, \quad x = \pm\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
f\left( \pm\sqrt{2}, 1 \right) = 2,
\qquad
f\left( \pm\sqrt{2}, -1 \right) = -2
$$
$$
\max f = 2 \ \text{ at } \left( \pm\sqrt{2}, 1 \right),
\qquad
\min f = -2 \ \text{ at } \left( \pm\sqrt{2}, -1 \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As in the previous exercise, $\nabla g = (2x, 2y)$ does not vanish on the constraint, so the extrema are among the solutions of the Lagrange system. The first equation can be written $2x\,(y - \lambda) = 0$ and splits into two cases.

If $x = 0$, the constraint gives $y = \pm\sqrt{3}$, and the second equation is satisfied with $\lambda = 0$; at these points $f = 0$. If $y = \lambda$, the second equation becomes $x^2 = 2y^2$, and substituting into the constraint gives $3y^2 = 3$, that is, $y = \pm 1$ and $x^2 = 2$. One obtains four points, at which $f = x^2 y = 2y$ equals $2$ or $-2$. The circle is closed and bounded, so the constrained **maximum** is $2$, at the points $\left( \pm\sqrt{2}, 1 \right)$, and the **minimum** is $-2$, at the points $\left( \pm\sqrt{2}, -1 \right)$. The points $\left( 0, \pm\sqrt{3} \right)$ satisfy the Lagrange condition but are not absolute extrema on the constraint.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the maximum and minimum of $f(x, y) = xy$ on the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 8$.

$$
\begin{cases}
y = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
x = 8\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + 4y^2 = 8
\end{cases}
\implies
y = 16\lambda^2 y
$$
$$
y = 0 \implies x = 0, \quad \text{but } (0, 0) \text{ does not lie on the constraint}
$$
$$
\lambda^2 = \frac{1}{16}
\implies
\begin{cases}
\lambda = \dfrac{1}{4}: & y = \dfrac{x}{2}, \quad 2x^2 = 8, \quad (2, 1), \ (-2, -1) \\[8pt]
\lambda = -\dfrac{1}{4}: & y = -\dfrac{x}{2}, \quad 2x^2 = 8, \quad (2, -1), \ (-2, 1)
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\max f = 2 \ \text{ at } (2, 1) \text{ and } (-2, -1),
\qquad
\min f = -2 \ \text{ at } (2, -1) \text{ and } (-2, 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gradient of the constraint is $\nabla g = (2x, 8y)$, zero only at the origin, which does not lie on the ellipse; the **Lagrange multiplier theorem** therefore applies at every point of the constraint. Substituting the second equation into the first gives $y = 2\lambda \cdot 8\lambda y = 16\lambda^2 y$. The case $y = 0$ would lead to $x = 0$, which is excluded, therefore $y \neq 0$ and one can divide by $y$, obtaining $\lambda = \pm\dfrac{1}{4}$.

With $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{4}$ the first equation gives $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$, and the constraint becomes $x^2 + x^2 = 8$, that is, $x = \pm 2$. With $\lambda = -\dfrac{1}{4}$ we have $y = -\dfrac{x}{2}$, and one obtains the other two points. The ellipse is closed and bounded, so comparing the values gives the **maximum** $2$ and the **minimum** $-2$.

</div>
