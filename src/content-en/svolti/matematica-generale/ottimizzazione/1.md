## Exercise 1

Find and classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3x .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x + y - 3 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = x + 2y = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x = -2y \\[4pt]
-4y + y - 3 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x = 2 \\[4pt]
y = -1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f_{xx} = 2, \qquad f_{xy} = 1, \qquad f_{yy} = 2
$$
$$
H(2, -1) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(2, -1) = 4 - 1 = 3 > 0,
\qquad
f_{xx} = 2 > 0
$$
$$
f(2, -1) = 4 - 2 + 1 - 6 = -3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it has partial derivatives at every point of the plane, and by **Fermat's theorem in two variables** its relative extrema are among the points where the gradient is zero. From the second equation one obtains $x = -2y$; substituting into the first gives $-3y - 3 = 0$, that is, $y = -1$, and hence $x = 2$. The only stationary point is $(2, -1)$.

The second derivatives are constant, therefore the Hessian is the same at every point. Its determinant is positive and $f_{xx}$ is positive, so by the **Hessian test** the point $(2, -1)$ is a **relative minimum** point, with value $f(2, -1) = -3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find and classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = x^3 - 3x + y^2 - 2y .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 3x^2 - 3 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y - 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x = \pm 1 \\[4pt]
y = 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f_{xx} = 6x, \qquad f_{xy} = 0, \qquad f_{yy} = 2
$$
$$
H(1, 1) = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(1, 1) = 12 > 0,
\qquad
f_{xx}(1, 1) = 6 > 0
$$
$$
H(-1, 1) = \begin{pmatrix} -6 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det H(-1, 1) = -12 < 0
$$
$$
f(1, 1) = 1 - 3 + 1 - 2 = -3,
\qquad
f(-1, 1) = -1 + 3 + 1 - 2 = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two equations of the system each contain only one variable, so they are solved separately. The first gives $x^2 = 1$, that is, $x = 1$ or $x = -1$; the second gives $y = 1$. The stationary points are therefore $(1, 1)$ and $(-1, 1)$.

The derivative $f_{xx} = 6x$ depends on the point, so the Hessian must be computed at each one. At $(1, 1)$ the determinant is positive and $f_{xx} > 0$, therefore the point is a **relative minimum** point, with value $-3$. At $(-1, 1)$ the determinant is negative, therefore the point is a **saddle** point. As a check, along the line $y = 1$ the function equals $x^3 - 3x - 1$, which has a maximum at $x = -1$, while along the line $x = -1$ it equals $y^2 - 2y + 2$, which has a minimum at $y = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and classify the stationary points of
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 y - 2xy + y^2 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2xy - 2y = 2y\,(x - 1) = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = x^2 - 2x + 2y = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
y = 0: \quad x^2 - 2x = 0 \iff x = 0 \ \text{ or } \ x = 2
$$
$$
x = 1: \quad 1 - 2 + 2y = 0 \iff y = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
f_{xx} = 2y, \qquad f_{xy} = 2x - 2, \qquad f_{yy} = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(0, 0) &= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, &\quad \det H(0, 0) &= -4 < 0 \\[6pt]
H(2, 0) &= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, &\quad \det H(2, 0) &= -4 < 0 \\[6pt]
H\left( 1, \frac{1}{2} \right) &= \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, &\quad \det H\left( 1, \frac{1}{2} \right) &= 2 > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f\left( 1, \frac{1}{2} \right) = \frac{1}{2} - 1 + \frac{1}{4} = -\frac{1}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation is a product equal to zero, so it splits into two cases, $y = 0$ or $x = 1$, and each case is substituted into the second equation. With $y = 0$ the second becomes $x^2 - 2x = x\,(x - 2) = 0$, which gives $x = 0$ and $x = 2$. With $x = 1$ the second becomes $2y - 1 = 0$, which gives $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$. There are therefore three stationary points, $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $\left( 1, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$.

At the points $(0, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$ the determinant of the Hessian is negative, therefore by the **Hessian test** they are two **saddle points**. At the point $\left( 1, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ the determinant is positive and $f_{xx} = 1 > 0$, therefore the point is a **relative minimum** point, with value $-\dfrac{1}{4}$. The minimum is not absolute, because along the line $x = 3$ the function equals $3y + y^2$, and at $y = -\dfrac{3}{2}$ this value is $-\dfrac{9}{4}$, smaller than $-\dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>
