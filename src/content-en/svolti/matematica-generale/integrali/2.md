## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\int x \sqrt{x + 1}\,dx .
$$

$$
t = x + 1,
\qquad
x = t - 1,
\qquad
dx = dt
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int x \sqrt{x + 1}\,dx
&= \int (t - 1)\,t^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt
= \int \left( t^{\frac{3}{2}} - t^{\frac{1}{2}} \right) dt \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{5}\,t^{\frac{5}{2}} - \frac{2}{3}\,t^{\frac{3}{2}} + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{5}\,(x + 1)^{\frac{5}{2}} - \frac{2}{3}\,(x + 1)^{\frac{3}{2}} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression inside the square root contains $x + 1$, and $x$ appears outside it. The substitution $t = x + 1$ turns both factors into powers of $t$. It gives $x = t - 1$, and differentiation gives $dx = dt$. Multiplying $(t - 1)$ by $t^{\frac{1}{2}}$ gives a difference of powers, which can be integrated using the **power rule for integration**. Return to $x$ by substituting $t = x + 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute, for $x > 0$,
$$
\int \frac{1}{x + \sqrt{x}}\,dx .
$$

$$
t = \sqrt{x},
\qquad
x = t^2,
\qquad
dx = 2t\,dt
$$
$$
\int \frac{1}{x + \sqrt{x}}\,dx = \int \frac{2t}{t^2 + t}\,dt = \int \frac{2t}{t\,(t + 1)}\,dt = \int \frac{2}{t + 1}\,dt = 2 \ln(t + 1) + c
$$
$$
\int \frac{1}{x + \sqrt{x}}\,dx = 2 \ln\left( 1 + \sqrt{x} \right) + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **substitution** $t = \sqrt{x}$ eliminates the root, because $x = t^2$ and $\sqrt{x} = t$ for $x > 0$ and $t > 0$. Differentiating $x = t^2$ with respect to $t$ gives $dx = 2t\,dt$. The denominator becomes $t^2 + t = t\,(t + 1)$, and the factor $t$ cancels with the one in $2t$. What remains is $\dfrac{2}{t + 1}$, which has antiderivative $2 \ln \lvert t + 1 \rvert$; the absolute value is omitted because $t + 1 > 0$. Finally we substitute $t = \sqrt{x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\int \frac{e^{2x}}{e^{x} + 1}\,dx .
$$

$$
t = e^{x},
\qquad
dt = e^{x}\,dx
$$
$$
\int \frac{e^{2x}}{e^{x} + 1}\,dx = \int \frac{e^{x}}{e^{x} + 1}\,e^{x}\,dx = \int \frac{t}{t + 1}\,dt
$$
$$
\int \frac{t}{t + 1}\,dt = \int \left( 1 - \frac{1}{t + 1} \right) dt = t - \ln(t + 1) + c
$$
$$
\int \frac{e^{2x}}{e^{x} + 1}\,dx = e^{x} - \ln\left( e^{x} + 1 \right) + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write $e^{2x} = e^{x} \cdot e^{x}$. One of the two factors, together with $dx$, gives the differential $dt = e^{x}\,dx$ of the new variable $t = e^{x}$. The other becomes $t$. The resulting rational function $\dfrac{t}{t + 1}$ has numerator and denominator of the same degree. The identity $t = (t + 1) - 1$ gives $1 - \dfrac{1}{t + 1}$, whose terms can be integrated directly. Since $t + 1 = e^{x} + 1 > 0$, the logarithm needs no absolute value. Returning to $x$ gives the result.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Compute
$$
\int_0^3 \frac{x}{\sqrt{x + 1}}\,dx .
$$

$$
t = \sqrt{x + 1},
\qquad
x = t^2 - 1,
\qquad
dx = 2t\,dt,
\qquad
x = 0 \mapsto t = 1,
\qquad
x = 3 \mapsto t = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^3 \frac{x}{\sqrt{x + 1}}\,dx
&= \int_1^2 \frac{t^2 - 1}{t}\,2t\,dt
= 2 \int_1^2 \left( t^2 - 1 \right) dt \\[6pt]
&= 2 \left[ \frac{t^3}{3} - t \right]_1^2
= 2 \left[ \left( \frac{8}{3} - 2 \right) - \left( \frac{1}{3} - 1 \right) \right] \\[6pt]
&= 2 \left( \frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3} \right)
= \frac{8}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The substitution $t = \sqrt{x + 1}$ eliminates the square root. Squaring gives $x = t^2 - 1$ and hence $dx = 2t\,dt$. Since the integral is definite, **the limits also change**. At $x = 0$, $t = \sqrt{1} = 1$, and at $x = 3$, $t = \sqrt{4} = 2$. The factor $t$ in the denominator cancels with the same factor in $2t$, leaving the polynomial $2\left( t^2 - 1 \right)$. The **definite integral evaluation formula** gives the difference between the values of the antiderivative at $2$ and $1$, without returning to $x$. The integral equals $\dfrac{8}{3}$, which is positive because the integrand is positive on $(0, 3]$.

</div>
