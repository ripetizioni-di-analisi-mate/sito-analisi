## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\int x\,e^{3x}\,dx .
$$

$$
f(x) = x,
\qquad
g'(x) = e^{3x},
\qquad
f'(x) = 1,
\qquad
g(x) = \frac{e^{3x}}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int x\,e^{3x}\,dx
&= x \cdot \frac{e^{3x}}{3} - \int \frac{e^{3x}}{3}\,dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x\,e^{3x}}{3} - \frac{e^{3x}}{9} + c
= e^{3x} \left( \frac{x}{3} - \frac{1}{9} \right) + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is a product of a polynomial and an exponential, so **integration by parts** applies. The factor to differentiate is the polynomial $x$, whose derivative is the constant $1$. The factor to integrate is $e^{3x}$, with antiderivative $\dfrac{e^{3x}}{3}$. This choice leaves the simpler integral $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{e^{3x}}{3}\,dx$, which equals $\dfrac{e^{3x}}{9}$. Reversing the roles of the factors would leave the integral of $\dfrac{3x^2}{2}\,e^{3x}$, with a higher degree.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute, for $x > 0$,
$$
\int x^2 \ln x\,dx .
$$

$$
f(x) = \ln x,
\qquad
g'(x) = x^2,
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{1}{x},
\qquad
g(x) = \frac{x^3}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int x^2 \ln x\,dx
&= \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \int \frac{x^3}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx
= \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \frac{1}{3} \int x^2\,dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \frac{x^3}{9} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the logarithm is the **factor to differentiate** because $\ln x$ has the readily available derivative $\dfrac{1}{x}$, while finding an antiderivative would itself require integration by parts. The factor to integrate is $x^2$, with antiderivative $\dfrac{x^3}{3}$. In the remaining integral, the factor $\dfrac{1}{x}$ lowers the degree of $x^3$ by one, leaving the basic integral of $\dfrac{x^2}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\int x^2 e^{-x}\,dx .
$$

$$
\int x^2 e^{-x}\,dx = -x^2 e^{-x} - \int 2x \left( -e^{-x} \right) dx = -x^2 e^{-x} + 2 \int x\,e^{-x}\,dx
$$
$$
\int x\,e^{-x}\,dx = -x\,e^{-x} - \int \left( -e^{-x} \right) dx = -x\,e^{-x} - e^{-x} + c
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int x^2 e^{-x}\,dx
&= -x^2 e^{-x} + 2 \left( -x\,e^{-x} - e^{-x} \right) + c \\[6pt]
&= -\left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right) e^{-x} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor to differentiate is the polynomial $x^2$. The factor to integrate is $e^{-x}$, with antiderivative $-e^{-x}$. The first application of **integration by parts** lowers the polynomial degree from $2$ to $1$ and leaves $\displaystyle\int x\,e^{-x}\,dx$. Applying the formula again with the polynomial as the factor to differentiate reduces the degree to $0$, leaving a basic integral. Substituting the second result into the first and factoring out $-e^{-x}$ gives the antiderivative. For a polynomial of degree $n$ multiplied by an exponential, $n$ applications of integration by parts are needed. As a check, the derivative of $-\left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right) e^{-x}$ is $-(2x + 2)\,e^{-x} + \left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right) e^{-x} = x^2 e^{-x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Compute
$$
\int_1^e (\ln x)^2\,dx .
$$

$$
\int (\ln x)^2\,dx = x\,(\ln x)^2 - \int x \cdot 2 \ln x \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = x\,(\ln x)^2 - 2 \int \ln x\,dx
$$
$$
\int \ln x\,dx = x \ln x - x + c
$$
$$
\int (\ln x)^2\,dx = x\,(\ln x)^2 - 2x \ln x + 2x + c
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^e (\ln x)^2\,dx
&= \Big[ x\,(\ln x)^2 - 2x \ln x + 2x \Big]_1^e \\[6pt]
&= (e - 2e + 2e) - (0 - 0 + 2)
= e - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand contains no obvious factor to integrate, so choose $g'(x) = 1$ with $g(x) = x$ and take $f(x) = (\ln x)^2$ as the factor to differentiate. The chain rule gives $f'(x) = 2 \ln x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x}$, and $\dfrac{1}{x}$ cancels with $x$. The remaining integral of $\ln x$ is evaluated **by parts** in the same way and equals $x \ln x - x$. The resulting antiderivative gives the definite integral by the **definite integral evaluation formula**, with $\ln e = 1$ and $\ln 1 = 0$. The result $e - 2 \approx 0{,}72$ is positive because the integrand is positive on $(1, e]$.

</div>
