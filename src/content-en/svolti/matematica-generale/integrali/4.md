## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\int x\,e^{3x}\,dx .
$$

$$
f(x) = x,
\qquad
g'(x) = e^{3x},
\qquad
f'(x) = 1,
\qquad
g(x) = \frac{e^{3x}}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int x\,e^{3x}\,dx
&= x \cdot \frac{e^{3x}}{3} - \int \frac{e^{3x}}{3}\,dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x\,e^{3x}}{3} - \frac{e^{3x}}{9} + c
= e^{3x} \left( \frac{x}{3} - \frac{1}{9} \right) + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is the product of a polynomial and an exponential, so we apply **integration by parts**. We choose as finite factor the polynomial $x$, whose derivative is the constant $1$, and as differential factor $e^{3x}$, which has the basic antiderivative $\dfrac{e^{3x}}{3}$. With this choice the remaining integral, $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{e^{3x}}{3}\,dx$, is simpler than the original one, and it equals $\dfrac{e^{3x}}{9}$. The opposite choice would lead to integrating $\dfrac{3x^2}{2}\,e^{3x}$, with a higher degree.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute, for $x > 0$,
$$
\int x^2 \ln x\,dx .
$$

$$
f(x) = \ln x,
\qquad
g'(x) = x^2,
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{1}{x},
\qquad
g(x) = \frac{x^3}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int x^2 \ln x\,dx
&= \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \int \frac{x^3}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx
= \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \frac{1}{3} \int x^2\,dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x^3}{3} \ln x - \frac{x^3}{9} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the logarithm must be taken as the **finite factor**, because the derivative of $\ln x$, $\dfrac{1}{x}$, is easily known, while an antiderivative of it would in turn require an integration by parts. The differential factor is then $x^2$, with antiderivative $\dfrac{x^3}{3}$. In the remaining integral the factor $\dfrac{1}{x}$ lowers the degree of $x^3$ by one, and the basic integral of $\dfrac{x^2}{3}$ remains.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\int x^2 e^{-x}\,dx .
$$

$$
\int x^2 e^{-x}\,dx = -x^2 e^{-x} - \int 2x \left( -e^{-x} \right) dx = -x^2 e^{-x} + 2 \int x\,e^{-x}\,dx
$$
$$
\int x\,e^{-x}\,dx = -x\,e^{-x} - \int \left( -e^{-x} \right) dx = -x\,e^{-x} - e^{-x} + c
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int x^2 e^{-x}\,dx
&= -x^2 e^{-x} + 2 \left( -x\,e^{-x} - e^{-x} \right) + c \\[6pt]
&= -\left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right) e^{-x} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The finite factor is the polynomial $x^2$ and the differential factor is $e^{-x}$, which has antiderivative $-e^{-x}$. A first **integration by parts** lowers the degree of the polynomial from $2$ to $1$ and leaves the integral $\displaystyle\int x\,e^{-x}\,dx$. To this the formula is applied again, still with the polynomial as finite factor, and the degree drops to $0$, that is, a basic integral remains. Substituting the second result into the first and factoring out $-e^{-x}$ gives the antiderivative. In general, for a polynomial of degree $n$ multiplied by an exponential, $n$ integrations by parts are needed. As a check, the derivative of $-\left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right) e^{-x}$ is $-(2x + 2)\,e^{-x} + \left( x^2 + 2x + 2 \right) e^{-x} = x^2 e^{-x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Compute
$$
\int_1^e (\ln x)^2\,dx .
$$

$$
\int (\ln x)^2\,dx = x\,(\ln x)^2 - \int x \cdot 2 \ln x \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = x\,(\ln x)^2 - 2 \int \ln x\,dx
$$
$$
\int \ln x\,dx = x \ln x - x + c
$$
$$
\int (\ln x)^2\,dx = x\,(\ln x)^2 - 2x \ln x + 2x + c
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^e (\ln x)^2\,dx
&= \Big[ x\,(\ln x)^2 - 2x \ln x + 2x \Big]_1^e \\[6pt]
&= (e - 2e + 2e) - (0 - 0 + 2)
= e - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand does not contain an evident differential factor, so we choose $g'(x) = 1$, with $g(x) = x$, and take as finite factor $f(x) = (\ln x)^2$. By the chain rule $f'(x) = 2 \ln x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x}$, and the factor $\dfrac{1}{x}$ cancels with $x$. What remains is the integral of $\ln x$, which is again computed **by parts** with the same procedure and equals $x \ln x - x$. With the antiderivative found we apply the **fundamental formula of calculus**, with $\ln e = 1$ and $\ln 1 = 0$. The result $e - 2 \approx 0.72$ is positive, as it must be, because the integrand is positive on $(1, e]$.

</div>
