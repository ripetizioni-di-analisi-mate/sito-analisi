## Exercise 1

The marginal cost of a firm is $C'(q) = 0.3\,q^2 - 6q + 50$ and the fixed cost is $400$ euros. Find the total cost function, the cost of $20$ units and the additional cost of raising production from $10$ to $20$ units, with the average cost of each of the additional units.

$$
C(q) = 400 + \int_0^{q} \left( 0.3\,x^2 - 6x + 50 \right) dx = 400 + 0.1\,q^3 - 3q^2 + 50q
$$
$$
C(20) = 400 + 800 - 1200 + 1000 = 1000
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C(20) - C(10)
&= \int_{10}^{20} \left( 0.3\,q^2 - 6q + 50 \right) dq
= \Big[ 0.1\,q^3 - 3q^2 + 50q \Big]_{10}^{20} \\[6pt]
&= (800 - 1200 + 1000) - (100 - 300 + 500)
= 600 - 300
= 300
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{C(20) - C(10)}{20 - 10} = \frac{300}{10} = 30
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total cost is an antiderivative of the marginal cost, so by the formula for the **total cost from marginal cost** we integrate $C'$ from $0$ to $q$ and add the fixed cost $C(0) = 400$. With integration variable $x$, an antiderivative of $C'$ is $0.1\,x^3 - 3x^2 + 50x$, which equals $0$ at $x = 0$. The cost of $20$ units is $1000$ euros. The additional cost of going from $10$ to $20$ units is the integral of the marginal cost between $10$ and $20$, that is, $300$ euros, and it coincides with $C(20) - C(10)$, where $C(10) = 700$. Divided by the $10$ additional units, it gives the **average value** of the marginal cost on the interval, $30$ euros per unit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The price of a good in the first $6$ months of the year is $p(t) = 12 + 6t - t^2$ euros, with $t$ in months. Compute the average price over the half-year and the instants at which the price is equal to the average price.

$$
\bar{p} = \frac{1}{6} \int_0^6 \left( 12 + 6t - t^2 \right) dt = \frac{1}{6} \left[ 12t + 3t^2 - \frac{t^3}{3} \right]_0^6 = \frac{1}{6}\,(72 + 108 - 72) = 18
$$
$$
12 + 6t - t^2 = 18
\iff
t^2 - 6t + 6 = 0
\iff
t = 3 \pm \sqrt{3}
$$
$$
t_1 = 3 - \sqrt{3} \approx 1.27,
\qquad
t_2 = 3 + \sqrt{3} \approx 4.73
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The price varies continuously over time, so the **average value** over the half-year is the integral mean, that is, the integral of $p$ on $[0, 6]$ divided by $6$. The average price is $18$ euros. By the **integral mean value theorem** the price is equal to $18$ euros at least at one instant; solving the second-degree equation, with reduced discriminant $9 - 6 = 3$, we find two instants, both within the half-year. The price is equal to the average price at about $1.27$ and $4.73$ months.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The inverse demand of a good is $p_d(q) = \dfrac{60}{q + 2}$ and the inverse supply is $p_s(q) = 2q + 2$, with $q \geq 0$. Find the market equilibrium and the consumer and producer surpluses.

$$
\frac{60}{q + 2} = 2q + 2
\iff
(2q + 2)(q + 2) = 60
\iff
q^2 + 3q - 28 = 0
\iff
q = 4 \ \text{ or } \ q = -7
$$
$$
q^* = 4,
\qquad
p^* = 2 \cdot 4 + 2 = 10
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_C
&= \int_0^4 \left( \frac{60}{q + 2} - 10 \right) dq
= \Big[ 60 \ln(q + 2) - 10q \Big]_0^4 \\[6pt]
&= 60 \ln 6 - 40 - 60 \ln 2
= 60 \ln 3 - 40
\approx 25.92
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
S_P = \int_0^4 \big( 10 - (2q + 2) \big)\,dq = \Big[ 8q - q^2 \Big]_0^4 = 32 - 16 = 16
$$

::grafico{f="60/(x+2)+0*sqrt(x);2*x+2+0*sqrt(x)" area="60/(x+2);10;0;4|10;2*x+2;0;4" punti="4,10,pieno" segmenti="0,10,6,10;4,0,4,10" xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-4 ymax=36 nomi="q,p" legenda="p = \dfrac{60}{q + 2};p = 2q + 2" titolo="Demand and supply meet at $(4, 10)$; above the line $p = 10$ the consumer surplus, $60 \ln 3 - 40$, below it the producer surplus, $16$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At **equilibrium** the demand price is equal to the supply price; multiplying by $q + 2 > 0$ and expanding the product gives $2q^2 + 6q + 4 = 60$, that is, $q^2 + 3q - 28 = 0$. The only acceptable solution, because it is non-negative, is $q^* = 4$, with price $p^* = 10$ euros.

The **consumer surplus** is the integral from $0$ to $q^*$ of the difference between the price consumers would be willing to pay and the equilibrium price. The antiderivative of $\dfrac{60}{q + 2}$ is $60 \ln(q + 2)$ by the composite form, and the difference $\ln 6 - \ln 2 = \ln 3$ gives $S_C = 60 \ln 3 - 40$, about $25.92$ euros. The **producer surplus** is the integral of the difference between the equilibrium price and the supply price, and it equals $16$ euros. As a check, the producer's region is a triangle with base $4$ and height $10 - 2 = 8$, with area $16$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

An investment produces for $5$ years a continuous cash flow with intensity $f(t) = 1000 + 200t$ euros per year. Compute the present value of the flow with instantaneous intensity of interest $\delta = 0.05$, and compare it with the total received.

$$
V = \int_0^5 (1000 + 200t)\,e^{-0.05\,t}\,dt
$$
$$
u = 1000 + 200t,
\qquad
dv = e^{-0.05\,t}\,dt,
\qquad
du = 200\,dt,
\qquad
v = -20\,e^{-0.05\,t}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&= \Big[ -20\,(1000 + 200t)\,e^{-0.05\,t} \Big]_0^5 + 4000 \int_0^5 e^{-0.05\,t}\,dt \\[6pt]
&= \left( -40\,000\,e^{-0.25} + 20\,000 \right) + 4000 \cdot 20 \left( 1 - e^{-0.25} \right) \\[6pt]
&= 100\,000 - 120\,000\,e^{-0.25}
\approx 6543.91
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\int_0^5 (1000 + 200t)\,dt = \Big[ 1000t + 100t^2 \Big]_0^5 = 5000 + 2500 = 7500
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of the **present value of a continuous flow**, we integrate over the horizon $[0, 5]$ the intensity of the flow multiplied by the discount factor $e^{-0.05\,t}$. The integrand is the product of a first-degree polynomial and an exponential, so it is integrated **by parts**, with the polynomial as finite factor. An antiderivative of $e^{-0.05\,t}$ is $-\dfrac{e^{-0.05\,t}}{0.05} = -20\,e^{-0.05\,t}$. In the boundary term, at $t = 5$ we have $1000 + 200 \cdot 5 = 2000$, and at $t = 0$ we have $1000$. The remaining integral, multiplied by $20 \cdot 200 = 4000$, is a basic exponential integral. Collecting the terms in $e^{-0.25}$ gives $V \approx 6543.91$ euros. The total received, without discounting, is $7500$ euros, and the present value is smaller, as it must be, because every future receipt is discounted.

</div>
