## Exercise 1

Compute the area of the region bounded by the parabola $y = x^2$ and by the line $y = 2x + 3$.

$$
x^2 = 2x + 3
\iff
x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0
\iff
x = -1 \ \text{ or } \ x = 3
$$
$$
x = 0: \quad 2x + 3 = 3 > 0 = x^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A
&= \int_{-1}^{3} \left( 2x + 3 - x^2 \right) dx
= \left[ x^2 + 3x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-1}^{3} \\[6pt]
&= (9 + 9 - 9) - \left( 1 - 3 + \frac{1}{3} \right)
= 9 + \frac{5}{3}
= \frac{32}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;2*x+3" area="2*x+3;x^2;-1;3" punti="-1,1,pieno;3,9,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=12 legenda="y = x^2;y = 2x + 3" titolo="The region between the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 2x + 3$, with the intersections $(-1, 1)$ and $(3, 9)$; its area is $\dfrac{32}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two graphs alone bound the region, so the limits of integration are the x-coordinates of their **intersection points**, $x = -1$ and $x = 3$, found by equating the two expressions. The graphs do not cross between those points, so an interior point determines which curve lies above. At $x = 0$, the line has value $3$ and the parabola has value $0$. Thus the line lies above. The theorem on the **area between two curves** gives the integral of the upper function minus the lower function. The result is $\dfrac{32}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the area of the region between the graphs of $f(x) = x^3$ and $g(x) = 4x$.

$$
x^3 = 4x
\iff
x\left( x^2 - 4 \right) = 0
\iff
x \in \{-2, 0, 2\}
$$
$$
x^3 - 4x \geq 0 \ \text{ on } [-2, 0],
\qquad
4x - x^3 \geq 0 \ \text{ on } [0, 2]
$$
$$
A = \int_{-2}^{0} \left( x^3 - 4x \right) dx + \int_{0}^{2} \left( 4x - x^3 \right) dx
$$
$$
\int_{-2}^{0} \left( x^3 - 4x \right) dx = \left[ \frac{x^4}{4} - 2x^2 \right]_{-2}^{0} = 0 - (4 - 8) = 4,
\qquad
\int_{0}^{2} \left( 4x - x^3 \right) dx = \left[ 2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_{0}^{2} = 8 - 4 = 4
$$
$$
A = 4 + 4 = 8
$$

::grafico{f="x^3;4*x" area="x^3;4*x;-2;0|4*x;x^3;0;2" punti="-2,-8,pieno;0,0,pieno;2,8,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-10 ymax=10 legenda="y = x^3;y = 4x" titolo="The graphs of $y = x^3$ and $y = 4x$ cross at $x = -2$, $0$, $2$. On $[-2, 0]$ the cubic lies above the line, and on $[0, 2]$ the line lies above the cubic. Each region has area $4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The graphs meet at three points, so they enclose two regions with a different upper curve in each. The sign of $x^3 - 4x = x\,(x - 2)(x + 2)$ follows from the signs of its three factors. On $(-2, 0)$, two factors are negative and one is positive, so their product is positive and the cubic lies above. On $(0, 2)$, only one factor is negative, so the line lies above. **Split the interval** at $x = 0$ and subtract the lower function from the upper function on each part. Integrating $x^3 - 4x$ over all of $[-2, 2]$ would give $0$ because the two signed parts cancel. The areas are equal because both functions are odd and the figure is symmetric about the origin. The total area is $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the area of the region bounded by the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and by the line $y = \dfrac{5}{2} - x$.

$$
\frac{1}{x} = \frac{5}{2} - x
\iff
x^2 - \frac{5}{2}\,x + 1 = 0
\iff
2x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0
\iff
x = \frac{1}{2} \ \text{ or } \ x = 2
$$
$$
x = 1: \quad \frac{5}{2} - 1 = \frac{3}{2} > 1 = \frac{1}{x}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A
&= \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{2} \left( \frac{5}{2} - x - \frac{1}{x} \right) dx
= \left[ \frac{5}{2}\,x - \frac{x^2}{2} - \ln x \right]_{\frac{1}{2}}^{2} \\[6pt]
&= (5 - 2 - \ln 2) - \left( \frac{5}{4} - \frac{1}{8} - \ln \frac{1}{2} \right)
= 3 - \ln 2 - \frac{9}{8} - \ln 2 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{15}{8} - 2 \ln 2
\approx 0{,}49
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="1/x+0*sqrt(x);2.5-x" area="2.5-x;1/x;0.5;2" punti="0.5,2,pieno;2,0.5,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=3 legenda="y = \dfrac{1}{x};y = \dfrac{5}{2} - x" titolo="The region between the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and the line $y = \dfrac{5}{2} - x$, for $\dfrac{1}{2} \leq x \leq 2$; its area is $\dfrac{15}{8} - 2\ln 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The intersection points satisfy $x \neq 0$, so multiplying the equation by $x$ and then by $2$ gives a quadratic with discriminant $25 - 16 = 9$ and roots $\dfrac{5 \pm 3}{4}$, namely $2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$. At $x = 1$, the line has value $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and the hyperbola has value $1$, so the line is above the hyperbola between the intersections. The interval $\left[ \dfrac{1}{2}, 2 \right]$ is contained in $(0, +\infty)$, where $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is continuous and has antiderivative $\ln x$. The calculation uses $\ln \dfrac{1}{2} = -\ln 2$, so the term $-\ln \dfrac{1}{2}$ in the lower-limit evaluation is $+\ln 2$, and subtracting that evaluation gives $-\ln 2$. The area is $\dfrac{15}{8} - 2\ln 2$, approximately $0{,}49$.

</div>
