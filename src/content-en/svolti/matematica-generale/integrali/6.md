## Exercise 1

Compute the area of the region bounded by the parabola $y = x^2$ and by the line $y = 2x + 3$.

$$
x^2 = 2x + 3
\iff
x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0
\iff
x = -1 \ \text{ or } \ x = 3
$$
$$
x = 0: \quad 2x + 3 = 3 > 0 = x^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A
&= \int_{-1}^{3} \left( 2x + 3 - x^2 \right) dx
= \left[ x^2 + 3x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-1}^{3} \\[6pt]
&= (9 + 9 - 9) - \left( 1 - 3 + \frac{1}{3} \right)
= 9 + \frac{5}{3}
= \frac{32}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;2*x+3" area="2*x+3;x^2;-1;3" punti="-1,1,pieno;3,9,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=12 legenda="y = x^2;y = 2x + 3" titolo="The region between the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 2x + 3$, with the intersections $(-1, 1)$ and $(3, 9)$; its area is $\dfrac{32}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The region is bounded only by the two graphs, so the bounds of integration are the abscissas of the **intersection points**, which are found by equating the two expressions, and they are $x = -1$ and $x = 3$. To know which curve lies above, one interior point is enough, because between the two intersections the graphs do not cross; at $x = 0$ the line equals $3$ and the parabola $0$, therefore the line lies above. By the theorem on the **area between two curves** we integrate the difference between the higher function and the lower one. The result is $\dfrac{32}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the area of the region between the graphs of $f(x) = x^3$ and of $g(x) = 4x$.

$$
x^3 = 4x
\iff
x\left( x^2 - 4 \right) = 0
\iff
x \in \{-2, 0, 2\}
$$
$$
x^3 - 4x \geq 0 \ \text{ on } [-2, 0],
\qquad
4x - x^3 \geq 0 \ \text{ on } [0, 2]
$$
$$
A = \int_{-2}^{0} \left( x^3 - 4x \right) dx + \int_{0}^{2} \left( 4x - x^3 \right) dx
$$
$$
\int_{-2}^{0} \left( x^3 - 4x \right) dx = \left[ \frac{x^4}{4} - 2x^2 \right]_{-2}^{0} = 0 - (4 - 8) = 4,
\qquad
\int_{0}^{2} \left( 4x - x^3 \right) dx = \left[ 2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_{0}^{2} = 8 - 4 = 4
$$
$$
A = 4 + 4 = 8
$$

::grafico{f="x^3;4*x" area="x^3;4*x;-2;0|4*x;x^3;0;2" punti="-2,-8,pieno;0,0,pieno;2,8,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-10 ymax=10 legenda="y = x^3;y = 4x" titolo="The graphs of $y = x^3$ and of $y = 4x$ cross at $x = -2$, $0$, $2$; on $[-2, 0]$ the cubic lies above and on $[0, 2]$ the line, and each region has area $4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two graphs meet at three points, so they bound two regions, and in each of them a different curve lies above. The sign of $x^3 - 4x = x\,(x - 2)(x + 2)$ is obtained from the product of the three factors. On $(-2, 0)$ two factors are negative and one positive, so the product is positive and the cubic lies above; on $(0, 2)$ only one factor is negative, so the line lies above. We **split the interval** at the crossing point $x = 0$ and in each part subtract the lower function from the higher one. Integrating $x^3 - 4x$ over the whole of $[-2, 2]$ would give $0$, because the two parts cancel each other. The two areas are equal, because both functions are odd and the figure is symmetric with respect to the origin, and the total area equals $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the area of the region bounded by the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and by the line $y = \dfrac{5}{2} - x$.

$$
\frac{1}{x} = \frac{5}{2} - x
\iff
x^2 - \frac{5}{2}\,x + 1 = 0
\iff
2x^2 - 5x + 2 = 0
\iff
x = \frac{1}{2} \ \text{ or } \ x = 2
$$
$$
x = 1: \quad \frac{5}{2} - 1 = \frac{3}{2} > 1 = \frac{1}{x}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A
&= \int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{2} \left( \frac{5}{2} - x - \frac{1}{x} \right) dx
= \left[ \frac{5}{2}\,x - \frac{x^2}{2} - \ln x \right]_{\frac{1}{2}}^{2} \\[6pt]
&= (5 - 2 - \ln 2) - \left( \frac{5}{4} - \frac{1}{8} - \ln \frac{1}{2} \right)
= 3 - \ln 2 - \frac{9}{8} - \ln 2 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{15}{8} - 2 \ln 2
\approx 0.49
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="1/x+0*sqrt(x);2.5-x" area="2.5-x;1/x;0.5;2" punti="0.5,2,pieno;2,0.5,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=3 legenda="y = \dfrac{1}{x};y = \dfrac{5}{2} - x" titolo="The region between the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and the line $y = \dfrac{5}{2} - x$, for $\dfrac{1}{2} \leq x \leq 2$; its area is $\dfrac{15}{8} - 2\ln 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The intersections have $x \neq 0$, so the equation can be multiplied by $x$, and then by $2$, giving a second-degree equation with discriminant $25 - 16 = 9$ and roots $\dfrac{5 \pm 3}{4}$, that is, $2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$. At $x = 1$ the line equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and the hyperbola $1$, therefore between the intersections the line lies above. The interval $\left[ \dfrac{1}{2}, 2 \right]$ lies in $(0, +\infty)$, where $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is continuous and has antiderivative $\ln x$. In the computation we use $\ln \dfrac{1}{2} = -\ln 2$, so the term $-\ln \dfrac{1}{2}$ inside the second parenthesis equals $+\ln 2$, and subtracting the parenthesis it becomes $-\ln 2$. The area equals $\dfrac{15}{8} - 2\ln 2$, about $0.49$.

</div>
