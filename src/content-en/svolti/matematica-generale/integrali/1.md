## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\int \left( 6x^2 - 4x + 3 - \frac{2}{x} \right) dx .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \left( 6x^2 - 4x + 3 - \frac{2}{x} \right) dx
&= 6 \int x^2\,dx - 4 \int x\,dx + 3 \int dx - 2 \int \frac{1}{x}\,dx \\[6pt]
&= 6 \cdot \frac{x^3}{3} - 4 \cdot \frac{x^2}{2} + 3x - 2 \ln \lvert x \rvert + c \\[6pt]
&= 2x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 2 \ln \lvert x \rvert + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **linearity** of the indefinite integral, each term can be integrated separately with any constant multiplier taken outside the integral. The powers $x^2$ and $x$ are integrated using $\dfrac{x^{a + 1}}{a + 1}$. The constant $3$ has antiderivative $3x$. The term $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is the only power excluded from that rule and has antiderivative $\ln \lvert x \rvert$. The individual constants of integration combine into a single constant $c$. As a check, differentiating the result gives the integrand $6x^2 - 4x + 3 - \dfrac{2}{x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute, for $x > 0$,
$$
\int \frac{(x + 1)^2}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx .
$$

$$
\frac{(x + 1)^2}{\sqrt{x}} = \frac{x^2 + 2x + 1}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}} = x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2\,x^{\frac{1}{2}} + x^{-\frac{1}{2}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \left( x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2\,x^{\frac{1}{2}} + x^{-\frac{1}{2}} \right) dx
&= \frac{x^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\dfrac{5}{2}} + 2 \cdot \frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\dfrac{3}{2}} + \frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\dfrac{1}{2}} + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{5}\,x^{\frac{5}{2}} + \frac{4}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2\sqrt{x} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quotient has no immediate antiderivative, so first rewrite it as a sum of powers. Expanding the square of the binomial and dividing each term by $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$ subtracts $\dfrac{1}{2}$ from the exponents. Each exponent differs from $-1$, so the **power rule for integration** applies. Dividing by $\dfrac{5}{2}$, $\dfrac{3}{2}$, and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $\dfrac{2}{5}$, $\dfrac{2}{3}$, and $2$, respectively. The last term can be written as $2\sqrt{x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\text{(a)} \ \int \frac{2x + 1}{x^2 + x + 5}\,dx,
\qquad
\text{(b)} \ \int x \left( x^2 - 4 \right)^5 dx .
$$

$$
\text{(a)} \quad \left( x^2 + x + 5 \right)' = 2x + 1,
\qquad
\int \frac{2x + 1}{x^2 + x + 5}\,dx = \ln\left( x^2 + x + 5 \right) + c
$$
$$
\text{(b)} \quad \int x \left( x^2 - 4 \right)^5 dx = \frac{1}{2} \int 2x \left( x^2 - 4 \right)^5 dx = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\left( x^2 - 4 \right)^6}{6} + c = \frac{\left( x^2 - 4 \right)^6}{12} + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator in the first integral is exactly the derivative of the denominator, so the integral has the **composite form** $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$ with $g(x) = x^2 + x + 5$. The discriminant of $g$ is $1 - 20 < 0$, and the coefficient of $x^2$ is positive. Therefore $g(x) > 0$ for every $x$, so the absolute value can be omitted.

In the second integral, the power $g(x)^5$ has inner function $g(x) = x^2 - 4$ with derivative $2x$. The factor $x$ in the integrand differs from the derivative by a factor of $2$, so multiply and divide by $2$ and apply the composite form $\displaystyle\int g^{a}\,g'\,dx = \dfrac{g^{a + 1}}{a + 1} + c$ with $a = 5$. Differentiating the result gives $\dfrac{6\left( x^2 - 4 \right)^5 \cdot 2x}{12} = x \left( x^2 - 4 \right)^5$, the original integrand.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the antiderivative $F$ of
$$
f(x) = \frac{6x}{3x^2 + 1} + e^{2x}
$$
such that $F(0) = 3$.

$$
\int \left( \frac{6x}{3x^2 + 1} + e^{2x} \right) dx = \ln\left( 3x^2 + 1 \right) + \frac{e^{2x}}{2} + c
$$
$$
F(0) = \ln 1 + \frac{1}{2} + c = \frac{1}{2} + c = 3
\iff
c = \frac{5}{2}
$$
$$
F(x) = \ln\left( 3x^2 + 1 \right) + \frac{e^{2x}}{2} + \frac{5}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivative of $3x^2 + 1$ is $6x$, so the first term has the composite form $\dfrac{g'}{g}$ and antiderivative $\ln\left( 3x^2 + 1 \right)$. The absolute value is unnecessary because $3x^2 + 1 > 0$. The second term has the form $e^{g(x)}\,g'(x)$ with $g(x) = 2x$ apart from the factor $2$, so its antiderivative is $\dfrac{e^{2x}}{2}$. By the theorem on **antiderivatives of the same function**, all antiderivatives of $f$ are obtained by adding a constant $c$, and $F(0) = 3$ determines that constant. Since $\ln 1 = 0$ and $e^{0} = 1$, the condition becomes $\dfrac{1}{2} + c = 3$, so $c = \dfrac{5}{2}$.

</div>
