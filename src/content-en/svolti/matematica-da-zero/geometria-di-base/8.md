## Exercise 1

A box shaped like a rectangular prism measures $20$ cm by $15$ cm by $10$ cm. Find its volume in cubic centimeters and liters.

$$
V = 20 \cdot 15 \cdot 10 = 3000\ \text{cm}^3
$$
$$
3000\ \text{cm}^3 = \frac{3000}{1000}\ \text{dm}^3 = 3\ \text{dm}^3 = 3\ \text{liters}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of a rectangular prism is the product of its three dimensions. Since $20 \cdot 15 = 300$ and $300 \cdot 10 = 3000$, its volume is $3000\ \text{cm}^3$. In simple terms, the bottom of the box holds $300$ cubes with edge length $1$ cm, and there are $10$ layers of cubes.

A cubic decimeter contains $1000$ cubic centimeters, so to convert cubic centimeters to cubic decimeters, divide by $1000$. This gives $3\ \text{dm}^3$, and since a liter is a cubic decimeter, the box holds $3$ liters.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A container is shaped like a cube with edge length $30$ cm. How many liters of water can it hold?

$$
30\ \text{cm} = 3\ \text{dm}
$$
$$
V = 3^3 = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 27\ \text{dm}^3 = 27\ \text{liters}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Expressing the edge length in decimeters gives the capacity directly in liters because one liter is one cubic decimeter. One decimeter equals $10$ cm, so $30$ cm equals $3$ dm.

The volume of the cube is its edge length cubed, or $3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 27$, so the volume is $27\ \text{dm}^3$ and the container holds $27$ liters. Using the edge length in centimeters gives $30 \cdot 30 \cdot 30 = 27\,000\ \text{cm}^3$, and dividing by $1000$ again gives $27$ liters.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pool shaped like a rectangular prism is $10$ m long, $5$ m wide, and $1.5$ m deep. A pump fills it at $25$ liters of water per minute. Find how many liters are needed to fill it and how many hours the pump takes.

$$
V = 10 \cdot 5 \cdot 1.5 = 75\ \text{m}^3
$$
$$
75\ \text{m}^3 = 75 \cdot 1000\ \text{liters} = 75\,000\ \text{liters}
$$
$$
\frac{75\,000}{25} = 3000\ \text{minutes}
$$
$$
\frac{3000}{60} = 50\ \text{hours}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of the pool is the product of its three dimensions. We have $10 \cdot 5 = 50$ and $50 \cdot 1.5 = 75$, so the volume is $75\ \text{m}^3$. One cubic meter contains $1000$ liters, so filling the pool takes $75\,000$ liters.

The pump delivers $25$ liters per minute, so the number of minutes is $\dfrac{75\,000}{25} = 3000$ because $25 \cdot 3000 = 75\,000$. One hour contains $60$ minutes, and $3000$ minutes is $\dfrac{3000}{60} = 50$ hours. The pump therefore takes $50$ hours.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A can is shaped like a cylinder with base radius $5$ cm and height $12$ cm. Find its volume in terms of $\pi$ and using $\pi \approx 3.14$, and express the approximate result in liters.

$$
V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \cdot 5^2 \cdot 12 = \pi \cdot 25 \cdot 12 = 300\pi\ \text{cm}^3
$$
$$
V \approx 300 \cdot 3.14 = 942\ \text{cm}^3
$$
$$
942\ \text{cm}^3 = \frac{942}{1000}\ \text{liters} = 0.942\ \text{liters}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of a cylinder is the base area times the height. The base is a disk of radius $5$ cm, so its area is $\pi \cdot 5^2 = 25\pi\ \text{cm}^2$. Multiplying by the height gives $25 \cdot 12 = 300$, so the volume is $300\pi\ \text{cm}^3$.

Using $\pi \approx 3.14$, the volume is $300 \cdot 3.14 = 942\ \text{cm}^3$. One liter contains $1000\ \text{cm}^3$, so divide by $1000$. The can holds approximately $0.942$ liters.

</div>
