## Exercise 1

The legs of a right triangle measure $12$ cm and $16$ cm. Find its hypotenuse.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= \sqrt{12^2 + 16^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{144 + 256} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{400} \\[6pt]
&= 20\ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the legs. The squares of the legs are $12 \cdot 12 = 144$ and $16 \cdot 16 = 256$, and their sum is $400$. The hypotenuse is therefore the number whose square is $400$, namely $\sqrt{400} = 20$, because $20 \cdot 20 = 400$.

Pythagorean triples give the same result. The leg lengths $12$ and $16$ are $4 \cdot 3$ and $4 \cdot 4$, so the side lengths are those of the triple $(3, 4, 5)$ multiplied by $4$, and the hypotenuse is $4 \cdot 5 = 20$. The hypotenuse measures $20$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A right triangle has a hypotenuse of $25$ cm and a leg of $7$ cm. Find the other leg.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \sqrt{25^2 - 7^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{625 - 49} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{576} \\[6pt]
&= 24\ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the Pythagorean theorem, $a^2 + 7^2 = 25^2$. The square of the unknown leg is therefore found by subtracting the square of the known leg from the square of the hypotenuse, giving $625 - 49 = 576$. The leg is therefore $\sqrt{576}$, and since $24 \cdot 24 = 576$, we have $\sqrt{576} = 24$.

The other leg measures $24$ cm. The result is less than the hypotenuse length, as expected because the hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangle has sides of $8$ cm and $15$ cm. Find its diagonal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{64 + 225} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{289} \\[6pt]
&= 17\ \text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonal divides the rectangle into two right triangles whose legs are the sides of the rectangle and whose hypotenuse is the diagonal. By the Pythagorean theorem, the square of the diagonal is $64 + 225 = 289$. Since $17 \cdot 17 = 289$, the diagonal measures $17$ cm. The three numbers $8$, $15$, and $17$ form one of the most common Pythagorean triples.

</div>

## Exercise 4

An isosceles triangle has a base of $16$ cm and equal sides of $10$ cm. Find its altitude to the base, its area, and its perimeter.

$$
\frac{16}{2} = 8\ \text{cm}
$$
$$
h = \sqrt{10^2 - 8^2} = \sqrt{100 - 64} = \sqrt{36} = 6\ \text{cm}
$$
$$
A = \frac{16 \cdot 6}{2} = \frac{96}{2} = 48\ \text{cm}^2
$$
$$
2p = 16 + 10 + 10 = 36\ \text{cm}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 16,0 8,6" segmenti="8,0,8,6" linee="8,0.6 8.6,0.6 8.6,0" etichette="3.4,-1,8 cm;11.4,-1,8 cm;8.3,3,6 cm;2.3,3.6,10 cm;12.4,3.6,10 cm" xmin=-1 xmax=17 ymin=-1.6 ymax=6.8 titolo="The altitude divides the isosceles triangle into two right triangles with hypotenuse $10$ cm and one leg $8$ cm, so the altitude is $6$ cm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In an isosceles triangle, the altitude to the base meets the midpoint of the base and divides the triangle into two congruent right triangles. In each, the hypotenuse is the equal side of $10$ cm, and one leg is half the base, or $8$ cm. The other leg is the altitude, and by the **Pythagorean theorem** its length is $\sqrt{100 - 64} = \sqrt{36} = 6$ cm.

Once the altitude is known, the area of the triangle is base times height divided by $2$. The product is $16 \cdot 6 = 96$ and half of it is $48$, so the area is $48\ \text{cm}^2$. The perimeter is the sum of the three side lengths, or $16 + 10 + 10 = 36$ cm.

</div>
