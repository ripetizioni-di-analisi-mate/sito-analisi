## Exercise 1

Two angles of a triangle measure $35^\circ$ and $85^\circ$. Find the third angle and classify the triangle by its angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma &= 180^\circ - (35^\circ + 85^\circ) \\[6pt]
&= 180^\circ - 120^\circ \\[6pt]
&= 60^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In every triangle, the **sum of the interior angles** is $180^\circ$. The third angle is therefore found by subtracting the sum of the two known angles from $180^\circ$. Their sum is $35 + 85 = 120$, and $180 - 120 = 60$, so the third angle measures $60^\circ$.

The angles measure $35^\circ$, $85^\circ$, and $60^\circ$, all less than $90^\circ$. The triangle therefore has three acute angles, so it is an **acute triangle**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One acute angle of a right triangle measures $28^\circ$. Find the other acute angle.

$$
\text{other acute angle} = 90^\circ - 28^\circ = 62^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangle is right, so one of its angles measures $90^\circ$. The other two angles add up to $180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$, so they are complementary. The required angle is therefore the complement of $28^\circ$, which measures $90^\circ - 28^\circ = 62^\circ$.

The sum of all three angles gives the same result. Indeed, $90 + 28 = 118$ and $180 - 118 = 62$. The other acute angle measures $62^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In an isosceles triangle, each base angle measures $35^\circ$. Find the angle opposite the base and classify the triangle by its angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{angle opposite the base} &= 180^\circ - 2 \cdot 35^\circ \\[6pt]
&= 180^\circ - 70^\circ \\[6pt]
&= 110^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$
::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 8,0 4,2.801" etichette="1.2,0.35,35°;6.8,0.35,35°;4,1.9,110°;4,-0.5,base" xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-1.2 ymax=3.6 titolo="An isosceles triangle with base angles of $35^\circ$ and a vertex angle of $110^\circ$"}


<div class="spiegazione">

The triangle is isosceles, so the two base angles are equal and both measure $35^\circ$. Their sum is $2 \cdot 35^\circ = 70^\circ$. Since the sum of the three angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$, the angle opposite the base measures $180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$.

The $110^\circ$ angle is greater than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$, so it is obtuse. The triangle is therefore **obtuse** as well as isosceles.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A right trapezoid has two right angles, and one of the other two angles measures $65^\circ$. Find the fourth angle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta &= 360^\circ - (90^\circ + 90^\circ + 65^\circ) \\[6pt]
&= 360^\circ - 245^\circ \\[6pt]
&= 115^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A trapezoid is a quadrilateral, and the **sum of the interior angles** of a quadrilateral is $360^\circ$. The three known angles are the two right angles and the $65^\circ$ angle, and their sum is $90 + 90 + 65 = 245$. The fourth angle therefore measures $360^\circ - 245^\circ = 115^\circ$.

To check, add the four angles. The result is $90 + 90 + 65 + 115 = 360$, the sum of the angles of a quadrilateral.

</div>
