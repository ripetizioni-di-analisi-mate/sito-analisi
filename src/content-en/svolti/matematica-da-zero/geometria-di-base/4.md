## Exercise 1

A parallelogram has base $12$ cm, height $5$ cm, and a slanted side of $6$ cm. Find its area and perimeter.

$$
A = b \cdot h = 12 \cdot 5 = 60\ \text{cm}^2
$$
$$
2p = 2 \cdot (12 + 6) = 2 \cdot 18 = 36\ \text{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a parallelogram is base times height, as for a rectangle, because the parallelogram can be rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and height. Thus its area is $A = 12 \cdot 5 = 60\ \text{cm}^2$. The slanted side is not used to calculate the area.

The perimeter, by contrast, is the sum of the four side lengths. The opposite sides of the parallelogram are equal, so it has two sides of $12$ cm and two slanted sides of $6$ cm. Its perimeter is therefore twice $12 + 6 = 18$, or $36$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A triangle has area $30\ \text{cm}^2$ and base $12$ cm. Find the altitude to that base.

$$
\frac{12 \cdot h}{2} = 30
$$
$$
12 \cdot h = 2 \cdot 30 = 60
$$
$$
h = \frac{60}{12} = 5\ \text{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a triangle is base times height divided by $2$. If half of the product $12 \cdot h$ equals $30$, the full product is twice that, or $60$. The height is therefore the number that, when multiplied by $12$, gives $60$. Since $12 \cdot 5 = 60$, the height is $5$ cm.

To check, recalculate the area using $h = 5$. The result is $\dfrac{12 \cdot 5}{2} = \dfrac{60}{2} = 30$, the given area.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rhombus has diagonals of $12$ cm and $9$ cm. Find its area.

$$
A = \frac{D \cdot d}{2} = \frac{12 \cdot 9}{2} = \frac{108}{2} = 54\ \text{cm}^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rhombus occupies half of the rectangle whose side lengths are the lengths of its diagonals, so its area is the product of the diagonals divided by $2$. Their product is $12 \cdot 9 = 108$, and half of $108$ is $54$. The area of the rhombus is therefore $54\ \text{cm}^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A trapezoid has a longer base of $14$ cm, a shorter base of $6$ cm, and a height of $5$ cm. Find its area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{(B + b) \cdot h}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(14 + 6) \cdot 5}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{20 \cdot 5}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{100}{2} = 50\ \text{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 14,0 11,5 5,5" segmenti="5,0,5,5" linee="5,0.5 5.5,0.5 5.5,0" etichette="6.4,-1,14 cm;7.4,5.4,6 cm;3.3,2.3,5 cm" xmin=-0.8 xmax=14.8 ymin=-1.6 ymax=6.2 titolo="The trapezoid with bases of $14$ cm and $6$ cm and height $5$ cm has area $\dfrac{(14 + 6) \cdot 5}{2} = 50\ \text{cm}^2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A trapezoid and a copy rotated through half a turn form a parallelogram with base $B + b$ and height $h$. The area of the trapezoid is therefore half the area of this parallelogram, or $\dfrac{(B + b) \cdot h}{2}$.

The sum of the bases is $14 + 6 = 20$, and multiplying by the height gives $20 \cdot 5 = 100$. Half of $100$ is $50$, so the area of the trapezoid is $50\ \text{cm}^2$.

</div>
