## Exercise 1

A rectangular garden measures $25$ m by $18$ m and needs a fence around its entire boundary. The fencing costs $3$ euros per meter. Calculate the cost of the fencing.

$$
2p = 2 \cdot (25 + 18) = 2 \cdot 43 = 86\ \text{m}
$$
$$
\text{cost} = 86 \cdot 3 = 258\ \text{euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fencing runs around the entire garden, so its length equals the perimeter of the rectangle. The sum of the base and height is $25 + 18 = 43$, and the perimeter is twice that, or $86$ m.

Each meter of fencing costs $3$ euros, so the cost is $86 \cdot 3$. Since $80 \cdot 3 = 240$ and $6 \cdot 3 = 18$, the cost is $240 + 18 = 258$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The floor of a rectangular room measures $5$ m by $4$ m. It will be covered with square tiles of side length $25$ cm. Calculate the number of tiles needed.

$$
5\ \text{m} = 500\ \text{cm}, \qquad 4\ \text{m} = 400\ \text{cm}
$$
$$
\frac{500}{25} = 20 \ \text{tiles along one side}
$$
$$
\frac{400}{25} = 16 \ \text{tiles along the other side}
$$
$$
20 \cdot 16 = 320 \ \text{tiles}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 5,0 5,4 0,4" segmenti="1,0,1,4;2,0,2,4;3,0,3,4;4,0,4,4;0,1,5,1;0,2,5,2;0,3,5,3" etichette="2.1,-0.6,5 m;5.2,1.9,4 m" xmin=-0.4 xmax=6.2 ymin=-1 ymax=4.4 titolo="Each dashed square has side length $1$ m and contains $4 \cdot 4 = 16$ tiles of side length $25$ cm. There are $5 \cdot 4 = 20$ squares, so there are $20 \cdot 16 = 320$ tiles"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The room and tile dimensions are given in different units, so convert the room dimensions to centimeters. To convert meters to centimeters, multiply by $100$, so the room measures $500$ cm by $400$ cm.

The $500$ cm side fits $\dfrac{500}{25} = 20$ tiles because $25 \cdot 20 = 500$. The $400$ cm side fits $\dfrac{400}{25} = 16$ tiles because $25 \cdot 16 = 400$. The tiles therefore form $16$ rows of $20$, for a total of $20 \cdot 16 = 320$.

The areas give the same result. The room has area $500 \cdot 400 = 200\,000\ \text{cm}^2$ and one tile has area $25 \cdot 25 = 625\ \text{cm}^2$. The quotient $\dfrac{200\,000}{625}$ equals $320$ because $625 \cdot 320 = 200\,000$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A flower bed is shaped like a disk with radius $3$ m. Using $\pi \approx 3.14$, find the length of its boundary and its area. One bag of soil covers $10\ \text{m}^2$. Find the number of bags needed to cover the entire flower bed.

$$
C = 2\pi r \approx 2 \cdot 3.14 \cdot 3 = 18.84\ \text{m}
$$
$$
A = \pi r^2 \approx 3.14 \cdot 9 = 28.26\ \text{m}^2
$$
$$
\frac{28.26}{10} = 2.826
$$
$$
\text{bags needed} = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The boundary of the flower bed is a circle, and its length is $2\pi r$. The product $2 \cdot 3$ equals $6$. Since $6 \cdot 3 = 18$ and $6 \cdot 0.14 = 0.84$, we have $6 \cdot 3.14 = 18.84$, so the boundary is approximately $18.84$ m long.

The area of the disk is $\pi r^2$, and the square of the radius is $3 \cdot 3 = 9$. Since $9 \cdot 3 = 27$ and $9 \cdot 0.14 = 1.26$, we have $9 \cdot 3.14 = 28.26$, so the flower bed has an area of approximately $28.26\ \text{m}^2$.

One bag covers $10\ \text{m}^2$, so the number of bags is found by dividing the area by $10$, giving $2.826$. Two bags cover only $20\ \text{m}^2$, less than the area of the flower bed, so $3$ bags are needed.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A ladder $5$ m long leans against a vertical wall, with its foot on a level floor $1.4$ m from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach?

$$
\begin{aligned}
h &= \sqrt{5^2 - 1.4^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{25 - 1.96} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{23.04} \\[6pt]
&= 4.8\ \text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 1.4,0 0,4.8" linee="-1,0 3,0;0,0 0,5.4;1.4,0 0,4.8;0,0.3 0.3,0.3 0.3,0" etichette="0.3,-0.5,1.4 m;-0.6,2.3,h;0.95,2.6,5 m" xmin=-3 xmax=4.6 ymin=-0.9 ymax=5.6 titolo="The ladder, the wall, and the floor form a right triangle with hypotenuse $5$ m and one leg $1.4$ m"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The wall is vertical and the floor is horizontal, so they form a right angle. The ladder, the section of wall below its top, and the section of floor between the wall and its foot therefore form a right triangle. The ladder is the hypotenuse, of length $5$ m. The distance from the wall is one leg of length $1.4$ m, and the required height is the other leg.

By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the square of the height is $5^2 - 1.4^2$. We have $5^2 = 25$ and $1.4^2 = 1.96$ because $14 \cdot 14 = 196$. The difference is $25 - 1.96 = 23.04$. Since $48 \cdot 48 = 2304$, we have $4.8 \cdot 4.8 = 23.04$, so the ladder reaches a height of $4.8$ m.

</div>
