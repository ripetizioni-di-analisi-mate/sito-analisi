## Exercise 1

Find the complement and supplement of an angle of $27^\circ$.

$$
\text{complement} = 90^\circ - 27^\circ = 63^\circ
$$
$$
\text{supplement} = 180^\circ - 27^\circ = 153^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two angles are complementary if their sum is $90^\circ$. The complement of $27^\circ$ is therefore the angle that must be added to $27^\circ$ to reach $90^\circ$, namely $90^\circ - 27^\circ$. Since $90 - 20 = 70$ and $70 - 7 = 63$, the complement measures $63^\circ$.

Two angles are supplementary if their sum is $180^\circ$, so the supplement of $27^\circ$ is $180^\circ - 27^\circ$. Since $180 - 20 = 160$ and $160 - 7 = 153$, the supplement measures $153^\circ$. The supplement exceeds the complement by $90^\circ$, and indeed $153 - 63 = 90$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two angles are supplementary and one is three times the other. Find their measures.

$$
\text{smaller angle} + \text{larger angle} = 180^\circ
$$
$$
1 + 3 = 4 \ \text{equal parts}
$$
$$
\text{one part} = \frac{180^\circ}{4} = 45^\circ
$$
$$
\text{larger angle} = 3 \cdot 45^\circ = 135^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two angles are supplementary, so their sum is $180^\circ$. If the smaller angle represents one part, the larger angle, which is three times as large, represents $3$ equal parts. Together the two angles therefore make up $1 + 3 = 4$ equal parts, and these $4$ parts measure $180^\circ$.

Each part therefore measures $\dfrac{180^\circ}{4} = 45^\circ$ because $4 \cdot 45 = 180$. The smaller angle represents one part, or $45^\circ$, and the larger angle measures $3 \cdot 45^\circ = 135^\circ$. The angles measure $45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$, and their sum is $45 + 135 = 180$, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two lines intersect and form four angles. One of them measures $72^\circ$. Find the measures of the other three.

$$
\text{adjacent angle} = 180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ
$$
$$
\text{angle opposite } 72^\circ = 72^\circ
$$
$$
\text{angle opposite } 108^\circ = 108^\circ
$$
::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-4,0 4,0;-1.236,-3.804 1.236,3.804" etichette="1.3,0.9,72°;-0.95,1.3,108°;-1.3,-0.95,72°;0.95,-1.3,108°" xmin=-4.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-4.2 ymax=4.2 titolo="Two intersecting lines form angles of $72^\circ$ and $108^\circ$. Vertical angles are equal and adjacent angles are supplementary"}


<div class="spiegazione">

The $72^\circ$ angle shares a side with each of its two adjacent angles, and in each pair the other two sides form a line. Together the angles in each pair therefore form a straight angle, so each adjacent angle measures $180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ$.

The fourth angle is vertically opposite the $72^\circ$ angle. By the property of **vertical angles**, the two angles are equal, so the fourth angle also measures $72^\circ$. The other three angles therefore measure $108^\circ$, $72^\circ$, and $108^\circ$. The four angles add up to $72 + 108 + 72 + 108 = 360$, a full turn.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Two intersecting lines form four angles. Two vertical angles have a sum of $140^\circ$. Find the measures of all four angles and identify which are acute and which are obtuse.

$$
\text{each vertical angle} = \frac{140^\circ}{2} = 70^\circ
$$
$$
\text{each of the other two} = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Vertical angles are equal, so the sum of $140^\circ$ consists of two angles of equal measure. Each therefore measures half of $140^\circ$, or $70^\circ$.

Each of the other two angles is adjacent to an angle of $70^\circ$ and forms a straight angle with it, so each measures $180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$. The four angles therefore measure $70^\circ$, $110^\circ$, $70^\circ$, and $110^\circ$. The $70^\circ$ angles are acute because they measure less than $90^\circ$, and the $110^\circ$ angles are obtuse because they measure more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$.

</div>
