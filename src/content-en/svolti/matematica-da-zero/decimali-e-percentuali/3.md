## Exercise 1

Convert the fraction $\dfrac{3}{8}$ to a decimal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
30 : 8 &= 3 \quad \text{with remainder } 6 \\[6pt]
60 : 8 &= 7 \quad \text{with remainder } 4 \\[6pt]
40 : 8 &= 5 \quad \text{with remainder } 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{3}{8} = 0.375
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction $\dfrac{3}{8}$ is the quotient $3 : 8$. The number $3$ is less than $8$, so the whole-number part is $0$. Continue with the tenths, writing $3$ whole units as $30$ tenths.

Since $8 \cdot 3 = 24$, the division $30 : 8$ gives $3$ tenths with remainder $30 - 24 = 6$. The remaining $6$ tenths are $60$ hundredths, and $60 : 8$ gives $7$ hundredths with remainder $60 - 56 = 4$. The remaining $4$ hundredths are $40$ thousandths, and $40 : 8 = 5$ with remainder $0$.

The remainder is $0$, so the division ends and $\dfrac{3}{8} = 0.375$ is a **terminating decimal**. The same conclusion follows from the rule about denominators because the only prime factor of $8 = 2^3$ is $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Without dividing, determine whether the fractions $\dfrac{9}{15}$ and $\dfrac{7}{12}$ give terminating or repeating decimals. Then divide to check.

$$
\frac{9}{15} = \frac{3}{5}
\qquad\qquad
12 = 2^2 \cdot 3
$$
$$
\frac{3}{5} = 0.6
\qquad\qquad
\frac{7}{12} = 0.58333\ldots = 0.58\overline{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rule for distinguishing terminating and repeating decimals applies to a fraction **in lowest terms**. The fraction $\dfrac{9}{15}$ simplifies when its numerator and denominator are divided by $3$, giving $\dfrac{3}{5}$. The denominator $5$ has only one prime factor, $5$, so the decimal **terminates**. Indeed, $\dfrac{3}{5} = 0.6$ because $30$ tenths divided by $5$ gives $6$ tenths.

The fraction $\dfrac{7}{12}$ is already in lowest terms because $7$ is prime and does not divide $12$. The denominator $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$ contains the factor $3$, so the decimal **repeats**.

In the division $7 : 12$, we obtain $70 : 12 = 5$ with remainder $10$, then $100 : 12 = 8$ with remainder $4$, then $40 : 12 = 3$ with remainder $4$. The remainder $4$ recurs, so the digit $3$ repeats from this point on. The number is $0.58\overline{3}$, a mixed repeating decimal with nonrepeating part $58$ and repeating block $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Convert the fraction $\dfrac{5}{11}$ to a decimal and identify its repeating block.

$$
\begin{aligned}
50 : 11 &= 4 \quad \text{with remainder } 6 \\[6pt]
60 : 11 &= 5 \quad \text{with remainder } 5 \\[6pt]
50 : 11 &= 4 \quad \text{with remainder } 6
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{5}{11} = 0.4545\ldots = 0.\overline{45}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator $5$ is less than $11$, so the whole-number part is $0$. Start by dividing $50$ tenths by $11$. Since $11 \cdot 4 = 44$, this gives the digit $4$ with remainder $6$. Then $60 : 11$ gives the digit $5$ with remainder $5$ because $11 \cdot 5 = 55$.

The remainder $5$ equals the original numerator, so division starts again at $50 : 11$ and the digits $4$ and $5$ repeat indefinitely. Therefore $\dfrac{5}{11}$ is a **pure repeating** decimal with repeating block $45$ and is written $0.\overline{45}$. The block begins immediately after the decimal point, so there is no nonrepeating part.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Convert the fraction $\dfrac{13}{6}$ to a decimal and identify the whole-number part, the nonrepeating part and the repeating block.

$$
13 : 6 = 2 \quad \text{with remainder } 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
10 : 6 &= 1 \quad \text{with remainder } 4 \\[6pt]
40 : 6 &= 6 \quad \text{with remainder } 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{13}{6} = 2.1666\ldots = 2.1\overline{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is greater than the denominator, so the fraction is greater than $1$. Since $6 \cdot 2 = 12$, the division $13 : 6$ gives a whole-number part of $2$ with remainder $1$.

The remainder $1$ equals $10$ tenths, and $10 : 6$ gives the digit $1$ with remainder $4$. Then $40$ hundredths divided by $6$ gives the digit $6$ with remainder $4$ because $6 \cdot 6 = 36$. The remainder $4$ recurs, so the digit is always $6$ from this point on.

The number $2.1\overline{6}$ is a **mixed** repeating decimal, with whole-number part $2$, nonrepeating part $1$ and repeating block $6$. The denominator $6 = 2 \cdot 3$ contains the factor $3$, which agrees with the fact that the decimal repeats.

</div>
