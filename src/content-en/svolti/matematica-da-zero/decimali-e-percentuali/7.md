## Exercise 1

On a map with scale $1 : 25\,000$, two mountain huts are $7.2$ cm apart. Find the actual distance in kilometers.

$$
7.2 \cdot 25\,000 = 180\,000 \text{ cm}
$$
$$
180\,000 \text{ cm} = 1800 \text{ m} = 1.8 \text{ km}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **scale** $1 : 25\,000$ means that each centimeter on the map corresponds to $25\,000$ centimeters on the ground. The actual distance is therefore $25\,000$ times the measured distance. In the product $7.2 \cdot 25\,000$, multiply the first factor by $10$ and divide the second by $10$, leaving the product unchanged. This gives $72 \cdot 2500$, which is $72 \cdot 25 = 1800$ multiplied by $100$, or $180\,000$ cm.

Since $1$ m $= 100$ cm, dividing by $100$ gives $1800$ m. Since $1$ km $= 1000$ m, dividing again by $1000$ gives $1.8$ km. The two huts are therefore **separated by** $1.8$ km.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A recipe for $4$ people calls for $300$ g of flour and $180$ g of sugar. Find the quantities needed for $6$ people.

$$
4 : 300 = 6 : x
\qquad
x = \frac{300 \cdot 6}{4} = \frac{1800}{4} = 450 \text{ g}
$$
$$
4 : 180 = 6 : y
\qquad
y = \frac{180 \cdot 6}{4} = \frac{1080}{4} = 270 \text{ g}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of people and the amount of each ingredient are **directly proportional** because doubling the number of people doubles each amount. By the rule of three, the values are in the same ratio, so the amount of flour $x$ for $6$ people satisfies $4 : 300 = 6 : x$.

The term $x$ is an extreme, so it equals the product of the means $300 \cdot 6 = 1800$ divided by the other extreme $4$, or $450$ g. Similarly, the amount of sugar is $y = \dfrac{1080}{4} = 270$ g.

The same result follows by observing that a group of $6$ people is $\dfrac{6}{4} = 1.5$ times as large as a group of $4$ people. Indeed, $300 \cdot 1.5 = 450$ and $180 \cdot 1.5 = 270$. For $6$ people the amounts **needed** are therefore $450$ g of flour and $270$ g of sugar.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A car uses $6$ liters of fuel per $100$ km. Find how many liters it uses to travel $350$ km and how many kilometers it can travel on $45$ liters.

$$
100 : 6 = 350 : x
\qquad
x = \frac{6 \cdot 350}{100} = \frac{2100}{100} = 21 \text{ liters}
$$
$$
6 : 100 = 45 : y
\qquad
y = \frac{100 \cdot 45}{6} = \frac{4500}{6} = 750 \text{ km}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At a constant fuel consumption rate, distance traveled in kilometers and fuel used in liters are **directly proportional**. In the first proportion, $x$ is an extreme, so it equals the product of the means $6 \cdot 350 = 2100$ divided by $100$, giving $21$ liters.

In the second question, the amount of fuel is known and the distance is unknown. The ratios of liters to kilometers are equal, so $6 : 100 = 45 : y$. The term $y$ is an extreme, so $y = \dfrac{4500}{6} = 750$ km because $6 \cdot 750 = 4500$. The car **uses** $21$ liters for $350$ km and **travels** $750$ km on $45$ liters.

</div>

## Exercise 4

It takes $24$ square tiles with sides $25$ cm long to cover the length of a wall when placed side by side. Find how many tiles would cover the same length if their sides were $30$ cm long.

$$
24 \cdot 25 = 600 \text{ cm}
$$
$$
30 \cdot y = 600
\qquad
y = \frac{600}{30} = 20
$$

::grafico{f="600/x+0*sqrt(x)" punti="25,24,pieno;30,20,pieno" segmenti="25,0,25,24;30,0,30,20" xmin=-10 xmax=90 ymin=-5 ymax=40 nomi="x,y" legenda="y = \dfrac{600}{x}" titolo="The number $y$ of tiles as a function of the side length $x$ in centimeters for a wall $600$ cm long. With $x = 25$, it takes $24$ tiles, and with $x = 30$, it takes $20$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The wall has the same length in both cases. Therefore the side length of the tiles and the number of tiles are **inversely proportional** because fewer tiles are needed when the tiles are larger, and their product is the length of the wall.

With the first tiles, the length is $24 \cdot 25 = 600$ cm. With the new tiles, the number $y$ must satisfy $30 \cdot y = 600$, so $y = \dfrac{600}{30} = 20$. The number **needed** is $20$ tiles, fewer than $24$, because the new tiles are larger.

</div>
