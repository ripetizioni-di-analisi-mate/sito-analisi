## Exercise 1

A jacket costs $120$ euros and is on sale at a discount of $25\%$. Find the discounted price.

$$
1 - \frac{25}{100} = 1 - 0.25 = 0.75
$$
$$
120 \cdot 0.75 = 90 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A discount of $25\%$ multiplies the price by the **multiplier** $1 - 0.25 = 0.75$. Since $0.75 = \dfrac{3}{4}$, the product $120 \cdot 0.75$ equals three quarters of $120$. One quarter of $120$ is $30$, so three quarters is $90$.

The same result follows by calculating the discount and subtracting it. Taking $25\%$ of $120$ gives $\dfrac{25 \cdot 120}{100} = 30$ euros, and $120 - 30 = 90$. The **discounted price** is $90$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bill rises from $75$ euros to $84$ euros. Find the percentage increase.

$$
84 - 75 = 9
$$
$$
\frac{9}{75} = \frac{3}{25} = \frac{3 \cdot 4}{25 \cdot 4} = \frac{12}{100}
$$
$$
\frac{9}{75} \cdot 100 = \frac{12}{100} \cdot 100 = 12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **percentage change** is found by dividing the difference between the final and initial values by the *initial* value and multiplying by $100$. The difference is $84 - 75 = 9$ euros, and the initial value is $75$ euros.

The fraction $\dfrac{9}{75}$ simplifies on dividing numerator and denominator by $3$, giving $\dfrac{3}{25}$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $4$ gives the equivalent fraction $\dfrac{12}{100}$ with denominator $100$. Therefore $\dfrac{9}{75} \cdot 100 = 12$, and indeed $75 \cdot 12 = 900$. The change is positive, so the bill **increased** by $12\%$. As a check, $12\%$ of $75$ is $\dfrac{12 \cdot 75}{100} = 9$ and $75 + 9 = 84$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

After a $10\%$ increase, the price of a subscription is $77$ euros. Find the price before the increase.

$$
V \cdot 1.1 = 77
$$
$$
V = \frac{77}{1.1} = \frac{770}{11} = 70 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An increase of $10\%$ multiplies the original price $V$ by the factor $1 + 0.1 = 1.1$. The final price is therefore $V \cdot 1.1 = 77$, so $V$ can be found by dividing $77$ by $1.1$.

To divide the decimals, multiply the dividend and divisor by $10$, giving $\dfrac{770}{11} = 70$. The **original price** was $70$ euros. The check confirms the result because $10\%$ of $70$ is $7$ and $70 + 7 = 77$.

Subtracting $10\%$ from $77$ euros instead gives $77 - 7.70 = 69.30$ euros, which differs from the original price. This is because the $10\%$ increase was calculated on $70$ euros, while $10\%$ of $77$ euros is a larger amount.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A television costs $500$ euros. The store applies a discount of $20\%$ and then a further discount of $15\%$ to the already discounted price. Calculate the final price and the overall percentage discount.

$$
500 \cdot 0.8 = 400 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
400 \cdot 0.85 = 340 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
0.8 \cdot 0.85 = 0.68
\qquad
1 - 0.68 = 0.32 = 32\%
$$

The $15\%$ discount applies to $400$ euros and brings the price to $340$ euros"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two changes are **successive percentage changes**, so the price is multiplied first by one factor and then by the other. The first discount has a multiplier of $1 - 0.20 = 0.8$ and brings the price to $500 \cdot 0.8 = 400$ euros. The second discount has a multiplier of $1 - 0.15 = 0.85$ and applies to $400$ euros. Taking $15\%$ of $400$ gives $60$ euros, so the final price is $400 - 60 = 340$ euros.

The overall multiplier is the product of the two multipliers. Since $8 \cdot 85 = 680$ and the factors have three decimal places in all, their product is $0.680 = 0.68$. The final price is therefore $68\%$ of the original price, and the overall discount is $100\% - 68\% = 32\%$. Indeed, $32\%$ of $500$ is $160$ euros, and $500 - 160 = 340$.

The **overall discount** is $32\%$, less than the sum $20\% + 15\% = 35\%$, because the second discount applies to a price that has already been reduced.

</div>
