## Exercise 1

Calculate
$$
7.25 + 3.8 - 4.6 .
$$

$$
\begin{array}{r}
7.25 \\
+\ \ 3.80 \\
\hline
11.05
\end{array}
\qquad\qquad
\begin{array}{r}
11.05 \\
-\ \ 4.60 \\
\hline
6.45
\end{array}
$$
$$
7.25 + 3.8 - 4.6 = 6.45
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Addition and subtraction have the same precedence, so they are performed in the order written, from left to right. To align the decimal points in columns, write $3.8$ as $3.80$ so that both addends have two decimal places.

In the addition, the hundredths give $5 + 0 = 5$. The tenths give $2 + 8 = 10$, or $10$ tenths, which make one whole. Therefore, write $0$ in the tenths column and carry $1$ to the ones column. The ones then give $7 + 3 + 1 = 11$, and the sum is $11.05$.

For the subtraction, write $4.6$ as $4.60$. The hundredths give $5 - 0 = 5$. In the tenths column, $0 - 6$ cannot be performed as written, so borrow one whole, worth $10$ tenths, and calculate $10 - 6 = 4$. There are now $10$ ones left, and $10 - 4 = 6$. The **result** is $6.45$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate
$$
1.25 \cdot 3.2 .
$$

$$
125 \cdot 32 = 4000
$$
$$
1.25 \cdot 3.2 = 4.000 = 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the rule for **multiplying** decimals, multiply the numbers without their decimal points, namely $125$ and $32$. To find the product, split $32$ into $30 + 2$ and apply the distributive property. Since $125 \cdot 30 = 3750$ and $125 \cdot 2 = 250$, we have $125 \cdot 32 = 3750 + 250 = 4000$.

The factor $1.25$ has two decimal places and the factor $3.2$ has one, so the product has $2 + 1 = 3$ decimal places. Thus $4000$ becomes $4.000$. Zeros at the end of the fractional part do not change the number, so the **product** is $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate
$$
\frac{6.3}{0.09} .
$$

$$
\frac{6.3}{0.09} = \frac{6.3 \cdot 100}{0.09 \cdot 100} = \frac{630}{9} = 70
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisor $0.09$ has two decimal places. Multiplying it by $100$ moves the decimal point two places to the right and gives $9$, a natural number. The dividend must be multiplied by the same number because the invariance property of division ensures that the quotient stays the same. Thus $6.3 \cdot 100 = 630$, with a zero added.

This leaves the division of natural numbers $\dfrac{630}{9}$. Since $9 \cdot 70 = 630$, the **quotient** is $70$. The result is greater than $6.3$, as always happens when a positive number is divided by a decimal between $0$ and $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate
$$
(2.5 + 0.7) \cdot 0.5 - \frac{0.36}{0.4} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2.5 + 0.7) \cdot 0.5 - \frac{0.36}{0.4}
&= 3.2 \cdot 0.5 - \frac{0.36}{0.4} \\[6pt]
&= 1.6 - \frac{3.6}{4} \\[6pt]
&= 1.6 - 0.9 \\[6pt]
&= 0.7
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the order of operations, first calculate the sum $2.5 + 0.7 = 3.2$ because $5$ tenths plus $7$ tenths gives $12$ tenths, or $1$ whole and $2$ tenths.

Next perform the multiplication and division. For the product $3.2 \cdot 0.5$, calculate $32 \cdot 5 = 160$. The factors have two decimal places in all, so the product is $1.60 = 1.6$. For the division, multiply both the dividend and divisor by $10$ to obtain $\dfrac{3.6}{4}$. Since $3.6$ is $36$ tenths and $\dfrac{36}{4} = 9$, the quotient is $9$ tenths, or $0.9$.

Finally, $1.6 - 0.9$ is $16$ tenths minus $9$ tenths, or $7$ tenths. The **value** of the expression is $0.7$.

</div>
