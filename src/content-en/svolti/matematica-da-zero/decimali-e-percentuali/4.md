## Exercise 1

Write each of the numbers $0.48$ and $3.125$ as a fraction in lowest terms.

$$
0.48 = \frac{48}{100} = \frac{12}{25}
$$
$$
3.125 = \frac{3125}{1000} = \frac{125}{40} = \frac{25}{8}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $0.48$ has two decimal places, so it equals $48$ hundredths, or $\dfrac{48}{100}$. The numerator and denominator are both divisible by $4$ because $48 = 4 \cdot 12$ and $100 = 4 \cdot 25$. Dividing by $4$ gives $\dfrac{12}{25}$, which is in lowest terms because $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$ and $25 = 5^2$ have no common prime factors.

The number $3.125$ has three decimal places, so it equals $\dfrac{3125}{1000}$. Dividing first by $25$ gives $\dfrac{3125}{25} = 125$ and $\dfrac{1000}{25} = 40$. Then dividing by $5$ gives $\dfrac{125}{5} = 25$ and $\dfrac{40}{5} = 8$. The fraction **in lowest terms** is $\dfrac{25}{8}$ because $25 = 5^2$ and $8 = 2^3$ have no common factors other than one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the fractions equivalent to $0.\overline{36}$ and $2.\overline{7}$.

$$
0.\overline{36} = \frac{36}{99} = \frac{4}{11}
$$
$$
2.\overline{7} = \frac{27 - 2}{9} = \frac{25}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $0.\overline{36}$ is a pure repeating decimal with a two-digit repeating block. By the rule for finding the **equivalent fraction**, the numerator is the number written without its decimal point up to the end of the first block, namely $036 = 36$, minus the number formed by the digits before the block, namely $0$. The denominator consists of two copies of $9$, one for each digit in the block, and has no zeros because there is no nonrepeating part. This gives $\dfrac{36}{99}$, which reduces on division by $9$ to $\dfrac{4}{11}$.

The number $2.\overline{7}$ has whole-number part $2$ and repeating block $7$. Written without the point up to the end of the first block, the number is $27$, and the digits before the block form the number $2$, so the numerator is $27 - 2 = 25$. The block contains one digit, so the denominator is $9$. The equivalent fraction is $\dfrac{25}{9}$, already in lowest terms.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the fractions equivalent to $0.1\overline{6}$ and $1.2\overline{45}$.

$$
0.1\overline{6} = \frac{16 - 1}{90} = \frac{15}{90} = \frac{1}{6}
$$
$$
1.2\overline{45} = \frac{1245 - 12}{990} = \frac{1233}{990} = \frac{137}{110}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $0.1\overline{6}$ is a **mixed** repeating decimal with nonrepeating part $1$ and repeating block $6$. Written without the point up to the end of the first block, the number is $016 = 16$, and the digits before the block form the number $01 = 1$, so the numerator is $16 - 1 = 15$. The repeating block and the nonrepeating part each have one digit, so the denominator is a $9$ followed by a $0$, or $90$. The fraction $\dfrac{15}{90}$ simplifies on division by $15$ to give $\dfrac{1}{6}$.

In $1.2\overline{45}$ the whole-number part is $1$, the nonrepeating part is $2$ and the repeating block is $45$. Written without the point up to the end of the first block, the number is $1245$, and the digits before the block form $12$, so the numerator is $1245 - 12 = 1233$. The block has two digits and the nonrepeating part has one, so the denominator is $990$. The numerator and denominator are divisible by $9$ because the digit sums are $9$ for $1233$ and $18$ for $990$, both multiples of $9$. Since $1233 : 9 = 137$ and $990 : 9 = 110$, we obtain $\dfrac{137}{110}$. This fraction is in lowest terms because $110 = 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 11$ and $137$ is divisible by none of $2$, $5$ and $11$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Evaluate the expression
$$
0.\overline{3} + 1.\overline{6} \cdot 0.6 .
$$

$$
0.\overline{3} = \frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}
\qquad
1.\overline{6} = \frac{16 - 1}{9} = \frac{15}{9} = \frac{5}{3}
\qquad
0.6 = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3} + \frac{5}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{5}
&= \frac{1}{3} + \frac{15}{15} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{3} + 1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Repeating decimals have infinitely many decimal places and cannot be written out in full in columns, so first convert them to their **equivalent fractions**. The number $0.\overline{3}$ has repeating block $3$ and becomes $\dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. For $1.\overline{6}$ the numerator is $16 - 1 = 15$ and the denominator is $9$, so the fraction is $\dfrac{15}{9} = \dfrac{5}{3}$. The terminating decimal $0.6$ can also be written as a fraction, $\dfrac{6}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.

By the order of operations, perform the multiplication first. The product $\dfrac{5}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{5}$ has numerator $15$ and denominator $15$, so it equals $1$. Finally, $\dfrac{1}{3} + 1 = \dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{3}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$. The **value** of the expression is $\dfrac{4}{3}$, or $1.\overline{3}$.

</div>
