## Exercise 1

Order the following numbers from least to greatest
$$
1.3 \qquad 1.25 \qquad 1.07 \qquad 1.5
$$
and plot them on the number line.

$$
1.30 \qquad 1.25 \qquad 1.07 \qquad 1.50
$$
$$
1.07 < 1.25 < 1.3 < 1.5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The four numbers have the same whole-number part, $1$, so to **compare** them we examine the decimal places. To compare the digits, write each number with two decimal places, adding a zero where needed, giving $1.3 = 1.30$ and $1.5 = 1.50$.

The fractional parts are now $30$, $25$, $07$ and $50$ hundredths. The number $1.07$ has $0$ tenths and is the smallest. Next come $1.25$ with $2$ tenths, $1.30$ with $3$ tenths and $1.50$ with $5$ tenths. Therefore the **increasing order** is $1.07 < 1.25 < 1.3 < 1.5$.

On the number line the segment from $1$ to $1.6$ is divided into tenths. The number $1.07$ lies just before $1.1$, while $1.25$ lies halfway between $1.2$ and $1.3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Round $5.4762$ to the nearest tenth, hundredth and thousandth.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{to the nearest tenth:} && 5.4762 \approx 5.5 \\[6pt]
&\text{to the nearest hundredth:} && 5.4762 \approx 5.48 \\[6pt]
&\text{to the nearest thousandth:} && 5.4762 \approx 5.476
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbol $\approx$ is read “approximately equal to” and indicates a rounded value. When **rounding**, look at the digit immediately after the rounding place.

To round to the nearest tenth, locate the digit $4$ and examine the hundredths digit, which is $7$. Since $7$ is greater than or equal to $5$, the tenths digit increases by $1$ to become $5$, giving $5.5$.

To round to the nearest hundredth, locate the digit $7$ and examine the thousandths digit, which is $6$. Again, $6$ is greater than or equal to $5$, so the hundredths digit becomes $8$, giving $5.48$.

To round to the nearest thousandth, locate the digit $6$ and examine the next digit, which is $2$. Since $2$ is less than $5$, the digit $6$ stays the same and the result is $5.476$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Round $9.996$ and $0.0349$ to the nearest hundredth.

$$
9.996 \approx 10.00
\qquad\qquad
0.0349 \approx 0.03
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In $9.996$, the hundredths digit is $9$ and the next digit is $6$. Since $6$ is greater than or equal to $5$, the number of hundredths increases by $1$. The number $9.99$ contains $999$ hundredths, and adding one gives $1000$ hundredths, or $10$ whole units. Therefore the result to the nearest hundredth is $10.00$. The two zeros after the decimal point indicate that the number was rounded to the nearest hundredth.

In $0.0349$, the hundredths digit is $3$ and the thousandths digit is $4$. Since $4$ is less than $5$, the hundredths digit stays $3$ and the remaining digits are dropped. The rounded value is therefore $0.03$. The digit $9$ at the end has no effect because only the digit immediately after the rounding place is used.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $2.35 \cdot 1.7$ and round the result to the nearest hundredth.

$$
235 \cdot 17 = 2350 + 1645 = 3995
$$
$$
2.35 \cdot 1.7 = 3.995 \approx 4.00
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the rule for **multiplying** decimals, multiply $235$ and $17$. Splitting $17$ into $10 + 7$ gives $235 \cdot 10 = 2350$ and $235 \cdot 7 = 1645$, so the product is $3995$. The factors have $2 + 1 = 3$ decimal places in all, so $2.35 \cdot 1.7 = 3.995$.

To round to the nearest hundredth, look at the thousandths digit, which is $5$. Since $5$ is greater than or equal to $5$, the number of hundredths increases by $1$. The $399$ hundredths in $3.99$ therefore become $400$ hundredths, or $4$ whole units. The **rounded result** is $4.00$.

</div>
