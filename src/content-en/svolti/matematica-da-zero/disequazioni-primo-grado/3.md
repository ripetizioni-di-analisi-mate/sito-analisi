## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
3(x - 2) < x + 4 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x - 6 &< x + 4 \\[6pt]
3x - x &< 4 + 6 \\[6pt]
2x &< 10 \\[6pt]
x &< \frac{10}{2} \\[6pt]
x &< 5
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x < 5$, that is, the interval $(-\infty, 5)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

We expand the product $3(x - 2)$ using the **distributive property**. The number $3$ multiplies both $x$ and $-2$, so $3(x - 2) = 3x - 6$.

We then move $x$ to the left-hand side, where it becomes $-x$, and $-6$ to the right, where it becomes $+6$. This gives $2x < 10$. The coefficient $2$ is positive, so dividing by $2$ preserves the direction and the solutions are $x < 5$. As a check, when $x = 4$ the left-hand side equals $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ and the right-hand side equals $8$, and $6 < 8$ is true. When $x = 5$, both sides equal $9$, so $5$ is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
2(3 - x) \geq 4(x + 3) .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
6 - 2x &\geq 4x + 12 \\[6pt]
-2x - 4x &\geq 12 - 6 \\[6pt]
-6x &\geq 6 \\[6pt]
x &\leq \frac{6}{-6} \\[6pt]
x &\leq -1
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x \leq -1$, that is, the interval $(-\infty, -1]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the distributive property, the left-hand side becomes $2 \cdot 3 - 2 \cdot x = 6 - 2x$ and the right-hand side becomes $4 \cdot x + 4 \cdot 3 = 4x + 12$.

The term $4x$ moves to the left-hand side as $-4x$ and the number $6$ moves to the right as $-6$. This gives $-6x \geq 6$. The coefficient $-6$ is negative, so dividing by $-6$ **reverses** the inequality and $\geq$ becomes $\leq$. The quotient $\dfrac{6}{-6}$ equals $-1$, so the solutions are $x \leq -1$.

As a check, when $x = -1$ the left-hand side equals $2 \cdot 4 = 8$ and the right-hand side equals $4 \cdot 2 = 8$, and $8 \geq 8$ is true.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
(x + 2)^2 - x(x + 1) > 7 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + 4x + 4 - x^2 - x &> 7 \\[6pt]
3x + 4 &> 7 \\[6pt]
3x &> 3 \\[6pt]
x &> 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x > 1$, that is, the interval $(1, +\infty)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression $(x + 2)^2$ is the **square of a binomial**. Its expansion is the square of the first term plus twice the product of the terms plus the square of the second term: $x^2 + 2 \cdot x \cdot 2 + 4 = x^2 + 4x + 4$. The product $x(x + 1)$ equals $x^2 + x$, and the minus sign before it changes the sign of both terms, so we write $-x^2 - x$.

The terms $x^2$ and $-x^2$ sum to zero, so the inequality is **linear** even though squares appeared at first. The terms left are $4x - x = 3x$ and the number $4$. Moving $4$ gives $3x > 3$, and dividing by $3$, which is positive, gives the solutions $x > 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve the inequality
$$
(x - 3)(x + 3) - (x - 1)^2 \leq 6 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 - 9 - \left( x^2 - 2x + 1 \right) &\leq 6 \\[6pt]
x^2 - 9 - x^2 + 2x - 1 &\leq 6 \\[6pt]
2x - 10 &\leq 6 \\[6pt]
2x &\leq 16 \\[6pt]
x &\leq 8
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x \leq 8$, that is, the interval $(-\infty, 8]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The product $(x - 3)(x + 3)$ is the **product of a sum and a difference**, so it equals the difference of squares $x^2 - 9$. The expression $(x - 1)^2$ is the square of a binomial and equals $x^2 - 2x + 1$. The minus sign before it changes the signs of all three terms, which become $-x^2 + 2x - 1$.

The terms $x^2$ and $-x^2$ cancel. The numbers give $-9 - 1 = -10$, so the left-hand side is $2x - 10$. Moving $-10$ to the right-hand side gives $2x \leq 6 + 10 = 16$. The coefficient $2$ is positive, so dividing by $2$ preserves the direction and the solutions are $x \leq 8$.

</div>
