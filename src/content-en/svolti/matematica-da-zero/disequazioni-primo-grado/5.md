## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
2(x + 1) > 2x - 3 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + 2 &> 2x - 3 \\[6pt]
2x - 2x &> -3 - 2 \\[6pt]
0 \cdot x &> -5
\end{aligned}
$$

The inequality is always true, so the solutions are all real numbers, that is, the set $\mathbb{R}$.

::grafico{f="2*x+2;2*x-3" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-12 ymax=12 punti="1,4,pieno;1,-1,pieno" segmenti="1,-1,1,4" etichette="1.2,1.3,5" legenda="y = 2x + 2;y = 2x - 3" titolo="The left-hand side $2x + 2$ exceeds the right-hand side $2x - 3$ by $5$ for every value of $x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the distributive property, the left-hand side becomes $2x + 2$. Moving $2x$ to the left and $2$ to the right, the terms containing $x$ give $2x - 2x = 0 \cdot x$ and the numbers give $-3 - 2 = -5$.

The left-hand side $0 \cdot x$ equals $0$ for any $x$, leaving the comparison $0 > -5$, which is true. Therefore every number is a solution and the inequality is **always true**. For example, when $x = 10$, the two sides equal $22$ and $17$, and when $x = -10$, they equal $-18$ and $-23$. In both cases the left-hand side exceeds the right by $5$. When we graph both sides for every $x$, they form two parallel lines, and the first is always $5$ units above the second. For example, when $x = 1$, their values are $4$ and $-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
3x - (x - 4) < 2(x + 1) .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x - x + 4 &< 2x + 2 \\[6pt]
2x + 4 &< 2x + 2 \\[6pt]
2x - 2x &< 2 - 4 \\[6pt]
0 \cdot x &< -2
\end{aligned}
$$

The inequality is never true, so its solution set is $\emptyset$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The minus sign before $(x - 4)$ changes the sign of both terms, so $-(x - 4) = -x + 4$. The left-hand side becomes $3x - x + 4 = 2x + 4$, and the right-hand side becomes $2x + 2$ by the distributive property.

After moving the terms, the coefficient of $x$ is $2 - 2 = 0$ and the right-hand side reduces to $2 - 4 = -2$. This gives $0 \cdot x < -2$, that is, the comparison $0 < -2$, which is false for every value of $x$. Therefore the inequality is **never true** because the left-hand side always exceeds the right by $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the two inequalities
$$
(x + 1)^2 \geq x(x + 2) + 1
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
(x + 1)^2 > x(x + 2) + 1 .
$$

$$
(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1
$$
$$
x(x + 2) + 1 = x^2 + 2x + 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + 2x + 1 - x^2 - 2x - 1 &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
0 \cdot x &\geq 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The first inequality is always true, with solutions $\mathbb{R}$. The second becomes $0 \cdot x > 0$ and is never true, with solution set $\emptyset$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side is the square of a binomial and equals $x^2 + 2x + 1$. By the distributive property, the right-hand side equals $x^2 + 2x + 1$. The two sides are therefore the **same expression**, and they have the same value for every $x$.

Moving everything to the left-hand side, each term cancels with its opposite, leaving $0 \cdot x$. With the symbol $\geq$, the final comparison is $0 \geq 0$, which is true because $0 = 0$, so the first inequality is **always true**. With the symbol $>$, the final comparison is $0 > 0$, which is false because a number is not greater than itself, so the second inequality is **never true**.

The two inequalities differ only in whether their symbols allow equality, and they have different solutions. For example, when $x = 3$ both sides equal $16$, so the statement $16 \geq 16$ is true, whereas $16 > 16$ is false.

</div>
