## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
2x - 3 > 7 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &> 7 + 3 \\[6pt]
2x &> 10 \\[6pt]
x &> \frac{10}{2} \\[6pt]
x &> 5
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x > 5$, that is, the interval $(5, +\infty)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **first equivalence principle**, the term $-3$ moves to the right-hand side with its sign changed, so it becomes $+3$. The right-hand side becomes $7 + 3 = 10$.

The coefficient of $x$ is $2$, which is positive. By the second principle, we divide both sides by $2$ and the direction stays the same. Since $\dfrac{10}{2} = 5$, the solutions are $x > 5$. As a check, when $x = 6$ the left-hand side equals $12 - 3 = 9$, and $9 > 7$ is true.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
5x + 4 \leq 2x - 8 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x - 2x &\leq -8 - 4 \\[6pt]
3x &\leq -12 \\[6pt]
x &\leq \frac{-12}{3} \\[6pt]
x &\leq -4
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x \leq -4$, that is, the interval $(-\infty, -4]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

We move the terms containing $x$ to the left-hand side and the numbers to the right. The term $2x$ moves to the left and becomes $-2x$. The number $4$ moves to the right and becomes $-4$. Combining like terms gives $5x - 2x = 3x$ on the left-hand side and $-8 - 4 = -12$ on the right.

The coefficient $3$ is positive, so dividing by $3$ preserves the sign $\leq$ of the inequality. The quotient $\dfrac{-12}{3}$ equals $-4$ because a negative number divided by a positive number is negative. The solutions are $x \leq -4$, and the number $-4$ is included because the symbol allows equality. As a check, when $x = -4$ the two sides equal $5 \cdot (-4) + 4 = -16$ and $2 \cdot (-4) - 8 = -16$, and $-16 \leq -16$ is true.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
3 - 4x < x + 13 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-4x - x &< 13 - 3 \\[6pt]
-5x &< 10 \\[6pt]
x &> \frac{10}{-5} \\[6pt]
x &> -2
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x > -2$, that is, the interval $(-2, +\infty)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $x$ moves from the right-hand side to the left as $-x$, and the number $3$ moves from the left-hand side to the right as $-3$. We obtain $-4x - x = -5x$ on the left-hand side and $13 - 3 = 10$ on the right.

The coefficient of $x$ is $-5$, which is *negative*. By the **second equivalence principle**, dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality, so the symbol $<$ becomes $>$. The quotient $\dfrac{10}{-5}$ equals $-2$, so the solutions are $x > -2$.

Checking with a number confirms the direction. When $x = 0$, which belongs to the interval, the two sides equal $3$ and $13$, and $3 < 13$ is true. When $x = -3$, which is outside it, the two sides equal $3 + 12 = 15$ and $-3 + 13 = 10$, and $15 < 10$ is false.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve the inequality
$$
7 - 2x \geq 3 - 6x .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x + 6x &\geq 3 - 7 \\[6pt]
4x &\geq -4 \\[6pt]
x &\geq \frac{-4}{4} \\[6pt]
x &\geq -1
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x \geq -1$, that is, the interval $[-1, +\infty)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $-6x$ moves to the left-hand side and becomes $+6x$. The number $7$ moves to the right-hand side and becomes $-7$. We obtain $-2x + 6x = 4x$ on the left-hand side and $3 - 7 = -4$ on the right.

In this exercise, both sides contained a term with a negative coefficient, but after transposition the coefficient of $x$ is $4$, which is positive. Therefore dividing by $4$ preserves the sign $\geq$ of the inequality. The quotient $\dfrac{-4}{4}$ equals $-1$, and the solutions are $x \geq -1$. As a check, when $x = -1$ the two sides equal $7 + 2 = 9$ and $3 + 6 = 9$, and $9 \geq 9$ is true.

</div>
