## Exercise 1

Marta has $20$ euros. She must buy a notebook for $3.50$ euros and wants to use the rest to buy pens at $1.50$ euros each. What is the maximum number of pens she can buy?

$$
3.5 + 1.5\,x \leq 20
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.5\,x &\leq 20 - 3.5 \\[6pt]
1.5\,x &\leq 16.5 \\[6pt]
x &\leq \frac{16.5}{1.5} \\[6pt]
x &\leq 11
\end{aligned}
$$

Marta can buy at most $11$ pens.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the number of pens. The pens cost $1.50$ euros each, so $x$ pens cost $1.5\,x$ euros, and including the notebook the total is $3.5 + 1.5\,x$ euros. The phrase *at most* $20$ euros is represented by the symbol $\leq$.

Moving $3.5$ to the right-hand side gives $20 - 3.5 = 16.5$. The coefficient $1.5$ is positive, so dividing by $1.5$ preserves the direction. To calculate $\dfrac{16.5}{1.5}$, we multiply the numerator and denominator by $10$ to obtain $\dfrac{165}{15} = 11$.

The number of pens is an integer, and the largest integer satisfying $x \leq 11$ is $11$. As a check, $11$ pens cost $16.50$ euros and the notebook brings the total to exactly $20$ euros. With $12$ pens the total would be $3.50 + 18 = 21.50$ euros. Therefore Marta can buy **at most** $11$ pens.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A shop offers two bicycle rental plans. Plan A costs $3$ euros per hour. Plan B charges $5$ euros up front and then $2$ euros per hour. For how many hours of rental does plan B cost less than plan A? Hours are counted as whole numbers.

$$
5 + 2x < 3x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2x - 3x &< -5 \\[6pt]
-x &< -5 \\[6pt]
x &> 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Plan B costs less for rentals of $6$ hours or more.

::grafico{f="3*x;5+2*x" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=30 nomi="x,euro" punti="5,15,pieno" segmenti="5,0,5,15" legenda="\text{plan A: } 3x;\text{plan B: } 5 + 2x" titolo="Both plans cost $15$ euros for $5$ hours. For $x > 5$, plan B costs less"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the number of hours. Under plan A the cost is $3x$ euros, and under plan B it is $5 + 2x$ euros. We use the symbol $<$ to express the requirement that plan B *cost less*, with the cost of plan B on the left-hand side.

Moving $3x$ to the left-hand side and $5$ to the right gives $-x < -5$. Multiplying by $-1$ reverses the inequality, so $x > 5$.

The number $5$ is excluded because for $5$ hours both plans cost $15$ euros. The hours are whole numbers, so the first number satisfying $x > 5$ is $6$. For $6$ hours plan A costs $18$ euros and plan B costs $5 + 12 = 17$ euros. Therefore plan B **costs less** for $6$ hours or more. On the graph the two lines intersect at the point with $x = 5$ and a cost of $15$ euros, and for $x > 5$ the line for plan B lies below the one for plan A.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two plans are available for home electricity. Plan A costs $12$ euros per month plus $0.20$ euros for each kWh used. Plan B has no fixed charge and costs $0.26$ euros per kWh. For what level of monthly consumption does plan A cost less than plan B?

$$
12 + 0.2\,x < 0.26\,x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
0.2\,x - 0.26\,x &< -12 \\[6pt]
-0.06\,x &< -12 \\[6pt]
x &> \frac{-12}{-0.06} \\[6pt]
x &> 200
\end{aligned}
$$

Plan A costs less when consumption exceeds $200$ kWh per month.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the monthly consumption in kWh. Plan A costs $12 + 0.2\,x$ euros and plan B costs $0.26\,x$ euros. We want the first cost to be less than the second.

Moving $0.26\,x$ to the left-hand side gives $0.2\,x - 0.26\,x = -0.06\,x$, and moving $12$ to the right-hand side gives $-12$. The coefficient $-0.06$ is negative, so dividing by $-0.06$ **reverses** the inequality. The quotient of two negative numbers is positive, and $\dfrac{12}{0.06} = \dfrac{1200}{6} = 200$, obtained by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $100$.

The solutions are $x > 200$. Consumption of $200$ kWh is excluded because for that amount both plans cost $52$ euros. Indeed, $12 + 0.2 \cdot 200 = 12 + 40 = 52$ and $0.26 \cdot 200 = 52$. Therefore plan A **costs less** when consumption exceeds $200$ kWh per month.

</div>

## Exercise 4

One side of a rectangle is $4$ cm longer than the other. Its perimeter must be at least $20$ cm and at most $32$ cm. What values can the shorter side take?

$$
2p = 2\,\big( x + (x + 4) \big) = 4x + 8
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
4x + 8 \geq 20 \\
4x + 8 \leq 32
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
4x \geq 12 \\
4x \leq 24
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq 3 \\
x \leq 6
\end{cases}
$$

The shorter side can measure from $3$ cm to $6$ cm, with both endpoints included, that is, $3 \leq x \leq 6$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the length in cm of the shorter side, so the other side measures $x + 4$. The perimeter $2p$ is twice the sum of the two different side lengths, that is, $2(2x + 4) = 4x + 8$.

The problem imposes two conditions that must hold simultaneously. The phrase *at least* $20$ cm is expressed as $\geq 20$, and *at most* $32$ cm as $\leq 32$. Therefore we write a **system**. In the first inequality, moving $8$ gives $4x \geq 12$. Dividing by $4$, which is positive, gives $x \geq 3$. In the second, the same procedure gives $4x \leq 24$, that is, $x \leq 6$.

The numbers common to the two conditions are $3 \leq x \leq 6$. A length must be positive, and all these values are positive, so the answer makes sense. As a check, when $x = 3$ the sides are $3$ cm and $7$ cm and the perimeter is $20$ cm. When $x = 6$ the sides are $6$ cm and $10$ cm and the perimeter is $32$ cm.

</div>
