## Exercise 1

Solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 1 > 0 \\
3x \leq 12
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x > -1 \\
x \leq 4
\end{cases}
$$

The solutions are $-1 < x \leq 4$, that is, the interval $(-1, 4]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

We solve each inequality of the system separately. In the first, moving $1$ to the right-hand side gives $x > -1$. In the second, dividing both sides by $3$, which is positive, gives $x \leq 4$.

The solutions of the **system** are the numbers satisfying *both* conditions, that is, the numbers greater than $-1$ and also less than or equal to $4$. The number $-1$ is excluded because $-1 > -1$ is false, and $4$ is included because $4 \leq 4$ is true. The solutions are therefore $-1 < x \leq 4$. For example, $0$ is a solution, whereas $5$ satisfies the first inequality but not the second because $3 \cdot 5 = 15$ and $15 \leq 12$ is false.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3x + 1 \geq x - 5 \\
4 - x > 2x + 1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x - x &\geq -5 - 1 \\[6pt]
2x &\geq -6 \\[6pt]
x &\geq -3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
-x - 2x &> 1 - 4 \\[6pt]
-3x &> -3 \\[6pt]
x &< 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $-3 \leq x < 1$, that is, the interval $[-3, 1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first inequality, moving $x$ to the left-hand side and $1$ to the right gives $2x \geq -6$. Dividing by $2$, which is positive, preserves the direction, and the quotient $\dfrac{-6}{2}$ equals $-3$. The solutions of the first inequality are therefore $x \geq -3$.

In the second inequality, moving $2x$ to the left-hand side and $4$ to the right gives $-3x > -3$. The coefficient $-3$ is negative, so dividing by $-3$ **reverses** the inequality and gives $x < 1$.

In the **graph of the system**, the line for $x \geq -3$ starts at $-3$ with a solid dot and extends to the right. The line for $x < 1$ extends from the left up to $1$, with an open circle. Both lines are present between $-3$ and $1$, so the solutions are $-3 \leq x < 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2(x - 1) > x + 3 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{x}{2} + 1 < 3
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x - 2 &> x + 3 \\[6pt]
x &> 5
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 2 &< 6 \\[6pt]
x &< 4
\end{aligned}
$$

The system has no solutions, so its solution set is $\emptyset$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first inequality, the distributive property gives $2x - 2$ on the left-hand side. Moving $x$ to the left and $-2$ to the right gives $2x - x > 3 + 2$, that is, $x > 5$.

In the second inequality, we multiply all terms by $2$, which is positive, to clear the denominator: $\dfrac{x}{2}$ becomes $x$, the number $1$ becomes $2$, and the number $3$ becomes $6$. Moving $2$ gives $x < 6 - 2$, that is, $x < 4$.

The first condition is satisfied by numbers greater than $5$, and the second by numbers less than $4$. A number greater than $5$ is also greater than $4$, so it cannot satisfy the second condition. Therefore the system **has no solutions**.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2 > 0 \\
5 - x \geq 0 \\
3x - 3 \geq 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x > -2 \\
x \leq 5 \\
x \geq 1
\end{cases}
$$

The solutions are $1 \leq x \leq 5$, that is, the interval $[1, 5]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The first inequality gives $x > -2$ when we move $2$ to the right-hand side. In the second, moving $5$ gives $-x \geq -5$. Multiplying by $-1$ reverses the inequality, so $x \leq 5$. In the third, moving $-3$ gives $3x \geq 3$, and dividing by $3$, which is positive, gives $x \geq 1$.

The graph has three lines, and the solutions of the system are the numbers where *all three* overlap. To the right of $1$ the condition $x > -2$ is already satisfied because every number greater than or equal to $1$ is also greater than $-2$. The remaining conditions are therefore $x \geq 1$ and $x \leq 5$, and the solutions are $1 \leq x \leq 5$. Both endpoints are included because both symbols allow equality.

</div>
