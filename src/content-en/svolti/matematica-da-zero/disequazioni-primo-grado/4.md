## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{x}{2} - 1 > \frac{x}{3} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
6 \cdot \frac{x}{2} - 6 \cdot 1 &> 6 \cdot \frac{x}{3} \\[6pt]
3x - 6 &> 2x \\[6pt]
3x - 2x &> 6 \\[6pt]
x &> 6
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x > 6$, that is, the interval $(6, +\infty)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators are $2$ and $3$, and their least common multiple is $\operatorname{mcm}(2, 3) = 6$. We multiply every term on both sides by $6$. The number $6$ is positive, so by the **second equivalence principle** the direction stays the same.

Each fraction simplifies: $6 \cdot \dfrac{x}{2} = 3x$ because $6 : 2 = 3$, and $6 \cdot \dfrac{x}{3} = 2x$ because $6 : 3 = 2$. The number $1$ must also be multiplied by $6$ and becomes $6$. This gives $3x - 6 > 2x$, and moving the terms gives $x > 6$.

As a check, when $x = 12$ the left-hand side equals $6 - 1 = 5$ and the right-hand side equals $4$, and $5 > 4$ is true.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{x + 1}{3} - \frac{x - 2}{4} \leq 1 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(x + 1) - 3(x - 2) &\leq 12 \\[6pt]
4x + 4 - 3x + 6 &\leq 12 \\[6pt]
x + 10 &\leq 12 \\[6pt]
x &\leq 2
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x \leq 2$, that is, the interval $(-\infty, 2]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The least common multiple of the denominators is $\operatorname{mcm}(3, 4) = 12$. We multiply both sides by $12$, which is positive, so the direction stays the same. The first fraction becomes $\dfrac{12}{3} \cdot (x + 1) = 4(x + 1)$, the second becomes $\dfrac{12}{4} \cdot (x - 2) = 3(x - 2)$, and the right-hand side becomes $12 \cdot 1 = 12$.

There is a minus sign before the second fraction, so $-3(x - 2)$ equals $-3x + 6$ because the product of $-3$ and $-2$ is $+6$. Combining like terms gives $4x - 3x = x$ and $4 + 6 = 10$. Moving $10$ gives $x \leq 12 - 10$, that is, $x \leq 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality
$$
\frac{2x - 1}{6} - \frac{x + 3}{4} \geq \frac{x}{3} - 1 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(2x - 1) - 3(x + 3) &\geq 4x - 12 \\[6pt]
4x - 2 - 3x - 9 &\geq 4x - 12 \\[6pt]
x - 11 &\geq 4x - 12 \\[6pt]
x - 4x &\geq -12 + 11 \\[6pt]
-3x &\geq -1 \\[6pt]
x &\leq \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions are $x \leq \dfrac{1}{3}$, that is, the interval $\left( -\infty, \dfrac{1}{3} \right]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators are $6$, $4$, and $3$. Factoring gives $6 = 2 \cdot 3$ and $4 = 2^2$, so the least common multiple is $2^2 \cdot 3 = 12$. We multiply all terms by $12$, which is positive, so the direction stays the same. The fractions become $2(2x - 1)$ because $12 : 6 = 2$, then $3(x + 3)$ because $12 : 4 = 3$, and $4x$ because $12 : 3 = 4$. The number $-1$ becomes $-12$.

The minus sign before $3(x + 3)$ changes the sign of both terms of the product, which becomes $-3x - 9$. On the left-hand side we therefore obtain $4x - 3x = x$ and $-2 - 9 = -11$.

Moving $4x$ to the left-hand side and $-11$ to the right gives $-3x \geq -1$. The coefficient $-3$ is negative, so dividing by $-3$ **reverses** the inequality. The quotient $\dfrac{-1}{-3}$ equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$ because the quotient of two negative numbers is positive. The solutions are $x \leq \dfrac{1}{3}$.

</div>
