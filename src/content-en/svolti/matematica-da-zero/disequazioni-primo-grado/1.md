## Exercise 1

Starting from $3 < 8$, write the inequalities obtained by subtracting $5$ from both sides, by multiplying both sides by $2$, and by multiplying both sides by $-4$.

$$
3 - 5 < 8 - 5
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
-2 < 3
$$
$$
3 \cdot 2 < 8 \cdot 2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
6 < 16
$$
$$
3 \cdot (-4) > 8 \cdot (-4)
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
-12 > -32
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the same number from both sides preserves the direction, by the **properties of inequalities**. Since $3 - 5 = -2$ and $8 - 5 = 3$, we obtain $-2 < 3$, which is true.

The number $2$ is positive, so multiplying by $2$ also leaves the direction unchanged. The products are $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ and $8 \cdot 2 = 16$, so we obtain $6 < 16$.

The number $-4$ is negative, so multiplying by $-4$ **reverses** the direction and the symbol $<$ becomes $>$. By the sign rule, $3 \cdot (-4) = -12$ and $8 \cdot (-4) = -32$. This gives $-12 > -32$, which is true because $-12$ is to the right of $-32$ on the number line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine which of the numbers $-1$, $0$, $2$, and $5$ are solutions of the inequality $3x - 4 > 2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x = -1 : \quad & 3 \cdot (-1) - 4 = -7 , \qquad -7 > 2 \ \text{ false} \\[6pt]
x = 0 : \quad & 3 \cdot 0 - 4 = -4 , \qquad -4 > 2 \ \text{ false} \\[6pt]
x = 2 : \quad & 3 \cdot 2 - 4 = 2 , \qquad 2 > 2 \ \text{ false} \\[6pt]
x = 5 : \quad & 3 \cdot 5 - 4 = 11 , \qquad 11 > 2 \ \text{ true}
\end{aligned}
$$

Of the four numbers, only $5$ is a solution.

<div class="spiegazione">

A number is a **solution** if substituting it for $x$ makes the inequality true. For each number we therefore evaluate the left-hand side $3x - 4$ and compare it with $2$.

When $x = -1$, the product $3 \cdot (-1)$ equals $-3$, and $-3 - 4 = -7$. The number $-7$ is less than $2$, so $-1$ is not a solution. When $x = 0$, the left-hand side equals $0 - 4 = -4$, which is less than $2$, so $0$ is also excluded.

When $x = 2$, the left-hand side equals $6 - 4 = 2$. The inequality $2 > 2$ is false because a number is not greater than itself, so $2$ is not a solution, although it would be with the symbol $\geq$. When $x = 5$, the left-hand side equals $15 - 4 = 11$, which is greater than $2$, so $5$ **is a solution**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the solutions $x \geq -2$ and $1 \leq x < 4$ in interval notation and graph them on the number line.

$$
x \geq -2
\qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
[-2, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The notation $x \geq -2$ includes $-2$ and all numbers to the right of $-2$, extending without end. The endpoint $-2$ is included, so we use a **square bracket**. On the other side the interval extends to the right, and $+\infty$ always takes a parenthesis. On the number line, we draw a solid dot at $-2$ and shade everything to its right.

The double inequality $1 \leq x < 4$ denotes the numbers that are both greater than or equal to $1$ and less than $4$. The number $1$ is included, so it takes a square bracket and a solid dot. The number $4$ is excluded because $4 < 4$ is false, so it takes a **parenthesis** and an open circle. For example, $1$, $2.5$, and $3.9$ belong to the interval $[1, 4)$, whereas $4$ and $0$ lie outside it.

</div>
