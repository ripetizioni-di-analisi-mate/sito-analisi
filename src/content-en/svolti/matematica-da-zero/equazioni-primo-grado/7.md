## Exercise 1

A number plus the difference between twice that number and $7$ equals $38$. Find the number.

$$
x = \text{number to be found}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x + (2x - 7) &= 38 \\[6pt]
3x - 7 &= 38 \\[6pt]
3x &= 45 \\[6pt]
x &= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

The required number is $15$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the number we seek. Twice that number is $2x$, and subtracting $7$ from this gives $2x - 7$. The problem states that the sum of $x$ and $2x - 7$ equals $38$, which gives the equation.

Combining like terms gives $3x - 7 = 38$. Moving $-7$ to the right-hand side gives $3x = 45$, and dividing by $3$ yields $x = 15$.

Checking against the statement gives $2 \cdot 15 - 7 = 23$ and $15 + 23 = 38$, so the number is $15$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find three consecutive odd numbers whose sum is $57$.

$$
x,\quad x + 2,\quad x + 4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x + (x + 2) + (x + 4) &= 57 \\[6pt]
3x + 6 &= 57 \\[6pt]
3x &= 51 \\[6pt]
x &= 17
\end{aligned}
$$

The numbers are $17$, $19$, and $21$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Two consecutive odd numbers differ by $2$, as in $5$ and $7$. If $x$ is the smallest of the three numbers, the other two are therefore $x + 2$ and $x + 4$.

The sum of the three numbers is $3x + 6$, and the problem states that it equals $57$. Moving $6$ to the right-hand side gives $3x = 51$, so $x = 17$.

The number $17$ is odd, so the solution makes sense. The three numbers are $17$, $19$, and $21$, and indeed $17 + 19 + 21 = 57$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A mother is $38$ years old and her son is $8$. How many years from now will the mother be twice as old as her son?

$$
x = \text{number of years that must pass}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
38 + x &= 2\,(8 + x) \\[6pt]
38 + x &= 16 + 2x \\[6pt]
38 - 16 &= 2x - x \\[6pt]
x &= 22
\end{aligned}
$$

In $22$ years the mother will be twice as old as her son.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the number of years that must pass. In $x$ years the mother will be $38 + x$ years old and her son will be $8 + x$ because they both age by the same number of years. The problem asks when the mother will be twice as old as her son, so the equation is $38 + x = 2\,(8 + x)$.

By the distributive property, the right-hand side becomes $16 + 2x$. Moving the numbers to the left-hand side and the terms containing $x$ to the right gives $22 = x$.

The number $22$ is positive, so the event lies in the future and the solution makes sense. In $22$ years the mother will be $60$ years old and her son will be $30$, and indeed $60 = 2 \cdot 30$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Marco is $15$ years older than Luca. Five years ago Marco was twice as old as Luca. How old are Marco and Luca today?

$$
x = \text{Luca's age today},
\qquad
x + 15 = \text{Marco's age today}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 15) - 5 &= 2\,(x - 5) \\[6pt]
x + 10 &= 2x - 10 \\[6pt]
10 + 10 &= 2x - x \\[6pt]
x &= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

Today Luca is $20$ years old and Marco is $35$.

<div class="spiegazione">

We choose Luca's age today as the unknown because Marco's age follows directly from it. If Luca is $x$ years old, Marco is $x + 15$.

Five years ago both were $5$ years younger. Luca was therefore $x - 5$ years old and Marco was $(x + 15) - 5 = x + 10$ years old. The problem states that Marco was then twice as old as Luca, so the equation is $x + 10 = 2\,(x - 5)$.

The distributive property gives $x + 10 = 2x - 10$. Applying the **transposition rule** gives $20 = x$.

Today Luca is $20$ years old and Marco is $35$. Five years ago they were $15$ and $30$ years old, and indeed $30 = 2 \cdot 15$. Their ages were positive even five years ago, so the solution makes sense.

</div>
