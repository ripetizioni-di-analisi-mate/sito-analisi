## Exercise 1

A sweater is discounted by $20\%$ and now costs $36$ euros. What was the price before the discount?

$$
x = \text{price before the discount (in euros)}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x - \frac{20}{100}\,x &= 36 \\[6pt]
\frac{80}{100}\,x &= 36 \\[6pt]
x &= 36 \cdot \frac{100}{80} \\[6pt]
x &= 45
\end{aligned}
$$

Before the discount, the sweater cost $45$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the price before the discount. The discount is $20\%$ of $x$, that is, $\dfrac{20}{100}\,x$, and the discounted price is the original price minus the discount.

Both terms on the left-hand side contain $x$, so we combine them. Since $x = \dfrac{100}{100}\,x$, the difference is $\dfrac{100}{100}\,x - \dfrac{20}{100}\,x = \dfrac{80}{100}\,x$. By the second principle, we multiply both sides by the reciprocal $\dfrac{100}{80}$. This gives $x = \dfrac{3600}{80} = 45$.

Checking gives a discount of $\dfrac{20}{100} \cdot 45 = 9$ euros, and $45 - 9 = 36$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has a perimeter of $56$ cm, and its base exceeds its height by $4$ cm. Find the lengths of the base and the height.

$$
x = \text{height (in cm)},
\qquad
x + 4 = \text{base (in cm)}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\,(x + 4 + x) &= 56 \\[6pt]
2\,(2x + 4) &= 56 \\[6pt]
4x + 8 &= 56 \\[6pt]
4x &= 48 \\[6pt]
x &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

The height is $12$ cm and the base is $16$ cm.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the height. The base exceeds the height by $4$ cm, so its length is $x + 4$. The perimeter of a rectangle is twice the sum of its base and height, so the equation is $2\,(x + 4 + x) = 56$.

In the expression $x + 4 + x$, we have $x + x = 2x$, and the distributive property gives $4x + 8 = 56$. Moving $8$ to the right-hand side gives $4x = 48$, so $x = 12$.

The height is $12$ cm and the base is $12 + 4 = 16$ cm. Both lengths are positive, and checking gives $2 \cdot (16 + 12) = 2 \cdot 28 = 56$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Giulia buys $3$ notebooks and $2$ pens for a total of $9.70$ euros. A pen costs $1.40$ euros less than a notebook. What is the price of one notebook and of one pen?

$$
x = \text{price of a notebook (in euros)},
\qquad
x - 1.40 = \text{price of a pen (in euros)}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 2\,(x - 1.40) &= 9.70 \\[6pt]
3x + 2x - 2.80 &= 9.70 \\[6pt]
5x &= 9.70 + 2.80 \\[6pt]
5x &= 12.50 \\[6pt]
x &= 2.50
\end{aligned}
$$

A notebook costs $2.50$ euros and a pen costs $1.10$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

We choose the price $x$ of one notebook as the unknown because the price of a pen can be expressed directly in terms of it as $x - 1.40$. The notebooks cost $3x$ and the pens cost $2\,(x - 1.40)$, for a total of $9.70$ euros.

The distributive property gives $2\,(x - 1.40) = 2x - 2.80$. Combining like terms and moving $-2.80$ to the right-hand side gives $5x = 12.50$. Dividing by $5$ yields $x = 2.50$.

A pen therefore costs $2.50 - 1.40 = 1.10$ euros, which is a positive price. Checking gives $3 \cdot 2.50 + 2 \cdot 1.10 = 7.50 + 2.20 = 9.70$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A car leaves a city and travels at $80$ km/h. One hour later, a second car leaves the same city along the same road at $100$ km/h. How many hours after it leaves does the second car catch up with the first, and how far from the city does this happen?

$$
x = \text{travel time of the second car (in hours)}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
80\,(x + 1) &= 100\,x \\[6pt]
80x + 80 &= 100x \\[6pt]
80 &= 100x - 80x \\[6pt]
80 &= 20x \\[6pt]
x &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
100 \cdot 4 = 400
$$

The second car catches up with the first after $4$ hours, $400$ km from the city.

::grafico{f="80*(x+1);100*x" punti="4,400,pieno" segmenti="4,0,4,400" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=600 nomi="x,km" legenda="s = 80(x + 1);s = 100x" titolo="Distance from the city as a function of the second car's travel time $x$ in hours. The solid line represents the first car, the dashed line represents the second, and the lines intersect at $(4, 400)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the travel time of the second car in hours. The first car left one hour earlier, so when the second car has traveled for $x$ hours, the first has traveled for $x + 1$ hours. By the formula $s = v \cdot t$, the first car has traveled $80\,(x + 1)$ km and the second has traveled $100\,x$ km.

When the second car catches up with the first, both are at the same point, so they have traveled the same distance along the road. This equality gives the equation $80\,(x + 1) = 100\,x$.

The distributive property gives $80x + 80 = 100x$. The **transposition rule** gives $80 = 20x$, and dividing by $20$ yields $x = 4$.

The distance from the city is the distance traveled by the second car in $4$ hours, that is, $100 \cdot 4 = 400$ km. Checking with the first car gives $80 \cdot 5 = 400$ km.

</div>
