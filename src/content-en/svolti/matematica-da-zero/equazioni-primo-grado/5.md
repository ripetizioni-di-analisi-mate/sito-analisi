## Exercise 1

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{12}{x} = 3 .
$$

$$
x \neq 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
12 &= 3x \\[6pt]
x &= \frac{12}{3} \\[6pt]
x &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $4$ differs from $0$, so the equation has solution $x = 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown occurs in the denominator, so the equation is **rational**. The denominator $x$ must be nonzero, and the domain restriction is $x \neq 0$.

Under this condition we can multiply both sides by $x$, by the second principle. The left-hand side reduces to $12$ and the right-hand side to $3x$. Dividing both sides by $3$ gives $x = 4$.

The number $4$ satisfies the domain restriction, so it is **admissible**. Checking gives $\dfrac{12}{4} = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{3}{x - 2} = \frac{4}{x + 1} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x - 2 \neq 0 \\
x + 1 \neq 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \neq 2 \\
x \neq -1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3\,(x + 1) &= 4\,(x - 2) \\[6pt]
3x + 3 &= 4x - 8 \\[6pt]
3x - 4x &= -8 - 3 \\[6pt]
-x &= -11 \\[6pt]
x &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $11$ differs from $2$ and from $-1$, so the equation has solution $x = 11$.

The solution $x = 11$ (solid dot) differs from both values and is admissible"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators are $x - 2$ and $x + 1$. The first equals zero when $x = 2$ and the second when $x = -1$. Both must be nonzero, so we write the two conditions together as a system.

The least common multiple of the denominators is the product $(x - 2)(x + 1)$. When we multiply both sides by this product, $x - 2$ cancels on the left-hand side, leaving $3\,(x + 1)$, and $x + 1$ cancels on the right, leaving $4\,(x - 2)$.

The distributive property gives $3x + 3 = 4x - 8$. The **transposition rule** gives $-x = -11$, and multiplying both sides by $-1$ yields $x = 11$.

The number $11$ satisfies the domain restrictions, so it is **admissible**. Checking gives $\dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{x}{x - 1} - 2 = \frac{1}{x - 1} .
$$

$$
x \neq 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 2\,(x - 1) &= 1 \\[6pt]
x - 2x + 2 &= 1 \\[6pt]
-x &= 1 - 2 \\[6pt]
-x &= -1 \\[6pt]
x &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $1$ does not satisfy the domain restriction, so the equation is inconsistent.

<div class="spiegazione">

The only denominator is $x - 1$, which equals zero when $x = 1$. The domain restriction is therefore $x \neq 1$.

We multiply all terms by $x - 1$. The first term becomes $x$, the term $-2$ becomes $-2\,(x - 1)$, and the right-hand side becomes $1$. The term $-2$ has no denominator, but we must multiply it as well. Expanding the product gives $x - 2x + 2 = 1$, and therefore $x = 1$.

The number $1$ is precisely the value excluded by the domain restriction. Substituting it into the original equation gives $\dfrac{1}{0}$, which is undefined. We **discard** the candidate solution, so the equation **has no solutions** and is inconsistent.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{2}{x - 3} - \frac{1}{x + 3} = \frac{10}{x^2 - 9} .
$$

$$
x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x \neq 3 \\
x \neq -3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\,(x + 3) - (x - 3) &= 10 \\[6pt]
2x + 6 - x + 3 &= 10 \\[6pt]
x + 9 &= 10 \\[6pt]
x &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $1$ differs from $3$ and from $-3$, so the equation has solution $x = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $x^2 - 9$ is a difference of squares because $9 = 3^2$. It therefore factors as $(x - 3)(x + 3)$, and the least common multiple of the three denominators is precisely $(x - 3)(x + 3)$.

The product $(x - 3)(x + 3)$ is zero when either factor is zero, that is, when $x = 3$ or $x = -3$. The domain restrictions exclude both values, and we write the restrictions together as a system.

When we multiply by $(x - 3)(x + 3)$, the first term becomes $2\,(x + 3)$, the second becomes $-(x - 3)$, and the right-hand side becomes $10$. The minus sign changes the sign of both terms of $x - 3$, so we write $-x + 3$. This gives $x + 9 = 10$, that is, $x = 1$.

The number $1$ satisfies the domain restrictions, so it is **admissible**. To check, the left-hand side equals $\dfrac{2}{-2} - \dfrac{1}{4} = -1 - \dfrac{1}{4} = -\dfrac{5}{4}$ and the right-hand side equals $\dfrac{10}{1 - 9} = \dfrac{10}{-8} = -\dfrac{5}{4}$.

</div>
