## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $5\,(x - 2) + 3 = 3\,(x - 1) + 2\,(x - 2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5\,(x - 2) + 3 &= 3\,(x - 1) + 2\,(x - 2) \\[6pt]
5x - 10 + 3 &= 3x - 3 + 2x - 4 \\[6pt]
5x - 7 &= 5x - 7 \\[6pt]
5x - 5x &= -7 + 7 \\[6pt]
0 \cdot x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation is an identity, so every number is a solution.

<div class="spiegazione">

We expand the products using the distributive property. On the left-hand side, $5x - 10 + 3 = 5x - 7$. On the right-hand side, the terms containing $x$ give $3x + 2x = 5x$ and the numbers give $-3 - 4 = -7$, so the right-hand side is also $5x - 7$.

The two sides are the same expression. Moving the terms gives $0 \cdot x = 0$, which is true for every value of $x$. Therefore the equation is an **identity**, and the original equality is an identity.

For example, when $x = 0$ both sides equal $-7$, and when $x = 3$ both equal $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $3\,(x - 2) + x = 2\,(2x - 1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\,(x - 2) + x &= 2\,(2x - 1) \\[6pt]
3x - 6 + x &= 4x - 2 \\[6pt]
4x - 6 &= 4x - 2 \\[6pt]
4x - 4x &= -2 + 6 \\[6pt]
0 \cdot x &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation is inconsistent, so it has no solutions.

::grafico{f="4*x-6;4*x-2" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-14 ymax=16 legenda="y = 4x - 6;y = 4x - 2" titolo="The lines $y = 4x - 6$ and $y = 4x - 2$ are parallel and have no points in common, so the equation $3\,(x - 2) + x = 2\,(2x - 1)$ is inconsistent"}

<div class="spiegazione">

On the left-hand side, $3\,(x - 2) = 3x - 6$, and adding $x$ gives $4x - 6$. On the right-hand side, $2\,(2x - 1) = 4x - 2$.

Using the **transposition rule**, we move $4x$ to the left-hand side and $-6$ to the right. The terms containing $x$ cancel, giving $0 \cdot x = 4$. The left-hand side equals $0$ whatever number we substitute for $x$, so it never equals $4$. The equation is therefore **inconsistent**.

The left-hand side $4x - 6$ is always less than the right-hand side $4x - 2$, and the difference is always $4$. For example, when $x = 1$, the two sides equal $-2$ and $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $2\,(x + 3) - (x - 1) = 2x + 7$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\,(x + 3) - (x - 1) &= 2x + 7 \\[6pt]
2x + 6 - x + 1 &= 2x + 7 \\[6pt]
x + 7 &= 2x + 7 \\[6pt]
x - 2x &= 7 - 7 \\[6pt]
-x &= 0 \\[6pt]
x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation is conditional and has solution $x = 0$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The product $2\,(x + 3)$ gives $2x + 6$. The minus sign before $(x - 1)$ changes the sign of both terms, so we write $-x + 1$. The left-hand side reduces to $x + 7$.

The **transposition rule** gives $-x = 0$. The coefficient of $x$ is $-1$, which is nonzero, so the equation is **conditional**. Multiplying both sides by $-1$ gives $x = 0$.

The solution is the number $0$. To check, the left-hand side equals $2 \cdot 3 - (-1) = 6 + 1 = 7$ and the right-hand side equals $0 + 7 = 7$, so the solution is correct.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{x}{2} + \frac{x}{3} = \frac{5x}{6} + 1 .
$$

$$
\operatorname{mcm}(2, 3, 6) = 6
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 2x &= 5x + 6 \\[6pt]
5x - 5x &= 6 \\[6pt]
0 \cdot x &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation is inconsistent, so it has no solutions.

<div class="spiegazione">

The least common multiple of the denominators $2$, $3$, and $6$ is $6$. Multiplying all terms by $6$ gives $6 \cdot \dfrac{x}{2} = 3x$, $6 \cdot \dfrac{x}{3} = 2x$, $6 \cdot \dfrac{5x}{6} = 5x$, and $6 \cdot 1 = 6$.

On the left-hand side, $3x + 2x = 5x$. Moving $5x$ to the left-hand side gives $0 \cdot x = 6$, which is false for every value of $x$. The equation is therefore **inconsistent**.

The left-hand side is $\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{x}{3} = \dfrac{3x}{6} + \dfrac{2x}{6} = \dfrac{5x}{6}$ for every $x$, whereas the right-hand side is $\dfrac{5x}{6} + 1$. Therefore the right-hand side always exceeds the left by $1$.

</div>
