## Exercise 1

At $7$ in the morning the thermometer reads $-4$ °C. By $14$\:00 the temperature has risen $9$ degrees, and from then until midnight it falls $7$ degrees. Find the temperature at $14$\:00 and at midnight.

$$
(-4) + (+9) = +5
$$
$$
(+5) + (-7) = -2
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-6.7,0 6.9,0;6.55,0.16 6.9,0 6.55,-0.16;-6,-0.12 -6,0.12;-5,-0.12 -5,0.12;-4,-0.12 -4,0.12;-3,-0.12 -3,0.12;-2,-0.12 -2,0.12;-1,-0.12 -1,0.12;0,-0.22 0,0.22;1,-0.12 1,0.12;2,-0.12 2,0.12;3,-0.12 3,0.12;4,-0.12 4,0.12;5,-0.12 5,0.12;6,-0.12 6,0.12" punti="-4,0,pieno;5,0,pieno;-2,0,pieno" vettori="-4,0.45,4.9,0.45;5,1.05,-1.9,1.05" unicolore=1 etichette="-6.26,-0.6,−6;-5.26,-0.6,−5;-4.26,-0.6,−4;-3.26,-0.6,−3;-2.26,-0.6,−2;-1.26,-0.6,−1;-0.1,-0.6,0;0.8,-0.6,+1;1.8,-0.6,+2;2.8,-0.6,+3;3.8,-0.6,+4;4.8,-0.6,+5;5.8,-0.6,+6;-0.9,0.62,+9;1.1,1.22,−7" xmin=-7 xmax=7.2 ymin=-1 ymax=1.6 titolo="The temperature rises $9$ degrees from $-4$ °C to $+5$ °C, then falls $7$ degrees to $-2$ °C"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A rise in temperature corresponds to a positive number and a drop to a negative number. A rise of $9$ degrees therefore means adding $+9$. The numbers $-4$ and $+9$ have opposite signs, and $+9$ has the greater absolute value, so the sum is $+(9 - 4) = +5$. At $14$\:00 the temperature is $+5$ °C.

A drop of $7$ degrees means adding $-7$. The numbers $+5$ and $-7$ have opposite signs, and $-7$ has the greater absolute value, so the sum is $-(7 - 5) = -2$. At midnight the temperature is therefore $-2$ °C, below zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a mountain town, the day's low temperature is $-11$ °C and its high is $+6$ °C. The temperature range is the difference between the high and low temperatures. Find the temperature range.

$$
(+6) - (-11) = (+6) + (+11) = +17
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We find the temperature range by subtracting the low from the high. To subtract $-11$, we add its opposite $+11$, so $(+6) - (-11) = 6 + 11 = 17$. The **temperature range** is therefore $17$ degrees.

We can also read the result from the number line. From $-11$ to $0$ there are $11$ units, and from $0$ to $+6$ there are another $6$ units, for a total of $11 + 6 = 17$ units.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A diver is at an elevation of $-18$ m. The diver rises $7$ m, then descends $12$ m, and finally rises $5$ m. Find the diver's final elevation and distance in meters below sea level.

$$
\begin{aligned}
& (-18) + (+7) + (-12) + (+5) \\[6pt]
={} & [(+7) + (+5)] + [(-18) + (-12)] \\[6pt]
={} & (+12) + (-30) \\[6pt]
={} & -18
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Elevations above sea level are positive, and those below are negative. Moving up corresponds to adding a positive number, and moving down corresponds to adding a negative number. The final elevation is therefore the sum of the initial elevation $-18$ and the displacements $+7$, $-12$, and $+5$.

We group the upward displacements together and the negative numbers together. The positive numbers give $7 + 5 = 12$, that is, $+12$. The negative numbers give $-(18 + 12) = -30$. The numbers $+12$ and $-30$ have opposite signs, and $-30$ has the greater absolute value, so the sum is $-(30 - 12) = -18$.

The final elevation is $-18$ m, **equal** to the initial elevation, because the ascents and the descent cancel each other out, given that $7 + 5 = 12$. The diver is therefore $18$ m below sea level.

</div>
