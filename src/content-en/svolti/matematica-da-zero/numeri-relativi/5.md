## Exercise 1

Calculate
$$
(-3)^2 , \qquad (-3)^3 , \qquad (-1)^9 , \qquad (-10)^4 .
$$

$$
(-3)^2 = (-3) \cdot (-3) = +9
$$
$$
(-3)^3 = (-3) \cdot (-3) \cdot (-3) = -27
$$
$$
(-1)^9 = -1
$$
$$
(-10)^4 = +10\,000
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-29,0 11.4,0;11.05,0.16 11.4,0 11.05,-0.16;-27,-0.2 -27,0.2;-24,-0.2 -24,0.2;-21,-0.2 -21,0.2;-18,-0.2 -18,0.2;-15,-0.2 -15,0.2;-12,-0.2 -12,0.2;-9,-0.2 -9,0.2;-6,-0.2 -6,0.2;-3,-0.2 -3,0.2;0,-0.3 0,0.3;3,-0.2 3,0.2;6,-0.2 6,0.2;9,-0.2 9,0.2" punti="-3,0,pieno;9,0,pieno;-27,0,pieno" etichette="-0.5,-0.9,0;-4.2,-0.9,−3;8.4,-0.9,+9;-28.2,-0.9,−27;-4.5,0.8,(−3)¹;7.5,0.8,(−3)²;-28,0.8,(−3)³" xmin=-30 xmax=12 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.8 titolo="The powers of $-3$ with exponents $1$, $2$, and $3$ alternate in sign and increase in absolute value ($3$, $9$, $27$)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A power is a product of factors all equal to the base, and its absolute value is the corresponding power of the absolute value of the base. Its sign depends on whether the exponent is even or odd because the exponent counts the negative factors in the product.

In $(-3)^2$, the exponent $2$ is even, so there is an even number of negative factors and the power is **positive**. It equals $3 \cdot 3 = 9$. In $(-3)^3$, the exponent $3$ is odd, so the power is **negative** and equals $-(3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3) = -27$.

In $(-1)^9$, the exponent $9$ is odd, so the power is negative. Its absolute value is $1^9 = 1$, so $(-1)^9 = -1$. In $(-10)^4$, the exponent $4$ is even, and $10^4$ is written as the digit $1$ followed by four zeros, so the power equals $+10\,000$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate both numbers in each pair and compare them:
$$
-4^2 \ \text{and} \ (-4)^2 , \qquad -2^3 \ \text{and} \ (-2)^3 , \qquad -(-3)^2 \ \text{and} \ (-3)^2 .
$$

$$
-4^2 = -(4 \cdot 4) = -16 , \qquad (-4)^2 = (-4) \cdot (-4) = +16
$$
$$
-2^3 = -(2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2) = -8 , \qquad (-2)^3 = (-2) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-2) = -8
$$
$$
-(-3)^2 = -(+9) = -9 , \qquad (-3)^2 = +9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the expression $-4^2$, the base of the power is $4$ because the sign $-$ and the number $4$ are not enclosed in parentheses. First we evaluate the power, $4^2 = 16$, and then take its opposite, so $-4^2 = -16$. In $(-4)^2$, on the other hand, the base is $-4$, and the even exponent gives a positive result, $+16$. The two numbers are therefore **different**, and they are opposites.

In the second pair, $-2^3$ is the opposite of $2^3 = 8$, that is, $-8$. We also have $(-2)^3 = -8$ because the base is negative and the exponent $3$ is odd. With an odd exponent the two numbers are therefore **equal**.

In the third pair, we first evaluate the power $(-3)^2 = +9$ because the exponent is even. The sign $-$ in front denotes the opposite, so $-(-3)^2 = -9$, while $(-3)^2 = +9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate
$$
(-2)^3 + (-3)^2 - (-1)^5 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-2)^3 + (-3)^2 - (-1)^5
&= -8 + (+9) - (-1) \\[6pt]
&= -8 + 9 + 1 \\[6pt]
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the order of operations, we evaluate powers first. The base $-2$ has an odd exponent, so $(-2)^3 = -8$. The base $-3$ has an even exponent, so $(-3)^2 = +9$. The base $-1$ has an odd exponent, and $(-1)^5 = -1$.

Then we remove the parentheses. The sign $+$ before $+9$ leaves the number unchanged, and the sign $-$ before $-1$ changes it to $+1$. In the algebraic sum $-8 + 9 + 1$, the positive terms give $10$, and $10 - 8 = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate
$$
-2^4 + (-2)^4 - (-3)^3 : (-3)^2 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& -2^4 + (-2)^4 - (-3)^3 : (-3)^2 \\[6pt]
={} & -16 + (+16) - (-27) : (+9) \\[6pt]
={} & -16 + 16 - (-3) \\[6pt]
={} & -16 + 16 + 3 \\[6pt]
={} & 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First we evaluate the four powers. In $-2^4$ the base is $2$, so the value is the opposite of $2^4 = 16$, that is, $-16$. In $(-2)^4$ the base is $-2$ and the exponent $4$ is even, so $(-2)^4 = +16$. Then $(-3)^3 = -27$ because the exponent is odd, and $(-3)^2 = +9$ because the exponent is even.

After the powers, we perform the division. In the quotient $(-27) : (+9)$, the numbers have opposite signs and $27 : 9 = 3$, so the quotient is $-3$. The sign $-$ before $-3$ changes it to $+3$.

In the algebraic sum $-16 + 16 + 3$, the first two terms are opposites and their sum is $0$, so the result is $3$.

</div>
