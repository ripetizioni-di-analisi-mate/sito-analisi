## Exercise 1

Write the absolute value and the opposite of each of the numbers $-8$, $+5$, $0$, $-13$.

$$
|-8| = 8 , \qquad -(-8) = +8
$$
$$
|+5| = 5 , \qquad -(+5) = -5
$$
$$
|0| = 0 , \qquad -(0) = 0
$$
$$
|-13| = 13 , \qquad -(-13) = +13
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **absolute value** of an integer is the number written without its sign, so $|-8| = 8$, $|+5| = 5$, and $|-13| = 13$. Zero has no sign, so its absolute value is $0$.

The **opposite** of a number has the same absolute value with its sign reversed. This is why the opposite of $-8$ is $+8$, the opposite of $+5$ is $-5$, and the opposite of $-13$ is $+13$. The opposite of $0$ is still $0$ because zero is the only number that coincides with its reflection across zero on the number line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Insert the symbol $<$ or $>$ between the two numbers in each pair:
$$
-3 \;\ldots\; +1 , \qquad -9 \;\ldots\; -4 , \qquad 0 \;\ldots\; -6 , \qquad +7 \;\ldots\; +2 .
$$

$$
-3 < +1 , \qquad -9 < -4 , \qquad 0 > -6 , \qquad +7 > +2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first pair consists of a negative number and a positive number. Every negative number is less than every positive number, so $-3 < +1$.

In the second pair, both numbers are negative. Of two negative numbers, the greater one has the *smaller* absolute value. Since $4 < 9$, the number $-4$ is greater than $-9$, that is, $-9 < -4$. Indeed, on the number line, $-4$ lies to the right of $-9$.

Zero is greater than every negative number, so $0 > -6$. Of two positive numbers, the greater one has the greater absolute value, so $+7 > +2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the following numbers in increasing order:
$$
+4 , \quad -2 , \quad +7 , \quad -9 , \quad 0 , \quad -5 , \quad +1 .
$$

$$
-9 < -5 < -2 < 0 < +1 < +4 < +7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Increasing order runs from least to greatest, or from left to right on the number line. We first list the negative numbers $-2$, $-9$, $-5$. Among negative numbers, the one with the greater absolute value is smaller, and the absolute values here are $2$, $9$, $5$. Therefore the order of the negative numbers is $-9 < -5 < -2$.

Zero comes after the negative numbers. Finally, we list the positive numbers $+1$, $+4$, $+7$ in the order of their absolute values. We therefore obtain the order $-9 < -5 < -2 < 0 < +1 < +4 < +7$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Write all integers with absolute value less than $3$, in increasing order.

$$
|x| < 3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = -2 , \ -1 , \ 0 , \ +1 , \ +2
$$
$$
-2 < -1 < 0 < +1 < +2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero on the number line. We are therefore looking for the integers less than $3$ units from zero. The possible absolute values are $0$, $1$, and $2$.

Only zero has absolute value $0$. An absolute value of $1$ corresponds to two numbers, $+1$ and $-1$, which are opposites. Similarly, an absolute value of $2$ corresponds to $+2$ and $-2$. There are therefore five such numbers, and in increasing order they are $-2$, $-1$, $0$, $+1$, $+2$. The numbers $-3$ and $+3$ are excluded because their absolute value equals $3$ rather than being less than it.

</div>
