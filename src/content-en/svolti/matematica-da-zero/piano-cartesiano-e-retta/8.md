## Exercise 1

A taxi charges an initial fare of $4$ euros plus $2.50$ euros for each kilometer traveled. Write the function that gives the cost $y$ of a ride of $x$ kilometers. Calculate the cost of a $3$ km ride and the number of kilometers you can travel for $19$ euros.

$$
y = 2.5\,x + 4 = \frac{5}{2}x + 4
$$
$$
x = 3: \quad y = \frac{5}{2} \cdot 3 + 4 = \frac{15}{2} + 4 = 7.5 + 4 = 11.5
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{2}x + 4 &= 19 \\[6pt]
\frac{5}{2}x &= 15 \\[6pt]
x &= 15 \cdot \frac{2}{5} = 6
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2.5*x+4" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=0 ymax=20 nomi="km,euro" punti="3,11.5,pieno;6,19,pieno" segmenti="3,0,3,11.5;0,11.5,3,11.5;6,0,6,19;0,19,6,19" legenda="y = \dfrac{5}{2}x + 4" titolo="The graph of the fare as a function of distance in kilometers is a line with y-intercept $4$ and slope $\dfrac{5}{2}$. A $3$ km ride costs $11.50$ euros, and $19$ euros pays for $6$ km"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The fare consists of a fixed charge of $4$ euros and an amount that increases by $2.50$ euros per kilometer, or $2.5 \cdot x$ euros for $x$ kilometers. Adding the two parts gives $y = 2.5\,x + 4$, whose graph is a **straight line** with slope $2.5 = \dfrac{5}{2}$ and y-intercept $4$. The y-intercept is the initial fare, and the slope is the cost per kilometer.

For $x = 3$, we have $\dfrac{5}{2} \cdot 3 = \dfrac{15}{2} = 7.5$, and adding $4$ gives a cost of $11.50$ euros. For the second question, the cost of $19$ euros is known and we need to find $x$. Subtracting $4$ gives $\dfrac{5}{2}x = 15$, and multiplying by the reciprocal $\dfrac{2}{5}$ gives $x = \dfrac{30}{5} = 6$. Thus, $19$ euros pays for $6$ km.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A gym offers two memberships. The first charges a sign-up fee of $40$ euros and $20$ euros per month. The second charges a sign-up fee of $20$ euros and $30$ euros per month. After how many months is the total cost the same? Which membership costs less for just one month?

$$
\begin{cases}
y = 20x + 40 \\
y = 30x + 20
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
30x + 20 &= 20x + 40 \\[6pt]
10x &= 20 \\[6pt]
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
y = 20 \cdot 2 + 40 = 80
$$
$$
x = 1: \quad 20 \cdot 1 + 40 = 60, \qquad 30 \cdot 1 + 20 = 50
$$

::grafico{f="20*x+40;30*x+20" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=200 nomi="mesi,euro" punti="2,80,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,80" legenda="y = 20x + 40;y = 30x + 20" titolo="The two memberships cost the same after $2$ months, when each costs $80$ euros. Before $2$ months, the second costs less, and afterward the first costs less"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the number of months and $y$ the total cost in euros. For the first membership the cost is $y = 20x + 40$, and for the second it is $y = 30x + 20$. The costs are the same when the two values of $y$ agree, so we look for the solution of the **system**. Both equations give $y$, so substitution allows us to set their right-hand sides equal.

In the equation $30x + 20 = 20x + 40$, subtracting $20x$ and $20$ from both sides gives $10x = 20$, and therefore $x = 2$. Substituting gives $y = 80$. After $2$ months the two memberships **cost the same**, $80$ euros each. For just one month, the first costs $60$ euros and the second $50$ euros, so the **second** costs less. In the graph, the line for the second membership is steeper because its monthly fee is higher, and after $2$ months it rises above the line for the first.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At a stationery store, $9$ large notebooks and $6$ small notebooks cost a total of $39$ euros. Each large notebook costs $1.50$ euros more than a small one. Find the price of each size of notebook.

$$
\begin{cases}
9x + 6y = 39 \\
x = y + \dfrac{3}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
9\left( y + \frac{3}{2} \right) + 6y &= 39 \\[6pt]
9y + \frac{27}{2} + 6y &= 39 \\[6pt]
15y &= 39 - \frac{27}{2} = \frac{51}{2} \\[6pt]
y &= \frac{51}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{15} = \frac{51}{30} = \frac{17}{10}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = \frac{17}{10} + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{17}{10} + \frac{15}{10} = \frac{32}{10} = \frac{16}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the price of a large notebook in euros and $y$ the price of a small one. The total cost gives the first equation, $9x + 6y = 39$. The second piece of information says that $x$ exceeds $y$ by $1.50 = \dfrac{3}{2}$, so $x = y + \dfrac{3}{2}$. Both conditions must hold at the same time, so write them as a **system**.

The second equation gives $x$, so use the **substitution method**. The product $9 \cdot \dfrac{3}{2}$ equals $\dfrac{27}{2}$, and adding $9y$ and $6y$ gives $15y + \dfrac{27}{2} = 39$. Since $39 = \dfrac{78}{2}$, the difference $39 - \dfrac{27}{2}$ equals $\dfrac{51}{2}$. Dividing by $15$ gives $y = \dfrac{51}{30}$, which reduces by a factor of $3$ to $\dfrac{17}{10} = 1.70$. Finally, $x = 1.70 + 1.50 = 3.20$.

A large notebook therefore costs $3.20$ euros and a small one $1.70$ euros. Checking gives $9 \cdot 3.20 + 6 \cdot 1.70 = 28.80 + 10.20 = 39$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

On a square-grid map, Anna's house is at $A(-3, 1)$ and her school is at $S(9, 6)$. The side of each square represents $50$ meters. Find the straight-line distance in meters between the house and the school, and locate the point halfway between them on the map.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{AS} &= \sqrt{\big(9 - (-3)\big)^2 + (6 - 1)^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{169} = 13
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
13 \cdot 50 = 650\,\text{m}
$$
$$
M\left( \frac{-3 + 9}{2}, \frac{1 + 6}{2} \right) = M\left( 3, \frac{7}{2} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The straight-line distance is the length of segment $AS$, so we use the formula for the **distance between two points**. The difference between the abscissas is $9 - (-3) = 12$, and the difference between the ordinates is $6 - 1 = 5$. Their squares are $144$ and $25$, and their sum is $169$. The square root of $169$ is $13$ because $13 \cdot 13 = 169$. The distance on the map is therefore $13$ grid units.

Each side of a square represents $50$ meters, so the actual distance is $13 \cdot 50 = 650$ meters. The point halfway between is the midpoint of the segment. The average of the abscissas is $\dfrac{-3 + 9}{2} = \dfrac{6}{2} = 3$ and the average of the ordinates is $\dfrac{1 + 6}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}$, so the point is $M\left( 3, \dfrac{7}{2} \right)$, which lies $3$ squares to the right and $3.5$ squares above the origin of the map.

</div>
