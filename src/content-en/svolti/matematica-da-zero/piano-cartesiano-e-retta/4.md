## Exercise 1

When possible, write the equations of the lines $3x + 2y - 8 = 0$ and $4x - 12 = 0$ in slope-intercept form. For each, give the slope, if defined, and the y-intercept, if it exists.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 2y - 8 &= 0 \\[6pt]
2y &= -3x + 8 \\[6pt]
y &= -\frac{3}{2}x + 4
\end{aligned}
\qquad\qquad
m = -\frac{3}{2}, \quad q = 4
$$
$$
4x - 12 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 4x = 12 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first equation, isolate $y$. Move $3x$ and $-8$ to the right-hand side, changing their signs, to obtain $2y = -3x + 8$. Then divide both sides by $2$, that is, divide each term by $2$. Dividing the term $-3x$ by $2$ gives $-\dfrac{3}{2}x$, and dividing $8$ by $2$ gives $4$. The **slope** is the coefficient of $x$, so $m = -\dfrac{3}{2}$, and the y-intercept is $q = 4$. The slope is negative, so the line falls.

In the second equation, $y$ is absent, that is, its coefficient is $0$. Adding $12$ and dividing by $4$ gives $x = 3$, the **vertical** line consisting of the points with abscissa $3$. A vertical line cannot be written in the form $y = mx + q$, so its slope is undefined. The line does not cross the $y$-axis because it is parallel to it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the points where the line $2x - 3y - 6 = 0$ crosses the axes, and draw the line.

$$
x = 0: \quad -3y - 6 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad -3y = 6 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad y = -2
$$
$$
y = 0: \quad 2x - 6 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2x = 6 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = 3
$$
$$
\text{points} \quad (0, -2) \quad \text{and} \quad (3, 0)
$$

::grafico{f="2*x/3-2" xmin=-3 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="0,-2,pieno;3,0,pieno" legenda="2x - 3y - 6 = 0" titolo="The line $2x - 3y - 6 = 0$ crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, -2)$ and the $x$-axis at $(3, 0)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Points on the $y$-axis have abscissa $0$, so to find where the line crosses the $y$-axis, substitute $x = 0$. The equation becomes $-3y - 6 = 0$. Moving $-6$ to the right-hand side gives $-3y = 6$, and dividing by $-3$ gives $y = -2$. The first point is therefore $(0, -2)$.

Points on the $x$-axis have ordinate $0$, so substitute $y = 0$. The equation becomes $2x - 6 = 0$, so $2x = 6$ and $x = 3$. The second point is $(3, 0)$. Exactly one line passes through two points, so draw the line through $(0, -2)$ and $(3, 0)$. Its slope-intercept form is $y = \dfrac{2}{3}x - 2$, and the y-intercept $-2$ agrees with the ordinate of the first point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the points $A(1, 3)$ and $B(-1, -1)$ lie on the line $5x - 2y + 1 = 0$. Then find the value of $k$ for which point $C(k, 8)$ lies on the same line.

$$
A(1, 3): \quad 5 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 5 - 6 + 1 = 0
$$
$$
B(-1, -1): \quad 5 \cdot (-1) - 2 \cdot (-1) + 1 = -5 + 2 + 1 = -2 \neq 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
5k - 2 \cdot 8 + 1 &= 0 \\[6pt]
5k - 15 &= 0 \\[6pt]
5k &= 15 \\[6pt]
k &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A point lies on the line when substituting its coordinates for $x$ and $y$ makes the equation true. For $A$, the left-hand side equals $5 - 6 + 1 = 0$, so the equality holds and $A$ **lies** on the line. For $B$, the products are $5 \cdot (-1) = -5$ and $-2 \cdot (-1) = 2$, and the left-hand side equals $-5 + 2 + 1 = -2$. Since $-2$ differs from $0$, point $B$ does not lie on the line.

For point $C$, substitute $x = k$ and $y = 8$. The product $2 \cdot 8$ equals $16$, and $-16 + 1 = -15$, so the equation becomes $5k - 15 = 0$. Adding $15$ gives $5k = 15$, and dividing by $5$ gives $k = 3$. The point is $C(3, 8)$, and indeed $15 - 16 + 1 = 0$.

</div>
