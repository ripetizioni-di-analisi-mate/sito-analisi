## Exercise 1

Identify the quadrant or axis containing each of the points
$$
A(5, -2), \quad B(-1, -6), \quad C(0, 4), \quad D(-3, 0), \quad E(-2, 7) .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(5, -2) &: \quad x > 0, \ y < 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{quadrant IV} \\[6pt]
B(-1, -6) &: \quad x < 0, \ y < 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{quadrant III} \\[6pt]
C(0, 4) &: \quad x = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{axis } y \\[6pt]
D(-3, 0) &: \quad y = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{axis } x \\[6pt]
E(-2, 7) &: \quad x < 0, \ y > 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{quadrant II}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Examine the signs of both coordinates of each point. Point $A$ has a positive abscissa and a negative ordinate, so it lies to the right of the $y$-axis and below the $x$-axis, in **quadrant IV**. Both coordinates of $B$ are negative, so it lies in quadrant III. Point $E$ has a negative abscissa and a positive ordinate, so it lies in quadrant II.

Point $C$ has abscissa $0$, so it is reached from $O$ by moving vertically only and lies on the $y$-axis. Point $D$ has ordinate $0$, so it lies on the $x$-axis. Points on the axes do not belong to any quadrant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Draw the quadrilateral with vertices $A(-2, -1)$, $B(4, -1)$, $C(4, 3)$, and $D(-2, 3)$. Explain why it is a rectangle and find its perimeter and area if one unit on each axis is $1\,\text{cm}$.

$$
\overline{AB} = 4 - (-2) = 6\,\text{cm},
\qquad
\overline{BC} = 3 - (-1) = 4\,\text{cm}
$$
$$
2p = 2 \cdot (6 + 4) = 2 \cdot 10 = 20\,\text{cm}
$$
$$
A = 6 \cdot 4 = 24\,\text{cm}^2
$$

::grafico{xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-3 ymax=5 uguale=1 poligoni="-2,-1 4,-1 4,3 -2,3" punti="-2,-1,pieno,A;4,-1,pieno,B;4,3,pieno,C;-2,3,pieno,D" etichette="1,-1.6,6;4.3,1,4" titolo="The rectangle with vertices $A(-2, -1)$, $B(4, -1)$, $C(4, 3)$, and $D(-2, 3)$ has sides parallel to the axes, with lengths $6$ and $4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Points $A$ and $B$ have the same ordinate $-1$, so side $AB$ is horizontal. Likewise, $D$ and $C$ have the same ordinate $3$, so side $DC$ is horizontal. Points $B$ and $C$ have the same abscissa $4$, so side $BC$ is vertical. The same holds for $AD$, with abscissa $-2$. A horizontal side and a vertical side are perpendicular, so the quadrilateral has four right angles and is a **rectangle**.

The length of the horizontal side $AB$ is the difference between the abscissas, $4 - (-2) = 4 + 2 = 6$. The length of the vertical side $BC$ is the difference between the ordinates, $3 - (-1) = 3 + 1 = 4$. The perimeter is twice the sum of the base and height, or $2 \cdot 10 = 20\,\text{cm}$, and the area is the product of the base and height, or $6 \cdot 4 = 24\,\text{cm}^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Point $P$ has coordinates $P(2k - 6, 5)$, where $k$ is a number. Find the value of $k$ for which $P$ lies on the $y$-axis and the values of $k$ for which $P$ lies in quadrant II.

$$
\text{axis } y: \quad 2k - 6 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2k = 6 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad k = 3
$$
$$
\text{quadrant II}: \quad 2k - 6 < 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2k < 6 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad k < 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A point lies on the $y$-axis when its abscissa is $0$. The abscissa of $P$ is $2k - 6$, so we solve the equation $2k - 6 = 0$. Adding $6$ to both sides gives $2k = 6$, and dividing by $2$ gives $k = 3$. For this value, the point is $P(0, 5)$, which lies on the $y$-axis.

A point lies in quadrant II when its abscissa is negative and its ordinate is positive. The ordinate of $P$ is $5$, which is positive for every value of $k$, so it remains to require that the abscissa be negative. We solve the inequality $2k - 6 < 0$ as in the chapter on linear inequalities. Adding $6$ gives $2k < 6$. Dividing by the positive number $2$ preserves the direction of the inequality, so $k < 3$. For example, when $k = 1$, the point is $P(-4, 5)$, which lies in **quadrant II**.

</div>
