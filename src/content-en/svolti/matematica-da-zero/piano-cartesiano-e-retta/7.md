## Exercise 1

Find the intersection of the lines $y = 2x - 3$ and $3x + y = 7$ by substitution.

$$
\begin{cases}
y = 2x - 3 \\
3x + y = 7
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + (2x - 3) &= 7 \\[6pt]
5x - 3 &= 7 \\[6pt]
5x &= 10 \\[6pt]
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
y = 2 \cdot 2 - 3 = 4 - 3 = 1
$$
$$
\text{point of intersection} \quad (2, 1)
$$

::grafico{f="2*x-3;7-3*x" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="2,1,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,1;0,1,2,1" legenda="y = 2x - 3;3x + y = 7" titolo="The lines $y = 2x - 3$ and $3x + y = 7$ meet at $(2, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coordinates of the intersection satisfy both equations, so write them as a **system**. The first equation already gives $y$ in terms of $x$, so use the **substitution method**. In the second equation, replace $y$ with $2x - 3$ to obtain an equation in the single unknown $x$. Adding $3x$ and $2x$ gives $5x - 3 = 7$. Adding $3$ gives $5x = 10$, and dividing by $5$ gives $x = 2$.

Substitute $x = 2$ into the first equation to obtain $y = 1$. Checking in the second equation gives $3 \cdot 2 + 1 = 7$, so the lines meet at $(2, 1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Use elimination to solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3x + 2y = 4 \\
5x - 2y = 12
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3x + 2y) + (5x - 2y) &= 4 + 12 \\[6pt]
8x &= 16 \\[6pt]
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3 \cdot 2 + 2y &= 4 \\[6pt]
2y &= 4 - 6 = -2 \\[6pt]
y &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the two equations, the coefficients of $y$ are $2$ and $-2$, which are opposites. In the **elimination method**, add the corresponding sides of the equations, so the terms $2y$ and $-2y$ cancel. This leaves $3x + 5x = 8x$ on the left and $4 + 12 = 16$ on the right, so $8x = 16$ and $x = 2$.

To find $y$, substitute $x = 2$ into the first equation. This gives $6 + 2y = 4$. Subtracting $6$ gives $2y = -2$, and dividing by $2$ gives $y = -1$. The solution of the system is therefore $x = 2$, $y = -1$. Checking in the second equation gives $5 \cdot 2 - 2 \cdot (-1) = 10 + 2 = 12$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Use elimination to solve the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 3y = 1 \\
3x + 4y = 2
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
6x + 9y = 3 \\
6x + 8y = 4
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(6x + 9y) - (6x + 8y) &= 3 - 4 \\[6pt]
y &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + 3 \cdot (-1) &= 1 \\[6pt]
2x - 3 &= 1 \\[6pt]
2x &= 4 \\[6pt]
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In this system, neither unknown has equal or opposite coefficients, so first multiply the equations. Multiply the first equation by $3$ and the second by $2$ so that $x$ has coefficient $6$ in both. Multiply every term, including the right-hand side. This gives $6x + 9y = 3$ and $6x + 8y = 4$.

Subtracting the second equation from the first eliminates the $6x$ terms. This leaves $9y - 8y = y$ on the left and $3 - 4 = -1$ on the right, so $y = -1$. Substituting into the first equation of the original system gives $2x - 3 = 1$, so $2x = 4$ and $x = 2$. The solution is $x = 2$, $y = -1$. Checking in the second equation gives $3 \cdot 2 + 4 \cdot (-1) = 6 - 4 = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether each system has a unique solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions, and interpret the result in terms of the lines.
$$
\text{a)}\ \begin{cases}
2x - y = 3 \\
4x - 2y = 1
\end{cases}
\qquad\qquad
\text{b)}\ \begin{cases}
x - 3y = 2 \\
-2x + 6y = -4
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\text{a)} \quad
\begin{aligned}
(4x - 2y) - 2 \cdot (2x - y) &= 1 - 2 \cdot 3 \\[6pt]
0 &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{b)} \quad
\begin{aligned}
(-2x + 6y) + 2 \cdot (x - 3y) &= -4 + 2 \cdot 2 \\[6pt]
0 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In system a), multiply the first equation by $2$ to get $4x - 2y = 6$. Subtracting this equation from the second eliminates both $x$ and $y$ and leaves $0 = 1 - 6$, or $0 = -5$, which is false. No pair of numbers satisfies both equations, so the system is **inconsistent**. In slope-intercept form, the lines are $y = 2x - 3$ and $y = 2x - \dfrac{1}{2}$. The two lines have the same slope $2$ and different y-intercepts, so they are distinct and parallel and never meet.

In system b), multiply the first equation by $2$ to get $2x - 6y = 4$. Adding this equation to the second eliminates both unknowns and leaves $0 = 0$, which holds for every pair. The second equation is the first multiplied by $-2$, so the equations represent the same line and the system has **infinitely many solutions**. Every point on the line $x - 3y = 2$ is a solution, for example $(2, 0)$ and $(5, 1)$.

</div>
