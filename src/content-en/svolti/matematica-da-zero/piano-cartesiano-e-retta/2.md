## Exercise 1

Calculate the distance between $A(-3, 2)$ and $B(5, 2)$ and find the midpoint of segment $AB$.

$$
\overline{AB} = 5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8
$$
$$
M\left( \frac{-3 + 5}{2}, \frac{2 + 2}{2} \right) = M\left( \frac{2}{2}, \frac{4}{2} \right) = M(1, 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two points have the same ordinate $2$, so the segment $AB$ is horizontal. Its length is the difference between the larger abscissa, $5$, and the smaller abscissa, $-3$. Since subtracting $-3$ is equivalent to adding $3$, we obtain $\overline{AB} = 8$.

The coordinates of the **midpoint** are the averages of the corresponding endpoint coordinates. For the abscissa, we calculate $\dfrac{-3 + 5}{2} = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1$, and for the ordinate, $\dfrac{2 + 2}{2} = 2$. Thus, $M(1, 2)$. The point $M$ is at distance $4$ from both endpoints, because $1 - (-3) = 4$ and $5 - 1 = 4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the distance between $A(-2, -3)$ and $B(4, 5)$ and find the midpoint of segment $AB$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{AB} &= \sqrt{\big(4 - (-2)\big)^2 + \big(5 - (-3)\big)^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{36 + 64} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{100} = 10
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
M\left( \frac{-2 + 4}{2}, \frac{-3 + 5}{2} \right) = M\left( \frac{2}{2}, \frac{2}{2} \right) = M(1, 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The points have different abscissas and different ordinates, so we use the formula for the **distance between two points**. The difference between the abscissas is $4 - (-2) = 4 + 2 = 6$, and the difference between the ordinates is $5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8$. These two numbers are the lengths of the legs of the right triangle with hypotenuse $AB$. Their squares are $36$ and $64$, the sum is $100$, and the square root of $100$ is $10$ because $10 \cdot 10 = 100$.

For the midpoint, take the averages of the coordinates. The sum of the abscissas is $-2 + 4 = 2$ and the sum of the ordinates is $-3 + 5 = 2$. Dividing by $2$ gives $M(1, 1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Point $M(2, 1)$ is the midpoint of segment $AB$, and $A$ has coordinates $A(-1, 5)$. Find the coordinates of $B$ and the length of $AB$.

$$
\frac{-1 + x_B}{2} = 2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad -1 + x_B = 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x_B = 5
$$
$$
\frac{5 + y_B}{2} = 1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 5 + y_B = 2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad y_B = -3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{AB} &= \sqrt{\big(5 - (-1)\big)^2 + (-3 - 5)^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{6^2 + (-8)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{100} = 10
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The abscissa of the midpoint is the average of the abscissas of $A$ and $B$. The abscissa of $M$ is known to be $2$, so we write the equation $\dfrac{-1 + x_B}{2} = 2$ with unknown $x_B$. Multiplying both sides by $2$ gives $-1 + x_B = 4$, and adding $1$ gives $x_B = 5$. Similarly, multiplying the equation for the ordinate by $2$ gives $5 + y_B = 2$, and subtracting $5$ gives $y_B = -3$. The required point is therefore $B(5, -3)$.

To find the length, use the distance formula. The differences between the coordinates are $5 - (-1) = 6$ and $-3 - 5 = -8$, and their squares are $36$ and $64$ because the square of $-8$ is positive. Therefore, $\overline{AB} = \sqrt{100} = 10$. To check, observe that $M$ is at distance $5$ from $A$, which is half of $10$, because $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate the perimeter of the triangle with vertices $A(1, 1)$, $B(7, 1)$, and $C(4, 5)$ and determine whether the triangle is isosceles.

$$
\overline{AB} = 7 - 1 = 6
$$
$$
\overline{AC} = \sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (5 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5
$$
$$
\overline{BC} = \sqrt{(4 - 7)^2 + (5 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5
$$
$$
2p = 6 + 5 + 5 = 16
$$

::grafico{xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-1 ymax=6 uguale=1 poligoni="1,1 7,1 4,5" punti="1,1,pieno,A;7,1,pieno,B;4,5,pieno,C" segmenti="4,1,4,5" etichette="3.8,0.4,6;1.9,3.3,5;5.9,3.3,5" titolo="In triangle $ABC$, sides $AC$ and $BC$ have length $5$. The dashed altitude from $C$ meets the base at its midpoint $(4, 1)$ on $AB$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Points $A$ and $B$ have the same ordinate $1$, so side $AB$ is horizontal and has length $7 - 1 = 6$. The other two sides are slanted, so we use the formula for the **distance between two points**. For $AC$, the differences are $4 - 1 = 3$ and $5 - 1 = 4$. For $BC$, they are $4 - 7 = -3$ and $5 - 1 = 4$. In both cases, the sum of the squares is $9 + 16 = 25$, and the square root of $25$ is $5$.

The perimeter is the sum of the three side lengths, $6 + 5 + 5 = 16$. The sides $AC$ and $BC$ have the same length, so the triangle is **isosceles** with base $AB$.

</div>
