## Exercise 1

Complete the table of values for the function $f(x) = 3 - 2x$ for $x = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2$, and plot the resulting points.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-2) &= 3 - 2 \cdot (-2) = 3 + 4 = 7 \\[6pt]
f(-1) &= 3 - 2 \cdot (-1) = 3 + 2 = 5 \\[6pt]
f(0) &= 3 - 2 \cdot 0 = 3 \\[6pt]
f(1) &= 3 - 2 \cdot 1 = 1 \\[6pt]
f(2) &= 3 - 2 \cdot 2 = -1
\end{aligned}
$$

| $x$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f(x)$ | $7$ | $5$ | $3$ | $1$ | $-1$ |

::grafico{f="3-2*x" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=8 punti="-2,7,pieno;-1,5,pieno;0,3,pieno;1,1,pieno;2,-1,pieno" legenda="y = 3 - 2x" titolo="The five points in the table for $f(x) = 3 - 2x$ lie on a line that falls by $2$ for every step of $1$ to the right"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For each value of $x$, substitute the number for $x$ and perform the multiplication first, then the subtraction. For $x = -2$, the product is $2 \cdot (-2) = -4$. Subtracting $-4$ is equivalent to adding $4$, so $f(-2) = 7$. For $x = -1$, the same procedure gives $3 + 2 = 5$. For $x = 0$, $1$, and $2$, the products are $0$, $2$, and $4$, and the results are $3$, $1$, and $-1$.

Each column of the table gives a point on the graph, with $x$ as the abscissa and $f(x)$ as the ordinate. The points are $(-2, 7)$, $(-1, 5)$, $(0, 3)$, $(1, 1)$, and $(2, -1)$. Whenever $x$ increases by $1$, the value of the function decreases by $2$, so the points lie on a **straight line**, the one with equation $y = -2x + 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f(x) = x^2 - 4$. Calculate $f(3)$, $f(-1)$, and $f(-2)$. Then determine whether the points $P(3, 5)$ and $Q(-1, -5)$ lie on the graph of $f$.

$$
f(3) = 3^2 - 4 = 9 - 4 = 5
$$
$$
f(-1) = (-1)^2 - 4 = 1 - 4 = -3
$$
$$
f(-2) = (-2)^2 - 4 = 4 - 4 = 0
$$
$$
P(3, 5): \quad f(3) = 5 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad P \text{ lies on the graph}
$$
$$
Q(-1, -5): \quad f(-1) = -3 \neq -5 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad Q \text{ does not lie on the graph}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When evaluating the function, perform the exponentiation before the subtraction. The square of a negative number is positive, so $(-1)^2 = 1$ and $(-2)^2 = 4$. This gives $f(3) = 5$, $f(-1) = -3$, and $f(-2) = 0$.

A point lies on the graph when its ordinate equals the value of the function at its abscissa. Point $P$ has abscissa $3$ and ordinate $5$, and $f(3) = 5$, so $P$ **lies** on the graph. Point $Q$ has abscissa $-1$ and ordinate $-5$, whereas $f(-1) = -3$. The ordinates differ, so $Q$ **does not lie** on the graph. The point on the graph with abscissa $-1$ is $(-1, -3)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether $A(-1, -2)$ and $B(2, 4)$ lie on the line $y = 3x + 1$. Then find the value of $k$ for which point $C(k, 7)$ lies on the line $y = 2x + 1$.

$$
A(-1, -2): \quad 3 \cdot (-1) + 1 = -3 + 1 = -2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad A \text{ lies on the line}
$$
$$
B(2, 4): \quad 3 \cdot 2 + 1 = 7 \neq 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad B \text{ does not lie on the line}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2k + 1 &= 7 \\[6pt]
2k &= 6 \\[6pt]
k &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To check whether a point lies on a line, substitute its abscissa into the equation and compare the result with its ordinate. For $A$, this gives $3 \cdot (-1) + 1 = -2$, exactly the ordinate of $A$, so $A$ **lies** on the line. For $B$, this gives $7$, whereas the ordinate of $B$ is $4$, so $B$ does not lie on the line.

In the second part, the abscissa of $C$ is unknown. Point $C$ lies on the line $y = 2x + 1$ when its ordinate $7$ equals $2k + 1$, so we solve the equation $2k + 1 = 7$. Subtracting $1$ from both sides gives $2k = 6$, and dividing by $2$ gives $k = 3$. The point is therefore $C(3, 7)$, and indeed $2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7$.

</div>
