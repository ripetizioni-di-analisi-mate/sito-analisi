## Exercise 1

Determine whether the lines in each pair are parallel, perpendicular, or neither.

- $r: y = 4x - 1$ and $s: y = -\dfrac{1}{4}x + 3$;
- $r: y = 2x + 5$ and $s: 4x - 2y + 1 = 0$;
- $r: y = 2x$ and $s: y = -2x + 1$.

$$
4 \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{4} \right) = -1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{perpendicular}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
4x - 2y + 1 &= 0 \\[6pt]
-2y &= -4x - 1 \\[6pt]
2y &= 4x + 1 \\[6pt]
y &= 2x + \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
m_r = m_s = 2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{parallel}
$$
$$
2 \cdot (-2) = -4 \neq -1, \quad 2 \neq -2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{neither parallel nor perpendicular}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first pair, the slopes are $4$ and $-\dfrac{1}{4}$. Their product is $-\dfrac{4}{4} = -1$, so the lines are **perpendicular**.

In the second pair, the line $s$ is in implicit form, so first solve for $y$. Moving $4x + 1$ to the right-hand side gives $-2y = -4x - 1$, and changing the signs on both sides gives $2y = 4x + 1$. Dividing by $2$ gives $y = 2x + \dfrac{1}{2}$. The two lines have the same slope $2$ and different y-intercepts, $5$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so they are **parallel** and distinct.

In the third pair, the slopes $2$ and $-2$ are different, so the lines are not parallel. Their product is $-4$, which differs from $-1$, so the lines are not perpendicular either.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $r$ be the line $y = -3x + 4$. Write the equations of the line parallel to $r$ and the line perpendicular to $r$ that pass through $P(2, 1)$.

$$
\text{parallel}: \quad m = -3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 1 &= -3\,(x - 2) \\[6pt]
y - 1 &= -3x + 6 \\[6pt]
y &= -3x + 7
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{perpendicular}: \quad m' = -\frac{1}{-3} = \frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 1 &= \frac{1}{3}\,(x - 2) \\[6pt]
y - 1 &= \frac{1}{3}x - \frac{2}{3} \\[6pt]
y &= \frac{1}{3}x + \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-3*x+7;x/3+1/3" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="2,1,pieno,P" legenda="y = -3x + 7;y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + \dfrac{1}{3}" titolo="Through $P(2, 1)$ pass both the line parallel to $y = -3x + 4$, with slope $-3$, and the perpendicular line, with slope $\dfrac{1}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two parallel lines have the same slope, so the line parallel to $r$ has $m = -3$. Using the **point-slope formula** gives $y - 1 = -3\,(x - 2)$. The product $-3\,(x - 2)$ is $-3x + 6$, and adding $1$ to both sides gives $y = -3x + 7$.

The slope of the perpendicular line is the negative reciprocal of $-3$. The reciprocal of $-3$ is $-\dfrac{1}{3}$, and its negative is $\dfrac{1}{3}$. The point-slope formula gives $y - 1 = \dfrac{1}{3}\,(x - 2)$, and the product on the right-hand side is $\dfrac{1}{3}x - \dfrac{2}{3}$. Adding $1 = \dfrac{3}{3}$ gives $-\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{3}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3}$, so the perpendicular line is $y = \dfrac{1}{3}x + \dfrac{1}{3}$. Checking at $P$ gives $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{3} = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $r$ be the line $x + 2y - 4 = 0$, and let $P(4, 3)$. Write the equations of the line parallel to $r$ and the line perpendicular to $r$ that pass through $P$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 2y - 4 &= 0 \\[6pt]
2y &= -x + 4 \\[6pt]
y &= -\frac{1}{2}x + 2
\end{aligned}
\qquad\qquad
m = -\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 3 &= -\frac{1}{2}\,(x - 4) \\[6pt]
y - 3 &= -\frac{1}{2}x + 2 \\[6pt]
y &= -\frac{1}{2}x + 5
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
m' = -\frac{1}{-\frac{1}{2}} = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 3 &= 2\,(x - 4) \\[6pt]
y - 3 &= 2x - 8 \\[6pt]
y &= 2x - 5
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line $r$ is in implicit form, so first solve for $y$ to read off the slope. Moving $x - 4$ to the right-hand side gives $2y = -x + 4$, and dividing by $2$ gives $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + 2$. The slope of $r$ is therefore $m = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The parallel line through $P$ has the same slope. The product $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,(x - 4)$ equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}x + 2$, and adding $3$ gives $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + 5$.

For the perpendicular line, find the negative reciprocal of $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. The reciprocal of $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $-2$, and its negative is $2$. Indeed, $-\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 = -1$. The point-slope formula gives $y - 3 = 2x - 8$, so the **perpendicular line** is $y = 2x - 5$. Checking at $P$ gives $2 \cdot 4 - 5 = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the value of $k$ for which the line $y = (k - 1)\,x + 3$ is parallel to the line $y = 5x - 2$, and the value of $k$ for which it is perpendicular to the line $y = 2x + 1$.

$$
\text{parallel}: \quad k - 1 = 5 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad k = 6
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{perpendicular}: \quad (k - 1) \cdot 2 &= -1 \\[6pt]
k - 1 &= -\frac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
k &= 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope of the given line is the coefficient of $x$, namely $k - 1$. Two lines are parallel when they have the same slope, so set $k - 1 = 5$. Adding $1$ to both sides gives $k = 6$. For this value, the line is $y = 5x + 3$, **parallel** to $y = 5x - 2$.

Two lines are perpendicular when the product of their slopes is $-1$, so set $(k - 1) \cdot 2 = -1$. Dividing by $2$ gives $k - 1 = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, and adding $1$ gives $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$. For this value, the slope is $\dfrac{1}{2} - 1 = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the line is $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + 3$, **perpendicular** to $y = 2x + 1$.

</div>
