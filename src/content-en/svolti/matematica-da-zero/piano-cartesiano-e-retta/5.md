## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the line through $P(-2, 5)$ with slope $m = -3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 5 &= -3\,\big(x - (-2)\big) \\[6pt]
y - 5 &= -3\,(x + 2) \\[6pt]
y - 5 &= -3x - 6 \\[6pt]
y &= -3x - 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the **point-slope formula** $y - y_0 = m\,(x - x_0)$ with $x_0 = -2$, $y_0 = 5$, and $m = -3$. Since subtracting $-2$ is equivalent to adding $2$, the right-hand side contains $x + 2$. Multiplying $-3$ by each term gives $-3x - 6$. Finally, add $5$ to both sides. Since $-6 + 5 = -1$, the equation of the line is $y = -3x - 1$.

Check the result by substituting the abscissa of $P$. When $x = -2$, we have $y = -3 \cdot (-2) - 1 = 6 - 1 = 5$, the ordinate of $P$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the line through $A(1, -1)$ and $B(4, 5)$.

$$
m = \frac{5 - (-1)}{4 - 1} = \frac{6}{3} = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - (-1) &= 2\,(x - 1) \\[6pt]
y + 1 &= 2x - 2 \\[6pt]
y &= 2x - 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two points have different abscissas, so the line is not vertical, and its slope can be calculated using the formula for a **line through two points**. The difference between the ordinates is $5 - (-1) = 6$ and the difference between the abscissas is $4 - 1 = 3$, so $m = \dfrac{6}{3} = 2$. In words, going from $A$ to $B$ takes us $3$ units to the right and $6$ units up.

Next, use the point-slope formula with the point $A$. The term $y - (-1)$ equals $y + 1$, and $2\,(x - 1) = 2x - 2$. Subtracting $1$ from both sides gives $y = 2x - 3$. Checking at $B$ gives $2 \cdot 4 - 3 = 5$, the ordinate of $B$, so $B$ also lies on the line.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the equation of the line through $A(-2, 3)$ and $B(4, 0)$ in implicit form with integer coefficients.

$$
m = \frac{0 - 3}{4 - (-2)} = \frac{-3}{6} = -\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 3 &= -\frac{1}{2}\,(x + 2) \\[6pt]
y - 3 &= -\frac{1}{2}x - 1 \\[6pt]
y &= -\frac{1}{2}x + 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2y &= -x + 4 \\[6pt]
x + 2y - 4 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-x/2+2" xmin=-3 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="-2,3,pieno,A;4,0,pieno,B" segmenti="-2,3,4,3;4,3,4,0" etichette="1,3.35,6;4.2,1.4,3" legenda="x + 2y - 4 = 0" titolo="Going from $A(-2, 3)$ to $B(4, 0)$ takes us $6$ units to the right and $3$ units down, so $m = \dfrac{-3}{6} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference between the ordinates is $0 - 3 = -3$ and the difference between the abscissas is $4 - (-2) = 6$. The slope is therefore $\dfrac{-3}{6}$, which reduces by a factor of $3$ to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Using the point-slope formula with the point $A$ gives $y - 3 = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,(x + 2)$. The product of $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $x + 2$ is $-\dfrac{1}{2}x - 1$ because $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 = 1$. Adding $3$ to both sides gives the slope-intercept form $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + 2$.

To obtain integer coefficients, multiply both sides by $2$ to get $2y = -x + 4$. Moving all terms to the left-hand side gives the **implicit form** $x + 2y - 4 = 0$. Checking at $B(4, 0)$ gives $4 + 0 - 4 = 0$, and checking at $A(-2, 3)$ gives $-2 + 6 - 4 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Write the equation of the line through $A(0, 1)$ and $B(2, 5)$ and determine whether the points $A$, $B$, and $C(5, 11)$ are collinear, that is, whether they lie on the same line.

$$
m = \frac{5 - 1}{2 - 0} = \frac{4}{2} = 2,
\qquad
q = 1
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
y = 2x + 1
$$
$$
C(5, 11): \quad 2 \cdot 5 + 1 = 10 + 1 = 11
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope of line $AB$ is $\dfrac{4}{2} = 2$. Point $A$ has abscissa $0$, so it lies on the $y$-axis, and its ordinate $1$ is the **y-intercept** of the line. Thus, we can write the slope-intercept form $y = 2x + 1$ directly, without using the point-slope formula.

The three points are collinear when $C$ also lies on line $AB$. Substituting the abscissa $5$ of $C$ gives $2 \cdot 5 + 1 = 11$, exactly the ordinate of $C$. Thus, $C$ lies on the line and the three points **are collinear**.

</div>
