## Exercise 1

Calculate $\dfrac{3}{8} + \dfrac{1}{8}$ and $\dfrac{2}{5} + \dfrac{1}{3}$.

$$
\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{3 + 1}{8} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{3}
&= \frac{6}{15} + \frac{5}{15} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{6 + 5}{15} = \frac{11}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first sum, the two fractions have the same denominator, so we add the numerators and keep the denominator $8$. We simplify the result $\dfrac{4}{8}$ by dividing by $4$, giving $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

In the second sum, the denominators $5$ and $3$ are different. Their least common multiple is $15$ because they are distinct primes. Since $15 : 5 = 3$, the fraction $\dfrac{2}{5}$ becomes $\dfrac{2 \cdot 3}{15} = \dfrac{6}{15}$. Since $15 : 3 = 5$, the fraction $\dfrac{1}{3}$ becomes $\dfrac{5}{15}$. Adding the numerators gives $\dfrac{11}{15}$, which is already in lowest terms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\dfrac{5}{6} - \dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{1}{3}$.

$$
\operatorname{mcm}(6, 4, 3) = 12
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{6} - \frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{3}
&= \frac{10}{12} - \frac{9}{12} + \frac{4}{12} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{10 - 9 + 4}{12} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{5}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators are $6 = 2 \cdot 3$, $4 = 2^2$, and $3$. The least common multiple includes $2$ raised to the greatest exponent and the factor $3$, so it equals $2^2 \cdot 3 = 12$.

We rewrite each fraction with denominator $12$. We multiply by $2$ in $\dfrac{5}{6}$, by $3$ in $\dfrac{3}{4}$, and by $4$ in $\dfrac{1}{3}$. We perform the operations on the numerators from left to right, so $10 - 9 = 1$ and then $1 + 4 = 5$. The result is $\dfrac{5}{12}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $3 - \dfrac{7}{4} + \dfrac{5}{6}$.

$$
3 = \frac{3}{1} ,
\qquad
\operatorname{mcm}(1, 4, 6) = 12
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3 - \frac{7}{4} + \frac{5}{6}
&= \frac{36}{12} - \frac{21}{12} + \frac{10}{12} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{36 - 21 + 10}{12} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{25}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We write the integer $3$ as a fraction with denominator $1$. The least common multiple of $4$ and $6$ is $12$, so $3 = \dfrac{3 \cdot 12}{12} = \dfrac{36}{12}$, $\dfrac{7}{4} = \dfrac{21}{12}$, and $\dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{10}{12}$.

In the numerator we calculate $36 - 21 = 15$ and then $15 + 10 = 25$. The result $\dfrac{25}{12}$ is an improper fraction because $25 > 12$. The numbers $25 = 5^2$ and $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$ have no common prime factors, so the fraction is in lowest terms.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $-\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{5}{6}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} - \frac{5}{6}
&= -\frac{8}{12} + \frac{3}{12} - \frac{10}{12} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{-8 + 3 - 10}{12} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{-15}{12} \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{5}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The least common denominator of $3$, $4$, and $6$ is $12$. The three fractions become $\dfrac{8}{12}$, $\dfrac{3}{12}$, and $\dfrac{10}{12}$, and each retains its sign.

In the numerator, we add integers. The signs of $-8$ and $3$ differ, so we subtract their absolute values, $8 - 3 = 5$, and keep the sign of $-8$. Therefore $-8 + 3 = -5$, and then $-5 - 10 = -15$.

The numerator is negative and the denominator positive, so by the rule of signs the fraction is negative. Finally, we divide $15$ and $12$ by $3$, giving $-\dfrac{5}{4}$.

</div>
