## Exercise 1

Verify that the fractions $\dfrac{6}{10}$, $\dfrac{12}{20}$, and $\dfrac{21}{35}$ are equivalent to $\dfrac{3}{5}$. Then find the missing number in the equality $\dfrac{4}{9} = \dfrac{\ \square\ }{36}$.

$$
\frac{3 \cdot 2}{5 \cdot 2} = \frac{6}{10} ,
\qquad
\frac{3 \cdot 4}{5 \cdot 4} = \frac{12}{20} ,
\qquad
\frac{3 \cdot 7}{5 \cdot 7} = \frac{21}{35}
$$
$$
36 : 9 = 4
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{4}{9} = \frac{4 \cdot 4}{9 \cdot 4} = \frac{16}{36}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $10$ is $5 \cdot 2$, and the numerator $6$ is $3 \cdot 2$. We therefore obtain $\dfrac{6}{10}$ from $\dfrac{3}{5}$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $2$, and by the **invariance property** the two fractions are equivalent. Similarly, $\dfrac{12}{20}$ is obtained by multiplying by $4$, and $\dfrac{21}{35}$ by multiplying by $7$.

In the second part, the denominator changes from $9$ to $36$. Since $36 : 9 = 4$, the denominator has been multiplied by $4$. To obtain an equivalent fraction, we must also multiply the numerator by $4$, so the missing number is $16$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Reduce the fraction $\dfrac{30}{42}$ to lowest terms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{30}{42}
&= \frac{30 : 2}{42 : 2} = \frac{15}{21} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{15 : 3}{21 : 3} = \frac{5}{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numbers $30$ and $42$ are both even, so they can be divided by $2$. This gives $\dfrac{15}{21}$, which has the same value by the invariance property.

The sum of the digits of $15$ is $6$ and that of $21$ is $3$, so both numbers are divisible by $3$. Dividing gives $\dfrac{5}{7}$. The numbers $5$ and $7$ are distinct primes, so their only common divisor is $1$ and the fraction $\dfrac{5}{7}$ is **in lowest terms**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Reduce the fraction $\dfrac{150}{360}$ to lowest terms using the greatest common divisor.

$$
150 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2 ,
\qquad
360 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(150, 360) = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30
$$
$$
\frac{150}{360} = \frac{150 : 30}{360 : 30} = \frac{5}{12}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We factor both numbers into primes. We have $150 = 2 \cdot 75 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 25 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2$ and $360 = 8 \cdot 45 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$.

The common factors are $2$, $3$, and $5$. The greatest common divisor is obtained by taking each factor with its smaller exponent. For all three factors that exponent is $1$ in at least one of the two factorizations, so $\operatorname{MCD}(150, 360) = 30$.

Dividing numerator and denominator by $30$ gives $\dfrac{5}{12}$ in a single step because $150 : 30 = 5$ and $360 : 30 = 12$. After this division no common factor remains, so the fraction $\dfrac{5}{12}$ is in lowest terms.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether the fractions in each pair are equivalent: $\dfrac{14}{21}$ and $\dfrac{22}{33}$, then $\dfrac{15}{24}$ and $\dfrac{10}{18}$.

$$
\frac{14}{21} = \frac{14 : 7}{21 : 7} = \frac{2}{3} ,
\qquad
\frac{22}{33} = \frac{22 : 11}{33 : 11} = \frac{2}{3}
$$
$$
\frac{15}{24} = \frac{15 : 3}{24 : 3} = \frac{5}{8} ,
\qquad
\frac{10}{18} = \frac{10 : 2}{18 : 2} = \frac{5}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two fractions are equivalent if they give the same fraction when reduced to lowest terms. In the first pair, $14$ and $21$ are multiples of $7$, while $22$ and $33$ are multiples of $11$. Both fractions become $\dfrac{2}{3}$, so $\dfrac{14}{21}$ and $\dfrac{22}{33}$ **are equivalent**.

In the second pair, $\dfrac{15}{24}$ becomes $\dfrac{5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{10}{18}$ becomes $\dfrac{5}{9}$. The reduced fractions have the same numerator and different denominators, so they have different values, and $\dfrac{15}{24}$ and $\dfrac{10}{18}$ **are not equivalent**.

</div>
