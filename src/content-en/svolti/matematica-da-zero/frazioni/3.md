## Exercise 1

Determine which of the two fractions $\dfrac{5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{7}{12}$ is greater.

$$
\operatorname{mcm}(8, 12) = 24
$$
$$
\frac{5}{8} = \frac{5 \cdot 3}{8 \cdot 3} = \frac{15}{24} ,
\qquad
\frac{7}{12} = \frac{7 \cdot 2}{12 \cdot 2} = \frac{14}{24}
$$
$$
15 > 14
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{5}{8} > \frac{7}{12}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,1.6 15,1.6 15,2.6 0,2.6;0,0 14,0 14,1 0,1" linee="0,1.6 24,1.6 24,2.6 0,2.6 0,1.6;3,1.6 3,2.6;6,1.6 6,2.6;9,1.6 9,2.6;12,1.6 12,2.6;15,1.6 15,2.6;18,1.6 18,2.6;21,1.6 21,2.6;0,0 24,0 24,1 0,1 0,0;2,0 2,1;4,0 4,1;6,0 6,1;8,0 8,1;10,0 10,1;12,0 12,1;14,0 14,1;16,0 16,1;18,0 18,1;20,0 20,1;22,0 22,1" etichette="24.8,2.1,5 eighths = 15 out of 24;24.8,0.5,7 twelfths = 14 out of 24" xmin=-0.3 xmax=40 ymin=-0.3 ymax=2.9 titolo="With the same whole divided into $24$ parts, $\dfrac{5}{8}$ takes up $15$ parts and $\dfrac{7}{12}$ takes up $14$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two fractions have different denominators, so we rewrite them with their **least common denominator**. Since $8 = 2^3$ and $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, the least common multiple is $2^3 \cdot 3 = 24$.

We have $24 : 8 = 3$, so we multiply the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ by $3$, giving $\dfrac{15}{24}$. Similarly, $24 : 12 = 2$, so $\dfrac{7}{12}$ becomes $\dfrac{14}{24}$.

The parts now have the same size, and $15$ parts are more than $14$. Consequently, $\dfrac{5}{8}$ is **greater** than $\dfrac{7}{12}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the fractions $\dfrac{2}{3}$, $\dfrac{3}{5}$, $\dfrac{7}{10}$, and $\dfrac{4}{15}$ in increasing order.

$$
\operatorname{mcm}(3, 5, 10, 15) = 30
$$
$$
\frac{2}{3} = \frac{20}{30} ,
\qquad
\frac{3}{5} = \frac{18}{30} ,
\qquad
\frac{7}{10} = \frac{21}{30} ,
\qquad
\frac{4}{15} = \frac{8}{30}
$$
$$
8 < 18 < 20 < 21
$$
$$
\frac{4}{15} < \frac{3}{5} < \frac{2}{3} < \frac{7}{10}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $30$ is a multiple of $3$, $5$, $10$, and $15$. No smaller positive number is a multiple of all four because the only positive multiple of $15$ less than $30$ is $15$, which is not a multiple of $10$. Therefore the least common denominator is $30$.

We rewrite each fraction by multiplying its numerator and denominator by the quotient of $30$ and its denominator. We multiply by $10$ in $\dfrac{2}{3}$, by $6$ in $\dfrac{3}{5}$, by $3$ in $\dfrac{7}{10}$, and by $2$ in $\dfrac{4}{15}$.

The four fractions now have the same denominator, so their order is that of their numerators, $8 < 18 < 20 < 21$. The increasing order is therefore $\dfrac{4}{15} < \dfrac{3}{5} < \dfrac{2}{3} < \dfrac{7}{10}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compare the fractions $\dfrac{11}{13}$ and $\dfrac{6}{7}$ using cross products.

$$
11 \cdot 7 = 77 ,
\qquad
13 \cdot 6 = 78
$$
$$
77 < 78
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{11}{13} < \frac{6}{7}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators $13$ and $7$ are coprime, so their common denominator is their product, $13 \cdot 7 = 91$. With this denominator the two fractions become $\dfrac{11 \cdot 7}{91} = \dfrac{77}{91}$ and $\dfrac{6 \cdot 13}{91} = \dfrac{78}{91}$.

To compare them, we therefore need only the two numerators, that is, the **cross products** $11 \cdot 7 = 77$ and $13 \cdot 6 = 78$. Since $77 < 78$, the fraction $\dfrac{11}{13}$ is **less** than $\dfrac{6}{7}$. The two fractions are very close because their difference is $\dfrac{78}{91} - \dfrac{77}{91} = \dfrac{1}{91}$.

</div>
