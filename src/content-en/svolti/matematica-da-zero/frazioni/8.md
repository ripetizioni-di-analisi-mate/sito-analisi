## Exercise 1

A tank contains $36$ liters of water and is filled to $\dfrac{4}{9}$ of its capacity. Find the capacity of the tank.

$$
\frac{36}{4} = 9 \text{ liters}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
9 \cdot 9 = 81 \text{ liters}
$$
$$
36 : \frac{4}{9} = 36 \cdot \frac{9}{4} = 9 \cdot 9 = 81 \text{ liters}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 4,0 4,1 0,1" linee="0,0 9,0 9,1 0,1 0,0;1,0 1,1;2,0 2,1;3,0 3,1;4,0 4,1;5,0 5,1;6,0 6,1;7,0 7,1;8,0 8,1" etichette="0.4,-0.55,4 parts = 36 liters;4.6,-0.55,9 parts = 81 liters" xmin=-0.3 xmax=16 ymin=-1 ymax=1.4 titolo="The $4$ shaded parts contain $36$ liters, so each part holds $9$ and the $9$ parts of the tank hold $81$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

We know the part, $36$ liters, and want to find the whole. The $36$ liters correspond to $4$ parts, each $\dfrac{1}{9}$ of the whole, so one part contains $\dfrac{36}{4} = 9$ liters. The full tank consists of $9$ parts, so its capacity is $9 \cdot 9 = 81$ liters.

Dividing the part by the fraction gives the same number because $36 \cdot \dfrac{9}{4} = 9 \cdot 9$. The capacity of the tank is therefore $81$ liters.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A shirt is discounted by $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of its price and costs $36$ euros after the discount. Find the price before the discount.

$$
1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}
$$
$$
\frac{36}{3} = 12 \text{ euros}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
12 \cdot 4 = 48 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discount is $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the full price, so the customer pays $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the full price. The $36$ euros therefore correspond to $3$ parts, each one fourth of the price.

One part is worth $\dfrac{36}{3} = 12$ euros, and the full price consists of $4$ parts, or $12 \cdot 4 = 48$ euros. The price before the discount is $48$ euros. As a check, the discount is $\dfrac{48}{4} = 12$ euros and $48 - 12 = 36$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Anna spends $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of her savings on a phone and then $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of what is left on a pair of headphones. In the end she has $120$ euros left. Find the amount of her original savings.

$$
1 - \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{5} ,
\qquad
1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}
$$
$$
\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
120 : \frac{2}{5} = 120 \cdot \frac{5}{2} = 60 \cdot 5 = 300 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After buying the phone, Anna has $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of her savings left. The second fraction, $\dfrac{1}{3}$, refers to what is left rather than to her original savings. After buying the headphones, she therefore has $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of $\dfrac{3}{5}$ of her savings left, or $\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{2}{5}$ of her savings.

The fraction $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of her savings is worth $120$ euros. One fifth is therefore worth $\dfrac{120}{2} = 60$ euros, and all her savings are worth $60 \cdot 5 = 300$ euros. Her original savings amounted to $300$ euros.

As a check, the phone costs $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of $300$ euros, or $120$ euros, leaving $180$ euros. The headphones cost one third of $180$ euros, or $60$ euros, leaving $180 - 60 = 120$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 4

To fence a vegetable garden, $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of the fencing has already been put up and $14$ m are still needed. Find the total length of the fence.

$$
1 - \frac{5}{6} = \frac{1}{6}
$$
$$
14 \cdot 6 = 84 \text{ m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fencing already in place is $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of the fence, so the missing part is $\dfrac{6}{6} - \dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{1}{6}$ of the fence.

The remaining $14$ m therefore make up one sixth. The entire fence consists of $6$ equal parts, so its length is $14 \cdot 6 = 84$ m. The total length of the fence is $84$ m.

</div>
