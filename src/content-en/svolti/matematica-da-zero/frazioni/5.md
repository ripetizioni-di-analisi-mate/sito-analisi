## Exercise 1

Calculate $\dfrac{8}{21} \cdot \dfrac{7}{12}$ and $4 \cdot \dfrac{5}{6}$.

$$
\frac{8}{21} \cdot \frac{7}{12} = \frac{2 \cdot 1}{3 \cdot 3} = \frac{2}{9}
$$
$$
4 \cdot \frac{5}{6} = \frac{4}{1} \cdot \frac{5}{6} = \frac{2 \cdot 5}{1 \cdot 3} = \frac{10}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first product, we simplify before multiplying. The numerator $8$ and denominator $12$ are divisible by $4$ and become $2$ and $3$. The numerator $7$ and denominator $21$ are divisible by $7$ and become $1$ and $3$. This **cross-cancellation** is possible because the product is a single fraction with all these factors above and below the bar. Multiplying the remaining numerators and denominators gives $\dfrac{2}{9}$.

In the second product we write the number $4$ as $\dfrac{4}{1}$. The numbers $4$ and $6$ are divisible by $2$ and become $2$ and $3$. The product is $\dfrac{10}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\dfrac{9}{10} : \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{4}{7} : 2$.

$$
\frac{9}{10} : \frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{10} \cdot \frac{5}{3} = \frac{3 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 1} = \frac{3}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{4}{7} : 2 = \frac{4}{7} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2 \cdot 1}{7 \cdot 1} = \frac{2}{7}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To divide by a fraction, we multiply by its **reciprocal**. The reciprocal of $\dfrac{3}{5}$ is $\dfrac{5}{3}$, so the first division becomes $\dfrac{9}{10} \cdot \dfrac{5}{3}$. We cross-cancel $9$ with $3$ by dividing by $3$, and $5$ with $10$ by dividing by $5$. The result is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

We write the number $2$ as $\dfrac{2}{1}$ and its reciprocal is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. We cancel a common factor from $4$ and $2$, giving $\dfrac{2}{7}$. Dividing by $2$ means taking half, and indeed half of four sevenths is two sevenths.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^2 \cdot \dfrac{8}{27}$ and $\left( -\dfrac{2}{3} \right)^3 : \dfrac{4}{9}$.

$$
\left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^2 \cdot \frac{8}{27} = \frac{9}{16} \cdot \frac{8}{27} = \frac{1 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 3} = \frac{1}{6}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left( -\frac{2}{3} \right)^3 : \frac{4}{9}
&= -\frac{8}{27} : \frac{4}{9} \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{8}{27} \cdot \frac{9}{4} \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{2 \cdot 1}{3 \cdot 1} = -\frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We evaluate the power before the product. We have $\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^2 = \dfrac{3^2}{4^2} = \dfrac{9}{16}$. In the product we cancel a common factor from $9$ and $27$ by dividing by $9$, and from $8$ and $16$ by dividing by $8$, so the result is $\dfrac{1}{6}$.

In the second expression the base is negative and the exponent $3$ is odd, so the power is negative and equals $-\dfrac{2^3}{3^3} = -\dfrac{8}{27}$. Dividing by $\dfrac{4}{9}$ becomes multiplying by $\dfrac{9}{4}$. We cross-cancel $8$ with $4$ by dividing by $4$, and $9$ with $27$ by dividing by $9$. The product of a negative and a positive number is negative, so the result is $-\dfrac{2}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $\dfrac{5}{6} \cdot \dfrac{9}{10} : \dfrac{3}{8}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{9}{10} : \frac{3}{8}
&= \frac{1 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 2} : \frac{3}{8} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3}{4} : \frac{3}{8} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{8}{3} \\[6pt]
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We multiply and divide from left to right in the order in which these operations appear. In the product $\dfrac{5}{6} \cdot \dfrac{9}{10}$, we cross-cancel $5$ with $10$ by dividing by $5$, and $9$ with $6$ by dividing by $3$. This leaves $\dfrac{1 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 2} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

We then divide by $\dfrac{3}{8}$, that is, multiply by its reciprocal $\dfrac{8}{3}$. The $3$ in the numerator cancels with the $3$ in the denominator, and we simplify the $8$ in the numerator with the $4$ in the denominator because $8 : 4 = 2$. The result is the integer $2$.

</div>
