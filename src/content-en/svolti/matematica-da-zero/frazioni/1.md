## Exercise 1

Classify the fractions $\dfrac{3}{8}$, $\dfrac{17}{5}$, $\dfrac{12}{3}$, and $\dfrac{6}{6}$ as proper, improper, or equal to an integer.

$$
3 < 8 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{3}{8} \text{ is proper}
$$
$$
17 > 5 , \quad 17 : 5 = 3 \text{ remainder } 2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{17}{5} \text{ is improper}
$$
$$
12 = 3 \cdot 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{12}{3} \text{ equals an integer, } \frac{12}{3} = 4
$$
$$
6 = 6 \cdot 1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{6}{6} \text{ equals an integer, } \frac{6}{6} = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each fraction, we compare the numerator with the denominator. In $\dfrac{3}{8}$, the numerator $3$ is less than the denominator $8$, so the fraction is **proper** and is less than $1$.

In $\dfrac{17}{5}$, the numerator is greater than the denominator, so the fraction is **improper**. The division $17 : 5$ has remainder $2$, so $17$ is not a multiple of $5$ and the fraction does not equal an integer.

The numerator $12$ is a multiple of the denominator $3$ because $12 = 3 \cdot 4$, so $\dfrac{12}{3}$ **equals an integer** and its value is $4$. Likewise, $6$ is a multiple of $6$ because $6 = 6 \cdot 1$, so $\dfrac{6}{6}$ equals an integer and its value is $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write each of the fractions $\dfrac{17}{5}$ and $\dfrac{29}{6}$ as the sum of an integer and a proper fraction, and identify the consecutive integers between which each fraction lies.

$$
17 : 5 = 3 \text{ with remainder } 2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{17}{5} = 3 + \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
29 : 6 = 4 \text{ with remainder } 5
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{29}{6} = 4 + \frac{5}{6}
$$
$$
3 < \frac{17}{5} < 4 ,
\qquad
4 < \frac{29}{6} < 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the division $17 : 5$, the quotient is $3$ because $5 \cdot 3 = 15$, and the remainder is $17 - 15 = 2$. Therefore $17$ fifths make $3$ wholes, or $15$ fifths, with $2$ fifths left over. Hence $\dfrac{17}{5} = 3 + \dfrac{2}{5}$.

In the division $29 : 6$, the quotient is $4$ because $6 \cdot 4 = 24$, and the remainder is $29 - 24 = 5$. Therefore $\dfrac{29}{6} = 4 + \dfrac{5}{6}$.

The remaining proper fraction is greater than $0$ and less than $1$. Consequently, $\dfrac{17}{5}$ lies between $3$ and $4$, and $\dfrac{29}{6}$ lies between $4$ and $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write all the fractions with denominator $4$ between $1$ and $2$, excluding the endpoints, and plot them on the number line.

$$
1 = \frac{4}{4} ,
\qquad
2 = \frac{8}{4}
$$
$$
\frac{4}{4} < \frac{5}{4} < \frac{6}{4} < \frac{7}{4} < \frac{8}{4}
$$
$$
\frac{5}{4} , \qquad \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2} , \qquad \frac{7}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare $1$ and $2$ with fractions whose denominator is $4$, we write them in fourths as well. One whole contains $4$ fourths, so $1 = \dfrac{4}{4}$ and $2 = \dfrac{8}{4}$.

The fractions with denominator $4$ between $\dfrac{4}{4}$ and $\dfrac{8}{4}$ have numerators greater than $4$ and less than $8$ because all the parts have the same size. The possible numerators are $5$, $6$, and $7$, so the fractions we want are $\dfrac{5}{4}$, $\dfrac{6}{4}$, and $\dfrac{7}{4}$. The second one can be simplified by dividing its numerator and denominator by $2$, giving $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

On the number line, we divide the segment from $1$ to $2$ into $4$ equal parts. The three interior division points correspond to the three fractions we found.

</div>
