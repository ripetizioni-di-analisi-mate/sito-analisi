## Exercise 1

A recipe for $4$ people calls for $300$ g of flour and $120$ g of sugar. Calculate how much flour and sugar are needed to make the same recipe for $3$ people.

$$
\frac{3}{4} \cdot 300 = \frac{300}{4} \cdot 3 = 75 \cdot 3 = 225 \text{ g}
$$
$$
\frac{3}{4} \cdot 120 = \frac{120}{4} \cdot 3 = 30 \cdot 3 = 90 \text{ g}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given amounts serve $4$ people, and $3$ people are $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of $4$ people. Therefore we need $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the given amount of each ingredient.

To find $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of $300$ g, we divide by the denominator to obtain the amount for one person, $\dfrac{300}{4} = 75$ g. Then we multiply by the numerator, so $3$ people need $225$ g of flour. Similarly, one person needs $\dfrac{120}{4} = 30$ g of sugar, so $3$ people need $90$ g of sugar.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electricity bill totals $96$ euros. Heating accounts for $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the bill, hot water for $\dfrac{1}{4}$, and lighting for the rest. Find the cost of each item and the fraction of the bill spent on lighting.

$$
\frac{3}{8} \cdot 96 = 12 \cdot 3 = 36 \text{ euros} ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{4} \cdot 96 = 24 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
96 - 36 - 24 = 36 \text{ euros}
$$
$$
1 - \frac{3}{8} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{8}{8} - \frac{3}{8} - \frac{2}{8} = \frac{3}{8}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 5,0 5,1 0,1" linee="0,0 8,0 8,1 0,1 0,0;1,0 1,1;2,0 2,1;3,0 3,1;4,0 4,1;5,0 5,1;6,0 6,1;7,0 7,1" etichette="0.3,-0.45,heating;3.4,-0.45,water;5.6,-0.45,lighting" xmin=-0.3 xmax=8.3 ymin=-0.8 ymax=1.3 titolo="The bill in eighths, with $3$ eighths for heating, $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{2}{8}$ for hot water, and the remaining $3$ eighths for lighting"}

<div class="spiegazione">

One eighth of the bill is $\dfrac{96}{8} = 12$ euros, so $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of it is $12 \cdot 3 = 36$ euros. One fourth of $96$ euros is $\dfrac{96}{4} = 24$ euros. The cost of lighting is what remains, or $96 - 36 - 24 = 36$ euros.

We find the fraction spent on lighting by subtracting the other two fractions from the whole, whose value is $1 = \dfrac{8}{8}$. The least common denominator of $8$ and $4$ is $8$, and $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{2}{8}$, so $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the bill remains. This agrees with the calculation in euros because heating also accounts for $\dfrac{3}{8}$ of the bill and the cost is the same, $36$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cyclist has to travel $42$ km. On the first day he covers $\dfrac{2}{7}$ of the route and on the second day $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the route. Find how many kilometers remain and what fraction of the route they represent.

$$
\frac{2}{7} \cdot 42 = 6 \cdot 2 = 12 \text{ km} ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{3} \cdot 42 = 14 \text{ km}
$$
$$
42 - 12 - 14 = 16 \text{ km}
$$
$$
1 - \frac{2}{7} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{21}{21} - \frac{6}{21} - \frac{7}{21} = \frac{8}{21}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both fractions refer to the entire route of $42$ km. One seventh of the route is $\dfrac{42}{7} = 6$ km, so the cyclist travels $6 \cdot 2 = 12$ km on the first day. One third of the route is $\dfrac{42}{3} = 14$ km. He travels $12 + 14 = 26$ km in two days, so $16$ km remain.

We find the remaining fraction by subtracting the two fractions from the whole. The least common denominator of $7$ and $3$ is $21$, giving $\dfrac{8}{21}$ of the route. As a check, $\dfrac{8}{21}$ of $42$ km is $\dfrac{42}{21} \cdot 8 = 2 \cdot 8 = 16$ km.

</div>
