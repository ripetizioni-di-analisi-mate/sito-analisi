## Exercise 1

Calculate
$$
\sqrt{144} ,
\qquad
\sqrt{\frac{49}{81}} ,
\qquad
\sqrt[3]{-64} .
$$

$$
\sqrt{144} = 12
\qquad
\text{because } 12 \geq 0 \text{ and } 12^2 = 144
$$
$$
\sqrt{\frac{49}{81}} = \frac{7}{9}
\qquad
\text{because } \frac{7}{9} \geq 0 \text{ and } \left( \frac{7}{9} \right)^2 = \frac{49}{81}
$$
$$
\sqrt[3]{-64} = -4
\qquad
\text{because } (-4)^3 = -64
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $144$ appears among the squares of the numbers from $1$ to $15$ and is the square of $12$. Since $12$ is positive, the definition of the **square root** gives $\sqrt{144} = 12$.

To square a fraction, square its numerator and denominator. Since $7^2 = 49$ and $9^2 = 81$, the square of $\dfrac{7}{9}$ is $\dfrac{49}{81}$, so $\sqrt{\dfrac{49}{81}} = \dfrac{7}{9}$.

The **cube root** also exists for negative numbers. The cube of $-4$ is $(-4) \cdot (-4) \cdot (-4) = 16 \cdot (-4) = -64$, so $\sqrt[3]{-64} = -4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\sqrt{1764}$ using prime factorization.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
1764 & 2 \\
882 & 2 \\
441 & 3 \\
147 & 3 \\
49 & 7 \\
7 & 7 \\
1 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
1764 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7^2
$$
$$
\sqrt{1764} = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 42
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When factoring in columns, divide at each step by the smallest prime factor of the number. The number $1764$ is even, so dividing by $2$ gives $882$, which is also even and gives $441$. The digits of $441$ add to $9$, so $441$ is divisible by $3$, giving $147$. For the same reason, $147$ gives $49$. Finally, $49 = 7 \cdot 7$.

In the factorization $1764 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7^2$, every exponent is even, so $1764$ is a **perfect square**. Halving the exponents gives its square root, and we obtain $\sqrt{1764} = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 42$. The result is correct because $42^2 = 1764$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\sqrt[3]{2744}$ using prime factorization.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
2744 & 2 \\
1372 & 2 \\
686 & 2 \\
343 & 7 \\
49 & 7 \\
7 & 7 \\
1 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
2744 = 2^3 \cdot 7^3
$$
$$
\sqrt[3]{2744} = 2 \cdot 7 = 14
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing $2744$ by $2$ three times gives $343$. The number $343$ is odd and divisible by neither $3$ nor $5$, while $343 = 7 \cdot 49 = 7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7$. Therefore $2744 = 2^3 \cdot 7^3$.

Every exponent is a multiple of $3$, so $2744$ is a **perfect cube**. Dividing the exponents by $3$ gives the cube root, and we obtain $\sqrt[3]{2744} = 2 \cdot 7 = 14$. The result is correct because $14^3 = 196 \cdot 14 = 2744$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the two consecutive integers that bound $\sqrt{58}$, then find the two consecutive tenths that bound it.

$$
7^2 = 49 , \qquad 8^2 = 64
$$
$$
49 < 58 < 64 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 7 < \sqrt{58} < 8
$$
$$
7.6^2 = 57.76 , \qquad 7.7^2 = 59.29
$$
$$
57.76 < 58 < 59.29 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 7.6 < \sqrt{58} < 7.7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Of two positive numbers, the one with the larger square is larger. The perfect squares closest to $58$ are $49 = 7^2$ and $64 = 8^2$. Since $58$ lies between $49$ and $64$, the root $\sqrt{58}$ lies between $7$ and $8$.

The number $58$ is closer to $64$ than to $49$, so try decimals starting at $7.5$ and working upward. We have $7.6^2 = 7.6 \cdot 7.6 = 57.76$, which is less than $58$, and $7.7^2 = 7.7 \cdot 7.7 = 59.29$, which is greater than $58$. Therefore $\sqrt{58}$ lies between $7.6$ and $7.7$.

</div>
