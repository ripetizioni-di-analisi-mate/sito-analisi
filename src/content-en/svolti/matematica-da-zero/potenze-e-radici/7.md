## Exercise 1

Rationalize
$$
\frac{14}{\sqrt{7}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{14}{\sqrt{7}}
&= \frac{14 \cdot \sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{7} \cdot \sqrt{7}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{14\sqrt{7}}{7} \\[6pt]
&= 2\sqrt{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is $\sqrt{7}$, so multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{7}$. By the **invariance property** of fractions, the new fraction is equivalent to the original one.

The denominator becomes $\sqrt{7} \cdot \sqrt{7} = 7$ because the square of a square root is the radicand. The number $14$ is divisible by $7$ with quotient $2$, so the fraction simplifies and the result is $2\sqrt{7}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rationalize
$$
\frac{5}{2\sqrt{10}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{2\sqrt{10}}
&= \frac{5 \cdot \sqrt{10}}{2\sqrt{10} \cdot \sqrt{10}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{5\sqrt{10}}{2 \cdot 10} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{5\sqrt{10}}{20} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sqrt{10}}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The only radical in the denominator is $\sqrt{10}$, while the factor $2$ is already an integer. Therefore multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{10}$. The denominator becomes $2 \cdot \sqrt{10} \cdot \sqrt{10} = 2 \cdot 10 = 20$.

The numerator $5\sqrt{10}$ and denominator $20$ share the factor $5$. Dividing both by $5$ gives $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize
$$
\frac{3}{\sqrt{12}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{\sqrt{12}}
&= \frac{3}{2\sqrt{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3 \cdot \sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2 \cdot 3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{6} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Rationalization changes only the way the number is written"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Before rationalizing, simplify the root in the denominator. Since $12 = 4 \cdot 3$, taking out the factor gives $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$.

The radical in the denominator is now $\sqrt{3}$, so multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3}$. The denominator becomes $2 \cdot 3 = 6$ and the numerator becomes $3\sqrt{3}$. The common factor $3$ cancels, and the result is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate
$$
\frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} - \sqrt{27} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} - \sqrt{27} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
&= \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} - 3\sqrt{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \\[6pt]
&= 2\sqrt{3} - 3\sqrt{3} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{6\sqrt{3} - 9\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{3}}{3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(6 - 9 + 1)\sqrt{3}}{3} \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first term has a radical in the denominator. Multiplying the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3}$ gives $\dfrac{6\sqrt{3}}{3}$, which simplifies to $2\sqrt{3}$. The second term simplifies by taking a factor out of the radical because $27 = 9 \cdot 3$ and therefore $\sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3}$.

All three terms contain $\sqrt{3}$ and can be added using the common denominator $3$. Write $2\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{6\sqrt{3}}{3}$ and $3\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{9\sqrt{3}}{3}$. Combine the **like radicals** in the numerator, and since $6 - 9 + 1 = -2$, the result is $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

</div>
