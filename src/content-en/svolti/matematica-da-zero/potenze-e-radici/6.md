## Exercise 1

Simplify $\sqrt{48}$ and $\sqrt{200}$ by taking all possible factors out of each radical.

$$
\sqrt{48} = \sqrt{16 \cdot 3} = \sqrt{16} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
\sqrt{200} = \sqrt{100 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{100} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 10\sqrt{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To **take a factor out** of a radical, write the radicand as the product of its largest perfect-square factor and another number. Since $48 = 2^4 \cdot 3$, the largest perfect-square factor of $48$ is $2^4 = 16$, and $48 = 16 \cdot 3$. The rule for the root of a product gives $\sqrt{16 \cdot 3} = \sqrt{16} \cdot \sqrt{3}$, and since $\sqrt{16} = 4$, the result is $4\sqrt{3}$.

Similarly, $200 = 2^3 \cdot 5^2$, and the largest perfect-square factor of $200$ is $2^2 \cdot 5^2 = 100$. Since $\sqrt{100} = 10$, we have $\sqrt{200} = 10\sqrt{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{50} - \sqrt{18}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{8} + \sqrt{50} - \sqrt{18}
&= \sqrt{4 \cdot 2} + \sqrt{25 \cdot 2} - \sqrt{9 \cdot 2} \\[6pt]
&= 2\sqrt{2} + 5\sqrt{2} - 3\sqrt{2} \\[6pt]
&= (2 + 5 - 3)\sqrt{2} \\[6pt]
&= 4\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,1.3,2.828,1.3,√8;2.828,0.9,9.899,0.9,√50;9.899,0.5,5.657,0.5,√18" linee="0,0 10.4,0;5.657,-0.05 5.657,0.05" punti="5.657,0,pieno" etichette="5.1,-0.4,4√2" xmin=-0.5 xmax=11 ymin=-0.6 ymax=1.8 titolo="Move forward to add the lengths $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$, then move backward to subtract the length $\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$, arriving at $4\sqrt{2} \approx 5.66$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The three radicands are different, so their radicals cannot be combined immediately. Each radicand is the product of a perfect square and $2$ because $8 = 4 \cdot 2$, $50 = 25 \cdot 2$ and $18 = 9 \cdot 2$. Taking out the square roots of $4$, $25$ and $9$, namely $2$, $5$ and $3$, gives three radicals with radicand $2$.

The three radicals are **like** radicals, so by the distributive property their coefficients can be added. Since $2 + 5 - 3 = 4$, the result is $4\sqrt{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\sqrt{6} \cdot \sqrt{3} + \sqrt{98}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{6} \cdot \sqrt{3} + \sqrt{98}
&= \sqrt{6 \cdot 3} + \sqrt{98} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{18} + \sqrt{98} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{9 \cdot 2} + \sqrt{49 \cdot 2} \\[6pt]
&= 3\sqrt{2} + 7\sqrt{2} \\[6pt]
&= 10\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the order of operations, multiplication precedes addition. Reading the rule for the **root of a product** from right to left gives $\sqrt{6} \cdot \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{18}$.

Then take factors out of the radicals. Since $18 = 9 \cdot 2$ and $\sqrt{9} = 3$, we have $\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$. Since $98 = 49 \cdot 2$ and $\sqrt{49} = 7$, we have $\sqrt{98} = 7\sqrt{2}$. The two radicals are like radicals, so their sum is $(3 + 7)\sqrt{2} = 10\sqrt{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate
$$
\sqrt{75} - 2\sqrt{27} + \frac{\sqrt{60}}{\sqrt{5}} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{75} - 2\sqrt{27} + \frac{\sqrt{60}}{\sqrt{5}}
&= \sqrt{25 \cdot 3} - 2\sqrt{9 \cdot 3} + \sqrt{\frac{60}{5}} \\[6pt]
&= 5\sqrt{3} - 2 \cdot 3\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{12} \\[6pt]
&= 5\sqrt{3} - 6\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} \\[6pt]
&= 5\sqrt{3} - 6\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} \\[6pt]
&= (5 - 6 + 2)\sqrt{3} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first radicand is $75 = 25 \cdot 3$, so $\sqrt{75} = 5\sqrt{3}$. The second is $27 = 9 \cdot 3$, so $\sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3}$, and the term $2\sqrt{27}$ becomes $2 \cdot 3\sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3}$.

Reading the rule for the **root of a quotient** from right to left gives $\dfrac{\sqrt{60}}{\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{60}{5}} = \sqrt{12}$. Since $12 = 4 \cdot 3$, we obtain $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$.

The three radicals have radicand $3$, so they are like radicals. Their coefficients add to $5 - 6 + 2 = 1$, so the result is $1 \cdot \sqrt{3} = \sqrt{3}$.

</div>
