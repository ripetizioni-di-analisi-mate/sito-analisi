## Exercise 1

Calculate $2^4 \cdot 5^4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^4 \cdot 5^4
&= (2 \cdot 5)^4 \\[6pt]
&= 10^4 \\[6pt]
&= 10\,000
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two powers have different bases and the *same exponent* $4$. Reading the **power of a product** rule from right to left, multiply the bases and keep the exponent. Since $2 \cdot 5 = 10$, this gives $10^4$.

The power $10^4$ is written as the digit $1$ followed by four zeros, so the result is $10\,000$. The direct calculation $16 \cdot 625$ gives the same number.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate
$$
\left( \frac{2}{5} \right)^3 \cdot \left( \frac{5}{4} \right)^3 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left( \frac{2}{5} \right)^3 \cdot \left( \frac{5}{4} \right)^3
&= \left( \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{4} \right)^3 \\[6pt]
&= \left( \frac{2 \cdot 5}{5 \cdot 4} \right)^3 \\[6pt]
&= \left( \frac{2}{4} \right)^3 \\[6pt]
&= \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^3 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1^3}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two powers have the same exponent $3$, so by the **power of a product** rule, multiply the bases first. In the product of the two fractions, the factor $5$ appears in both numerator and denominator, so it cancels, leaving $\dfrac{2}{4}$.

The fraction $\dfrac{2}{4}$ in lowest terms is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. By the **power of a quotient** rule, cube the numerator and denominator. Since $1^3 = 1$ and $2^3 = 8$, the result is $\dfrac{1}{8}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate
$$
\frac{6^5}{2^5 \cdot 3^3} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6^5}{2^5 \cdot 3^3}
&= \frac{(2 \cdot 3)^5}{2^5 \cdot 3^3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2^5 \cdot 3^5}{2^5 \cdot 3^3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3^5}{3^3} \\[6pt]
&= 3^{5-3} \\[6pt]
&= 3^2 = 9
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the base $6$ as the product $2 \cdot 3$. By the **power of a product** rule, we have $6^5 = 2^5 \cdot 3^5$.

The factor $2^5$ occurs in both numerator and denominator, so it cancels because $\dfrac{2^5}{2^5} = 1$. This leaves the quotient $\dfrac{3^5}{3^3}$, in which we subtract the exponents. Since $5 - 3 = 2$, the result is $3^2 = 9$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate
$$
\frac{4^3 \cdot 9^2}{6^4} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4^3 \cdot 9^2}{6^4}
&= \frac{\left( 2^2 \right)^3 \cdot \left( 3^2 \right)^2}{2^4 \cdot 3^4} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2^6 \cdot 3^4}{2^4 \cdot 3^4} \\[6pt]
&= 2^{6-4} \\[6pt]
&= 2^2 = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the bases $4$, $9$ and $6$ using the primes $2$ and $3$. Since $4 = 2^2$ and $9 = 3^2$, the **power of a power** rule gives $4^3 = 2^{2 \cdot 3} = 2^6$ and $9^2 = 3^{2 \cdot 2} = 3^4$. Since $6 = 2 \cdot 3$, the **power of a product** rule gives $6^4 = 2^4 \cdot 3^4$.

The factor $3^4$ appears in both numerator and denominator, so it cancels. This leaves $\dfrac{2^6}{2^4}$, and by the quotient rule the exponent is $6 - 4 = 2$. The result is therefore $2^2 = 4$.

</div>
