## Exercise 1

Calculate $4^{-1} + 2^{-2} + 7^0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{-1} + 2^{-2} + 7^0
&= \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2^2} + 1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + 1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1 + 1 + 4}{4} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of a **negative exponent**, we have $4^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{4^1} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ and $2^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{2^2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. By the definition of a **zero exponent**, we have $7^0 = 1$.

Add the three fractions using the common denominator $4$ by writing $1 = \dfrac{4}{4}$. The numerator is $1 + 1 + 4 = 6$. The fraction $\dfrac{6}{4}$ simplifies on dividing numerator and denominator by $2$, so the result is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate
$$
\left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{-2} - \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{0} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{-2} - \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{0}
&= \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{2} - 1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{9}{4} - 1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{9 - 4}{4} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{5}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A power with a negative exponent is the reciprocal of the power with the corresponding positive exponent. The reciprocal of $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is $\dfrac{3}{2}$, so $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{-2} = \left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^{2} = \dfrac{3^2}{2^2} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.

The base $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is nonzero, so by the definition of a **zero exponent** the second power equals $1$. Write $1 = \dfrac{4}{4}$, and the difference is $\dfrac{9 - 4}{4} = \dfrac{5}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate
$$
\frac{2^{-3} \cdot 2^5}{2^4} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2^{-3} \cdot 2^5}{2^4}
&= \frac{2^{-3+5}}{2^4} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2^2}{2^4} \\[6pt]
&= 2^{2-4} \\[6pt]
&= 2^{-2} = \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The properties of powers still hold with a negative exponent. In the numerator the bases are the same, so add the exponents. Since $-3 + 5 = 2$, the numerator is $2^2$.

In the quotient, subtract the exponents. Since $2 - 4 = -2$, this gives $2^{-2}$. By the definition of a **negative exponent**, we have $2^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{2^2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The result agrees with direct calculation because $2^{-3} \cdot 2^5 = \dfrac{1}{8} \cdot 32 = 4$ and $\dfrac{4}{2^4} = \dfrac{4}{16} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate
$$
\frac{3^{-2} \cdot 9^2}{\left( 3^{-1} \right)^3} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3^{-2} \cdot 9^2}{\left( 3^{-1} \right)^3}
&= \frac{3^{-2} \cdot \left( 3^2 \right)^2}{3^{-3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3^{-2} \cdot 3^4}{3^{-3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3^{2}}{3^{-3}} \\[6pt]
&= 3^{2-(-3)} \\[6pt]
&= 3^{5} = 243
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write all the powers with base $3$. Since $9 = 3^2$, the **power of a power** rule gives $9^2 = 3^{2 \cdot 2} = 3^4$. Similarly, $\left( 3^{-1} \right)^3 = 3^{(-1) \cdot 3} = 3^{-3}$.

In the numerator, add the exponents. Since $-2 + 4 = 2$, this gives $3^2$. In the quotient, subtract the exponents. Their difference is $2 - (-3) = 2 + 3 = 5$ because subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding its opposite. The result is $3^5 = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 243$.

</div>
