## Exercise 1

A square vegetable garden has an area of $20.25$ m². Find its side length and the length of fencing needed to enclose it, in meters.

$$
\ell = \sqrt{20.25} = \sqrt{\frac{2025}{100}} = \frac{\sqrt{2025}}{\sqrt{100}}
$$
$$
2025 = 3^4 \cdot 5^2
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\sqrt{2025} = 3^2 \cdot 5 = 45
$$
$$
\ell = \frac{45}{10} = 4.5 \text{ m}
$$
$$
2p = 4 \cdot 4.5 = 18 \text{ m}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 4.5,0 4.5,4.5 0,4.5" linee="0,0 4.5,0 4.5,4.5 0,4.5 0,0" etichette="1.7,-0.55,4.5 m;4.75,2.15,4.5 m;1.3,2.15,20.25 m²" xmin=-0.6 xmax=6.2 ymin=-1 ymax=5 titolo="The square garden of area $20.25$ m² has side length $\sqrt{20.25} = 4.5$ m and perimeter $4 \cdot 4.5 = 18$ m"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a square is the square of its side length, so the side length $\ell$ is the positive number whose square is $20.25$, that is, the **square root** of $20.25$. Write the decimal as a fraction, $20.25 = \dfrac{2025}{100}$, and apply the rule for the root of a quotient to find the square roots of the numerator and denominator separately.

The number $2025$ ends in $25$, so it is divisible by $25 = 5^2$, and the quotient is $81 = 3^4$. The exponents are even in the factorization $2025 = 3^4 \cdot 5^2$, so halving them gives $\sqrt{2025} = 3^2 \cdot 5 = 45$. Since $\sqrt{100} = 10$, the side length is $\dfrac{45}{10} = 4.5$ m.

The fence follows all four sides, so its length is the perimeter, $2p = 4 \cdot 4.5 = 18$ m. Therefore **18 meters of fencing** are needed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A laboratory culture contains $500$ bacteria, and their number doubles every hour. The number of bacteria after $n$ hours is $500 \cdot 2^n$. Find the number of bacteria after $6$ hours and the number $2$ hours before the start, assuming the population also doubled every hour before the start.

$$
500 \cdot 2^6 = 500 \cdot 64 = 32\,000
$$
$$
500 \cdot 2^{-2} = 500 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = 125
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each hour the number of bacteria is multiplied by $2$, so after $6$ hours it has been multiplied by $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 2^6 = 64$. The number of bacteria after $6$ hours is therefore $500 \cdot 64 = 32\,000$.

Two hours before the start corresponds to $n = -2$. By the definition of a **negative exponent**, we have $2^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, so the number of bacteria was $500 \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} = 125$. This agrees with the doubling rate because $125$ becomes $250$ after one hour and $500$ after two hours. Two hours before the start there were therefore **125** bacteria.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cube-shaped box has a volume of $3375$ cm³. Find its edge length and the area of paper needed to cover every face.

$$
3375 = 3^3 \cdot 5^3
$$
$$
\ell = \sqrt[3]{3375} = 3 \cdot 5 = 15 \text{ cm}
$$
$$
A = 6 \cdot \ell^2 = 6 \cdot 15^2 = 6 \cdot 225 = 1350 \text{ cm}^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of a cube is the cube of its edge length, so the edge length is the **cube root** of the volume. The number $3375$ ends in $5$, so it is divisible by $5$. Dividing three times by $5$ gives $675$, $135$ and $27$. Since $27 = 3^3$, we have $3375 = 5^3 \cdot 3^3$.

The exponents are multiples of $3$, so the cube root can be found by dividing them by $3$, giving an edge length of $3 \cdot 5 = 15$ cm. The cube has $6$ square faces of side length $15$ cm, each with area $15^2 = 225$ cm². Therefore the paper must cover an area of $6 \cdot 225 = 1350$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 4

Light travels about $3 \cdot 10^5$ km in one second, and the average distance between Earth and the Sun is about $1.5 \cdot 10^8$ km. Calculate how many seconds it takes sunlight to reach Earth, and express the result in minutes and seconds.

$$
t = \frac{1.5 \cdot 10^8}{3 \cdot 10^5}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
t
&= \frac{1.5}{3} \cdot 10^{8-5} \\[6pt]
&= 0.5 \cdot 10^3 \\[6pt]
&= 500 \text{ s}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
500 = 8 \cdot 60 + 20
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
t = 8 \text{ min } 20 \text{ s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Light travels $3 \cdot 10^5$ km each second, so the time in seconds is the quotient of the total distance and the distance traveled in one second. The quotient of the coefficients is $\dfrac{1.5}{3} = 0.5$, and the rule for the **quotient of powers with the same base** gives $\dfrac{10^8}{10^5} = 10^{3}$.

To calculate the product $0.5 \cdot 10^3$, move the decimal point three places to the right, giving $500$ seconds. There are $60$ seconds in a minute, and dividing $500$ by $60$ gives quotient $8$ and remainder $20$ because $8 \cdot 60 = 480$ and $500 - 480 = 20$. Sunlight therefore takes about **8 minutes and 20 seconds**.

</div>
