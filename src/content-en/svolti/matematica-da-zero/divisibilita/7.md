## Exercise 1

The floor of a rectangular room measures $420$ cm by $300$ cm. It is to be covered with the largest possible identical square tiles without cutting any tiles. What is the side length of a tile, and how many tiles are needed?

$$
420 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7, \qquad 300 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(420, 300) = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 60
$$
$$
420 : 60 = 7, \qquad 300 : 60 = 5, \qquad 7 \cdot 5 = 35
$$

Each tile has side length $60$ cm, and $35$ tiles are needed.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 7,0 7,5 0,5" linee="0,0 7,0 7,5 0,5 0,0" segmenti="1,0,1,5;2,0,2,5;3,0,3,5;4,0,4,5;5,0,5,5;6,0,6,5;0,1,7,1;0,2,7,2;0,3,7,3;0,4,7,4" etichette="2.8,-0.8,420 cm;-2.3,2.4,300 cm;0.15,5.35,60 cm" xmin=-2.6 xmax=7.5 ymin=-1.4 ymax=6 titolo="The room measuring $420$ cm by $300$ cm covered by $7 \cdot 5 = 35$ square tiles of side length $\operatorname{MCD}(420, 300) = 60$ cm"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The tiles cannot be cut, so a whole number of tiles must fit along the $420$ cm side, and the side length of a tile must be a divisor of $420$. For the same reason it must be a divisor of $300$. The side length we seek is therefore the greatest common divisor, or **GCD**.

The factorizations are $420 = 42 \cdot 10 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 \cdot 2 \cdot 5$ and $300 = 3 \cdot 100 = 3 \cdot 2^2 \cdot 5^2$. The common factors with their smaller exponents are $2^2$, $3$, and $5$, so the GCD is $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 60$.

There are $420 : 60 = 7$ tiles along the longer side and $300 : 60 = 5$ along the shorter side. The tiles therefore form $5$ rows of $7$, and in all we need $35$ tiles with side length $60$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bakery has $48$ cookies and $60$ chocolates and wants to make as many identical bags as possible, using all the sweets. How many bags can it make, and what goes in each one?

$$
48 = 2^4 \cdot 3, \qquad 60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(48, 60) = 2^2 \cdot 3 = 12
$$
$$
48 : 12 = 4, \qquad 60 : 12 = 5
$$

The bakery can make $12$ bags, each with $4$ cookies and $5$ chocolates.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bags are identical, so the $48$ cookies are divided equally among them and the number of bags must be a divisor of $48$. Likewise, it must be a divisor of $60$. The largest possible number of bags is therefore $\operatorname{MCD}(48, 60)$.

The number $48$ factors as $16 \cdot 3 = 2^4 \cdot 3$ and $60$ as $4 \cdot 15 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$. The common factors with their smaller exponents are $2^2$ and $3$, so the GCD is $12$. Each bag contains $48 : 12 = 4$ cookies and $60 : 12 = 5$ chocolates, and $12$ bags are made.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three ribbons of lengths $90$ cm, $135$ cm, and $225$ cm are to be cut into pieces of equal length, as long as possible, with no waste. How long is each piece, and how many pieces are there altogether?

$$
90 = 2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5, \qquad 135 = 3^3 \cdot 5, \qquad 225 = 3^2 \cdot 5^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(90, 135, 225) = 3^2 \cdot 5 = 45
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
90 : 45 &= 2 \\[6pt]
135 : 45 &= 3 \\[6pt]
225 : 45 &= 5 \\[6pt]
2 + 3 + 5 &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Each piece is $45$ cm long, and there are $10$ pieces.

<div class="spiegazione">

The length of one piece must divide each of the three lengths because every ribbon is cut without waste. The greatest possible length is therefore the **GCD** of the three numbers.

The factorizations are $90 = 2 \cdot 45 = 2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$, $135 = 27 \cdot 5 = 3^3 \cdot 5$, and $225 = 9 \cdot 25 = 3^2 \cdot 5^2$. The factor $2$ appears only in $90$, so it does not enter the GCD. The factor $3$ appears in all three with smallest exponent $2$, and $5$ with smallest exponent $1$. Therefore the GCD is $9 \cdot 5 = 45$.

The first ribbon gives $2$ pieces, the second $3$, and the third $5$, so there are $10$ pieces of length $45$ cm in all.

</div>
