## Exercise 1

Marco goes to the gym every $4$ days and Anna every $10$ days. They met at the gym today. In how many days will they meet there again?

$$
4 = 2^2, \qquad 10 = 2 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm}(4, 10) = 2^2 \cdot 5 = 20
$$

Marco and Anna will meet again in $20$ days.

<div class="spiegazione">

Counting from today, Marco goes to the gym after $4, 8, 12, 16, 20, \dots$ days, that is, at multiples of $4$ days. Anna goes after $10, 20, 30, \dots$ days, at multiples of $10$ days. They meet when the number of days is a multiple of both numbers, and the first such number is the **LCM**.

The factor $2$ has exponent $2$ in $4$ and exponent $1$ in $10$, so take $2^2$. The factor $5$ appears only in $10$. Therefore $\operatorname{mcm}(4, 10) = 4 \cdot 5 = 20$, and they will meet again in $20$ days.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two bus routes leave from a terminal. Buses on route A leave every $12$ minutes and those on route B every $18$ minutes. They leave together at $8.00$. At what time will they next leave together?

$$
12 = 2^2 \cdot 3, \qquad 18 = 2 \cdot 3^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm}(12, 18) = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 = 36
$$
$$
8.00 + 36 \text{ minutes} = 8.36
$$

The two buses will next leave together at $8.36$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Buses on route A leave after a number of minutes that is a multiple of $12$, and buses on route B after a number of minutes that is a multiple of $18$. The first simultaneous departure after $8.00$ therefore occurs after $\operatorname{mcm}(12, 18)$ minutes.

By the **LCM rule**, take $2^2$ because the larger exponent of $2$ occurs in $12$, and $3^2$ because the larger exponent of $3$ occurs in $18$. This gives $4 \cdot 9 = 36$. In the meantime, a bus on route A departs $36 : 12 = 3$ times and a bus on route B departs $36 : 18 = 2$ times. Adding $36$ minutes to $8.00$ gives the time $8.36$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Plates for a party come in packs of $12$, cups in packs of $15$, and napkins in packs of $20$. We want the same number of plates, cups, and napkins and can buy only whole packs. What is the smallest number of each item we can buy, and how many packs of each are needed?

$$
12 = 2^2 \cdot 3, \qquad 15 = 3 \cdot 5, \qquad 20 = 2^2 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm}(12, 15, 20) = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 60
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
60 : 12 &= 5 \\[6pt]
60 : 15 &= 4 \\[6pt]
60 : 20 &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

At least $60$ of each item are needed, that is, $5$ packs of plates, $4$ packs of cups, and $3$ packs of napkins.

<div class="spiegazione">

When whole packs of plates are bought, the number of plates is a multiple of $12$. Likewise, the number of cups is a multiple of $15$ and the number of napkins is a multiple of $20$. The common quantity for all three items must therefore be a common multiple of $12$, $15$, and $20$, and the smallest is the **LCM**.

The factorizations contain $2$, $3$, and $5$, with largest exponents $2$, $1$, and $1$. Therefore the LCM is $4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 60$. Dividing $60$ by the number of items in each type of pack gives $5$ packs of plates, $4$ packs of cups, and $3$ packs of napkins.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The number of eggs collected on a farm in one day is greater than $100$ and less than $150$. The eggs can all be put in cartons of $6$, or all in cartons of $8$, or all in cartons of $9$, with none left over. How many eggs are there?

$$
6 = 2 \cdot 3, \qquad 8 = 2^3, \qquad 9 = 3^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm}(6, 8, 9) = 2^3 \cdot 3^2 = 72
$$
$$
72 \cdot 1 = 72, \qquad 72 \cdot 2 = 144, \qquad 72 \cdot 3 = 216
$$

There are $144$ eggs.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of eggs is a multiple of $6$ because they can be put in cartons of $6$ with none left over. For the same reason it is a multiple of $8$ and of $9$. It is therefore a common multiple of the three numbers. The common multiples of several numbers are the multiples of their **LCM**, which is $2^3 \cdot 3^2 = 8 \cdot 9 = 72$.

The multiples of $72$ are $72$, $144$, $216$, and so on. The number $72$ is less than $100$ and the number $216$ is greater than $150$, whereas $144$ lies between $100$ and $150$. Therefore there are $144$ eggs.

</div>
