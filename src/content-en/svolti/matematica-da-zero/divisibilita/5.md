## Exercise 1

Find $\operatorname{MCD}(20, 30)$ by listing the divisors.

$$
\text{divisors of } 20: \quad 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20
$$
$$
\text{divisors of } 30: \quad 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30
$$
$$
\text{common divisors}: \quad 1, 2, 5, 10
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(20, 30) = 10
$$

::venn{zone="ab" elementi="a:4,20;ab:1,2,5,10;b:3,6,15,30" nomi="20,30" titolo="Divisors of $20$ and $30$: the common divisors $1, 2, 5, 10$ are in the center, and the greatest is $\operatorname{MCD}(20, 30) = 10$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisors of $20$ come from the pairs $1 \cdot 20$, $2 \cdot 10$, and $4 \cdot 5$. Those of $30$ come from $1 \cdot 30$, $2 \cdot 15$, $3 \cdot 10$, and $5 \cdot 6$. The numbers appearing in both lists are $1$, $2$, $5$, and $10$. The largest is $10$, so the **greatest common divisor** is $10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find $\operatorname{MCD}(72, 120)$ using prime factorization.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
72 & 2 \\
36 & 2 \\
18 & 2 \\
9 & 3 \\
3 & 3 \\
1 &
\end{array}
\qquad\qquad
\begin{array}{r|l}
120 & 2 \\
60 & 2 \\
30 & 2 \\
15 & 3 \\
5 & 5 \\
1 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
72 = 2^3 \cdot 3^2, \qquad 120 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(72, 120) = 2^3 \cdot 3 = 24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find both factorizations using a two-column division table. Divide $72$ three times by $2$ and twice by $3$. Divide $120$ three times by $2$, once by $3$, and once by $5$.

By the **GCD rule**, take the common factors with their smaller exponents. The factor $2$ appears in both numbers with exponent $3$, so take $2^3 = 8$. The factor $3$ has exponent $2$ in $72$ and exponent $1$ in $120$, so take $3^1 = 3$. The factor $5$ appears only in $120$ and does not enter the GCD. This gives $8 \cdot 3 = 24$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $\operatorname{MCD}(126, 210, 294)$.

$$
126 = 2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7
$$
$$
210 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7
$$
$$
294 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(126, 210, 294) = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 42
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find the factorizations as follows. The number $126$ is even, $126 : 2 = 63$, and $63 = 3^2 \cdot 7$. The number $210$ is $21 \cdot 10 = 3 \cdot 7 \cdot 2 \cdot 5$. The number $294$ is even, $294 : 2 = 147$, the digits of $147$ add up to $12$, and $147 : 3 = 49 = 7^2$.

The factors common to *all three* numbers are $2$, $3$, and $7$. The factor $5$ appears only in $210$. For each common factor, the smallest exponent is $1$ because $2$ always has exponent $1$, $3$ has exponent $1$ in $210$ and $294$, and $7$ has exponent $1$ in $126$ and $210$. Therefore the **GCD** is $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 42$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether the numbers in each pair are coprime: $45$ and $56$, and $39$ and $65$.

$$
45 = 3^2 \cdot 5, \qquad 56 = 2^3 \cdot 7 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \operatorname{MCD}(45, 56) = 1
$$
$$
39 = 3 \cdot 13, \qquad 65 = 5 \cdot 13 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \operatorname{MCD}(39, 65) = 13
$$

The numbers $45$ and $56$ are coprime. The numbers $39$ and $65$ are not.

<div class="spiegazione">

Two numbers are **coprime** when their GCD is $1$. The prime factorizations of $45$ and $56$ have no common factors because the factors of $45$ are only $3$ and $5$, while those of $56$ are only $2$ and $7$. The only common divisor is therefore $1$, and the two numbers are coprime even though neither is prime.

The factorizations of $39$ and $65$, however, have the common factor $13$, so $\operatorname{MCD}(39, 65) = 13$. Since their GCD differs from $1$, $39$ and $65$ **are not coprime**.

</div>
