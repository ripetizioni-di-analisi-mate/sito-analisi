## Exercise 1

Determine whether $51$ and $53$ are prime.

$$
5 + 1 = 6 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad 51 = 3 \cdot 17
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
53 : 2 &= 26 \quad \text{remainder } 1 \\[6pt]
53 : 3 &= 17 \quad \text{remainder } 2 \\[6pt]
53 : 5 &= 10 \quad \text{remainder } 3 \\[6pt]
53 : 7 &= 7 \quad \text{remainder } 4
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $51$ is composite. The number $53$ is prime.

<div class="spiegazione">

The digits of $51$ add up to $6$, a multiple of $3$, so by the **divisibility test** for $3$, the number $51$ is divisible by $3$. Division gives $51 : 3 = 17$, so $51 = 3 \cdot 17$ has a divisor other than $1$ and $51$ and is **composite**.

For $53$, test the primes in order. The first prime whose square exceeds $53$ is $11$ because $7 \cdot 7 = 49$ is less than $53$ and $11 \cdot 11 = 121$ is greater. Therefore it is enough to divide by $2$, $3$, $5$, and $7$. None of the divisions has remainder $0$, so $53$ is **prime**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine whether $133$ and $157$ are prime.

$$
133 : 7 = 19 \quad \text{remainder } 0 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad 133 = 7 \cdot 19
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
157 : 2 &= 78 \quad \text{remainder } 1 \\[6pt]
157 : 3 &= 52 \quad \text{remainder } 1 \\[6pt]
157 : 5 &= 31 \quad \text{remainder } 2 \\[6pt]
157 : 7 &= 22 \quad \text{remainder } 3 \\[6pt]
157 : 11 &= 14 \quad \text{remainder } 3
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $133$ is composite. The number $157$ is prime.

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $133$ is odd, so it is not divisible by $2$. Its digit sum is $7$, which is not a multiple of $3$, and its last digit is neither $0$ nor $5$, so $133$ is divisible by neither $3$ nor $5$. Division by $7$, however, has remainder $0$ because $7 \cdot 19 = 70 + 63 = 133$. Therefore $133$ is **composite**.

For $157$, the first prime we do not need to test is $13$ because $11 \cdot 11 = 121$ is less than $157$ and $13 \cdot 13 = 169$ is greater. We therefore test $157$ for divisibility by $2$, $3$, $5$, $7$, and $11$. The first three checks use the divisibility tests: $157$ is odd, its digit sum is $13$, and it ends in $7$. Division by $7$ and by $11$ gives remainder $3$ in each case because $7 \cdot 22 = 154$ and $11 \cdot 14 = 154$. None of the divisions has remainder $0$, so $157$ is **prime**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the prime numbers between $60$ and $80$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
62, 64, 66, 68, 70, 72, 74, 76, 78 &\quad \text{even} \\[6pt]
63 = 3 \cdot 21, \quad 69 = 3 \cdot 23 &\quad \text{multiples of } 3 \\[6pt]
65 = 5 \cdot 13, \quad 75 = 5 \cdot 15 &\quad \text{multiples of } 5 \\[6pt]
77 = 7 \cdot 11 &\quad \text{multiple of } 7
\end{aligned}
$$

The prime numbers between $60$ and $80$ are $61, 67, 71, 73, 79$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the **sieve of Eratosthenes** to the numbers from $61$ through $79$. Since $9 \cdot 9 = 81$ exceeds $79$, a composite number in this interval must have a prime divisor less than $9$, so it is enough to check multiples of $2$, $3$, $5$, and $7$.

First eliminate the even numbers. The remaining odd numbers are $61, 63, 65, 67, 69, 71, 73, 75, 77, 79$. The digits of $63$ add up to $9$ and the digits of $69$ add up to $15$, so both numbers are multiples of $3$. The numbers $65$ and $75$ end in $5$, so both are multiples of $5$. Finally, the multiples of $7$ between $60$ and $80$ are $63$, $70$, and $77 = 7 \cdot 11$, and only $77$ has not yet been eliminated. The numbers that have not been eliminated are **prime**.

</div>
