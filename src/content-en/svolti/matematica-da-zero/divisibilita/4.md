## Exercise 1

Find the prime factorization of $84$.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
84 & 2 \\
42 & 2 \\
21 & 3 \\
7 & 7 \\
1 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
84 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $84$ ends in $4$, so it is even and can be divided by $2$, giving $42$. The number $42$ is also even, and $42 : 2 = 21$. The number $21$ is odd and its digits add up to $3$, so divide it by $3$ to obtain $7$. The number $7$ is prime, so divide it by itself to reach $1$.

The prime factors are the numbers in the right-hand column. The factor $2$ appears twice and is written as a power, so the **factorization** is $84 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the prime factorization of $300$.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
300 & 2 \\
150 & 2 \\
75 & 3 \\
25 & 5 \\
5 & 5 \\
1 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
300 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $300$ is even, so divide it by $2$ to obtain $150$. The number $150$ is also even and $150 : 2 = 75$. The number $75$ is odd and its digits add up to $12$, a multiple of $3$, so divide it by $3$ to obtain $25$. The number $25$ is not divisible by $3$ because its digits add up to $7$, but it ends in $5$, so we can divide by $5$ twice.

The factors $2$ and $5$ each appear twice, so $300 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5^2$. The same result is obtained by writing $300 = 3 \cdot 100$ and $100 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2$ because the **factorization** is unique.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the prime factorization of $1\,008$.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
1008 & 2 \\
504 & 2 \\
252 & 2 \\
126 & 2 \\
63 & 3 \\
21 & 3 \\
7 & 7 \\
1 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
1\,008 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $1\,008$ is even. Dividing by $2$ gives $504$, then $504 : 2 = 252$, then $252 : 2 = 126$, and then $126 : 2 = 63$, since each intermediate quotient used in the next division is even. The number $63$ is odd, so $2$ appears exactly four times.

The digits of $63$ add up to $9$, so $63$ is divisible by $3$ and $63 : 3 = 21$. The digit sum of $21$ is also a multiple of $3$, and $21 : 3 = 7$, which is prime. Therefore $3$ appears twice and the **factorization** is $1\,008 = 2^4 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7$. Multiplying the factors gives the original number because $16 \cdot 9 = 144$ and $144 \cdot 7 = 1\,008$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the prime factorization of $2\,145$.

$$
\begin{array}{r|l}
2145 & 3 \\
715 & 5 \\
143 & 11 \\
13 & 13 \\
1 &
\end{array}
$$
$$
2\,145 = 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 11 \cdot 13
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $2\,145$ is odd, so it is not divisible by $2$. The sum of its digits is $2 + 1 + 4 + 5 = 12$, a multiple of $3$, so divide it by $3$ to obtain $715$. The digits of $715$ add up to $13$, which is not a multiple of $3$, but its last digit is $5$, so divide by $5$ to obtain $143$.

The number $143$ is odd, its digits add up to $8$, and its last digit is $3$, so it is divisible by none of $2$, $3$, or $5$. Nor is it divisible by $7$ because $7 \cdot 20 = 140$ and the remainder is $3$. Now use the **divisibility test** for $11$. The digits in odd positions are $3$ and $1$, with sum $4$, and the digit in the even position is $4$. The difference is $0$, so $143$ is divisible by $11$, and $143 : 11 = 13$, which is prime. Therefore $2\,145 = 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 11 \cdot 13$.

</div>
