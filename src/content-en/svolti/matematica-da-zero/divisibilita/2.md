## Exercise 1

For each of the numbers $3\,470$, $2\,815$, and $9\,136$, determine whether it is divisible by $2$, by $5$, and by $10$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{number} & \text{last digit} & 2 & 5 & 10 \\ \hline
3\,470 & 0 & \text{yes} & \text{yes} & \text{yes} \\
2\,815 & 5 & \text{no} & \text{yes} & \text{no} \\
9\,136 & 6 & \text{yes} & \text{no} & \text{no}
\end{array}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $2$, $5$, and $10$, we need only look at the **last digit**. The number $3\,470$ ends in $0$, which is even and also appears in the tests for $5$ and $10$, so $3\,470$ is divisible by all three.

The number $2\,815$ ends in $5$. The digit $5$ is odd, so $2\,815$ is not divisible by $2$, but it passes the test for $5$. It is not divisible by $10$ because its last digit is not $0$.

The number $9\,136$ ends in $6$, which is even, so $9\,136$ is divisible by $2$. It does not end in $0$ or $5$, so it is divisible by neither $5$ nor $10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine whether $4\,572$ and $7\,359$ are divisible by $3$ and by $9$.

$$
4 + 5 + 7 + 2 = 18
$$
$$
7 + 3 + 5 + 9 = 24
$$

The number $4\,572$ is divisible by $3$ and by $9$. The number $7\,359$ is divisible by $3$ but not by $9$.

<div class="spiegazione">

For $3$ and $9$, we use the **sum of the digits**. For $4\,572$ the sum is $18$, which is a multiple of $9$ because $18 = 9 \cdot 2$, and also of $3$ because $18 = 3 \cdot 6$. Therefore $4\,572$ is divisible by $9$ and by $3$.

For $7\,359$ the sum is $24$. The number $24 = 3 \cdot 8$ is a multiple of $3$, so $7\,359$ is divisible by $3$. However, $24$ is not a multiple of $9$ because it lies between two consecutive multiples of $9$, namely $18 = 9 \cdot 2$ and $27 = 9 \cdot 3$, so $7\,359$ **is not divisible** by $9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether $5\,316$ and $6\,175$ are divisible by $4$ and by $25$.

$$
5\,316 \;\longrightarrow\; 16 = 4 \cdot 4
$$
$$
6\,175 \;\longrightarrow\; 75 = 25 \cdot 3
$$

The number $5\,316$ is divisible by $4$ but not by $25$. The number $6\,175$ is divisible by $25$ but not by $4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

For $4$ and $25$, look at the **last two digits**. The last two digits of $5\,316$ form $16$, a multiple of $4$, so $5\,316$ is divisible by $4$. The number $16$ is not one of the endings $00$, $25$, $50$, or $75$, so $5\,316$ is not divisible by $25$.

The last two digits of $6\,175$ form $75$, one of the endings in the test for $25$, so $6\,175$ is divisible by $25$. The number $75$ is odd and therefore is not a multiple of $4$, so $6\,175$ is not divisible by $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether $7\,293$ and $4\,816$ are divisible by $11$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{number } 7\,293 \\[6pt]
\text{odd positions} &= 3 + 2 = 5 \\[6pt]
\text{even positions} &= 9 + 7 = 16 \\[6pt]
16 - 5 &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text{number } 4\,816 \\[6pt]
\text{odd positions} &= 6 + 8 = 14 \\[6pt]
\text{even positions} &= 1 + 4 = 5 \\[6pt]
14 - 5 &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $7\,293$ is divisible by $11$. The number $4\,816$ is not divisible by $11$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Number the digits from the right. In $7\,293$, the digits in odd positions are the ones digit, $3$, and the hundreds digit, $2$. The digits in even positions are $9$ and $7$. The difference between the two sums is $16 - 5 = 11$, a multiple of $11$, so $7\,293$ is **divisible** by $11$. Indeed, $11 \cdot 663 = 7\,293$.

In $4\,816$, the digits in odd positions are $6$ and $8$, with sum $14$, and those in even positions are $1$ and $4$, with sum $5$. The difference is $9$, which is neither $0$ nor a multiple of $11$, so $4\,816$ is not divisible by $11$.

</div>
