## Exercise 1

Find $\operatorname{mcm}(6, 8)$ by listing the multiples.

$$
\text{multiples of } 6: \quad 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, \dots
$$
$$
\text{multiples of } 8: \quad 8, 16, 24, 32, \dots
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm}(6, 8) = 24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

List the multiples other than $0$ of both numbers, adding $6$ each time in the first list and $8$ in the second. The numbers $12$ and $18$ are not multiples of $8$, and $16$ is not a multiple of $6$. The first number appearing in both lists is $24$, so the **least common multiple** is $24$.

## Exercise 2

Find $\operatorname{mcm}(18, 24)$ using prime factorization.

$$
18 = 2 \cdot 3^2, \qquad 24 = 2^3 \cdot 3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{mcm}(18, 24) &= 2^3 \cdot 3^2 \\[6pt]
&= 8 \cdot 9 \\[6pt]
&= 72
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $18$ factors as $2 \cdot 9 = 2 \cdot 3^2$, and $24$ factors as $8 \cdot 3 = 2^3 \cdot 3$. By the **LCM rule**, take every factor that appears, whether shared or unshared, with its largest exponent. The factor $2$ has exponent $1$ in $18$ and $3$ in $24$, so take $2^3$. The factor $3$ has exponent $2$ in $18$ and $1$ in $24$, so take $3^2$.

The result is $8 \cdot 9 = 72$. Indeed, $72 = 18 \cdot 4$ and $72 = 24 \cdot 3$, so $72$ is a multiple of both numbers.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $\operatorname{mcm}(12, 20, 45)$.

$$
12 = 2^2 \cdot 3, \qquad 20 = 2^2 \cdot 5, \qquad 45 = 3^2 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{mcm}(12, 20, 45) &= 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \\[6pt]
&= 4 \cdot 9 \cdot 5 \\[6pt]
&= 180
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three factorizations contain the factors $2$, $3$, and $5$. The largest exponent of $2$ is $2$, in $12$ and $20$. The largest exponent of $3$ is $2$, in $45$. The largest exponent of $5$ is $1$. Therefore the **LCM** is $2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$.

Calculate the product in two steps, $4 \cdot 9 = 36$ and $36 \cdot 5 = 180$. Indeed, $180 = 12 \cdot 15 = 20 \cdot 9 = 45 \cdot 4$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find $\operatorname{MCD}(40, 56)$ and $\operatorname{mcm}(40, 56)$, and check that their product equals $40 \cdot 56$.

$$
40 = 2^3 \cdot 5, \qquad 56 = 2^3 \cdot 7
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD}(40, 56) = 2^3 = 8
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm}(40, 56) = 2^3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 = 280
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
8 \cdot 280 &= 2\,240 \\[6pt]
40 \cdot 56 &= 2\,240
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The only common prime factor is $2$, with exponent $3$ in both numbers, so the **GCD** is $2^3 = 8$. The LCM includes all the factors, $2^3$, $5$, and $7$, giving $8 \cdot 35 = 280$.

The product of the GCD and LCM is $8 \cdot 280 = 2\,240$. The product of the two numbers is $40 \cdot 56 = 2\,240$ because $40 \cdot 50 = 2\,000$ and $40 \cdot 6 = 240$. The two products agree, as the rule for the **product of the GCD and LCM** predicts, so the calculation checks out.

</div>
