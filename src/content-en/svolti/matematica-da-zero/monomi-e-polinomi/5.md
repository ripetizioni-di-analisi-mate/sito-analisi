## Exercise 1

Expand $(x + 5)^2$ and $(3a - 2)^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 5)^2 &= x^2 + 2 \cdot x \cdot 5 + 5^2 \\[6pt]
&= x^2 + 10x + 25
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(3a - 2)^2 &= (3a)^2 - 2 \cdot 3a \cdot 2 + 2^2 \\[6pt]
&= 9a^2 - 12a + 4
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the formula for the **square of a binomial**: the square of the first term, plus twice the product, plus the square of the second term. The terms of the first binomial are $x$ and $5$, so twice their product is $2 \cdot x \cdot 5 = 10x$ and the square of the second is $25$.

In the second binomial, the first term is the monomial $3a$, and its square is $(3a)^2 = 9a^2$ because both the coefficient and the variable are squared. The terms $3a$ and $-2$ have opposite signs, so twice their product has a minus sign and equals $-2 \cdot 3a \cdot 2 = -12a$. The square of $-2$ is $4$, which, like every square, is positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}x - 4y \right)^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left( \frac{1}{2}x - 4y \right)^2 &= \left( \frac{1}{2}x \right)^2 - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}x \cdot 4y + (4y)^2 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{4}x^2 - 4xy + 16y^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two terms of the binomial are $\dfrac{1}{2}x$ and $-4y$. The square of the first is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot x^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}x^2$, and the square of the second is $16y^2$.

To find twice the product, multiply the coefficients, giving $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$ and then $1 \cdot 4 = 4$. The sign is negative because the two terms have opposite signs, so twice their product is $-4xy$. The expansion is therefore $\dfrac{1}{4}x^2 - 4xy + 16y^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate the product $(2x + 3y)(2x - 3y)$, then use the same formula to calculate $43 \cdot 37$ mentally.

$$
(2x + 3y)(2x - 3y) = (2x)^2 - (3y)^2 = 4x^2 - 9y^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
43 \cdot 37 &= (40 + 3)(40 - 3) \\[6pt]
&= 40^2 - 3^2 \\[6pt]
&= 1600 - 9 = 1591
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two binomials have the same terms $2x$ and $3y$, added in the first and subtracted in the second. Therefore, the formula for the **product of a sum and a difference** applies, and the product is the difference of the squares $(2x)^2 = 4x^2$ and $(3y)^2 = 9y^2$.

The numbers $43$ and $37$ have mean $40$, and each lies $3$ units from $40$. Therefore $43 = 40 + 3$ and $37 = 40 - 3$, and their product is the product of a sum and a difference. The result is $40^2 - 3^2 = 1600 - 9 = 1591$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Simplify the expression $(x + 3)^2 - (x + 2)(x - 2) - 6x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(x + 3)^2 - (x + 2)(x - 2) - 6x \\[6pt]
&= x^2 + 6x + 9 - \left( x^2 - 4 \right) - 6x \\[6pt]
&= x^2 + 6x + 9 - x^2 + 4 - 6x \\[6pt]
&= 13
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first special product is a binomial square and equals $x^2 + 6x + 9$. The second is the product of a sum and a difference and equals $x^2 - 4$. We write the result as $\left( x^2 - 4 \right)$ because the minus sign before the product applies to all its terms.

The minus sign therefore changes $x^2$ into $-x^2$ and $-4$ into $+4$. The terms $x^2$ and $-x^2$ are opposites, as are $6x$ and $-6x$, so their sum is $0$. This leaves $9 + 4 = 13$. The expression is therefore **constant** and equals $13$ for *every* value of $x$. For example, with $x = 1$ we have $16 - 3 \cdot (-1) - 6 = 16 + 3 - 6 = 13$.

</div>
