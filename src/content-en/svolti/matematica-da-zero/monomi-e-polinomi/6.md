## Exercise 1

Expand $(a + b + 2)^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(a + b + 2)^2 \\[6pt]
&= a^2 + b^2 + 2^2 \\[6pt]
&\quad + 2 \cdot a \cdot b + 2 \cdot a \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot b \cdot 2 \\[6pt]
&= a^2 + b^2 + 4 + 2ab + 4a + 4b
\end{aligned}
$$

Apply the formula for the **square of a trinomial**. The three terms are $a$, $b$, and $2$, and their squares are $a^2$, $b^2$, and $4$.

There are three pairs of terms: $a$ and $b$, $a$ and $2$, and $b$ and $2$. Doubling each product gives $2ab$, $2 \cdot a \cdot 2 = 4a$, and $2 \cdot b \cdot 2 = 4b$, all positive because the three terms are positive. The expansion has six terms.

## Exercise 2

Expand $\left( x^2 - x + 3 \right)^2$ and write the result in descending order.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left( x^2 - x + 3 \right)^2 \\[6pt]
&= x^4 + x^2 + 9 \\[6pt]
&\quad + 2 \cdot x^2 \cdot (-x) + 2 \cdot x^2 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot (-x) \cdot 3 \\[6pt]
&= x^4 + x^2 + 9 - 2x^3 + 6x^2 - 6x \\[6pt]
&= x^4 - 2x^3 + 7x^2 - 6x + 9
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three terms of the trinomial are $x^2$, $-x$, and $3$. Their squares are $\left( x^2 \right)^2 = x^4$, $(-x)^2 = x^2$, and $9$, all positive.

For each pair, the sign rule determines the sign of twice the product. Twice the product of $x^2$ and $-x$ is $-2x^3$, which is negative. For $x^2$ and $3$ it is $6x^2$, which is positive, and for $-x$ and $3$ it is $-6x$, which is negative.

There are two like terms, $x^2$ and $6x^2$, with sum $7x^2$. Writing the terms from highest to lowest degree gives the polynomial **in descending order**, $x^4 - 2x^3 + 7x^2 - 6x + 9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Expand $(x + 3)^3$ and $(2a - 1)^3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^3 &= x^3 + 3 \cdot x^2 \cdot 3 + 3 \cdot x \cdot 3^2 + 3^3 \\[6pt]
&= x^3 + 9x^2 + 27x + 27
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(2a - 1)^3 &= (2a)^3 - 3 \cdot (2a)^2 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 2a \cdot 1^2 - 1^3 \\[6pt]
&= 8a^3 - 12a^2 + 6a - 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the formula for the **cube of a binomial**. For $(x + 3)^3$, the cube of the first term is $x^3$, the first product multiplied by three is $3 \cdot x^2 \cdot 3 = 9x^2$, the second is $3 \cdot x \cdot 9 = 27x$, and the cube of the second term is $3^3 = 27$.

For $(2a - 1)^3$ the terms are $2a$ and $-1$, and the signs alternate. The cube of $2a$ is $8a^3$. The first product multiplied by three contains the square of $2a$, which is $4a^2$, so the product equals $-3 \cdot 4a^2 \cdot 1 = -12a^2$. The second product multiplied by three is $3 \cdot 2a \cdot 1 = 6a$, positive because it contains the square of $-1$. The last term is $(-1)^3 = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Simplify the expression $(x - 1)^3 - x\,(x - 3)(x + 3) + 3x^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(x - 1)^3 - x\,(x - 3)(x + 3) + 3x^2 \\[6pt]
&= x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 1 - x\left( x^2 - 9 \right) + 3x^2 \\[6pt]
&= x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 1 - x^3 + 9x + 3x^2 \\[6pt]
&= 12x - 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first term is a binomial cube and equals $x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 1$. In the second term, $(x - 3)(x + 3)$ is the product of a sum and a difference and equals $x^2 - 9$.

By the distributive property, multiplying the monomial $-x$ by $x^2 - 9$ then gives $-x^3 + 9x$. Now combine like terms. The terms $x^3$ and $-x^3$ are opposites, as are $-3x^2$ and $3x^2$, so their sum is $0$. This leaves $3x + 9x = 12x$ and $-1$, so the expression equals $12x - 1$.

</div>
