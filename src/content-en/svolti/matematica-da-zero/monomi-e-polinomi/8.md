## Exercise 1

A gym offers two memberships. Plan A costs $40$ euros per month. Plan B charges a signup fee of $30$ euros and then $25$ euros per month. Write the cost of $m$ months under each plan and the difference between the two costs, then calculate this difference for $m = 6$.

$$
A = 40m , \qquad B = 30 + 25m
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A - B &= 40m - (30 + 25m) \\[6pt]
&= 40m - 30 - 25m \\[6pt]
&= 15m - 30
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
15 \cdot 6 - 30 = 90 - 30 = 60 \text{ euros}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With plan A, each month costs $40$ euros, so $m$ months cost $40 \cdot m = 40m$ euros. With plan B, the signup fee of $30$ euros is paid only once, followed by $25$ euros for each of the $m$ months, so the cost is $30 + 25m$ euros.

In the difference, the minus sign before $30 + 25m$ reverses the signs of both terms. The like terms $40m$ and $-25m$ give $15m$, so the difference is $15m - 30$ euros.

For $m = 6$, the difference is $60$ euros. Indeed, plan A costs $40 \cdot 6 = 240$ euros and plan B costs $30 + 25 \cdot 6 = 30 + 150 = 180$ euros, and $240 - 180 = 60$. Over six months, plan B is therefore **less expensive** because it costs $60$ euros less.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A square flower bed has side length $x$ meters. Each side is lengthened by $3$ meters, so the larger bed is still square. Write an expression for the increase in area and evaluate it for $x = 5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^2 - x^2 &= x^2 + 6x + 9 - x^2 \\[6pt]
&= 6x + 9
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
6 \cdot 5 + 9 = 30 + 9 = 39 \text{ m}^2
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="5,0 8,0 8,8 5,8;0,5 5,5 5,8 0,8" linee="0,0 8,0 8,8 0,8 0,0;5,0 5,8;0,5 5,5" etichette="2.2,2.4,x²;6.15,2.4,3x;2.2,6.3,3x;6.3,6.3,9;2.4,-0.6,x;6.3,-0.6,3;-0.6,2.4,x;-0.6,6.3,3" xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-1 ymax=9 titolo="The new flower bed has side length $x + 3$. The shaded region is the increase in area, made up of two rectangles of area $3x$ and a square of area $9$, for a total of $6x + 9$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a square is the square of its side length. The original bed therefore has area $x^2$, and the new bed, with side length $x + 3$, has area $(x + 3)^2$. The increase is the difference between the two areas.

The **square of a binomial** gives $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$. The terms $x^2$ and $-x^2$ are opposites, so the increase is $6x + 9$ square meters. The figure shows the same result because two strips of area $3 \cdot x = 3x$ and a small corner square of area $3 \cdot 3 = 9$ are added around the original square.

For $x = 5$ the increase is $39$ m². Indeed, the bed grows from $5 \cdot 5 = 25$ m² to $8 \cdot 8 = 64$ m², and $64 - 25 = 39$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider three consecutive natural numbers. Show that the product of the first and third equals the square of the middle number minus $1$, and check the equality with the numbers $4$, $5$, and $6$.

$$
n , \qquad n + 1 , \qquad n + 2
$$
$$
n\,(n + 2) = n^2 + 2n
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(n + 1)^2 - 1 &= n^2 + 2n + 1 - 1 \\[6pt]
&= n^2 + 2n
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
4 \cdot 6 = 24 , \qquad 5^2 - 1 = 25 - 1 = 24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $n$ denote the first number. Consecutive natural numbers differ by $1$, so the middle number is $n + 1$ and the third is $n + 2$. The variable lets us write the calculation once for *all* triples of consecutive numbers.

By the distributive property, the product of the first and third is $n^2 + 2n$. Expanding the square of the middle number using the binomial square formula gives $(n + 1)^2 = n^2 + 2n + 1$, and subtracting $1$ again gives $n^2 + 2n$. The two expressions are equal, so the equality holds **for every** triple of consecutive numbers.

With $n = 4$, the numbers are $4$, $5$, and $6$. The product $4 \cdot 6$ equals $24$, and $5^2 - 1$ also equals $24$, as expected.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A rectangular photo is $x$ centimeters high and $2x$ centimeters long, so its length is twice its height. A frame $1$ cm wide surrounds the photo on all sides. Write an expression for the area of the frame and evaluate it for $x = 10$.

$$
\text{length } 2x + 2 , \qquad \text{height } x + 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x + 2)(x + 2) - 2x \cdot x &= 2x^2 + 4x + 2x + 4 - 2x^2 \\[6pt]
&= 6x + 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
6 \cdot 10 + 4 = 64 \text{ cm}^2
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 22,0 22,1 0,1;0,11 22,11 22,12 0,12;0,1 1,1 1,11 0,11;21,1 22,1 22,11 21,11" linee="0,0 22,0 22,12 0,12 0,0;1,1 21,1 21,11 1,11 1,1" etichette="9.4,5.4,photo;9.8,-1.1,2x + 2;-2.4,5.4,x + 2;4.6,12.7,1 cm wide frame" xmin=-4 xmax=24 ymin=-2 ymax=14 titolo="With $x = 10$, the outer rectangle measures $22$ cm by $12$ cm and the photo measures $20$ cm by $10$ cm. The frame is the shaded region and has area $264 - 200 = 64$ cm$^2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The frame adds $1$ cm to the left and $1$ cm to the right of the photo, so the outside length is $2x + 2$. Similarly, it adds $1$ cm above and $1$ cm below, giving an outside height of $x + 2$. The frame's area is the area of the outer rectangle minus the area of the photo, which is $2x \cdot x = 2x^2$.

The **product of polynomials** $(2x + 2)(x + 2)$ has four terms: $2x^2$, $4x$, $2x$, and $4$. Subtracting $2x^2$ gives the opposite terms $2x^2$ and $-2x^2$, leaving $4x + 2x + 4 = 6x + 4$ square centimeters.

For $x = 10$ the photo measures $20$ cm by $10$ cm and the area of the frame is $64$ cm². Indeed, the outer rectangle measures $22$ cm by $12$ cm and has area $22 \cdot 12 = 264$ cm², while the photo has area $20 \cdot 10 = 200$ cm², so $264 - 200 = 64$.

</div>
