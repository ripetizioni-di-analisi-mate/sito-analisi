## Exercise 1

Marta has $50$ euros. She buys $4$ notebooks at $3$ euros each and $2$ pens at $2$ euros each. How many euros does she have left?

$$
\begin{aligned}
50 - (4 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 2)
&= 50 - (12 + 4) \\[6pt]
&= 50 - 16 \\[6pt]
&= 34
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The notebooks cost $4 \cdot 3$ euros and the pens cost $2 \cdot 2$ euros, so the total cost is $4 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 2$. The entire cost must be subtracted from the $50$ euros, so the expression $4 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 2$ is enclosed in parentheses.

In $4 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 2$, multiplication precedes addition, so we first calculate $4 \cdot 3 = 12$ and $2 \cdot 2 = 4$, which add up to $16$. The total cost is therefore $16$ euros, so Marta has $50 - 16 = 34$ euros **left**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A family's monthly water bill consists of a fixed charge of $9$ euros plus $2$ euros for each cubic meter of water used. In one month the family uses $23$ cubic meters. How much does it pay that month? How much does it pay in $3$ months if it uses the same amount of water each month?

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 + 2 \cdot 23
&= 9 + 46 \\[6pt]
&= 55
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3 \cdot (9 + 2 \cdot 23)
&= 3 \cdot 55 \\[6pt]
&= 165
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water used costs $2 \cdot 23 = 46$ euros because each cubic meter costs $2$ euros. To this we add the fixed charge of $9$ euros. In the expression $9 + 2 \cdot 23$, multiplication precedes addition, so parentheses are unnecessary and the **monthly bill** is $9 + 46 = 55$ euros.

In $3$ months the family pays three times the bill for one month. The entire monthly bill must be multiplied by $3$, so we put the expression $9 + 2 \cdot 23$ in parentheses and obtain $3 \cdot 55 = 165$ euros. By the distributive property, we can obtain the same result as $3 \cdot 9 + 3 \cdot 46 = 27 + 138 = 165$, that is, three fixed charges plus three times the usage charge. In $3$ months the family therefore **pays** $165$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cookie recipe for $6$ people calls for $250$ grams of flour and $3$ eggs. How much flour and how many eggs are needed to make cookies for $18$ people?

$$
18 : 6 = 3
$$
$$
250 \cdot 3 = 750 ,
\qquad
3 \cdot 3 = 9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First find how many groups of $6$ people, the number served by the recipe, fit into the $18$ people to be served. We have $18 : 6 = 3$, so the amounts must be tripled.

The amount of flour becomes $250 \cdot 3 = 750$ grams and the number of eggs becomes $3 \cdot 3 = 9$. For $18$ people, we therefore **need** $750$ grams of flour and $9$ eggs.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A floor is covered with $14$ rows of $9$ tiles each. The tiles are sold only in boxes of $10$. How many boxes must be bought? How many tiles will be left over?

$$
14 \cdot 9 = 126
$$
$$
126 = 10 \cdot 12 + 6
$$
$$
12 + 1 = 13 ,
\qquad
13 \cdot 10 - 126 = 130 - 126 = 4
$$

Buying $13$ boxes of $10$ gives $130$ tiles, with $4$ left over"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of tiles needed is $14 \cdot 9 = 126$ because there are $14$ rows with $9$ tiles in each row. To find how many boxes are needed, divide $126$ by $10$, the number of tiles in one box. The **division with remainder** gives quotient $12$ and remainder $6$ because $10 \cdot 12 = 120$ and $126 - 120 = 6$.

With $12$ boxes there are $120$ tiles, leaving us $6$ tiles short. Only whole boxes are sold, so to obtain those $6$ tiles we buy one more box, bringing the number of boxes to $12 + 1 = 13$. The **number of boxes to buy** is therefore $13$. There are $13$ boxes containing $130$ tiles in total, so $130 - 126 = 4$ tiles are **left over**.

</div>
