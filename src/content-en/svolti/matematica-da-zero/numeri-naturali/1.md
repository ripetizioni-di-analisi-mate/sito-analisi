## Exercise 1

Write the number $5\,203$ as a sum of thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5\,203
&= 5 \text{ thousands} + 2 \text{ hundreds} + 0 \text{ tens} + 3 \text{ ones} \\[6pt]
&= 5\,000 + 200 + 0 + 3 \\[6pt]
&= 5\,000 + 200 + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Read the digits starting from the right. The digit $3$ is in the ones place, $0$ is in the tens place, $2$ is in the hundreds place, and $5$ is in the thousands place.

Replace each digit with its value. Since one thousand is $1\,000$, the $5$ thousands are worth $5\,000$. Since one hundred is $100$, the $2$ hundreds are worth $200$. There are $0$ tens, so their value is $0$ and they can be left out of the sum because adding $0$ does not change the result. Therefore $5\,203 = 5\,000 + 200 + 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the following numbers in increasing order:
$$
3\,045, \quad 345, \quad 30\,045, \quad 3\,405, \quad 3\,054 .
$$

$$
345 < 3\,045 < 3\,054 < 3\,405 < 30\,045
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First count the digits in each number. The number $345$ has three digits, the numbers $3\,045$, $3\,405$, and $3\,054$ have four digits, and the number $30\,045$ has five. Of two numbers with different numbers of digits, the one with more digits is larger, so $345$ is the smallest and $30\,045$ is the largest.

The three four-digit numbers remain to be ordered. All three have $3$ thousands, so we move on to the hundreds. The number $3\,405$ has $4$ hundreds, while $3\,045$ and $3\,054$ have $0$, so $3\,405$ is the largest of the three. The numbers $3\,045$ and $3\,054$ also have the same hundreds digit, and in the tens place $4$ is less than $5$, so $3\,045 < 3\,054$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Plot $4$ and $11$ on the number line and write all the natural numbers between $4$ and $11$, excluding $4$ and $11$. How many are there?

$$
5, \ 6, \ 7, \ 8, \ 9, \ 10
$$
$$
11 - 4 - 1 = 6
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the line, start at $0$ and mark off unit lengths to the right. The point for $4$ is $4$ steps from the start, and the point for $11$ is $11$ steps from the start. These are the two solid dots in the figure.

The numbers between $4$ and $11$ are those lying between the two points, namely $5$, $6$, $7$, $8$, $9$, and $10$, running from the successor of $4$ to the predecessor of $11$. Counting gives $6$ numbers **in between**. We can also obtain this count by calculation. From $4$ to $11$ there are $11 - 4 = 7$ steps, with an intermediate point between each step and the next, so there is one fewer intermediate point than there are steps, or $7 - 1 = 6$.
