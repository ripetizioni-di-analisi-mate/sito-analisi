## Exercise 1

Perform the division $38 : 5$ and write the result in the form dividend $=$ divisor $\cdot$ quotient $+$ remainder.

$$
5 \cdot 7 = 35 \leq 38 ,
\qquad
5 \cdot 8 = 40 > 38
$$
$$
38 - 35 = 3
$$
$$
38 = 5 \cdot 7 + 3

The quotient is the number of times the divisor $5$ fits into the dividend $38$. Look for multiples of $5$ close to $38$. The product $5 \cdot 7 = 35$ does not exceed $38$, whereas $5 \cdot 8 = 40$ does exceed it, so $5$ fits $7$ times into $38$ and the **quotient** is $7$.

The remainder is what is left over, namely $38 - 35 = 3$. The remainder $3$ is less than the divisor $5$, as required, so the division is correct and $38 = 5 \cdot 7 + 3$. In the figure, $7$ jumps of $5$ lead to $35$, and the distance left to reach $38$ is the **remainder**.

## Exercise 2

Perform the division $157 : 12$.

$$
12 \cdot 10 = 120 ,
\qquad
12 \cdot 13 = 156 \leq 157 ,
\qquad
12 \cdot 14 = 168 > 157
$$
$$
157 - 156 = 1
$$
$$
157 = 12 \cdot 13 + 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisor is $12$. To find the quotient quickly, start with a product that can be calculated mentally, $12 \cdot 10 = 120$, which is less than $157$. Thus $12$ fits at least $10$ times into $157$. After accounting for $120$, the amount remaining is $157 - 120 = 37$. From $37$ we can subtract $12$ another $3$ times because $12 \cdot 3 = 36$.

In all, $12$ fits $10 + 3 = 13$ times. Indeed, $12 \cdot 13 = 156$, whereas $12 \cdot 14 = 168$ exceeds $157$. The **quotient** is $13$ and the **remainder** is $157 - 156 = 1$, which is less than $12$. Therefore $157 = 12 \cdot 13 + 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a division problem, the divisor is $9$, the quotient is $14$, and the remainder is $5$. Find the dividend.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{dividend}
&= 9 \cdot 14 + 5 \\[6pt]
&= 126 + 5 \\[6pt]
&= 131
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In division with remainder, the dividend equals the divisor times the quotient, plus the remainder. The given values are the divisor $9$, the quotient $14$, and the remainder $5$. The remainder $5$ is less than the divisor $9$, so the values are consistent.

First calculate the product using the distributive property, $9 \cdot 14 = 9 \cdot 10 + 9 \cdot 4 = 90 + 36 = 126$. Then add the remainder, $126 + 5 = 131$. The **dividend** is therefore $131$. As a check, $131 : 9$ gives quotient $14$ and remainder $5$ because $9 \cdot 14 = 126$ and $131 - 126 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find all natural numbers that, when divided by $6$, give a quotient of $4$.

$$
n = 6 \cdot 4 + r ,
\qquad
r = 0, \ 1, \ 2, \ 3, \ 4, \ 5
$$
$$
n = 24, \ 25, \ 26, \ 27, \ 28, \ 29
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A number $n$ that, when divided by $6$, gives quotient $4$ can be written as $n = 6 \cdot 4 + r$, where $r$ is the remainder. The remainder is always less than the divisor, so $r$ can only be $0$, $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$, or $5$.

Since $6 \cdot 4 = 24$, we find the numbers by adding each possible remainder to $24$. The **numbers we seek** are therefore $24$, $25$, $26$, $27$, $28$, and $29$. The number $30$ is excluded because $30 = 6 \cdot 5$, so dividing it by $6$ gives quotient $5$.

</div>
