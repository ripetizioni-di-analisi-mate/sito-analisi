## Exercise 1

Evaluate the powers $3^4$, $2^5$, $10^4$, $1^9$, $0^5$, and $6^1$.

$$
3^4 = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 81
$$
$$
2^5 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 32
$$
$$
10^4 = 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 10\,000
$$
$$
1^9 = 1 ,
\qquad
0^5 = 0 ,
\qquad
6^1 = 6
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponent tells us how many times the base appears as a factor. For $3^4$, we multiply four factors each equal to $3$. The partial products are $3 \cdot 3 = 9$, then $9 \cdot 3 = 27$, and then $27 \cdot 3 = 81$. For $2^5$, the partial products are $4$, $8$, $16$, and $32$.

The power $10^4$ is written as the digit $1$ followed by four zeros because each factor of $10$ adds a zero. The power $1^9$ equals $1$ because a product whose factors all equal $1$ is equal to $1$. The power $0^5$ equals $0$ because a product with a factor of $0$ equals $0$. Finally, $6^1 = 6$ because, by definition, a power with exponent $1$ equals its base.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write each product as a power and evaluate it: $7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7$ and $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7 \cdot 7
&= 7^4 \\[6pt]
&= 49 \cdot 49 \\[6pt]
&= 2\,401
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2
&= 2^6 \\[6pt]
&= 8 \cdot 8 \\[6pt]
&= 64
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first product the factor $7$ appears four times, so the base is $7$ and the exponent is $4$. Thus the product is $7^4$. To evaluate it, group the factors in pairs using the associative property. Each pair gives $7 \cdot 7 = 49$. By the distributive property, $49 \cdot 49 = 49 \cdot (50 - 1) = 49 \cdot 50 - 49$. Since $49 \cdot 100 = 4\,900$ and $50$ is half of $100$, we have $49 \cdot 50 = 2\,450$, so $49 \cdot 49 = 2\,450 - 49 = 2\,401$.

In the second product the factor $2$ appears six times, so the product is $2^6$. Grouping the factors in threes gives $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 8$ for each group, so $2^6 = 8 \cdot 8 = 64$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine which number is greater in each pair: $2^5$ or $5^2$, and $4^3$ or $3^4$.

$$
2^5 = 32 ,
\qquad
5^2 = 25
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2^5 > 5^2
$$
$$
4^3 = 64 ,
\qquad
3^4 = 81
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
4^3 < 3^4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare two powers, calculate their values. We have $2^5 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 32$ and $5^2 = 5 \cdot 5 = 25$. Since $32 > 25$, the power $2^5$ is **greater** than $5^2$, even though the two powers use the same numbers $2$ and $5$ with the base and exponent interchanged.

Similarly, $4^3 = 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 = 64$ and $3^4 = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 81$. Since $64 < 81$, this time the greater power is $3^4$. Switching the base and exponent therefore changes the value of the power here. To determine which of the two powers is greater, calculate their values.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Write the number $30\,708$ using powers of $10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
30\,708
&= 30\,000 + 700 + 8 \\[6pt]
&= 3 \cdot 10\,000 + 7 \cdot 100 + 8 \\[6pt]
&= 3 \cdot 10^4 + 7 \cdot 10^2 + 8
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Starting from the right, the digit $8$ is in the ones place, $0$ is in the tens place, $7$ is in the hundreds place, $0$ is in the thousands place, and $3$ is in the ten-thousands place. The $0$ digits contribute nothing, so $30\,708 = 30\,000 + 700 + 8$.

Next, write each place value as its digit times a power of $10$. Since $10\,000 = 10^4$ and $100 = 10^2$, we obtain $30\,708 = 3 \cdot 10^4 + 7 \cdot 10^2 + 8$. The exponent of each power of $10$ is the number of digits to the right of the digit in question.

</div>
