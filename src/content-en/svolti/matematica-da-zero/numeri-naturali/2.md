## Exercise 1

Calculate $487 + 356$, adding the hundreds, tens, and ones separately.

$$
\begin{aligned}
487 + 356
&= (400 + 300) + (80 + 50) + (7 + 6) \\[6pt]
&= 700 + 130 + 13 \\[6pt]
&= 843
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The addends are $487$ and $356$. Write each as a sum using the place value of its digits, giving $487 = 400 + 80 + 7$ and $356 = 300 + 50 + 6$. The terms of a sum can be reordered, so we add the hundreds together, then the tens, then the ones.

The hundreds give $400 + 300 = 700$, the tens give $80 + 50 = 130$, and the ones give $7 + 6 = 13$. Finally, $700 + 130 = 830$ and $830 + 13 = 843$. Therefore the **sum** is $843$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $602 - 275$ and check the result.

$$
\begin{aligned}
602 - 275
&= 602 - 200 - 70 - 5 \\[6pt]
&= 402 - 70 - 5 \\[6pt]
&= 332 - 5 \\[6pt]
&= 327
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad 275 + 327 = 602
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The minuend is $602$ and the subtrahend is $275$. Since $275 = 200 + 70 + 5$, subtracting $275$ is equivalent to subtracting first $200$, then $70$, then $5$.

We calculate $602 - 200 = 402$. Next, $402 - 70 = 332$ because $402 - 2 = 400$ and $400 - 68 = 332$. Finally, $332 - 5 = 327$. The **difference** is therefore $327$.

To check, add the difference to the subtrahend. We have $275 + 327 = 602$ because the hundreds give $200 + 300 = 500$, the tens give $70 + 20 = 90$, the ones give $5 + 7 = 12$, and $500 + 90 + 12 = 602$. The sum equals the minuend, so the subtraction is correct.

</div>

## Exercise 3

How much must be added to $386$ to reach $1\,000$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,000 - 386
&= 1\,000 - 400 + 14 \\[6pt]
&= 600 + 14 \\[6pt]
&= 614
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad 386 + 614 = 1\,000
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The amount needed to get from $386$ to $1\,000$ is the number that, when added to $386$, gives $1\,000$. By the definition of subtraction, this number is the difference $1\,000 - 386$.

To do the calculation mentally, subtract $400$ instead of $386$. The number $400$ exceeds $386$ by $14$, so subtracting $400$ takes away $14$ too much, and this $14$ must be added back. We have $1\,000 - 400 = 600$ and $600 + 14 = 614$. The check confirms the result because $386 + 614 = 1\,000$. Thus, starting from $386$, we **need** $614$ more units to reach $1\,000$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $1\,250 - 475 + 325$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,250 - 475 + 325
&= 775 + 325 \\[6pt]
&= 1\,100
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression contains a subtraction and an addition, which have the same precedence. Operations with the same precedence are performed from left to right, so $1\,250 - 475$ is calculated first.

We have $1\,250 - 400 = 850$ and $850 - 75 = 775$. Then we add $325$, obtaining $775 + 325 = 1\,100$ because $775 + 25 = 800$ and $800 + 300 = 1\,100$. The **value** of the expression is therefore $1\,100$.

</div>
