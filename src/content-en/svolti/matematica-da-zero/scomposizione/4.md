## Exercise 1

Factor the binomial $x^3 - 27$.

$$
27 = 3^3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^3 - 27 &= x^3 - 3^3 \\[6pt]
&= (x - 3)(x^2 + 3x + 9)
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^3-27" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-40 ymax=100 punti="3,0,pieno" nomi="x,y" titolo="The graph of $y = x^3 - 27 = (x - 3)(x^2 + 3x + 9)$ crosses the $x$-axis only at $x = 3$ because the quadratic factor $x^2 + 3x + 9$ is never zero"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $27$ is the cube of $3$ because $3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 27$. The binomial is therefore a **difference of cubes**, with $a = x$ and $b = 3$. By the identity $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$, the first factor is $x - 3$ and the second is $x^2 + x \cdot 3 + 3^2 = x^2 + 3x + 9$.

The second factor is the **quadratic factor in a difference of cubes** because the middle term is $3x$ and twice the product of the bases would be $6x$. Therefore, it is irreducible and the factorization is complete. For $x = 1$ the binomial equals $1 - 27 = -26$ and the product equals $(-2) \cdot 13 = -26$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor the binomial $8x^3 + 1$.

$$
8x^3 = (2x)^3 ,
\qquad
1 = 1^3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
8x^3 + 1 &= (2x)^3 + 1^3 \\[6pt]
&= (2x + 1)\left( (2x)^2 - 2x \cdot 1 + 1^2 \right) \\[6pt]
&= (2x + 1)(4x^2 - 2x + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $8x^3$ is the cube of $2x$ because $(2x)^3 = 2^3 \cdot x^3 = 8x^3$, and $1$ is the cube of $1$. The binomial is therefore a **sum of cubes**, with $a = 2x$ and $b = 1$. By the identity $a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$, the first factor is $2x + 1$.

The second factor contains the square of $2x$, namely $4x^2$, the product $2x \cdot 1 = 2x$ with a minus sign, and the square of $1$. This gives $4x^2 - 2x + 1$, the quadratic factor in a sum of cubes, which is therefore irreducible.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor the binomial $2x^3 - 16$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^3 - 16 &= 2\,(x^3 - 8) \\[6pt]
&= 2\,(x^3 - 2^3) \\[6pt]
&= 2\,(x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $16$ is not a cube, but the two terms have a common factor $2$. **Factoring out the common factor** gives $2\,(x^3 - 8)$ because $\dfrac{16}{2} = 8$.

The number $8$ is the cube of $2$, so $x^3 - 8$ is a **difference of cubes** with $a = x$ and $b = 2$. The first factor is $x - 2$ and the second is $x^2 + 2x + 4$ because $x \cdot 2 = 2x$ and $2^2 = 4$. The polynomial therefore equals $2\,(x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4)$.

</div>
