## Exercise 1

Factor the polynomial $12x^2 - 18x$.

$$
\operatorname{MCD}(12, 18) = 6
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
12x^2 - 18x &= 6x \cdot 2x - 6x \cdot 3 \\[6pt]
&= 6x\,(2x - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 18,0 18,3 0,3" linee="0,0 18,0 18,6 0,6 0,0;0,3 18,3" etichette="6.5,1.3,6x · (2x − 3);6.5,4.3,6x · 3;8.2,-1.4,6x = 18;-4.3,2.7,2x = 6" xmin=-6 xmax=19 ymin=-2.5 ymax=7 titolo="For $x = 3$, the rectangle with side lengths $6x = 18$ and $2x = 6$ has area $12x^2 = 108$. Removing the rectangle with side lengths $6x$ and $3$ and area $18 \cdot 3 = 54$ leaves the rectangle with side lengths $6x$ and $2x - 3$, whose area $54$ equals $12x^2 - 18x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two terms have coefficients $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$ and $18 = 2 \cdot 3^2$, so their greatest common divisor is $2 \cdot 3 = 6$. The variable $x$ appears in both terms, with exponent $2$ in the first and $1$ in the second. The smaller exponent is $1$. The common factor is therefore $6x$.

Dividing each term by $6x$ gives $\dfrac{12x^2}{6x} = 2x$ and $\dfrac{18x}{6x} = 3$. By **factoring out the common factor**, the polynomial can be written as $6x\,(2x - 3)$. For $x = 1$ the polynomial equals $12 - 18 = -6$ and the product equals $6 \cdot (-1) = -6$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor the polynomial $8x^3 - 12x^2 + 20x$.

$$
\operatorname{MCD}(8, 12, 20) = 4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
8x^3 - 12x^2 + 20x &= 4x \cdot 2x^2 - 4x \cdot 3x + 4x \cdot 5 \\[6pt]
&= 4x\,(2x^2 - 3x + 5)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients are $8 = 2^3$, $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, and $20 = 2^2 \cdot 5$. The only prime factor common to all three numbers is $2$, with smallest exponent $2$, so the greatest common divisor is $2^2 = 4$. The variable $x$ appears in all three terms, with smallest exponent $1$, so the common factor is $4x$.

Dividing the three terms by $4x$ gives $2x^2$, $3x$, and $5$, with the signs of the original terms. The result is therefore $4x\,(2x^2 - 3x + 5)$. In the trinomial $2x^2 - 3x + 5$ the coefficients $2$, $3$, and $5$ have no common divisor other than $1$, and $x$ does not appear in the last term, so no further common factor can be taken out.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor the polynomial $x^3 - 5x^2 + 2x - 10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^3 - 5x^2 + 2x - 10 &= x^2\,(x - 5) + 2\,(x - 5) \\[6pt]
&= (x - 5)(x^2 + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The four terms have no factor common to all of them because $x$ does not appear in the last term and the coefficients $1$, $5$, $2$, $10$ have greatest common divisor $1$. Therefore, use **factoring by grouping**. Factoring $x^2$ out of the first two terms leaves $x - 5$. Factoring $2$ out of the last two also leaves $x - 5$ because $2 \cdot 5 = 10$.

Both groups have the common factor $x - 5$. Factoring it out gives $(x - 5)(x^2 + 2)$. The factor $x^2 + 2$ is irreducible since $x^2 \geq 0$, so $x^2 + 2 \geq 2$ is never zero. The factorization is therefore **complete**.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Factor the polynomial $x^3 + 2x^2 - 9x - 18$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^3 + 2x^2 - 9x - 18 &= x^2\,(x + 2) - 9\,(x + 2) \\[6pt]
&= (x + 2)(x^2 - 9) \\[6pt]
&= (x + 2)(x + 3)(x - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Group the first two terms together and the last two terms together. Factoring $x^2$ out of the first group leaves $x + 2$. In the second group, factor out $-9$ because $-9 \cdot x = -9x$ and $-9 \cdot 2 = -18$. This also leaves $x + 2$. Factoring out $x + 2$ gives $(x + 2)(x^2 - 9)$.

The factor $x^2 - 9$ is a **difference of squares** because $9 = 3^2$, so it factors as $(x + 3)(x - 3)$. All the factors have degree $1$, so the factorization is **complete**. For $x = 1$ the polynomial equals $1 + 2 - 9 - 18 = -24$ and the product equals $3 \cdot 4 \cdot (-2) = -24$.

</div>
