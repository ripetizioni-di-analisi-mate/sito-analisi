## Exercise 1

Factor the binomial $16x^2 - 9$.

$$
16x^2 = (4x)^2 ,
\qquad
9 = 3^2
$$
$$
16x^2 - 9 = (4x + 3)(4x - 3)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The binomial is the difference of two squares. The base of $16x^2$ is $4x$ because $(4x)^2 = 4x \cdot 4x = 16x^2$, and the base of $9$ is $3$. By the **difference of squares** identity, the binomial is the product of the sum of the bases and their difference, namely $(4x + 3)(4x - 3)$.

For $x = 1$ the binomial equals $16 - 9 = 7$ and the product equals $7 \cdot 1 = 7$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor the trinomial $9x^2 - 12x + 4$.

$$
9x^2 = (3x)^2 ,
\qquad
4 = 2^2 ,
\qquad
2 \cdot 3x \cdot 2 = 12x
$$
$$
9x^2 - 12x + 4 = (3x - 2)^2
$$

What remains is the square with side length $3x - 2 = 4$ and area $36 - 12 - 12 + 4 = 16$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first and last terms are squares with bases $3x$ and $2$. Twice the product of the bases is $2 \cdot 3x \cdot 2 = 12x$, which matches the middle term up to its sign. The middle term has a minus sign, so the trinomial is the **square of the binomial** $3x - 2$.

For $x = 1$, the trinomial equals $9 - 12 + 4 = 1$ and $(3 - 2)^2 = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor the polynomial $2x^3 - 12x^2 + 18x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^3 - 12x^2 + 18x &= 2x\,(x^2 - 6x + 9) \\[6pt]
&= 2x\,(x - 3)^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three terms have common factor $2x$ because $\operatorname{MCD}(2, 12, 18) = 2$ and the variable $x$ appears in all terms with smallest exponent $1$. **Factoring out the common factor** gives $2x\,(x^2 - 6x + 9)$, since $\dfrac{12x^2}{2x} = 6x$ and $\dfrac{18x}{2x} = 9$.

In the trinomial $x^2 - 6x + 9$ the terms $x^2$ and $9$ are squares with bases $x$ and $3$, and twice their product, $2 \cdot x \cdot 3 = 6x$, appears with a minus sign. The trinomial is therefore the **square of the binomial** $x - 3$, and the polynomial equals $2x\,(x - 3)^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Factor the binomial $x^4 - 81$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^4 - 81 &= (x^2)^2 - 9^2 \\[6pt]
&= (x^2 + 9)(x^2 - 9) \\[6pt]
&= (x^2 + 9)(x + 3)(x - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $x^4$ is the square of $x^2$ because $x^2 \cdot x^2 = x^4$, and $81$ is the square of $9$. The binomial is therefore a **difference of squares** and factors as $(x^2 + 9)(x^2 - 9)$.

The second factor, $x^2 - 9$, is itself a difference of squares with bases $x$ and $3$, so it equals $(x + 3)(x - 3)$. The first factor, $x^2 + 9$, is a sum of squares and is irreducible since $x^2 + 9 \geq 9$ never equals zero. The factorization is therefore **complete**.

</div>
