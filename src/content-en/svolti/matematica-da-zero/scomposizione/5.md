## Exercise 1

Find the GCD and LCM of the polynomials $4x + 8$ and $x^2 - 4$.

$$
4x + 8 = 2^2\,(x + 2) ,
\qquad
x^2 - 4 = (x + 2)(x - 2)
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD} = x + 2
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm} = 4\,(x + 2)(x - 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first polynomial can be factored by **taking out the common factor** $4$, and $4 = 2^2$. The second is a **difference of squares** because $4 = 2^2$, so it equals $(x + 2)(x - 2)$.

The only factor common to both polynomials is $x + 2$, which has exponent $1$ in both, so the $\operatorname{MCD}$ is $x + 2$. The $\operatorname{mcm}$ includes all the factors, common or otherwise, each with its largest exponent: $2^2$, $x + 2$, and $x - 2$. The $\operatorname{mcm}$ is therefore $4\,(x + 2)(x - 2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the GCD and LCM of the polynomials $3x^2 - 3x$ and $x^2 - 2x + 1$.

$$
3x^2 - 3x = 3x\,(x - 1) ,
\qquad
x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD} = x - 1
$$
$$
\operatorname{mcm} = 3x\,(x - 1)^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring $3x$ out of the first polynomial leaves $x - 1$. The second polynomial is the **square of the binomial** $x - 1$ because $x^2$ and $1$ are squares with bases $x$ and $1$, and twice the product, $2 \cdot x \cdot 1 = 2x$, appears with a minus sign.

The common factor is $x - 1$. Its exponent in the first polynomial is $1$ and in the second is $2$, so it appears in the $\operatorname{MCD}$ with the smaller exponent, $1$, and in the $\operatorname{mcm}$ with the larger exponent, $2$. The $\operatorname{mcm}$ also contains the unshared factors $3$ and $x$, so it is $3x\,(x - 1)^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the GCD and LCM of the polynomials $2x + 6$, $x^2 - 9$, and $x^2 + 6x + 9$.

$$
2x + 6 = 2\,(x + 3)
$$
$$
x^2 - 9 = (x + 3)(x - 3)
$$
$$
x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2
$$
$$
\operatorname{MCD} = x + 3 ,
\qquad
\operatorname{mcm} = 2\,(x + 3)^2 (x - 3)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor the first polynomial by taking out $2$. The second is a **difference of squares** with bases $x$ and $3$. The third is the **square of the binomial** $x + 3$ because $9 = 3^2$ and twice the product, $2 \cdot x \cdot 3 = 6x$, appears with a plus sign.

The factor $x + 3$ is the only one present in all three polynomials, with smallest exponent $1$, so the $\operatorname{MCD}$ is $x + 3$. The $\operatorname{mcm}$ includes $2$, $x - 3$, and $x + 3$ with its largest exponent, which is $2$ in the third polynomial. The $\operatorname{mcm}$ is therefore $2\,(x + 3)^2 (x - 3)$.

</div>
