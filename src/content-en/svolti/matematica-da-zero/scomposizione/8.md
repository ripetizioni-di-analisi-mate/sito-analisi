## Exercise 1

A square garden has side length $58\,\mathrm{m}$. A square pond with side length $42\,\mathrm{m}$ lies at its center. Use the difference of squares to calculate the area of the garden without the pond.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 58^2 - 42^2 \\[6pt]
&= (58 + 42)(58 - 42) \\[6pt]
&= 100 \cdot 16 \\[6pt]
&= 1600\,\mathrm{m^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The garden has area $58^2$ and the pond has area $42^2$, so the remaining area is the difference $58^2 - 42^2$. By the **difference of squares** identity, this equals the product of the sum of the side lengths, $58 + 42 = 100$, and their difference, $58 - 42 = 16$.

The product $100 \cdot 16 = 1600$ can be calculated mentally, whereas the squares $58^2 = 3364$ and $42^2 = 1764$ require two multiplications. The area of the garden without the pond is therefore $1600\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has area $x^2 + 7x + 10$, in $\mathrm{cm^2}$, and one side measures $x + 2$, in $\mathrm{cm}$, with $x > 0$. Find the length of the other side and calculate the perimeter of the rectangle for $x = 3$.

$$
\begin{cases}
a + b = 7 \\
a \cdot b = 10
\end{cases}
\qquad
a = 2 , \quad b = 5
$$
$$
x^2 + 7x + 10 = (x + 2)(x + 5)
$$
$$
x = 3
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x + 2 = 5 , \quad x + 5 = 8
$$
$$
2p = 2 \cdot (5 + 8) = 26\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 8,0 8,5 0,5" linee="0,0 8,0 8,5 0,5 0,0" etichette="3.4,-0.7,x + 5 = 8;-2.6,2.4,x + 2 = 5;2.9,2.4,area 40 cm²" xmin=-3 xmax=9 ymin=-1.4 ymax=5.6 titolo="For $x = 3$ the rectangle has side lengths $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and area $3^2 + 7 \cdot 3 + 10 = 40\,\mathrm{cm^2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a rectangle is the product of its side lengths. To find the other side, factor the area into a product that contains the factor $x + 2$. In the trinomial $x^2 + 7x + 10$ the coefficient of $x^2$ is $1$, and two numbers with sum $7$ and product $10$ are $2$ and $5$. By the rule for **factoring a trinomial using a sum and a product**, the area is $(x + 2)(x + 5)$, so the other side measures $x + 5$.

With $x = 3$ the sides measure $3 + 2 = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $3 + 5 = 8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The perimeter is twice the sum of the side lengths, or $2 \cdot 13 = 26\,\mathrm{cm}$. The area for $x = 3$ is $9 + 21 + 10 = 40\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, which agrees with $5 \cdot 8 = 40$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A car travels $120\,\mathrm{km}$ on its outward trip at a speed of $x\,\mathrm{km/h}$, with $x > 0$, and $120\,\mathrm{km}$ on its return trip at a speed of $(x + 20)\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Write the total travel time as a single rational expression and evaluate it for $x = 60$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{120}{x} + \frac{120}{x + 20} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{120\,(x + 20) + 120\,x}{x\,(x + 20)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{240x + 2400}{x\,(x + 20)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{240\,(x + 10)}{x\,(x + 20)}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = 60
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t = \frac{240 \cdot 70}{60 \cdot 80} = \frac{16800}{4800} = \frac{7}{2}\,\mathrm{h}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The time for each leg is the distance divided by the speed, so the outward trip takes $\dfrac{120}{x}$ hours and the return trip takes $\dfrac{120}{x + 20}$ hours. A speed is positive, so $x > 0$ and also $x + 20 > 0$, and the denominators are always nonzero.

The common denominator is $x\,(x + 20)$. Multiply the first numerator by $x + 20$ and the second by $x$, giving the sum $120x + 2400 + 120x = 240x + 2400$. Factoring out $240$ gives $\dfrac{240\,(x + 10)}{x\,(x + 20)}$.

With $x = 60$ the numerator equals $240 \cdot 70 = 16800$ and the denominator equals $60 \cdot 80 = 4800$. Dividing both by $2400$ gives $\dfrac{7}{2}$, so the trip takes $\dfrac{7}{2}$ hours, or $3$ hours and $30$ minutes. The two fractions give the same result separately because $\dfrac{120}{60} + \dfrac{120}{80} = 2 + \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}$.

</div>
