## Exercise 1

Which of the following years is a leap year?

- A) 1900
- B) 2100
- C) 2000
- D) 2030
- E) 1700

$$
\begin{aligned}
2000 &= 400 \cdot 5 \\[6pt]
1900 &= 100 \cdot 19, \qquad 1900 = 400 \cdot 4 + 300
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **leap-year rule**, a century year is a leap year only if it is divisible by $400$. The year 2000 is divisible by $400$, so it is a leap year. Each of 1700, 1900 and 2100 is divisible by $4$ and $100$ but not by $400$, so none is a leap year. The year 2030 is not even divisible by $4$. Options A, B and E result from testing only divisibility by $4$. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A training course meets every day, including holidays, from January 1 through March 31, 2028, inclusive. The year 2028 is a leap year. How many days does the course last?

- A) $90$
- B) $89$
- C) $92$
- D) $91$
- E) $60$

$$
31 + 29 + 31 = 91
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The period includes all of January, February and March, with both endpoints counted. January and March each have $31$ days. February has $29$ because 2028 is a leap year, so the course runs for $91$ days. Option A, $90$, uses $28$ days for February or counts the days *elapsed* between January 1 and March 31, excluding one endpoint. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

February 14, 2028 is a Monday, and 2028 is a leap year. What day of the week is February 14, 2029?

- A) Tuesday
- B) Wednesday
- C) Thursday
- D) Monday
- E) Sunday

$$
366 = 52 \cdot 7 + 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

February 29, 2028 falls between February 14, 2028 and February 14, 2029, so the two dates are $366$ days apart. Dividing $366$ by $7$ leaves a remainder of $2$. The weekday advances two places from Monday to Wednesday. Option A, Tuesday, is correct for an interval of $365$ days. It can be tempting if one considers only that 2029 is not a leap year. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
