## Exercise 1

A drawer contains $8$ white socks, $6$ black socks and $4$ gray socks. What is the smallest number of socks you must draw without looking to guarantee two of the same color?

- A) $3$
- B) $5$
- C) $10$
- D) $4$
- E) $16$

$$
\underbrace{1 + 1 + 1}_{\text{one sock of each color}} + 1 = 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **worst case** the first three socks have three different colors, so three draws do not guarantee a matching pair. The fourth sock must match one of those colors, giving a pair. The quantities $8$, $6$ and $4$ do not affect this calculation. Option A, $3$, would work if there were only two colors. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

From that same drawer, containing $8$ white socks, $6$ black socks and $4$ gray socks, what is the smallest number of socks you must draw without looking to guarantee two black ones?

- A) $10$
- B) $8$
- C) $14$
- D) $12$
- E) $16$

$$
8 + 4 + 2 = 14
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the color is specified, in the **worst case** every nonblack sock comes out first, namely the $8$ white socks and the $4$ gray socks. After those $12$ draws, only black socks remain. Another $2$ are needed, for a total of $14$. Option D, $12$, stops after the nonblack socks. Option A overlooks the gray socks that might also be drawn before the two black ones. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Candidates take part in a competitive exam. Count all $366$ possible birthdays, including February 29. What is the smallest number of candidates that guarantees at least three were born on the same day of the year?

- A) $732$
- B) $367$
- C) $1099$
- D) $731$
- E) $733$

$$
k \cdot n + 1 = 2 \cdot 366 + 1 = 733
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The days of the year are the boxes and the candidates are the objects. With $732$ candidates it is possible for every date to be the birthday of exactly two people, so three matching birthdays are not guaranteed. By the **pigeonhole principle** with $k = 2$ and $n = 366$, a group of $733$ guarantees at least one birthday shared by three people. Option B, $367$, guarantees only two matching birthdays. Option A counts everyone in the worst case without the final candidate. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
