## Exercise 1

Knife is to cut as thermometer is to:

- A) fever
- B) measure
- C) heat
- D) doctor
- E) warm

| pair | connecting sentence |
| --- | --- |
| knife : cut | a knife is used to cut |
| thermometer : measure | a thermometer is used to measure |
| thermometer : fever | a fever is what the thermometer detects |
| thermometer : doctor | a doctor uses the thermometer |

<div class="spiegazione">

A knife is a tool and cutting is its function, so the connecting sentence is “A knife is used to cut.” With thermometer, the sentence becomes “A thermometer is used to …”. The only verb among the options that completes it correctly is *measure*. Option E is a verb, but a thermometer is not used to warm things. Options A and C name what a thermometer detects, and option D names someone who uses it. These therefore express relationships other than **tool and function**. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Generous is to stingy as brave is to:

- A) bold
- B) strong
- C) hero
- D) fearful
- E) bravery

| pair | relationship |
| --- | --- |
| generous : stingy | opposites |
| brave : bold | synonyms |
| brave : strong | similar meanings |
| brave : hero | quality and a person who possesses it |
| brave : fearful | opposites |
| brave : bravery | adjective and noun from the same word family |

<div class="spiegazione">

A stingy person gives nothing, whereas a generous person gives willingly, so “generous” and “stingy” have opposite meanings. The relationship is **opposition**, so the missing word must be the opposite of “brave,” namely *fearful*. Option A appeals to anyone who recognizes that the words in the second pair must be related but does not check the type of relationship: “bold” is a synonym of “brave.” Option B has a meaning close to “brave,” and its own opposite is “weak.” The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Ticket office is to ticket as pharmacy is to:

- A) medication
- B) pharmacist
- C) written prescription
- D) illness
- E) hospital

| connecting sentence | with the option | does it complete the sentence? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| you buy a ticket at a ticket office | you buy medication at a pharmacy | yes |
|  | you buy the pharmacist at a pharmacy | no |
|  | you buy the written prescription at a pharmacy | no |
|  | you buy the illness at a pharmacy | no |
|  | you buy the hospital at a pharmacy | no |

<div class="spiegazione">

The sentence “A ticket office has something to do with a ticket” is too general, because a pharmacy has something to do with all the suggested words. The **precise connecting sentence** “You buy a ticket at a ticket office” gives “You buy … at a pharmacy,” which only *medication* completes. Option B pairs the pharmacy with a person who works there, corresponding to “ticket office is to ticket agent.” Option C refers to the written prescription you present at the pharmacy. What you buy there is the medication it authorizes. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
