## Exercise 1

Consider the following argument:

“Since the company cafeteria began offering a vegetarian menu alongside its regular menu, the number of meals served each day has risen from $150$ to $210$. Therefore, introducing the vegetarian menu brought more customers to the cafeteria.”

Which of the following statements, if true, weakens the argument?

- A) The vegetarian menu costs the same as the regular menu.
- B) During the same period, an office with $80$ employees who had previously eaten elsewhere moved into the building.
- C) Many cafeteria customers order from the vegetarian menu.
- D) Before introducing the vegetarian menu, the cafeteria served $150$ meals a day.
- E) The cafeteria is open from 12:00 to 14:30.

| option | effect on the argument | reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | none | does not address the link between the menu and customers |
| B | weakens | offers another possible cause of the increase |
| C | strengthens | customers do in fact choose the new menu |
| D | none | repeats a premise |
| E | none | does not address the link between the menu and customers |

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument concludes that the vegetarian menu is the **cause** of the increase in meals served. If $80$ new employees moved into the building during that period, the increase of $60$ meals per day can be explained without the vegetarian menu. Option B therefore **weakens** the argument. Option C points in the opposite direction because it shows that customers do choose the new menu. Options A and E concern the cafeteria but do not address the link between the menu and customers, and option D repeats information from the text, so all three are **irrelevant**. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Consider the following argument:

“Luca has a high school diploma, so he is eligible for the competitive exam for a position as a local police officer.”

Which of the following statements, when added to the premises, makes the conclusion necessarily true?

- A) Only candidates with a high school diploma are eligible for the exam.
- B) Luca has a high school diploma.
- C) Many candidates for the exam have a high school diploma.
- D) Anyone without a high school diploma is ineligible for the exam.
- E) Having a high school diploma is enough to be eligible for the exam.

$$
D = \text{has a diploma},
\qquad
P = \text{is eligible}
$$

| option | implication | given “Luca has a diploma” |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $P \Rightarrow D$ | $P$ does not follow |
| B | no implication, repeats the premise | $P$ does not follow |
| D | not $D \Rightarrow$ not $P$, equivalent to $P \Rightarrow D$ | $P$ does not follow |
| E | $D \Rightarrow P$ | $P$ follows (modus ponens) |

<div class="spiegazione">

The conclusion is necessarily true if having a diploma is a **sufficient condition** for eligibility, since it then follows from “Luca has a diploma” by modus ponens that Luca is eligible. Option E says exactly this. Options A and D state the same implication in two ways: D is the contrapositive of A. Both say that a diploma is *necessary*. A necessary requirement alone does not establish whether someone who meets it is eligible, since the exam rules may impose other requirements, such as age or a driver’s license. Option B repeats the premise, and option C concerns the other candidates. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A colleague says, “People who have taken the course on the new case management software no longer make mistakes when recording cases.” Which of the following statements, if true, proves the colleague’s statement false?

- A) Anna took the course in May and made mistakes recording cases in June.
- B) Bruno and Carla did not take the course and made no mistakes recording cases in June.
- C) The course lasts two days.
- D) Those who did not take the course made an average of $5$ mistakes per month.
- E) The new case management software was installed in March.

$$
C = \text{took the course},
\qquad
N = \text{makes no mistakes}
\qquad\qquad
\text{claim: } C \Rightarrow N
$$

| option | $C$ | $N$ | effect on $C \Rightarrow N$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | T | F | counterexample, makes it false |
| B | F | T | consistent |
| D | F | F for at least one person | consistent |

<div class="spiegazione">

The colleague claims that taking the course is a **sufficient condition** for making no mistakes: “If someone has taken the course, then they make no mistakes.” An implication is false only when the antecedent is true and the consequent is false. Anna took the course and made mistakes, so she provides a **counterexample** and the colleague’s statement is false. Option B shows only that the course is not necessary to avoid mistakes, and the colleague never claimed that it was. Option D concerns those who did not take the course and shows that at least one of them made mistakes. It says nothing about errors among those who took the course. Options C and E are irrelevant. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
