## Exercise 1

At a company, “All first-floor employees have degrees” and “All part-time employees have degrees” are true. Which conclusion necessarily follows?

- A) All first-floor employees are part-time
- B) Some first-floor employees are part-time
- C) No first-floor employees are part-time
- D) All graduates work on the first floor or are part-time
- E) None of the above conclusions

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,abc,bc,b" nomi="1° p.,laureati,part-time" etichetteNomi="1st fl.,grads,part-time" titolo="Both first-floor and part-time employees lie inside the graduates’ circle. The region common to all three circles may be empty or nonempty"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The middle term “graduates” appears in both premises as the broader category, so the argument has the invalid form known as the **undistributed middle**. The premises say only that first-floor and part-time employees are both within the group of graduates. In a company where only full-time employees work on the first floor, options A and B are false. In a company where everyone on the first floor works part-time, C is false. Option D reverses the direction of the premises and is false if a graduate works full-time on the second floor. Thus there is a **counterexample** to each of the four options. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which of the following arguments is valid?

- A) All musicians in the orchestra read music; Sara reads music; so Sara is a musician in the orchestra
- B) Some doctors are researchers; some researchers are teachers; so some doctors are teachers
- C) No member of the club is underage; Giulia is underage; therefore Giulia is not a member of the club
- D) All members of the club pay a fee; Marco is not a member of the club; therefore Marco does not pay a fee
- E) Some employees are not graduates; some graduates are not managers; so some employees are not managers

| argument | form | counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | affirming the consequent | Sara plays in a band |
| B | two particular premises | doctors, researchers, and teachers, with no doctor who teaches |
| C | valid | none |
| D | denying the antecedent | Marco pays the guest fee |
| E | two particular premises | all employees are managers |

<div class="spiegazione">

In argument C, if Giulia were a member of the club, she would not be a minor, by the first premise. Since she is a minor, she is not a member. The argument is therefore valid and is a case of the **syllogisms about an individual**. Argument A affirms the consequent, because people other than orchestra musicians can read music. Argument D denies the antecedent. Arguments B and E have two particular premises. For E, consider an office where all employees are managers and some graduates are outside consultants. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

“All auditors are accountants” and “No accountant is a member of the bar” are true. Which of the following conclusions does *not* necessarily follow?

- A) No auditor is a member of the bar
- B) Everyone who is not a member of the bar is an accountant
- C) No member of the bar is an auditor
- D) No member of the bar is an accountant
- E) All auditors are accountants who are not members of the bar

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,b,c,u" nomi="revisori,comm.,avvocati" etichetteNomi="auditors,acct.,lawyers" universo=1 titolo="People who are neither accountants nor members of the bar can lie outside all three circles"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Options A, C, D, and E follow from the premises. Option A follows by the **valid forms**. Options C and D follow because the categories in a statement with “no” can be interchanged, and E follows by combining the first premise with A. Option B concerns everyone who is not a member of the bar. The premises say nothing about people outside the three circles. An engineer who is neither a member of the bar nor an accountant therefore provides a **counterexample** to B. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
