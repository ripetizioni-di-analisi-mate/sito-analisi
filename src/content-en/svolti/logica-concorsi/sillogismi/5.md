## Exercise 1

“Some registry office employees belong to the union” and “All union members receive the union newsletter” are true. Which conclusion necessarily follows?

- A) All registry office employees receive the union newsletter
- B) Some registry office employees do not receive the union newsletter
- C) Everyone who receives the union newsletter is a union member
- D) Some registry office employees receive the union newsletter
- E) Some union members are not registry office employees

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="a,ac,abc,bc,c" nomi="anagr.,sind.,newsletter" etichetteNomi="registry,union,newsletter" elementi="abc:×" titolo="Union members lie inside the circle for newsletter recipients, so the registry office employee $\times$ who belongs to the union lies in the region common to the three circles"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The second premise makes the regions for union members outside the newsletter recipients empty. The first places a $\times$ in the overlap between registry office employees and union members. The only region of that overlap that remains colored is also inside the newsletter recipients’ circle. Therefore, at least one registry office employee receives the newsletter. This is the third of the **valid forms**: “Some $A$ are $B$, all $B$ are $C$, so some $A$ are $C$.” Option A generalizes to all employees what the premises establish for one. Option B is tempting if we read “some” as “some do and others do not.” The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At a hotel, “Some tourists who arrived today are French” and “No French tourist booked the guided tour” are true. Which conclusion necessarily follows?

- A) Some tourists who arrived today did not book the guided tour
- B) No tourist who arrived today booked the guided tour
- C) Some tourists who arrived today booked the guided tour
- D) Everyone who booked the guided tour arrived today
- E) Some French tourists did not arrive today

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="a,ab,ac,b,c" nomi="arrivati,visita,francesi" etichetteNomi="arrivals,tour,French" elementi="ac:×" titolo="The overlap between French tourists and people who booked the tour is empty, so the French tourist $\times$ who arrived today lies outside the tour group"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The second premise makes the regions common to French tourists and people who booked the guided tour empty. The first premise places a $\times$ in the overlap between today’s arrivals and French tourists. The only colored region in that overlap lies outside the tour group. There is therefore a tourist who arrived today and did not book the tour. This is the fourth of the **valid forms**: “Some $A$ are $B$, no $B$ is $C$, so some $A$ are not $C$.” Option B is tempting if we treat “some” as “all,” but tourists who arrived today and are not French may have booked the tour. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

“All the painters in the exhibition are also sculptors” and “Some painters in the exhibition are foreign nationals” are true. Which conclusion necessarily follows?

- A) All sculptors in the exhibition are painters
- B) Some foreign nationals are not sculptors
- C) Some foreign nationals are sculptors
- D) All foreign nationals are painters in the exhibition
- E) Some sculptors are not foreign nationals

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,abc,b,bc,c" nomi="pittori,scultori,stranieri" etichetteNomi="painters,sculptors,foreign" elementi="abc:×" titolo="The painters’ circle lies inside the sculptors’ circle, so the foreign painter $\times$ lies in the region common to all three circles"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the middle term is “painters,” the subject of both premises. The first premise makes the regions for painters outside the sculptors empty. The second puts a $\times$ among the painters who are foreign nationals, and that colored region lies inside the sculptors’ circle. The foreign painter is therefore a foreign national who is also a sculptor. This is the fifth of the **valid forms**: “All $B$ are $A$, some $B$ are $C$, so some $A$ are $C$.” Options B and E can be false, for example in an exhibition where every artist is a painter, a sculptor, and a foreign national. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
