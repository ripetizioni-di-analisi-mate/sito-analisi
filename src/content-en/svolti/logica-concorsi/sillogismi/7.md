## Exercise 1

At a conference, “No foreign guest booked the gala dinner” and “Everyone who booked the gala dinner received a parking voucher” are true. Which of the following statements *cannot* be true?

- A) Some foreign guests received a parking voucher
- B) Some of the people who booked the gala dinner are foreign guests
- C) No foreign guests received a parking voucher
- D) Everyone who received a parking voucher booked the gala dinner
- E) Some of the people who received a parking voucher are not foreign guests

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="a,ac,bc,c" nomi="stran.,cena,buono" etichetteNomi="foreign,dinner,voucher" titolo="The dinner guests have no members in common with the foreign guests and all have vouchers. Foreign guests may or may not have vouchers"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first premise says that no one belongs to both the foreign guests and the gala dinner group. Option B asserts that a foreign guest booked the dinner, so it is the **contradictory** of the first premise and cannot be true. Options A, C, and D describe possible situations because the premises do not say whether foreign guests received vouchers. Option E is true as soon as someone has booked the dinner, because that person has a voucher and is not a foreign guest. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A competition has $6$ judges. “All the judges are former athletes” and “Some former athletes are coaches” are true. Which of the following statements *can* be true but is *not* necessarily true?

- A) Some judges in the competition are not former athletes
- B) Some coaches are former athletes
- C) No former athlete is a coach
- D) No judge in the competition is a former athlete
- E) Some judges in the competition are coaches

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,abc,b,bc,c" nomi="giudici,ex atleti,allenatori" etichetteNomi="judges,former athletes,coaches" titolo="The regions for judges outside the former athletes are empty. The coach who is a former athlete may or may not also be a judge"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Options A, C, and D are always false. A and D contradict the first premise, because all $6$ judges are former athletes, while C is the **contradictory** of the second premise. Option B is always true because the two categories in an affirmative statement with “some” can be interchanged. That leaves E. If the coach who is a former athlete also serves as a judge, then E is true. If none of the judges coaches, the premises are still true and E is false. So E is possible but not necessary. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

“All civil protection volunteers took the first aid course” and “Some civil protection volunteers are firefighters” are true. Which of the following statements is *necessarily false*?

- A) Some firefighters did not take the first aid course
- B) Everyone who took the first aid course is a civil protection volunteer
- C) Some civil protection volunteers are not firefighters
- D) No firefighter took the first aid course
- E) All firefighters are civil protection volunteers

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,abc,b,bc,c" nomi="volont.,corso,vigili" etichetteNomi="vols.,course,firefighters" elementi="abc:×" titolo="The volunteer $\times$ who is a firefighter lies inside the group who took the first aid course"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **valid forms**, “Some firefighters took the first aid course” follows from the premises, because the volunteer who is a firefighter took the course. Option D is the **contradictory** of this conclusion, so it must be false. Option A is possible because firefighters who are not volunteers may not have taken the course. Options B, C, and E are possible because the premises do not rule them out. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
