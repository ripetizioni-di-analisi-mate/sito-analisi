## Exercise 1

What is the negation of “All service counters at the post office are open”?

- A) No service counter at the post office is open
- B) Some service counters at the post office are open
- C) At least one service counter at the post office is not open
- D) Most service counters at the post office are not open
- E) Exactly one service counter at the post office is not open

| situation | all are open | at least one is not open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $5$ service counters open out of $5$ | T | F |
| $3$ service counters open out of $5$ | F | T |
| $0$ service counters open out of $5$ | F | T |

<div class="spiegazione">

The negation must be true exactly when the given statement is false. “All service counters are open” is false as soon as one counter is closed, so by the **rules for negating the four forms** its negation is “At least one service counter is not open.” The table shows that the two statements always have opposite truth values. Option A is tempting if we negate “all” with “none.” In fact, with $3$ counters open out of $5$, the given statement and option A are both false. Options D and E are true only in some of the situations in which the given statement is false. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the negation of “Some regional trains depart late”?

- A) All regional trains depart late
- B) Some regional trains do not depart late
- C) Some trains that depart late are not regional trains
- D) All trains that depart late are regional trains
- E) No regional train departs late

$$
\text{some } R \text{ are } T
\quad\xrightarrow{\ \text{negation}\ }\quad
\text{no } R \text{ is } T
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

“Some regional trains depart late” asserts that *at least one* regional train departs late. This is false only when no regional train departs late, so the negation is “**No regional train departs late**.” Option B is tempting if we read “some” in its everyday sense of “some do and others do not.” In logic, “Some $R$ are $T$” and “Some $R$ are not $T$” can both be true, so they are not each other’s negations. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the negation of “Every applicant admitted to the oral test passed both the written test and the practical test”?

- A) No applicant admitted to the oral test passed both the written test and the practical test
- B) At least one applicant admitted to the oral test did not pass the written test or did not pass the practical test
- C) At least one applicant admitted to the oral test passed neither the written test nor the practical test
- D) At least one applicant admitted to the oral test passed the written test and did not pass the practical test
- E) Every applicant who was not admitted to the oral test passed both the written test and the practical test

| written | practical | “written and practical” | “not written or not practical” |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T | T | T | F |
| T | F | F | T |
| F | T | F | T |
| F | F | F | T |

<div class="spiegazione">

The statement is universal, so its negation asserts that there is an admitted applicant who does *not* have the property “passed the written and practical tests.” By **De Morgan’s laws**, the negation of “written and practical” is “not written or not practical,” with inclusive “or,” as the table shows. This gives option B. Options C and D each describe only one of the three rows in which the compound property is false. An applicant who passed the written test but not the practical test makes B true and C false. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
