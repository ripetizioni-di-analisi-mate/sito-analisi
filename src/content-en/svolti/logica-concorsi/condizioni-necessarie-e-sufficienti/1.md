## Exercise 1

The public library's rules state, “Living in the municipality is enough to get a library card.” Which statement has the same meaning?

- A) Only people who live in the municipality get a library card.
- B) People who do not live in the municipality do not get a library card.
- C) If someone lives in the municipality, then they get a library card.
- D) All and only the people who live in the municipality get a library card.
- E) If someone gets a library card, then they live in the municipality.

$$
N = \text{lives in the municipality},
\qquad
T = \text{gets a library card}
$$

| choice | implication | relation to the given statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $T \Rightarrow N$ | converse |
| B | not $N \Rightarrow$ not $T$ | inverse |
| C | $N \Rightarrow T$ | same implication |
| D | $N \Rightarrow T$ and $T \Rightarrow N$ | adds the converse |
| E | $T \Rightarrow N$ | converse |

<div class="spiegazione">

The word “enough” introduces a **sufficient condition**, so the rule translates as “if someone is a resident, then they get a library card.” Choice C states this same implication in “if … then …” form. Choices A and E say that residency is necessary, reversing the direction of the arrow. Choice B is the inverse, which is equivalent to the converse. Choice D adds the converse to the given implication and therefore says more than the rules provide. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For an integer $n$, what kind of condition is “$n$ is a multiple of $10$” for “$n$ is a multiple of $5$”?

- A) necessary but not sufficient
- B) sufficient but not necessary
- C) necessary and sufficient
- D) neither necessary nor sufficient
- E) necessary and sufficient only for positive numbers

| $n$ | multiple of $10$ | multiple of $5$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $20$ | T | T |
| $30$ | T | T |
| $15$ | F | T |
| $7$ | F | F |

::venn{tipo="incluso" zone="ab" nomi="multipli di 5,multipli di 10" etichetteNomi="multiples of 5,multiples of 10" elementi="a:5,15,25;ab:10,20,30" titolo="The multiples of $10$ are contained in the set of multiples of $5$, while $5$, $15$, and $25$ are multiples of $5$ but not of $10$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

If $n$ is a multiple of $10$, then $n = 10k = 5 \cdot 2k$ for an integer $k$, so $n$ is a multiple of $5$. Thus being a multiple of $10$ is sufficient. The converse does not hold because $15$ is a multiple of $5$ but not of $10$. This **counterexample** shows that the condition is not necessary. The same counterexample applies to positive numbers, so choice E is false. Choosing A reverses the roles of the two statements. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An apartment building's rules state, “A car may park in the courtyard only if it displays a permit.” If the rule is followed, which statement must be true?

- A) Every car displaying a permit is parked in the courtyard.
- B) A car that is not parked in the courtyard does not display a permit.
- C) Displaying a permit is enough to get a parking space in the courtyard.
- D) A car is parked in the courtyard if and only if it displays a permit.
- E) Every car parked in the courtyard displays a permit.

$$
P = \text{the car is parked in the courtyard},
\qquad
E = \text{the car displays a permit}
$$

| choice | implication | follows from the rule? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $E \Rightarrow P$ | no |
| B | not $P \Rightarrow$ not $E$ | no |
| C | $E \Rightarrow P$ | no |
| D | $P \Rightarrow E$ and $E \Rightarrow P$ | no |
| E | $P \Rightarrow E$ | yes |

<div class="spiegazione">

“Only if” introduces a **necessary condition**, so the rule translates as “if a car is parked in the courtyard, then it displays a permit.” Choice E says exactly that. Choices A and C treat the permit as sufficient, reading “only if” as “if.” Neither follows: a car with a permit may be parked on the street without breaking the rule. Choice B is the inverse, which is equivalent to the converse, and choice D also includes the converse. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
