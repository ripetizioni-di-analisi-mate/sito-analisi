## Exercise 1

If the statement “If there is a strike, the library stays closed” is true, which situation *cannot* occur?

- A) There is no strike and the library stays closed.
- B) There is no strike and the library is open.
- C) There is a strike and the library is open.
- D) There is a strike and the library stays closed.
- E) The library stays closed.

| strike | library closed | if there is a strike, the library is closed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T | T | T |
| T | F | F |
| F | T | T |
| F | F | T |

<div class="spiegazione">

An implication is false in just one case, when its antecedent is true and its consequent is false, as the rule for **when an implication is false** states. Here the case is “there is a strike and the library is open,” choice C, which corresponds to the second row of the table. Choices A, B, and D correspond to other rows in which the statement is true. Choice A may look plausible if one reads the statement as “the library is closed only if there is a strike,” yet the library may also be closed for a holiday. Choice E is consistent with the first and third rows. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

If the statement “Simone goes to the gym only if he has finished work by 19\:00” is true, which statement *cannot* be true?

- A) Simone goes to the gym and has not finished work by 19\:00.
- B) Simone has finished work by 19\:00 and does not go to the gym.
- C) Simone has not finished work by 19\:00 and does not go to the gym.
- D) Simone has finished work by 19\:00 and goes to the gym.
- E) Simone does not go to the gym.

| goes to the gym | finished by 19\:00 | the statement is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yes | yes | true |
| yes | no | false |
| no | yes | true |
| no | no | true |

<div class="spiegazione">

What follows “only if” is the consequent, so the statement translates as “if Simone goes to the gym, then he has finished work by 19\:00.” The only case that makes it false has a true antecedent and a false consequent: “Simone goes to the gym and has not finished by 19\:00,” choice A. Choice B can look plausible if one reads “only if” as “if” and rules out “he has finished and does not go to the gym.” That case is allowed because finishing early does not oblige Simone to go to the gym. Choices C, D, and E are consistent with the statement. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The statements “If Matteo takes his vacation in August, Teresa also takes hers in August” and “If Teresa takes her vacation in August, Andrea stays in the office in August” are true. Which situation *cannot* occur?

- A) Matteo takes his vacation in August and Andrea stays in the office in August.
- B) Andrea stays in the office in August and Teresa does not take her vacation in August.
- C) Teresa takes her vacation in August and Matteo does not.
- D) Matteo takes his vacation in August and Andrea does not stay in the office in August.
- E) Andrea does not stay in the office in August and Matteo does not take his vacation in August.

| Matteo | Teresa | Andrea in the office | situation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yes | yes | yes | A |
| no | no | yes | B |
| no | yes | yes | C |
| no | no | no | E |

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **transitive property**, the two statements imply “if Matteo takes his vacation in August, Andrea stays in the office in August.” The forbidden case is “Matteo takes his vacation and Andrea does not stay in the office,” choice D. For each other choice, the table gives a situation in which both statements are true, so A, B, C, and E are all possible. Choice C can look plausible if one reverses the first statement, and choice E if one uses the inverse of the chain. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
