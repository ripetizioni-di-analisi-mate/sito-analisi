## Exercise 1

An office has these rules: “If Franco goes on vacation, Maria fills in for him” and “If Maria fills in for Franco, Carlo also works on Saturdays.” Which statement must be true?

- A) If Carlo works on Saturdays, Franco is on vacation.
- B) If Carlo does not work on Saturdays, Franco is not on vacation.
- C) If Franco does not go on vacation, Carlo does not work on Saturdays.
- D) If Maria fills in for Franco, Franco is on vacation.
- E) Franco is on vacation and Carlo works on Saturdays.

| chain | consequence |
| --- | --- |
| $F \Rightarrow M \Rightarrow C$ | $F \Rightarrow C$ |
| not $C \Rightarrow$ not $M \Rightarrow$ not $F$ | not $C \Rightarrow$ not $F$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $F$ stand for “Franco goes on vacation,” $M$ for “Maria fills in for him,” and $C$ for “Carlo works on Saturdays.” By the **transitive property**, the two rules imply “if $F$, then $C$.” Its contrapositive is “if Carlo does not work on Saturdays, Franco is not on vacation,” choice B. Choice A is the converse of the forward implication, and C is its inverse. Neither follows from the rules, because Carlo may work on Saturdays for other reasons. Choice D is the converse of the first rule, and E asserts a fact, whereas the rules say only what happens *if* Franco goes on vacation. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

We know that “Anyone who plays a team sport takes the evening course” and “Anyone who takes the evening course leaves the office at 18\:00.” Luisa does not leave the office at 18\:00. Which conclusion follows?

- A) Luisa plays a team sport.
- B) Luisa takes the evening course but does not play a team sport.
- C) Luisa does not play a team sport.
- D) Whether Luisa plays a team sport cannot be determined.
- E) Luisa takes the evening course.

| forward chain | backward chain |
| --- | --- |
| team sport $\Rightarrow$ course $\Rightarrow$ leaves at 18\:00 | does not leave at 18\:00 $\Rightarrow$ no course $\Rightarrow$ no team sport |

<div class="spiegazione">

The given fact about Luisa negates the last link in the chain, so follow the chain backward by repeating **modus tollens**. Luisa does not leave at 18\:00, so she does not take the evening course, since anyone who takes it leaves at 18\:00. Since she does not take the course, she does not play a team sport. Choices B and E contradict the first step, because Luisa does not take the course. Choice D can look plausible if one confuses modus tollens with affirming the consequent, which instead starts from a true consequent. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three rules hold: “If the train is late, Paolo takes a taxi,” “If Paolo arrives after 12\:00, he takes a taxi,” and “If Paolo arrives after 12\:00, he misses the meeting.” Which statement must be true?

- A) If Paolo does not take a taxi, the train is not late and Paolo arrives by 12\:00.
- B) If the train is late, Paolo misses the meeting.
- C) If Paolo takes a taxi, he arrives after 12\:00.
- D) If Paolo misses the meeting, he arrives after 12\:00.
- E) If Paolo takes a taxi, the train is late.

| rule | contrapositive |
| --- | --- |
| delay $\Rightarrow$ taxi | no taxi $\Rightarrow$ no delay |
| after 12\:00 $\Rightarrow$ taxi | no taxi $\Rightarrow$ by 12\:00 |
| after 12\:00 $\Rightarrow$ misses the meeting | does not miss the meeting $\Rightarrow$ by 12\:00 |

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two rules have the same consequent, “Paolo takes a taxi.” By the contrapositive of the first, if Paolo does not take a taxi, the train is not late. By the contrapositive of the second, if Paolo does not take a taxi, he does not arrive after 12\:00, which means he arrives by 12\:00. Combining the two conclusions gives choice A. Choice B joins two arrows that do not connect. A delay leads to a taxi, not to arriving after 12\:00, so the **arrows do not form a chain**. Choices C, D, and E are the converses of the given rules. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
