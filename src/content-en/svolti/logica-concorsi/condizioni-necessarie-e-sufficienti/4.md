## Exercise 1

An exam has this rule: “If a candidate passes the written test, their name appears on the list of candidates admitted to the oral exam.” Giulia's name does not appear on the list. Which conclusion is correct?

- A) Giulia passed the written test.
- B) Anyone on the list passed the written test.
- C) Whether Giulia passed the written test cannot be determined.
- D) Giulia did not pass the written test.
- E) Anyone who does not pass the written test does not appear on the list.

|  | statement |
| --- | --- |
| rule | passes the written exam $\Rightarrow$ appears on the list |
| given fact | Giulia does not appear on the list |
| conclusion (modus tollens) | Giulia did not pass the written exam |

<div class="spiegazione">

The second premise negates the consequent of the rule, so **modus tollens** applies. If Giulia had passed the written test, her name would appear on the list. Her name does not appear, so she did not pass. Choice C can look plausible if one misses modus tollens, but the conclusion is certain. Choices B and E are the converse and the inverse. They do not follow from the rule because, for example, the list may include candidates who were admitted provisionally. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A courier guarantees, “If a shipment uses express service, it arrives within $24$ hours.” Stefano's shipment arrived within $24$ hours. Which statement is correct?

- A) Stefano's shipment used express service.
- B) Whether Stefano's shipment used express service cannot be determined.
- C) Stefano's shipment did not use express service.
- D) Every shipment that arrives within $24$ hours uses express service.
- E) A shipment that does not use express service does not arrive within $24$ hours.

| express service | arrives within $24$ hours | the guarantee is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yes | yes | satisfied |
| yes | no | violated |
| no | yes | satisfied |
| no | no | satisfied |

<div class="spiegazione">

The second premise affirms the consequent of the guarantee. In the table there are two rows in which the shipment arrives within $24$ hours, one with express service and one without, and both satisfy the guarantee. Thus neither A nor C follows from the premises, and inferring A would be **affirming the consequent**. A regular shipment may arrive quickly, for example if the recipient lives near the warehouse. Choices D and E are the converse and the inverse of the guarantee. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which argument is valid?

- A) If I take the train, I arrive on time. I arrived on time. Therefore I took the train.
- B) If I take the train, I arrive on time. I did not take the train. Therefore I did not arrive on time.
- C) I arrive on time only if I take the train. I did not take the train. Therefore I did not arrive on time.
- D) I arrive on time only if I take the train. I took the train. Therefore I arrived on time.
- E) I arrive on time only if I take the train. I did not arrive on time. Therefore I did not take the train.

| argument | first premise | second premise | type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | $T \Rightarrow P$ | $P$ | affirming the consequent |
| B | $T \Rightarrow P$ | not $T$ | denying the antecedent |
| C | $P \Rightarrow T$ | not $T$ | modus tollens |
| D | $P \Rightarrow T$ | $T$ | affirming the consequent |
| E | $P \Rightarrow T$ | not $P$ | denying the antecedent |

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $T$ stand for “I take the train” and $P$ for “I arrive on time.” In arguments C, D, and E the first premise contains “only if,” so it translates as “if $P$, then $T$.” In argument C the second premise negates the consequent $T$, so “not $P$” follows by **modus tollens**. Arguments A and D affirm the consequent, and B and E deny the antecedent. In all four cases, therefore, there is a situation in which the premises are true and the conclusion false. Argument E can look plausible if one reads “only if” as “if.” The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
