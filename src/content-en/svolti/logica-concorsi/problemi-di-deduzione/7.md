## Exercise 1

Someone left the office printer on over the weekend. The manager knows exactly one of four coworkers did it and questions them. Livio says, “Marta did it.” Marta says, “Oscar did it.” Nadia says, “I didn't do it.” Oscar says, “Marta is lying.” Exactly one statement is true. Who left the printer on?

- A) Livio
- B) Marta
- C) Nadia
- D) Oscar
- E) The information is insufficient to determine this

| culprit | Livio | Marta | Nadia | Oscar | true |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Livio | F | F | T | T | $2$ |
| Marta | T | F | T | T | $3$ |
| Nadia | F | F | F | T | $1$ |
| Oscar | F | T | T | F | $2$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

Marta's and Oscar's statements are **contradictory** because Oscar says Marta's claim is false. Thus one of them is true. Since exactly one statement is true, Livio's and Nadia's statements are false. Nadia's “I didn't do it” is false, so **Nadia** did it. The table confirms that if Nadia is the culprit, Oscar makes the only true statement. Option D takes Marta's accusation as true without counting the true statements. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A prize is hidden in exactly one of three boxes, colored red, green, and blue. The red box says “The prize is here,” the green says “The prize is not here,” and the blue says “The prize is not in the red box.” Exactly one inscription is true. Which box contains the prize?

- A) in the red one
- B) in the blue one
- C) None of them, because the conditions are contradictory
- D) The information is insufficient to determine this
- E) in the green one

| box containing the prize | red | green | blue | true |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| red | T | T | F | $2$ |
| green | F | F | T | $1$ |
| blue | F | T | T | $2$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

The inscriptions on the red and blue boxes are **contradictory**: the first says the prize is in the red box and the second says it is not. Therefore, one is true. Since there is exactly one true inscription, the inscription on the green box is false. It says “The prize is not here,” so the prize is in the **green box**. As a check, with the prize in the green box, the blue inscription is true and the red one is false. Option A takes the first inscription at face value. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

After a meeting someone left the lights on in the room. Exactly one of Pietro, Rocco, Sergio, and Tommaso did it. Pietro says, “Either Rocco or Sergio did it.” Rocco says, “I didn't do it.” Sergio says, “Tommaso did it.” Tommaso says, “Pietro didn't do it.” Exactly two of the four statements are true. Who left the lights on?

- A) Pietro
- B) Rocco
- C) Sergio
- D) Tommaso
- E) The information is insufficient to determine this

| culprit | Pietro | Rocco | Sergio | Tommaso | true |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pietro | F | T | F | F | $1$ |
| Rocco | T | F | F | T | $2$ |
| Sergio | T | T | F | T | $3$ |
| Tommaso | F | T | T | T | $3$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

No pair of statements is contradictory here. Test each of the four possible culprits and count the true statements, using the method for **problems with a fixed number of true statements**. If Rocco did it, Pietro's and Tommaso's statements are true, while Rocco's and Sergio's are false, giving two true statements. For each of the other three possible culprits the number of true statements is $1$ or $3$. Thus **Rocco** did it. Option C finds one case in which Pietro's statement holds and stops without counting the others. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
