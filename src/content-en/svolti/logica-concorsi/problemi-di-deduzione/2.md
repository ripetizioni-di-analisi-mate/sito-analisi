## Exercise 1

Aldo, Bea, Ciro, Dina, and Ezio are waiting in line at a post office counter. Aldo is first. Bea is immediately behind Ciro. Dina is behind Ezio. Ezio is not second. Who is third in line?

- A) Ciro
- B) Bea
- C) Ezio
- D) Dina
- E) The information is insufficient to determine this

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\
\text{Aldo} & \text{Ciro} & \text{Bea} & \text{Ezio} & \text{Dina}
\end{array}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Aldo is first, so the other four occupy positions $2$ through $5$. Position $2$ cannot belong to Ezio, or to Dina, who is behind Ezio, or to Bea, who is immediately behind Ciro. Therefore, Ciro occupies position $2$ and Bea, immediately behind him, occupies position $3$. Positions $4$ and $5$ remain, with Ezio ahead of Dina. Option A puts Bea ahead of Ciro, reversing the clue. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Five cars (red, blue, green, white, and black) occupy five parking spaces in a row, numbered $1$ through $5$ from the left. The blue car is immediately to the right of the red car. The green car is to the left of the black car. The white car is in space $2$. The black car is not in space $5$. Which car is in space $3$?

- A) the black one
- B) the red one
- C) the green one
- D) the blue one
- E) The information is insufficient to determine this

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}
1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\
\text{green} & \text{white} & \text{black} & \text{red} & \text{blue}
\end{array}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The red and blue cars occupy consecutive spaces, with the blue on the right. Since the white car occupies space $2$, the pair can occupy only spaces $3$ and $4$ or spaces $4$ and $5$. If it occupied spaces $3$ and $4$, the green and black cars would have spaces $1$ and $5$ remaining. The black car would have to occupy space $5$ because the green car is to its left. This contradicts the last clue. Therefore, the red car is in space $4$, the blue in $5$, the green in $1$, and the black in $3$. The strongest constraint, the **pair of consecutive spaces**, reduces the possibilities to two. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Six coworkers, Franco, Gina, Ivo, Lea, Mino, and Nora, are waiting in line at the cafeteria. Gina is third. Nora is immediately behind Ivo. Franco is neither first nor last. Mino is behind Nora. Lea is ahead of Gina. How many people stand between Franco and Mino?

- A) $2$
- B) $4$
- C) $0$
- D) $3$
- E) $1$

$$
\begin{array}{cccccc}
1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\
\text{Lea} & \text{Franco} & \text{Gina} & \text{Ivo} & \text{Nora} & \text{Mino}
\end{array}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Ivo, Nora, and Mino stand in that order, with Ivo and Nora consecutive. Since Gina occupies position $3$, Ivo and Nora cannot occupy positions $1$ and $2$, as that would leave no place for Lea ahead of Gina. Thus the pair is behind Gina and Mino is behind the pair. The only placement is Ivo in position $4$, Nora in $5$, and Mino in $6$. Lea is ahead of Gina and Franco is not first, so Lea is first and Franco second. Between positions $2$ and $6$ are positions $3$, $4$, and $5$, giving $3$ people. Option B uses the difference between the positions, $6 - 2 = 4$, which counts one person too many. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
