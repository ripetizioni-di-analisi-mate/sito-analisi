## Exercise 1

A notice admits candidates who have a diploma or at least three years of experience in the field. Which of the following candidates is *ineligible*?

- A) Bianca, who has a diploma and five years of experience
- B) Carlo, who has a diploma and one year of experience
- C) Dario, who has no diploma and has four years of experience
- D) Elisa, who has no diploma and has two years of experience
- E) Franco, who has a diploma and no experience

| candidate | diploma $D$ | at least three years of experience $E$ | $D \lor E$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bianca | T | T | T |
| Carlo | T | F | T |
| Dario | F | T | T |
| Elisa | F | F | F |
| Franco | T | F | T |

<div class="spiegazione">

The notice does not say "but not both," so "or" is the **inclusive disjunction** $D \lor E$, true when the candidate meets at least one of the two requirements. The disjunction is false only when both requirements are missing. Elisa has no diploma and only two years of experience, so she alone is ineligible. Option A appeals to someone who reads "or" as exclusive and assumes that meeting both requirements rules a candidate out. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A department has the rule "Either Elena or Franco, but not both, works the night shift." This week Franco works the night shift. Which of the following statements must be true?

- A) Elena works the night shift
- B) Elena does not work the night shift
- C) If Elena does not work the night shift, neither does Franco
- D) Elena works the night shift if and only if Franco does
- E) It is impossible to determine whether Elena works the night shift

| $E$ | $F$ | either $E$ or $F$, but not both |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T | T | F |
| T | F | T |
| F | T | **T** |
| F | F | F |

<div class="spiegazione">

The rule is an **exclusive disjunction**, true when exactly one of the two works the night shift. Since Franco works the shift, the only row of the table consistent with the rule has $E$ false and $F$ true, so Elena does not work the shift. Option E would be correct with inclusive "or," which allows both to work, but the text rules out this case with "but not both." Option C has a true antecedent and a false consequent, so it is false. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which of the following statements is equivalent to "Gianni works on either Saturday or Sunday, but not on both days"?

- A) Gianni works on Saturday or Sunday
- B) Gianni works on Saturday if and only if he works on Sunday
- C) Gianni works on Saturday if and only if he does not work on Sunday
- D) If Gianni works on Saturday, then he does not work on Sunday
- E) Gianni works on neither Saturday nor Sunday

| $S$ | $D$ | either $S$ or $D$, but not both | $S \Leftrightarrow \lnot D$ | $S \Rightarrow \lnot D$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T | T | F | F | F |
| T | F | T | T | T |
| F | T | T | T | T |
| F | F | F | F | **T** |

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $S$ = "Gianni works on Saturday" and $D$ = "Gianni works on Sunday." The **exclusive disjunction** is true when $S$ and $D$ have different values, that is, when $S$ has the same value as $\lnot D$. It is therefore equivalent to the **biconditional** $S \Leftrightarrow \lnot D$, as shown by the two matching columns in the table. Option D differs in the last row because it is also true when Gianni works on neither day, a case the given statement rules out. Option B is the negation of the given statement. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
