## Exercise 1

If "Tommaso gets the transfer only if he submits his application by the end of March" is true, which of the following must be true?

- A) If Tommaso submits his application by the end of March, he gets the transfer
- B) If Tommaso does not submit his application by the end of March, he does not get the transfer
- C) Tommaso gets the transfer if and only if he submits his application by the end of March
- D) If Tommaso does not get the transfer, he did not submit his application by the end of March
- E) Tommaso submits his application by the end of March and gets the transfer

$$
T \text{ only if } D
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T \Rightarrow D
\equiv
\lnot D \Rightarrow \lnot T
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $T$ = "Tommaso gets the transfer" and $D$ = "Tommaso submits his application by the end of March." The expression **only if** introduces the consequent, so the statement means $T \Rightarrow D$. Taking the **contrapositive** shows that this implication is equivalent to $\lnot D \Rightarrow \lnot T$, which is option B. Option A reads "only if" as "if" and reverses the implication, even though submitting the application on time does not guarantee the transfer. Option D is the inverse, which does not follow from the given statement. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A company has the rule "The meeting is held in the Verdi room unless the room is occupied." Today the meeting is not held in the Verdi room. Which of the following statements must be true?

- A) The Verdi room is occupied
- B) The Verdi room is unoccupied
- C) The meeting was canceled
- D) It is impossible to determine whether the Verdi room is occupied
- E) The meeting is held in another room

$$
\lnot O \Rightarrow R \equiv \lnot R \Rightarrow O,
\qquad
\lnot R \text{ true}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
O \text{ true}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $R$ = "The meeting is held in the Verdi room" and $O$ = "The Verdi room is occupied." The statement with **unless** means "if the room is unoccupied, the meeting is held in the Verdi room," that is, $\lnot O \Rightarrow R$. In the Italian expression "a meno che … non," the word "non" does not negate the condition. The contrapositive is $\lnot R \Rightarrow O$. Since today's meeting is not held in the Verdi room, the room is occupied. Options C and E concern facts that the rule does not mention, so neither must be true. Option B comes from reading the Italian "non" as a negation. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

If "It is not true that Lorenzo left early but did not let anyone know" is true, which of the following must be true?

- A) Lorenzo did not leave early and did not let anyone know
- B) Lorenzo let someone know
- C) Lorenzo did not leave early
- D) If Lorenzo left early, he let someone know
- E) Lorenzo let someone know and did not leave early

$$
\lnot (U \land \lnot V) \equiv \lnot U \lor V \equiv U \Rightarrow V
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $U$ = "Lorenzo left early" and $V$ = "Lorenzo let someone know." The word "but" has the same truth conditions as "and," so the statement is $\lnot (U \land \lnot V)$. By **De Morgan's laws**, it is equivalent to $\lnot U \lor V$. Expressing an implication as a disjunction shows that it is equivalent to $U \Rightarrow V$, that is, to option D. Options B, C, and E describe cases that are consistent with the statement, but none must be true. If Lorenzo did not leave early and did not let anyone know, the given statement is true and options B and E are false. If he left early and let someone know, the given statement is true and option C is false. Option A comes from negating both parts but retaining "and." The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
