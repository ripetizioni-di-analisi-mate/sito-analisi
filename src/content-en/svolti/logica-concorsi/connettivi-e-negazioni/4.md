## Exercise 1

What is the negation of the statement "If Paolo wins the race, he celebrates with his coworkers"?

- A) Paolo wins the race and does not celebrate with his coworkers
- B) If Paolo wins the race, he does not celebrate with his coworkers
- C) If Paolo does not win the race, he does not celebrate with his coworkers
- D) Paolo does not win the race and celebrates with his coworkers
- E) If Paolo celebrates with his coworkers, he won the race

$$
\lnot (V \Rightarrow F) \equiv V \land \lnot F
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $V$ = "Paolo wins the race" and $F$ = "Paolo celebrates with his coworkers." The implication $V \Rightarrow F$ is false only when $V$ is true and $F$ is false, so by the rule for **negating an implication** the answer is the conjunction "Paolo wins the race and does not celebrate with his coworkers." Options B, C, and E contain "if," so they are implications and can be ruled out immediately. Option B is the most common mistake because it negates only the consequent. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the negation of the statement "The employee did not request reimbursement or submitted the receipts"?

- A) The employee did not request reimbursement and did not submit the receipts
- B) If the employee requested reimbursement, they submitted the receipts
- C) The employee requested reimbursement or did not submit the receipts
- D) If the employee requested reimbursement, they did not submit the receipts
- E) The employee requested reimbursement and did not submit the receipts

$$
\lnot (\lnot C \lor R) \equiv \lnot \lnot C \land \lnot R \equiv C \land \lnot R
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $C$ = "The employee requested reimbursement" and $R$ = "The employee submitted the receipts." The statement is the disjunction $\lnot C \lor R$, and by **De Morgan's laws** its negation is $C \land \lnot R$. Expressing an implication as a disjunction shows that the given statement is equivalent to "if the employee requested reimbursement, they submitted the receipts," which is option B. Thus option B repeats the original statement. Option C negates both parts but retains "or." The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the negation of the statement "If Sofia passed the written and oral exams, she is placed on the ranking list"?

- A) Sofia passed neither the written nor the oral exam and is not placed on the ranking list
- B) Sofia passed the written and oral exams and is not placed on the ranking list
- C) If Sofia did not pass the written exam or did not pass the oral exam, she is not placed on the ranking list
- D) If Sofia passed the written and oral exams, she is not placed on the ranking list
- E) If Sofia is placed on the ranking list, she passed the written and oral exams

$$
\lnot \big( (S \land O) \Rightarrow G \big) \equiv (S \land O) \land \lnot G
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $S$ = "Sofia passed the written exam," $O$ = "Sofia passed the oral exam," and $G$ = "Sofia is placed on the ranking list." The antecedent is the conjunction $S \land O$ and stays intact because the **negation of an implication** asserts the antecedent and negates only the consequent. This gives "Sofia passed the written and oral exams and is not placed on the ranking list." Option A also denies the antecedent. Options C, D, and E are implications, so they can be ruled out. In particular, C is the inverse and E is the converse of the given statement. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
