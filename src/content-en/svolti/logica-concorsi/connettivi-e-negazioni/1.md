## Exercise 1

What is the negation of the statement "Marta is at the office and Luca is on a business trip"?

- A) Marta is not at the office and Luca is not on a business trip
- B) Marta is not at the office or Luca is not on a business trip
- C) Marta is at the office and Luca is not on a business trip
- D) Marta is at the office or Luca is on a business trip
- E) Marta is not at the office or Luca is on a business trip

$$
\lnot (M \land L) \equiv \lnot M \lor \lnot L
$$

| $M$ | $L$ | $M \land L$ | $\lnot M \lor \lnot L$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T | T | T | F |
| T | F | F | T |
| F | T | F | T |
| F | F | F | T |

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $M$ = "Marta is at the office" and $L$ = "Luca is on a business trip." The statement is therefore the conjunction $M \land L$. By **De Morgan's laws**, the negation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the two negations, namely "Marta is not at the office or Luca is not on a business trip." In the table, the columns for $M \land L$ and $\lnot M \lor \lnot L$ have opposite values in every row. Option A negates both parts but retains "and," so it is false even when Marta is at the office and Luca is not on a business trip, a case in which the original statement is false. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the negation of the statement "Stefano knows French or knows Spanish"?

- A) Stefano knows French and knows Spanish
- B) Stefano does not know French or does not know Spanish
- C) Stefano knows French or Spanish, but not both
- D) Stefano knows neither French nor Spanish
- E) Stefano knows French and does not know Spanish

$$
\lnot (F \lor S) \equiv \lnot F \land \lnot S
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $F$ = "Stefano knows French" and $S$ = "Stefano knows Spanish," the statement is the inclusive disjunction $F \lor S$, which is false only when Stefano knows neither language. By **De Morgan's laws**, its negation is therefore $\lnot F \land \lnot S$, that is, "Stefano knows neither French nor Spanish." Option B negates both parts but retains "or," so it is true even when Stefano knows French and does not know Spanish, a case in which the original statement is true. Option C is exclusive "or," which has a different meaning. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the negation of the statement "The warehouse is open but the manager is not present"?

- A) The warehouse is not open or the manager is present
- B) The warehouse is not open and the manager is not present
- C) The warehouse is not open and the manager is present
- D) The warehouse is open and the manager is present
- E) If the manager is present, the warehouse is not open

$$
\lnot (A \land \lnot R) \equiv \lnot A \lor \lnot \lnot R \equiv \lnot A \lor R
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $A$ = "The warehouse is open" and $R$ = "The manager is present." The word "but" has the same truth conditions as "and," so the statement is $A \land \lnot R$. By **De Morgan's laws**, its negation is $\lnot A \lor \lnot \lnot R$. Since double negation restores the original statement, this gives $\lnot A \lor R$, that is, "The warehouse is not open or the manager is present." Options C and D each describe only one case in which the original statement is false, whereas its negation must cover them all. Option E is equivalent to "the manager is not present or the warehouse is not open," which is true even when the original statement is true. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
