## Exercise 1

At a language school with $80$ students, $40$ study English, $30$ Spanish, and $25$ German. There are $12$ who study English and Spanish, $8$ who study English and German, $6$ who study Spanish and German, and $3$ who study all three languages. The pairwise counts include those who study all three. How many students study exactly one language?

- A) $52$
- B) $17$
- C) $72$
- D) $95$
- E) $8$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{English only} &= 40 - 9 - 5 - 3 = 23 \\[6pt]
\text{Spanish only} &= 30 - 9 - 3 - 3 = 15 \\[6pt]
\text{German only} &= 25 - 5 - 3 - 3 = 14 \\[6pt]
23 + 15 + 14 &= 52
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="a,b,c" nomi="I,S,T" elementi="a:23;b:15;c:14;ab:9;ac:5;bc:3;abc:3;u:8" titolo="Students studying English ($I$), Spanish ($S$), and German ($T$): the shaded regions contain students who study exactly one language"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Fill in the diagram **from the center outward**. The center contains $3$ students, so the regions for exactly two languages contain $12 - 3 = 9$, $8 - 3 = 5$, and $6 - 3 = 3$ students. In each language circle, subtracting the regions already filled gives $23$, $15$, and $14$ students studying only that language. Thus, $52$ students study **exactly one** language. Choice C counts those who study at least one language, and choice B counts those who study exactly two. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Each of the $60$ employees at a company uses at least one of the three programs $A$, $B$, and $C$. The programs are used by $40$, $30$, and $20$ employees, respectively, and $5$ employees use all three. How many employees use exactly two programs?

- A) $30$
- B) $25$
- C) $35$
- D) $15$
- E) $20$

$$
\begin{cases}
e_1 + e_2 + 5 = 60 \\
e_1 + 2\,e_2 + 3 \cdot 5 = 40 + 30 + 20
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
e_1 + e_2 = 55 \\
e_1 + 2\,e_2 = 75
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
e_1 = 35 \\
e_2 = 20
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $e_1$ be the number of employees who use **exactly one** of the programs and $e_2$ the number who use **exactly two**. Everyone uses at least one program, so $e_1 + e_2 + 5 = 60$. In the sum $40 + 30 + 20 = 90$, each employee is counted once for each program they use, so $e_1 + 2\,e_2 + 15 = 90$. Subtracting the first equation from the second gives $e_2 = 75 - 55 = 20$. Choice C is the value of $e_1$. Choice A, namely $90 - 60 = 30$, is the excess of the sum over the total. It ignores the two extra counts contributed by each person who uses all three programs. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A competitive exam has $50$ applicants. Each may submit one or more of three credentials, or none: a university degree, a language certificate, and a computer skills certificate. The degree is submitted by $35$ applicants, the language certificate by $24$, and the computer skills certificate by $15$. Of the applicants, $5$ submit all three and $6$ submit none. How many submit exactly one credential?

- A) $31$
- B) $20$
- C) $19$
- D) $39$
- E) $24$

$$
\begin{cases}
e_1 + e_2 + 5 = 50 - 6 \\
e_1 + 2\,e_2 + 3 \cdot 5 = 35 + 24 + 15
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
e_1 + e_2 = 39 \\
e_1 + 2\,e_2 = 59
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
e_1 = 19 \\
e_2 = 20
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $50 - 6 = 44$ applicants with at least one credential, so $e_1 + e_2 + 5 = 44$, where $e_1$ and $e_2$ count the applicants who submit **exactly one** and **exactly two** credentials. In the sum $35 + 24 + 15 = 74$, those with two credentials are counted twice and those with three are counted three times, so $e_1 + 2\,e_2 + 15 = 74$. The two equations give $e_2 = 20$ and hence $e_1 = 39 - 20 = 19$. Choice A results from overlooking the $6$ applicants without credentials. Choice B is the value of $e_2$. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
