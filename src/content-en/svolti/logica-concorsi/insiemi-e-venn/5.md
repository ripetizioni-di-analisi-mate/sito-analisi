## Exercise 2

At a hospital, consider the categories “doctors,” “surgeons,” and “women.” Which description corresponds to the correct Venn diagram?

- A) The circle for surgeons lies inside the circle for doctors, and the circle for women overlaps both and extends outside the circle for doctors
- B) The circle for surgeons lies inside the circle for doctors, and the circle for women is separate from both
- C) The three circles overlap pairwise and have a region common to all three, and each extends outside the other two
- D) The three circles are nested
- E) The circle for surgeons lies inside the circle for doctors, and the circle for women overlaps the circle for doctors without intersecting the circle for surgeons

| Region | Does it exist? | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doctors, surgeons, women | Yes | A woman who is a surgeon |
| Doctors and women, not surgeons | Yes | A woman who is a pediatrician |
| Doctors and surgeons, not women | Yes | A man who is a surgeon |
| Surgeons who are not doctors | No | Every surgeon is a doctor |
| Women who are not doctors | Yes | A woman who is a nurse |

<div class="spiegazione">

Every surgeon is a doctor, so the circle for surgeons is **included** in the circle for doctors. There are women who are surgeons, so the circle for women intersects the circle for surgeons. Some women are doctors but not surgeons, and others are not doctors, so the circle for women enters the part of the doctors’ circle outside the surgeons’ circle and extends outside the doctors’ circle. Description A meets all these requirements. Description E comes from the false assumption that all surgeons are men. Description C puts some surgeons outside the circle for doctors. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

| Pair | Relationship | Example |

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $12$ is a multiple of both $4$ and $6$, while $4$ is a multiple of $4$ only and $6$ is a multiple of $6$ only. The first two circles therefore **overlap** without either containing the other. Multiples of $4$ and multiples of $6$ are even, so the circle for odd numbers is **disjoint** from both. Diagram 4 shows two overlapping circles and a separate third circle. Diagram 3 is tempting if we think that every multiple of $6$ is also a multiple of $4$, but $6$ is not. Diagram 2 overlooks the number $12$. The correct answer is **D**.
