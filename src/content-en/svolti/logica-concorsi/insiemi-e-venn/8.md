## Exercise 1

In an office of $40$ employees, $28$ drink coffee and $22$ drink tea. What is the smallest possible number who drink both?

- A) $22$
- B) $10$
- C) $6$
- D) $50$
- E) $0$

$$
|C \cap T| \geq |C| + |T| - |U| = 28 + 22 - 40 = 10
$$

::venn{zone="ab" nomi="C,T" elementi="a:18;ab:10;b:12" titolo="With the smallest possible overlap, everyone drinks at least one of the two beverages and $10$ drink both"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Adding the numbers of coffee drinkers and tea drinkers gives $28 + 22 = 50$, but there are only $40$ employees, so at least $50 - 40 = 10$ employees are counted twice. This is the **minimum size of the intersection**. It is attained when everyone drinks at least one of the two beverages, as in the diagram. Choice A is the *maximum*, attained when all the tea drinkers also drink coffee. Choice C is the difference $28 - 22$. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an apartment building, $70\%$ of households have a car and $60\%$ have a bicycle. What percentage of households are guaranteed to have both?

- A) at least $30\%$
- B) at least $42\%$
- C) exactly $60\%$
- D) exactly $30\%$
- E) exactly $40\%$

$$
70\% + 60\% - 100\% = 30\%
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The universal set consists of $100\%$ of the households. By the **lower bound on the intersection**, the percentage of households with both vehicles is at least $70\% + 60\% - 100\% = 30\%$. The exact percentage cannot be determined from these data because it can range from $30\%$ to $60\%$. Choice B, namely $0.7 \cdot 0.6 = 42\%$, multiplies the percentages as though owning a car and owning a bicycle were independent, which the problem does not assume. Choice D takes the minimum as the exact value. Choice C is the maximum, attained only if every household with a bicycle also has a car. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a group of people, $80\%$ speak English, $70\%$ speak French, and $60\%$ speak Spanish. What is the smallest possible percentage who speak all three languages?

- A) $33.6\%$
- B) $20\%$
- C) $60\%$
- D) $50\%$
- E) $10\%$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{do not speak English} &= 20\% \\[6pt]
\text{do not speak French} &= 30\% \\[6pt]
\text{do not speak Spanish} &= 40\% \\[6pt]
\text{all three} &\geq 100\% - (20\% + 30\% + 40\%) = 10\%
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A person does not speak all three languages if they do not speak at least one of them, so the set of those who do not speak all three is the union of the three **complements**. This union contains at most $20\% + 30\% + 40\% = 90\%$ of the people, so at least $10\%$ speak all three languages. The minimum is attained when nobody lacks two languages, for example, if $20\%$ speak only French and Spanish, $30\%$ only English and Spanish, $40\%$ only English and French, and $10\%$ all three. Choice A is the product $0.8 \cdot 0.7 \cdot 0.6$, which assumes independence. Choice D comes from applying the two-set formula only to English and French. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
