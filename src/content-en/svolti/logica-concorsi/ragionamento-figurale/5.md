## Exercise 1

Which figure does *not* follow the same rule as the other four?

::figura{tipo="opzioni" celle="triangolo; punto no; punto ne; punto s | quadrato grigio; punto no; punto ne; punto so; punto se | pentagono; punto no; punto ne; punto so; punto se; punto s | esagono grigio; punto no; punto ne; punto so; punto se; punto s | triangolo grigio; punto no; punto ne; punto s"}

- A) figure A
- B) figure B
- C) figure C
- D) figure D
- E) figure E

| figure | sides of the polygon | dots |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $3$ | $3$ |
| B | $4$ | $4$ |
| C | $5$ | $5$ |
| D | $6$ | $5$ |
| E | $3$ | $3$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

Two polygons are unfilled and three are gray, so fill divides the figures into two groups without singling out an odd one. The common rule concerns a **relationship between the parts** of the cell: in figures A, B, C, and E, the number of dots equals the number of sides of the polygon. In figure D the hexagon has $6$ sides and there are $5$ dots, so D is the only figure that does not follow the rule. Figure E may draw attention because the triangle appears twice, but this repetition does not distinguish E from A. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An office uses five signs. Four follow a common rule and one does not. Which sign is the odd one out?

::figura{tipo="opzioni" celle="triangolo o; quadrato pieno e | quadrato pieno o; triangolo e | quadrato pieno o; pentagono e | pentagono o; esagono pieno e | esagono pieno o; pentagono e" titolo="The five office signs"}

- A) figure A
- B) figure B
- C) figure C
- D) figure D
- E) figure E

| sign | sides on the left | sides on the right | shape with more sides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | $3$ unfilled | $4$ solid | solid |
| B | $4$ solid | $3$ unfilled | solid |
| C | $4$ solid | $5$ unfilled | unfilled |
| D | $5$ unfilled | $6$ solid | solid |
| E | $6$ solid | $5$ unfilled | solid |

<div class="spiegazione">

Each sign contains a solid polygon and an unfilled one, and their numbers of sides always differ by one. Thus neither fill nor side count alone singles out a sign. The common rule relates fill to the number of sides: in signs A, B, D, and E the polygon with *more* sides is solid. In sign C the solid square has $4$ sides and the unfilled pentagon has $5$, so the polygon with fewer sides is solid. Looking only at position, the solid polygon is on the left in signs B, C, and E. But this divides the signs into groups of three and two, whereas an **odd-figure-out** rule must leave only one figure outside the group. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which figure does *not* follow the same rule as the other four?

::figura{tipo="opzioni" celle="freccia r45; cerchio pieno ne; cerchio so | freccia r135; cerchio pieno se; cerchio no | freccia r-45; cerchio pieno no; cerchio se | freccia r-135; cerchio pieno so; cerchio ne | freccia r-45; cerchio pieno ne; cerchio so"}

- A) figure A
- B) figure B
- C) figure C
- D) figure D
- E) figure E

| figure | the arrow points toward | solid circle | unfilled circle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | top right | top right | bottom left |
| B | bottom right | bottom right | top left |
| C | top left | top left | bottom right |
| D | bottom left | bottom left | top right |
| E | top left | top right | bottom left |

<div class="spiegazione">

In each figure the arrow lies along a diagonal of the cell, and two circles, one solid and one unfilled, occupy opposite corners. In figures A, B, C, and D the two circles are at the ends of the diagonal along which the arrow lies, and the arrow points toward the **solid circle**. In figure E the arrow points to the top left, while the circles are at the top right and bottom left on the other diagonal. Thus E is the only figure in which the arrow does not point toward the solid circle. Figures C and E have arrows with the same orientation, so orientation alone does not identify the odd figure out. The correct answer is **E**.

</div>
