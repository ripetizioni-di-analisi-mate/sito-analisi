## Exercise 1

How many triangles are in the figure?

::figura{tipo="linee" segmenti="0,10,10,10;0,10,5,0;10,10,5,0;5,0,3.5,10;5,0,6.5,10;2.5,5,7.5,5" titolo="A triangle with two segments from the top vertex and a horizontal segment halfway up"}

- A) $6$
- B) $8$
- C) $10$
- D) $12$
- E) $14$

$$
\text{triangles with a base on the midheight segment} = \frac{3 \cdot 4}{2} = 6
$$

$$
\text{triangles with a base on the base of the original triangle} = \frac{3 \cdot 4}{2} = 6
$$

$$
6 + 6 = 12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the sloping segments start at the top vertex, so every triangle has that vertex and its opposite side lies on one of the two horizontal segments. The two interior sloping segments divide the horizontal segment halfway up into three parts. By the rule for **triangles with a common vertex**, $\dfrac{3 \cdot 4}{2} = 6$ triangles have their base on this segment. The same count applies to the base of the original triangle, which is also divided into three parts. Thus the total number of triangles is $12$. Counting triangles with only one of the two possible base levels gives $6$, and counting only those made up of one or two parts gives $10$. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle $4$ grid squares wide and $2$ grid squares high is cut from a sheet of graph paper as shown. How many squares of any size can be traced along the grid lines?

::figura{tipo="linee" segmenti="0,2,10,2;0,4.5,10,4.5;0,7,10,7;0,2,0,7;2.5,2,2.5,7;5,2,5,7;7.5,2,7.5,7;10,2,10,7" titolo="The rectangle cut from the graph paper"}

- A) $8$
- B) $11$
- C) $12$
- D) $14$
- E) $30$

$$
\text{squares of side length } 1 = 4 \cdot 2 = 8,
\qquad
\text{squares of side length } 2 = 3 \cdot 1 = 3
$$

$$
8 + 3 = 11
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rectangle is $2$ grid squares high, so squares can have side length $1$ or $2$. The squares of side length $1$ are the individual grid squares: $4$ per row in $2$ rows, giving $8$. A square of side length $2$ takes up the full height and two adjacent columns. There are $3$ pairs of adjacent columns, so the number of squares of side length $2$ is $3$. This gives $11$ squares in all. Counting one square of side length $2$ for every column gives $8 + 4 = 12$. Using the formula for a $3 \times 3$ grid gives $14$, and counting all rectangles gives $30$. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a warehouse identical cubic boxes are stacked on a square base with $3 \times 3$ positions. The figure shows the top view. Each number gives the number of boxes stacked in that position. How many boxes must be added to make a solid $3 \times 3 \times 3$ block of boxes?

::figura{tipo="matrice" celle="lettera:3 | lettera:1 | lettera:2 | lettera:2 | lettera:0 | lettera:1 | lettera:3 | lettera:2 | lettera:2" titolo="Top view of the stacks of boxes"}

- A) $11$
- B) $13$
- C) $16$
- D) $18$
- E) $27$

$$
3 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 0 + 1 + 3 + 2 + 2 = 16
$$

$$
27 - 16 = 11
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **top view**, each number gives the height of a stack. The number of boxes present is therefore the sum of the nine numbers, $16$. A solid $3 \times 3 \times 3$ block contains $27$ boxes, so $27 - 16 = 11$ boxes are missing. The same result is obtained by adding the number of boxes each position needs to reach $3$, giving $0 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 3 + 2 + 0 + 1 + 1 = 11$. The answer $16$ counts the boxes already present. The answer $18$ counts only one box in each of the $9$ positions, subtracting $9$ from $27$. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
