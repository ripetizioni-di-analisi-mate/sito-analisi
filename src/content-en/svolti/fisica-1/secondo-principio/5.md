## Exercise 1

A diatomic ideal gas ($\gamma = 1.4$), with $n = 0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$, goes through a Carnot cycle between $T_1 = 500\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_2 = 300\,\mathrm{K}$. In the isothermal expansion the volume goes from $V_A = 4.0\,\mathrm{L}$ to $V_B = 12.0\,\mathrm{L}$. Compute the heats exchanged in the two isothermal processes, the work per cycle, the efficiency and the volumes $V_C$ and $V_D$ at the end of the adiabatic expansion and of the adiabatic compression.

::grafico{curve="t<1?(4+(8)*(t-0)):(t<2?(12+(31.03)*(t-1)):(t<3?(43.03+(-28.69)*(t-2)):((14.34+(-10.34)*(t-3))))),t<1?20.79/(4+(8)*(t-0)):(t<2?56.16/(12+(31.03)*(t-1))^1.4:(t<3?12.47/(43.03+(-28.69)*(t-2)):(36.19/(14.34+(-10.34)*(t-3))^1.4))),0,4" vettori="7.2,2.89,8.8,2.36;24.4,0.641,30.6,0.467;31.6,0.395,25.8,0.483;10.2,1.4,8.14,1.92" punti="4,5.2,pieno,A;12,1.73,pieno,B;43.03,0.29,pieno,C;14.34,0.87,pieno,D" xmin=0 xmax=48 ymin=-0.3 ymax=6 nomi="V,p" titolo="The cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$: the adiabats $BC$ and $DA$ connect the isotherms at $500\,\mathrm{K}$ and at $300\,\mathrm{K}$"}

$$
Q_1 = nRT_1 \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A},
\qquad
Q_2 = -\frac{T_2}{T_1}\, Q_1,
\qquad
L = Q_1 + Q_2,
\qquad
\eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}
$$
$$
T_1 V_B^{\gamma - 1} = T_2 V_C^{\gamma - 1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_C = V_B \left( \frac{T_1}{T_2} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}},
\qquad
V_D = V_A \left( \frac{T_1}{T_2} \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}}
$$
$$
Q_1 = 0.50 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 500 \cdot \ln 3\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.28 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
Q_2 = -\frac{300}{500} \cdot 2283\,\mathrm{J} \approx -1.37 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
L \approx 2283\,\mathrm{J} - 1370\,\mathrm{J} \approx 913\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
\eta_C = 1 - \frac{300}{500} = 0.40
$$
$$
\left( \frac{5}{3} \right)^{2.5} \approx 3.59,
\qquad
V_C \approx 12.0\,\mathrm{L} \cdot 3.59 \approx 43\,\mathrm{L},
\qquad
V_D \approx 4.0\,\mathrm{L} \cdot 3.59 \approx 14\,\mathrm{L}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Along the isotherm $A \to B$ the internal energy of the ideal gas remains constant, therefore the heat absorbed equals the work of isothermal expansion. The heat exchanged along the other isotherm is obtained from the relation of the Carnot cycle $\dfrac{Q_2}{Q_1} = -\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$; it is negative because the gas releases it to the cold reservoir. The work is the sum of the two heats, since there is no heat exchange along the adiabats, and the **efficiency** is $0.40$; as a check, $\dfrac{913}{2283} \approx 0.40$. The volumes $V_C$ and $V_D$ follow from the relation $T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$ of reversible adiabats, with exponent $\dfrac{1}{\gamma - 1} = 2.5$. The ratio $\dfrac{V_C}{V_D}$ turns out to equal $\dfrac{V_B}{V_A} = 3$, and indeed computing $Q_2 = nRT_2 \ln\dfrac{V_D}{V_C}$ gives the same value $-1.37 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A Carnot engine has efficiency $0.30$, releases heat to a reservoir at $280\,\mathrm{K}$ and produces $1.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of work per cycle. The working fluid is one mole of ideal gas. Compute the temperature of the hot reservoir, the heats exchanged in one cycle, the change in entropy of the gas in the isothermal expansion and the ratio $\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$ of the volumes at the end and at the beginning of this expansion.

$$
\eta_C = 0.30, \qquad T_2 = 280\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad L = 1.2\,\mathrm{kJ}, \qquad n = 1.0\,\mathrm{mol}
$$
$$
T_1 = \frac{T_2}{1 - \eta_C},
\qquad
Q_1 = \frac{L}{\eta_C},
\qquad
Q_2 = L - Q_1,
\qquad
\Delta S_{AB} = \frac{Q_1}{T_1} = nR \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A}
$$
$$
T_1 = \frac{280\,\mathrm{K}}{0.70} = 400\,\mathrm{K},
\qquad
Q_1 = \frac{1.2\,\mathrm{kJ}}{0.30} = 4.0\,\mathrm{kJ},
\qquad
Q_2 = -2.8\,\mathrm{kJ}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{AB} = \frac{4000\,\mathrm{J}}{400\,\mathrm{K}} = 10\,\mathrm{J/K},
\qquad
\frac{V_B}{V_A} = e^{\frac{\Delta S_{AB}}{nR}} = e^{\frac{10}{8.314}} \approx 3.3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Carnot efficiency is $1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$, therefore $\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = 0.70$ and the hot reservoir is at $400\,\mathrm{K}$. By the definition of efficiency, the heat absorbed is the work divided by the efficiency, and the heat released is the difference between the heat absorbed and the work. The isothermal expansion is reversible and occurs at the constant temperature $T_1$, therefore the change in **entropy** of the gas is $\dfrac{Q_1}{T_1}$. For an isothermal process of the ideal gas the same change equals $nR \ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$; equating the two expressions gives the ratio of the volumes, about $3.3$. In the isothermal compression the entropy of the gas decreases by $\dfrac{2800}{280} = 10\,\mathrm{J/K}$, and along the reversible adiabats it does not change, therefore in a cycle the total change is zero, as it must be for a state function.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two moles of monatomic ideal gas ($\gamma = \dfrac{5}{3}$) go through a Carnot cycle. The isothermal expansion occurs at $T_1 = 800\,\mathrm{K}$ and doubles the volume; the subsequent adiabatic expansion takes the volume from $V_B = 2.0\,\mathrm{L}$ to $V_C = 16\,\mathrm{L}$. Compute the temperature $T_2$ of the cold reservoir, the efficiency, the heat absorbed, the work per cycle and the work done by the gas in each of the two adiabatic processes.

$$
T_2 = T_1 \left( \frac{V_B}{V_C} \right)^{\gamma - 1},
\qquad
\eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1},
\qquad
Q_1 = nRT_1 \ln 2,
\qquad
L = \eta_C\, Q_1
$$
$$
L_{BC} = -\Delta U_{BC} = n c_V\, (T_1 - T_2),
\qquad
L_{DA} = -\Delta U_{DA} = -\,n c_V\, (T_1 - T_2)
$$
$$
T_2 = 800\,\mathrm{K} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{8} \right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = \frac{800\,\mathrm{K}}{4} = 200\,\mathrm{K},
\qquad
\eta_C = 1 - \frac{200}{800} = 0.75
$$
$$
Q_1 = 2 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 800 \cdot \ln 2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 9.22 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
L \approx 0.75 \cdot 9221\,\mathrm{J} \approx 6.92 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
L_{BC} = 2 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 600\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.50 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
L_{DA} \approx -1.50 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expansion $B \to C$ is reversible adiabatic, therefore $T V^{\gamma - 1}$ remains constant; with $\gamma - 1 = \dfrac{2}{3}$ and a volume that becomes eight times larger, the temperature is divided by $8^{\frac{2}{3}} = 4$ and the cold reservoir is at $200\,\mathrm{K}$. The Carnot efficiency is then $0.75$. In the isothermal process at $800\,\mathrm{K}$ the volume doubles and the heat absorbed equals the work of expansion, $nRT_1 \ln 2$. In an adiabatic process $Q = 0$, therefore by the first law the work done by the gas is $-\Delta U = -n c_V\, \Delta T$, with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$. The two adiabatic processes have opposite changes in temperature, therefore their works are **opposite** and cancel in the work per cycle; hence the net work is due to the isothermal processes alone, $L = Q_1 + Q_2$, with $Q_2 = -\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}\, Q_1 \approx -2.31 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>
