## Exercise 1

In an insulated container $0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ are mixed. Neglecting the heat capacity of the container and with $c = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, compute the equilibrium temperature and the change in entropy of the universe.

$$
m_1 = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}, \quad T_1 = 353\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad m_2 = 0.60\,\mathrm{kg}, \quad T_2 = 293\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
m_1 c\, (T_1 - T_e) = m_2 c\, (T_e - T_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_e = \frac{m_1 T_1 + m_2 T_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = m_1 c \ln\frac{T_e}{T_1} + m_2 c \ln\frac{T_e}{T_2}
$$
$$
T_e = \frac{0.40 \cdot 353 + 0.60 \cdot 293}{1.00}\,\mathrm{K} = 317\,\mathrm{K} = 44\,^\circ\mathrm{C}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S_{\text{u}} &= 1674\,\mathrm{J/K} \cdot \ln\frac{317}{353} + 2512\,\mathrm{J/K} \cdot \ln\frac{317}{293} \\[6pt]
&\approx -180.1\,\mathrm{J/K} + 197.7\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 17.6\,\mathrm{J/K}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The container is insulated, therefore the heat released by the hot water equals the heat absorbed by the cold water, and the equilibrium temperature is the average of the temperatures weighted by the masses. The universe consists of the two masses of water, because the container exchanges nothing. Each mass changes temperature with constant specific heat, therefore its change in entropy is $mc \ln\dfrac{T_{\text{final}}}{T_{\text{initial}}}$, negative for the hot water and positive for the cold water. The gain exceeds the loss, since the same heat is absorbed at lower temperatures than those at which it is released, and the entropy of the universe **increases** by about $17.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Mixing water at different temperatures is therefore irreversible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

$0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is immersed in an insulated calorimeter, of negligible heat capacity, containing $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $30\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. With $c = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ and $\lambda_f = 3.33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, verify that the ice melts completely, and compute the equilibrium temperature and the change in entropy of the universe.

$$
m_g = 0.10\,\mathrm{kg}, \quad T_0 = 273\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad m_a = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}, \quad T_a = 303\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
m_g \lambda_f = 3.33 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J} < m_a c\, (T_a - T_0) \approx 6.28 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
m_a c\, (T_a - T_e) = m_g \lambda_f + m_g c\, (T_e - T_0)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_e = \frac{m_a c\, T_a + m_g c\, T_0 - m_g \lambda_f}{(m_a + m_g)\, c}
$$
$$
T_e = \frac{2093 \cdot 303 + 418.6 \cdot 273 - 33\,300}{2512}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 284.7\,\mathrm{K} \approx 11.7\,^\circ\mathrm{C}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = \frac{m_g \lambda_f}{T_0} + m_g c \ln\frac{T_e}{T_0} + m_a c \ln\frac{T_e}{T_a}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} \approx 122.0\,\mathrm{J/K} + 17.6\,\mathrm{J/K} - 130.1\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 9.5\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before writing the balance, the final state must be established. The heat needed to melt all the ice is less than the heat that the water would release by cooling down to $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, therefore the ice melts completely and the final temperature is above $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. The balance equates the heat released by the warm water to the heat absorbed by the ice, which first melts at $273\,\mathrm{K}$ and then, as water, warms up to $T_e$. The entropy is computed by adding three contributions along reversible processes between the same states: the **melting** at constant temperature, the warming of the meltwater and the cooling of the warm water. The sum is positive, as the principle of entropy increase predicts for the isolated system formed by the calorimeter and its contents.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An engine operates between a reservoir at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ and one at $300\,\mathrm{K}$; in each cycle it absorbs $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ and produces $420\,\mathrm{J}$ of work. Compute the change in entropy of the universe in one cycle and the work lost compared with a reversible engine that absorbs the same heat, verifying that it equals $T_2\, \Delta S_{\text{u}}$.

$$
T_1 = 600\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad T_2 = 300\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad Q_1 = 1200\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad L = 420\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
Q_2 = L - Q_1,
\qquad
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = -\frac{Q_1}{T_1} - \frac{Q_2}{T_2},
\qquad
L_{\text{rev}} = Q_1 \left( 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1} \right)
$$
$$
Q_2 = 420\,\mathrm{J} - 1200\,\mathrm{J} = -780\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = -\frac{1200\,\mathrm{J}}{600\,\mathrm{K}} + \frac{780\,\mathrm{J}}{300\,\mathrm{K}} = -2.0\,\mathrm{J/K} + 2.6\,\mathrm{J/K} = 0.60\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
L_{\text{rev}} = 1200\,\mathrm{J} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 600\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
L_{\text{rev}} - L = 180\,\mathrm{J} = 300\,\mathrm{K} \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The working fluid of the engine goes through a cycle, therefore its entropy at the end of the cycle equals the initial one, and the change in entropy of the universe is the sum of those of the two reservoirs. The hot reservoir releases $1200\,\mathrm{J}$ at $600\,\mathrm{K}$ and the cold reservoir receives $780\,\mathrm{J}$ at $300\,\mathrm{K}$, therefore $\Delta S_{\text{u}} = 0.60\,\mathrm{J/K} > 0$ and the engine is **irreversible**. A reversible engine between the same reservoirs has efficiency $0.50$ and, with the same heat absorbed, would produce $600\,\mathrm{J}$. The difference of $180\,\mathrm{J}$ is the **lost work**, and it coincides with the product of the temperature of the cold reservoir and the entropy produced, as the relation between entropy and lost work establishes.

</div>

## Exercise 4

One mole of monatomic ideal gas is enclosed by a piston in a cylinder with insulating walls, at $T_A = 400\,\mathrm{K}$ and $p_A = 4.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The external pressure is suddenly brought to $p_e = 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and kept constant, and the gas expands until equilibrium with $p_B = p_e$. Compute the final temperature, the change in entropy of the gas and the temperature the gas would reach with a reversible adiabatic expansion down to the same pressure.

::grafico{curve="t,4*(8.314/t)^1.6667,8.314,19.1" segmenti="0,1,25,1" punti="8.314,4,pieno,A;23.28,1,pieno,B;19.1,1,vuoto,B'" xmin=0 xmax=26 ymin=-0.3 ymax=4.6 nomi="V,p" titolo="Initial state $A$, final state $B$ of the irreversible expansion and state $B'$ reached by the reversible adiabat at the same pressure, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$"}

$$
Q = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta U = -L,
\qquad
n c_V\, (T_B - T_A) = -\,p_e\, (V_B - V_A) = -\,nR \left( T_B - \frac{p_e}{p_A}\, T_A \right)
$$
$$
T_B = T_A\, \frac{c_V + R\, \dfrac{p_e}{p_A}}{c_p}
= 400\,\mathrm{K} \cdot \frac{\dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4}}{\dfrac{5}{2}} = 280\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
\Delta S = n c_p \ln\frac{T_B}{T_A} - nR \ln\frac{p_B}{p_A}
= 8.314 \cdot \left( \frac{5}{2} \ln 0.70 + \ln 4 \right)\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 4.1\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
T_{B'} = T_A \left( \frac{p_e}{p_A} \right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} = 400\,\mathrm{K} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^{0.4} \approx 230\,\mathrm{K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The walls are insulating, therefore $Q = 0$ and the first law gives $\Delta U = -L$. The gas expands against the constant external pressure $p_e$, therefore the work it does is $p_e\, (V_B - V_A)$; the volumes are expressed with the equation of state, $V_B = \dfrac{nRT_B}{p_e}$ and $V_A = \dfrac{nRT_A}{p_A}$, and the remaining equation has $T_B$ as its only unknown. It follows that $T_B = 280\,\mathrm{K}$, and the gas does the work $n c_V\, (T_A - T_B) \approx 1.50\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The expansion is adiabatic but irreversible, therefore the entropy is computed with the formula for the **entropy of the ideal gas** between the states $A$ and $B$; the result is $\Delta S \approx 4.1\,\mathrm{J/K} > 0$ even though $Q = 0$, in agreement with the theorem on entropy in irreversible processes. The gas is isolated, therefore this is also the entropy produced in the universe. The reversible adiabat, for which $T p^{\frac{1 - \gamma}{\gamma}}$ is constant, would bring the gas to about $230\,\mathrm{K}$ with $\Delta S = 0$; the final state of the irreversible expansion is warmer and has a larger volume, as the figure shows, because the gas does less work.

</div>
