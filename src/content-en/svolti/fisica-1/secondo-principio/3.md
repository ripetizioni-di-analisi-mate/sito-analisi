## Exercise 1

A heat of $2.0\,\mathrm{kJ}$ passes by conduction, through a metal rod, from a reservoir at $400\,\mathrm{K}$ to a reservoir at $250\,\mathrm{K}$; the state of the rod does not change. Compute the change in entropy of each reservoir and of the universe.

$$
T_1 = 400\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad T_2 = 250\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad Q = 2.0\,\mathrm{kJ}
$$
$$
\Delta S_1 = \frac{-Q}{T_1}, \qquad \Delta S_2 = \frac{Q}{T_2}, \qquad \Delta S_{\text{u}} = Q \left( \frac{1}{T_2} - \frac{1}{T_1} \right)
$$
$$
\Delta S_1 = -\frac{2000\,\mathrm{J}}{400\,\mathrm{K}} = -5.0\,\mathrm{J/K},
\qquad
\Delta S_2 = \frac{2000\,\mathrm{J}}{250\,\mathrm{K}} = 8.0\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = -5.0\,\mathrm{J/K} + 8.0\,\mathrm{J/K} = 3.0\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A reservoir exchanges heat at constant temperature, therefore its change in entropy is the heat absorbed divided by its temperature. The hot reservoir absorbs $-2.0\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and its entropy decreases; the cold reservoir absorbs $+2.0\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and its entropy increases by more, because the same heat is divided by a lower temperature. The rod returns to the same state, therefore its entropy does not change, and the universe consists of the two reservoirs. The entropy of the universe **increases** by $3.0\,\mathrm{J/K}$, in agreement with the principle of entropy increase, since conduction from a hot body to a cold one is an irreversible process.

</div>

## Exercise 2

$2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water is heated from $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $80\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Compute the change in entropy of the water, taking the specific heat $c = 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$ as constant.

$$
m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}, \qquad T_A = 293\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad T_B = 353\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
dS = \frac{\delta Q}{T} = \frac{m c\, dT}{T}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta S = \int_{T_A}^{T_B} \frac{m c\, dT}{T} = m c \ln\frac{T_B}{T_A}
$$
$$
\Delta S = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 4186\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)} \cdot \ln\frac{353}{293} \approx 8372\,\mathrm{J/K} \cdot 0.186 \approx 1.56 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Entropy is a state function, therefore its change is computed along a reversible process between the initial state and the final one, whatever the way in which the water is heated. Consider heating the water with a succession of reservoirs at increasing temperatures, each one infinitesimally hotter than the water; in each step the water absorbs $\delta Q = mc\, dT$ at the temperature $T$, and the integral of $\dfrac{dT}{T}$ is the logarithm of the ratio of the temperatures, which must be expressed in kelvins. The result is positive, since the water absorbs heat. As a check, the heat absorbed is $Q = mc\,\Delta T \approx 5.02 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}$, and divided by the average temperature $323\,\mathrm{K}$ it gives about $1.55 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J/K}$, a value close to the exact one because the temperature interval is small compared with the absolute temperature.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of ice of mass $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ melts completely in a room at $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, which behaves as a reservoir; the resulting water stays at $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. With the latent heat of fusion $\lambda_f = 3.33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, compute the change in entropy of the ice, of the room and of the universe.

$$
m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}, \qquad T_g = 273\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad T_s = 298\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
Q = m \lambda_f, \qquad \Delta S_g = \frac{m\lambda_f}{T_g}, \qquad \Delta S_s = -\frac{m\lambda_f}{T_s}
$$
$$
Q = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3.33 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J/kg} = 1.665 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta S_g = \frac{1.665 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}}{273\,\mathrm{K}} \approx 610\,\mathrm{J/K},
\qquad
\Delta S_s = -\frac{1.665 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}}{298\,\mathrm{K}} \approx -559\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} \approx 610\,\mathrm{J/K} - 559\,\mathrm{J/K} = 51\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During melting the temperature of the ice stays at $273\,\mathrm{K}$, therefore its change in entropy is the latent heat absorbed divided by this temperature. The room releases the same heat at the constant temperature of $298\,\mathrm{K}$, and its entropy decreases by a smaller amount, because the denominator is larger. The sum is positive, therefore the entropy of the universe **increases** by about $51\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Melting in a warmer room is indeed irreversible, since heat passes between bodies at different temperatures.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A copper block of mass $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and specific heat $c = 385\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$, at a temperature of $400\,\mathrm{K}$, is immersed in a lake at $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and reaches thermal equilibrium. Compute the change in entropy of the copper, of the lake and of the universe.

$$
m = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}, \qquad T_A = 400\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad T_L = 300\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{Cu}} = m c \ln\frac{T_L}{T_A}, \qquad Q_L = m c\, (T_A - T_L), \qquad \Delta S_L = \frac{Q_L}{T_L}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{Cu}} = 385\,\mathrm{J/K} \cdot \ln\frac{300}{400} \approx -111\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
Q_L = 385\,\mathrm{J/K} \cdot 100\,\mathrm{K} = 3.85 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
\Delta S_L = \frac{3.85 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{J}}{300\,\mathrm{K}} \approx 128\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} \approx -110.8\,\mathrm{J/K} + 128.3\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 17.6\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lake has a very large heat capacity and behaves as a reservoir, therefore the copper cools down to $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and the temperature of the lake does not change. The change in entropy of the copper is computed as for a body with constant specific heat, along a reversible cooling between the same states, and it is negative because the copper releases heat. The lake absorbs the heat released by the copper, $mc\,(T_A - T_L)$, at the constant temperature of $300\,\mathrm{K}$. The sum of the two changes is positive, therefore the entropy of the universe **increases** by about $17.6\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The sign is the expected one, since cooling in contact with a colder body is irreversible.

</div>
