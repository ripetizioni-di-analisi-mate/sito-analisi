## Exercise 1

A monatomic ideal gas goes through the quasi-static cycle $ABCDA$ formed by two isochoric and two isobaric processes. In the state $A$ one has $p_A = 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $V_A = 2.0\,\mathrm{L}$; $A \to B$ is an isochoric heating up to $p_B = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $B \to C$ an isobaric expansion up to $V_C = 6.0\,\mathrm{L}$, $C \to D$ an isochoric cooling down to $1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $D \to A$ an isobaric compression. Compute the work per cycle, the efficiency and the efficiency of a Carnot engine between the maximum and the minimum temperature of the cycle.

::grafico{vettori="2,1,2,3;2,3,6,3;6,3,6,1;6,1,2,1" area="3;1;2;6" punti="2,1,pieno,A;2,3,pieno,B;6,3,pieno,C;6,1,pieno,D" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.8 nomi="V,p" titolo="The cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$: the work is the area of the rectangle"}

$$
L = (p_B - p_A)(V_C - V_A)
$$
$$
Q_{AB} = n c_V\, \Delta T = \frac{3}{2}\, V_A\, (p_B - p_A),
\qquad
Q_{BC} = n c_p\, \Delta T = \frac{5}{2}\, p_B\, (V_C - V_A)
$$
$$
\eta = \frac{L}{Q_{AB} + Q_{BC}},
\qquad
\frac{T_{\min}}{T_{\max}} = \frac{T_A}{T_C} = \frac{p_A V_A}{p_C V_C}
$$
$$
L = 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 800\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
Q_{AB} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} = 600\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
Q_{BC} = \frac{5}{2} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 3000\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\eta = \frac{800\,\mathrm{J}}{3600\,\mathrm{J}} \approx 0.22,
\qquad
\eta_C = 1 - \frac{1.0 \cdot 2.0}{3.0 \cdot 6.0} = 1 - \frac{1}{9} \approx 0.89
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cycle is quasi-static, therefore the work per cycle is the area enclosed in the $(V, p)$ plane, that is, the product of the sides of the rectangle; since the cycle is traversed clockwise the work is positive. To compute the efficiency the heat absorbed is needed. In an isochoric process $\Delta T$ is obtained from the equation of state, $nR\,\Delta T = V\,\Delta p$, and for a monatomic gas $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$; in an isobaric process $nR\,\Delta T = p\,\Delta V$ and $c_p = \dfrac{5}{2}R$. The gas warms up along $A \to B$ and $B \to C$, where it absorbs heat, and cools down along $C \to D$ and $D \to A$, where it releases heat. Therefore $Q_{\text{in}} = 3600\,\mathrm{J}$ and the **efficiency** is about $0.22$. The temperature is proportional to $pV$, therefore the minimum is at $A$ and the maximum at $C$, with $T_C = 9\, T_A$. The Carnot efficiency between these temperatures is about $0.89$. The cycle absorbs heat at temperatures not higher than $T_C$ and releases it at temperatures not lower than $T_A$, therefore by the **Clausius inequality** its efficiency does not exceed the Carnot efficiency between the extreme temperatures; here it is much smaller, since the cycle exchanges heat over a whole range of temperatures and not only at the two extreme temperatures. As a check, the heat released is $1800\,\mathrm{J} + 1000\,\mathrm{J} = 2800\,\mathrm{J}$ and $3600\,\mathrm{J} - 2800\,\mathrm{J} = 800\,\mathrm{J}$ coincides with the work.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One mole of monatomic ideal gas goes through the quasi-static cycle $ABCA$: $A \to B$ is an isothermal expansion at $T_A = 600\,\mathrm{K}$ up to twice the initial volume, $B \to C$ an isobaric compression down to the initial volume, $C \to A$ an isochoric heating up to the state $A$. Compute the heats exchanged, the work per cycle and the efficiency.

$$
V_B = 2 V_A, \qquad p_B = \frac{p_A}{2}, \qquad T_C = T_A\, \frac{V_C}{V_B} = \frac{T_A}{2} = 300\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
Q_{AB} = nRT_A \ln 2,
\qquad
Q_{BC} = n c_p\, (T_C - T_A),
\qquad
Q_{CA} = n c_V\, (T_A - T_C)
$$
$$
Q_{AB} = 8.314 \cdot 600 \cdot \ln 2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3458\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
Q_{BC} = \frac{5}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot (-300)\,\mathrm{J} \approx -6236\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
Q_{CA} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 300\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3741\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
L = Q_{AB} + Q_{BC} + Q_{CA} \approx 963\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
\eta = \frac{L}{Q_{AB} + Q_{CA}} \approx \frac{963\,\mathrm{J}}{7199\,\mathrm{J}} \approx 0.134
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Along the isotherm the internal energy of the ideal gas does not change, therefore the heat absorbed equals the work of expansion $nRT_A \ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$. Along the isobaric process the volume is halved at constant pressure, and by the equation of state the temperature is also halved, going to $300\,\mathrm{K}$; the gas releases the heat $n c_p\, \Delta T$, with $c_p = \dfrac{5}{2}R$. Along the isochoric process the temperature returns to $600\,\mathrm{K}$ and the gas absorbs $n c_V\, \Delta T$, with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$. In a cycle $\Delta U = 0$, therefore the work is the sum of the three heats. The heat absorbed is the sum of the positive heats only, and the **efficiency** turns out to be about $0.134$. As a check, the work is computed directly: $L_{AB} = Q_{AB}$, $L_{BC} = p_B\,(V_A - V_B) = -nRT_C \approx -2494\,\mathrm{J}$ and $L_{CA} = 0$, and the sum again gives $963\,\mathrm{J}$. A Carnot engine between $600\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$ would have efficiency $0.50$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The ideal Otto cycle describes a gasoline engine. One mole of air, treated as a diatomic ideal gas with $\gamma = 1.4$, starts from the state $A$ at $T_A = 300\,\mathrm{K}$ and goes through: $A \to B$ reversible adiabatic compression with compression ratio $r = \dfrac{V_A}{V_B} = 8$; $B \to C$ isochoric heating up to $T_C = 1500\,\mathrm{K}$; $C \to D$ reversible adiabatic expansion up to $V_D = V_A$; $D \to A$ isochoric cooling. Compute $T_B$, $T_D$, the efficiency of the cycle and compare it with the Carnot efficiency between the extreme temperatures.

::grafico{curve="t<1?(8+(-7)*(t-0)):(t<2?(1+(0)*(t-1)):(t<3?(1+(7)*(t-2)):((8+(0)*(t-3))))),t<1?18.38/(8+(-7)*(t-0))^1.4:(t<2?(18.38+(21.62)*(t-1)):(t<3?40/(1+(7)*(t-2))^1.4:((2.176+(-1.176)*(t-3))))),0,4" vettori="5.2,1.83,3.8,2.84;1,27,1,31.4;3.8,6.17,5.2,3.98" punti="8,1,pieno;1,18.38,pieno,B;1,40,pieno,C;8,2.18,pieno" etichette="7.75,5,D;8.25,0.4,A" xmin=-0.5 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=44 nomi="V,p" titolo="The Otto cycle in the $(V, p)$ plane, with volumes in units of $V_B$ and pressures in units of $p_A$: two adiabats and two isochoric processes"}

$$
T_B = T_A\, r^{\gamma - 1}, \qquad T_D = \frac{T_C}{r^{\gamma - 1}}
$$
$$
Q_{BC} = n c_V\, (T_C - T_B), \qquad |Q_{DA}| = n c_V\, (T_D - T_A)
$$
$$
\eta = 1 - \frac{|Q_{DA}|}{Q_{BC}} = 1 - \frac{T_D - T_A}{T_C - T_B} = 1 - \frac{\dfrac{T_C}{r^{\gamma - 1}} - \dfrac{T_B}{r^{\gamma - 1}}}{T_C - T_B} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma - 1}}
$$
$$
r^{\gamma - 1} = 8^{0.4} \approx 2.297
$$
$$
T_B \approx 300\,\mathrm{K} \cdot 2.297 \approx 689\,\mathrm{K},
\qquad
T_D \approx \frac{1500\,\mathrm{K}}{2.297} \approx 653\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
\eta = 1 - \frac{1}{2.297} \approx 0.565,
\qquad
\eta_C = 1 - \frac{300\,\mathrm{K}}{1500\,\mathrm{K}} = 0.80
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Along the reversible adiabats $T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$ holds; therefore in the compression, in which the volume becomes one eighth, the temperature is multiplied by $8^{0.4}$, and in the expansion it is divided by the same factor. The gas exchanges heat only along the isochoric processes; it absorbs heat along $B \to C$ and releases it along $D \to A$, in both cases with $Q = n c_V\, \Delta T$. The ratio of the two heats depends neither on $n$ nor on $c_V$. Since $T_A = \dfrac{T_B}{r^{\gamma - 1}}$ and $T_D = \dfrac{T_C}{r^{\gamma - 1}}$, the numerator is the denominator divided by $r^{\gamma - 1}$, and the **efficiency** depends only on the compression ratio, $\eta = 1 - r^{1 - \gamma} \approx 0.565$. The Carnot efficiency between $300\,\mathrm{K}$ and $1500\,\mathrm{K}$ is $0.80$; the Otto cycle has a smaller efficiency, as follows from the Clausius inequality, since it absorbs heat at temperatures between $689\,\mathrm{K}$ and $1500\,\mathrm{K}$ and releases it between $653\,\mathrm{K}$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$. As a numerical check, $1 - \dfrac{653 - 300}{1500 - 689} \approx 1 - 0.435 = 0.565$.

</div>
