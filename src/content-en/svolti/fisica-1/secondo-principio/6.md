## Exercise 1

Two moles of monatomic ideal gas are heated from $300\,\mathrm{K}$ to $450\,\mathrm{K}$. Compute the heat absorbed and the change in entropy of the gas in the case of an isochoric heating and in the case of an isobaric heating.

$$
n = 2.0\,\mathrm{mol}, \qquad T_A = 300\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad T_B = 450\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad c_V = \frac{3}{2}R, \qquad c_p = \frac{5}{2}R
$$
$$
\Delta S_V = n c_V \ln\frac{T_B}{T_A},
\qquad
\Delta S_p = n c_p \ln\frac{T_B}{T_A}
$$
$$
Q_V = n c_V\, \Delta T = 2.0 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 150\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.74\,\mathrm{kJ},
\qquad
\Delta S_V = 2.0 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 1.5\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 10.1\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
Q_p = n c_p\, \Delta T = 2.0 \cdot \frac{5}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 150\,\mathrm{J} \approx 6.24\,\mathrm{kJ},
\qquad
\Delta S_p = 2.0 \cdot \frac{5}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 1.5\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 16.9\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the isochoric process the volume does not change, therefore in the formula for the **entropy of the ideal gas** the term $nR \ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$ is zero and what remains is $n c_V \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A}$. In the isobaric process the pressure does not change, and with the form of the formula that contains the pressures what remains is $n c_p \ln\dfrac{T_B}{T_A}$. The two processes have the same initial state and the same final temperature, but different final states, because the final volume is different. The isobaric heating requires more heat, since part of it becomes work of expansion, and it produces a larger change in entropy, in the ratio $\dfrac{c_p}{c_V} = \dfrac{5}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cylinder contains $1.5\,\mathrm{mol}$ of ideal gas in contact with a reservoir at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. The gas is compressed slowly, reversibly, from $30\,\mathrm{L}$ to $10\,\mathrm{L}$. Compute the change in entropy of the gas, the heat exchanged, the change in entropy of the reservoir and that of the universe.

$$
n = 1.5\,\mathrm{mol}, \qquad T = 300\,\mathrm{K}, \qquad V_A = 30\,\mathrm{L}, \qquad V_B = 10\,\mathrm{L}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{gas}} = nR \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A},
\qquad
Q = T\, \Delta S_{\text{gas}},
\qquad
\Delta S_{\text{res}} = -\frac{Q}{T}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{gas}} = 1.5 \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx -13.7\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
Q \approx 300\,\mathrm{K} \cdot (-13.70\,\mathrm{J/K}) \approx -4.11\,\mathrm{kJ},
\qquad
\Delta S_{\text{res}} \approx +13.7\,\mathrm{J/K},
\qquad
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The process is a reversible isothermal one, therefore the change in entropy of the gas is $nR \ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$, negative because the volume decreases. Since the temperature is constant, the heat exchanged is $T\, \Delta S_{\text{gas}}$; it is negative, that is, the gas releases $4.11\,\mathrm{kJ}$ to the reservoir, which coincide with the work received in the compression. The reservoir absorbs the same heat at the same temperature, therefore its entropy increases by the same amount by which that of the gas decreases. The entropy of the universe **does not change**, as must happen in a reversible process.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rigid, insulated container is divided by a partition into two parts: the first, of volume $5.0\,\mathrm{L}$, contains one mole of ideal gas at $300\,\mathrm{K}$; the second, of volume $15.0\,\mathrm{L}$, is empty. The partition is broken and the gas occupies the whole container. Compute the change in entropy of the gas and of the universe, and the work that the gas would have done going from the same initial state to the same final volume with a reversible isothermal expansion at $300\,\mathrm{K}$.

$$
Q = 0, \qquad L = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Delta U = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_B = T_A = 300\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
\Delta S = nR \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A},
\qquad
L_{\text{rev}} = nRT \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A} = T\, \Delta S
$$
$$
\Delta S = 1.0 \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln\frac{20.0}{5.0}\,\mathrm{J/K} = 8.314 \cdot \ln 4\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 11.5\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\text{u}} = \Delta S \approx 11.5\,\mathrm{J/K},
\qquad
L_{\text{rev}} \approx 300\,\mathrm{K} \cdot 11.53\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 3.46\,\mathrm{kJ}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The walls are rigid and insulating, therefore the gas exchanges neither work nor heat and, by the first law, its internal energy does not change. The internal energy of the ideal gas depends only on the temperature, therefore the final temperature is still $300\,\mathrm{K}$. Free expansion is irreversible and $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$ cannot be used for the entropy; since **entropy** is a state function, it is computed along a reversible isotherm between the same states, which gives $nR \ln 4 \approx 11.5\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The insulated container exchanges nothing with the outside, therefore the entropy of the universe increases by the same amount. Along the reversible isotherm the gas would have produced $3.46\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of work, which is lost in the free expansion and which equals $T\,\Delta S_{\text{u}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

$0.40\,\mathrm{mol}$ of diatomic ideal gas go from the state $A$, with $p_A = 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $V_A = 10\,\mathrm{L}$, to the state $B$, with $p_B = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $V_B = 5.0\,\mathrm{L}$. Compute the change in entropy of the gas with the general formula and verify it along the path formed by the isobaric compression $A \to C$ down to $V_B$ and the isochoric heating $C \to B$.

::grafico{vettori="10,1,5,1;5,1,5,3" segmenti="10,1,5,3" punti="10,1,pieno,A;5,3,pieno,B;5,1,pieno,C" xmin=-0.5 xmax=12 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.6 nomi="V,p" titolo="The states $A$ and $B$ in the $(V, p)$ plane, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$: the change in entropy is the same along the path $A \to C \to B$ and along every other process between $A$ and $B$"}

$$
\frac{T_B}{T_A} = \frac{p_B V_B}{p_A V_A} = \frac{3.0 \cdot 5.0}{1.0 \cdot 10} = 1.5,
\qquad
c_V = \frac{5}{2}R, \qquad c_p = \frac{7}{2}R
$$
$$
\Delta S = n c_V \ln\frac{T_B}{T_A} + nR \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta S &= 0.40 \cdot 8.314 \cdot \left( \frac{5}{2} \ln 1.5 + \ln 0.5 \right)\,\mathrm{J/K} \\[6pt]
&\approx 3.326 \cdot (1.0137 - 0.6931)\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 1.07\,\mathrm{J/K}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{AC} = n c_p \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A} = 0.40 \cdot \frac{7}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 0.5\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx -8.07\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{CB} = n c_V \ln\frac{p_B}{p_A} = 0.40 \cdot \frac{5}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot \ln 3\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 9.13\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$
$$
\Delta S_{AC} + \Delta S_{CB} \approx -8.07\,\mathrm{J/K} + 9.13\,\mathrm{J/K} \approx 1.07\,\mathrm{J/K}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the ideal gas $T$ is proportional to $pV$, therefore the ratio of the temperatures is obtained from the data without computing the individual temperatures; for completeness $T_A \approx 301\,\mathrm{K}$ and $T_B \approx 451\,\mathrm{K}$. The general formula for the **entropy of the ideal gas** gives $\Delta S \approx 1.07\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The heating makes the entropy increase and the compression makes it decrease; the first effect prevails, therefore $\Delta S > 0$. Along the isobaric process $A \to C$ the temperature is proportional to the volume, therefore $\dfrac{T_C}{T_A} = \dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$ and $n c_p$ is used; along the isochoric process $C \to B$ the temperature is proportional to the pressure, therefore $\dfrac{T_B}{T_C} = \dfrac{p_B}{p_A}$ and $n c_V$ is used. The sum of the two contributions coincides with the value of the general formula, as must happen because entropy is a **state function**.

</div>
