## Exercise 1

A wheel of radius $R = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ rotates at $120$ revolutions per minute; from a certain instant it slows down with constant angular acceleration and stops after completing $15$ revolutions. Compute the angular acceleration, the stopping time and, for a point on the rim at the instant braking begins, the tangential and centripetal accelerations.

$$
\omega_0 = 120 \cdot \frac{2\pi}{60\,\mathrm{s}} = 4\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 12.6\,\mathrm{rad/s}, \qquad \Delta\theta = 15 \cdot 2\pi\,\mathrm{rad} = 30\pi\,\mathrm{rad}
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\,\Delta\theta, \quad \omega = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha = -\frac{\omega_0^2}{2\,\Delta\theta}, \qquad t_a = \frac{0 - \omega_0}{\alpha} = \frac{2\,\Delta\theta}{\omega_0}
$$
$$
\alpha = -\frac{16\pi^2}{60\pi}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = -\frac{4\pi}{15}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = -0.838\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}, \qquad t_a = \frac{60\pi}{4\pi}\,\mathrm{s} = 15\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
a_t = \alpha R = -0.838 \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -0.251\,\mathrm{m/s^2}, \qquad a_n = \omega_0^2 R = (4\pi)^2 \cdot 0.30\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 47.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angular acceleration is constant, therefore the laws of **uniformly accelerated circular motion** hold. The data are first converted to SI units, taking into account that one revolution corresponds to $2\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$ and one minute to $60\,\mathrm{s}$. The initial and final angular velocities and the angle described are known, while the time is unknown, so the time-independent relation $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\,\Delta\theta$ is used. The angular acceleration turns out to be negative, as it must be for a rotation that slows down, and the stopping time is $15\,\mathrm{s}$; in that time the average angular velocity is $\dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$, which corresponds to one revolution per second, in agreement with the $15$ revolutions. At the beginning of braking the tangential acceleration is opposite to the velocity and has magnitude $0.251\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, while the centripetal acceleration is much larger, $47.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, because the wheel is still rotating fast.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car travels around a circular curve of radius $R = 80\,\mathrm{m}$. At the instant its speed is $v = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the car is speeding up with tangential acceleration $a_t = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Compute the centripetal acceleration, the magnitude of the acceleration and the angle that the acceleration forms with the velocity.

$$
a_n = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{80\,\mathrm{m}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_n^2} = \sqrt{2.0^2 + 5.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \sqrt{29}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 5.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\tan\beta = \frac{a_n}{a_t} = \frac{5.0}{2.0} = 2.5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \beta = 68^\circ
$$

::grafico{curve="80*cos(t),80*sin(t),0.6,1.6" vettori="40,69.28,31.34,74.28,aₜ;40,69.28,27.5,47.63,aₙ;40,69.28,18.84,52.63,a" segmenti="0,0,40,69.28;31.34,74.28,18.84,52.63;27.5,47.63,18.84,52.63" punti="40,69.28,pieno;0,0,pieno" xmin=-10 xmax=90 ymin=-10 ymax=90 uguale=1 titolo="Arc of the curve of radius $80\,\mathrm{m}$: the acceleration of the car is the sum of $a_t$, tangent in the direction of motion, and of $a_n$, directed toward the center (vectors on a different scale from distances)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity of the car changes both in magnitude and in direction, therefore the acceleration has two components, according to the theorem on **tangential and centripetal acceleration**. The centripetal component depends only on the speed and the radius at that instant and equals $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The two components are orthogonal, so the magnitude of the acceleration is obtained with the Pythagorean theorem. The angle between $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{v}$ is read off the right triangle with legs $a_t$, along the velocity, and $a_n$, perpendicular to it; it turns out that $\beta = 68^\circ$, therefore the acceleration points mostly toward the inside of the curve. If the car braked with the same $|a_t|$, the tangential component would point opposite to the velocity and the angle would be $180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A car starts from rest on a circular track of radius $R = 50\,\mathrm{m}$ and increases its speed with constant tangential acceleration $a_t = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Compute after how much time the centripetal acceleration becomes equal to the tangential acceleration, what arc the car has traveled at that instant, and what the magnitude of the acceleration is then.

$$
v(t) = a_t\,t, \qquad a_n(t) = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{a_t^2\,t^2}{R}
$$
$$
a_n(t_1) = a_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a_t^2\,t_1^2}{R} = a_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{R}{a_t}}
$$
$$
s(t_1) = \frac{1}{2}\,a_t\,t_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,a_t \cdot \frac{R}{a_t} = \frac{R}{2}, \qquad |\vec{a}(t_1)| = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_t^2} = \sqrt{2}\,a_t
$$
$$
t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{50\,\mathrm{m}}{1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 5.77\,\mathrm{s}, \qquad s = 25\,\mathrm{m}, \qquad v = 8.66\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad |\vec{a}| = 2.12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tangential acceleration is constant, therefore along the track the speed grows as in a uniformly accelerated straight-line motion, $v = a_t\,t$, and the arc traveled is $s = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a_t\,t^2$. The centripetal acceleration $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$ instead grows with the square of time, so at the start it is zero and at some instant it reaches $a_t$. Equating the two accelerations one obtains $t_1 = 5.77\,\mathrm{s}$. Substituting $t_1^2 = \dfrac{R}{a_t}$ into the arc traveled one finds $s = \dfrac{R}{2} = 25\,\mathrm{m}$, a result that does not depend on $a_t$. At that instant the two components are equal, therefore the acceleration forms an angle of $45^\circ$ with the velocity and has magnitude $\sqrt{2}\,a_t = 2.12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

</div>
