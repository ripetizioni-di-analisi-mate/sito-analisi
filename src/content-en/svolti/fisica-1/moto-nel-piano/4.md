## Exercise 1

A point moves in the plane with equation of motion $\vec{r}(t) = 3.0\,t\;\hat{\imath} + \left( 4.0\,t - 2.0\,t^2 \right)\hat{\jmath}$, with $\vec{r}$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. Determine the equation of the trajectory, the velocity and the acceleration as functions of time, the time and position at which the magnitude of the velocity is minimum, and the average velocity between $t = 0$ and $t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
x = 3t, \quad y = 4t - 2t^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = \frac{x}{3}, \qquad y = \frac{4}{3}\,x - \frac{2}{9}\,x^2
$$
$$
\vec{v}(t) = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = 3\,\hat{\imath} + (4 - 4t)\,\hat{\jmath}\ \ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad \vec{a}(t) = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt} = -4\,\hat{\jmath}\ \ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v(t) = \sqrt{9 + (4 - 4t)^2} \ \text{ minimum for } \ 4 - 4t = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v_{\min} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad \vec{r}(1) = (3.0,\ 2.0)\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_m = \frac{\vec{r}(2) - \vec{r}(0)}{2\,\mathrm{s}} = \frac{6\,\hat{\imath} + 0\,\hat{\jmath}}{2}\ \mathrm{m/s} = 3.0\,\hat{\imath}\ \ \mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{curve="3*t,4*t-2*t^2,0,2" vettori="0,0,1.2,1.6,v(0);3,2,4.2,2,v(1);6,0,7.2,-1.6,v(2)" punti="0,0,pieno;3,2,pieno;6,0,pieno" xmin=-0.8 xmax=8 ymin=-2 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="Trajectory $y = \dfrac{4}{3}\,x - \dfrac{2}{9}\,x^2$ for $0 \le t \le 2\,\mathrm{s}$, with the velocity at times $0$, $1$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ drawn to scale"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The components of the position vector are the equations of motion of the projections, $x = 3t$ and $y = 4t - 2t^2$. Solving the first for $t$ and substituting into the second eliminates time, and the **trajectory** turns out to be an arc of the parabola $y = \dfrac{4}{3}\,x - \dfrac{2}{9}\,x^2$. The velocity is obtained by differentiating component by component, and differentiating again one finds a constant acceleration, directed along $-\hat{\jmath}$; the motion is therefore of projectile type, with acceleration of magnitude $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The component $v_x = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is constant, so the magnitude of the velocity is minimum when $v_y$ vanishes, that is, at $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ at the vertex $(3, 2)$ of the parabola. Between $t = 0$ and $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the point returns to height $y = 0$, therefore the displacement is horizontal and the **average velocity** is $3.0\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point moves with equation of motion $\vec{r}(t) = 3\cos(2t)\,\hat{\imath} + 3\sin(2t)\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $\vec{r}$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. Verify that the motion is uniform circular, compute velocity and acceleration, and compare the magnitudes of the average velocity and of the average acceleration between $t = 0$ and $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{s}$ with the magnitudes of the instantaneous velocity and acceleration.

$$
|\vec{r}(t)| = \sqrt{9\cos^2(2t) + 9\sin^2(2t)} = 3\,\mathrm{m}, \qquad \theta(t) = 2t \ \Longrightarrow\ \omega = 2\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\vec{v}(t) = -6\sin(2t)\,\hat{\imath} + 6\cos(2t)\,\hat{\jmath}\ \ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad |\vec{v}| = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\vec{a}(t) = -12\cos(2t)\,\hat{\imath} - 12\sin(2t)\,\hat{\jmath} = -4\,\vec{r}(t)\ \ \mathrm{m/s^2}, \qquad |\vec{a}| = 12\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \frac{v^2}{R}
$$
$$
\vec{r}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \vec{r}(0) = (-3 - 3)\,\hat{\imath} = -6\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m}, \qquad |\vec{v}_m| = \frac{6\,\mathrm{m}}{\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{s}} = \frac{12}{\pi}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 3.82\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\vec{v}\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \vec{v}(0) = (-6 - 6)\,\hat{\jmath} = -12\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad |\vec{a}_m| = \frac{12\,\mathrm{m/s}}{\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{s}} = \frac{24}{\pi}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 7.64\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance from the origin is constant and equal to $3\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the trajectory is the circle with center $O$ and radius $3\,\mathrm{m}$; the angular position is $\theta = 2t$, so the angular velocity is constant and the motion is **uniform circular**. Differentiating with the chain rule one obtains a velocity of constant magnitude $\omega R = 6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the acceleration turns out to be $-4\,\vec{r} = -\omega^2\,\vec{r}$, directed toward the center, as stated by the theorem on **centripetal acceleration**. The interval $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$ is half a period, because $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$; in half a revolution the point goes from $(3, 0)$ to the opposite point $(-3, 0)$ and the velocity goes from $6\,\hat{\jmath}$ to $-6\,\hat{\jmath}$. The average velocity has magnitude $3.82\,\mathrm{m/s}$, less than $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, because the displacement is the diameter and not the arc of half the circle. In the same way the average acceleration has magnitude $7.64\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, less than $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The average and instantaneous quantities coincide only when the interval tends to zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point moves with equation of motion $\vec{r}(t) = 1.0\,t^2\;\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,t\;\hat{\jmath}$, with $\vec{r}$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. At time $t = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ compute velocity, acceleration, tangential and normal components of the acceleration and radius of curvature of the trajectory.

$$
\vec{v}(t) = 2t\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath}, \qquad \vec{a}(t) = 2\,\hat{\imath} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \vec{v}(1) = 2\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad \vec{a}(1) = 2\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2^2 + 2^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.83\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
a_t = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{v}}{v} = \frac{2 \cdot 2 + 0 \cdot 2}{2\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.41\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
a_n = \sqrt{|\vec{a}|^2 - a_t^2} = \sqrt{4 - 2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.41\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
\rho = \frac{v^2}{a_n} = \frac{8}{\sqrt{2}}\,\mathrm{m} = 4\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m} = 5.66\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Differentiating the position vector twice one finds the velocity, which varies with time, and a constant acceleration $2\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. At $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$ velocity and acceleration form an angle of $45^\circ$, therefore the acceleration has both a component along the velocity and a perpendicular component. For the **intrinsic components** one projects $\vec{a}$ onto the direction of $\vec{v}$; the tangential component is $\dfrac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{v}}{v}$, positive because the magnitude of the velocity is increasing, and the normal component is obtained with the Pythagorean theorem. The radius of curvature is the radius of the circle that approximates the trajectory at the point $(1, 2)$, and it equals $\rho = \dfrac{v^2}{a_n} = 5.66\,\mathrm{m}$. The trajectory, obtained by eliminating $t = \dfrac{y}{2}$, is the parabola $x = \dfrac{y^2}{4}$ with horizontal axis.

</div>
