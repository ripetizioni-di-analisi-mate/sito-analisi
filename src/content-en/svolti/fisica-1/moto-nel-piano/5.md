## Exercise 1

From the top of a tower of height $h = 30\,\mathrm{m}$ a stone is thrown with velocity $v_0 = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ inclined at $\theta = 30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Neglecting air resistance, compute after how much time and at what distance from the base of the tower the stone hits the ground, and the magnitude of the velocity at impact.

Origin on the ground at the base of the tower, $y$ axis pointing upward.

$$
v_{0x} = v_0\cos\theta = 17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad v_{0y} = v_0\sin\theta = 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
y(t) = h + v_{0y}\,t - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_f = \frac{v_{0y} + \sqrt{v_{0y}^2 + 2gh}}{g}
$$
$$
t_f = \frac{10.0 + \sqrt{100 + 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 30}}{9.81}\,\mathrm{s} = \frac{10.0 + 26.2}{9.81}\,\mathrm{s} = 3.69\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
d = v_{0x}\,t_f = 17.3\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 3.69\,\mathrm{s} = 64.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_f = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2gh} = \sqrt{400 + 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 30}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 31.4\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{curve="17.3205*t,30+10*t-4.905*t^2,0,3.6943" punti="0,30,pieno;64,0,pieno;17.66,35.10,pieno" vettori="0,30,3.46,32,v₀" linee="0,0 0,30" xmin=-8 xmax=72 ymin=-5 ymax=40 uguale=1 titolo="Launch from the $30\,\mathrm{m}$ tower: the stone rises to $35.1\,\mathrm{m}$ and hits the ground $64.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The landing point is lower than the launch point, therefore the range formulas do not apply and the landing time is obtained from the condition $y(t) = 0$, which is a quadratic equation in $t$. The product of the solutions is $-\dfrac{2h}{g} < 0$, so one solution is negative and must be discarded, and the landing time is the only positive solution, $3.69\,\mathrm{s}$. The horizontal motion is uniform, therefore the distance from the base is $v_{0x}\,t_f = 64.0\,\mathrm{m}$. For the magnitude of the velocity at impact the **speed as a function of height** applies. The stone descends $30\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the launch point, so $v_f^2 = v_0^2 + 2gh$ and $v_f = 31.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The same value is obtained from the components at impact, $v_x = 17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_y = v_{0y} - g\,t_f = -26.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball is kicked from the ground with velocity $v_0 = 18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ inclined at $45^\circ$. At $d = 30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the launch point there is a wall $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$ high. Neglecting air resistance and the size of the ball, determine whether the ball clears the wall and by what margin, and whether at that point it is rising or falling.

$$
y(x) = x\tan\theta - \frac{g}{2\,v_0^2\cos^2\theta}\,x^2
$$
$$
y(30\,\mathrm{m}) = 30\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 1 - \frac{9.81 \cdot 30^2}{2 \cdot 18^2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{m} = (30 - 27.25)\,\mathrm{m} = 2.75\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
2.75\,\mathrm{m} - 2.5\,\mathrm{m} = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t_d = \frac{d}{v_0\cos\theta} = \frac{30\,\mathrm{m}}{12.7\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 2.36\,\mathrm{s}, \qquad v_y(t_d) = v_0\sin\theta - g\,t_d = (12.7 - 23.1)\,\mathrm{m/s} = -10.4\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{curve="12.728*t,12.728*t-4.905*t^2,0,2.5949" linee="30,0 30,2.5" punti="30,2.75,pieno" etichette="25.2,0.8,wall" xmin=-4 xmax=36 ymin=-2 ymax=10 uguale=1 titolo="The ball passes over the wall, $30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the launch, at height $2.75\,\mathrm{m}$, already on the descending part of the trajectory"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal distance of the wall is known, therefore the equation of the **parabolic trajectory** directly gives the height of the ball when it passes over the wall, without first computing the time. With $\tan 45^\circ = 1$ and $\cos^2 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$ the height turns out to be $2.75\,\mathrm{m}$, greater than the height of the wall, so the ball **clears the wall** with a margin of $25\,\mathrm{cm}$. To know whether the ball is rising or falling one needs the sign of $v_y$ at that instant. The instant is obtained from the uniform horizontal motion and equals $2.36\,\mathrm{s}$; at that instant $v_y < 0$, therefore the ball is falling. The same result follows from the symmetry of the trajectory, since the range is $\dfrac{v_0^2}{g} = 33.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the highest point is halfway, at $16.5\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, before the wall.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A basketball player shoots from a height of $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$ at an angle of $45^\circ$ toward the basket, which is at a height of $3.05\,\mathrm{m}$ and at a horizontal distance of $6.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Neglecting air resistance and the size of the ball, compute the magnitude of the velocity with which the ball must leave the hand and the time it takes to reach the basket.

Origin at the launch point: the basket has coordinates $x = 6.0\,\mathrm{m}$, $\Delta y = 1.05\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\Delta y = x\tan\theta - \frac{g\,x^2}{2\,v_0^2\cos^2\theta} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_0^2 = \frac{g\,x^2}{2\cos^2\theta\,\left( x\tan\theta - \Delta y \right)}
$$
$$
v_0^2 = \frac{9.81 \cdot 6.0^2}{2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot (6.0 - 1.05)}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = \frac{353.2}{4.95}\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} = 71.3\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}, \qquad v_0 = 8.45\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
t = \frac{x}{v_0\cos\theta} = \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{m}}{5.97\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 1.00\,\mathrm{s}, \qquad v_y = v_0\sin\theta - g\,t = -3.88\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The target has known coordinates relative to the launch point, therefore one requires the **parabolic trajectory** to pass through the point $(6.0,\ 1.05)\,\mathrm{m}$. The only unknown is $v_0$, which appears only in the coefficient of $x^2$, so the equation is solved directly for $v_0^2$. The denominator $x\tan\theta - \Delta y$ is positive, therefore the solution exists, and the ball must leave at $8.45\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The time is obtained from the uniform horizontal motion and is about $1.00\,\mathrm{s}$. The vertical component of the velocity at that instant is negative, therefore the ball reaches the basket **from above**, on the descending part of the trajectory, as is needed to go into the hoop.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A target hangs at a height $H = 10\,\mathrm{m}$, at horizontal distance $d = 20\,\mathrm{m}$ from the launch point, which is on the ground. A projectile is launched with $v_0 = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ aimed directly at the target, and at the same instant the target comes loose and falls from rest. Neglecting air resistance, show that the projectile hits the target and compute the height of the impact.

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{H}{d} = \frac{10}{20} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \theta = 26.6^\circ
$$
$$
\text{projectile: } y_p(t) = v_0\sin\theta\;t - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2, \qquad \text{target: } y_b(t) = H - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2
$$
$$
x_p(t^*) = d \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t^* = \frac{d}{v_0\cos\theta}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y_p(t^*) &= v_0\sin\theta\,\frac{d}{v_0\cos\theta} - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^{*2} \\[6pt]
&= d\tan\theta - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^{*2} = H - \frac{1}{2}\,g\,t^{*2} = y_b(t^*)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
t^* = \frac{\sqrt{d^2 + H^2}}{v_0} = \frac{22.4\,\mathrm{m}}{25\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 0.894\,\mathrm{s}, \qquad y^* = 10\,\mathrm{m} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.894^2\,\mathrm{m} = 6.08\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
t_{\text{fall}} = \sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}} = 1.43\,\mathrm{s} > t^*
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The projectile reaches the vertical through the target when $x_p = d$, that is, at time $t^* = \dfrac{d}{v_0\cos\theta}$, and at that instant the two heights are compared. Since the launch is aimed at the target, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{H}{d}$, therefore $v_0\sin\theta\,t^* = d\tan\theta = H$. Both bodies have the same acceleration $-g\,\hat{\jmath}$, so in the same time both drop by $\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^{*2}$ relative to the motion they would have without gravity, and the two heights coincide for any $v_0$. For the impact to occur in flight, the meeting must precede the target's arrival at the ground, that is, $t^* < \sqrt{\dfrac{2H}{g}}$ must hold; with the data the condition is satisfied, since $0.894\,\mathrm{s} < 1.43\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore the projectile **hits the target**. The impact occurs at a height of $6.08\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
