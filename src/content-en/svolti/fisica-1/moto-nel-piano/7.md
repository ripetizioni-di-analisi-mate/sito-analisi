## Exercise 1

Rain falls vertically with velocity $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the ground. A car travels on a horizontal road at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute the magnitude of the velocity of the rain relative to the car and the angle that the drops form with the vertical, as seen by a passenger.

$S$: ground; $S'$: car, with $\vec{V} = 15\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$; $y$ axis pointing upward.

$$
\vec{v} = \vec{v}\,' + \vec{V} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \vec{v}\,' = \vec{v} - \vec{V}
$$
$$
\vec{v}\,' = -8.0\,\hat{\jmath} - 15\,\hat{\imath} = (-15,\ -8.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad v' = \sqrt{15^2 + 8.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 17\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\tan\varphi = \frac{|v'_x|}{|v'_y|} = \frac{15}{8.0} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \varphi = 62^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity of the rain is known in the frame of the ground, and it is sought in the frame of the car, which is in uniform translational motion relative to the ground. From the **velocity addition** $\vec{v} = \vec{v}\,' + \vec{V}$ one obtains $\vec{v}\,' = \vec{v} - \vec{V}$, therefore relative to the car a horizontal component of $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed backward is added to the vertical velocity of the drops. The two contributions are orthogonal, so the magnitude is $17\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by the Pythagorean theorem. The drops appear inclined at $62^\circ$ to the vertical, and in the frame of the car they move from the front toward the back.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A river has parallel banks $d = 120\,\mathrm{m}$ apart and the current has uniform velocity $V = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A boat moves relative to the water with velocity of magnitude $v' = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
1. The boat heads perpendicular to the banks: compute the crossing time, the downstream displacement and the magnitude of the velocity relative to the bank.
2. Compute in which direction the boat must head to arrive exactly opposite the starting point, and the crossing time in this case.

$S$: bank, $x$ axis along the current, $y$ axis perpendicular to the banks; $S'$: water, $\vec{V} = V\,\hat{\imath}$.

$$
\text{case 1: } \vec{v}\,' = v'\,\hat{\jmath}, \qquad \vec{v} = V\,\hat{\imath} + v'\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
t_1 = \frac{d}{v'} = \frac{120\,\mathrm{m}}{4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 30\,\mathrm{s}, \qquad x_1 = V\,t_1 = 45\,\mathrm{m}, \qquad v = \sqrt{V^2 + v'^2} = 4.27\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\text{case 2: } \vec{v}\,' = -v'\sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + v'\cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath}, \qquad v_x = V - v'\sin\varphi = 0
$$
$$
\sin\varphi = \frac{V}{v'} = \frac{1.5}{4.0} = 0.375 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \varphi = 22.0^\circ
$$
$$
v = v'\cos\varphi = \sqrt{v'^2 - V^2} = 3.71\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad t_2 = \frac{d}{v} = \frac{120\,\mathrm{m}}{3.71\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 32.4\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,-1.5,3.708,v';-1.5,3.708,0,3.708,V;0,0,0,3.708,v" etichette="-0.42,1.55,φ" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2 ymin=-0.6 ymax=4.6 uguale=1 titolo="Case 2, velocities in $\mathrm{m/s}$: the boat heads upstream by $\varphi = 22.0^\circ$, so that $\vec{v}\,' + \vec{V}$ is perpendicular to the banks"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the frame of the bank the velocity of the boat is $\vec{v} = \vec{v}\,' + \vec{V}$, by **velocity addition**. In the first case only $\vec{v}\,'$ has a component perpendicular to the banks, therefore the crossing time is $\dfrac{d}{v'} = 30\,\mathrm{s}$, as if there were no current; in the same time the current carries the boat $45\,\mathrm{m}$ downstream. In the second case the boat must have zero component along the current relative to the bank, so the component of $\vec{v}\,'$ along the current must compensate $V$. One obtains $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{V}{v'}$, which has a solution because $V < v'$, and the boat heads upstream at $22.0^\circ$ from the perpendicular. The velocity relative to the bank is then less than $v'$, therefore the crossing takes longer, $32.4\,\mathrm{s}$ against $30\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A boy on a train moving at $V = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a straight track throws a ball vertically upward with velocity $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the train, and catches it at the same height. Neglecting air resistance, describe the motion of the ball in the frame of the train and in the frame of the ground, and compute the time of flight, the maximum height and the distance traveled by the ball relative to the ground during the flight.

$S'$: train; $S$: ground, with $\vec{V} = 15\,\hat{\imath}\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\vec{v}\,'_0 = 6.0\,\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{m/s}, \qquad \vec{v}_0 = \vec{v}\,'_0 + \vec{V} = (15,\ 6.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad \vec{a} = \vec{a}\,' = -g\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\,v_{0y}}{g} = \frac{2 \cdot 6.0}{9.81}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.22\,\mathrm{s}, \qquad H = \frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g} = \frac{36}{19.62}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.83\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
R = v_{0x}\,T = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 1.22\,\mathrm{s} = 18.3\,\mathrm{m}, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{6.0}{15} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \theta = 21.8^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the frame of the train the ball starts with vertical velocity and has acceleration $-g\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore it rises and falls back along the same vertical, like a vertical throw from rest. By **velocity addition**, in the frame of the ground the initial velocity is $(15,\ 6.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and by the **invariance of acceleration** the acceleration is still $-g\,\hat{\jmath}$. So relative to the ground the motion is that of a projectile launched at $21.8^\circ$ above the horizontal, with a parabolic trajectory. The time of flight and the maximum height depend only on the vertical component of the velocity, which is the same in the two frames, and they equal $1.22\,\mathrm{s}$ and $1.83\,\mathrm{m}$ for both observers. During the flight the ball moves forward relative to the ground by $18.3\,\mathrm{m}$, exactly as much as the train, and for this reason it falls back into the boy's hand.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Two cars approach an intersection between two perpendicular roads. Car $A$ travels east at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and at time $t = 0$ is $100\,\mathrm{m}$ west of the intersection; car $B$ travels north at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and at time $t = 0$ is $150\,\mathrm{m}$ south of the intersection. The velocities are constant. Compute the velocity of $B$ relative to $A$, the time at which the distance between the two cars is minimum and the value of this distance.

Origin at the intersection, $x$ axis pointing east, $y$ axis pointing north.

$$
\vec{r}_A(t) = (-100 + 20\,t)\,\hat{\imath}, \qquad \vec{r}_B(t) = (-150 + 15\,t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{u} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A = (-20,\ 15)\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad |\vec{u}| = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\vec{s}(t) = \vec{r}_B - \vec{r}_A = \vec{s}_0 + \vec{u}\,t, \qquad \vec{s}_0 = (100,\ -150)\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
D^2(t) = |\vec{s}_0 + \vec{u}\,t|^2 = 625\,t^2 - 8500\,t + 32500 \ \ (\mathrm{m^2})
$$
$$
\frac{d(D^2)}{dt} = 1250\,t - 8500 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t^* = 6.8\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\vec{s}(t^*) = (100 - 136,\ -150 + 102)\,\mathrm{m} = (-36,\ -48)\,\mathrm{m}, \qquad D_{\min} = \sqrt{36^2 + 48^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 60\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vector $\vec{s} = \vec{r}_B - \vec{r}_A$ is the position of $B$ in the frame attached to $A$, which translates with constant velocity; by the **Galilean transformations**, in that frame $B$ moves in uniform straight-line motion with the **relative velocity** $\vec{u} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A$, of magnitude $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The distance between the cars is the magnitude of $\vec{s}$, and it is minimum when its square is minimum, which is a second-degree polynomial in $t$ with positive leading coefficient. Setting the derivative to zero one finds $t^* = 6.8\,\mathrm{s}$, and at that instant the two cars are $60\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. In $6.8\,\mathrm{s}$ car $A$ has passed the intersection by $36\,\mathrm{m}$ and $B$ is still $48\,\mathrm{m}$ to the south, therefore the two cars **do not collide**. At the instant of minimum distance $\vec{s}$ is orthogonal to $\vec{u}$, and indeed $(-36)(-20) + (-48)(15) = 0$.

</div>
