## Exercise 1

A very wide open tank has a hole of area $1.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ in its side wall, $2.45\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface of the water. Compute the exit speed of the water, the flow rate through the hole and the volume of water that flows out in one minute, assuming that the level stays practically constant.

$$
v = \sqrt{2 g h} ,
\qquad
Q = a v ,
\qquad
V = Q\, \Delta t
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.45}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{48.07}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.93\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
Q = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 6.93\,\mathrm{m^3/s} = 6.9 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s} ,
\qquad
V = 6.93 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 60\,\mathrm{m^3} = 4.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tank is open and very wide compared with the hole, so the speed at which the free surface descends is negligible; both the free surface and the jet are in contact with air at pressure $p_0$. The hypotheses of **Torricelli's theorem** are satisfied, and the exit speed is that of a body in free fall from a height of $2.45\,\mathrm{m}$.

The flow rate is the product of the area of the hole and the speed, with the area expressed in square meters, $1.0\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. With a constant level the flow rate does not change, therefore in $60\,\mathrm{s}$ about $42$ liters flow out.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An open tank, resting on the ground, contains water up to the height $H = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}$. In the vertical wall there is a small hole at $y = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the ground. Neglecting air resistance, compute the exit speed, the time the jet takes to reach the ground and the distance from the wall at which it hits the ground. Then determine at what height the hole should be made to maximize this distance.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="1.5+t,0.5-t^2/6,0,1.7321" linee="0.5,2.6 0.5,0 1.5,0 1.5,0.4;1.5,0.6 1.5,2.6;1.5,0 3.6,0" poligoni="0.5,0 1.5,0 1.5,2 0.5,2" vettori="1.5,0.5,2.1,0.5,v" punti="3.232,0,pieno" segmenti="1.25,0.5,1.25,2" etichette="1.02,1.2,h;2.95,0.2,d" xmin=0 xmax=3.8 ymin=-0.2 ymax=2.7 titolo="The jet leaves horizontally at $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the ground and hits the ground at $d = 2\sqrt{h y} \approx 1.73\,\mathrm{m}$ from the wall"}

$$
v = \sqrt{2 g h} ,
\qquad
h = H - y ,
\qquad
y = \frac{1}{2} g t^2 ,
\qquad
d = v t
$$
$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2y}{g}} ,
\qquad
d = \sqrt{2 g h} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2y}{g}} = 2\sqrt{h y} = 2\sqrt{(H - y)\, y}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.50}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.42\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
t = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.50}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 0.319\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
d = 2\sqrt{1.50 \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{m} = 2\sqrt{0.75}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.73\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{d}{dy}\Big[ (H - y)\, y \Big] = H - 2y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = \frac{H}{2} = 1.00\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
d_{\max} = 2\sqrt{\frac{H}{2} \cdot \frac{H}{2}} = H = 2.00\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hole is at depth $h = H - y = 1.50\,\mathrm{m}$ below the free surface, and by **Torricelli's theorem** the water leaves horizontally with speed $\sqrt{2gh}$. Outside the tank each drop moves like a projectile launched horizontally from height $y$: the horizontal motion is uniform with speed $v$, the vertical motion is a free fall starting from rest. The time of fall depends only on $y$, and the distance $d$ is the product of the horizontal speed and the time.

In the product $g$ cancels and what remains is $d = 2\sqrt{hy}$, which does not depend on $g$. To maximize $d$ it suffices to maximize the radicand $(H - y)\,y$, a downward-opening parabola with vertex at $y = \dfrac{H}{2}$. The hole at half height gives the maximum distance, which is **equal to the height of the water**, $2.00\,\mathrm{m}$.

As a check, $v t = 5.42 \cdot 0.319\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.73\,\mathrm{m}$, the same value as the formula $2\sqrt{hy}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A closed tank contains water, and above the water there is compressed air at absolute pressure $p = 1.50 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. A small hole is $1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ below the surface of the water and the jet flows out into the atmosphere, at pressure $p_0 = 1.013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The tank is much wider than the hole. Compute the exit speed and compare it with the one that would be obtained with the tank open.

$$
p + \rho g h = p_0 + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(p - p_0)}{\rho} + 2 g h}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.487 \cdot 10^5}{1000} + 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.00}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{97.4 + 19.6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10.8\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_0 = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{19.6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.43\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Torricelli's theorem assumes that the free surface is at the same pressure as the jet; here the pressure above the water is greater than $p_0$, so we go back to **Bernoulli's theorem**. It is applied between a point of the surface of the water, at pressure $p$, height $h$ relative to the hole and negligible speed because the tank is very wide, and a point of the jet, at pressure $p_0$, height zero and speed $v$.

Compared with the case of the open tank, the term $\dfrac{2\,(p - p_0)}{\rho}$ appears, due to the overpressure of the air, which here is about five times the term $2gh$. The exit speed is therefore **more than twice** the one obtained with the tank open. For $p = p_0$ the formula reduces to $v = \sqrt{2gh}$, as it must.

</div>

## Exercise 4

An open vertical cylindrical tank has cross section $A = 0.80\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and contains water up to the height $H = 1.80\,\mathrm{m}$. A hole of area $a = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ opens in the bottom. Assuming that at every instant the exit speed is given by Torricelli's theorem with the height $h(t)$ of the water at that instant, compute the time needed to empty the tank and the time the level takes to drop to $\dfrac{H}{4}$.

$$
-A\, \frac{dh}{dt} = a\sqrt{2 g h}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{dh}{\sqrt{h}} = -\frac{a}{A}\sqrt{2g}\, dt
$$
$$
\int_H^{h(t)} \frac{dh}{\sqrt{h}} = -\frac{a}{A}\sqrt{2g}\, t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2\sqrt{h(t)} - 2\sqrt{H} = -\frac{a}{A}\sqrt{2g}\, t
$$
$$
\sqrt{h(t)} = \sqrt{H} - \frac{a}{A}\sqrt{\frac{g}{2}}\; t ,
\qquad
h(T) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T = \frac{A}{a}\sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}
$$
$$
T = \frac{0.80}{4.0 \cdot 10^{-4}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.80}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 2000 \cdot 0.606\,\mathrm{s} = 1.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\sqrt{h(t_1)} = \frac{\sqrt{H}}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_1 = \frac{T}{2} = 6.1 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hole is much smaller than the cross section of the tank, $\dfrac{a}{A} = 5 \cdot 10^{-4}$, so the level drops slowly and at every instant **Torricelli's theorem** can be used with the height at that instant. By the **continuity equation** the volume that flows out of the hole in the time $dt$, $a v\, dt$, equals the volume by which the water in the tank goes down, $-A\, dh$; the minus sign appears because $dh$ is negative. We obtain a separable differential equation, for which we refer to the topic on differential equations in Analysis 1.

Separating the variables and integrating from $0$ to $t$, with $h(0) = H$, we obtain $\sqrt{h}$ as a decreasing linear function of time. The tank is empty when $\sqrt{h} = 0$, that is, after about $1.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{s}$, **about twenty minutes**. The level $\dfrac{H}{4}$ corresponds to $\sqrt{h} = \dfrac{\sqrt{H}}{2}$, which is reached at half the total time. The level therefore drops quickly at the beginning and more and more slowly, because the exit speed decreases with $h$.

As a dimensional check, $\dfrac{A}{a}$ is a pure number and $\sqrt{\dfrac{2H}{g}}$ has the dimensions of $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = \mathrm{s}$.

</div>
