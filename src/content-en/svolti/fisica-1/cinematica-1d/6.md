## Exercise 1

A point moves along the $x$-axis with acceleration $a(t) = 6t - 4$, with $a$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $t$ in seconds. At the instant $t = 0$ it is at the origin with velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the velocity and the equation of motion, establish whether the point ever reverses its motion, and compute the position, the velocity and the distance traveled at the instant $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
v(t) = v_0 + \int_0^t a(s)\,ds = 2 + \int_0^t (6s - 4)\,ds = 3t^2 - 4t + 2
$$
$$
x(t) = x_0 + \int_0^t v(s)\,ds = \int_0^t (3s^2 - 4s + 2)\,ds = t^3 - 2t^2 + 2t
$$
$$
\Delta = (-4)^2 - 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = -8 < 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v(t) = 3t^2 - 4t + 2 > 0 \ \text{ for every } t
$$
$$
x(3) = 27 - 18 + 6 = 15\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
v(3) = 27 - 12 + 2 = 17\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
d = x(3) - x(0) = 15\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is known as a function of time, so the **equation of motion from the acceleration** applies, that is, $a$ is integrated between $0$ and $t$ and the initial velocity is added, then $v$ is integrated and the initial position is added. The point reverses its motion only where the velocity changes sign. The quadratic $3t^2 - 4t + 2$ has negative discriminant and positive leading coefficient, therefore it is positive for every $t$, and the point **never reverses** its motion. Consequently the distance traveled between $0$ and $3\,\mathrm{s}$ coincides with the displacement, $15\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, differentiating $x(t)$ twice gives back $a(t) = 6t - 4$, and $x(0) = 0$, $v(0) = 2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart starts from rest at the origin with acceleration $a(t) = 4 - 2t$, with $a$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $t$ in seconds. Determine the maximum velocity and the position at which it is reached, the next instant at which the cart stops and the position at that instant.

$$
v(t) = \int_0^t (4 - 2s)\,ds = 4t - t^2,
\qquad
x(t) = \int_0^t (4s - s^2)\,ds = 2t^2 - \frac{t^3}{3}
$$
$$
\frac{dv}{dt} = a(t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 2\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
v_{\max} = v(2) = 8 - 4 = 4\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x(2) = 8 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{16}{3}\,\mathrm{m} = 5.33\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v(t) = t\,(4 - t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 0 \ \text{ or } \ t = 4\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
x(4) = 32 - \frac{64}{3} = \frac{32}{3}\,\mathrm{m} = 10.7\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial conditions are $x_0 = 0$ and $v_0 = 0$, so the velocity and the equation of motion are the integrals between $0$ and $t$ of the acceleration and of the velocity. The velocity is maximum where its derivative, namely the acceleration, vanishes going from positive to negative, and this happens at $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$; the maximum velocity is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, reached at $x = 5.33\,\mathrm{m}$. After that instant the acceleration is negative and the cart slows down, until the velocity vanishes again at $t = 4\,\mathrm{s}$ at $x = 10.7\,\mathrm{m}$. The velocity is positive for $0 < t < 4\,\mathrm{s}$, so in that interval the cart always moves forward and the position $x(4)$ is also the distance traveled. As a check, the graph of $v(t)$ is a parabola symmetric with respect to the line $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$, so the areas under the graph over the intervals $[0, 2]$ and $[2, 4]$ are equal, and indeed $x(4) = 2\,x(2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A car starts from rest with an acceleration that decreases linearly in time, $a(t) = a_0 \left( 1 - \dfrac{t}{T} \right)$ for $0 \leq t \leq T$, with $a_0 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $T = 10\,\mathrm{s}$. Compute the velocity and the distance traveled at the instant $T$, and compare the distance with that of a uniformly accelerated motion that reaches the same velocity in the same time.

$$
v(t) = \int_0^t a_0 \left( 1 - \frac{s}{T} \right) ds = a_0 \left( t - \frac{t^2}{2T} \right)
$$
$$
x(t) = \int_0^t a_0 \left( s - \frac{s^2}{2T} \right) ds = a_0 \left( \frac{t^2}{2} - \frac{t^3}{6T} \right)
$$
$$
v(T) = \frac{a_0\,T}{2} = \frac{3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 10\,\mathrm{s}}{2} = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x(T) = \frac{a_0\,T^2}{3} = \frac{3.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (10\,\mathrm{s})^2}{3} = 100\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
x_{\mathrm{UAM}} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{v(T)}{T} \cdot T^2 = \frac{a_0\,T^2}{4} = 75\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The car starts from rest at the origin, so velocity and position are obtained by integrating twice starting from zero. At the instant $T$ the terms combine into $\dfrac{a_0\,T}{2}$ for the velocity and into $a_0\,T^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{6} \right) = \dfrac{a_0\,T^2}{3}$ for the position, therefore the car reaches $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after traveling $100\,\mathrm{m}$. The final velocity is also the area of the triangle under the graph of $a(t)$, which has base $T$ and height $a_0$. A uniformly accelerated motion that reaches $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $10\,\mathrm{s}$ has acceleration $\dfrac{a_0}{2}$ and travels only $75\,\mathrm{m}$. The difference depends on the shape of $a(t)$. The actual acceleration is larger at the beginning, so the velocity increases earlier and the graph of $v(t)$ lies **above the line** of the uniformly accelerated motion; therefore the area under the graph of $v(t)$, that is, the distance traveled, is larger.

</div>
