## Exercise 1

A point moves along the $x$-axis with equation of motion $x(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 5$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. Determine the velocity and the acceleration, identify the type of motion, find the instant and the position of the reversal of motion, and compute, in the interval $0 \leq t \leq 4\,\mathrm{s}$, the displacement, the distance traveled and the average velocity.

$$
v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = 6t - 12,
\qquad
a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt} = 6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
x(t) = x_0 + v_0\,t + \frac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2
\quad\text{with}\quad
x_0 = 5\,\mathrm{m},\ \ v_0 = -12\,\mathrm{m/s},\ \ a = 6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v(t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 2\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
x(2) = 12 - 24 + 5 = -7\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
x(0) = 5\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x(4) = 48 - 48 + 5 = 5\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta x = 0,
\qquad
v_m = 0
$$
$$
d = |x(2) - x(0)| + |x(4) - x(2)| = 12\,\mathrm{m} + 12\,\mathrm{m} = 24\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is the derivative of the equation of motion and the acceleration is the derivative of the velocity. The acceleration is constant, so the motion is **uniformly accelerated**, and comparing the law with $x_0 + v_0\,t + \dfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$ one reads $x_0 = 5\,\mathrm{m}$, $v_0 = -12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $a = 6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. For $0 \leq t < 2\,\mathrm{s}$ the velocity is negative and the acceleration positive, therefore the point moves in the negative orientation while slowing down; at the instant $2\,\mathrm{s}$ it stops at $x = -7\,\mathrm{m}$ and then starts again in the positive orientation, faster and faster. At the instant $4\,\mathrm{s}$ the point has returned to the initial position, so the displacement and the average velocity are zero. The point has, however, reversed its motion, therefore the distance traveled is computed separately on the two intervals in which there is no reversal, and it equals $24\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point moves along the $x$-axis with equation of motion $x(t) = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t + 1$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds, for $t \geq 0$. Determine the instants at which the point is at rest, the intervals in which the motion is accelerated and those in which it is decelerated, and, in the interval $0 \leq t \leq 3\,\mathrm{s}$, the average velocity and the distance traveled.

$$
v(t) = 6t^2 - 18t + 12 = 6\,(t - 1)(t - 2),
\qquad
a(t) = 12t - 18 = 6\,(2t - 3)
$$
$$
v(t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 1\,\mathrm{s} \ \text{ or } \ t = 2\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
a(t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}
$$

| interval | sign of $v$ | sign of $a$ | motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 < t < 1$ | $+$ | $-$ | decelerated |
| $1 < t < 1.5$ | $-$ | $-$ | accelerated |
| $1.5 < t < 2$ | $-$ | $+$ | decelerated |
| $t > 2$ | $+$ | $+$ | accelerated |

$$
x(0) = 1\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x(1) = 6\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x(2) = 5\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x(3) = 10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_m = \frac{x(3) - x(0)}{3\,\mathrm{s}} = \frac{9\,\mathrm{m}}{3\,\mathrm{s}} = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
d = |6 - 1| + |5 - 6| + |10 - 5| = 5\,\mathrm{m} + 1\,\mathrm{m} + 5\,\mathrm{m} = 11\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x^3-9*x^2+12*x+1+0*sqrt(x*(3.2-x))" punti="0,1,pieno;1,6,pieno;2,5,pieno;3,10,pieno" segmenti="1,0,1,6;2,0,2,5" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=14 nomi="t,x" titolo="Position–time graph of $x(t) = 2t^3 - 9t^2 + 12t + 1$: the point advances to $x = 6\,\mathrm{m}$, moves back to $x = 5\,\mathrm{m}$ between $1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$, then advances again"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Differentiating the equation of motion gives a velocity of degree two, which vanishes at the instants $1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$; at these instants the point is at rest and, since $v$ changes sign, it **reverses its motion**. The acceleration is the derivative of $v$ and changes sign at $t = 1.5\,\mathrm{s}$. To establish whether the motion is accelerated or decelerated, the signs of $v$ and $a$ are compared. The magnitude of the velocity increases where the signs coincide and decreases where they are opposite, as the table summarizes.

The average velocity depends only on the extreme positions and equals $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The distance traveled, instead, requires the positions at the instants of reversal, because between $1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ the point moves back by $1\,\mathrm{m}$; adding the stretches traveled without reversal gives $11\,\mathrm{m}$, more than the $9\,\mathrm{m}$ of displacement.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point moves along the $x$-axis with equation of motion $x(t) = 2t + 8\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}}$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds, for $t \geq 0$. Determine the initial velocity and acceleration, the instant and the position at which the point reverses its motion, and the velocity the motion tends to at long times.

$$
v(t) = 2 - 4\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}},
\qquad
a(t) = 2\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}}
$$
$$
v(0) = 2 - 4 = -2\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
a(0) = 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v(t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e^{-\frac{t}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t^* = 2\ln 2 = 1.39\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x(t^*) = 4\ln 2 + 8 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 4\ln 2 + 4 = 6.77\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\lim_{t \to +\infty} v(t) = 2\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\lim_{t \to +\infty} a(t) = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is obtained by differentiating the equation of motion with the chain rule, which for the exponential term gives $8 \cdot \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) e^{-\frac{t}{2}} = -4\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}}$; differentiating again gives the acceleration, positive for every $t$. At the initial instant the point moves in the negative orientation and the acceleration is positive, so the motion is decelerated. The velocity vanishes when $e^{-\frac{t}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, at $t^* = 2\ln 2 = 1.39\,\mathrm{s}$; before that instant $v < 0$, after it $v > 0$, therefore the point **reverses its motion** and $x(t^*) = 6.77\,\mathrm{m}$ is the minimum position reached. At long times the exponential term tends to zero, so the velocity tends to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the motion tends to become uniform.

</div>
