## Exercise 1

A subway train starts from rest at a station and accelerates uniformly at $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $8.0\,\mathrm{s}$; then it proceeds at constant velocity for $24\,\mathrm{s}$, and finally it brakes uniformly until it stops at the next station in $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$. Compute the maximum velocity, the deceleration, the distance between the two stations and the average velocity.

The axis is oriented in the direction of motion; the data are $a_1 = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $t_1 = 8.0\,\mathrm{s}$, $t_2 = 24\,\mathrm{s}$, $t_3 = 6.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
v_{\max} = a_1\,t_1 = 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{s} = 12\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
a_3 = -\frac{v_{\max}}{t_3} = -2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
d = \frac{1}{2}\,v_{\max}\,t_1 + v_{\max}\,t_2 + \frac{1}{2}\,v_{\max}\,t_3 = v_{\max}\left( \frac{t_1 + t_3}{2} + t_2 \right)
$$
$$
d = 12\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot (7.0\,\mathrm{s} + 24\,\mathrm{s}) = 372\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_m = \frac{d}{t_1 + t_2 + t_3} = \frac{372\,\mathrm{m}}{38\,\mathrm{s}} = 9.8\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="min(1.5*x,12,2*(38-x))+0*sqrt(x*(38-x))" area="min(1.5*x,12,2*(38-x));0;0;38" xmin=-5 xmax=45 ymin=-2 ymax=14 nomi="t,v" titolo="Velocity of the train in $\mathrm{m/s}$: the area of the trapezoid, $48 + 288 + 36 = 372$, is the distance between the stations in meters"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first phase the velocity increases uniformly starting from zero, so at the end it equals $a_1\,t_1 = 12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is the velocity of the second phase. In the third phase the velocity goes from $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to zero in $6.0\,\mathrm{s}$, therefore the acceleration is $-2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The velocity graph consists of three segments and bounds a trapezoid with the time axis. Since **the displacement is the area under the velocity graph**, the distance between the stations is the sum of the areas of two triangles and a rectangle, $48\,\mathrm{m} + 288\,\mathrm{m} + 36\,\mathrm{m} = 372\,\mathrm{m}$. The average velocity is the distance divided by the total time of $38\,\mathrm{s}$, and it turns out to be $9.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, less than the maximum velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point moves along the $x$-axis with velocity $v(t) = 6 - t$, with $v$ in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and $t$ in seconds. In the interval $0 \leq t \leq 10\,\mathrm{s}$ determine the instant at which the point reverses its motion, the displacement, the distance traveled, the average velocity and the average speed.

The graph of $v(t)$ is a straight line that crosses the time axis at $t = 6\,\mathrm{s}$; the areas are computed separately on the two intervals in which $v$ has constant sign.

$$
v(t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 6\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
A_+ = \int_0^6 (6 - t)\,dt = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot 6 = 18\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
A_- = \int_6^{10} (t - 6)\,dt = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 4 = 8\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x = A_+ - A_- = 10\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
d = A_+ + A_- = 26\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v_m = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{10\,\mathrm{m}}{10\,\mathrm{s}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\frac{d}{\Delta t} = \frac{26\,\mathrm{m}}{10\,\mathrm{s}} = 2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="6-x+0*sqrt(x*(10-x))" area="6-x;0;0;6|0;6-x;6;10" xmin=-2 xmax=12 ymin=-6 ymax=8 nomi="t,v" titolo="The triangle above the axis has area $18$ and the one below has area $8$: the displacement is $18 - 8 = 10\,\mathrm{m}$, the distance traveled $18 + 8 = 26\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The point reverses its motion when the velocity changes sign, that is, at the instant $t = 6\,\mathrm{s}$. Before that instant $v > 0$ and the point moves forward, after it $v < 0$ and the point moves back. By the theorem on the **displacement as the area** under the velocity graph, the displacement is the signed area, so the triangle below the axis is subtracted; the distance traveled, instead, is the sum of the two areas taken with positive sign. The displacement is $10\,\mathrm{m}$ and the distance traveled $26\,\mathrm{m}$. Consequently the average velocity is $1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, whereas the average speed is $2.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. As a check, the equation of motion with $x(0) = 0$ is $x(t) = 6t - \dfrac{t^2}{2}$, and $x(10) = 60 - 50 = 10\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point moves along the $x$-axis and at the instant $t = 0$ it is at $x_0 = 5\,\mathrm{m}$. The graph of its velocity consists of three segments: from $(0, 0)$ to $(4\,\mathrm{s}, 8\,\mathrm{m/s})$, then horizontal up to $6\,\mathrm{s}$, then from $(6\,\mathrm{s}, 8\,\mathrm{m/s})$ to $(10\,\mathrm{s}, -8\,\mathrm{m/s})$. Determine the acceleration in each phase, the position at the instant $10\,\mathrm{s}$, the farthest position reached and the distance traveled.

::grafico{f="min(2*x,8,8-4*(x-6))+0*sqrt(x*(10-x))" area="min(2*x,8,8-4*(x-6));0;0;8|0;8-4*(x-6);8;10" xmin=-2 xmax=12 ymin=-10 ymax=10 nomi="t,v" titolo="Velocity graph: the areas above the axis are $16$, $16$ and $8$, the one below the axis is $8$; the velocity vanishes at the instant $t = 8\,\mathrm{s}$"}

The accelerations are the slopes of the three segments; the displacements are the areas, with sign.

$$
a_1 = \frac{8 - 0}{4 - 0} = 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
a_2 = 0,
\qquad
a_3 = \frac{-8 - 8}{10 - 6} = -4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v(t) = 8 - 4\,(t - 6) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = 8\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\Delta x_{0 \to 4} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 8 = 16\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta x_{4 \to 6} = 2 \cdot 8 = 16\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta x_{6 \to 8} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 8 = 8\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\Delta x_{8 \to 10} = -\frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 8 = -8\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
x(10) = x_0 + 16 + 16 + 8 - 8 = 37\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
x_{\max} = x(8) = 5 + 16 + 16 + 8 = 45\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
d = 16 + 16 + 8 + 8 = 48\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph of $v(t)$ consists of segments, so in each phase the acceleration is constant and equals the slope of the segment. In the third stretch the velocity vanishes at $t = 8\,\mathrm{s}$, and from that instant the point moves back; for this reason the third stretch must be divided into two triangles, one above and one below the axis. The position is obtained by adding the signed areas to the initial position. The point moves forward as long as $v > 0$, therefore the farthest position is reached at $t = 8\,\mathrm{s}$ and equals $45\,\mathrm{m}$. At the instant $10\,\mathrm{s}$ the point has returned to $x = 37\,\mathrm{m}$, and the distance traveled is the sum of the areas taken with positive sign, that is, $48\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
