## Exercise 1

On a straight road a cyclist covers the first $12\,\mathrm{km}$ at the constant speed of $24\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and the next $12\,\mathrm{km}$ at the constant speed of $16\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Compute the average velocity over the whole route, in $\mathrm{km/h}$ and in $\mathrm{m/s}$.

The axis is oriented in the direction of motion; the cyclist never reverses the motion, so the total displacement is $2d$, with $d = 12\,\mathrm{km}$.

$$
t_1 = \frac{d}{v_1},
\qquad
t_2 = \frac{d}{v_2},
\qquad
v_m = \frac{2d}{t_1 + t_2}
$$
$$
v_m = \frac{2d}{\dfrac{d}{v_1} + \dfrac{d}{v_2}} = \frac{2\,v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}
$$
$$
v_m = \frac{2 \cdot 24 \cdot 16}{24 + 16}\,\mathrm{km/h} = 19.2\,\mathrm{km/h} = \frac{19.2}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.33\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In each stretch the motion is uniform, therefore the time taken is the ratio of the length of the stretch to the velocity: $t_1 = 0.50\,\mathrm{h}$ and $t_2 = 0.75\,\mathrm{h}$. The **average velocity** is the total displacement divided by the total time, so it equals $\dfrac{24\,\mathrm{km}}{1.25\,\mathrm{h}}$. Since the two stretches have the same length, $d$ cancels and the result depends only on the two velocities. Therefore the average velocity is $19.2\,\mathrm{km/h}$, that is, $5.33\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and it is less than the arithmetic mean $20\,\mathrm{km/h}$, because the cyclist spends more time on the slower stretch. As a check, the result lies between the two velocities and is closer to the smaller one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On a straight road car $A$ starts from point $P$ at the constant speed of $90\,\mathrm{km/h}$; at the same instant car $B$ starts from point $Q$, $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ away from $P$, and moves toward $A$ at the constant speed of $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Determine after how much time and at what distance from $P$ the two cars pass each other.

The $x$-axis is chosen with the origin at $P$ and the positive orientation from $P$ toward $Q$, and $t = 0$ at the instant of departure; the data are $v_A = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $v_B = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $D = 1200\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
x_A(t) = v_A\,t,
\qquad
x_B(t) = D - v_B\,t
$$
$$
x_A(t) = x_B(t)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v_A\,t = D - v_B\,t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{D}{v_A + v_B}
$$
$$
x = v_A\,t = \frac{v_A\,D}{v_A + v_B}
$$
$$
t = \frac{1200\,\mathrm{m}}{45\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 26.67\,\mathrm{s} \approx 26.7\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
x = 25\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 26.67\,\mathrm{s} = 667\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="25*x+0*sqrt(x);1200-20*x+0*sqrt(x)" punti="26.67,666.7,pieno" segmenti="26.67,0,26.67,666.7" xmin=-5 xmax=45 ymin=-150 ymax=1350 nomi="t,x" titolo="Equations of motion $x_A(t) = 25\,t$ and $x_B(t) = 1200 - 20\,t$, in meters: the lines intersect at the instant $t = 26.7\,\mathrm{s}$ at the position $x = 667\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two cars must be described in the same reference frame. With the axis oriented from $P$ toward $Q$, car $B$ moves in the negative orientation, so its velocity is $-v_B$ and its equation of motion is $D - v_B\,t$. The cars pass each other when they occupy the same position, therefore one imposes $x_A(t) = x_B(t)$, which is a linear equation in $t$. The solution $t = \dfrac{D}{v_A + v_B}$ shows that the initial distance decreases at the rate $v_A + v_B$. The cars **pass each other** after $26.7\,\mathrm{s}$ at $667\,\mathrm{m}$ from $P$. As a check, $x_B = 1200\,\mathrm{m} - 20\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 26.67\,\mathrm{s} = 667\,\mathrm{m}$, the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A bus leaves a stop and travels on a straight road at the constant speed of $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$. From the same stop, $40\,\mathrm{s}$ later, a car leaves and travels along the same road at the constant speed of $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the instant and the position at which the car catches up with the bus.

The $x$-axis is chosen with the origin at the stop and the orientation of the motion, and $t = 0$ at the departure of the bus; the data are $v_b = 15\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $v_a = 25\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $t_0 = 40\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
x_b(t) = v_b\,t,
\qquad
x_a(t) = v_a\,(t - t_0) \quad \text{for } t \geq t_0
$$
$$
v_b\,t = v_a\,(t - t_0)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{v_a\,t_0}{v_a - v_b}
$$
$$
t = \frac{25\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 40\,\mathrm{s}}{10\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 100\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
x = v_b\,t = 15\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 100\,\mathrm{s} = 1500\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The car starts with a delay $t_0$, so $t - t_0$ appears in its equation of motion, and the law holds only from $t_0$ on. The meeting is the solution of $x_b(t) = x_a(t)$; the equation has a positive solution because $v_a > v_b$, that is, because the car is faster. The solution $t = 100\,\mathrm{s}$ is greater than $t_0$, so it is acceptable. Therefore the car **catches up with the bus** at the instant $t = 100\,\mathrm{s}$, $1500\,\mathrm{m}$ from the stop. As a check, when the car starts the bus is at $600\,\mathrm{m}$, and since this distance decreases by $10\,\mathrm{m}$ every second it becomes zero after $60\,\mathrm{s}$, that is, at $t = 40\,\mathrm{s} + 60\,\mathrm{s} = 100\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
