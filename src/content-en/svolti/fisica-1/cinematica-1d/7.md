## Exercise 1

A boat is moving in a straight line at $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ when the engine is switched off. From that instant the water slows it down with an acceleration $a = -k\,v$, with $k = 0.20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Compute the velocity after $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, the distance traveled in those $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, the time in which the velocity halves and the total distance the boat travels before stopping.

The axis is oriented in the direction of motion, with the origin and $t = 0$ at the point and the instant at which the engine is switched off; the data are $v_0 = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $k = 0.20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

$$
\frac{dv}{dt} = -k\,v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v(t) = v_0\,e^{-k t},
\qquad
x(t) = \frac{v_0}{k}\left(1 - e^{-k t}\right)
$$
$$
v(5.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot e^{-1} = 2.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x(5.0\,\mathrm{s}) = \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{0.20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}}\left(1 - e^{-1}\right) = 30\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.632 = 19\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
e^{-k\,t_{\mathrm{d}}} = \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t_{\mathrm{d}} = \frac{\ln 2}{k} = \frac{0.693}{0.20\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 3.5\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\lim_{t \to +\infty} x(t) = \frac{v_0}{k} = 30\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="6*exp(-0.2*x)+0*sqrt(x)" area="6*exp(-0.2*x);0;0;5" segmenti="5,0,5,2.207" xmin=-2 xmax=18 ymin=-1 ymax=7 nomi="t,v" titolo="Velocity of the boat, $v(t) = 6\,e^{-0.2\,t}$: the shaded area, between $0$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$, is the distance traveled in that interval, $19\,\mathrm{m}$; the total area under the graph is $30\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration is proportional to the velocity and of opposite sign, so the theorem on **motion with acceleration proportional to velocity** applies, which is obtained by separating the variables. The velocity decreases exponentially, and after $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, that is, after a time equal to $\dfrac{1}{k}$, it has been reduced by a factor $e$, down to $2.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The distance traveled is obtained from the equation of motion and is $19\,\mathrm{m}$. The halving time $t_{\mathrm{d}}$, that is, the time in which the velocity halves, is $3.5\,\mathrm{s}$ regardless of the starting value, because $\dfrac{v(t + t_{\mathrm{d}})}{v(t)} = e^{-k\,t_{\mathrm{d}}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for every $t$. The velocity tends to zero without vanishing, but the distance traveled has a finite limit, therefore the boat travels $30\,\mathrm{m}$ in total.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A small sphere is released from rest in a viscous liquid. The weight, the buoyant force of the liquid and the viscous drag give it a downward acceleration $a = A - k\,v$, with $A = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $k = 3.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Determine the terminal velocity, the instant at which the velocity reaches $95\%$ of the terminal velocity, and the velocity and the distance traveled after $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

The axis is oriented downward, with the origin at the starting point; the data are $v_0 = 0$, $A = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $k = 3.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

$$
\frac{dv}{dt} = A - k\,v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v(t) = v_L\left(1 - e^{-k t}\right),
\qquad
v_L = \frac{A}{k} = \frac{6.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{3.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
1 - e^{-k t_{95}} = 0.95
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t_{95} = \frac{\ln 20}{k} = \frac{3.00}{3.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x(t) = \int_0^t v_L\left(1 - e^{-k s}\right) ds = v_L\,t - \frac{v_L}{k}\left(1 - e^{-k t}\right)
$$
$$
v(1.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot \left(1 - e^{-3}\right) = 1.9\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x(1.0\,\mathrm{s}) = 2.0\,\mathrm{m} - \frac{2.0}{3.0}\left(1 - e^{-3}\right)\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m} - 0.633\,\mathrm{m} = 1.37\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The terminal velocity is the value at which the acceleration $A - k\,v$ vanishes, so $v_L = \dfrac{A}{k} = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Separating the variables with $v(0) = 0$ gives the law $v(t) = v_L\left(1 - e^{-kt}\right)$, already derived for the **terminal velocity**. The condition $v = 0.95\,v_L$ is equivalent to $e^{-kt} = 0.05 = \dfrac{1}{20}$, therefore after $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the sphere moves practically at constant velocity, $1.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The distance is obtained by integrating the velocity with $x(0) = 0$. The first term $v_L\,t$ is the distance the sphere would travel if it moved at the terminal velocity from the start, and the second is the shortfall due to the initial phase, which tends to $\dfrac{v_L}{k} = 0.67\,\mathrm{m}$. After $1.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the sphere has traveled $1.37\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object sliding on a liquid surface is slowed by a resistance proportional to the square of the velocity, with acceleration $a = -c\,v^2$, where $c = 0.010\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. The initial velocity is $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine after how much time the velocity halves and the distance traveled in that time, and verify the result with the velocity as a function of position.

The axis is oriented in the direction of motion, with the origin and $t = 0$ at the initial point and instant; the data are $v_0 = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $c = 0.010\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$.

$$
\frac{dv}{dt} = -c\,v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_{v_0}^{v} \frac{du}{u^2} = -c\,t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{v_0} + c\,t
$$
$$
v(t) = \frac{v_0}{1 + c\,v_0\,t},
\qquad
x(t) = \int_0^t \frac{v_0}{1 + c\,v_0\,s}\,ds = \frac{1}{c}\,\ln\left(1 + c\,v_0\,t\right)
$$
$$
v = \frac{v_0}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1 + c\,v_0\,t = 2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{1}{c\,v_0} = \frac{1}{0.010\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} \cdot 20\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
x = \frac{\ln 2}{c} = \frac{0.693}{0.010\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}} = 69\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v\,\frac{dv}{dx} = -c\,v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v(x) = v_0\,e^{-c\,x},
\qquad
v_0\,e^{-c\,x} = \frac{v_0}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{\ln 2}{c} = 69\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The acceleration depends on the velocity, so the variables are separated. The velocity is positive and does not vanish, therefore one can divide by $v^2$; the integral of $\dfrac{1}{u^2}$ is $-\dfrac{1}{u}$, and it follows that $\dfrac{1}{v}$ increases linearly in time. The velocity halves when $1 + c\,v_0\,t = 2$, that is, after $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and integrating the velocity gives the distance traveled, $69\,\mathrm{m}$. For the verification the **acceleration as a function of position** is used. Writing $a = v\,\dfrac{dv}{dx}$ and dividing by $v > 0$ gives $\dfrac{dv}{dx} = -c\,v$, whose solution is an exponential in the position, and the condition $v = \dfrac{v_0}{2}$ again gives $69\,\mathrm{m}$. The units are consistent, because $c\,v^2$ is measured in $\mathrm{m^{-1}} \cdot \mathrm{m^2/s^2} = \mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $c\,v_0\,t$ is a pure number.

</div>
