## Exercise 1

A rigid container holds $2.00\,\mathrm{mol}$ of helium, a monatomic ideal gas, at $300\,\mathrm{K}$. The gas is heated up to $450\,\mathrm{K}$. Compute the heat absorbed, the work and the change in internal energy, and the ratio of the final to the initial pressure.

$$
L = 0, \qquad \Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T, \qquad Q = \Delta U + L = n\,c_V\,\Delta T, \qquad c_V = \frac{3}{2}R
$$
$$
Q = \Delta U = 2.00 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 150\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.74 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\frac{p_f}{p_i} = \frac{T_f}{T_i} = \frac{450}{300} = 1.50
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The container is rigid, therefore the process is **isochoric** and the gas does no work, because $dV = 0$ at every instant. By the first law all the heat absorbed increases the internal energy, and for the ideal gas $\Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T$ with $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$ for a monatomic gas. The gas absorbs **$3.74\,\mathrm{kJ}$**, and its internal energy increases by the same amount.

Volume and number of moles are constant, therefore by the equation of state the ratio $\dfrac{p}{T}$ stays constant and the pressure increases in the same ratio as the absolute temperature, that is, by a factor of one and a half.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cylinder closed by a freely sliding piston contains $0.500\,\mathrm{mol}$ of diatomic ideal gas at the constant pressure of $1.00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The gas is heated slowly and its volume goes from $10.0\,\mathrm{L}$ to $15.0\,\mathrm{L}$. Compute the initial and final temperatures, the work done by the gas, the change in internal energy and the heat absorbed.

$$
T = \frac{pV}{nR}, \qquad L = p\,(V_f - V_i), \qquad \Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T = \frac{c_V}{R}\,p\,\Delta V, \qquad Q = \Delta U + L
$$
$$
T_i = \frac{1.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 10.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.500 \cdot 8.314}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 241\,\mathrm{K},
\qquad
T_f = \frac{1.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 15.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.500 \cdot 8.314}\,\mathrm{K} \approx 361\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
L = 1.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 500\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta U = \frac{5}{2} \cdot 500\,\mathrm{J} = 1250\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad Q = (1250 + 500)\,\mathrm{J} = 1750\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The piston is free and the heating is slow, therefore the process is a quasi-static **isobaric** one. The temperatures are obtained from the equation of state in the two states. The work is the integral of the pressure, which is constant, therefore $L = p\,\Delta V$, positive because the gas expands.

For the change in internal energy we use the fact that at constant pressure $nR\,\Delta T = p\,\Delta V$, therefore $\Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T = \dfrac{c_V}{R}\,p\,\Delta V$, with $\dfrac{c_V}{R} = \dfrac{5}{2}$ for a diatomic gas. The heat absorbed is obtained from the first law, $Q = \Delta U + L$, and coincides with $n\,c_p\,\Delta T = \dfrac{7}{2}\,p\,\Delta V$. The gas **absorbs $1750\,\mathrm{J}$**, of which $500\,\mathrm{J}$ become work, according to the ratio $2 : 5 : 7$ typical of the isobaric process of a diatomic gas.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A monatomic ideal gas goes from the state $A$ ($V_A = 2.0\,\mathrm{L}$, $p_A = 1.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) to the state $B$ ($V_B = 4.0\,\mathrm{L}$, $p_B = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$) along two different paths: the path $ACB$, formed by an isochoric process followed by an isobaric one, and the path $ADB$, formed by an isobaric process followed by an isochoric one. For each path compute the work, the change in internal energy and the heat absorbed.

::grafico{vettori="2,1,2,3;2,3,4,3;2,1,4,1;4,1,4,3" punti="2,1,pieno,A;4,3,pieno,B;2,3,pieno,C;4,1,pieno,D" area="3;1;2;4" segmenti="2,0,2,1;4,0,4,1" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=4 nomi="V,p" titolo="The paths $ACB$ and $ADB$ in the $(V, p)$ plane, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$; the difference between the two works is the shaded area"}

$$
\Delta U = n\,c_V\,(T_B - T_A) = \frac{3}{2}\,(p_B V_B - p_A V_A)
$$
$$
\Delta U = \frac{3}{2}\,(3.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3} - 1.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3})\,\mathrm{J} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 1000\,\mathrm{J} = 1500\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
L_{ACB} = p_B\,(V_B - V_A) = 3.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 600\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad Q_{ACB} = 1500\,\mathrm{J} + 600\,\mathrm{J} = 2100\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
L_{ADB} = p_A\,(V_B - V_A) = 1.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 200\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad Q_{ADB} = 1500\,\mathrm{J} + 200\,\mathrm{J} = 1700\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Internal energy is a state function, therefore $\Delta U$ is the same along the two paths. For the ideal gas $\Delta U = n\,c_V\,\Delta T$, and since $nT = \dfrac{pV}{R}$ one can write $\Delta U = \dfrac{c_V}{R}\,(p_B V_B - p_A V_A)$, without knowing $n$; with $\dfrac{c_V}{R} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ one obtains $1500\,\mathrm{J}$.

The work, instead, depends on the path. On the isochoric processes $AC$ and $DB$ the work is zero, therefore along $ACB$ only the isobaric process $CB$ at the pressure $p_B$ counts, and along $ADB$ only the isobaric process $AD$ at the pressure $p_A$. In the $(V, p)$ plane the two works are the areas under the horizontal segments, and their difference, $400\,\mathrm{J}$, is the area of the rectangle $ACBD$. The heat is obtained for each path from the **first law**, $Q = \Delta U + L$; the heat also depends on the path, and the differences in $Q$ and in $L$ between the two paths are equal, as the equality of the $\Delta U$ requires.

</div>

## Exercise 4

$1.00\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ vaporizes completely at the constant pressure of $1.013 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The volume goes from $1.04 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ (liquid) to $1.67\,\mathrm{m^3}$ (vapor). Compute the heat absorbed, the work done and the change in internal energy ($\lambda_v = 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J/kg}$).

$$
Q = m\,\lambda_v, \qquad L = p\,(V_v - V_{\ell}), \qquad \Delta U = Q - L
$$
$$
Q = 1.00 \cdot 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J} = 2.26 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
L = 1.013 \cdot 10^5 \cdot (1.67 - 0.00104)\,\mathrm{J} \approx 1.69 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\Delta U = (2.26 \cdot 10^6 - 0.169 \cdot 10^6)\,\mathrm{J} \approx 2.09 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Vaporization occurs at constant pressure, therefore it is an isobaric process even though the system is not an ideal gas. The heat absorbed is the latent heat of vaporization of the mass of water. The work is $p\,\Delta V$, because the pressure is constant, and the volume of the liquid is negligible compared with that of the vapor.

The **first law** holds for any system, therefore the change in internal energy is $Q - L$. About $7.5\,\%$ of the heat absorbed becomes work of expansion against the atmosphere, and the rest, **$2.09 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{J}$**, increases the internal energy, because it serves to separate the molecules by overcoming the attractive forces that hold them together in the liquid.

</div>
