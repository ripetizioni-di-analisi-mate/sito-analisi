## Exercise 1

$0.200\,\mathrm{mol}$ of ideal gas expands quasi-statically at the constant temperature of $300\,\mathrm{K}$, from a volume of $5.00\,\mathrm{L}$ to a volume of $15.0\,\mathrm{L}$. Compute the initial and final pressures, the work done by the gas, the heat absorbed and the change in internal energy.

$$
p = \frac{nRT}{V}, \qquad \Delta U = 0, \qquad Q = L = nRT \ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}
$$
$$
p_i = \frac{0.200 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 300}{5.00 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{Pa} \approx 9.98 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa},
\qquad
p_f = \frac{p_i}{3} \approx 3.33 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
L = 0.200 \cdot 8.314 \cdot 300 \cdot \ln 3\,\mathrm{J} = 498.8 \cdot 1.0986\,\mathrm{J} \approx 548\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
Q = L \approx 548\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad \Delta U = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial pressure is obtained from the equation of state with the volume in cubic meters. The temperature is constant, therefore by **Boyle's law** $pV$ stays constant and, since the volume triples, the pressure is reduced to one third.

The internal energy of the ideal gas depends only on the temperature, therefore in an isothermal process $\Delta U = 0$ and by the first law $Q = L$. The work is the integral of $p = \dfrac{nRT}{V}$ between the two volumes, which gives the logarithm of the ratio of the volumes; in the ratio the units cancel, therefore the volumes can be left in liters. The gas absorbs **$548\,\mathrm{J}$** of heat and returns all of it as work.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Air, treated as a diatomic ideal gas with $\gamma = 1.40$, is at $p_i = 1.00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$, $V_i = 1.00\,\mathrm{L}$, $T_i = 300\,\mathrm{K}$ and is compressed adiabatically and quasi-statically down to $V_f = 0.125\,\mathrm{L}$. Compute the final temperature and pressure, the work done by the gas and the change in internal energy.

$$
T_f = T_i \left( \frac{V_i}{V_f} \right)^{\gamma - 1}, \qquad p_f = p_i \left( \frac{V_i}{V_f} \right)^{\gamma}, \qquad L = \frac{p_i V_i - p_f V_f}{\gamma - 1}, \qquad \Delta U = -L
$$
$$
\frac{V_i}{V_f} = 8, \qquad T_f = 300 \cdot 8^{0.40}\,\mathrm{K} = 300 \cdot 2.297\,\mathrm{K} \approx 689\,\mathrm{K}
$$
$$
p_f = 1.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 8^{1.40}\,\mathrm{Pa} = 1.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 18.38\,\mathrm{Pa} \approx 1.84 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{Pa}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
L &= \frac{1.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 1.00 \cdot 10^{-3} - 1.838 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.125 \cdot 10^{-3}}{0.40}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{100 - 229.7}{0.40}\,\mathrm{J} \approx -324\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\Delta U = -L \approx 324\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The process is adiabatic and quasi-static, therefore the relations $TV^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$ and $pV^{\gamma} = \text{constant}$ hold; from each one the final quantity is isolated. The compression ratio is $8$, and the powers $8^{0.40}$ and $8^{1.40}$ are computed with a calculator.

The work of an adiabatic process is obtained from $\dfrac{p_i V_i - p_f V_f}{\gamma - 1}$, with the products $pV$ in joules. The result is negative, because in a compression it is the surroundings that do work on the gas. The heat exchanged is zero, therefore by the first law $\Delta U = -L$ and the internal energy **increases by $324\,\mathrm{J}$**, consistently with the rise in temperature.

As an independent check, the gas contains $n = \dfrac{p_i V_i}{R T_i} \approx 0.0401\,\mathrm{mol}$, and $n\,c_V\,\Delta T = 0.0401 \cdot \dfrac{5}{2} \cdot 8.314 \cdot 389\,\mathrm{J} \approx 324\,\mathrm{J}$. Ignition in Diesel engines is based on this phenomenon: the compressed air reaches temperatures high enough to ignite the diesel fuel.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A monatomic ideal gas is in the state $A$ with $p_A = 4.00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and $V_A = 2.00\,\mathrm{L}$, and expands quasi-statically up to $4.00\,\mathrm{L}$. Compare the final pressure, the work and the final temperature in the following two cases: isothermal expansion (final state $B$) and adiabatic expansion (final state $C$).

::grafico{f="8/x;4*(2/x)^(5/3)" punti="2,4,pieno,A;4,2,pieno,B;4,1.26,pieno,C" segmenti="4,0,4,2" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=5 nomi="V,p" etichette="4.45,2.05,isotherm;4.3,0.7,adiabat" titolo="Isothermal expansion $AB$ and adiabatic expansion $AC$ from the same state, with $V$ in liters and $p$ in $10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$; the adiabatic work is smaller because the curve lies lower"}

$$
\text{isothermal:}\quad p_B = p_A\,\frac{V_A}{V_B}, \qquad L_{AB} = p_A V_A \ln\frac{V_B}{V_A}, \qquad T_B = T_A
$$
$$
\text{adiabatic:}\quad p_C = p_A \left( \frac{V_A}{V_C} \right)^{\gamma}, \qquad L_{AC} = \frac{p_A V_A - p_C V_C}{\gamma - 1}, \qquad \frac{T_C}{T_A} = \left( \frac{V_A}{V_C} \right)^{\gamma - 1}
$$
$$
p_A V_A = 4.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2.00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} = 800\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad \gamma = \frac{5}{3}
$$
$$
p_B = 2.00 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}, \qquad L_{AB} = 800 \ln 2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 555\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
p_C = 4.00 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 2^{-\frac{5}{3}}\,\mathrm{Pa} \approx 1.26 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}, \qquad \frac{T_C}{T_A} = 2^{-\frac{2}{3}} \approx 0.630
$$
$$
L_{AC} = \frac{800 - 1.26 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 4.00 \cdot 10^{-3}}{\frac{2}{3}}\,\mathrm{J} = \frac{3}{2}\,(800 - 504)\,\mathrm{J} \approx 444\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **isothermal** process the product $pV$ is constant, therefore doubling the volume halves the pressure; the work is $nRT \ln\dfrac{V_B}{V_A}$, and the product $nRT$ coincides with $p_A V_A$ by the equation of state, therefore there is no need to know $n$ or $T_A$. In the **adiabatic** process we use $pV^{\gamma} = \text{constant}$ with $\gamma = \dfrac{5}{3}$ for a monatomic gas, and the pressure drops to $31.5\,\%$ of the initial value.

The adiabat is steeper than the isotherm, therefore for the same final volume the pressure is lower, and the area under the curve, that is, the work, is smaller: $444\,\mathrm{J}$ versus $555\,\mathrm{J}$. In the isothermal process the gas receives heat from the surroundings and keeps its temperature; in the adiabatic process the work is done at the expense of the internal energy, and the final absolute temperature is $63\,\%$ of the initial one.

</div>
