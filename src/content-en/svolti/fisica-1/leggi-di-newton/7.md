## Exercise 1

A stone of mass $m = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$, tied to an ideal string of length $r = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, rotates on a smooth horizontal table around the other end of the string, fixed to a pivot, completing $2.0$ revolutions per second. Compute the tension of the string. The string breaks when the tension exceeds $40\,\mathrm{N}$; compute the maximum number of revolutions per second.

$$
\omega = 2\pi f,
\qquad
T = m\,\omega^2 r
$$
$$
\omega = 2\pi \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 12.6\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
T = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (12.57\,\mathrm{rad/s})^2 \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m} = 25.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
T_{\max} = m\,\omega_{\max}^2\,r
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{T_{\max}}{m\,r}} = \sqrt{\frac{40\,\mathrm{N}}{0.20\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{m}}} = 15.8\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
f_{\max} = \frac{\omega_{\max}}{2\pi} = 2.5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The table is smooth, therefore in the vertical direction weight and normal force balance, and the only force in the horizontal plane is the tension of the string, directed toward the pivot, that is, toward the center of the circle. The tension alone provides the **centripetal force**, so $T = m\omega^2 r$. The angular velocity is obtained from the rotation frequency, $\omega = 2\pi f$, and the tension equals $25.3\,\mathrm{N}$.

The tension grows with the square of the angular velocity. Setting $T = 40\,\mathrm{N}$ one finds the maximum angular velocity, $15.8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, which corresponds to $2.5$ revolutions per second. As a check, the ratio of the tensions is $\dfrac{40}{25.3} = 1.58$ and the ratio of the frequencies is $\dfrac{2.52}{2.0} = 1.26$, whose square is about $1.58$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car of mass $m = 1000\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels at $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$ around a flat curve of radius $r = 80\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the required friction force and the minimum value of the coefficient of static friction that allows the car to take the curve. On wet asphalt the coefficient drops to $\mu_s = 0.30$; compute the maximum speed on the curve.

$$
v = 72\,\mathrm{km/h} = \frac{72}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
f_s = m\,\frac{v^2}{r} = 1000\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \frac{(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{80\,\mathrm{m}} = 5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
f_s \leq \mu_s\,m g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_s \geq \frac{v^2}{g\,r} = \frac{400}{9.81 \cdot 80} = 0.51
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \sqrt{\mu_s\,g\,r} = \sqrt{0.30 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 80\,\mathrm{m}} = 15.3\,\mathrm{m/s} = 55\,\mathrm{km/h}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces acting on the car are its weight, the normal force and the static friction between tires and road. The road is level, therefore $N = mg$ and the only horizontal force is friction, which provides the centripetal force $m\dfrac{v^2}{r} = 5.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$. The friction is static, because the contact point of the tires does not skid sideways on the road.

The law of static friction requires the needed force not to exceed $\mu_s N$; so the coefficient must be at least $0.51$, a typical value for dry asphalt. On wet asphalt the condition for the **curve on a level road** gives a maximum speed of $15.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, that is, $55\,\mathrm{km/h}$, and above this value friction is not enough to provide the centripetal force and the car leaves the curve. The mass of the car does not appear in the maximum speed, because both the required force and the available friction are proportional to $m$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A conical pendulum consists of a small ball of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ hanging from an ideal string of length $\ell = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$. The ball describes a horizontal circle at constant speed, while the string forms an angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ with the vertical. Compute the tension of the string, the speed of the ball and the period of the motion.

$$
\begin{cases}
T \cos\theta - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
T \sin\theta = m\,\dfrac{v^2}{r}
\end{cases}
\qquad
r = \ell \sin\theta
$$
$$
T = \frac{m g}{\cos\theta},
\qquad
v = \sqrt{g\,r \tan\theta},
\qquad
T_0 = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell \cos\theta}{g}}
$$
$$
T = \frac{0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.866} = 5.66\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
r = 1.2\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.500 = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 0.577} = 1.84\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
T_0 = \frac{2\pi \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{m}}{1.843\,\mathrm{m/s}} = 2.05\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2.6,2.944 -0.8,2.944;-1.7,2.944 0,0" curve="-1.7+1.7*cos(t),0.3*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno;-1.7,0,pieno,C" segmenti="-1.7,2.944,-1.7,0" vettori="0,0,-1.416,2.452,T;0,0,0,-2.452,P;0,0,-1.416,0,R" etichette="-1.58,2.194,θ" xmin=-4.3 xmax=2.1 ymin=-2.8 ymax=3.344 titolo="Conical pendulum: the vertical component of $\vec{T}$ balances the weight, the horizontal component $\vec{R}$ is the centripetal force, directed toward the center $C$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Only the weight and the tension of the string act on the ball. The ball moves on a horizontal circle of radius $r = \ell\sin\theta$, therefore its acceleration is horizontal and directed toward the center, while along the vertical the acceleration is zero. Decomposing the tension, the vertical component $T\cos\theta$ balances the weight and the horizontal component $T\sin\theta$ is the **centripetal force**.

The first equation gives $T = 5.66\,\mathrm{N}$. Dividing the second equation by the first one obtains $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{g r}$, the same relation as for the banked curve, from which $v = 1.84\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The period, denoted by $T_0$ to distinguish it from the tension $T$, is the time to travel around the circle, $2.05\,\mathrm{s}$; substituting $r$ and $v$ one obtains the formula $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell\cos\theta}{g}}$, which gives the same value and shows that the period does not depend on the mass.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A roller-coaster car, of total mass $m = 300\,\mathrm{kg}$, travels along the inside of a vertical loop of radius $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the minimum speed it must have at the highest point to remain in contact with the rail. Then compute the force of the rail at the highest point, if the speed there is $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and at the lowest point, on exiting the loop, if the speed there is $17\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\text{highest point:}\quad N_A + m g = m\,\frac{v_A^2}{r},
\qquad
N_A \geq 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v_A \geq \sqrt{g\,r}
$$
$$
v_{\min} = \sqrt{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m}} = 7.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
N_A = m \left( \frac{v_A^2}{r} - g \right) = 300\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left( \frac{100}{5.0} - 9.81 \right) \mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.06 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\text{lowest point:}\quad N_B - m g = m\,\frac{v_B^2}{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N_B = m \left( g + \frac{v_B^2}{r} \right)
$$
$$
N_B = 300\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \left( 9.81 + \frac{289}{5.0} \right) \mathrm{m/s^2} = 300\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 67.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.03 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the two points considered the velocity is horizontal and the radial component of the net force must equal $m\dfrac{v^2}{r}$, directed toward the center of the loop. At the highest point the center is below, and both the weight and the force of the rail are directed downward; so their sum provides the centripetal force. The rail can only push, therefore $N_A \geq 0$, and this condition gives the minimum speed of the **loop-the-loop**, $7.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

With $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at the highest point the required centripetal force, $m\dfrac{v^2}{r} = 6.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$, exceeds the weight $2.94 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$, and the rail provides the difference, $3.06 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N}$. At the lowest point the center is above, therefore the force of the rail, upward, must balance the weight and in addition provide the centripetal force. The result is $N_B = 2.03 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N}$, about $6.9$ times the weight, and the passengers feel pressed against the seat with a force equal to $6.9$ times their own weight.

</div>
