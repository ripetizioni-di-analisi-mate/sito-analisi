## Exercise 1

Two blocks of masses $m_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, connected by a horizontal ideal string, rest on a smooth table. Block $m_1$, which is in front, is pulled with a horizontal force $F = 30\,\mathrm{N}$. Compute the acceleration of the system and the tension of the string.

$$
\begin{cases}
F - T = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
T = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2},
\qquad
T = \frac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,F
$$
$$
a = \frac{30\,\mathrm{N}}{6.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
T = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 10\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible, therefore the two blocks have the same acceleration $a$; the string is ideal, therefore it pulls the two blocks with the same tension $T$, the rear block forward and the front one backward. Along the vertical each block is in equilibrium and, since the table is smooth, the only horizontal forces are $F$ and the tensions.

Adding the two equations the tension is eliminated, and one obtains the acceleration of the system treated as a **single body** of mass $6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ subject only to the external force $F$. The second equation then gives $T = 10\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore the string transmits to the rear block the fraction $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1 + m_2} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ of the applied force, because that block has one third of the total mass.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An Atwood machine consists of two bodies of masses $m_1 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, hanging from the ends of an ideal string that passes over an ideal pulley. The system starts from rest. Compute the acceleration, the tension of the string, the time taken by the heavier body to descend $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the velocity reached at that instant.

$$
\begin{cases}
m_1 g - T = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
T - m_2 g = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\,m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g
$$
$$
a = \frac{2.0}{8.0} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
T = \frac{2 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 3.0}{8.0}\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 36.8\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2\,h}{a}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{m}}{2.4525\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}} = 0.90\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
v = \sqrt{2\,a\,h} = 2.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body of mass $m_1$ is heavier, therefore it descends, while $m_2$ rises; for each body the positive sense is chosen as that of its motion. On each body act its weight and the tension of the string, which for the **Atwood machine** with ideal string and pulley is the same on both sides. Adding the two equations the tension is eliminated and one obtains the acceleration, $2.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$; substituting one finds the tension, $36.8\,\mathrm{N}$.

The tension lies between the weight of $m_2$, $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$, and the weight of $m_1$, $49.1\,\mathrm{N}$. Indeed $m_2$ accelerates upward, therefore the string pulls it with a force greater than its weight; $m_1$ accelerates downward, therefore the string holds it back with a force less than its weight. The acceleration is constant and the system starts from rest, so the motion is uniformly accelerated and the body descends $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ in $0.90\,\mathrm{s}$ and reaches $2.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a horizontal table, with friction coefficients $\mu_s = 0.30$ and $\mu_d = 0.20$. The block is connected, by an ideal string that passes over an ideal pulley fixed to the edge of the table, to a body of mass $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hanging vertically. The system is released from rest. Determine whether it sets in motion and, if so, compute the acceleration and the tension of the string.

$$
m_2\,g = 29.4\,\mathrm{N} > \mu_s\,m_1\,g = 0.30 \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 17.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
T - \mu_d\,m_1 g = m_1\,a \\[4pt]
m_2 g - T = m_2\,a
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = \frac{m_2 - \mu_d\,m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,g,
\qquad
T = m_2\,(g - a)
$$
$$
a = \frac{3.0 - 0.20 \cdot 6.0}{9.0} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = \frac{1.8}{9.0} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 1.962)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 23.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before writing the equations of motion, one checks that motion occurs. If the system remained at rest, the string would have to support the hanging body with a tension equal to its weight, $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$, and static friction on the block would have to balance the same force. Static friction can reach at most $\mu_s N_1 = \mu_s m_1 g = 17.7\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore equilibrium **is not possible** and the system sets in motion, with $m_2$ descending.

During the motion the friction on the block is kinetic and equals $\mu_d m_1 g = 11.8\,\mathrm{N}$, directed opposite to the motion. The second law is written for the two bodies along the string, as for the **body pulled by a hanging body**; adding the equations one obtains $a = 1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and from the second equation $T = 23.5\,\mathrm{N}$. As a check, the first equation gives $T = 11.8\,\mathrm{N} + 6.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 1.962\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 23.5\,\mathrm{N}$, the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A block of mass $m_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a plane inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$, with friction coefficients $\mu_s = 0.15$ and $\mu_d = 0.10$. The block is connected, by an ideal string parallel to the plane that passes over an ideal pulley at the top of the plane, to a body of mass $m_2 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ hanging vertically. The system starts from rest. Determine in which sense it moves and compute the acceleration and the tension of the string.

$$
m_2\,g = 29.4\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
m_1\,g \sin\alpha = 19.6\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
\mu_s\,m_1 g \cos\alpha = 5.10\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
m_2\,g - m_1\,g \sin\alpha = 9.81\,\mathrm{N} > 5.10\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
m_2 g - T = m_2\,a \\[4pt]
T - m_1 g \sin\alpha - \mu_d\,m_1 g \cos\alpha = m_1\,a
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \frac{m_2 - m_1 \sin\alpha - \mu_d\,m_1 \cos\alpha}{m_1 + m_2}\,g,
\qquad
T = m_2\,(g - a)
$$
$$
a = \frac{29.43\,\mathrm{N} - 19.62\,\mathrm{N} - 3.40\,\mathrm{N}}{7.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = \frac{6.41\,\mathrm{N}}{7.0\,\mathrm{kg}} = 0.916\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
T = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot (9.81 - 0.916)\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 26.7\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sense of motion is not known a priori, therefore the forces that tend to move the system are compared. The weight of the hanging body, $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$, tends to make the block move up the plane; the component of the block's weight along the plane, $19.6\,\mathrm{N}$, tends to make it slide down. The difference, $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$, acts in the sense in which $m_2$ descends, and it exceeds the maximum static friction $\mu_s m_1 g\cos\alpha = 5.10\,\mathrm{N}$; so the system **moves** with $m_2$ descending and the block moving up.

Once the sense is established, the kinetic friction on the block is directed down along the plane and equals $\mu_d m_1 g\cos\alpha = 3.40\,\mathrm{N}$. The second law for the two bodies, with the positive sense in agreement with the motion, gives a system in the unknowns $a$ and $T$; adding the equations one obtains $a = 0.916\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and from the first $T = 26.7\,\mathrm{N}$. The tension is less than the weight of the hanging body, consistently with the fact that this body accelerates downward.

</div>
