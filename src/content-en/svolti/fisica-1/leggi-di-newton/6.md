## Exercise 1

A body of mass $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ hanging from a vertical spring stretches it, at equilibrium, by $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Compute the spring constant and the mass that, hanging from the same spring, produces an extension of $6.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
k\,x_1 - m_1 g = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k = \frac{m_1 g}{x_1}
$$
$$
k = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.040\,\mathrm{m}} = 196\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
k\,x_2 = m_2 g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m_2 = \frac{k\,x_2}{g} = m_1\,\frac{x_2}{x_1} = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \frac{6.5}{4.0} = 1.3\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equilibrium the forces acting on the body are its weight, downward, and the elastic force, upward, which by **Hooke's law** has magnitude $kx$. The net force is zero, therefore $kx = mg$, and from the measured extension one obtains $k = 196\,\mathrm{N/m}$. The extensions must be expressed in meters, because the spring constant is measured in $\mathrm{N/m}$.

With the second mass the same equilibrium condition holds. Since the extension is proportional to the hanging weight, the ratio of the masses equals the ratio of the extensions, and the mass sought is $1.3\,\mathrm{kg}$. The second computation does not require the numerical value of $k$, and it is the working principle of the dynamometer, which compares an unknown weight with a known weight through the extensions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two springs with constants $k_1 = 300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $k_2 = 600\,\mathrm{N/m}$ are used to hang a body of mass $m = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. Compute the total extension at equilibrium when the springs are connected in series and when they are connected in parallel, with the body hung so that the two springs stretch by the same amount. In the series case also compute the extension of each spring.

$$
k_{\text{ser}} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2} = \frac{300 \cdot 600}{900}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 200\,\mathrm{N/m},
\qquad
k_{\text{par}} = k_1 + k_2 = 900\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
m g = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 29.43\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
x_{\text{ser}} = \frac{m g}{k_{\text{ser}}} = \frac{29.43\,\mathrm{N}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 14.7\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
x_{\text{par}} = \frac{m g}{k_{\text{par}}} = \frac{29.43\,\mathrm{N}}{900\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 3.27\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x_1 = \frac{m g}{k_1} = 9.81\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
x_2 = \frac{m g}{k_2} = 4.91\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two configurations are solved by replacing the springs with the equivalent spring given by the rule for **springs in series and in parallel**. In series the reciprocal of the equivalent constant is the sum of the reciprocals, therefore $k_{\text{ser}} = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, a value smaller than both constants; in parallel the constants add, therefore $k_{\text{par}} = 900\,\mathrm{N/m}$. At equilibrium the equivalent spring supports the weight, and the extensions are $14.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $3.27\,\mathrm{cm}$.

In series the springs have negligible mass, so each is stretched by the entire weight of the body, $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$. The softer spring stretches by $9.81\,\mathrm{cm}$, the other by $4.91\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the sum $14.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ coincides with the extension computed with the equivalent spring. In parallel, instead, the two springs have the same extension and share the weight in proportion to their constants, and the first supports $300 \cdot 0.0327\,\mathrm{N} = 9.81\,\mathrm{N}$, the second twice as much.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a plane inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$ and is held by a spring with constant $k = 150\,\mathrm{N/m}$, parallel to the plane and fixed at the top of the plane. Compute the extension of the spring at equilibrium if the plane is smooth. If instead there is friction between block and plane with $\mu_s = 0.20$, determine the range of extensions for which the block remains in equilibrium.

$$
\text{smooth plane:}\quad k\,x - m g \sin\alpha = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{m g \sin\alpha}{k} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.500\,\mathrm{N}}{150\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 6.54\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\text{with friction:}\quad k\,x - m g \sin\alpha + f_s = 0,
\qquad
|f_s| \leq \mu_s\,m g \cos\alpha
$$
$$
m g \sin\alpha - \mu_s\,m g \cos\alpha \leq k\,x \leq m g \sin\alpha + \mu_s\,m g \cos\alpha
$$
$$
9.81\,\mathrm{N} - 3.40\,\mathrm{N} \leq k\,x \leq 9.81\,\mathrm{N} + 3.40\,\mathrm{N}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4.27\,\mathrm{cm} \leq x \leq 8.81\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 0,3.464;1.949,2.339 2.555,1.989 2.905,2.595 2.299,2.945" linee="0,3.464 0.45,4.244;0.175,3.767 0.622,3.659 0.64,3.349 0.918,3.488 0.936,3.178 1.214,3.317 1.232,3.007 1.51,3.146 1.529,2.836 1.807,2.975 2.124,2.642" vettori="2.427,2.467,2.427,0.467,P;2.427,2.467,3.293,3.967,N;2.427,2.467,1.561,2.967,F" riferimento="4.1,3.3,-30,1.1,x,y" etichette="5.0,0.12,α" xmin=-0.4 xmax=6.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=4.6 titolo="On the smooth plane the elastic force $\vec{F}$, parallel to the plane, balances the component $mg\sin\alpha$ of the weight"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The axes of the inclined plane are used. On the smooth plane the block is subject to its weight, the normal force and the elastic force, directed up along the plane because the spring is stretched. At equilibrium the elastic force balances the component of the weight along the plane, $mg\sin\alpha = 9.81\,\mathrm{N}$, therefore the extension is $6.54\,\mathrm{cm}$.

With friction the block can also remain at rest in other positions, because static friction provides the difference between the two forces along the plane. If the spring is slightly stretched, friction is directed upward, in the same sense as the elastic force; if it is greatly stretched, friction is directed downward, opposite to the elastic force. In both cases the magnitude of friction cannot exceed $\mu_s N = \mu_s mg\cos\alpha = 3.40\,\mathrm{N}$. So the elastic force must lie between $6.41\,\mathrm{N}$ and $13.2\,\mathrm{N}$, and the block is in **equilibrium** for all extensions between $4.27\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8.81\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value found for the smooth plane lies at the center of the interval, as it must, because it corresponds to zero friction.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A body of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from a spring with constant $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ fixed to the ceiling of an elevator. Compute the extension of the spring, with the body at rest relative to the car, when the elevator is at rest and when it accelerates upward with $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At another moment the measured extension is $3.9\,\mathrm{cm}$; compute the acceleration of the elevator.

$$
k\,x - m g = m\,a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{m\,(g + a)}{k},
\qquad
a = \frac{k\,x}{m} - g
$$
$$
a = 0:
\quad
x_0 = \frac{0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{100\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 4.91\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
a = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}:
\quad
x = \frac{0.50\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 11.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{100\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 5.91\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x = 3.9\,\mathrm{cm}:
\quad
a = \frac{100\,\mathrm{N/m} \cdot 0.039\,\mathrm{m}}{0.50\,\mathrm{kg}} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 7.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = -2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body is at rest relative to the car, therefore in the inertial frame of the building it has the same acceleration as the elevator. The forces acting on the body are its weight and the elastic force, upward; with the vertical axis pointing upward the second law gives $kx - mg = ma$. The spring plays the role of the scale in the computation of the **apparent weight**, and the elastic force equals $m(g + a)$.

With the elevator at rest the extension is $4.91\,\mathrm{cm}$; with the upward acceleration the spring must also provide the force $ma$, and the extension rises to $5.91\,\mathrm{cm}$. Solving the same equation for $a$, an extension of $3.9\,\mathrm{cm}$, smaller than that at rest, corresponds to $a = -2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, that is, to a **downward acceleration** of $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The datum does not distinguish between an elevator starting downward and one braking on the way up, because the spring measures only the acceleration. The device is an accelerometer.

</div>
