## Exercise 1

A body of mass $m = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially at rest at the origin of a smooth horizontal plane, is subject to a force directed along the $x$-axis with component $F_x(x) = b\,x^2$, where $b = 3.0\,\mathrm{N/m^2}$. Compute the work of the force when the body moves from $x = 0$ to $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the speed of the body at $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
L = \int_0^{x_1} b\,x^2\,dx = \frac{b\,x_1^3}{3} ,
\qquad
L = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 - 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 b\, x_1^3}{3 m}}
$$
$$
L = \frac{3.0 \cdot 2.0^3}{3}\,\mathrm{J} = 8.0\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 8.0}{1.0}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force depends on the position, so the work is computed with the definition of the **work of a variable force**, as the integral of the component $F_x$ between the initial and final positions. An antiderivative of $b\,x^2$ is $\dfrac{b\,x^3}{3}$, and the work equals $8.0\,\mathrm{J}$. The plane is smooth and horizontal, therefore the weight and the normal force do no work and the total work coincides with that of the force $F_x$. By the work-energy theorem, with zero initial speed, the work equals the final kinetic energy, from which $v = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The dimensions are consistent, because $\mathrm{N/m^2} \cdot \mathrm{m^3} = \mathrm{N \cdot m} = \mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart of mass $m = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially at rest at $x = 0$ on a smooth horizontal rail, is pushed along the $x$-axis by a force that varies with position: $F_x$ increases linearly from $0$ to $10\,\mathrm{N}$ between $x = 0$ and $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, stays equal to $10\,\mathrm{N}$ up to $x = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and decreases linearly to $0$ at $x = 7.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the speed of the cart at $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and at $x = 7.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

::grafico{f="max(0,min(5*x,10,35-5*x))" area="max(0,min(5*x,10,35-5*x));0;0;7" segmenti="2,0,2,10;5,0,5,10" xmin=-0.6 xmax=8.2 ymin=-1 ymax=12 nomi="x,F" titolo="The work of the force between $x = 0$ and $x = 7$ is the area of the trapezoid: $10\,\mathrm{J}$ on the first stretch, $30\,\mathrm{J}$ on the second, $10\,\mathrm{J}$ on the third"}

$$
L_{0 \to 2} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{J} = 10\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
L_{2 \to 5} = 3.0 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{J} = 30\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
L_{5 \to 7} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10\,\mathrm{J} = 10\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2} m v^2 = L
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 L}{m}}
$$
$$
v(2.0\,\mathrm{m}) = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 10}{4.0}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{5.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.2\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v(7.0\,\mathrm{m}) = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 50}{4.0}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The work of a variable force along the $x$-axis is the **signed area** under the graph of $F_x$; here $F_x \geq 0$, so the area is positive and is computed with elementary shapes, without writing the expression of the force. The first and third stretches are triangles with base $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $10\,\mathrm{N}$, the second is a rectangle with base $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$; the total work is $50\,\mathrm{J}$. The rail is smooth and horizontal, therefore the total work is that of the force, and by the work-energy theorem it coincides with the kinetic energy reached by the cart starting from rest. At $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ the work is $10\,\mathrm{J}$ and at $x = 7.0\,\mathrm{m}$ it is $50\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves along the $x$-axis subject only to a conservative force with potential energy $U(x) = a\,x^4 - b\,x^2$, where $a = 1.0\,\mathrm{J/m^4}$ and $b = 8.0\,\mathrm{J/m^2}$.

1. Determine the equilibrium positions and their stability, and compute the force at $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$.
2. The particle passes through $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ with speed $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine the turning points of the motion.
3. Compute the minimum speed the particle must have at $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ to get past the position $x = 0$.

$$
U(x) = x^4 - 8x^2 ,
\qquad
F_x(x) = -U'(x) = -4x^3 + 16x = -4x\,(x^2 - 4) ,
\qquad
U''(x) = 12x^2 - 16
$$
$$
U'(x) = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x \in \{-2, 0, 2\} ,
\qquad
U''(\pm 2) = 32 > 0 ,
\qquad
U''(0) = -16 < 0
$$
$$
F_x(1.0) = -4 + 16 = 12\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
E = U(2) + \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = (16 - 32)\,\mathrm{J} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.50 \cdot 4.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = -12\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
x^4 - 8x^2 = -12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(x^2 - 2)(x^2 - 6) = 0 ,
\qquad
x_1 = \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.41\,\mathrm{m} ,
\quad
x_2 = \sqrt{6}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.45\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
E \geq U(0) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2} m v^2 \geq 16\,\mathrm{J}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v \geq \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 16}{0.50}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="x^4-8*x^2;-12" punti="-2,-16,pieno;2,-16,pieno;0,0,pieno;1.414,-12,vuoto,x₁;2.449,-12,vuoto,x₂" etichette="-3.1,-11,E" xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-19 ymax=8 nomi="x,U" titolo="$U(x) = x^4 - 8x^2$: stable minima at $x = \pm 2$, unstable maximum at $x = 0$; with $E = -12\,\mathrm{J}$ the motion is confined between $x_1 = \sqrt{2}$ and $x_2 = \sqrt{6}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equilibrium positions are the points where the force is zero, that is, since $F_x = -U'$, the points where the derivative of $U$ vanishes. By the **stability criterion**, at $x = \pm 2\,\mathrm{m}$ the second derivative is positive, so they are minima of $U$ and positions of stable equilibrium; at $x = 0$ the second derivative is negative, so it is a maximum and the equilibrium is unstable. At $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ the force equals $12\,\mathrm{N}$ and is positive, that is, directed toward the minimum at $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, as the reading of the curve predicts.

The mechanical energy is conserved because only a conservative force acts, and it is computed at the point where position and speed are known: $E = -12\,\mathrm{J}$. The turning points are the solutions of $U(x) = E$; the equation is biquadratic and, setting $t = x^2$, it becomes $t^2 - 8t + 12 = 0$, with solutions $t = 2$ and $t = 6$. Of the four solutions in $x$, the particle reaches only those of the well that contains $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, that is, $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{6}$, because between the two wells there is the maximum $U(0) = 0 > E$. Therefore the motion is **confined** between $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.45\,\mathrm{m}$.

To get past $x = 0$ the mechanical energy must be at least $U(0) = 0$, that is, the kinetic energy at $x = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, where $U = -16\,\mathrm{J}$, must be at least $16\,\mathrm{J}$. The minimum speed is therefore $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$; with this value the particle approaches $x = 0$ with speed tending to zero, and with a larger value it gets past it.

</div>
