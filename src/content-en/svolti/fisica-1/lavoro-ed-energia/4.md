## Exercise 1

A spring with spring constant $k = 500\,\mathrm{N/m}$, compressed by $x = 12\,\mathrm{cm}$, is held against a block of mass $m = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ resting on a smooth horizontal plane. When the spring is released, the block separates at the moment the spring reaches its rest length. Compute the speed of the block after separation.

$$
\frac{1}{2} k x^2 = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = x\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$
$$
v = 0.12 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{500}{0.20}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.12 \cdot 50\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane is smooth and horizontal, so the weight and the normal force do no work and the only force that does work is the elastic force, which is conservative. By the **conservation of mechanical energy**, the initial elastic potential energy $\dfrac{1}{2} k x^2$, with the block at rest, is converted into kinetic energy when the spring returns to its rest length and its potential energy becomes zero. The compression must be expressed in meters, $x = 0.12\,\mathrm{m}$. As a dimensional check, $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathrm{N/m}}{\mathrm{kg}}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{\mathrm{kg/s^2}}{\mathrm{kg}}} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, and multiplied by a length it gives a speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a smooth horizontal plane at speed $v_0 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and hits the free end of a spring of constant $k = 600\,\mathrm{N/m}$, fixed to a wall. Compute the maximum compression of the spring and the speed of the block when the compression is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\frac{1}{2} m v_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} k x_{\max}^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x_{\max} = v_0 \sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2} m v_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 + \frac{1}{2} k x^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 - \frac{k}{m}\, x^2}
$$
$$
x_{\max} = 3.0 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1.5}{600}}\,\mathrm{m} = 3.0 \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{m} = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{9.0 - 400 \cdot 0.10^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{5.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{f="0.03*x^2;6.75" segmenti="10,0,10,3;10,3,10,6.75;15,0,15,6.75" etichette="10.4,1.4,U;10.4,4.8,K;1,7.3,E" punti="15,6.75,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=18 ymin=-0.8 ymax=8.5 nomi="x,E" titolo="Elastic potential energy $U = \dfrac{1}{2} k x^2$ and mechanical energy $E = 6.75\,\mathrm{J}$: with $x = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ we have $U = 3.0\,\mathrm{J}$ and $K = 3.75\,\mathrm{J}$; the maximum compression is $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ ($x$ in centimeters, energies in joules)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

During the compression, besides the weight and the normal force, which do no work, only the elastic force acts, so the mechanical energy is conserved and equals $E = \dfrac{1}{2} m v_0^2 = 6.75\,\mathrm{J}$. At maximum compression the block is momentarily at rest, therefore all the energy is elastic potential energy; we obtain $x_{\max} = 0.15\,\mathrm{m}$. At an intermediate compression the energy is partly kinetic and partly potential. For $x = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ the potential energy is $3.0\,\mathrm{J}$, so the kinetic energy is $3.75\,\mathrm{J}$ and the speed is about $2.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. As the graph shows, the kinetic energy is the vertical distance between the line $E$ and the parabola $U$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A small ball of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ is dropped from rest from a height $h = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ above the free end of a vertical spring of constant $k = 400\,\mathrm{N/m}$ resting on the ground. Compute the maximum compression of the spring, neglecting air resistance.

$$
m g\,(h + x) = \frac{1}{2} k x^2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2} k x^2 - m g\, x - m g h = 0
$$
$$
x = \frac{m g + \sqrt{(m g)^2 + 2 k\, m g h}}{k}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
m g &= 0.40 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 3.924\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
x &= \frac{3.924 + \sqrt{3.924^2 + 2 \cdot 400 \cdot 3.924 \cdot 0.50}}{400}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3.924 + 39.81}{400}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.109\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces that do work on the ball are the weight and, during contact, the elastic force; both are conservative, so the mechanical energy is conserved. We choose zero height at the point of maximum compression. At the start and at maximum compression the ball is at rest; at the start the energy is only gravitational potential energy, $mg\,(h + x)$, because the ball is above the zero level by the height $h$ plus the compression $x$. At maximum compression the energy is only elastic potential energy, $\dfrac{1}{2} k x^2$.

Equating the two energies gives a quadratic equation in $x$. The solutions have opposite signs, because the product of the roots is $-\dfrac{2mgh}{k} < 0$, and the compression is the positive solution. The **maximum compression** is therefore about $11\,\mathrm{cm}$. As a check, neglecting the term $mgx$ we would have $x \approx \sqrt{\dfrac{2mgh}{k}} \approx 0.099\,\mathrm{m}$, slightly less than the result, as it must be because that term adds energy.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A block of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is released from rest on a smooth plane inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$, at a distance $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$, measured along the plane, from the free end of a spring of constant $k = 1000\,\mathrm{N/m}$ fixed at the base of the plane and parallel to it. Compute the maximum compression of the spring.

$$
m g \sin\alpha\,(d + x) = \frac{1}{2} k x^2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2} k x^2 - m g \sin\alpha\, x - m g \sin\alpha\, d = 0
$$
$$
x = \frac{m g \sin\alpha + \sqrt{(m g \sin\alpha)^2 + 2 k\, m g \sin\alpha\, d}}{k}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
m g \sin\alpha &= 2.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} = 9.81\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
x &= \frac{9.81 + \sqrt{9.81^2 + 2 \cdot 1000 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.0}}{1000}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{9.81 + 140.4}{1000}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.150\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane is smooth, so the normal force does no work, and the forces that do work are the weight and the elastic force, both conservative. From the start to maximum compression the block travels the distance $d + x$ along the plane, therefore it drops by the height $(d + x)\sin\alpha$ and loses the gravitational potential energy $mg\sin\alpha\,(d + x)$. Since the block is at rest at the start and at maximum compression, by the **conservation of mechanical energy** that energy becomes entirely elastic potential energy. The equation has the same structure as that of the vertical spring, with $mg\sin\alpha$ in place of $mg$, and the positive solution is $x \approx 0.150\,\mathrm{m} = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
