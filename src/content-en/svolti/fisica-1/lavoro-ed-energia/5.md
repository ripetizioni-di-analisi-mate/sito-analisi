## Exercise 1

A sled with its rider, of total mass $m = 25\,\mathrm{kg}$, starts from rest at the top of a hill of height $h = 12\,\mathrm{m}$ and reaches the foot of the hill with speed $v = 13\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute the mechanical energy dissipated during the descent.

$$
L_{\text{nc}} = E_f - E_i = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 - m g h ,
\qquad
E_{\text{diss}} = -L_{\text{nc}} = m g h - \frac{1}{2} m v^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
m g h &= 25 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 12\,\mathrm{J} = 2943\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
\frac{1}{2} m v^2 &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 25 \cdot 13^2\,\mathrm{J} = 2112.5\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
E_{\text{diss}} &= (2943 - 2112.5)\,\mathrm{J} \approx 8.3 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With zero height at the foot of the hill, the initial mechanical energy is only potential and the final one only kinetic. If there were no friction the two energies would be equal; the final energy is instead smaller, and by the **theorem on the work of nonconservative forces** the difference is the work, negative, of the friction with the snow and of air resistance. The dissipated energy is the absolute value of that work, about $830\,\mathrm{J}$, that is, $28\,\%$ of the initial energy. The computation does not require knowing the shape of the slope or the coefficients of friction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts from rest at the top $A$ of a plane inclined at $\alpha = 35^\circ$, of length $d = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$, and at the base $B$ continues on a horizontal plane. On both stretches the coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_d = 0.25$. Compute the speed of the block at the base of the inclined plane and the distance it travels on the horizontal plane before stopping.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="0,0 4.096,0 0,2.868" linee="-0.5,0 12.3,0" punti="0,2.868,pieno,A;4.096,0,pieno,B;11.47,0,pieno,C" etichette="3.2,0.22,α" xmin=-0.6 xmax=12.6 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.8 uguale=1 titolo="The block slides from $A$ to $B$ along the rough inclined plane and stops at $C$ on the horizontal plane, after about $7.4\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
-\mu_d\, m g \cos\alpha\, d = \frac{1}{2} m v_B^2 - m g\, d \sin\alpha
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_B^2 = 2 g d\,(\sin\alpha - \mu_d \cos\alpha)
$$
$$
-\mu_d\, m g\, s = 0 - \frac{1}{2} m v_B^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s = \frac{v_B^2}{2 \mu_d\, g} = \frac{d\,(\sin\alpha - \mu_d \cos\alpha)}{\mu_d}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_B^2 &= 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 5.0 \cdot (0.574 - 0.25 \cdot 0.819)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} \approx 36.2\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} \\[6pt]
v_B &\approx 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
s &= \frac{36.2}{2 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 9.81}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 7.4\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the inclined plane the normal force is $mg\cos\alpha$, so friction has magnitude $\mu_d\,mg\cos\alpha$ and, being opposite to the displacement, does the work $-\mu_d\,mg\cos\alpha\,d$. With zero height at the base, the mechanical energy goes from $mg\,d\sin\alpha$ to $\dfrac{1}{2} m v_B^2$, and by the **theorem on the work of nonconservative forces** the change equals the work of friction. The mass cancels and we obtain $v_B \approx 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

On the horizontal plane the normal force is $mg$ and the potential energy does not change. Friction does the work $-\mu_d\,mg\,s$, which cancels the kinetic energy the block has at $B$; from this it follows that $s \approx 7.4\,\mathrm{m}$. The second form of the formula for $s$ shows that the result depends neither on the mass nor on $g$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block is launched with speed $v_0 = 8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ up a plane inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$, with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_d = 0.20$ and coefficient of static friction $\mu_s = 0.40$. Compute the distance traveled on the way up and the speed with which the block passes back through the launch point.

$$
\text{ascent:} \quad -\mu_d\, m g \cos\alpha\, d = \left( 0 + m g\, d \sin\alpha \right) - \frac{1}{2} m v_0^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d = \frac{v_0^2}{2 g\,(\sin\alpha + \mu_d \cos\alpha)}
$$
$$
\text{descent:} \quad -\mu_d\, m g \cos\alpha\, d = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 - m g\, d \sin\alpha
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v = \sqrt{2 g d\,(\sin\alpha - \mu_d \cos\alpha)}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \frac{8.0^2}{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot (0.500 + 0.20 \cdot 0.866)}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{64}{13.21}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 4.8\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
v &= \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 4.85 \cdot (0.500 - 0.173)}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 5.6\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the way up friction points down the incline and on the way down it points up the incline, but in both cases it is opposite to the displacement, has magnitude $\mu_d\,mg\cos\alpha$ and does the work $-\mu_d\,mg\cos\alpha\,d$. The theorem on the work of nonconservative forces is applied to each stretch, with zero height at the launch point. On the way up the mechanical energy goes from $\dfrac{1}{2} m v_0^2$ to $mg\,d\sin\alpha$, and we obtain $d \approx 4.8\,\mathrm{m}$.

At the highest point the block stops; since $\tan 30^\circ \approx 0.577$ exceeds $\mu_s = 0.40$, static friction is not enough to hold it, so the block **slides back down**. On the way down the energy goes from $mg\,d\sin\alpha$ to $\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$. The return speed, about $5.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, is smaller than $v_0$, because over the trip up and back friction has dissipated energy; without friction the block would return with the initial speed.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A block of mass $m = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts from rest at a height $h = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ on a smooth curved track, which ends in a rough horizontal stretch of length $\ell = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$, with $\mu_d = 0.40$. At the end of the rough stretch, on a smooth horizontal plane, is the free end of a spring of constant $k = 900\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Compute the maximum compression of the spring and determine where the block finally comes to rest.

$$
m g h - \mu_d\, m g\, \ell = \frac{1}{2} k x^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \sqrt{\frac{2\,(m g h - \mu_d\, m g\, \ell)}{k}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_0 &= m g h = 1.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{J} = 19.62\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
|L_1| &= \mu_d\, m g\, \ell = 0.40 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{J} = 5.886\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
x &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot (19.62 - 5.886)}{900}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.17\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{E_0}{|L_1|} = \frac{h}{\mu_d\, \ell} = \frac{2.0}{0.40 \cdot 1.5} = 3.33\ldots ,
\qquad
s = \frac{E_0 - 3\,|L_1|}{\mu_d\, m g} = \frac{19.62 - 17.66}{3.924}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the curved track and on the plane of the spring there is no friction, so there the mechanical energy is conserved; on the rough stretch, at each complete crossing, friction does the work $-\mu_d\,mg\,\ell$, always the same because the normal force is $mg$. At the first maximum compression the block is at rest, therefore the energy remaining after one crossing is entirely elastic potential energy, and the **maximum compression** is about $17\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Then the block bounces back and crosses the rough stretch again, climbs the curved track, comes back down and so on, losing $5.886\,\mathrm{J}$ at each crossing. The initial energy is enough for $3.33$ crossings, so the block makes three complete crossings and stops during the fourth. After three crossings, the first toward the spring, the second toward the track, the third again toward the spring, the block has the energy $19.62 - 17.66 = 1.96\,\mathrm{J}$, bounces off the spring without losses and re-enters the rough stretch from the spring side. Friction cancels that energy after a distance $s = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the block stops on the rough stretch $0.50\,\mathrm{m}$ from the end on the spring side, that is, $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the end on the track side.

</div>
