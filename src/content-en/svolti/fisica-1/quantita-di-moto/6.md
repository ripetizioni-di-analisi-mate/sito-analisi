## Exercise 1

At an intersection a car $A$ of mass $1200\,\mathrm{kg}$, heading east at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$, collides with a car $B$ of mass $1800\,\mathrm{kg}$, heading north at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. After the collision the two cars stay locked together. Neglecting friction with the asphalt during the collision, compute the magnitude and direction of the velocity immediately after the collision and the fraction of kinetic energy dissipated.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,1.8,0,pA;0,0,0,1.8,pB;0,0,1.8,1.8,P" segmenti="1.8,0,1.8,1.8;0,1.8,1.8,1.8" xmin=-0.4 xmax=2.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=2.3 uguale=1 nomi="east,north" titolo="Momenta before the collision, in units of $10^4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$: $\vec{p}_A$ toward the east, $\vec{p}_B$ toward the north, and their sum $\vec{P}$, directed at $45^\circ$ between east and north"}

$$
\begin{cases}
m_A v_A = (m_A + m_B)\,V_x \\[6pt]
m_B v_B = (m_A + m_B)\,V_y
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_x = \frac{m_A v_A}{m_A + m_B},
\qquad
V_y = \frac{m_B v_B}{m_A + m_B}
$$
$$
V_x = \frac{1200 \cdot 15}{3000}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
V_y = \frac{1800 \cdot 10}{3000}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
V = \sqrt{V_x^2 + V_y^2} = 6.0\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 8.5\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{V_y}{V_x} = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 45^\circ
$$
$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1200 \cdot 15^2\,\mathrm{J} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1800 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{J} = 1.35 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J} + 0.90 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J} = 2.25 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_f = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3000 \cdot 72\,\mathrm{J} = 1.08 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
\frac{K_i - K_f}{K_i} = \frac{1.17}{2.25} = 0.52
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We take the $x$ axis toward the east and the $y$ axis toward the north. During the collision friction with the asphalt is negligible and the forces between the cars are internal, so momentum is conserved; in the plane this is an equality between vectors and provides **two equations**, one per component. The cars stay together, therefore the collision is perfectly inelastic and the unknowns are the two components of the common velocity; each equation contains a single unknown.

The two initial momenta have the same magnitude, $1.8 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, so the components of the final velocity are equal and the cars move **toward the northeast**, at $45^\circ$ from east, with a speed of about $8.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The final kinetic energy is computed with $V^2 = 72\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. The collision dissipates $1.17 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{J}$, that is $52\,\%$ of the initial energy, which goes into the deformation of the car bodies.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On an air table, where friction is negligible, a puck $A$ with velocity $v_A = 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ collides elastically with a puck $B$ of equal mass, at rest. After the collision puck $A$ moves in a direction that forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with the initial one. Compute the speeds of the two pucks after the collision and the direction of motion of $B$.

$$
\begin{cases}
\vec{v}_A = \vec{v}_A' + \vec{v}_B' \\[6pt]
v_A^2 = v_A'^2 + v_B'^2
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{v}_A' \cdot \vec{v}_B' = 0
$$
$$
v_A' = v_A \cos 30^\circ,
\qquad
v_B' = v_A \sin 30^\circ,
\qquad
\beta = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ
$$
$$
v_A' = 5.0 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 4.3\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_B' = 5.0 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The table is horizontal and frictionless, so in the plane of the table momentum is conserved; the collision is elastic, so kinetic energy is also conserved. The masses are equal and cancel, and by the theorem on the **oblique elastic collision between equal masses** the final velocities are perpendicular, so $\vec{v}_A'$ and $\vec{v}_B'$ are the legs of a right triangle with hypotenuse $\vec{v}_A$.

In the right triangle the angle between $\vec{v}_A$ and $\vec{v}_A'$ is $30^\circ$, so the adjacent leg is $v_A' = v_A \cos 30^\circ \approx 4.3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the opposite one is $v_B' = v_A \sin 30^\circ = 2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Puck $B$ moves off **at $60^\circ$** from the initial direction of $A$, on the opposite side with respect to $A$, because the perpendicular component of the total momentum must remain zero.

As a check, $4.33^2 + 2.5^2 = 18.75 + 6.25 = 25 = 5.0^2$, so kinetic energy is conserved.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body of mass $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves on a smooth horizontal plane with velocity $\vec{v}_1 = 3.0\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and collides with a body of mass $m_2 = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest. After the collision the first body has velocity $\vec{v}_1' = (2.0\,\hat{\imath} + 1.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Compute the velocity of the second body after the collision and determine whether the collision is elastic.

$$
m_1 \vec{v}_1 = m_1 \vec{v}_1' + m_2 \vec{v}_2'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{v}_2' = \frac{m_1\,(\vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_1')}{m_2}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_2' = \frac{2.0}{1.0}\,\big( (3.0 - 2.0)\,\hat{\imath} + (0 - 1.0)\,\hat{\jmath} \big)\,\mathrm{m/s} = (2.0\,\hat{\imath} - 2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2' = \sqrt{2.0^2 + 2.0^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.8\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
\tan\beta = \frac{-2.0}{2.0} = -1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta = -45^\circ
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 3.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 9.0\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
K' = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot (4.0 + 1.0)\,\mathrm{J} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1.0 \cdot (4.0 + 4.0)\,\mathrm{J} = 5.0\,\mathrm{J} + 4.0\,\mathrm{J} = 9.0\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane is smooth, so in the plane momentum is conserved; since $\vec{v}_1'$ is known, the vector equation contains the single unknown $\vec{v}_2'$ and is solved component by component. The second body moves off with velocity $(2.0,\ -2.0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, of magnitude about $2.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, at $45^\circ$ below the $x$ axis, while the first body is deflected by about $27^\circ$ above the axis, because $\tan\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The $y$ components of the final momenta, $2.0$ and $-2.0\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, have zero sum, like the initial $y$ component.

To determine the type of collision we compare the kinetic energies. Before and after the collision they are both $9.0\,\mathrm{J}$, so the collision **is elastic**. The masses are different, therefore the final velocities are not perpendicular; indeed $\vec{v}_1' \cdot \vec{v}_2' = 4.0 - 2.0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2} \neq 0$.

</div>
