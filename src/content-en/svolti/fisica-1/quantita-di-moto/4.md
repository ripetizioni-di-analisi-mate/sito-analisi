## Exercise 1

On a horizontal track with negligible friction, a cart of mass $m_1 = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ with velocity $v_1 = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ collides elastically with a cart of mass $m_2 = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ at rest. Compute the velocities of the two carts after the collision and verify the conservation of kinetic energy.

$$
\begin{cases}
m_1 v_1 = m_1 v_1' + m_2 v_2' \\[6pt]
v_2' - v_1' = v_1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1,
\qquad
v_2' = \frac{2 m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1
$$
$$
v_1' = \frac{0.20 - 0.40}{0.60} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = -2.0\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_2' = \frac{0.40}{0.60} \cdot 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
K = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.20 \cdot 6.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 3.6\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
K' = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.20 \cdot 2.0^2\,\mathrm{J} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.40 \cdot 4.0^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.4\,\mathrm{J} + 3.2\,\mathrm{J} = 3.6\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The track is horizontal and smooth, so no external forces act along the track and momentum is conserved. The collision is elastic, therefore kinetic energy is also conserved; for the **elastic collision in one dimension** this second condition is equivalent to the reversal of the relative velocity, $v_2' - v_1' = -(0 - v_1) = v_1$, which is a linear equation. The system of the two linear equations gives the formulas with the second body at rest.

The first cart has a smaller mass than the second, so it **bounces back** at $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while the second moves forward at $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The check on the kinetic energy, $3.6\,\mathrm{J}$ before and after, confirms the result; the momentum is also $1.2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ before and $-0.4 + 1.6 = 1.2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ after.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two spheres of masses $m_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_2 = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ move toward each other along the same line, with speeds $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and collide elastically. Compute the velocities after the collision.

$$
x \text{ axis in the direction of } m_1:\qquad v_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad v_2 = -6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_1' = \frac{(m_1 - m_2)\,v_1 + 2 m_2 v_2}{m_1 + m_2},
\qquad
v_2' = \frac{(m_2 - m_1)\,v_2 + 2 m_1 v_1}{m_1 + m_2}
$$
$$
v_1' = \frac{1.0 \cdot 3.0 + 2 \cdot 1.0 \cdot (-6.0)}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \frac{3.0 - 12}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_2' = \frac{(-1.0) \cdot (-6.0) + 2 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 3.0}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = \frac{6.0 + 12}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_{cm} = \frac{2.0 \cdot 3.0 + 1.0 \cdot (-6.0)}{3.0}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spheres move in opposite senses, so it is convenient to fix an axis and give the velocities a sign; with the axis in the direction of motion of the first sphere, the second has velocity $-6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The formulas of the **elastic collision in one dimension** hold with the velocities taken with their sign, and give $v_1' = -3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2' = 6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$; therefore each sphere goes back with the speed it had.

The result is explained by the **center-of-mass frame**. The total momentum is zero, so the center of mass is at rest and the laboratory frame coincides with the center-of-mass frame; in this frame an elastic collision in one dimension reverses the velocity of each body without changing its magnitude. As a check, the relative velocity goes from $v_1 - v_2 = 9.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $v_1' - v_2' = -9.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the kinetic energy is $9.0 + 18 = 27\,\mathrm{J}$ both before and after.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two spheres hang from vertical strings of the same length, inextensible and of negligible mass, and at equilibrium they are in contact with their centers at the same height. Sphere $A$, of mass $m_A = 0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$, is raised to a height $h = 45\,\mathrm{cm}$ with the string taut and released from rest; at the lowest point it collides elastically with sphere $B$, of mass $m_B = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$, at rest. Neglecting air, compute the maximum heights reached by the two spheres after the collision.

$$
v_A = \sqrt{2 g h},
\qquad
v_A' = \frac{m_A - m_B}{m_A + m_B}\,v_A = -\frac{1}{2}\,v_A,
\qquad
v_B' = \frac{2 m_A}{m_A + m_B}\,v_A = \frac{1}{2}\,v_A
$$
$$
h_A' = \frac{v_A'^2}{2 g} = \frac{1}{4}\,\frac{v_A^2}{2 g} = \frac{h}{4},
\qquad
h_B' = \frac{v_B'^2}{2 g} = \frac{h}{4}
$$
$$
v_A = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.45}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
|v_A'| = v_B' \approx 1.49\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
h_A' = h_B' = \frac{45\,\mathrm{cm}}{4} \approx 11\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem has three phases. During the descent of $A$ the tension of the string is perpendicular to the velocity and does no work, so mechanical energy is conserved and $A$ reaches the bottom with $v_A = \sqrt{2 g h}$. In the collision the strings are vertical and the external forces have no horizontal component, therefore horizontal momentum is conserved; the collision is elastic, so we apply the formulas of the **elastic collision** with $B$ at rest. With $m_B = 3\,m_A$ the coefficients are $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

After the collision each sphere rises conserving mechanical energy, and the height reached is proportional to the square of the speed. The two velocities have magnitude half of $v_A$, so **both spheres rise to $\dfrac{h}{4} \approx 11\,\mathrm{cm}$**, $A$ on the side from which it started and $B$ on the opposite side.

As an energy check, the initial potential energy is $m_A g h$, the total final one is $(m_A + m_B)\,g\,\dfrac{h}{4} = 4 m_A \cdot g\,\dfrac{h}{4} = m_A g h$; the collision is elastic and mechanical energy is conserved throughout the motion. The ratio $\dfrac{h_A'}{h} = \dfrac{h_B'}{h} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ depends only on the mass ratio, and neither on $g$ nor on $h$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A puck of mass $m_1 = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a smooth horizontal plane and collides elastically and head-on with a second puck at rest; after the collision the first puck goes back with one third of its initial speed. Compute the mass of the second puck and the fraction of the kinetic energy transferred to the second puck.

$$
v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1 = -\frac{1}{3}\,v_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
3\,(m_1 - m_2) = -(m_1 + m_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m_2 = 2\,m_1
$$
$$
m_2 = 2 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{kg} = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}
$$
$$
v_2' = \frac{2 m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_1 = \frac{2 m_1}{3 m_1}\,v_1 = \frac{2}{3}\,v_1
$$
$$
\frac{K_2'}{K_1} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{2}\,m_2\,v_2'^2}{\dfrac{1}{2}\,m_1\,v_1^2} = \frac{m_2}{m_1} \cdot \frac{4}{9} = 2 \cdot \frac{4}{9} = \frac{8}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The collision is elastic and head-on, so the motion stays on a line and the formulas of the **elastic collision in one dimension** with the second body at rest hold. The first puck goes back, therefore its final velocity has the opposite sign to $v_1$ and equals $-\dfrac{1}{3}\,v_1$. Equating this value to the formula and canceling $v_1 \neq 0$ we obtain a linear equation in the masses, which gives $m_2 = 2 m_1 = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. The result is consistent with the **special cases** of the elastic collision, because a body that bounces back must have a smaller mass than the target.

The velocity of the second puck is obtained from the second formula, $v_2' = \dfrac{2}{3}\,v_1$. The ratio between the kinetic energies is $\dfrac{8}{9}$, so the second puck receives about $89\,\%$ of the energy. The first puck keeps $\dfrac{1}{9}$ of its energy, because its speed is reduced to one third, and $\dfrac{8}{9} + \dfrac{1}{9} = 1$, as required by the conservation of kinetic energy.

</div>
