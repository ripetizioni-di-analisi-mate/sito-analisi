## Exercise 1

A uniform solid sphere starts from rest and rolls without slipping down an inclined plane, descending through a height difference $h = 1.4\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the velocity of the center of the sphere at the end of the descent and compare it with that of a block sliding without friction from the same height.

$$
M g h = \frac{1}{2}\left( M + \frac{I_C}{R^2} \right) v_C^2,
\qquad
I_C = \frac{2}{5} M R^2
$$
$$
M g h = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{5} M v_C^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_C = \sqrt{\frac{10\,g h}{7}}
$$
$$
v_C = \sqrt{\frac{10 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.4}{7}}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 4.43\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
v_{\text{block}} = \sqrt{2 g h} = 5.24\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In pure rolling friction is static and acts on the contact point, which is at rest, so it does no work; the normal force is perpendicular to the displacement. Therefore the only force that does work is the weight, and mechanical energy is conserved. The **kinetic energy in pure rolling** contains the translational part and the rotational part, and for the solid sphere it equals $\dfrac{7}{10} M v_C^2$. Mass and radius cancel, so the result holds for every uniform solid sphere. The sphere arrives with **$4.43\,\mathrm{m/s}$**, less than the block, because $\dfrac{2}{7}$ of the final kinetic energy of the sphere is in the rotation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform solid cylinder of mass $M = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ rolls without slipping down a plane inclined at $\beta = 30^\circ$. Compute the acceleration of the center, the angular acceleration, the static friction force and the minimum coefficient of static friction that allows pure rolling.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6.062,0 0,3.5;3.85,2.836 3.836,2.992 3.796,3.144 3.729,3.286 3.639,3.415 3.529,3.525 3.4,3.615 3.258,3.682 3.106,3.722 2.95,3.736 2.794,3.722 2.642,3.682 2.5,3.615 2.371,3.525 2.261,3.415 2.171,3.286 2.104,3.144 2.064,2.992 2.05,2.836 2.064,2.68 2.104,2.528 2.171,2.386 2.261,2.257 2.371,2.147 2.5,2.057 2.642,1.99 2.794,1.95 2.95,1.936 3.106,1.95 3.258,1.99 3.4,2.057 3.529,2.147 3.639,2.257 3.729,2.386 3.796,2.528 3.836,2.68" vettori="2.95,2.836,2.95,0.486,Mg;2.5,2.057,3.518,3.819,N;2.5,2.057,2.161,2.253,f" punti="2.95,2.836,pieno" riferimento="4.7,3.3,-30,1.0,x,y" etichette="5.1,0.12,β" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6.6 ymin=-0.5 ymax=4.5 titolo="Forces on the cylinder: the weight $M\vec{g}$ at the center, the normal force $\vec{N}$ and static friction $\vec{f}$ directed up the plane"}

$$
\begin{cases}
M g\sin\beta - f = M a_C \\[4pt]
f R = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2\,\alpha \\[4pt]
a_C = \alpha R
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f = \frac{1}{2} M a_C,
\qquad
a_C = \frac{2}{3}\,g\sin\beta
$$
$$
a_C = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.27\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{a_C}{R} = \frac{3.27}{0.10}\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} = 32.7\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}
$$
$$
f = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 3.27\,\mathrm{N} = 4.91\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
N = M g\cos\beta = 3.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.866\,\mathrm{N} = 25.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
f \leq \mu_s N
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_s \geq \frac{f}{N} = \frac{\tan\beta}{3} = 0.19
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We write the first cardinal equation along the plane, with the positive sense downward, and the second cardinal equation about the center of mass. About the center the weight has zero torque, because it is applied at $C$, and the normal force has zero torque, because its line of action passes through $C$; what remains is the torque of friction, with lever arm $R$. The **pure rolling** condition $a_C = \alpha R$ closes the system. From the second and third equations we obtain $f$ as a function of $a_C$, and the first gives $a_C = \dfrac{2}{3} g\sin\beta$, the case $k = \dfrac{1}{2}$ of rolling down an inclined plane. Perpendicular to the plane the acceleration is zero, so $N = M g\cos\beta$. Static friction cannot exceed $\mu_s N$, therefore pure rolling requires **$\mu_s \geq 0.19$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A solid sphere, a solid cylinder and a thin ring, all uniform, start from rest at the same instant from the top of an inclined plane of length $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, with $\sin\beta = 0.45$, and roll without slipping. Compute the time each one takes to travel the plane and the final velocity of the center.

$$
a_C = \frac{g\sin\beta}{1 + k},
\qquad
d = \frac{1}{2} a_C t^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{a_C}},
\qquad
v_C = a_C t = \sqrt{2 a_C d}
$$
$$
\text{sphere, } k = \frac{2}{5}: \quad a_C = \frac{5}{7} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 3.15\,\mathrm{m/s^2}, \quad t = 1.13\,\mathrm{s}, \quad v_C = 3.55\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\text{cylinder, } k = \frac{1}{2}: \quad a_C = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.94\,\mathrm{m/s^2}, \quad t = 1.17\,\mathrm{s}, \quad v_C = 3.43\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
\text{ring, } k = 1: \quad a_C = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.45\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 2.21\,\mathrm{m/s^2}, \quad t = 1.35\,\mathrm{s}, \quad v_C = 2.97\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the result on **rolling down an inclined plane** the acceleration of the center is constant and depends only on the coefficient $k = \dfrac{I_C}{M R^2}$, which equals $\dfrac{2}{5}$, $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $1$ for the three bodies. The motion of the center is therefore uniformly accelerated from rest, and time and final velocity follow from the equations of motion. The body with smaller $k$ has its mass closer to the axis, therefore a smaller fraction of the energy goes into rotation and the body accelerates more. The order of arrival is **sphere, cylinder, ring**, regardless of masses and radii. As a check, the speed of the sphere coincides with the one given by conservation of energy, $\sqrt{\dfrac{10\,g h}{7}}$ with $h = d\sin\beta = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A small uniform solid sphere starts from rest at a height $h$ on a track that ends with a loop-the-loop of radius $R = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, and rolls without slipping along the whole path. The radius of the sphere is negligible compared with $R$. Compute the minimum height $h$ for which the sphere goes around the whole loop while remaining in contact with the track.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="1,5.4 1.25,4.959 1.5,4.537 1.75,4.134 2,3.75 2.25,3.384 2.5,3.038 2.75,2.709 3,2.4 3.25,2.109 3.5,1.837 3.75,1.584 4,1.35 4.25,1.134 4.5,0.938 4.75,0.759 5,0.6 5.25,0.459 5.5,0.337 5.75,0.234 6,0.15 6.25,0.084 6.5,0.037 6.75,0.009 7,0 1,0" linee="7,0 7.261,0.017 7.518,0.068 7.765,0.152 8,0.268 8.218,0.413 8.414,0.586 8.587,0.782 8.732,1 8.848,1.235 8.932,1.482 8.983,1.739 9,2 8.983,2.261 8.932,2.518 8.848,2.765 8.732,3 8.587,3.218 8.414,3.414 8.218,3.587 8,3.732 7.765,3.848 7.518,3.932 7.261,3.983 7,4 6.739,3.983 6.482,3.932 6.235,3.848 6,3.732 5.782,3.587 5.586,3.414 5.413,3.218 5.268,3 5.152,2.765 5.068,2.518 5.017,2.261 5,2 5.017,1.739 5.068,1.482 5.152,1.235 5.268,1 5.413,0.782 5.586,0.586 5.782,0.413 6,0.268 6.235,0.152 6.482,0.068 6.739,0.017 7,0;-0.3,0 10.3,0" vettori="7,4,5.6,4,v" segmenti="0.4,0,0.4,5.4;0.4,5.4,1,5.4;7,4,9.5,4;9.5,0,9.5,4" punti="1,5.4,pieno,A;7,4,pieno,B" etichette="-0.15,2.7,h;9.65,2,2R" xmin=-0.5 xmax=10.5 ymin=-0.5 ymax=6.2 titolo="The sphere starts from rest at $A$ at height $h$ and at the highest point $B$ of the loop, at height $2R$, it must have sufficient speed"}

$$
\text{at } B: \quad m g + N = m\,\frac{v_B^2}{R},
\qquad
N \geq 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_B^2 \geq g R
$$
$$
m g h = m g\,2R + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{5}\,m v_B^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
h = 2R + \frac{7}{10}\,\frac{v_B^2}{g}
$$
$$
h_{\min} = 2R + \frac{7}{10}\,R = \frac{27}{10}\,R = 2.7 \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m} = 1.08\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the highest point $B$ the sphere travels along a circle of radius $R$, so the resultant of the forces toward the center, that is the weight plus the reaction of the track, provides the centripetal acceleration. The sphere stays in contact if the reaction is not negative, and this happens if $v_B^2 \geq g R$. The rolling is pure and static friction does no work, therefore mechanical energy is conserved from the start to $B$. For the solid sphere the **kinetic energy in pure rolling** is $\dfrac{7}{10} m v_B^2$, greater than that of a particle with the same speed. Substituting the minimum value $v_B^2 = g R$ we obtain **$h_{\min} = 2.7\,R = 1.08\,\mathrm{m}$**. For a particle sliding without friction the analogous calculation gives $2.5\,R$, so the sphere must start higher, because part of the energy goes into the rotation.

</div>
