## Exercise 1

A disk A, with moment of inertia $I_A = 0.020\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, rotates about its own vertical axis at $\omega_A = 40\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. A second coaxial disk B, with moment of inertia $I_B = 0.030\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ and initially at rest, is dropped onto A; because of friction between the surfaces the two disks end up rotating together. The axle is frictionless. Compute the final angular velocity and the dissipated kinetic energy.

$$
I_A\,\omega_A = (I_A + I_B)\,\omega_f
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega_f = \frac{I_A}{I_A + I_B}\,\omega_A = \frac{0.020}{0.050} \cdot 40\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 16\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
K_i = \frac{1}{2} I_A\,\omega_A^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.020 \cdot 40^2\,\mathrm{J} = 16\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
K_f = \frac{1}{2} (I_A + I_B)\,\omega_f^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.050 \cdot 16^2\,\mathrm{J} = 6.4\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
K_i - K_f = 9.6\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
\frac{K_i - K_f}{K_i} = 0.60
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The friction forces between the disks are internal to the system of the two disks. The external forces are the weights and the reactions of the axle: the weights are parallel to the axis and the reactions are applied on the axis, so their axial torque is zero. By the **conservation of angular momentum** the component $L_z$ is the same before and after, and at the end the two disks rotate with the same angular velocity, with moment of inertia $I_A + I_B$. The kinetic energy instead decreases, because friction between the sliding surfaces does negative work; **$60\%$** of the initial energy is transformed into heat. This is the rotational analogue of a perfectly inelastic collision.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal circular platform, which can be modeled as a uniform disk of mass $100\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$, rotates without friction about its own vertical axis at $0.50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$; a person of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ stands on the rim. The person walks to the center of the platform. Treating the person as a point mass, compute the final angular velocity and the work done by the person.

$$
I_1 = \frac{1}{2} M R^2 + m R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot 2.0^2 + 60 \cdot 2.0^2 = 200 + 240 = 440\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_2 = \frac{1}{2} M R^2 = 200\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
I_1\,\omega_1 = I_2\,\omega_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\omega_2 = \frac{440}{200} \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 1.10\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
W = \frac{1}{2} I_2\,\omega_2^2 - \frac{1}{2} I_1\,\omega_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 200 \cdot 1.10^2 - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 440 \cdot 0.50^2 = 121 - 55 = 66\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system consists of the platform and the person; the forces between the feet and the platform are internal. The external forces are the weights, parallel to the axis, and the reactions of the frictionless axle, so the axial torque of the external forces is zero and $L_z$ is conserved. The initial moment of inertia includes the person on the rim, at distance $R$ from the axis, while at the center the person is at zero distance and does not contribute. The moment of inertia decreases, therefore the platform rotates faster, at **$1.10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$**. The kinetic energy increases from $55\,\mathrm{J}$ to $121\,\mathrm{J}$; by the work-energy theorem the difference, **$66\,\mathrm{J}$**, is the work done by the person to move toward the axis against the effect of the rotation; the work is denoted by $W$ to distinguish it from the angular momentum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform rod of mass $M = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $L = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ hangs from one end $O$ on a frictionless hinge and hangs vertically at rest. A bullet of mass $m = 20\,\mathrm{g}$, traveling horizontally at $v_0 = 200\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a plane perpendicular to the axis of the hinge, strikes the rod at distance $d = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$ and stays embedded in it. Compute the angular velocity immediately after the collision and the maximum angle through which the rod rises.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="2.9,0.5 3.1,0.5 3.1,5.5 2.9,5.5" linee="2.4,5.5 3.6,5.5" vettori="0.4,1.5,2.7,1.5,v₀" segmenti="2.5,5.5,2.5,1.5;3,5.5,7.849,4.28" punti="3,5.5,pieno,O;3,1.5,pieno;0.4,1.5,pieno" etichette="2.05,3.4,d;3.55,4.75,θ" xmin=-0.3 xmax=8.4 ymin=0.1 ymax=6.1 titolo="The bullet strikes the rod at distance $d$ from the hinge $O$; after the collision the rod rises up to the maximum angle $\theta$, dashed"}

$$
m v_0 d = \left( \frac{1}{3} M L^2 + m d^2 \right) \omega
$$
$$
I_O = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 1.0^2 + 0.020 \cdot 0.80^2 = 0.6667 + 0.0128 = 0.679\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\omega = \frac{m v_0 d}{I_O} = \frac{0.020 \cdot 200 \cdot 0.80}{0.6795}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = \frac{3.2}{0.6795}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 4.71\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2} I_O\,\omega^2 = \left( M g\,\frac{L}{2} + m g d \right)(1 - \cos\theta)
$$
$$
1 - \cos\theta = \frac{\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.6795 \cdot 4.710^2}{2.0 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.50 + 0.020 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.80} = \frac{7.54}{9.97} = 0.756
$$
$$
\cos\theta = 0.244
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = 75.9^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During the collision the hinge exerts an impulsive reaction, so the momentum of the system is not conserved; the reaction, however, is applied on the axis and has zero torque about $O$, and the weight in the short time of the collision produces a negligible effect. By the result on **collisions with a body constrained to an axis**, the angular momentum about the axis is therefore conserved. Before the collision it is that of the bullet, $m v_0 d$, because the velocity is perpendicular to the segment of length $d$; after, it is $I_O\,\omega$, with the bullet embedded at distance $d$. After the collision the hinge is frictionless, and mechanical energy is conserved. When the rod rotates by the angle $\theta$, the center of mass of the rod rises by $\dfrac{L}{2}(1 - \cos\theta)$ and the bullet by $d\,(1 - \cos\theta)$. Equating the kinetic energy immediately after the collision to the increase in potential energy we obtain **$\theta = 75.9^\circ$**. As a check, the momentum goes from $m v_0 = 4.00\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ to $\left( M\,\dfrac{L}{2} + m d \right)\omega = 4.78\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$, so the hinge exerts on the rod an impulse directed in the direction of motion of the bullet; the kinetic energy drops from $400\,\mathrm{J}$ to $7.54\,\mathrm{J}$, as in every collision in which the bullet stays embedded.

</div>
