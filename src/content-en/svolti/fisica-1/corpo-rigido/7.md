## Exercise 1

A uniform ladder of mass $15\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $\ell = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ leans against a smooth vertical wall and forms an angle $\theta = 60^\circ$ with the floor. Compute the reactions of the wall and of the floor and the minimum coefficient of static friction between ladder and floor that allows equilibrium.

$$
\begin{cases}
N_p - f = 0 \\[4pt]
N - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
m g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\theta - N_p\,\ell\sin\theta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
N = m g = 15 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 147\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
f = N_p = \frac{m g}{2\tan\theta} = \frac{147.2}{2 \cdot 1.732}\,\mathrm{N} = 42.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
f \leq \mu_s N
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_s \geq \frac{f}{N} = \frac{1}{2\tan\theta} = 0.29
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ladder is acted on by the weight, applied at the midpoint by the **torque of the weight**, the reaction of the wall, horizontal because the wall is smooth, and at the foot the normal reaction and the friction of the floor. Friction is directed toward the wall, because the foot tends to slide away from it. There are three unknowns and the **equilibrium conditions** in the plane give three equations. With the pole at the foot the normal force and friction have zero torque; the weight has lever arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\theta$ and the reaction of the wall has lever arm $\ell\sin\theta$, that is the height of the point of support. The balance of horizontal forces gives $f = N_p$, that of vertical forces $N = m g$. The required friction does not exceed the maximum $\mu_s N$ if **$\mu_s \geq 0.29$**; the result depends neither on the mass nor on the length of the ladder.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform ladder of mass $m = 10\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $\ell = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ leans against a smooth vertical wall at an angle $\theta = 60^\circ$ with the floor; the coefficient of static friction with the floor is $\mu_s = 0.40$. A person of mass $M = 70\,\mathrm{kg}$ climbs the ladder. Compute the maximum distance $s$ from the foot, measured along the ladder, that the person can reach without the ladder slipping.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="2,0 2.173,0.1 0.173,3.564 0,3.464" linee="0,3.9 0,0 4,0" vettori="1,1.732,1,1.405,mg;0.56,2.494,0.56,0.205,Mg;2,0,2,2.62,N;2,0,0.95,0,f;0,3.464,1.05,3.464,Nₚ" punti="2,0,pieno,A;0.56,2.494,pieno,P" etichette="1.45,0.3,θ" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.2 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.9 titolo="The person at $P$, at distance $s$ from the foot $A$ along the ladder: in the limiting situation friction reaches the maximum value $\mu_s N$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
N_p - f = 0 \\[4pt]
N - (m + M)\,g = 0 \\[4pt]
m g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\theta + M g\,s\cos\theta - N_p\,\ell\sin\theta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f = \mu_s N = \mu_s (m + M)\,g = 0.40 \cdot 80 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 314\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
s = \frac{\mu_s (m + M)\,\ell\tan\theta - m\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}}{M} = \frac{0.40 \cdot 80 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 1.732 - 10 \cdot 2.0}{70}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{221.7 - 20}{70}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.88\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces are those of the exercise with the ladder alone, plus the weight of the person applied at distance $s$ from the foot. The **equilibrium conditions** again give $N = (m + M)\,g$ and $f = N_p$. With the pole at the foot, the torque equation shows that $N_p$, and therefore the required friction, grows with $s$, because the weight of the person has lever arm $s\cos\theta$. The limiting situation occurs when the required friction is equal to the maximum $\mu_s N$; substituting $N_p = \mu_s (m + M)\,g$ into the torque equation and dividing by $g\cos\theta$ we obtain $s$. The person can climb up to **$2.88\,\mathrm{m}$** from the foot; beyond that point the required friction exceeds the maximum value and the ladder slips.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform crate in the shape of a rectangular box, of mass $40\,\mathrm{kg}$, base $b = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $h = 1.2\,\mathrm{m}$, is at rest on a horizontal floor with coefficient of static friction $\mu_s = 0.30$. A horizontal force $F$, increasing from zero, is applied to an upper edge, perpendicular to a face. Determine whether the crate first starts to slide or to tip, and for which value of $F$; then determine the minimum height of the point of application for which the crate tips before sliding.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="1,0 4,0 4,6 1,6" linee="-0.5,0 7,0" vettori="0.35,6,1,6,F;2.5,3,2.5,0.4,mg;4,0,4,2.6,N;4,0,3.35,0,f" segmenti="2.5,-0.45,4,-0.45;2.5,-0.25,2.5,-0.65;4,-0.25,4,-0.65" punti="4,0,pieno,S;2.5,3,pieno" xmin=-0.8 xmax=7.2 ymin=-0.9 ymax=6.8 titolo="At the onset of tipping the crate rests only on the edge $S$, where $\vec{N}$ and $\vec{f}$ act; the weight has lever arm $\dfrac{b}{2}$ about $S$"}

$$
\text{tipping, torques about } S: \quad F h - m g\,\frac{b}{2} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_{\text{tip}} = \frac{m g\,b}{2h} = \frac{40 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.60}{2 \cdot 1.2}\,\mathrm{N} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\text{sliding:} \quad F > \mu_s N = \mu_s m g = 0.30 \cdot 40 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{N} = 118\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_{\text{tip}} = 98.1\,\mathrm{N} < 118\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\text{force at height } y: \quad \frac{m g\,b}{2y} \leq \mu_s m g
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y \geq \frac{b}{2\mu_s} = \frac{0.60}{2 \cdot 0.30}\,\mathrm{m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As long as the crate is in equilibrium, the normal force of the floor is distributed over the base; when the crate is about to tip about the edge $S$ opposite to the pushed side, the base lifts and the reaction of the floor is concentrated at $S$. At that instant, choosing the pole at $S$, the normal force and friction have zero torque, and the balance of torques is between the force $F$, with lever arm $h$, and the weight, applied at the center of the crate with lever arm $\dfrac{b}{2}$. The crate slides instead when $F$ exceeds the maximum static friction, and since $F$ is horizontal the normal force equals $m g$. The tipping value is smaller, so as $F$ increases the crate **tips at $F = 98.1\,\mathrm{N}$** before sliding. Applying the force at a height $y$, the tipping value becomes $\dfrac{m g\,b}{2y}$, and the crate tips before sliding if the force is applied at least $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ above the floor.

</div>
