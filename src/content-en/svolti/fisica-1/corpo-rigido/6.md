## Exercise 1

With a crowbar, a rigid rod of negligible mass, one wants to lift a boulder that presses on the end of the rod with a vertical force of $800\,\mathrm{N}$. The fulcrum is $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the boulder and $1.75\,\mathrm{m}$ from the opposite end, where one pushes vertically downward. Compute the minimum force to be applied and the reaction of the fulcrum in equilibrium.

$$
F_2\,b_2 = F_1\,b_1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_2 = \frac{b_1}{b_2}\,F_1 = \frac{0.25}{1.75} \cdot 800\,\mathrm{N} = 114\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
R_f - F_1 - F_2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_f = 800 + 114.3 = 914\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The crowbar is a **lever** with the fulcrum between the two forces. Choosing the pole at the fulcrum, the reaction of the fulcrum has zero torque and the torque equation contains only the two forces, both vertical and therefore perpendicular to the rod, with lever arms equal to the distances from the fulcrum. The effort force has a lever arm seven times larger, therefore a force seven times smaller than the weight of the boulder is enough, that is **$114\,\mathrm{N}$**. The condition on the resultant gives the reaction of the fulcrum, directed upward, which balances the two forces directed downward.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform beam of mass $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ rests horizontally on two supports placed at the ends $A$ and $B$. A crate of mass $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ is placed on the beam, $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Compute the reactions of the two supports.

$$
P_t = 50 \cdot 9.81 = 490.5\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
P_c = 30 \cdot 9.81 = 294.3\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
R_A + R_B - P_t - P_c = 0 \\[4pt]
R_B \cdot 4.0 - P_t \cdot 2.0 - P_c \cdot 1.0 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
R_B = \frac{490.5 \cdot 2.0 + 294.3 \cdot 1.0}{4.0}\,\mathrm{N} = 319\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
R_A = 784.8 - 318.8 = 466\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces are all vertical: the two weights and the two reactions, directed upward. By the **torque of the weight** the weight of the uniform beam is applied at the midpoint, $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. With the pole at $A$ the reaction $R_A$ has zero torque, so the torque equation contains the single unknown $R_B$; the condition on the resultant then gives $R_A$. Support $A$ bears the larger force, **$466\,\mathrm{N}$**, because the crate is closer to $A$. As a check, the torque equation about $B$, $R_A \cdot 4.0 = P_t \cdot 2.0 + P_c \cdot 3.0$, gives the same value of $R_A$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform horizontal rod of mass $8.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ is hinged to a vertical wall at the end $O$ and is supported by a rope of negligible mass fixed to the free end and to the wall, which forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with the rod. A load of mass $12\,\mathrm{kg}$ hangs from the free end. Compute the tension of the rope and the reaction of the hinge.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0.92 4,0.92 4,1.08 0,1.08" linee="0,-0.3 0,3.7;4,1 0,3.309" vettori="4,1,1.281,2.57,T;2,1,2,0.215,Mg;4,1,4,-0.177,mg;0,1,2.719,1.392,R" punti="0,1,pieno,O" etichette="3.05,1.15,30°" xmin=-0.6 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.8 titolo="Forces on the rod: the tension $\vec{T}$ along the rope, the weights of the rod and of the load, the reaction $\vec{R}$ of the hinge at $O$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
H - T\cos 30^\circ = 0 \\[4pt]
V + T\sin 30^\circ - M g - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
T\sin 30^\circ \cdot L - M g\,\dfrac{L}{2} - m g\,L = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T = \frac{\dfrac{M g}{2} + m g}{\sin 30^\circ} = \frac{39.24 + 117.7}{0.50}\,\mathrm{N} = 314\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
H = 313.9 \cdot 0.866\,\mathrm{N} = 272\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
V = (8.0 + 12) \cdot 9.81 - 313.9 \cdot 0.50 = 39.2\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{H^2 + V^2} = \sqrt{271.9^2 + 39.2^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 275\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is acted on by the weight of the rod, applied at the midpoint, the weight of the load at the end, the tension of the rope directed along the rope toward the wall and the reaction of the hinge, whose direction is unknown; for this reason the reaction is written with the unknown components $H$, horizontal, and $V$, vertical. There are three unknowns and the **equilibrium conditions** in the plane give three equations. With the pole at the hinge the components $H$ and $V$ have zero torque, so the torque equation contains only $T$; the tension has lever arm $L\sin 30^\circ$, because only its vertical component has a torque about $O$. The other two equations give $H$ and $V$. The reaction of the hinge is directed away from the wall and slightly upward, and its magnitude is **$275\,\mathrm{N}$**. The rope, inclined at only $30^\circ$, has a tension equal to about $1.6$ times the total weight of rod and load, because its vertical component must produce a torque with a reduced lever arm.

</div>
