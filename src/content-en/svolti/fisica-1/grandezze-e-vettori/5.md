## Exercise 1

A crate is dragged for $8.00\,\mathrm{m}$ on a horizontal floor by pulling a rope with a constant force of $60.0\,\mathrm{N}$, inclined at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Compute the work done by the force and the work the same force would do if it were horizontal.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,8,0,s;0,0,5.196,3,F" segmenti="5.196,3,5.196,0" etichette="1.2,0.25,θ" xmin=-0.6 xmax=8.8 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.6 uguale=1 titolo="The force $\vec{F}$ forms the angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ with the horizontal displacement $\vec{s}$"}

$$
L = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = F s \cos\theta
$$
$$
L = 60.0\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 8.00\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \cos 30^\circ = 480\,\mathrm{J} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 416\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
L_{\text{horiz}} = F s = 60.0\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 8.00\,\mathrm{m} = 480\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force is constant and the displacement is rectilinear, so the work is the **dot product** of the force and the displacement. The angle between the two vectors is $30^\circ$, because the displacement is horizontal and the rope is inclined at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. Only the horizontal component of the force, $F\cos 30^\circ \approx 52.0\,\mathrm{N}$, does work, while the vertical component is perpendicular to the displacement. The work is therefore about $416\,\mathrm{J}$, with three significant figures like the data. With the rope horizontal the angle is zero and the work is $480\,\mathrm{J}$, the maximum possible for a force of that magnitude over that displacement, since $\cos\theta \leq 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point particle undergoes the displacement $\vec{s} = (4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 1.0\,\hat{\jmath} + 2.0\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{m}$ while the constant force $\vec{F} = (3.0\,\hat{\imath} - 2.0\,\hat{\jmath} + 1.0\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on it. Compute the work of the force and the angle between the force and the displacement.

$$
L = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = F_x s_x + F_y s_y + F_z s_z,
\qquad
\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{F} \cdot \vec{s}}{F s}
$$
$$
L = (3.0 \cdot 4.0 + (-2.0) \cdot 1.0 + 1.0 \cdot 2.0)\,\mathrm{J} = (12 - 2.0 + 2.0)\,\mathrm{J} = 12\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
F = \sqrt{9.0 + 4.0 + 1.0}\,\mathrm{N} = \sqrt{14}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 3.74\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
s = \sqrt{16 + 1.0 + 4.0}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt{21}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 4.58\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{12\,\mathrm{J}}{\sqrt{14}\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \sqrt{21}\,\mathrm{m}} = \frac{12}{\sqrt{294}} \approx 0.700,
\qquad
\theta \approx 45.6^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the vectors given in components, the **dot product** is computed by adding the products of corresponding components, and the result in $\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ is a work in joules. The term $(-2.0) \cdot 1.0$ is negative because along the $y$-axis the force and the displacement have opposite orientations, and along that axis the force does negative (resistive) work. The total work is positive, so the angle between the force and the displacement is acute. The angle is obtained by comparing the two expressions of the dot product, $F s\cos\theta$ and the formula in components: dividing the dot product by the product of the magnitudes gives $\cos\theta \approx 0.700$, therefore the **angle** is about $45.6^\circ$. The ratio is dimensionless, as a cosine must be, because $\mathrm{J} = \mathrm{N\cdot m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides for $d = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ down a plane inclined at $\alpha = 30^\circ$. Compute the work done by the weight and the work done by the normal force of the plane ($g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$).

::grafico{curve="t,3-0.57735*t,0,5.196" vettori="1.732,2,4.33,0.5,s;1.732,2,1.732,0,P;1.732,2,2.598,3.5,N" segmenti="0,0,5.8,0;1.732,0.5,4.33,0.5" punti="1.732,2,pieno;4.33,0.5,pieno" etichette="4.45,0.12,α;1.25,1.15,h" xmin=-0.6 xmax=6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.8 uguale=1 titolo="The block descends $d = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ along the plane and $h = d\sin\alpha$ vertically; the normal force $\vec{N}$ is perpendicular to the displacement"}

$$
L_P = \vec{P} \cdot \vec{s} = m g\, d \cos(90^\circ - \alpha) = m g\, d \sin\alpha,
\qquad
L_N = \vec{N} \cdot \vec{s} = 0
$$
$$
\vec{P} = (0,\ -mg),
\qquad
\vec{s} = (d\cos\alpha,\ -d\sin\alpha)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{P} \cdot \vec{s} = 0 \cdot d\cos\alpha + (-mg)(-d\sin\alpha) = m g\, d\sin\alpha
$$
$$
L_P = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \sin 30^\circ = 29.4\,\mathrm{J} \approx 29\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The weight and the normal force are constant forces and the displacement is rectilinear, so the work of each is the dot product of the force and the displacement. The displacement is parallel to the plane, while the weight is vertical; the angle between them is $90^\circ - \alpha = 60^\circ$, and $\cos 60^\circ = \sin 30^\circ$. The same result is obtained in components with the $x$-axis horizontal and the $y$-axis vertical, and it shows that $L_P = m g h$ with $h = d\sin\alpha = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ the height descended. The same value is the product of the **component along the plane** of the weight, $m g\sin\alpha$, and $d$, because the component perpendicular to the plane does no work. The normal force is perpendicular to the plane, and hence to the displacement, therefore its work is **zero**. The work of the weight is about $29\,\mathrm{J}$, positive because the block descends.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A cart moves along a straight horizontal rail that forms an angle of $60^\circ$ with the $x$-axis, and let $\hat{u}$ be the unit vector of the rail that forms the angle $60^\circ$ with the positive $x$-semiaxis. The constant force $\vec{F} = (4.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$ acts on the cart, with $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$ in the horizontal plane. Compute the component of the force along the rail, the work done when the cart travels $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ in the orientation of $\hat{u}$, and the component of the force perpendicular to the rail.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,4,3,F;0,0,1,1.732,u" segmenti="-0.6,-1.039,2.6,4.503;4,3,2.299,3.982" xmin=-0.8 xmax=4.8 ymin=-1.2 ymax=4.8 uguale=1 titolo="The projection of $\vec{F}$ onto the rail, inclined at $60^\circ$, is the component $F_{\parallel} = \vec{F} \cdot \hat{u}$"}

$$
\hat{u} = \cos 60^\circ\,\hat{\imath} + \sin 60^\circ\,\hat{\jmath} = \frac{1}{2}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
F_{\parallel} = \vec{F} \cdot \hat{u} = \left( 4.0 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + 3.0 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)\mathrm{N} = (2.0 + 2.60)\,\mathrm{N} \approx 4.6\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
L = \vec{F} \cdot (d\,\hat{u}) = F_{\parallel}\, d = 4.60\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{m} \approx 9.2\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
F = \sqrt{4.0^2 + 3.0^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 5.0\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_{\perp} = \sqrt{F^2 - F_{\parallel}^2} = \sqrt{25 - 21.1}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The direction of the rail is described by the unit vector $\hat{u}$, which has components $\cos 60^\circ$ and $\sin 60^\circ$. The **component along a direction** is the dot product of the vector and the unit vector of that direction, so $F_{\parallel} \approx 4.6\,\mathrm{N}$. The displacement is $d\,\hat{u}$, therefore the work is $F_{\parallel}$ times $d$, about $9.2\,\mathrm{J}$. The force is the sum of its component along the rail and its perpendicular component, which are orthogonal to each other; by the Pythagorean theorem $F^2 = F_{\parallel}^2 + F_{\perp}^2$, and therefore the perpendicular component is about $2.0\,\mathrm{N}$. This component does no work and is balanced by the rail.

</div>
