## Exercise 1

To loosen a bolt, a force of magnitude $F = 80\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied in the plane of the wrench at the end of a wrench of length $r = 0.25\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the magnitude of the torque about the axis of the bolt when the force is perpendicular to the wrench and when it forms an angle of $60^\circ$ with it. Then determine the magnitude of the force inclined at $60^\circ$ that produces the same torque as the perpendicular force.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,2.5,0,r;2.5,0,3.5,1.732,F" segmenti="1.2,-2.252,3.8,2.252;0,0,1.875,-1.083" punti="0,0,pieno;2.5,0,pieno" etichette="0.8,-0.85,b;2.85,0.2,θ" xmin=-0.6 xmax=4.2 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.6 uguale=1 titolo="With the force inclined at $\theta = 60^\circ$ to the wrench the lever arm is $b = r\sin\theta$, smaller than the length $r$ of the wrench"}

$$
\tau = r F \sin\theta = F\, b,
\qquad
b = r\sin\theta
$$
$$
\theta = 90^\circ:
\qquad
\tau_1 = 0.25\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 80\,\mathrm{N} = 20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\theta = 60^\circ:
\qquad
\tau_2 = 0.25\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 80\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 17\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
F' = \frac{\tau_1}{r\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.25\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} \approx 92\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **torque** of the force about the axis of the bolt has magnitude $r F \sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the wrench and the force. For $\theta = 90^\circ$ the sine equals $1$, the lever arm coincides with the length of the wrench and the torque is the largest possible, $20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. For $\theta = 60^\circ$ the line of action of the force passes closer to the axis, as the figure shows, and the lever arm reduces to $r\sin 60^\circ \approx 0.22\,\mathrm{m}$, therefore the torque drops to about $17\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. To obtain $20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ again with the inclined force, $F$ is isolated from the torque formula, and a force of about $92\,\mathrm{N}$ is needed. The result confirms that a force perpendicular to the wrench is the most effective.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A force $\vec{F} = (3.0\,\hat{\jmath} + 4.0\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied at the point with position $\vec{r} = (1.0\,\hat{\imath} + 2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the pole $O$. Compute the torque $\vec{\tau}$ about $O$ and its magnitude, and verify that it is perpendicular to both $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{F}$.

$$
\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat{\imath} & \hat{\jmath} & \hat{k} \\
1.0 & 2.0 & 0 \\
0 & 3.0 & 4.0
\end{vmatrix}
\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{\tau} &= \big[ (2.0 \cdot 4.0 - 0 \cdot 3.0)\,\hat{\imath} + (0 \cdot 0 - 1.0 \cdot 4.0)\,\hat{\jmath} + (1.0 \cdot 3.0 - 2.0 \cdot 0)\,\hat{k} \big]\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
&= (8.0\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath} + 3.0\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\tau = \sqrt{64 + 16 + 9.0}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = \sqrt{89}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \approx 9.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\vec{\tau} \cdot \vec{r} = 8.0 \cdot 1.0 + (-4.0) \cdot 2.0 + 3.0 \cdot 0 = 0,
\qquad
\vec{\tau} \cdot \vec{F} = 8.0 \cdot 0 + (-4.0) \cdot 3.0 + 3.0 \cdot 4.0 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque is the **cross product** $\vec{r} \times \vec{F}$, in this order, and it is computed by expanding the symbolic determinant along the first row; the minus sign of the component along $\hat{\jmath}$ is already included in the formula $A_z B_x - A_x B_z$. The unit is the product of the units, so $\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The magnitude is obtained from the components with the Pythagorean theorem in space. The cross product is perpendicular to both factors, so the two dot products must be zero; the calculation confirms this and is a useful **check** against sign errors. By the right-hand rule, reversing the order and computing $\vec{F} \times \vec{r}$ would give the opposite vector.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A horizontal rod can rotate about a pivot at its end $O$, and the $x$-axis is oriented along the rod. Acting on the rod, in the $xy$-plane, are the force $\vec{F}_1$ of $30\,\mathrm{N}$ directed like $\hat{\jmath}$ and applied at $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$, and the force $\vec{F}_2$ of $50\,\mathrm{N}$ applied at $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$, which forms an angle of $30^\circ$ with the rod and is directed downward and to the right. Compute the resultant torque about $O$ and establish in which orientation the rod tends to rotate.

::grafico{linee="0,0 4,0" vettori="4,0,4,3,F₁;2,0,6.33,-2.5,F₂" punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="2.7,-0.35,30°" xmin=-0.6 xmax=6.8 ymin=-3 ymax=3.4 uguale=1 titolo="The rod of length $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ with the two applied forces (scale: one unit per $0.1\,\mathrm{m}$ and per $10\,\mathrm{N}$)"}

$$
\tau_z = x F_y - y F_x
$$
$$
\vec{F}_1 = (0,\ 30)\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
\vec{F}_2 = (50\cos 30^\circ,\ -50\sin 30^\circ)\,\mathrm{N} = (43.3,\ -25.0)\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\tau_{1z} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 30\,\mathrm{N} = 12\,\mathrm{N\cdot m},
\qquad
\tau_{2z} = 0.20\,\mathrm{m} \cdot (-25.0\,\mathrm{N}) = -5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\tau_z = \tau_{1z} + \tau_{2z} = 12\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} - 5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 7.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the points of application lie on the $x$-axis, so $y = 0$ and the $z$ component of the torque reduces to $x F_y$. It follows that the component $F_{2x}$, directed along the rod, has zero torque, because its line of action passes through the pivot. The force $\vec{F}_1$ pushes upward and has positive torque, that is, it tends to rotate the rod counterclockwise; the vertical component of $\vec{F}_2$ pushes downward and has negative torque. The **resultant torque** is the sum of the torques, with their signs, and equals $7.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ along $\hat{k}$. The positive sign indicates that the rod tends to rotate **counterclockwise**, as seen from the positive $z$-semiaxis.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The force $\vec{F} = (3.0\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied at the point $P = (2.0,\ 1.0)\,\mathrm{m}$ of the $xy$-plane. Compute the torque about the origin and the distance of the origin from the line of action of the force. Verify that the torque does not change if the same force is applied at the point $Q = (-1.0,\ -3.0)\,\mathrm{m}$.

::grafico{segmenti="-1.6,-3.8,3.8,3.4;0,0,0.8,-0.6" vettori="2,1,3.5,3,F;-1,-3,0.5,-1,F" punti="0,0,pieno;2,1,pieno,P;-1,-3,pieno,Q" etichette="0.15,-0.55,b" xmin=-2 xmax=4.2 ymin=-4 ymax=3.8 uguale=1 titolo="$P$ and $Q$ lie on the same line of action, which is at distance $b = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the origin"}

$$
\tau_z = x F_y - y F_x,
\qquad
b = \frac{\tau}{F}
$$
$$
\tau_z(P) = (2.0 \cdot 4.0 - 1.0 \cdot 3.0)\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
F = \sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2}\,\mathrm{N} = 5.0\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
b = \frac{5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{5.0\,\mathrm{N}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{PQ} = (-3.0,\ -4.0)\,\mathrm{m} \parallel \vec{F},
\qquad
\tau_z(Q) = \big( (-1.0) \cdot 4.0 - (-3.0) \cdot 3.0 \big)\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force and the position lie in the $xy$-plane, so the **torque** has only the $z$ component, given by $x F_y - y F_x$. The magnitude of the torque is the force times the lever arm, therefore the lever arm, that is, the distance of the origin from the line of action, is obtained by dividing the torque by the magnitude of the force and equals $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The vector $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ is parallel to $\vec{F}$, so $Q$ lies on the line of action of the force. Moving the point of application along the line of action leaves the lever arm unchanged, and indeed the direct calculation gives $5.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ again. In terms of the cross product, $\vec{r}_Q \times \vec{F} = \vec{r}_P \times \vec{F} + \overrightarrow{PQ} \times \vec{F}$, and the last term is **zero** because $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ and $\vec{F}$ are parallel.

</div>
