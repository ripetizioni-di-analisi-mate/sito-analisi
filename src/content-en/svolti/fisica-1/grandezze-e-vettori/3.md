## Exercise 1

A ball has a velocity of magnitude $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed at $150^\circ$ from the positive $x$-semiaxis, measured counterclockwise. Compute the components of the velocity. Then determine the magnitude and the angle of the displacement $\vec{s} = (-6.0\,\hat{\imath} - 8.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
v_x = v\cos\theta = 20 \cdot \cos 150^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = -20 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx -17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_y = v\sin\theta = 20 \cdot \sin 150^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,-6,-8,s" segmenti="-6,0,-6,-8;0,-8,-6,-8" xmin=-7.2 xmax=1.6 ymin=-9 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 titolo="The displacement $\vec{s} = (-6.0,\ -8.0)\,\mathrm{m}$ lies in the third quadrant"}

$$
s = \sqrt{s_x^2 + s_y^2} = \sqrt{(-6.0)^2 + (-8.0)^2}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt{100}\,\mathrm{m} = 10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\arctan\frac{s_y}{s_x} = \arctan\frac{-8.0}{-6.0} \approx 53.1^\circ,
\qquad
\theta = 53.1^\circ + 180^\circ = 233.1^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Given the magnitude and the angle, the **Cartesian components** are the magnitude times the cosine and the magnitude times the sine of the angle, and the formulas hold for any angle. The angle of $150^\circ$ lies in the second quadrant, where the cosine is negative and the sine positive; hence $v_x$ is negative and $v_y$ positive, and the velocity is $\vec{v} = (-17.3\,\hat{\imath} + 10.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

For the inverse problem the magnitude is obtained with the Pythagorean theorem and equals $10\,\mathrm{m}$. The two components of the displacement are negative, so the vector lies in the third quadrant, as the figure shows. The arctangent returns an angle between $-90^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, namely the angle of the opposite vector $(6.0,\ 8.0)\,\mathrm{m}$; therefore the **angle of the displacement** is obtained by adding $180^\circ$ and equals $233.1^\circ$ counterclockwise, that is, $126.9^\circ$ clockwise.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A hiker walks $3.0\,\mathrm{km}$ east and then $4.0\,\mathrm{km}$ northeast. Determine the magnitude and direction of the total displacement, taking the $x$-axis toward east and the $y$-axis toward north.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,3,0,d₁;3,0,5.828,2.828,d₂;0,0,5.828,2.828,R" etichette="0.9,0.18,θ" xmin=-0.6 xmax=6.6 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.6 uguale=1 titolo="The total displacement $\vec{R}$ goes from the tail of $\vec{d}_1$ to the tip of $\vec{d}_2$ (lengths in $\mathrm{km}$)"}

$$
\vec{R} = \vec{d}_1 + \vec{d}_2
$$
$$
\vec{d}_1 = (3.0,\ 0)\,\mathrm{km},
\qquad
\vec{d}_2 = (4.0\cos 45^\circ,\ 4.0\sin 45^\circ)\,\mathrm{km} = (2.83,\ 2.83)\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
\vec{R} = (3.0 + 2.83,\ 0 + 2.83)\,\mathrm{km} = (5.83,\ 2.83)\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{5.83^2 + 2.83^2}\,\mathrm{km} \approx 6.5\,\mathrm{km},
\qquad
\theta = \arctan\frac{2.83}{5.83} \approx 25.9^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagram shows the two displacements arranged with the tip-to-tail rule: the second starts where the first ends, and the total displacement is their **vector sum**. The northeast direction forms $45^\circ$ with east, so the components of the second displacement are equal and are $4.0 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{km} \approx 2.83\,\mathrm{km}$. The **operations in components** reduce the sum to two additions of numbers, one per axis. The magnitude of the sum is obtained with the Pythagorean theorem; since $R_x > 0$, the arctangent gives the angle directly. The hiker is about $6.5\,\mathrm{km}$ from the starting point, in the direction $25.9^\circ$ north of east. As a check, $6.5\,\mathrm{km}$ is less than the distance walked, $7.0\,\mathrm{km}$, as it must be for two non-aligned displacements.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A robot in a warehouse makes three successive displacements: $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at $0^\circ$, $8.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at $120^\circ$ and $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at $210^\circ$, with the angles measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-semiaxis. Determine the total displacement and the displacement that brings the robot back to the starting point.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,5,0,d₁;5,0,1,6.928,d₂;1,6.928,-2.464,4.928,d₃;0,0,-2.464,4.928,R" xmin=-3.4 xmax=6 ymin=-0.8 ymax=7.6 uguale=1 titolo="The three displacements arranged tip to tail and the total displacement $\vec{R}$ (lengths in meters)"}

$$
\vec{R} = \vec{d}_1 + \vec{d}_2 + \vec{d}_3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= 5.0\cos 0^\circ + 8.0\cos 120^\circ + 4.0\cos 210^\circ \\[6pt]
&= (5.0 - 4.00 - 3.46)\,\mathrm{m} = -2.46\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_y &= 5.0\sin 0^\circ + 8.0\sin 120^\circ + 4.0\sin 210^\circ \\[6pt]
&= (0 + 6.93 - 2.00)\,\mathrm{m} = 4.93\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{(-2.46)^2 + 4.93^2}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 5.5\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\theta = \arctan\frac{4.93}{-2.46} + 180^\circ \approx 116.6^\circ
$$
$$
-\vec{R} = (2.46\,\hat{\imath} - 4.93\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\theta' = 116.6^\circ + 180^\circ = 296.6^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each displacement is resolved with the formulas $d_x = d\cos\theta$ and $d_y = d\sin\theta$, and the $x$ component of the sum is the sum of the $x$ components, and likewise for $y$. The cosines of $120^\circ$ and $210^\circ$ are negative, so the second and third displacements move the robot back toward the left, and the component $R_x$ turns out negative. The vector $\vec{R}$ has $R_x < 0$ and $R_y > 0$, therefore it lies in the second quadrant, and $180^\circ$ is added to the angle given by the arctangent, $-63.4^\circ$. The displacement that brings the robot back to the start cancels $\vec{R}$, so it is its **opposite**: it has the same magnitude, $5.5\,\mathrm{m}$, and direction $296.6^\circ$, that is, $63.4^\circ$ below the positive $x$-semiaxis.

</div>
