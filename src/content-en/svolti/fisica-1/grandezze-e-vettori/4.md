## Exercise 1

Two perpendicular forces act on a point particle: $\vec{F}_1$ of magnitude $30\,\mathrm{N}$ along the positive $x$-semiaxis and $\vec{F}_2$ of magnitude $40\,\mathrm{N}$ along the positive $y$-semiaxis. Determine the magnitude and direction of the resultant.

$$
\vec{R} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = (30\,\hat{\imath} + 40\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2} = \sqrt{30^2 + 40^2}\,\mathrm{N} = \sqrt{2500}\,\mathrm{N} = 50\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\theta = \arctan\frac{F_2}{F_1} = \arctan\frac{40}{30} \approx 53.1^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **resultant** of several forces applied to the same point is their vector sum. The two forces are already resolved along the axes, so the components of the resultant are $30\,\mathrm{N}$ and $40\,\mathrm{N}$. Since the forces are perpendicular, the magnitude of the resultant is the hypotenuse of the right triangle that has the two forces as legs, that is, $50\,\mathrm{N}$. The angle with the $x$-axis has tangent $\dfrac{4}{3}$ and is about $53.1^\circ$. As a check, the magnitude $50\,\mathrm{N}$ lies between the difference and the sum of the magnitudes, $10\,\mathrm{N}$ and $70\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ring is pulled by three horizontal ropes with forces $\vec{F}_1$ of $50\,\mathrm{N}$ at $30^\circ$, $\vec{F}_2$ of $40\,\mathrm{N}$ at $135^\circ$ and $\vec{F}_3$ of $30\,\mathrm{N}$ at $270^\circ$, with the angles measured counterclockwise from the $x$-axis. Determine the resultant and the force $\vec{F}_{\text{eq}}$ which, added to the three, keeps the ring in equilibrium.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,4.33,2.5,F₁;0,0,-2.828,2.828,F₂;0,0,0,-3,F₃;0,0,1.502,2.328,R" xmin=-3.4 xmax=5 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.4 uguale=1 titolo="The three forces applied to the ring and their resultant $\vec{R}$ (scale: one unit per $10\,\mathrm{N}$)"}

$$
\vec{R} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3,
\qquad
\vec{R} + \vec{F}_{\text{eq}} = \vec{0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{F}_{\text{eq}} = -\vec{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= 50\cos 30^\circ + 40\cos 135^\circ + 30\cos 270^\circ \\[6pt]
&= (43.30 - 28.28 + 0)\,\mathrm{N} = 15.02\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_y &= 50\sin 30^\circ + 40\sin 135^\circ + 30\sin 270^\circ \\[6pt]
&= (25.00 + 28.28 - 30.00)\,\mathrm{N} = 23.28\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{15.02^2 + 23.28^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 27.7\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
\theta_R = \arctan\frac{23.28}{15.02} \approx 57.2^\circ
$$
$$
\vec{F}_{\text{eq}} = (-15.0\,\hat{\imath} - 23.3\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
F_{\text{eq}} = 27.7\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
\theta_{\text{eq}} = 57.2^\circ + 180^\circ = 237.2^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each force is resolved along the axes with $F_x = F\cos\theta$ and $F_y = F\sin\theta$, and the components are added separately. The force $\vec{F}_3$ at $270^\circ$ points downward, so it has zero $x$ component and $y$ component equal to $-30\,\mathrm{N}$. The resultant has both components positive, therefore it lies in the first quadrant and the arctangent gives its angle directly. The ring is in equilibrium when the sum of *all* the applied forces is zero, so the force to be added is the opposite of the resultant: the **equilibrant force** has magnitude $27.7\,\mathrm{N}$ and direction $237.2^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two forces of equal magnitude $F$ are applied to the same point and form an angle $\varphi$ with each other. Express the magnitude of the resultant as a function of $F$ and $\varphi$, and determine the angle $\varphi$ for which the resultant has magnitude $F$.

$$
\vec{F}_1 = (F,\ 0),
\qquad
\vec{F}_2 = (F\cos\varphi,\ F\sin\varphi)
$$
$$
\vec{R} = \big( F(1 + \cos\varphi),\ F\sin\varphi \big)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2 &= F^2 (1 + \cos\varphi)^2 + F^2 \sin^2\varphi \\[6pt]
&= F^2 \left( 1 + 2\cos\varphi + \cos^2\varphi + \sin^2\varphi \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2F^2 (1 + \cos\varphi)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
R = F\sqrt{2(1 + \cos\varphi)}
$$
$$
R = F
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2(1 + \cos\varphi) = 1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\cos\varphi = -\frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\varphi = 120^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The choice of axes is free, so it is convenient to orient the $x$-axis along the first force; the second force then forms the angle $\varphi$ with the $x$-axis, and its components are $F\cos\varphi$ and $F\sin\varphi$. Adding the components and applying the Pythagorean theorem gives $R^2$, which simplifies with the identity $\cos^2\varphi + \sin^2\varphi = 1$. The result contains the known cases: for $\varphi = 0^\circ$ the forces have the same orientation and $R = 2F$, for $\varphi = 180^\circ$ they are opposite and $R = 0$. Imposing $R = F$ gives $\cos\varphi = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, and since the angle between two vectors lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, the only solution is $\varphi = 120^\circ$. Therefore two equal forces forming an angle of $120^\circ$ have a **resultant equal** to each of them.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Two tugboats tow a ship with two horizontal ropes. The first exerts a force $\vec{F}_1$ of $60\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $30^\circ$ with respect to the $x$-axis, which is the direction in which the ship must advance. Determine the magnitude and direction of the force $\vec{F}_2$ of the second tugboat so that the resultant of the two forces is $100\,\mathrm{kN}$ along the $x$-axis.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,5.196,3,F₁;0,0,4.804,-3,F₂;0,0,10,0,R" segmenti="5.196,3,10,0;4.804,-3,10,0" xmin=-0.6 xmax=10.6 ymin=-3.6 ymax=3.6 uguale=1 titolo="The force $\vec{F}_2 = \vec{R} - \vec{F}_1$ completes the parallelogram that has $\vec{R}$ as its diagonal (scale: one unit per $10\,\mathrm{kN}$)"}

$$
\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = \vec{R}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{F}_2 = \vec{R} - \vec{F}_1
$$
$$
\vec{F}_1 = (60\cos 30^\circ,\ 60\sin 30^\circ)\,\mathrm{kN} = (51.96,\ 30.00)\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\vec{F}_2 = (100 - 51.96,\ 0 - 30.00)\,\mathrm{kN} = (48.04,\ -30.00)\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
F_2 = \sqrt{48.04^2 + 30.00^2}\,\mathrm{kN} \approx 56.6\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad
\theta_2 = \arctan\frac{-30.00}{48.04} \approx -32.0^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown force is obtained from the vector equation $\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = \vec{R}$ by subtracting $\vec{F}_1$ from both sides, so it is the **difference** $\vec{R} - \vec{F}_1$. In components the vector equation is equivalent to two scalar equations, one per axis. The $y$ component of the resultant is zero, therefore the $y$ component of $\vec{F}_2$ must cancel that of $\vec{F}_1$ and equals $-30.00\,\mathrm{kN}$. The $x$ component is positive, so $\vec{F}_2$ lies in the fourth quadrant and the arctangent gives the angle directly. Therefore the **second tugboat** must pull with about $56.6\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $32.0^\circ$ below the $x$-axis. The force $\vec{F}_2$ turns out smaller than $\vec{F}_1$ and more inclined, because it must provide the same transverse component with a smaller component along $x$.

</div>
