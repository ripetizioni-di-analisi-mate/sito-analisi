## Exercise 1

A body of mass $m = 0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to an ideal spring of constant $k = 80\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and is subject to a viscous force $-b\vec{v}$ with $b = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. Determine the natural angular frequency, the damping constant, the period of the damped oscillations, the quality factor, the fraction of the initial amplitude that remains after $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$, and the time in which the amplitude is halved.

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{80}{0.20}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{b}{2m} = \frac{0.40}{0.40}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$
$$
\omega_s = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2} = \sqrt{399}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 19.97\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
T_s = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_s} = 0.315\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
Q = \frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma} = 10
$$
$$
\frac{A(2.0\,\mathrm{s})}{A_0} = e^{-\gamma t} = e^{-2.0} = 0.135
$$
$$
e^{-\gamma t_{\mathrm{d}}} = \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t_{\mathrm{d}} = \frac{\ln 2}{\gamma} = 0.693\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Newton's second law gives $m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0$, which is the equation of the **damped oscillator** with $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ and $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m}$. Since $\gamma < \omega_0$, the damping is weak and the body oscillates with angular frequency $\omega_s$ and amplitude $A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$. With $\gamma$ much smaller than $\omega_0$ the damped period differs from the natural one, $0.314\,\mathrm{s}$, only in the third significant figure. The fraction of amplitude remaining is $e^{-\gamma t}$, about $13.5\%$ after $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. The halving time $t_{\mathrm{d}}$ is found by setting $e^{-\gamma t}$ equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and taking logarithms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An oscillator of mass $m = 0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ is weakly damped and oscillates with period $0.50\,\mathrm{s}$. After $5$ complete oscillations its amplitude has gone from $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine the damping constant, the coefficient $b$ of the viscous force, and the fraction of the initial energy that remains after the $5$ oscillations.

$$
\Delta t = 5 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{s} = 2.5\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\frac{A_2}{A_1} = e^{-\gamma\,\Delta t}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\gamma = \frac{1}{\Delta t}\ln\frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{1}{2.5}\ln\frac{10}{6.0}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 0.204\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$
$$
b = 2m\gamma = 2 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 0.204\,\mathrm{kg/s} = 0.123\,\mathrm{kg/s}
$$
$$
\frac{E_2}{E_1} = \frac{A_2^2}{A_1^2} = 0.60^2 = 0.36
$$

::grafico{f="10*exp(-0.2043*x)*cos(4*pi*x);10*exp(-0.2043*x);-10*exp(-0.2043*x)" punti="0,10,pieno;2.5,6,pieno" xmin=-0.2 xmax=5.4 ymin=-12 ymax=12 nomi="t,x" titolo="With $x$ in centimeters and $t$ in seconds: the amplitude goes from $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ to $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in $2.5\,\mathrm{s}$, and the oscillations stay between the curves $\pm 10\,e^{-\gamma t}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the weak damping regime the amplitude decreases as $A_0 e^{-\gamma t}$, so the ratio of two amplitudes separated by $\Delta t$ is $e^{-\gamma\,\Delta t}$, and taking logarithms gives $\gamma$. The $5$ oscillations last $2.5\,\mathrm{s}$. From the definition $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m}$ we obtain the coefficient $b$. The damping is weak, since $\gamma = 0.204\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ is much smaller than the angular frequency $\dfrac{2\pi}{0.50\,\mathrm{s}} = 12.6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$; therefore the energy is, to a good approximation, that of a harmonic oscillator of amplitude $A(t)$, proportional to the square of the amplitude, and $36\%$ of it remains.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to an ideal spring of constant $k = 50\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and immersed in a viscous fluid. Determine the value $b_c$ of the damping coefficient that gives critical damping. With $b = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ then determine the regime, the period of the oscillations and the fraction of amplitude that remains after one period.

$$
\gamma = \omega_0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{b_c}{2m} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_c = 2\sqrt{km} = 2\sqrt{50 \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{kg/s} = 10\,\mathrm{kg/s}
$$
$$
\omega_0 = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{6.0}{2 \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 6.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} < \omega_0
$$
$$
\omega_s = \sqrt{10^2 - 6.0^2}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
T_s = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_s} = 0.785\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
e^{-\gamma T_s} = e^{-6.0 \cdot 0.785} = e^{-4.71} = 0.0090
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The damping is critical when $\gamma = \omega_0$; substituting $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m}$ and $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ we obtain $b_c = 2\sqrt{km}$. With $b = 6.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, smaller than $b_c$, we have $\gamma < \omega_0$, so the regime is **weak damping** and the body oscillates. The angular frequency $\omega_s$ is, however, noticeably smaller than $\omega_0$, because $\gamma$ is of the same order of magnitude as $\omega_0$. After one period the amplitude has been reduced to less than $1\%$ of its initial value, therefore the body in practice performs only one appreciable oscillation.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A body of mass $m = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring of constant $k = 16\,\mathrm{N/m}$, is subject to a viscous force with $b = 8.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. At the instant $t = 0$ the body is at rest at the position $x_0 = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$. Verify that the damping is critical, write the equation of motion and compute the position at the instant $t = 0.50\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{16}{1.0}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{8.0}{2 \cdot 1.0}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = \omega_0
$$
$$
x(t) = (C_1 + C_2 t)\,e^{-4t},
\qquad
\dot{x}(t) = \bigl(C_2 - 4\,(C_1 + C_2 t)\bigr)\,e^{-4t}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x(0) = C_1 = 0.10 \\[4pt]
\dot{x}(0) = C_2 - 4\,C_1 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
C_1 = 0.10\,\mathrm{m},
\quad
C_2 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
x(t) = 0.10\,(1 + 4.0\,t)\,e^{-4.0\,t}\ \mathrm{m}
$$
$$
x(0.50\,\mathrm{s}) = 0.10 \cdot 3.0 \cdot e^{-2.0}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.0406\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the given data we have $\gamma = \omega_0$, so the damping is **critical** and the characteristic equation $r^2 + 8r + 16 = 0$ has the double root $r = -4$. The general solution is therefore $(C_1 + C_2 t)\,e^{-4t}$, and the constants are determined by imposing the two initial conditions *together*, position $x_0$ and zero velocity. After $0.50\,\mathrm{s}$ the body is $4.06\,\mathrm{cm}$ from equilibrium, without ever having passed it. Indeed $x(t) > 0$ for every $t \geq 0$, since both factors $1 + 4t$ and $e^{-4t}$ are positive.

</div>
