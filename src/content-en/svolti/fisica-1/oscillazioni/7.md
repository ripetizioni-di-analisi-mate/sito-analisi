## Exercise 1

A body of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, attached to an ideal spring of constant $k = 50\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and subject to a viscous force with $b = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, is driven by the force $F(t) = F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ with $F_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{N}$ and $\Omega = 8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Determine the amplitude and the phase lag of the steady state.

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = 10\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{b}{2m} = 1.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},
\qquad
\frac{F_0}{m} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
A = \frac{F_0}{m\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\Omega^2}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{4.0}{\sqrt{(100 - 64)^2 + 4 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 64}}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4.0}{\sqrt{1296 + 256}}\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{4.0}{39.4}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.102\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\tan\delta = \frac{2\gamma\Omega}{\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2} = \frac{16}{36} = 0.444,
\qquad
\delta = 0.418\,\mathrm{rad}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Newton's second law gives the equation of the forced oscillator, which divided by $m$ has the coefficients $2\gamma$ and $\omega_0^2$. Damping is present, so the transient dies out and the **steady state** remains, an oscillation at the angular frequency of the driving force with amplitude and phase lag given by the formulas of the theorem. The angular frequency $\Omega$ is smaller than $\omega_0$, therefore $\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2 > 0$, the tangent is positive and the phase lag lies in $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$; hence the displacement lags behind the force by $0.418\,\mathrm{rad}$. The amplitude is about $2.5$ times the static displacement $\dfrac{F_0}{k} = 0.040\,\mathrm{m}$, because $\Omega$ is already close to the natural angular frequency.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the oscillator of the previous exercise ($m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$, $k = 50\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $b = 1.0\,\mathrm{kg/s}$, $F_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{N}$) determine the resonant angular frequency, the maximum amplitude and the ratio of the maximum amplitude to the static displacement $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$.

$$
\gamma = 1.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} < \frac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}} = 7.07\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$
$$
\Omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2} = \sqrt{100 - 2}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 9.90\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
A_{\max} = \frac{F_0}{2m\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}} = \frac{2.0}{2 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 1.0 \cdot \sqrt{99}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.201\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{A_{\max}}{\dfrac{F_0}{k}} = \frac{0.201}{0.040} = 5.0,
\qquad
Q = \frac{\omega_0}{2\gamma} = 5.0
$$

::grafico{f="400/sqrt((100-x^2)^2+4*x^2)" punti="0,4,pieno;8,10.15,pieno;9.9,20.1,pieno" asintoti="x=10" xmin=-0.5 xmax=16 ymin=-3 ymax=24 nomi="Ω,A" titolo="Steady-state amplitude in centimeters, as a function of $\Omega$ in $\mathrm{rad/s}$: $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ as $\Omega \to 0$, $10.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ for $\Omega = 8.0$, maximum of $20.1\,\mathrm{cm}$ at $\Omega_r = 9.90$, close to $\omega_0 = 10$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The condition $\gamma < \dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$ is satisfied, so the amplitude of the steady state has a maximum and the theorem on **resonance** applies. The resonant angular frequency is slightly smaller than $\omega_0$, because the damping is small compared with $\omega_0$. At resonance the amplitude is about twice that found for $\Omega = 8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and five times the static displacement. The ratio coincides with the quality factor, as predicted by the approximation $A_{\max} \approx Q\,\dfrac{F_0}{k}$, valid for small damping.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to an ideal spring of constant $k = 800\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and is driven by a force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ with $F_0 = 4.0\,\mathrm{N}$; damping is negligible. Determine the angular frequencies of the driving force for which the particular solution has amplitude $1.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{800}{2.0}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
A = \frac{F_0}{m\,|\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2|}
$$
$$
\frac{4.0}{2.0 \cdot |400 - \Omega^2|} = 0.010
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|400 - \Omega^2| = 200
$$
$$
400 - \Omega^2 = 200
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Omega = \sqrt{200}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 14.1\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
400 - \Omega^2 = -200
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Omega = \sqrt{600}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 24.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With negligible damping we set $\gamma = 0$ in the steady-state formula, and for $\Omega \neq \omega_0$ the amplitude of the particular solution is $\dfrac{F_0}{m\,|\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2|}$. Requiring it to equal $0.010\,\mathrm{m}$ gives an equation with an absolute value, which splits into the two cases $400 - \Omega^2 = \pm 200$. Both cases give an admissible positive angular frequency, so there are **two** solutions, one smaller and one larger than $\omega_0$. In the first case the displacement is in phase with the force, in the second it is in antiphase, because $\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2$ changes sign. As a check, $\dfrac{4.0}{2.0 \cdot 200}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.010\,\mathrm{m}$ in both cases.

</div>
