## Exercise 1

A uniform rigid rod of mass $m$ and length $L = 0.90\,\mathrm{m}$ is suspended by one end from a frictionless horizontal pivot and oscillates with small amplitude in a vertical plane. The moment of inertia of the rod about a perpendicular axis through its center is $\dfrac{1}{12}mL^2$. Determine the period and the equivalent length.

$$
I = \frac{1}{12}mL^2 + m\left( \frac{L}{2} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{3}mL^2,
\qquad
d = \frac{L}{2}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\dfrac{1}{3}mL^2}{mg\,\dfrac{L}{2}}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2L}{3g}}
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 0.90}{3 \cdot 9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.55\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
\ell_r = \frac{I}{md} = \frac{2L}{3} = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates about a fixed axis that does not pass through its center of mass, so it is a **physical pendulum**, and for small oscillations the period is $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{mgd}}$. The moment of inertia about the pivot is obtained with the parallel-axis theorem, since the axis of the pivot is parallel to the axis through the center and is at distance $\dfrac{L}{2}$ from it. The mass cancels, so the period depends only on the length of the rod. The equivalent length $\dfrac{2L}{3}$ is the length of the simple pendulum with the same period; it is smaller than $L$ because the mass of the rod is also distributed close to the pivot.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform disk of mass $m$ and radius $R = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ hangs from a horizontal pivot through a point on its rim, perpendicular to the disk, and oscillates with small amplitude in its own plane. The moment of inertia of the disk about the perpendicular axis through its center is $\dfrac{1}{2}mR^2$. Determine the period and the equivalent length.

$$
I = \frac{1}{2}mR^2 + mR^2 = \frac{3}{2}mR^2,
\qquad
d = R
$$
$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgd}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{3R}{2g}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{3 \cdot 0.20}{2 \cdot 9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.10\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\ell_r = \frac{I}{md} = \frac{3R}{2} = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center of mass of the disk is its center, which is at distance $R$ from the pivot; therefore the disk is a physical pendulum with $d = R$. The **parallel-axis theorem** adds $mR^2$ to the moment of inertia about the center. Substituting into the period formula the mass cancels, and what remains is a period equal to that of a simple pendulum of length $\dfrac{3R}{2}$. As an order-of-magnitude check, a simple pendulum of length $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ has period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{0.30}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.10\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform rigid rod of mass $m$ and length $L = 1.00\,\mathrm{m}$ is suspended from a horizontal pivot through a point at distance $d$ from its center, with $0 < d \leq \dfrac{L}{2}$. The moment of inertia about the axis through the center is $\dfrac{1}{12}mL^2$. Determine the value of $d$ for which the period of small oscillations is minimum, and the minimum period.

$$
T(d) = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\dfrac{1}{12}mL^2 + md^2}{mgd}} = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{g}}\sqrt{\frac{L^2}{12\,d} + d}
$$
$$
h(d) = \frac{L^2}{12\,d} + d,
\qquad
h'(d) = -\frac{L^2}{12\,d^2} + 1 = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d^* = \frac{L}{\sqrt{12}} = 0.289\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
h(d^*) = 2d^* = \frac{L}{\sqrt{3}},
\qquad
T_{\min} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2d^*}{g}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.577}{9.81}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.52\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a pivot at distance $d$ from the center the parallel-axis theorem gives $I = \dfrac{1}{12}mL^2 + md^2$, and the formula of the physical pendulum gives the period as a function of $d$. The period is an increasing function of $h(d)$, so it is minimum where $h$ is minimum. The derivative $h'(d)$ is negative for $d < d^*$ and positive for $d > d^*$, therefore $d^*$ is the **minimum point**, and it belongs to the admissible interval because $0.289 < 0.50$. At $d^*$ the two terms of $h$ are equal, so $h(d^*) = 2d^*$. With the rod suspended from one end, that is, for $d = \dfrac{L}{2}$, the period is $1.64\,\mathrm{s}$, greater than the minimum; as $d \to 0^+$ the period tends to $+\infty$, because the lever arm of the weight becomes smaller and smaller.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A uniform disk of mass $m = 2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and radius $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ hangs by its center from a vertical wire and performs torsional oscillations about the axis of the wire with period $T = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}$. The moment of inertia of the disk about that axis is $\dfrac{1}{2}mR^2$. Determine the torsion constant of the wire.

$$
I\ddot{\theta} = -\kappa\,\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{\kappa}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\kappa = \frac{4\pi^2 I}{T^2}
$$
$$
I = \frac{1}{2}mR^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 0.10^2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2} = 0.010\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}
$$
$$
\kappa = \frac{4\pi^2 \cdot 0.010}{2.0^2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad} = 0.0987\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire exerts on the disk a restoring torque proportional to the angle of twist, $-\kappa\theta$. The rotational equation of motion for rotation about the axis of the wire gives $I\ddot{\theta} = -\kappa\theta$, which is the equation of harmonic motion with $\omega^2 = \dfrac{\kappa}{I}$; hence the system is a **torsion pendulum**. From the period formula we isolate $\kappa$. The measured period and the known moment of inertia of the disk thus determine the constant of the wire, and with the same wire the moments of inertia of other bodies can then be measured.

</div>
