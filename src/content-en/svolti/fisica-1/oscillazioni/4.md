## Exercise 1

A body of mass $m = 0.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ oscillates on a smooth horizontal plane attached to an ideal spring of constant $k = 160\,\mathrm{N/m}$, with amplitude $A = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine the mechanical energy and, at the position $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the velocity, the kinetic energy and the potential energy.

$$
E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 160 \cdot 0.050^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.20\,\mathrm{J}
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{160}{0.40}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 20\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
|v| = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = 20\sqrt{0.050^2 - 0.030^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20 \cdot 0.040\,\mathrm{m/s} = 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 160 \cdot 0.030^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.072\,\mathrm{J},
\qquad
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.40 \cdot 0.80^2\,\mathrm{J} = 0.128\,\mathrm{J}
$$

::grafico{f="8*x^2;200" punti="-5,200,pieno,−A;5,200,pieno,A;3,72,pieno" segmenti="3,0,3,72" vettori="3,72,3,200" etichette="3.25,140,K = 128 mJ;3.25,30,U = 72 mJ" xmin=-6.5 xmax=6.5 ymin=-30 ymax=270 nomi="x,E" titolo="With $x$ in centimeters and $E$ in millijoules: potential energy $U = \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$ and mechanical energy $E = 200\,\mathrm{mJ}$; at $x = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ the kinetic energy is the difference $E - U$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane is smooth, so the only force that does work is the elastic force, which is conservative, and the **mechanical energy** is conserved. At the extremes the velocity is zero, therefore all the energy is potential and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}kA^2$. The velocity at the given position is obtained from the relation between velocity and position in harmonic motion, and with it the kinetic energy is computed. As a check, $K + U = 0.128\,\mathrm{J} + 0.072\,\mathrm{J} = 0.20\,\mathrm{J}$, which is the mechanical energy. The same result is also obtained starting from the energy, since $K = E - U$ gives $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.128\,\mathrm{J}$ and hence $v = 0.80\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A harmonic oscillator has amplitude $A = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and period $T = 0.80\,\mathrm{s}$, and at the instant $t = 0$ it is at rest at $x = A$. Determine the positions where the kinetic energy equals the potential energy, the first instant at which this happens, and the positions where the kinetic energy is three times the potential energy.

$$
K = U
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U = \frac{E}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{4}kA^2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x = \pm\frac{A}{\sqrt{2}} = \pm 0.0707\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A\cos(\omega t) = \frac{A}{\sqrt{2}},
\qquad
\omega t_1 = \frac{\pi}{4}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_1 = \frac{\pi}{4\omega} = \frac{T}{8} = 0.10\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
K = 3U
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U = \frac{E}{4}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{8}kA^2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x = \pm\frac{A}{2} = \pm 0.050\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mechanical energy $E = \dfrac{1}{2}kA^2$ is constant and $K + U = E$. If $K = U$, each of the two energies is half of $E$, so $\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}kA^2$, and the positions are $\pm\dfrac{A}{\sqrt{2}}$. The body starts at rest from the positive extreme, so the equation of motion is $A\cos(\omega t)$, and the first positive solution of $\cos(\omega t) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ is $\omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, that is, **one eighth of the period**. If $K = 3U$, the potential energy is one quarter of $E$, therefore $x^2 = \dfrac{A^2}{4}$. None of the results depends on the mass or on the spring constant, because $K$ and $U$ are both fractions of $\dfrac{1}{2}kA^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a smooth horizontal plane with speed $v_0 = 2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and hits an ideal spring of constant $k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, fixed to a wall. Determine the maximum compression of the spring and the duration of the contact between the block and the spring.

$$
\frac{1}{2}mv_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}kA^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A = v_0\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2.0\sqrt{\frac{0.50}{200}}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0 \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{m} = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\Delta t = \frac{T}{2} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = \pi \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{s} = 0.157\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During the contact the weight, the normal force and the elastic force act on the block; the plane is smooth, so the only force that does work is the elastic force and the mechanical energy is conserved. At maximum compression the block is at rest, therefore the initial kinetic energy has become entirely elastic potential energy. While in contact with the spring, the block is a mass-spring oscillator that starts from the equilibrium position with velocity $v_0$. The block compresses the spring, returns to the rest position and leaves it with velocity $v_0$ in the opposite direction, so it performs **half an oscillation** and the contact lasts $\dfrac{T}{2}$. The duration does not depend on $v_0$, because of the isochronism of the oscillator. As a dimensional check, $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{N/m}}} = \sqrt{\mathrm{s^2}} = \mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A particle of mass $m = 0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves along the half-axis $x > 0$ subject to a conservative force with potential energy
$$
U(x) = \frac{\alpha}{x} + \beta x ,
\qquad
\alpha = 2.0\,\mathrm{J\cdot m},
\quad
\beta = 8.0\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
Determine the position of stable equilibrium and the period of small oscillations about it.

$$
U'(x) = -\frac{\alpha}{x^2} + \beta = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x_e = \sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}} = \sqrt{\frac{2.0}{8.0}}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
U''(x) = \frac{2\alpha}{x^3},
\qquad
U''(x_e) = \frac{2 \cdot 2.0}{0.50^3}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 32\,\mathrm{N/m} > 0
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{U''(x_e)}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{32}{0.50}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = 8.0\,\mathrm{rad/s},
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 0.785\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equilibrium positions are the points where the force $F = -U'(x)$ is zero, so we solve $U'(x) = 0$ on the positive half-axis, which has the unique solution $x_e = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}$. The second derivative at $x_e$ is positive, therefore $x_e$ is a minimum point of $U$ and the equilibrium is stable. By the theorem on **small oscillations** about a minimum, the number $U''(x_e)$ plays the role of the spring constant, and the angular frequency is $\sqrt{\dfrac{U''(x_e)}{m}}$. The units are consistent, since $\alpha$ is measured in $\mathrm{J\cdot m}$ and $\dfrac{2\alpha}{x^3}$ is measured in $\dfrac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{m^2}} = \mathrm{N/m}$.

</div>
