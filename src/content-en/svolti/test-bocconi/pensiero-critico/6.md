## Exercise 1

A storage unit contains six numbered boxes. Boxes 1, 2 and 3 are empty. Boxes 4 and 5 hold exactly one parcel each. Box 6 holds at most one parcel. Evaluate ‘The storage unit contains at least two parcels’, ‘The storage unit contains four parcels’ and ‘The storage unit contains exactly three parcels’.

$$
\text{Total parcels}=2+p_6,\qquad p_6\in\{0,1\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Boxes 4 and 5 ensure two parcels, so the first statement is true. Even with a parcel in box 6 the total is only three, so the second statement is false. If box 6 is empty, the total is two and the third statement is false. If it contains a parcel, the total is three and the third statement is true. Both cases fit the facts, so the third claim cannot be determined.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A center issues a kit upon each new registration. All the kits issued go to new registrants. At least ten people registered on Monday. On Tuesday five more registered than on Monday. Over the two days, at most twenty-nine kits were issued. Evaluate ‘Exactly seventeen kits were issued on Tuesday’.

Let $l$ be the number of people who registered on Monday. Since each new registrant receives one kit, the two-day total is

$$
\begin{aligned}
l+(l+5)&=2l+5\\[6pt]
2l+5&\leq29\\[6pt]
10\leq l&\leq12.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $l=12$, $12+5=17$ kits were issued on Tuesday and the total is 29. With $l=11$, $11+5=16$ kits were issued on Tuesday and the total is 27. Both values of $l$ satisfy the bounds and the other conditions. The first case supports the statement and the second makes it false, so the judgment is **Cannot be determined**.

</div>
