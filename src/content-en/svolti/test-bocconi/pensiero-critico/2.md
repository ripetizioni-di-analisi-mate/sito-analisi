## Exercise 1

Every plant in a nursery with spotted leaves has been moved into the greenhouse. None of the plants moved into the greenhouse is due to go on sale tomorrow. A plant marked R has spotted leaves. Determine whether at least one plant is definitely excluded from tomorrow's sale and whether every plant excluded from the sale has spotted leaves.

If $M$ is the set of plants with spotted leaves, $S$ the set of those moved into the greenhouse and $V$ the set for sale tomorrow, then

$$
R\in M,\qquad M\subseteq S,\qquad S\cap V=\varnothing.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since R belongs to $M$ and $M$ is contained in $S$, R is among the plants moved into the greenhouse. Because $S$ and $V$ are disjoint, R is definitely excluded from the sale. The second claim cannot be determined. A nursery where the only plants excluded from the sale have spotted leaves fits the facts. A healthy plant left out of the sale for an unspecified reason would also fit both rules and would make the second claim false.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An observatory has three telescopes. Each telescope is calibrated in the morning, in the afternoon or both. Exactly two are calibrated in the morning and exactly two in the afternoon. Determine how many are calibrated at both times and whether a given telescope calibrated in the morning must also be calibrated in the afternoon.

Let $M$ be the set of telescopes calibrated in the morning and $P$ the set calibrated in the afternoon.

$$
|M\cap P|=|M|+|P|-|M\cup P|=2+2-3=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The union $M\cup P$ contains all three telescopes because every telescope is calibrated at least once. When the two counts are added, a telescope calibrated at both times is counted twice. The sum is four, so exactly one telescope is counted twice. There are two telescopes in the morning. Only one of them reappears in the afternoon. For a specific morning telescope, the answer depends on which one is in the intersection.

</div>
