## Exercise 1

Two workshops check all vehicles they receive during one week. Workshop A reports 36 favorable results out of 90 checks. Workshop B reports 49 out of 140. Determine which workshop has the higher proportion and whether the figures show that changing workshops improves the result for a particular vehicle.

$$
\frac{36}{90}=\frac{2}{5}=40\%,\qquad \frac{49}{140}=\frac{7}{20}=35\%.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The totals differ in size, so the comparison requires dividing each number of favorable results by its own total. A has the higher proportion. The data describe two different groups of vehicles. If A had received vehicles in better condition, the observed difference would still be consistent with identical procedures. Since the passage does not isolate the effect of the workshop on individual vehicles, the improvement after switching cannot be determined.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An office logs incoming calls. Of Monday's 50 calls, 20 were received in the morning time band. Tuesday's summary counts 70 calls in total and states that 30 came before the lunch break, all in the morning time band. The lunch break begins before the end of the morning band. Can one conclude that Tuesday had a higher proportion of morning calls than Monday? Can Tuesday's exact proportion be found?

$$
\frac{20}{50}=\frac{2}{5}=40\%,\qquad \frac{30}{70}=\frac{3}{7}>\frac{2}{5}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second fraction is a *lower bound*. Tuesday had at least thirty calls in the morning band. Because $3\cdot5>2\cdot7$, even that lower bound exceeds Monday's proportion. The proportion is therefore definitely higher. On Tuesday, from the start of the break to the end of the morning band, the office could have received no calls or, for example, five. The proportions $\dfrac{30}{70}$ and $\dfrac{35}{70}$ are both possible, so the exact proportion cannot be determined.

</div>
