## Exercise 1

An exhibition displays only works on paper or on canvas. Every work on paper is framed. There is at least one work on canvas, and exactly two works are unframed. Evaluate I ‘The unframed works are on canvas’, II ‘Every work on canvas is unframed’, and III ‘There are at least two works on canvas’. Identify which are necessarily true.

| Proposition | Judgment |
| --- | --- |
| I | True |
| II | Cannot be determined |
| III | True |

<div class="spiegazione">

One of the two unframed works cannot be on paper, because every work on paper is framed. Since the passage gives only two media, it must be on canvas. The same reasoning applies to the other unframed work, so I is true and the two works on canvas establish III. II holds in an exhibition where those two works are the only works on canvas. A third, framed work on canvas would meet the same premises and make II false. Only **I and III** are therefore necessarily true.

</div>

## Exercise 2

If an operator has qualification Q, they can handle category P cases. Only operators on the day shift can handle P cases. Eli works only the day shift and Fede only the night shift. Evaluate I ‘Eli can handle P cases’ and II ‘Fede has qualification Q’.

$$
\text{Qualification Q}\implies \text{Handling P}\implies \text{Day shift}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If Fede had qualification Q, Fede could handle P cases and would have to work the day shift. Since Fede works only the night shift, qualification Q is ruled out. II is therefore false. Eli meets the shift requirement. A situation in which Eli has Q and handles P fits the rules. So does a situation in which Eli lacks Q and does not handle P. I therefore cannot be determined. The judgments are **Cannot be determined, False**.

</div>
