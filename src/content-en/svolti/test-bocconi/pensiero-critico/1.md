## Exercise 1

A museum has eleven galleries and opens exactly seven on Monday. On Tuesday it opens every gallery that was open on Monday and two more. Determine how many galleries remain closed on Tuesday and whether a given gallery closed on Monday must open on Tuesday.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Galleries open on Tuesday} &= 7+2=9, \\
\text{Galleries closed on Tuesday} &= 11-9=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two extra galleries are distinct from the seven on Monday, so nine are open. Two of the four galleries that were closed on Monday remain closed. For a specific gallery among those four, the passage permits two situations. If it is one of the two added galleries, it opens on Tuesday. If the two added galleries are chosen from the other three, it stays closed. Whether that gallery reopens therefore cannot be determined.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A laboratory accepts two types of sample, dry and liquid. It logs the arrival of twelve dry samples in the morning. By evening its register lists eighteen samples received throughout the day. Determine whether exactly six liquid samples must have arrived and find the largest possible number of liquid samples.

$$
0\leq \text{liquid samples}\leq 18-12=6.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The twelve dry samples are those logged in the morning, so they give a *lower bound* on the day's total of dry samples. All six remaining arrivals could be liquid, so the maximum is six. They could also include two further dry samples and four liquid samples. Both cases meet the two counts, so six liquid samples is a possible outcome whose occurrence cannot be determined.

</div>
