## Exercise 1

A publisher sends an article to press only if it has received both the review and the author's consent. The review has been received for article A, but the consent is still missing. Both have been received for article B. Determine what follows for each article.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sent to press} &\implies \text{Review and consent}, \\
\text{Consent missing} &\implies \text{Article not sent to press}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If A went to press, the author would have had to consent. Since consent is missing, A stays out of print. For B, both necessary conditions are met. An editorial office that sends B to press immediately satisfies the rule. So does an office that waits for the layout, because it still sends no article to press without the two requirements. The outcome for B cannot be determined.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An application enters the permanent archive if and only if it has a registration number and is signed. Every application in the permanent archive appears in the monthly summary. Application M has a registration number and does not appear in the summary. Application N appears in the summary. Determine what can be inferred about the signatures on M and N.

$$
\text{Number and signature}\implies \text{Permanent archive}\implies \text{Summary}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If M were signed, the number and the signature would qualify it for the permanent archive and it would appear in the summary. Its absence from the summary therefore rules out a signature. N's appearance in the summary is consistent with a signed application in the archive. It is also consistent with an unsigned application that appears only in the summary, since the passage does not restrict the summary to archived applications. Whether N is signed cannot be determined.

</div>
