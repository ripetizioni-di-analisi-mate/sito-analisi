## Exercise 1

> For much of the day, the market's inner courtyard is in shade. Stalls that sell sun-loving plants have therefore begun moving towards the entrance, while woodland plants remain along the wall. The stallholders decided individually whether to move after observing the light over several weeks.

Write one sentence that states the main idea.

The stallholders have arranged their stalls **to suit the light requirements of the plants on display**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The shade in the courtyard establishes the starting condition. The second sentence distinguishes what happens with sun-loving plants from what happens with woodland plants. The final sentence reports that stallholders based the changes on the light they had observed. The summary therefore gives the criterion that explains both cases.

</div>

## Exercise 2

> To reconstruct how an old landing stage was used, a team consulted payment records and letters kept by local families. The records list taxable goods in detail but give little information about residents' journeys. The letters describe some everyday crossings that rarely appear in the accounts. The letters also give an incomplete picture, since most of those preserved belonged to just three families.
>
> The report therefore gives two separate lists and states for each source which uses of the landing stage can be documented.

State the purpose of the passage.

The purpose is **to explain why the report separates the two sources and makes their limitations explicit**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The passage introduces a problem of historical reconstruction and describes what can be learned from each source. The two sources show different aspects of the landing stage's use and have particular gaps. The second paragraph links these differences to the structure of the report. An answer about the records alone misses the comparison. An answer ranking one source as more reliable for every use adds a judgement the passage leaves open.

</div>
