## Exercise 1

> The summer centre has extended its evening activities through to the first week of August. Workshops take place outdoors when the wind is moderate. On evenings with strong winds, only the photography workshop takes place in the sports hall.

A student has to choose between “All workshops continue in the sports hall in strong winds” and “In strong winds, only the photography workshop takes place in the sports hall”. Determine which version preserves the condition.

The wording supported is **the one that limits activity in the sports hall to the photography workshop**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Both options mention the sports hall and strong winds, so the relevant evidence is the sentence that mentions both. *Only* limits the rule to one workshop. The option using *all* extends that rule. The times and dates are available, but checking the condition in the final sentence is enough to choose between the two options.

</div>

## Exercise 2

> A platform shared contact details with partner magazines when readers had consented at enrolment. From January, new subscribers have to consent separately to each magazine. Existing arrangements stay in place for previously registered profiles until the user changes their settings.
>
> The message sent in December announced the new system and showed where to review preferences. Receiving the message, however, changed no settings.

Evaluate the statement “From January, separate consent for each magazine is required of new subscribers and is activated for existing profiles when they receive the message”. Identify the part that is supported and the part that should be rejected.

**The requirement for new subscribers from January** is supported. Receiving the message leaves existing profiles' settings unchanged.

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step is to check the two parts joined by *and* separately. The first restates the rule for new subscribers. The second calls for checking what happens to existing profiles. The first paragraph specifies a change by the user, and the second clarifies what happens on receipt of the message. Since receiving the message alone changes no settings, the statement as a whole lacks support.

</div>
