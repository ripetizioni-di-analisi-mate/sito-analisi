## Exercise 1

> A service offers one hundred appointment slots in the morning and forty in the afternoon. Seventy percent of the morning slots and 80% of the afternoon slots are booked. The report distinguishes percentages from actual bookings.

Determine when a greater share of the slots is booked and when a greater number of slots are booked.

The larger share is **in the afternoon** and the larger number is **in the morning**.

$$
100\cdot\frac{70}{100}=70, \qquad
40\cdot\frac{80}{100}=32.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Comparing $80\%$ with $70\%$ gives the larger share in the afternoon. To compare counts, apply each share to the number of slots in that period. The 100 morning slots yield 70 bookings, and the 40 afternoon slots yield 32. The result changes because the total available is different in each period.

</div>

## Exercise 2

> A market's report gives remote participation both as the number of exhibitors and as a share of registrations. In the first month, 30 exhibitors out of 120 took part remotely. In the second month, the figure was 36 out of 180.

| Month | Registrations | Remote exhibitors |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| First | 120 | 30 |
| Second | 180 | 36 |

Describe how the number and share of remote exhibitors change between the two months.

The number rises by **6 exhibitors**. The share falls by **5 percentage points**.

$$
\frac{30}{120}=\frac{1}{4}=25\%,\qquad
\frac{36}{180}=\frac{1}{5}=20\%.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare counts, subtract 30 exhibitors from 36. To compare shares, divide each count by the number of registrations in the same month. The number of remote exhibitors rose while the total number of registrations rose in a greater proportion. The same table supports both conclusions.

</div>
