## Exercise 1

Five distinct instruments are arranged in a row on a bench. The microscope must be at one of the two ends. How many arrangements are possible?

$$
N=2\cdot4!=2\cdot24=48.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are two possible positions for the microscope. After this choice there are four positions and four distinct instruments left. Permutations count their possible orders, so either end gives $4!$ arrangements. Putting the microscope first and putting it last give distinct arrangements.

</div>

## Exercise 2

All letters in MARMARA are used to form a sequence. How many distinct sequences are possible? In how many do the three copies of $A$ occupy consecutive positions?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\text{total}}&=\frac{7!}{2!\,3!\,2!}=210,\\[6pt]
N_{\text{consecutive}}&=\frac{5!}{2!\,2!}=30.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The seven positions contain two copies of $M$, three of $A$, and two of $R$. Exchanging identical copies leaves the sequence unchanged, so $7!$ is divided by the product of the factorials of the multiplicities. For the second count the three copies of $A$ form a single block $AAA$. There are five items to order, including two pairs of indistinguishable copies. Each ordering of the items gives one valid sequence.

</div>
