## Exercise 1

Forty gym members are classified by membership type and attendance at a session.

| Membership | Attended | Did not attend |
|---|---:|---:|
| Monthly | $15$ | $5$ |
| Annual | $10$ | $10$ |

Find the joint relative frequency of monthly membership and attendance, the marginal relative frequency of attendance, and the relative frequency of attendance among monthly members.

$$
f_{\text{joint}}=\frac{15}{40}=\frac38,\qquad
f_{\text{marginal}}=\frac{25}{40}=\frac58 .
$$
$$
f_{\text{conditional}}=\frac{15}{20}=\frac34.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The joint frequency uses the cell with $15$ monthly members who attended and the total of $40$. The marginal frequency adds the attendees from both types of membership and uses the same total. The last frequency concerns the monthly row alone, which contains $20$ members. Its denominator is therefore $20$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a pie chart of $120$ trips, the train, bus, and bicycle categories correspond to angles of $90^\circ,126^\circ,144^\circ$, respectively. Consider only trips made by train or bus. What is the relative frequency of bus trips within that group?

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\text{train}}&=120\cdot\frac{90}{360}=30,\\[6pt]
n_{\text{bus}}&=120\cdot\frac{126}{360}=42,\\[6pt]
f_{\text{bus}\mid\text{train or bus}}&=\frac{42}{30+42}=\frac7{12}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each angle is the same fraction of $360^\circ$ as its group is of the $120$ trips. The total compatible with the condition is the sum of the train and bus trips, or $72$. Of these, $42$ are by bus. The angles could also be divided directly using $\dfrac{126}{90+126}$ because both counts have the same scale factor.

</div>
