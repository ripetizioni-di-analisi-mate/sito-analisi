## Exercise 1

A club selects four photographs from nine. One particular photograph must be included. How many groups are possible?

$$
N=\binom83=\frac{8\cdot7\cdot6}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=56.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required photograph is already in the group. Three more are needed from the remaining eight. Since changing the order of the photographs leaves the group unchanged, the count uses combinations. There are $56$ groups.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three representatives are selected from eight candidates. Two candidates, $L$ and $M$, can each be selected, but they cannot serve in the same group. How many groups are allowed?

$$
N_{\text{total}}=\binom83=56,\qquad
N_{\text{excluded}}=\binom61=6,\qquad
N_{\text{allowed}}=56-6=50.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial count includes all unordered groups. An excluded group contains both $L$ and $M$, so the only remaining choice is a third member from the other six candidates. This describes exactly the excluded groups. Subtracting them leaves **fifty allowed groups**.

</div>
