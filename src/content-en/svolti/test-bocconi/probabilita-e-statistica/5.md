## Exercise 1

A container holds five working lamps and three faulty ones. Two lamps are drawn without replacement, each chosen uniformly from the lamps available. What is the probability that both work?

$$
P(\text{both work})=\frac58\cdot\frac47=\frac5{14}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first lamp works in five out of eight cases. After a working lamp is drawn, four working lamps remain among seven. The probability of the second success therefore depends on the outcome of the first draw. The multiplication rule for events multiplies the probability of the first success by the conditional probability of the second.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pair of marbles is drawn uniformly without replacement from four red and five yellow marbles. At least one marble in the pair is red. What is the probability that both are red?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\text{at least one red}}&=\binom92-\binom52=36-10=26,\\[6pt]
N_{\text{two red}}&=\binom42=6,\\[6pt]
P(\text{two red}\mid\text{at least one red})&=\frac{6}{26}=\frac3{13}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equally likely pairs consistent with the information exclude only those consisting of two yellow marbles. The denominator is therefore the total number of pairs minus the ten yellow pairs. Every all-red pair already meets the condition. The numerator therefore counts the six pairs chosen from the four red marbles.

</div>
