## Exercise 1

A tank contains $360\,\mathrm{L}$ of a mixture. Liquids $A$ and $B$ are in the ratio $3:5$. Another $25\,\mathrm{L}$ of liquid $A$ is added. Find the new ratio in lowest terms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_A&=360\cdot\frac{3}{3+5}=135\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]
q_B&=360-135=225\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]
q'_A&=135+25=160\,\mathrm{L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The new ratio is $160:225=32:45$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The $3$ parts of $A$ and $5$ parts of $B$ make $8$ parts of mixture. The initial amount $q_A$ is therefore $\dfrac38$ of the original volume. The addition changes the amount of $A$ and leaves the amount of $B$ unchanged. Dividing both entries of the ratio by $5$ puts it in lowest terms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular container has base dimensions $0.4\,\mathrm{m}$ and $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ and an internal height of $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. How many litres does it hold when $75\,\%$ full? Use $1\,\mathrm{L}=1\,000\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.4\,\mathrm{m}&=40\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
A&=40\cdot25\,\mathrm{cm}^2=1\,000\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[6pt]
V&=1\,000\cdot12\,\mathrm{cm}^3=12\,000\,\mathrm{cm}^3=12\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]
V_{\mathrm{liquid}}&=\frac{75}{100}\cdot12\,\mathrm{L}=9\,\mathrm{L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We express all three dimensions in centimetres so that the area is in square centimetres and the volume is in cubic centimetres. The volume at full height is $12\,\mathrm{L}$. Since the stated percentage refers to the capacity, the liquid volume is $\dfrac34$ of $12\,\mathrm{L}$, which is $9\,\mathrm{L}$.

</div>
