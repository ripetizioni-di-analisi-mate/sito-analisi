## Exercise 1

Solve $|3x+1|<7$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|3x+1|<7 &\Longleftrightarrow -7<3x+1<7\\[6pt]
&\Longleftrightarrow -8<3x<6\\[6pt]
&\Longleftrightarrow -\frac83<x<2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The solution set is $\left(-\dfrac83,2\right)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value measures the distance of $3x+1$ from zero. Distances strictly less than $7$ therefore correspond to values inside $(-7,7)$. Subtract $1$ and divide by $3>0$ in both comparisons. The endpoints are excluded because the distance would be exactly $7$ in each case.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{(x+2)(x-1)}{x-3}\ge0$.

| Interval | Numerator | Denominator | Quotient |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(-\infty,-2)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ |
| $(-2,1)$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $(1,3)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ |
| $(3,+\infty)$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ |

$$
S=[-2,1]\cup(3,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is zero at $-2$ and $1$. The denominator is nonzero at both points, so the quotient equals zero and these endpoints are included. Between the two zeros, numerator and denominator are both negative, so the quotient is positive. For $x>3$, they are both positive. The point $x=3$ is excluded from the domain because its denominator is zero.

</div>
