## Exercise 1

Determine which of $-\dfrac{9}{14}$ and $-\dfrac{13}{20}$ is greater.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{9}{14}&=-\frac{90}{140},\\[6pt]
-\frac{13}{20}&=-\frac{91}{140}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $-90>-91$, we have $-\dfrac9{14}>-\dfrac{13}{20}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The common denominator $140$ is positive, so we can compare the integers $-90$ and $-91$. On the number line the first is one unit to the right of the second. The unit in the original fractions is $\dfrac1{140}$, so the first number exceeds the second by $\dfrac1{140}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $z=-\dfrac56-\dfrac14\div\left(-\dfrac32\right)$ and find its distance from $-\dfrac14$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
z&=-\frac56-\frac14\cdot\left(-\frac23\right)\\[6pt]
 &=-\frac56+\frac16\\[6pt]
 &=-\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\left|-\frac23-\left(-\frac14\right)\right|
=\left|-\frac8{12}+\frac3{12}\right|
=\frac5{12}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Division precedes subtraction, so we first multiply $\dfrac14$ by the reciprocal $-\dfrac23$. Since the quotient is negative, subtracting it adds $\dfrac16$. The value of $z$ is $-\dfrac23$. Its distance from $-\dfrac14$ is the absolute value of the difference, which is **five twelfths**.

</div>
