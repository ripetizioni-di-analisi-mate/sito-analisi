## Exercise 1

A total of $34$ tickets were sold for an event. Concession tickets cost $6\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and full-price tickets cost $9\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The total revenue was $246\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find how many tickets were sold at each price.

$$
\begin{cases}
r+i=34,\\
6r+9i=246.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3i&=246-6\cdot34=42,\\[6pt]
i&=14,\\[6pt]
r&=34-14=20.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation counts the tickets. The second weights each ticket by its price. Multiply the first by $6$ and subtract it from the second to eliminate $r$. Then use the total number of tickets to obtain $r$. Checking the revenue gives $6\cdot20+9\cdot14=246$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the points common to the parabola $y=x^2-4x+3$ and the line $y=2x-5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-4x+3&=2x-5,\\[6pt]
x^2-6x+8&=0,\\[6pt]
(x-2)(x-4)&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $x=2$, the line gives $y=-1$. For $x=4$, it gives $y=3$. The intersections are $(2,-1)$ and $(4,3)$.

::grafico{f="x^2-4*x+3;2*x-5" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-5 ymax=8 punti="2,-1;4,3" titolo="The line and parabola meet at (2, −1) and (4, 3)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At a common point, the two expressions for $y$ must agree, so set them equal. The roots of the quadratic are the $x$-coordinates of the common points. The line gives the corresponding $y$-coordinate for each root. Substitution into the parabola confirms $4-8+3=-1$ and $16-16+3=3$.

</div>
