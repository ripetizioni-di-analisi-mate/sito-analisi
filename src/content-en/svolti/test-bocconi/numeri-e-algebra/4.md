## Exercise 1

Write $(2x+3)(x-4)-(x-2)^2$ as a polynomial with terms in descending order of degree.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x+3)(x-4)&=2x^2-8x+3x-12\\[6pt]
&=2x^2-5x-12,\\[6pt]
(x-2)^2&=x^2-4x+4.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(2x^2-5x-12)-(x^2-4x+4)=x^2-x-16.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When we expand the first product, the linear terms are $-8x$ and $3x$, which add to $-5x$. Then we subtract each term of the squared binomial. Subtracting $-4x$ adds $4x$, and subtracting the constant $4$ reduces the constant term by $4$. The result collects the quadratic, linear and constant terms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $\dfrac{(3x-2)^2-25}{3x-7}$ while preserving its domain, and evaluate it at $x=-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Domain:}\quad &x\ne\frac73,\\[6pt]
(3x-2)^2-25&=(3x-2-5)(3x-2+5)\\[6pt]
&=(3x-7)(3x+3),\\[6pt]
\frac{(3x-2)^2-25}{3x-7}
&=3x+3 \qquad \text{for } x\ne\frac73.
\end{aligned}
$$

At $x=-2$, the value is $3(-2)+3=-3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is a difference of squares, so it factors as the product of the difference and the sum of the bases. The factor $3x-7$ also appears in the denominator. Cancelling it is valid for $x\ne\dfrac73$ and preserves that exclusion. The value $x=-2$ is admissible and can be substituted into the simplified expression.

</div>
