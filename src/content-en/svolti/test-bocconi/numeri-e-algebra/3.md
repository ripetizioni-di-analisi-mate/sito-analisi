## Exercise 1

For a workshop, choose a day from $r$, $s$ and $t$ and a time slot from $0$ and $1$. Slot $1$ on day $s$ is unavailable. List the possible bookings as ordered pairs, with the day first.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{r,s,t\}, \qquad B=\{0,1\},\\[6pt]
A\times B&=\{(r,0),(r,1),(s,0),(s,1),(t,0),(t,1)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The required set is $\{(r,0),(r,1),(s,0),(t,0),(t,1)\}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each day can be paired with two time slots, so the Cartesian product contains $3\cdot2=6$ pairs. The restriction applies to one combination, $(s,1)$. Removing that pair leaves **five bookings**. The order distinguishes the day from the time slot.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Of $120$ people registered for an event, $68$ booked tour $A$, $53$ booked tour $B$ and $22$ booked neither. How many booked both? How many booked exactly one?

$$
\begin{aligned}
|A\cup B|&=120-22=98,\\[6pt]
|A\cap B|&=68+53-98=23,\\[6pt]
|A\setminus B|+|B\setminus A|
&=(68-23)+(53-23)=75.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{tipo="due" nomi="A,B" ambiente="U" universo=1 zone="ab" elementi="a:45;ab:23;b:30;u:22" titolo="The 23 people in the overlap booked both tours"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The $22$ people outside both sets form the complement of the union, so $98$ belong to at least one set. In the sum $68+53$, each person who booked both tours appears twice. The difference between that sum and $98$ is $23$, the size of the intersection. Subtracting $23$ from each tour's total gives $45$ people in tour $A$ only and $30$ in tour $B$ only.

</div>
