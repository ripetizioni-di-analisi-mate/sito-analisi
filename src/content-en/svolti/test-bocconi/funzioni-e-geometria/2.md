## Exercise 1

Let $g(x)=2x+4$ and $f(t)=\sqrt{t-2}$. Find the domain of $f\circ g$ and the input that gives the output $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f\circ g)(x)&=\sqrt{(2x+4)-2}=\sqrt{2x+2},\\
2x+2&\geq 0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x\geq -1.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2x+2}&=2,\\
2x+2&=4,\\
x&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $g$ accepts any real input. Its output becomes the input to the outer square-root function, so $g(x)\geq 2$ is required. On the resulting domain both sides of the equation are nonnegative, so squaring preserves equivalence. Substitution gives $\sqrt{4}=2$, confirming that $x=1$ is admissible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the inverse of $f(x)=7-2(x+1)^2$ defined for $x\leq -1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=7-2(x+1)^2,\\
(x+1)^2&=\frac{7-y}{2},\\
x+1&=-\sqrt{\frac{7-y}{2}},\\
x&=-1-\sqrt{\frac{7-y}{2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
f^{-1}(y)=-1-\sqrt{\frac{7-y}{2}}\qquad(y\leq 7).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the half-line $x\leq -1$, the quantity $x+1$ is nonpositive. Consequently, after isolating the square, take the negative of the square root. Since $7-y$ must be nonnegative, the domain of the inverse is $(-\infty,7]$. Each $y$ in this interval produces an $x\leq -1$, so every admissible value is reached.

</div>
