## Exercise 1

Find the domain of $g(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{2x+6}}{x-1}$ and calculate $g(-3)$ and $g(5)$.

$$
\begin{cases}
2x+6\geq 0\\
x-1\neq 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x\geq -3\\
x\neq 1.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_g&=[-3,1)\cup(1,+\infty),\\
g(-3)&=\frac{\sqrt{0}}{-4}=0,\\
g(5)&=\frac{\sqrt{16}}{4}=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square root is defined even when the radicand is zero, so $x=-3$ remains in the domain. The denominator is zero at $x=1$, so this point splits the interval. Both inputs $-3$ and $5$ belong to the domain and can therefore be substituted into the original formula.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the domain, zeros and range of $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_f&=\mathbb R\setminus\{3\},\\
f(x)&=\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3\qquad (x\neq 3).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
f(x)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x=-3,
\qquad
\operatorname{Im}f=\mathbb R\setminus\{6\}.
$$

::grafico{f="x+3" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=9 punti="-3,0,pieno;3,6,vuoto" titolo="The point $(3,6)$ is excluded. The zero is $x=-3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator of the original formula excludes $3$. For every other input the factor $x-3$ cancels, so the graph follows the line $y=x+3$ with a single point removed. The line passes through $(3,6)$ and reaches the value $6$ only at that point. Therefore $6$ is the only value excluded from the range. The point $(-3,0)$ remains and gives the only zero.

</div>
