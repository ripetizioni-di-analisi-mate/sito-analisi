## Exercise 1

Convert $135^\circ$ to radians and calculate the sine, cosine and tangent of the angle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
135^\circ&=\frac{135}{180}\pi=\frac{3\pi}{4},\\
\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}&=\sin\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\\
\cos\frac{3\pi}{4}&=-\cos\frac{\pi}{4}=-\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\\
\tan\frac{3\pi}{4}&=-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point on the unit circle for $135^\circ$ is the reflection across the vertical axis of the point for $45^\circ$. This reflection preserves the $y$-coordinate and reverses the sign of the $x$-coordinate. The sine is therefore positive and the cosine negative, with the same absolute value. Their quotient gives the tangent.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two points in a straight line with the foot of a tower are on the same side of the tower and $10\,\mathrm{m}$ apart. Moving away from the tower, the angle of elevation to the top, measured from a height of $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$, decreases from $45^\circ$ to $30^\circ$. Find the height $H$ of the tower.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H-1.5&=x\tan45^\circ=x,\\
H-1.5&=(x+10)\tan30^\circ=\frac{x+10}{\sqrt3},\\
(\sqrt3-1)x&=10,\\
x&=5(\sqrt3+1),\\
H&=\left(\frac{13}{2}+5\sqrt3\right)\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the shorter horizontal distance in meters, so the longer distance is $x+10$. The two right triangles share a vertical side of length $H-1.5$ in meters. Multiplying tangent by the adjacent side gives this same length in both triangles, so the two expressions can be equated. Finally, add the observation height to the calculated height above the observers.

</div>
