## Exercise 1

Find the horizontal intercept of the line through $(3,-1)$ and parallel to $3y-2x=6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3y-2x=6&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=\frac23x+2,\\
y+1&=\frac23(x-3),\\
y&=\frac23x-3.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
0=\frac23x-3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=\frac92.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolating $y$ in the given equation reveals the slope. A parallel line retains the same coefficient of $x$. Use point-slope form to impose that it passes through $(3,-1)$. The horizontal axis has equation $y=0$, so the intersection is $\left(\dfrac92,0\right)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the point on the horizontal axis equidistant from $A=(-2,1)$ and $B=(4,3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=\left(\frac{-2+4}{2},\frac{1+3}{2}\right)=(1,2),\\
m_{AB}&=\frac{3-1}{4-(-2)}=\frac13,\\
y-2&=-3(x-1),\\
0&=-3x+5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac53.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Points equidistant from the endpoints of a segment lie on its perpendicular bisector, the perpendicular line through the midpoint. The slope of $AB$ is $\dfrac13$, so the slope of its perpendicular bisector is $-3$. The required point is $P=\left(\dfrac53,0\right)$. The squared distance from $A$ is $\left(\dfrac{11}{3}\right)^2+1=\dfrac{130}{9}$, and the squared distance from $B$ is $\left(-\dfrac73\right)^2+9=\dfrac{130}{9}$.

</div>
