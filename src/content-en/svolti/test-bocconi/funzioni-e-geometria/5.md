## Exercise 1

A rectangle of area $48\,\mathrm{m}^2$ has side lengths in the ratio $\dfrac34$. Find the sides and diagonal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3k)(4k)&=48,\qquad k>0,\\
12k^2&=48,\\
k&=2,\\
d&=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A side-length ratio of $\dfrac34$ allows us to write the lengths in meters as $3k$ and $4k$ with the same scale $k$. The area fixes the product of these measurements. The length $k$ is positive, so its admissible value is $2$ and the sides are $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and $8\,\mathrm{m}$. The diagonal is the hypotenuse of the right triangle with these sides as its legs.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The inscribed disk is cut out of a square sheet with side length $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area of material that remains and the total length of its boundary.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\
A&=(12^2-\pi\cdot 6^2)\,\mathrm{cm}^2
  =(144-36\pi)\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\
L&=(4\cdot12+2\pi\cdot6)\,\mathrm{cm}
  =(48+12\pi)\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The disk is tangent to all four sides, so its diameter equals the side length of the square. Subtract the area of the disk from the original area. The four outer sides still form part of the boundary, and the cut adds the circumference of the circle. Points of tangency are isolated and contribute no length.
