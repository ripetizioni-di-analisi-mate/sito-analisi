## Exercise 1

Solve $3^{2x}-4\cdot 3^x+3=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=3^x,\qquad t>0,\\
t^2-4t+3&=0,\\
(t-1)(t-3)&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^x=1&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=0,\\
3^x=3&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $3^{2x}=(3^x)^2$, the substitution $t=3^x$ turns the exponential equation into a quadratic equation. Both roots of the quadratic are positive, so they belong to the range of the exponential function. The function $3^x$ is strictly increasing, so each output has a unique corresponding input.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\log_{\frac12}(x+2)-\log_{\frac12}(x-1)\geq -1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=(1,+\infty),\\
\log_{\frac12}\left(\frac{x+2}{x-1}\right)
&\geq \log_{\frac12}2,\\
\frac{x+2}{x-1}&\leq 2,\\
x+2&\leq 2x-2,\\
x&\geq 4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To combine the logarithms, both arguments must be positive. This gives $x>1$. The logarithm to base $\dfrac12$ is decreasing, so comparing arguments reverses the inequality. On the domain, $x-1$ is positive, so the following multiplication preserves the direction. The final interval $[4,+\infty)$ is contained in the domain.

</div>
