## Exercise 1

A cube has edge length $4$. Find the radii of its inscribed and circumscribed spheres, the volume of the circumscribed sphere, and the ratio of the volumes of the two spheres.

$$
r = \frac{l}{2} = 2
$$
$$
R = \frac{d}{2} = \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
V_R = \frac{4}{3}\,\pi \left( 2\sqrt{3} \right)^3 = \frac{4}{3}\,\pi \cdot 24\sqrt{3} = 32\sqrt{3}\,\pi
$$
$$
\frac{V_R}{V_r} = \left( \frac{R}{r} \right)^3 = \left( \sqrt{3} \right)^3 = 3\sqrt{3}
$$

The inscribed sphere has radius $2$, the circumscribed sphere has radius $2\sqrt{3}$ and volume $32\sqrt{3}\,\pi$, and the ratio of the volumes is $3\sqrt{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The center of both spheres is the center of the cube, the midpoint of each space diagonal. The **inscribed** sphere is tangent to all six faces, and the distance from the center to each face is half the edge length, so $r = 2$.

The **circumscribed** sphere passes through all eight vertices. The distance from the center to each vertex is half the cube's space diagonal, whose length is $l\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}$, so $R = 2\sqrt{3}$.

The volume requires the cube of the radius. We have $\left( 2\sqrt{3} \right)^3 = 8 \cdot 3\sqrt{3} = 24\sqrt{3}$, and multiplying by $\dfrac{4}{3}\,\pi$ gives $32\sqrt{3}\,\pi$, approximately $174.1$.

Any two spheres are similar, with scale factor equal to the ratio of their radii, here $\dfrac{2\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$. The ratio of the volumes of similar solids is the cube of the scale factor, so the ratio is $\left( \sqrt{3} \right)^3 = 3\sqrt{3}$. The result is independent of the edge length of the cube because both radii are proportional to it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cone has base radius $6$ and height $8$. Find the radius of the sphere inscribed in the cone and the ratio of its volume to the volume of the cone.

$$
a = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10
$$
$$
\mathcal{A} = \frac{12 \cdot 8}{2} = 48,
\qquad
p = \frac{10 + 10 + 12}{2} = 16
$$
$$
r = \frac{\mathcal{A}}{p} = \frac{48}{16} = 3
$$
$$
V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{4}{3}\,\pi \cdot 27 = 36\pi,
\qquad
V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{1}{3}\,\pi \cdot 36 \cdot 8 = 96\pi
$$
$$
\frac{V_{\text{sphere}}}{V_{\text{cone}}} = \frac{36\pi}{96\pi} = \frac{3}{8}
$$

The inscribed sphere has radius $3$ and the ratio of the volumes is $\dfrac{3}{8}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Solve the problem in an **axial section**, made by a plane containing the axis of the cone. The section of the cone is an isosceles triangle with base $2 \cdot 6 = 12$ and height $8$, whose equal sides are slant heights. The section of the sphere is a disk bounded by a great circle inscribed in this triangle. Thus the radius of the sphere is the inradius of the triangle.

The Pythagorean theorem gives a slant height of $10$. The inradius of a triangle is the ratio of its area $\mathcal{A}$ to its semiperimeter $p$. The triangle has area $48$ and semiperimeter $16$, so $r = 3$.

The sphere has volume $\dfrac{4}{3}\,\pi \cdot 3^3 = 36\pi$ and the cone has volume $96\pi$. The factor $\pi$ cancels in the ratio, and $\dfrac{36}{96} = \dfrac{3}{8}$. Thus the sphere occupies slightly more than one-third of the cone.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cone has base radius $3$ and height $6$. A cylinder of radius $x$ is inscribed in the cone, with its lower base on the cone's base and the circle bounding its upper base on the lateral surface of the cone.

a) Express the height $y$ of the cylinder in terms of $x$ and give the admissible values of $x$.

b) Find $x$ such that the lateral surface area of the cylinder is $4\pi$.

c) Find the ratio of the volume of the cylinder to that of the cone when $x = 2$.

d) Find the equilateral cylinder inscribed in the cone and calculate its volume.

$$
\frac{y}{6} = \frac{3 - x}{3}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y = 6 - 2x,
\qquad
0 < x < 3
$$
$$
2\pi x\,(6 - 2x) = 4\pi
\iff
x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0
\iff
x = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}
$$
$$
\text{for } x = 2
\quad
y = 2,
\qquad
\frac{V_{\text{cyl}}}{V_{\text{cone}}} = \frac{\pi \cdot 4 \cdot 2}{\frac{1}{3}\,\pi \cdot 9 \cdot 6} = \frac{8\pi}{18\pi} = \frac{4}{9}
$$
$$
6 - 2x = 2x
\iff
x = \frac{3}{2},
\qquad
V = \pi \cdot \frac{9}{4} \cdot 3 = \frac{27}{4}\,\pi
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-2,0 2,0 2,2 -2,2" linee="-3,0 3,0 0,6 -3,0;-2,0 -2,2 2,2 2,0" segmenti="0,0,0,6" etichette="0.15,6.15,V;0.15,-0.55,K;0.9,2.25,x;2.15,0.9,y;2.9,-0.55,B;-3.4,-0.55,A;2.15,2.2,P" xmin=-4 xmax=3.6 ymin=-1.15 ymax=6.75 legenda=0 titolo="Axial section of the cylinder inscribed in the cone of radius $3$ and height $6$. The cylinder has radius $x$ and height $y$, and point $P$ lies on the slant height $VB$"}

The cylinder's height is $y = 6 - 2x$ with $0 < x < 3$. Its lateral surface area equals $4\pi$ when $x = \dfrac{3 - \sqrt{5}}{2}$ or $x = \dfrac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$. For $x = 2$, the volume ratio is $\dfrac{4}{9}$. The equilateral cylinder has radius $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and volume $\dfrac{27}{4}\,\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

a) In the axial section, the cylinder appears as a rectangle inscribed in triangle $VAB$, with a vertex $P$ on side $VB$. The triangle formed by $B$, point $P$, and the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to the base has legs $3 - x$ and $y$. It is **similar** to triangle $VKB$ with legs $3$ and $6$, since they share the angle at $B$ and both are right triangles. The proportion between their legs gives $y = 2\,(3 - x) = 6 - 2x$. The radius is positive and the height must be positive, so $0 < x < 3$.

b) The lateral surface area is $2\pi x y = 2\pi x\,(6 - 2x)$. Set this equal to $4\pi$, divide by $4\pi$, and collect all terms on the left to obtain $x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0$, with discriminant $9 - 4 = 5$. The solutions are approximately $0.38$ and $2.62$. Both lie between $0$ and $3$, so **both are admissible**.

c) For $x = 2$, the height is $y = 2$ and the cylinder's volume is $8\pi$. The cone has volume $18\pi$, and the ratio is $\dfrac{4}{9}$.

d) The cylinder is equilateral if its height equals its diameter, that is, $6 - 2x = 2x$. This gives $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$, which is admissible, with height $3$ and volume $\dfrac{27}{4}\,\pi$.

</div>
