## Exercise 1

A regular square pyramid has base side $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its slant height, lateral surface area, total surface area, and volume.

$$
HM = \frac{10}{2} = 5
$$
$$
a = VM = \sqrt{VH^2 + HM^2} = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13
$$
$$
A_l = p \cdot a = 20 \cdot 13 = 260
$$
$$
A_t = A_l + B = 260 + 100 = 360
$$
$$
V = \frac{1}{3}\,B \cdot h = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 100 \cdot 12 = 400
$$

The slant height is $13\,\mathrm{cm}$, the lateral surface area is $260\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, the total surface area is $360\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, and the volume is $400\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The pyramid is **regular**, so the foot $H$ of its altitude is the center of the square base, which is also the center of its inscribed circle. Segment $HM$ is a radius of this circle, where $M$ is the midpoint of side $AB$. Its length is half the side length, or $5$.

Segment $VH$ is perpendicular to the base plane, so it is perpendicular to $HM$, and triangle $VHM$ has a right angle at $H$. By the three perpendiculars theorem, segment $VM$ is the altitude of face $VAB$ and hence the **slant height** of the pyramid. The Pythagorean theorem gives $a = \sqrt{144 + 25} = 13$.

The four lateral faces are congruent isosceles triangles with base $10$ and height $13$. The lateral surface area is the base semiperimeter times the slant height. The perimeter is $40$ and the semiperimeter $20$, giving $A_l = 260$, which also equals $4 \cdot \dfrac{10 \cdot 13}{2}$.

Adding the area of the square base, $100$, gives a total surface area of $360$. The volume is one-third of the product of the base area and height, namely $\dfrac{1200}{3} = 400$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A regular square pyramid has base side $8$ and lateral edge $9$. Find the height, slant height, total surface area, and volume.

$$
HA = \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{2} = 4\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
h = VH = \sqrt{VA^2 - HA^2} = \sqrt{81 - 32} = \sqrt{49} = 7
$$
$$
a = VM = \sqrt{VA^2 - AM^2} = \sqrt{81 - 16} = \sqrt{65}
$$
$$
A_t = p \cdot a + B = 16\sqrt{65} + 64
$$
$$
V = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 64 \cdot 7 = \frac{448}{3}
$$

The height is $7$, the slant height is $\sqrt{65}$, the total surface area is $64 + 16\sqrt{65}$, approximately $193$, and the volume is $\dfrac{448}{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The given length is the **lateral edge** $VA$, so to find the height use triangle $VHA$, which joins the apex, the center of the base, and one base vertex. This triangle has a right angle at $H$ because $VH$ is perpendicular to the base plane.

Segment $HA$ is half a diagonal of the square. A square of side $8$ has diagonal $8\sqrt{2}$, so $HA = 4\sqrt{2}$ and $HA^2 = 32$. By the Pythagorean theorem, $h^2 = 81 - 32 = 49$, hence $h = 7$.

For the slant height, use face $VAB$, an isosceles triangle with sides $9$, $9$, and $8$. Altitude $VM$ meets the midpoint $M$ of $AB$, so $AM = 4$ and $VM = \sqrt{81 - 16} = \sqrt{65}$. Triangle $VHM$ gives the same value, since $7^2 + 4^2 = 65$.

The base semiperimeter is $16$, so the lateral surface area is $16\sqrt{65}$. Adding the base area $64$ gives the total surface area. The volume is $\dfrac{1}{3} \cdot 64 \cdot 7 = \dfrac{448}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A regular pyramid has an equilateral triangular base of side $6\sqrt{3}$, and the pyramid has height $4$. Find its slant height, total surface area, volume, and the measure of the dihedral angle between a lateral face and the base.

$$
r = \frac{l}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{3}} = 3
$$
$$
a = \sqrt{h^2 + r^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = 5
$$
$$
B = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot \left( 6\sqrt{3} \right)^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 108 = 27\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
A_t = p \cdot a + B = 9\sqrt{3} \cdot 5 + 27\sqrt{3} = 72\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
V = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 27\sqrt{3} \cdot 4 = 36\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
\tan \theta = \frac{h}{r} = \frac{4}{3}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\theta = \arctan \frac{4}{3} \approx 53.1°
$$

The slant height is $5$, the total surface area is $72\sqrt{3}$, the volume is $36\sqrt{3}$, and the dihedral angle measures approximately $53.1°$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In a regular pyramid, the foot $H$ of the altitude is the center of the circle inscribed in the base. In an equilateral triangle, the inradius is one-third of the altitude $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,l$, that is, $\dfrac{l}{2\sqrt{3}}$. With $l = 6\sqrt{3}$, this gives $r = 3$.

The theorem for the **slant height of a right pyramid** gives $a^2 = h^2 + r^2$, so $a = 5$.

The area of the equilateral triangle is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,l^2$, and $l^2 = 36 \cdot 3 = 108$, so $B = 27\sqrt{3}$. The base semiperimeter is $\dfrac{18\sqrt{3}}{2} = 9\sqrt{3}$, giving a lateral surface area of $45\sqrt{3}$ and total surface area of $72\sqrt{3}$. The volume is one-third of the base area times the height, or $36\sqrt{3}$.

Radius $HM$ and slant height $VM$ are both perpendicular to the base side at point $M$, so angle $\widehat{VMH}$ is the normal section of the dihedral angle. In triangle $VHM$, with a right angle at $H$, the tangent of this angle is $\dfrac{4}{3}$, and the dihedral angle measures approximately $53.1°$.

</div>
