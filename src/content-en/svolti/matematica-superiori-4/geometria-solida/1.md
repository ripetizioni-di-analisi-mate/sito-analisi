## Exercise 1

A rectangular prism has dimensions $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, $3\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate its space diagonal, total surface area, and volume.

$$
d = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_t &= 2\,(ab + ac + bc) \\[6pt]
&= 2\,(2 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 6 + 3 \cdot 6) \\[6pt]
&= 2\,(6 + 12 + 18) \\[6pt]
&= 72
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
V = abc = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 = 36
$$

The diagonal has length $7\,\mathrm{cm}$, the total surface area is $72\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, and the volume is $36\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three given measurements are the **dimensions** of the rectangular prism, that is, the lengths of the three edges meeting at one vertex. Denote them by $a = 2$, $b = 3$, and $c = 6$.

Use the formula $d = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$ for the space diagonal, which follows by applying the Pythagorean theorem twice. First find the base diagonal, whose square is $a^2 + b^2 = 4 + 9 = 13$, then consider the triangle whose legs are the base diagonal and the vertical edge $c$. This triangle has a right angle because the vertical edge is perpendicular to the plane of the base and therefore to each line in the base through its foot. Consequently $d^2 = 13 + 36 = 49$ and $d = 7$.

The six faces of the rectangular prism form three pairs of congruent rectangles with areas $ab = 6$, $ac = 12$, and $bc = 18$. The total surface area is therefore twice the sum of these three areas, or $72$.

The volume is the product of the three dimensions, since this is a prism with base area $ab = 6$ and height $c = 6$. We obtain $V = 36$. The lengths are in centimeters, so the area is measured in square centimeters and the volume in cubic centimeters.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The space diagonal of a cube has length $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the edge length, total surface area, and volume of the cube.

$$
l\sqrt{3} = 9
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
l = \frac{9}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{9\sqrt{3}}{3} = 3\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
A_t = 6\,l^2 = 6 \cdot \left( 3\sqrt{3} \right)^2 = 6 \cdot 27 = 162
$$
$$
V = l^3 = \left( 3\sqrt{3} \right)^3 = 27 \cdot 3\sqrt{3} = 81\sqrt{3}
$$

The edge length is $3\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$, the total surface area is $162\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, and the volume is $81\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

A cube is a rectangular prism with all three dimensions equal to its edge length $l$. The diagonal formula therefore becomes $d = \sqrt{l^2 + l^2 + l^2} = \sqrt{3l^2} = l\sqrt{3}$, where we have used $l > 0$ to write $\sqrt{l^2} = l$.

Set $l\sqrt{3} = 9$ and solve for $l$ by dividing by $\sqrt{3}$. To **rationalize** the denominator, multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3}$. Since $\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 3$, this gives $\dfrac{9\sqrt{3}}{3} = 3\sqrt{3}$.

The cube has six square faces of side $l$, so its total surface area is $6\,l^2$. The square of the edge length is $\left( 3\sqrt{3} \right)^2 = 9 \cdot 3 = 27$, hence $A_t = 162$.

For the volume, cube the edge length. Evaluate the two factors separately, $3^3 = 27$ and $\left( \sqrt{3} \right)^3 = \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = 3\sqrt{3}$, then multiply them. The result is $V = 81\sqrt{3}$, approximately $140.3\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular prism has a square base, height $9$, and space diagonal $15$. Find the base side length, the volume, the total surface area, and the angle that the space diagonal makes with the base plane.

$$
AC = \sqrt{AG^2 - CG^2} = \sqrt{225 - 81} = \sqrt{144} = 12
$$
$$
AB = \frac{AC}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{12}{\sqrt{2}} = 6\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
V = AB^2 \cdot CG = 72 \cdot 9 = 648
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A_t &= 2 \cdot AB^2 + 4 \cdot AB \cdot CG \\[6pt]
&= 2 \cdot 72 + 4 \cdot 6\sqrt{2} \cdot 9 \\[6pt]
&= 144 + 216\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\tan \widehat{GAC} = \frac{CG}{AC} = \frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\widehat{GAC} = \arctan \frac{3}{4} \approx 36.9°
$$

The base side length is $6\sqrt{2}$, the volume is $648$, the total surface area is $144 + 216\sqrt{2}$, and the diagonal makes an angle of approximately $36.9°$ with the base.

<div class="spiegazione">

Label the base $ABCD$ and the space diagonal $AG$ as in the figure. Edge $CG$ is perpendicular to the plane of the base, so it is perpendicular to the base diagonal $AC$, and triangle $ACG$ has a right angle at $C$. In this triangle the hypotenuse is $AG = 15$ and one leg is $CG = 9$, so the **Pythagorean theorem** gives the other leg as $AC = 12$.

The base is a square, and the diagonal of a square of side $l$ has length $l\sqrt{2}$. Therefore $AB = \dfrac{12}{\sqrt{2}}$. Rationalizing the denominator gives $\dfrac{12\sqrt{2}}{2} = 6\sqrt{2}$, and the base area is $\left( 6\sqrt{2} \right)^2 = 72$.

The volume is the base area times the height, namely $648$. The total surface area includes the two square bases, each of area $72$, and the four lateral faces, rectangles with sides $6\sqrt{2}$ and $9$. This gives $144 + 216\sqrt{2}$.

The angle between the diagonal and the base plane is the angle between $AG$ and its orthogonal projection onto the base. The projection of $G$ is $C$ because $GC$ is perpendicular to the base, so the projection of $AG$ is $AC$ and the required angle is $\widehat{GAC}$. In right triangle $ACG$, the tangent of this angle is the ratio of the opposite leg $CG$ to the adjacent leg $AC$, namely $\dfrac{3}{4}$. A calculator gives an angle of approximately $36.9°$.

</div>
