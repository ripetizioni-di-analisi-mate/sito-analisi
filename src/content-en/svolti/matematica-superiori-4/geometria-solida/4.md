## Exercise 1

An oblique segment $PA$, with $A$ in plane $\alpha$, has length $17$, and its orthogonal projection onto $\alpha$ has length $15$. Find the distance from $P$ to the plane and the angle that line $PA$ makes with the plane.

$$
PH = \sqrt{PA^2 - HA^2} = \sqrt{289 - 225} = \sqrt{64} = 8
$$
$$
\sin \theta = \frac{PH}{PA} = \frac{8}{17}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\theta = \arcsin \frac{8}{17} \approx 28.1°
$$

The distance from $P$ to the plane is $8$, and line $PA$ makes an angle of approximately $28.1°$ with the plane.

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $H$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $P$ to the plane. The **orthogonal projection** of segment $PA$ is then segment $HA$, so $HA = 15$. The distance from $P$ to the plane is the length of the perpendicular segment $PH$.

Line $PH$ is perpendicular to $\alpha$, so it is perpendicular to every line in $\alpha$ passing through $H$, in particular to line $HA$. Thus triangle $PHA$ has a right angle at $H$, hypotenuse $PA = 17$, and leg $HA = 15$. The Pythagorean theorem gives $PH^2 = 289 - 225 = 64$, so $PH = 8$. This agrees with the theorem on the perpendicular segment, since $8 < 17$.

The angle between an oblique line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection, here angle $\theta = \widehat{PAH}$. In right triangle $PHA$, the sine of $\theta$ is the ratio of the opposite leg $PH$ to the hypotenuse $PA$, so $\sin\theta = \dfrac{8}{17}$. The angle is acute, and the arcsine returns precisely an angle between $0°$ and $90°$ in this case. A calculator gives approximately $28.1°$. The same angle follows from $\cos\theta = \dfrac{15}{17}$ or $\tan\theta = \dfrac{8}{15}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Point $P$ lies at a distance of $6$ from plane $\alpha$, and $H$ is its projection onto $\alpha$. A line $r$ in $\alpha$ is at a distance of $8$ from $H$. Find the distance from $P$ to line $r$ and the measure of the dihedral angle between plane $\alpha$ and the half-plane with boundary $r$ containing $P$.

$$
PA = \sqrt{PH^2 + HA^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10
$$
$$
\tan \widehat{PAH} = \frac{PH}{HA} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\widehat{PAH} = \arctan \frac{3}{4} \approx 36.9°
$$

The distance from $P$ to $r$ is $10$, and the dihedral angle measures approximately $36.9°$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Draw the perpendicular from $H$ to line $r$ and denote its foot by $A$. The distance from $H$ to $r$ is therefore $HA = 8$. Segment $PH$ is perpendicular to the plane and $HA$ is perpendicular to line $r$ in $\alpha$. The hypotheses of the **three perpendiculars theorem** hold, so $PA$ is also perpendicular to $r$, and the distance from $P$ to $r$ is the length of $PA$.

Triangle $PHA$ has a right angle at $H$ because $PH$ is perpendicular to every line of the plane through $H$. By the Pythagorean theorem, $PA = 10$.

The edge of the dihedral angle is line $r$. Rays $AH$ and $AP$ start at the same point $A$ on $r$, one in each face, and both are perpendicular to $r$. Therefore angle $\widehat{PAH}$ is the **normal section** of the dihedral angle. Its tangent is the ratio of the opposite leg $PH$ to the adjacent leg $HA$, or $\dfrac{3}{4}$, and it measures approximately $36.9°$. The three perpendiculars theorem is used twice in this exercise, since it identifies both the distance and the normal section.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In cube $ABCDEFGH$ with edge length $6$, consider the base diagonal $BD$ and the vertex $E$ directly above $A$. Find the distance from $E$ to line $BD$ and the dihedral angle with edge $BD$ formed by the base and plane $EBD$.

$$
AM = \frac{AC}{2} = \frac{6\sqrt{2}}{2} = 3\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
EM = \sqrt{AE^2 + AM^2} = \sqrt{36 + 18} = \sqrt{54} = 3\sqrt{6}
$$
$$
\tan \widehat{EMA} = \frac{AE}{AM} = \frac{6}{3\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\widehat{EMA} = \arctan \sqrt{2} \approx 54.7°
$$

The distance from $E$ to line $BD$ is $3\sqrt{6}$, approximately $7.35$, and the dihedral angle measures approximately $54.7°$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Edge $AE$ is perpendicular to edges $AB$ and $AD$, so by the **perpendicularity criterion** it is perpendicular to the base plane, and the projection of $E$ onto the base is $A$.

Find the foot of the perpendicular from $A$ to line $BD$. The diagonals of a square are perpendicular and intersect at their common midpoint $M$, so $AM$ is perpendicular to $BD$ and has half the length of diagonal $AC = 6\sqrt{2}$, namely $3\sqrt{2}$.

Segment $EA$ is perpendicular to the base, and $AM$ is perpendicular to line $BD$ in the base. By the three perpendiculars theorem, $EM$ is also perpendicular to $BD$, so the distance from $E$ to $BD$ is $EM$. Triangle $EAM$ has a right angle at $A$, and the Pythagorean theorem gives $EM^2 = 36 + 18 = 54$, that is, $EM = \sqrt{9 \cdot 6} = 3\sqrt{6}$.

Rays $MA$ and $ME$ are perpendicular to edge $BD$ at the same point $M$, one in the base and the other in plane $EBD$. Therefore angle $\widehat{EMA}$ is the normal section of the dihedral angle, and its tangent is $\dfrac{6}{3\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}$. The dihedral angle measures approximately $54.7°$.

</div>
