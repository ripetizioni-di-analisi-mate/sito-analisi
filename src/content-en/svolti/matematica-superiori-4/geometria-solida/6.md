## Exercise 1

An equilateral cylinder has total surface area $54\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. Find its radius, height, and volume.

$$
h = 2r
$$
$$
A_t = 2\pi r\,(h + r) = 2\pi r\,(2r + r) = 6\pi r^2
$$
$$
6\pi r^2 = 54\pi
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
r^2 = 9
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
r = 3
$$
$$
h = 6,
\qquad
V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \cdot 9 \cdot 6 = 54\pi
$$

The radius is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$, the height is $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the volume is $54\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

A cylinder is **equilateral** when its cross section by a plane containing the axis is a square. This section is a rectangle with sides $2r$ and $h$, so the condition is $h = 2r$. Thus the two unknowns reduce to one, the radius.

The total surface area of the cylinder is the sum of its lateral surface area $2\pi r h$ and its two base areas, $2\pi r^2$, giving $2\pi r\,(h + r)$. Substituting $h = 2r$ yields $2\pi r \cdot 3r = 6\pi r^2$.

Set this expression equal to the given value $54\pi$ and divide both sides by $6\pi$, which is different from $0$, giving $r^2 = 9$. The equation has solutions $r = 3$ and $r = -3$, but a radius is a positive length, so only $r = 3$ is admissible. The height is twice the radius, or $6$.

The volume of the cylinder is the base area times the height, giving $\pi \cdot 9 \cdot 6 = 54\pi$, approximately $169.6\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. The volume is measured in cubic centimeters because the lengths are in centimeters.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cone has slant height $10$ and lateral surface area $60\pi$. Find its radius, height, total surface area, volume, and the central angle of the circular sector obtained by unrolling its lateral surface.

$$
\pi r a = 60\pi
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
r = \frac{60}{10} = 6
$$
$$
h = \sqrt{a^2 - r^2} = \sqrt{100 - 36} = 8
$$
$$
A_t = \pi r\,(a + r) = 6\pi \cdot 16 = 96\pi
$$
$$
V = \frac{1}{3}\,\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\,\pi \cdot 36 \cdot 8 = 96\pi
$$
$$
\varphi = \frac{2\pi r}{a} = \frac{12\pi}{10} = \frac{6\pi}{5}
$$

The radius is $6$, the height is $8$, the total surface area is $96\pi$, the volume is $96\pi$, and the sector has central angle $\dfrac{6\pi}{5}$, or $216°$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The lateral surface area of the cone is $\pi r a$. With $a = 10$, we have $10\pi r = 60\pi$, so $r = 6$.

The height, radius, and slant height form a right triangle, half of an axial section, in which the slant height is the hypotenuse. By the Pythagorean theorem, $h^2 = 100 - 36 = 64$, so $h = 8$.

Adding the area of the base disk, $36\pi$, to the lateral surface area gives a total surface area of $96\pi$. The volume is one-third of the base area times the height, or $\dfrac{288\pi}{3} = 96\pi$.

Cutting the lateral surface along a generator and unrolling it onto a plane gives a **circular sector** whose radius is the slant height, $10$. The sector's arc length equals the circumference of the base, $2\pi r = 12\pi$. In radians, the central angle is the ratio of the arc length to the radius, so $\varphi = \dfrac{12\pi}{10} = \dfrac{6\pi}{5}$. To convert to degrees, multiply by $\dfrac{180°}{\pi}$ to obtain $216°$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $C$ and legs $AC = 6$ and $BC = 8$. The triangle makes one complete turn about hypotenuse $AB$. Find the volume and surface area of the solid formed.

$$
AB = \sqrt{36 + 64} = 10
$$
$$
CK = \frac{AC \cdot BC}{AB} = \frac{6 \cdot 8}{10} = \frac{24}{5}
$$
$$
V = \frac{1}{3}\,\pi\,CK^2 \cdot AK + \frac{1}{3}\,\pi\,CK^2 \cdot KB = \frac{1}{3}\,\pi\,CK^2 \cdot AB = \frac{1}{3}\,\pi \cdot \frac{576}{25} \cdot 10 = \frac{384}{5}\,\pi
$$
$$
A = \pi \cdot CK \cdot AC + \pi \cdot CK \cdot BC = \pi \cdot \frac{24}{5} \cdot 14 = \frac{336}{5}\,\pi
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="3.6+(0)*t,0+(4.8)*t,0,1" linee="0,0 3.6,4.8 10,0 0,0;-0.8,0 10.8,0" segmenti="0,0,3.6,-4.8;3.6,-4.8,10,0;3.6,0,3.6,4.8" etichette="-0.45,-0.6,A;10.1,-0.6,B;3.4,5.1,C;3.75,-0.6,K;1,2.8,6;7.1,2.8,8" xmin=-1.4 xmax=11.4 ymin=-5.4 ymax=5.7 legenda=0 titolo="Triangle $ABC$, with a right angle at $C$, rotates about hypotenuse $AB$. The dashed outline is the reflection of the triangle across the axis, and the common radius of the two cones is altitude $CK$"}

The volume is $\dfrac{384}{5}\,\pi$ and the surface area is $\dfrac{336}{5}\,\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

From $C$, draw the altitude $CK$ to the hypotenuse. Rotating about $AB$, triangle $AKC$ generates a cone with apex $A$, radius $CK$, and height $AK$, and triangle $KBC$ generates a cone with apex $B$, the same radius, and height $KB$. Thus the solid consists of **two cones** joined at their common base.

By the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse has length $10$. Write the area of the triangle in two ways, as half the product of the legs and as half the product of the hypotenuse and altitude $CK$. Equating them gives $CK = \dfrac{48}{10} = \dfrac{24}{5}$.

The cones have the same base, so $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\pi\,CK^2$ is a common factor in the sum of the volumes, and the sum of the heights is $AK + KB = AB = 10$. Thus we need not calculate $AK$ and $KB$ separately. We obtain $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\pi \cdot \dfrac{576}{25} \cdot 10 = \dfrac{1920}{25}\,\pi = \dfrac{384}{5}\,\pi$.

The surface consists of the two lateral surfaces. The first cone has slant height $AC = 6$ and the second has slant height $BC = 8$, and each lateral surface area equals $\pi$ times the radius times the slant height. Factoring out $\pi \cdot CK$ gives $\pi \cdot \dfrac{24}{5} \cdot 14 = \dfrac{336}{5}\,\pi$.

</div>
