## Exercise 1

A spherical surface has area $100\pi$. Find its radius, the volume of the sphere, and the area of the cross section made by a plane at a distance of $4$ from the center.

$$
4\pi R^2 = 100\pi
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
R^2 = 25
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
R = 5
$$
$$
V = \frac{4}{3}\,\pi R^3 = \frac{4}{3}\,\pi \cdot 125 = \frac{500}{3}\,\pi
$$
$$
r = \sqrt{R^2 - d^2} = \sqrt{25 - 16} = 3,
\qquad
A_{\text{section}} = \pi r^2 = 9\pi
$$

The radius is $5$, the volume is $\dfrac{500}{3}\,\pi$, and the cross section has area $9\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a spherical surface is $4\pi R^2$. Setting this equal to $100\pi$ and dividing by $4\pi$ gives $R^2 = 25$. A radius is a positive length, so only the solution $R = 5$ is admissible.

The volume of the sphere is $\dfrac{4}{3}\,\pi R^3$. With $R^3 = 125$, we obtain $\dfrac{500}{3}\,\pi$, approximately $523.6$.

For the cross section, use the theorem on **plane sections of a sphere**. The plane is at a distance of $d = 4$ from the center and $4 < 5$, so it cuts the sphere in a disk. The center of the section is the projection $O'$ of the sphere's center $O$ onto the plane. A point $P$ on the circle bounding the section is at a distance of $R$ from $O$, and triangle $OO'P$ has a right angle at $O'$. By the Pythagorean theorem, the radius of the section is $r = \sqrt{25 - 16} = 3$, and the area of the disk is $9\pi$.

With a plane at a distance of $5$ from the center, the cross section would reduce to a point because the plane would be tangent. At a distance greater than $5$, the plane would not meet the sphere.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solid consists of a cylinder of radius $3$ and height $5$ and a hemisphere of radius $3$ resting on the top base of the cylinder. Find the volume and the external surface area of the solid.

$$
V = \pi r^2 h + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{3}\,\pi r^3 = 45\pi + 18\pi = 63\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 2\pi r h + \pi r^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4\pi r^2 \\[6pt]
&= 30\pi + 9\pi + 18\pi \\[6pt]
&= 57\pi
\end{aligned}
$$

The volume is $63\pi$ and the external surface area is $57\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The solid breaks into two nonoverlapping parts, so its volume is the sum of their volumes. The cylinder has volume $\pi \cdot 9 \cdot 5 = 45\pi$. The hemisphere has half the volume of a sphere of radius $3$, namely $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 36\pi = 18\pi$. The total is $63\pi$, approximately $197.9$.

To find the surface area, identify the exposed parts. The top base of the cylinder coincides with the base disk of the hemisphere and lies inside the solid, so it does not count. Three parts remain.
- The lateral surface of the cylinder, of area $2\pi \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30\pi$.
- The bottom base of the cylinder, of area $9\pi$.
- The curved surface of the hemisphere, half the spherical surface, of area $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4\pi \cdot 9 = 18\pi$.

Adding gives $57\pi$, approximately $179.1$. Adding the total surface areas of the cylinder and the closed hemisphere would count the disk where they meet twice, although it is not part of the external surface.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sphere of radius $R$ is inscribed in a cylinder, meaning that it is tangent to both bases and the lateral surface. Show that the volume of the sphere is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the volume of the cylinder and that the spherical surface area equals the lateral surface area of the cylinder. Then, for $R = 4$, find the volume of the space between the sphere and the cylinder.

$$
r_{\text{cyl}} = R,
\qquad
h_{\text{cyl}} = 2R
$$
$$
V_{\text{cyl}} = \pi R^2 \cdot 2R = 2\pi R^3,
\qquad
\frac{V_{\text{sphere}}}{V_{\text{cyl}}} = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\,\pi R^3}{2\pi R^3} = \frac{2}{3}
$$
$$
A_l = 2\pi R \cdot 2R = 4\pi R^2 = A_{\text{sphere}}
$$
$$
V_{\text{cyl}} - V_{\text{sphere}} = 2\pi R^3 - \frac{4}{3}\,\pi R^3 = \frac{2}{3}\,\pi R^3 = \frac{2}{3}\,\pi \cdot 64 = \frac{128}{3}\,\pi
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="4*cos(t),4+4*sin(t),0,6.29" linee="-4,0 4,0 4,8 -4,8 -4,0" segmenti="0,4,4,4;0,0,0,8" etichette="1.8,4.2,R;0.15,3.6,O;4.2,6,2R" xmin=-4.6 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.6 ymax=8.6 legenda=0 titolo="Axial section of the sphere inscribed in the cylinder. The disk bounded by a great circle is inscribed in the square of side $2R$"}

For $R = 4$, the space between the sphere and the cylinder has volume $\dfrac{128}{3}\,\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the axial section, the disk bounded by a great circle is inscribed in a rectangle. The sphere is tangent to the lateral surface, so the cylinder's radius equals $R$. It is tangent to both bases, so the cylinder's height equals the diameter $2R$. The rectangle is therefore a square of side $2R$ and the cylinder is equilateral.

The volume of the cylinder is $\pi R^2 \cdot 2R = 2\pi R^3$. Dividing the sphere's volume by this value cancels the factor $\pi R^3$, leaving $\dfrac{4}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. The lateral surface area of the cylinder is $2\pi R \cdot 2R = 4\pi R^2$, precisely the area of the spherical surface. Both results are due to Archimedes.

The volume of the space between the two solids is the difference of the volumes, or $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the cylinder's volume, namely $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\pi R^3$. It is also the combined volume of the two cones whose bases are the cylinder's bases and whose common apex is the center of the sphere, as follows by comparing the hemisphere with the cylinder with a conical cavity using **Cavalieri's principle**. With $R = 4$, we have $R^3 = 64$ and the required volume is $\dfrac{128}{3}\,\pi$, approximately $134.0$.

</div>
