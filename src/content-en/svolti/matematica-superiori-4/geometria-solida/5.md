## Exercise 1

The frustum of a regular pyramid has square bases with side lengths $12$ and $6$ and height $4$. Find its slant height, total surface area, and volume.

$$
\frac{12}{2} - \frac{6}{2} = 6 - 3 = 3
$$
$$
a = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5
$$
$$
A_l = (p + p') \cdot a = (24 + 12) \cdot 5 = 180
$$
$$
A_t = A_l + B + B' = 180 + 144 + 36 = 360
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{h}{3} \left( B + B' + \sqrt{B B'} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{3} \left( 144 + 36 + \sqrt{144 \cdot 36} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{3} \left( 144 + 36 + 72 \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{3} \cdot 252 = 336
\end{aligned}
$$

The slant height is $5$, the total surface area is $360$, and the volume is $336$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The slant height of the frustum is the height of a lateral face, which is an isosceles trapezoid. To find it, cut the frustum with the plane through the centers of the bases perpendicular to two opposite sides. The cross section is an isosceles trapezoid with bases $12$ and $6$ and height $4$, and its legs are slant heights of the frustum.

From one endpoint of the shorter base, draw the perpendicular to the longer base. This forms a right triangle with one leg equal to the height $4$ and the other equal to the difference between the half-lengths of the bases, namely $3$. By the Pythagorean theorem, the slant height is $5$.

The base semiperimeters are $p = 24$ and $p' = 12$, so the lateral surface area is $(24 + 12) \cdot 5 = 180$. Indeed, each of the four faces is a trapezoid of area $\dfrac{(12 + 6) \cdot 5}{2} = 45$. The base areas are $144$ and $36$, giving a total surface area of $360$.

The volume formula contains the square root of the product of the base areas. Since $144 \cdot 36 = 5184 = 72^2$, we have $\sqrt{B B'} = 72$ and the volume is $\dfrac{4}{3} \cdot 252 = 336$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A conical frustum has base radii $5$ and $2$ and slant height $5$. Find its height, total surface area, and volume.

$$
R - r = 5 - 2 = 3
$$
$$
h = \sqrt{a^2 - (R - r)^2} = \sqrt{25 - 9} = 4
$$
$$
A_t = \pi\,(R + r)\,a + \pi R^2 + \pi r^2 = 35\pi + 25\pi + 4\pi = 64\pi
$$
$$
V = \frac{\pi h}{3} \left( R^2 + R r + r^2 \right) = \frac{4\pi}{3}\,(25 + 10 + 4) = \frac{4\pi}{3} \cdot 39 = 52\pi
$$

The height is $4$, the total surface area is $64\pi$, and the volume is $52\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The axial section of the conical frustum is an isosceles trapezoid with bases $2R = 10$ and $2r = 4$. Its legs are generators whose length is the slant height. From the endpoint of the smaller radius, drop a perpendicular to the larger base at point $K$, as shown. This forms a right triangle with hypotenuse equal to the slant height $a = 5$ and legs equal to the height $h$ and the difference of the radii $R - r = 3$. By the Pythagorean theorem, $h^2 = 25 - 9 = 16$, so $h = 4$.

The lateral surface area of the conical frustum is $\pi\,(R + r)\,a = \pi \cdot 7 \cdot 5 = 35\pi$. The bases are disks of areas $25\pi$ and $4\pi$. Adding gives the total surface area $64\pi$.

The volume formula contains the squares of the radii and their product, namely $25$, $4$, and $10$, with sum $39$. The volume is therefore $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} \cdot 39 = 52\pi$, approximately $163.4$. To check this, subtract the volumes of two cones. The removed cone is similar to the complete cone with scale factor $\dfrac{2}{5}$, so its height is $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the height of the complete cone. The difference between the heights is $4$, so the complete cone has height $\dfrac{20}{3}$ and the removed cone has height $\dfrac{8}{3}$. The difference of the volumes is $\dfrac{500}{9}\,\pi - \dfrac{32}{9}\,\pi = 52\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A regular square pyramid has base side $15$ and height $15$. A plane parallel to its base cuts it at a distance of $10$ from its apex. Find the area of the cross section and the volume of the resulting frustum.

$$
B' = B \left( \frac{x}{h} \right)^2 = 225 \cdot \left( \frac{10}{15} \right)^2 = 225 \cdot \frac{4}{9} = 100
$$
$$
V_{\text{pyramid}} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 225 \cdot 15 = 1125
$$
$$
V_{\text{removed}} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 100 \cdot 10 = \frac{1000}{3}
$$
$$
V_{\text{frustum}} = 1125 - \frac{1000}{3} = \frac{3375 - 1000}{3} = \frac{2375}{3}
$$

The cross section has area $100$ and the frustum has volume $\dfrac{2375}{3}$, approximately $791.7$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on a **cross section parallel to the base**, the section is a square similar to the base, with scale factor equal to the ratio of the distances from the apex, namely $\dfrac{10}{15} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. The ratio of the areas is the square of this factor, $\dfrac{4}{9}$, so the section has area $100$ and side length $10$.

The plane divides the pyramid into two parts. The part near the apex is a smaller pyramid whose base is the cross section and whose height is $10$. The other part is the frustum, of height $15 - 10 = 5$. The volume of the frustum is therefore the difference between the volume of the whole pyramid, $1125$, and the volume of the removed pyramid, $\dfrac{1000}{3}$. To subtract, write $1125 = \dfrac{3375}{3}$, yielding $\dfrac{2375}{3}$.

The formula for the volume of a frustum gives the same result with $h = 5$, $B = 225$, and $B' = 100$. Since $\sqrt{225 \cdot 100} = 150$, we find $\dfrac{5}{3}\,(225 + 100 + 150) = \dfrac{5}{3} \cdot 475 = \dfrac{2375}{3}$.

</div>
