## Exercise 1

A projectile is launched from the ground with initial velocity $v_0 = 20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at an angle $\theta$ above the horizontal, with $0 < \theta < 90^\circ$. Neglecting air resistance and taking $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, its range is
$$
R = \frac{2 v_0^2 \sin\theta\cos\theta}{g} .
$$
a) Express $R$ using the double-angle formula. b) Calculate the range for $\theta = 15^\circ$ and for $\theta = 75^\circ$. c) Show that two complementary angles give the same range and determine the maximum range.

$$
R = \frac{v_0^2 \cdot 2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g} = \frac{400}{10}\sin 2\theta = 40\sin 2\theta
$$
$$
R(15^\circ) = 40\sin 30^\circ = 40 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 20\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
R(75^\circ) = 40\sin 150^\circ = 40 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 20\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
R(90^\circ - \theta) = 40\sin(180^\circ - 2\theta) = 40\sin 2\theta = R(\theta)
$$
$$
\sin 2\theta = 1 \iff 2\theta = 90^\circ \iff \theta = 45^\circ ,
\qquad
R_{\max} = 40\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

a) The range formula contains the product $2\sin\theta\cos\theta$, which by the **sine double-angle formula** equals $\sin 2\theta$. Substituting $v_0^2 = 400\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ and $g = 10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ gives $R = 40\sin 2\theta$, in meters.

b) For $\theta = 15^\circ$, twice the angle is $30^\circ$, whose sine is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the range is $20\,\mathrm{m}$. For $\theta = 75^\circ$, twice the angle is $150^\circ$. This is the supplement of $30^\circ$, and supplementary angles have the same sine, so the range is again $20\,\mathrm{m}$. The calculation does not require the sine or cosine of $15^\circ$ or $75^\circ$ because the double-angle formula reduces the calculation to a special angle.

c) The complement of $\theta$ is $90^\circ - \theta$, and twice this angle is $180^\circ - 2\theta$, the supplement of $2\theta$. Since $\sin(180^\circ - 2\theta) = \sin 2\theta$, the two complementary angles give the **same range**. Part b), with $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$, gives an example.

The sine of an angle is at most $1$, so the range is at most $40\,\mathrm{m}$. The value $\sin 2\theta = 1$ is attained for $2\theta = 90^\circ$ because $2\theta$ lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. Therefore the range is **greatest** for $\theta = 45^\circ$ and equals $40\,\mathrm{m}$. The angle $45^\circ$ is its own complement and is the only angle with this property.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object oscillates along a line according to the equation
$$
s(t) = 3\sin(2t) + 4\cos(2t) ,
$$
with $s$ in centimeters and $t$ in seconds. a) Write $s(t)$ in the form $A\sin(2t + \varphi)$ and give the amplitude and period of the motion. b) Find the initial position and the first time $t > 0$ at which the object reaches its maximum positive displacement from the origin.

$$
A = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5 ,
\qquad
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = \dfrac{3}{5} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = \dfrac{4}{5}
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\varphi = \arctan\frac{4}{3}
$$
$$
s(t) = 5\sin(2t + \varphi) ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi}{2} = \pi\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
s(0) = 3\sin 0 + 4\cos 0 = 4\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
2t + \varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}
\iff
t = \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan\frac{4}{3} \right) = \frac{1}{2}\arctan\frac{3}{4} \approx 0.32\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

a) The equation of motion combines the sine and cosine of the same angle $2t$, so apply the **auxiliary-angle formula** with $a = 3$ and $b = 4$ and with $2t$ in place of $x$. The coefficient is $A = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$. The auxiliary angle has cosine $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and sine $\dfrac{4}{5}$, both positive, so it lies in the first quadrant. Since $a > 0$, it equals $\varphi = \arctan\dfrac{4}{3}$.

The motion is therefore simple harmonic, with **amplitude** $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The angular frequency is the coefficient of $t$, that is, $\omega = 2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and the period is $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} = \pi\,\mathrm{s}$.

b) At time $t = 0$ the sine equals $0$ and the cosine equals $1$, so the object starts at $s(0) = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The form obtained above gives the same value, $5\sin\varphi = 5 \cdot \dfrac{4}{5} = 4$.

The maximum positive displacement is the amplitude $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, attained when $\sin(2t + \varphi) = 1$, that is, when $2t + \varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. For $t > 0$ the argument $2t + \varphi$ is greater than $\varphi$, and $\varphi < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so the first applicable value is the one with $k = 0$. This gives $t = \dfrac{1}{2}\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \varphi \right)$.

Two acute complementary angles have reciprocal tangents, so $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \arctan\dfrac{4}{3} = \arctan\dfrac{3}{4}$. The first time the maximum is reached is $t = \dfrac{1}{2}\arctan\dfrac{3}{4}$, approximately $0.32\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangle $ABCD$ is inscribed in a semicircle of center $O$ and radius $1$, with side $AB$ on the diameter and vertices $C$ and $D$ on the semicircle. Let $x = B\widehat{O}C$, with $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

a) Express in terms of $x$ the area $S(x)$ and the perimeter $p(x)$ of the rectangle.
b) Find the value of $x$ that maximizes the area, and find the maximum area.
c) Write $p(x)$ in the form $r\sin(x + \varphi)$.
d) Find the maximum perimeter and the dimensions of the corresponding rectangle.

$$
\overline{AB} = 2\cos x ,
\qquad
\overline{BC} = \sin x
$$
$$
S(x) = 2\cos x \cdot \sin x = \sin 2x ,
\qquad
p(x) = 2\,(2\cos x + \sin x) = 2\sin x + 4\cos x
$$
$$
\sin 2x = 1 \iff 2x = \frac{\pi}{2} \iff x = \frac{\pi}{4} ,
\qquad
S_{\max} = 1
$$
$$
r = \sqrt{4 + 16} = 2\sqrt{5} ,
\qquad
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = \dfrac{2}{2\sqrt{5}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = \dfrac{4}{2\sqrt{5}} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}
\end{cases}
\qquad
p(x) = 2\sqrt{5}\sin(x + \varphi) ,
\quad
\varphi = \arctan 2
$$
$$
x + \varphi = \frac{\pi}{2}
\iff
x = \frac{\pi}{2} - \varphi ,
\qquad
\tan x = \frac{1}{\tan\varphi} = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\cos x = \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} ,
\qquad
\sin x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} ,
\qquad
\overline{AB} = \frac{4\sqrt{5}}{5} ,
\qquad
\overline{BC} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{5} ,
\qquad
p_{\max} = 2\sqrt{5}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,3.1416;0.25*cos(t),0.25*sin(t),0,0.6" linee="-0.825,0 -0.825,0.565 0.825,0.565 0.825,0" vettori="0,0,0.825,0.565" punti="-0.825,0,pieno,A;0.825,0,pieno,B;0.825,0.565,pieno,C;-0.825,0.565,pieno,D;0,0,pieno,O" etichette="0.33,0.08,x" xmin=-1.3 xmax=1.3 ymin=-0.3 ymax=1.3 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The rectangle $ABCD$ inscribed in a semicircle of radius $1$. With $x = B\widehat{O}C$ we have $\overline{OB} = \cos x$ and $\overline{BC} = \sin x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

a) Triangle $OBC$ is right-angled at $B$ because $BC$ is perpendicular to the diameter, and its hypotenuse is the radius $\overline{OC} = 1$. By right-triangle trigonometry, the leg adjacent to the angle $x$ is $\overline{OB} = \cos x$ and the opposite leg is $\overline{BC} = \sin x$. The rectangle is symmetric about the line through $O$ perpendicular to the diameter, so $\overline{AB} = 2\,\overline{OB} = 2\cos x$. Its area is the product of its dimensions and its perimeter is twice their sum.

b) By the **sine double-angle formula**, $2\sin x\cos x = \sin 2x$. Sine is at most $1$, and for $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ we have $0 < 2x < \pi$, so $\sin 2x = 1$ only at $2x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The area is **greatest** for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and equals $1$. In this case $\overline{AB} = \sqrt{2}$ and $\overline{BC} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so the base is twice the height.

c) The perimeter has the form $a\sin x + b\cos x$ with $a = 2$ and $b = 4$, so apply the **auxiliary-angle formula**. We have $r = \sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}$, and the auxiliary angle lies in the first quadrant, with $\tan\varphi = \dfrac{b}{a} = 2$, so $\varphi = \arctan 2$.

d) Sine is at most $1$, so the perimeter is at most $2\sqrt{5}$. The maximum occurs when $x + \varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and this value is admissible because $\varphi < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and therefore $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \varphi$ lies between $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The angles $x$ and $\varphi$ are complementary, so $\tan x = \dfrac{1}{\tan\varphi} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

From $\tan x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, with $x$ acute, we obtain $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \tan^2 x}} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}$ and $\sin x = \tan x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$. The dimensions are $\overline{AB} = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{5}} = \dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{5}$ and $\overline{BC} = \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5}$, and the perimeter $2\left( \dfrac{4\sqrt{5}}{5} + \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{5} \right) = 2\sqrt{5}$ confirms the maximum value. The rectangle of maximum area and the rectangle of maximum perimeter are therefore different.

</div>
