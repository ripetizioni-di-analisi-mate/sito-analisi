## Exercise 1

Write $f(x) = \sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x$ in the form $r\sin(x + \varphi)$ and find the maximum and minimum of $f$ and the points where they occur.

$$
a = 1 ,
\qquad
b = \sqrt{3} ,
\qquad
r = \sqrt{1 + 3} = 2
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = \dfrac{1}{2} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\varphi = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
f(x) = 2\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{3} \right)
$$
$$
x + \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi \iff x = \frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad
x + \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi \iff x = \frac{7\pi}{6} + 2k\pi
$$

::grafico{f="sin(x)+sqrt(3)*cos(x)" punti="0.524,2,pieno;3.665,-2,pieno;0,1.732,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-2.5 ymax=2.5 titolo="The graph of $\sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x = 2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$ has maximum $2$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and minimum $-2$ at $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression has the form $a\sin x + b\cos x$ with $a = 1$ and $b = \sqrt{3}$, which are not both zero, so the **auxiliary-angle formula** applies. The coefficient $r$ is the distance from the point $(a, b)$ to the origin, $r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = 2$.

The auxiliary angle $\varphi$ must satisfy both conditions $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{a}{r}$ and $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{b}{r}$, which form a system. The values $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ are the cosine and sine of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. This gives $f(x) = 2\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$.

The sine of any angle ranges between $-1$ and $1$, so $f$ ranges between $-2$ and $2$. The maximum $2$ occurs when $\sin\left( x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = 1$, that is, when $x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. Subtracting $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ because $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

The minimum $-2$ occurs when the sine equals $-1$, that is, at $x + \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. Since $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{9\pi - 2\pi}{6} = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, the minimum occurs at $x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$.

At $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ we have $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2} + \sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{3}{2} = 2$, in agreement with the maximum. The graph is the graph of $2\sin x$ shifted to the left by $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and passes through $\left( 0, \sqrt{3} \right)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write $g(x) = \cos x - \sin x$ in the form $r\sin(x + \varphi)$ and find its maximum points.

$$
a = -1 ,
\qquad
b = 1 ,
\qquad
r = \sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\varphi = \frac{3\pi}{4}
$$
$$
g(x) = \sqrt{2}\sin\left( x + \frac{3\pi}{4} \right)
$$
$$
x + \frac{3\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi \iff x = -\frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Rearrange the expression in the form $a\sin x + b\cos x$, that is, $g(x) = -1 \cdot \sin x + 1 \cdot \cos x$. The coefficient of the sine is therefore $a = -1$ and that of the cosine is $b = 1$. In the stated expression the cosine comes first, whereas in the formula $a$ is the coefficient of the sine.

By the **auxiliary-angle formula**, $r = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$. The conditions on the auxiliary angle are $\cos\varphi = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ after rationalizing $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. The negative cosine and positive sine place the angle in the second quadrant, and the second-quadrant angle corresponding to $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.

In this case $a < 0$, so the angle $\arctan\dfrac{b}{a} = \arctan(-1) = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ does not satisfy the conditions, and the rule in the definition gives $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \pi = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, the same value.

This gives $g(x) = \sqrt{2}\sin\left( x + \dfrac{3\pi}{4} \right)$, with maximum $\sqrt{2}$ and minimum $-\sqrt{2}$. The maximum occurs when $x + \dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, and since $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, the maximum points are $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$.

For $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ we have $\cos\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so $g\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given $h(x) = 3\sin x - 4\cos x + 2$, find the range of $h$ and the values of the real parameter $m$ for which the equation $h(x) = m$ has solutions.

$$
a = 3 ,
\qquad
b = -4 ,
\qquad
r = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\cos\varphi = \dfrac{3}{5} \\[8pt]
\sin\varphi = -\dfrac{4}{5}
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\varphi = \arctan\left( -\frac{4}{3} \right) = -\arctan\frac{4}{3}
$$
$$
h(x) = 5\sin(x + \varphi) + 2
$$
$$
-1 \leq \sin(x + \varphi) \leq 1
\iff
-5 \leq 5\sin(x + \varphi) \leq 5
\iff
-3 \leq h(x) \leq 7
$$
$$
h(x) = m \ \text{has solutions} \iff -3 \leq m \leq 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant term $2$ does not affect the shape of the function, so apply the **auxiliary-angle formula** only to $3\sin x - 4\cos x$, with $a = 3$ and $b = -4$. This gives $r = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

The auxiliary angle has positive cosine $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and negative sine $-\dfrac{4}{5}$, so it lies in the fourth quadrant. This angle is not a standard special angle. Since $a > 0$, the angle $\varphi = \arctan\dfrac{b}{a} = \arctan\left( -\dfrac{4}{3} \right)$ satisfies both conditions, and since arctangent is odd we can write $\varphi = -\arctan\dfrac{4}{3}$. Therefore $h(x) = 5\sin(x + \varphi) + 2$.

The sine of $x + \varphi$ takes every value between $-1$ and $1$ as $x$ varies, because $x + \varphi$ takes every real value. Multiplying the inequality by $5$ and then adding $2$ shows that $h$ takes exactly the values in the interval $[-3, 7]$.

The equation $h(x) = m$ has solutions if and only if $m$ is one of the values taken by $h$. Therefore the equation **has solutions** for $-3 \leq m \leq 7$ and has none for the other values of $m$.

The maximum $7$ occurs when $x + \varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, that is, at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + \arctan\dfrac{4}{3} + 2k\pi$. At that point $\sin x = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\cos x = -\dfrac{4}{5}$, and indeed $h = \dfrac{9}{5} + \dfrac{16}{5} + 2 = 7$.

</div>
