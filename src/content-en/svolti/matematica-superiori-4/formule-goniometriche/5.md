## Exercise 1

Given $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = 3$ with $0 < x < 2\pi$, calculate $\sin x$, $\cos x$, and $\tan x$ and identify the quadrant of $x$.

$$
t = 3 ,
\qquad
1 + t^2 = 10
$$
$$
\sin x = \frac{2t}{1 + t^2} = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5} ,
\qquad
\cos x = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2} = \frac{1 - 9}{10} = -\frac{4}{5}
$$
$$
\tan x = \frac{2t}{1 - t^2} = \frac{6}{-8} = -\frac{3}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given value is the tangent of the half-angle, so use the **tangent half-angle substitution** with $t = \tan\dfrac{x}{2} = 3$. The conditions are satisfied because the tangent of $\dfrac{x}{2}$ is defined and therefore $x \neq \pi + 2k\pi$. The common denominator in the formulas is $1 + t^2 = 1 + 9 = 10$.

The sine is $\dfrac{2 \cdot 3}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and the cosine is $\dfrac{1 - 9}{10} = -\dfrac{8}{10} = -\dfrac{4}{5}$. The substitution formulas contain no square roots, so the signs obtained are already correct.

For the tangent use the third formula, which holds because $\cos x \neq 0$, that is, because $1 - t^2 = -8$ differs from $0$. The result $-\dfrac{3}{4}$ agrees with the ratio $\dfrac{3}{5} : \left( -\dfrac{4}{5} \right)$.

The positive sine and negative cosine indicate that $x$ lies in the **second quadrant**. This agrees with the given information. Indeed, $0 < x < 2\pi$ implies $0 < \dfrac{x}{2} < \pi$, and in this interval tangent is positive only for $0 < \dfrac{x}{2} < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Moreover, $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = 3 > 1 = \tan\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, and tangent is increasing in the first quadrant, so $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < \dfrac{x}{2} < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Multiplying by $2$ gives $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < x < \pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify the identity
$$
\frac{1 + \sin x - \cos x}{1 + \sin x + \cos x} = \tan\frac{x}{2}
$$
using the tangent half-angle substitution, and state the values of $x$ for which it holds.

$$
1 + \sin x - \cos x = \frac{1 + t^2 + 2t - 1 + t^2}{1 + t^2} = \frac{2t^2 + 2t}{1 + t^2} = \frac{2t\,(t + 1)}{1 + t^2}
$$
$$
1 + \sin x + \cos x = \frac{1 + t^2 + 2t + 1 - t^2}{1 + t^2} = \frac{2t + 2}{1 + t^2} = \frac{2\,(t + 1)}{1 + t^2}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x \neq \pi + 2k\pi \\[4pt]
t \neq -1
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x \neq \pi + 2k\pi \\[4pt]
x \neq -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\frac{1 + \sin x - \cos x}{1 + \sin x + \cos x} = \frac{2t\,(t + 1)}{1 + t^2} \cdot \frac{1 + t^2}{2\,(t + 1)} = t = \tan\frac{x}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is $t = \tan\dfrac{x}{2}$, so transform the left-hand side using the **tangent half-angle substitution** with the aim of obtaining $t$. The substitution requires $x \neq \pi + 2k\pi$. At the excluded values the right-hand side is also undefined because $\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$.

In the numerator write $1$ as $\dfrac{1 + t^2}{1 + t^2}$ and substitute $\sin x = \dfrac{2t}{1 + t^2}$ and $\cos x = \dfrac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}$. The minus sign before the cosine reverses the signs in $1 - t^2$, leaving $2t^2 + 2t$ in the numerator, from which we factor out $2t$. In the denominator of the original fraction, $t^2$ and $-t^2$ cancel, leaving $2t + 2 = 2\,(t + 1)$.

The original fraction is defined when its denominator differs from $0$, that is, when $t \neq -1$. The condition $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = -1$ holds for $\dfrac{x}{2} = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$, that is, for $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. Both conditions must hold, so write them as a system.

Under these conditions, divide the two fractions and cancel $1 + t^2$ and $2\,(t + 1)$, leaving $t$. Therefore the identity **holds** for $x \neq \pi + 2k\pi$ and $x \neq -\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. At $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the right-hand side equals $\tan\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = -1$, but the left-hand side is undefined because its denominator is $1 - 1 + 0 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The line passing through $A(-1, 0)$ with slope $m = \dfrac{1}{3}$ meets the unit circle at $A$ and at a second point $P$. Find the coordinates of $P$ and the value of $\tan x$, where $x$ is the central angle corresponding to $P$.

$$
t = \tan\frac{x}{2} = m = \frac{1}{3} ,
\qquad
1 + t^2 = \frac{10}{9}
$$
$$
\cos x = \frac{1 - \dfrac{1}{9}}{\dfrac{10}{9}} = \frac{8}{9} \cdot \frac{9}{10} = \frac{4}{5} ,
\qquad
\sin x = \frac{\dfrac{2}{3}}{\dfrac{10}{9}} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{10} = \frac{3}{5}
$$
$$
P\left( \frac{4}{5}, \frac{3}{5} \right) ,
\qquad
\tan x = \frac{2t}{1 - t^2} = \frac{\dfrac{2}{3}}{\dfrac{8}{9}} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" linee="-1,0 1.3,0.767" vettori="0,0,0.8,0.6,P" punti="-1,0,pieno,A;0,0.333,pieno,T" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The line through $A(-1, 0)$ with slope $\dfrac{1}{3}$ meets the circle at $P\left( \dfrac{4}{5}, \dfrac{3}{5} \right)$ and the $y$-axis at $T\left( 0, \dfrac{1}{3} \right)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **geometric interpretation of the parameter**, the line joining $A(-1, 0)$ to the point $P(\cos x, \sin x)$ on the circle has slope $\dfrac{\sin x}{1 + \cos x}$, and by the tangent half-angle formula this quotient equals $\tan\dfrac{x}{2}$. Therefore the slope $m$ of the line is the parameter $t$ in the **tangent half-angle substitution**, and we set $t = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

The common denominator is $1 + \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{10}{9}$. The cosine has numerator $1 - \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{8}{9}$, and dividing by $\dfrac{10}{9}$ is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal $\dfrac{9}{10}$, giving $\dfrac{4}{5}$. The sine has numerator $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}$, and the same calculation gives $\dfrac{3}{5}$. The coordinates of $P$ are therefore $\left( \dfrac{4}{5}, \dfrac{3}{5} \right)$.

The point lies on the line because its equation is $y = \dfrac{1}{3}(x + 1)$ and substituting $x = \dfrac{4}{5}$ gives $y = \dfrac{1}{3} \cdot \dfrac{9}{5} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.

For $\tan x$, use the third substitution formula, which holds because $1 - t^2 = \dfrac{8}{9} \neq 0$. The result $\dfrac{3}{4}$ is also the ratio of the vertical to the horizontal coordinate of $P$. The point $P$ lies in the first quadrant, so the angle $x$ is acute, and the angle $O\widehat{A}P$, which measures $\dfrac{x}{2}$, has tangent $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

</div>
