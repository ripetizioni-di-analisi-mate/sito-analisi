## Exercise 1

Write the expressions $\sin 5x - \sin 3x$ and $\cos 7x + \cos 3x$ as products.

$$
\frac{5x + 3x}{2} = 4x ,
\qquad
\frac{5x - 3x}{2} = x
$$
$$
\sin 5x - \sin 3x = 2\cos 4x\sin x
$$
$$
\frac{7x + 3x}{2} = 5x ,
\qquad
\frac{7x - 3x}{2} = 2x
$$
$$
\cos 7x + \cos 3x = 2\cos 5x\cos 2x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each expression is a sum or difference of two functions of the same kind, so the **sum-to-product formulas** apply. Each formula uses half the sum and half the difference of the angles, so find these first.

For $\sin 5x - \sin 3x$ the half-sum is $\dfrac{8x}{2} = 4x$ and the half-difference is $\dfrac{2x}{2} = x$. The formula for a difference of two sines is $\sin p - \sin q = 2\cos\dfrac{p + q}{2}\sin\dfrac{p - q}{2}$. The cosine takes the half-sum as its argument and the sine takes the half-difference. This gives $2\cos 4x\sin x$.

For $\cos 7x + \cos 3x$ the half-sum is $\dfrac{10x}{2} = 5x$ and the half-difference is $\dfrac{4x}{2} = 2x$. The formula for the sum of two cosines is $\cos p + \cos q = 2\cos\dfrac{p + q}{2}\cos\dfrac{p - q}{2}$, so the expression equals $2\cos 5x\cos 2x$.

Both equalities hold for every real $x$ because the sum-to-product formulas hold for every pair of angles. A particular value gives a check. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the first expression equals $\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{2} - \sin\dfrac{3\pi}{2} = 1 - (-1) = 2$, and the product equals $2\cos 2\pi\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 2$.

The product form shows where the expressions are zero. For example, $\sin 5x - \sin 3x = 0$ when $\cos 4x = 0$ or $\sin x = 0$. See the chapter on trigonometric equations.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the exact values of $\sin 75^\circ\cos 15^\circ$ and $\cos 105^\circ + \cos 15^\circ$ without using the sine and cosine of $15^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin 75^\circ\cos 15^\circ &= \frac{1}{2}\big( \sin(75^\circ + 15^\circ) + \sin(75^\circ - 15^\circ) \big) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{2}\left( \sin 90^\circ + \sin 60^\circ \right) = \frac{1}{2}\left( 1 + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = \frac{2 + \sqrt{3}}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos 105^\circ + \cos 15^\circ &= 2\cos\frac{105^\circ + 15^\circ}{2}\cos\frac{105^\circ - 15^\circ}{2} \\[6pt]
&= 2\cos 60^\circ\cos 45^\circ = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first expression is the product of a sine and a cosine, so use the first of **Werner's formulas**, $\sin\alpha\cos\beta = \dfrac{1}{2}\big( \sin(\alpha + \beta) + \sin(\alpha - \beta) \big)$, with $\alpha = 75^\circ$ and $\beta = 15^\circ$. The sum and difference of the angles are $90^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, both special angles. Since $\sin 90^\circ = 1$ and $\sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, their sum is $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$, and half of it is $\dfrac{2 + \sqrt{3}}{4}$.

The second expression is a sum of two cosines, so use the third **sum-to-product formula**. Half the sum of the angles is $\dfrac{120^\circ}{2} = 60^\circ$ and half their difference is $\dfrac{90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ$, again two special angles. The product $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

The results can be compared with the table of values for angles other than the standard special angles. We have $\sin 75^\circ = \cos 15^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$, so the first product is $\dfrac{\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right)^2}{16} = \dfrac{8 + 2\sqrt{12}}{16} = \dfrac{8 + 4\sqrt{3}}{16} = \dfrac{2 + \sqrt{3}}{4}$, the same value as before. Werner's formulas and the sum-to-product formulas shorten the calculation because the angles combine to give special angles before their values are substituted.

In the second calculation, $\cos 105^\circ$ is negative and $\cos 15^\circ$ is positive. Their sum is positive because $\cos 15^\circ > \left| \cos 105^\circ \right| = \cos 75^\circ$, since cosine is decreasing in the first quadrant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Verify the identity
$$
\frac{\sin 3x + \sin x}{\cos 3x + \cos x} = \tan 2x
$$
and state the values of $x$ for which it holds.

$$
\sin 3x + \sin x = 2\sin 2x\cos x ,
\qquad
\cos 3x + \cos x = 2\cos 2x\cos x
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\cos 2x \neq 0 \\[4pt]
\cos x \neq 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[8pt]
x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\frac{2\sin 2x\cos x}{2\cos 2x\cos x} = \frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x} = \tan 2x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator and denominator are each the sum of two functions of the same kind with the same angles $3x$ and $x$, so convert both to products using the **sum-to-product formulas**. Half the sum of the angles is $\dfrac{4x}{2} = 2x$ and half their difference is $\dfrac{2x}{2} = x$. The sum of sines becomes $2\sin 2x\cos x$ and the sum of cosines becomes $2\cos 2x\cos x$.

To find where the identity holds, determine the domain restrictions. The left-hand side is defined when the denominator $2\cos 2x\cos x$ differs from $0$, that is, when both factors differ from $0$. The condition $\cos 2x \neq 0$ is equivalent to $2x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, and dividing by $2$ gives $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The condition $\cos x \neq 0$ is equivalent to $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$.

Under these conditions we can cancel the factor $2$ and the factor $\cos x$, which differs from $0$. This leaves the quotient $\dfrac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x}$, which is the tangent of $2x$.

Therefore the identity **holds** for $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the right-hand side exists and equals $\tan\pi = 0$, but the left-hand side is undefined because $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{2} + \cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$. This is why the second condition is part of the result.

</div>
