## Exercise 1

Find the exact values of $\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{8}$, $\cos\dfrac{7\pi}{8}$, and $\tan\dfrac{7\pi}{8}$ using the half-angle formulas.

$$
\alpha = \frac{7\pi}{4} ,
\qquad
\cos\frac{7\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} ,
\qquad
\sin\frac{7\pi}{4} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$
$$
\sin\frac{7\pi}{8} = +\sqrt{\frac{1 - \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2}
$$
$$
\cos\frac{7\pi}{8} = -\sqrt{\frac{1 + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{2}} = -\sqrt{\frac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{4}} = -\frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2}
$$
$$
\tan\frac{7\pi}{8} = \frac{1 - \cos\dfrac{7\pi}{4}}{\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{4}} = \frac{1 - \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = -\frac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = -\left( \sqrt{2} - 1 \right) = 1 - \sqrt{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle $\dfrac{7\pi}{8}$ is half of $\alpha = \dfrac{7\pi}{4}$, which is related to $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and lies in the fourth quadrant. Therefore $\cos\alpha = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\alpha = -\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and the **half-angle formulas** apply.

The quadrant of the half-angle determines the sign before the square root. We have $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \dfrac{7\pi}{8} < \pi$, so $\dfrac{7\pi}{8}$ is in the second quadrant, where sine is positive and cosine is negative. The sine therefore takes the sign $+$ and the cosine the sign $-$.

In the radicands, write $1 - \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{2}$. Dividing by $2$ gives $\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{4}$. The square root of the denominator $4$ is $2$, leaving the radical $\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}$ in the numerator. The cosine is calculated in the same way using $1 + \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

For the tangent, use the formula $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2} = \dfrac{1 - \cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha}$, which holds because $\alpha = \dfrac{7\pi}{4}$ differs from $k\pi$. This formula contains no radicals, so it gives the correct sign without checking the quadrant. Rationalizing the quotient $\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}$ by multiplying by $\sqrt{2}$ gives $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2} - 2}{2} = \sqrt{2} - 1$.

The tangent is $1 - \sqrt{2}$, a negative number, as expected in the second quadrant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{7}{25}$ with $\pi < \alpha < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, calculate $\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, $\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, and $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.

$$
\frac{\pi}{2} < \frac{\alpha}{2} < \frac{3\pi}{4}
$$
$$
\sin\frac{\alpha}{2} = +\sqrt{\frac{1 + \dfrac{7}{25}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{32}{50}} = \sqrt{\frac{16}{25}} = \frac{4}{5}
$$
$$
\cos\frac{\alpha}{2} = -\sqrt{\frac{1 - \dfrac{7}{25}}{2}} = -\sqrt{\frac{18}{50}} = -\sqrt{\frac{9}{25}} = -\frac{3}{5}
$$
$$
\tan\frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{\dfrac{4}{5}}{-\dfrac{3}{5}} = -\frac{4}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before applying the **half-angle formulas**, determine the quadrant of the half-angle. Dividing by $2$ throughout the inequality $\pi < \alpha < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ gives $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \dfrac{\alpha}{2} < \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$. The half-angle therefore lies in the second quadrant, where sine is positive and cosine is negative.

The sine half-angle formula contains $1 - \cos\alpha = 1 + \dfrac{7}{25} = \dfrac{32}{25}$ because $\cos\alpha$ is negative. Dividing by $2$ gives $\dfrac{16}{25}$, a perfect square with square root $\dfrac{4}{5}$. The quadrant determines the sign $+$.

The cosine formula contains $1 + \cos\alpha = 1 - \dfrac{7}{25} = \dfrac{18}{25}$, and half of this is $\dfrac{9}{25}$. Its square root is $\dfrac{3}{5}$, and the sign is $-$, again because of the quadrant.

The cosine of the half-angle differs from $0$, so the tangent is defined and equals the ratio $-\dfrac{4}{3}$. The radical-free formula $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2} = \dfrac{\sin\alpha}{1 + \cos\alpha}$ gives the same value. Indeed, in the third quadrant $\sin\alpha = -\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{49}{625}} = -\dfrac{24}{25}$, and $\dfrac{-\dfrac{24}{25}}{\dfrac{18}{25}} = -\dfrac{4}{3}$.

The figure shows that the point corresponding to the half-angle has coordinates $\left( -\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{4}{5} \right)$, that is, a negative cosine and a positive sine.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given $\tan\alpha = -\dfrac{3}{4}$ with $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$, calculate $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, $\sin\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$, and $\cos\dfrac{\alpha}{2}$.

$$
\cos^2\alpha = \frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{9}{16}} = \frac{16}{25}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\cos\alpha = \frac{4}{5} ,
\qquad
\sin\alpha = \tan\alpha\cos\alpha = -\frac{3}{5}
$$
$$
\tan\frac{\alpha}{2} = \frac{1 - \cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{5}}{-\dfrac{3}{5}} = -\frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
\frac{3\pi}{4} < \frac{\alpha}{2} < \pi
$$
$$
\sin\frac{\alpha}{2} = +\sqrt{\frac{1 - \dfrac{4}{5}}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{10}} = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{10} ,
\qquad
\cos\frac{\alpha}{2} = -\sqrt{\frac{1 + \dfrac{4}{5}}{2}} = -\sqrt{\frac{9}{10}} = -\frac{3\sqrt{10}}{10}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The half-angle formulas use $\cos\alpha$ and $\sin\alpha$, so first find these values from the tangent. By the identity $\cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{1}{1 + \tan^2\alpha}$, we have $\cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{16}{25}$. In the fourth quadrant the cosine is positive, so $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$, and the sine, obtained by multiplying the tangent by the cosine, is $-\dfrac{3}{5}$.

For the tangent use $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2} = \dfrac{1 - \cos\alpha}{\sin\alpha}$, which holds because $\alpha$ differs from $k\pi$. The formula contains no radicals, so the result already has the correct sign and $\tan\dfrac{\alpha}{2} = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.

For the sine and cosine of the half-angle, use the **half-angle formulas** and select the signs. Dividing by $2$ throughout the condition $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$ gives $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} < \dfrac{\alpha}{2} < \pi$, so the half-angle lies in the second quadrant. The sine therefore takes the sign $+$ and the cosine the sign $-$.

The radicands are $\dfrac{1}{10}$ and $\dfrac{9}{10}$. Rationalizing gives $\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{10}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{10}$, and similarly $\sqrt{\dfrac{9}{10}} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{10}}{10}$. The ratio of the sine and cosine just found is $-\dfrac{1}{3}$, which agrees with the tangent calculated earlier.

</div>
