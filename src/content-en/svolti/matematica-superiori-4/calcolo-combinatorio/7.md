## Exercise 1

Expand $(x + 3)^4$ using the binomial theorem.

$$
(x + 3)^4 = \binom{4}{0} x^4 + \binom{4}{1} x^3 \cdot 3 + \binom{4}{2} x^2 \cdot 3^2 + \binom{4}{3} x \cdot 3^3 + \binom{4}{4} \, 3^4
$$
$$
= 1 \cdot x^4 + 4 \cdot 3 \, x^3 + 6 \cdot 9 \, x^2 + 4 \cdot 27 \, x + 1 \cdot 81
$$
$$
= x^4 + 12x^3 + 54x^2 + 108x + 81
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the **binomial theorem** with $a = x$, $b = 3$, and $n = 4$. The expansion has $n + 1 = 5$ terms. In the term in position $k + 1$, the exponent of $x$ is $4 - k$, the exponent of $3$ is $k$, and the coefficient is $\dbinom{4}{k}$.

The binomial coefficients $\dbinom{4}{0}, \ldots, \dbinom{4}{4}$ form row $4$ of Pascal’s triangle, namely $1, 4, 6, 4, 1$. The powers of $3$ are $3^0 = 1$, $3^1 = 3$, $3^2 = 9$, $3^3 = 27$, and $3^4 = 81$.

In each term multiply the binomial coefficient by the power of $3$: $4 \cdot 3 = 12$, $6 \cdot 9 = 54$, and $4 \cdot 27 = 108$. The expansion is therefore $x^4 + 12x^3 + 54x^2 + 108x + 81$.

Check the result with $x = 1$. The left-hand side equals $4^4 = 256$ and the sum of the coefficients of the expansion is $1 + 12 + 54 + 108 + 81 = 256$, so the two values agree.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the coefficient of $x^5$ in the expansion of $(2x - 1)^7$.

$$
T_{k + 1} = \binom{7}{k} (2x)^{7 - k} (-1)^k = \binom{7}{k} \, 2^{7 - k} (-1)^k \, x^{7 - k}
$$
$$
7 - k = 5
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
k = 2
$$
$$
T_3 = \binom{7}{2} \, 2^5 \, (-1)^2 \, x^5 = 21 \cdot 32 \cdot 1 \cdot x^5 = 672 \, x^5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Writing out all eight terms would be lengthy, so use the **general term** $T_{k + 1} = \dbinom{n}{k} a^{n - k} b^k$ with $a = 2x$, $b = -1$, and $n = 7$. In the power $(2x)^{7 - k}$, the exponent applies both to the coefficient $2$ and to $x$, so $(2x)^{7 - k} = 2^{7 - k} x^{7 - k}$. The term $b = -1$ is raised to the power with its sign, giving $(-1)^k$.

The exponent of $x$ in the general term is $7 - k$. The required term has exponent $5$, so $7 - k = 5$ and $k = 2$. This value is admissible since $k$ is a natural number between $0$ and $7$.

With $k = 2$, the binomial coefficient is $\dbinom{7}{2} = \dfrac{7 \cdot 6}{2} = 21$, the power of $2$ is $2^5 = 32$, and the sign factor is $(-1)^2 = 1$. The product is $21 \cdot 32 = 672$, so the coefficient of $x^5$ is $672$, positive since $k$ is even.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the expansion of $\left( x - \dfrac{1}{x^2} \right)^9$, with $x \neq 0$, find the term independent of $x$ and the sum of all coefficients.

$$
T_{k + 1} = \binom{9}{k} x^{9 - k} \left( -x^{-2} \right)^k = \binom{9}{k} (-1)^k \, x^{9 - k - 2k} = \binom{9}{k} (-1)^k \, x^{9 - 3k}
$$
$$
9 - 3k = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
k = 3
$$
$$
T_4 = \binom{9}{3} (-1)^3 = 84 \cdot (-1) = -84
$$
$$
\text{sum of the coefficients} = \left( 1 - \frac{1}{1^2} \right)^9 = 0^9 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the **general term** of the expansion with $a = x$, $b = -\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, and $n = 9$. Write the term $b$ as a power of $x$ with a negative exponent, $b = -x^{-2}$, so that the powers of $x$ are multiplied by adding their exponents. We have $\left( -x^{-2} \right)^k = (-1)^k x^{-2k}$, and after multiplying by $x^{9 - k}$ the exponent of $x$ becomes $9 - k - 2k = 9 - 3k$.

The term is independent of $x$ when the exponent is zero, so $9 - 3k = 0$ and $k = 3$, a natural number between $0$ and $9$. The binomial coefficient is $\dbinom{9}{3} = \dfrac{9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7}{6} = 84$ and the sign factor is $(-1)^3 = -1$, so the term independent of $x$ is $-84$.

The sum of the coefficients of a polynomial in $x$ is obtained by substituting $x = 1$, since each power of $1$ then equals $1$. The same holds for this expansion, which also contains powers of $x$ with negative exponents. At $x = 1$ the base becomes $1 - 1 = 0$, so the sum of the coefficients is $0^9 = 0$. This equals the alternating sum of the binomial coefficients in row $9$, which is zero by the identity for the sum of binomial coefficients.

</div>
