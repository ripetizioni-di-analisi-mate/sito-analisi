## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $D_{n,2} = 42$ for the natural number $n$.

$$
n \geq 2
$$
$$
n \, (n - 1) = 42
$$
$$
n^2 - n - 42 = 0
$$
$$
n = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 168}}{2} = \frac{1 \pm 13}{2}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
n = 7 \quad \text{or} \quad n = -6
$$
$$
n = 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbol $D_{n,2}$ is defined only when $2$ objects are selected from at least $2$, so the **domain restriction** is $n \geq 2$, with $n$ a natural number.

By the formula for ordered selections without repetition, $D_{n,2}$ is the product of $2$ consecutive decreasing numbers starting with $n$, namely $n \, (n - 1)$. The equation therefore becomes $n \, (n - 1) = 42$. Expanding the product and moving everything to the left gives the quadratic equation $n^2 - n - 42 = 0$.

The discriminant is $\Delta = (-1)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot (-42) = 1 + 168 = 169$ and its square root is $13$. The quadratic formula gives $n = \dfrac{1 + 13}{2} = 7$ and $n = \dfrac{1 - 13}{2} = -6$.

The solution $-6$ is not a natural number and therefore does not satisfy the domain restriction, so discard it. The solution $n = 7$ satisfies $n \geq 2$, and checking gives $D_{7,2} = 7 \cdot 6 = 42$. The equation therefore has **exactly one solution**, $n = 7$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $C_{n,3} = 2 \, C_{n,2}$ for the natural number $n$.

$$
n \geq 3
$$
$$
\frac{n \, (n - 1)(n - 2)}{6} = 2 \cdot \frac{n \, (n - 1)}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{n \, (n - 1)(n - 2)}{6} = n \, (n - 1)
$$
$$
\frac{n - 2}{6} = 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
n - 2 = 6
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
n = 8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbol $C_{n,3}$ requires $n \geq 3$ and the symbol $C_{n,2}$ requires $n \geq 2$. Both conditions must hold, so the domain restriction is $n \geq 3$.

By the formula for **combinations without repetition**, $C_{n,k} = \dfrac{D_{n,k}}{k!}$, we have $C_{n,3} = \dfrac{n \, (n - 1)(n - 2)}{3!}$, with $3! = 6$, and $C_{n,2} = \dfrac{n \, (n - 1)}{2}$. On the right-hand side the factor $2$ cancels with the denominator $2$, leaving $n \, (n - 1)$.

Since $n \geq 3$, the product $n \, (n - 1)$ differs from $0$, so both sides can be divided by $n \, (n - 1)$. This gives $\dfrac{n - 2}{6} = 1$, or $n - 2 = 6$, and hence $n = 8$. Division is valid only because the factor differs from $0$. The values $n = 0$ and $n = 1$, which make the factor zero, do not satisfy the domain restriction.

The value $n = 8$ satisfies $n \geq 3$. Checking gives $C_{8,3} = 56$ and $2 \, C_{8,2} = 2 \cdot 28 = 56$, so the solution is $n = 8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation
$$
\frac{(n + 2)!}{n!} - \frac{n!}{(n - 1)!} = 37
$$
for the natural number $n$.

$$
n \geq 1
$$
$$
\frac{(n + 2)!}{n!} = \frac{(n + 2)(n + 1) \, n!}{n!} = (n + 2)(n + 1)
$$
$$
\frac{n!}{(n - 1)!} = \frac{n \, (n - 1)!}{(n - 1)!} = n
$$
$$
(n + 2)(n + 1) - n = 37
$$
$$
n^2 + 3n + 2 - n - 37 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
n^2 + 2n - 35 = 0
$$
$$
(n + 7)(n - 5) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
n = 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factorials are defined for natural numbers, so $(n - 1)!$ requires $n - 1 \geq 0$, or $n \geq 1$. The other factorials, $n!$ and $(n + 2)!$, are then defined as well.

To simplify the quotients use the relation $m! = m \cdot (m - 1)!$, which follows from the definition of a **factorial**. Applying it twice gives $(n + 2)! = (n + 2)(n + 1) \, n!$, so the first quotient equals $(n + 2)(n + 1)$. Applying it once gives $n! = n \, (n - 1)!$, so the second quotient equals $n$.

The equation becomes $(n + 2)(n + 1) - n = 37$. The product equals $n^2 + 3n + 2$, and moving everything to the left gives $n^2 + 2n - 35 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $2$ and product $-35$ are $7$ and $-5$, so the trinomial factors as $(n + 7)(n - 5)$ and the solutions of the quadratic equation are $n = -7$ and $n = 5$.

The value $-7$ is not a natural number and is discarded, whereas $n = 5$ satisfies $n \geq 1$. Checking gives $\dfrac{7!}{5!} - \dfrac{5!}{4!} = 42 - 5 = 37$, so the solution is $n = 5$.

</div>
