## Exercise 1

Six friends, including Anna and Bea, occupy six adjacent seats in a row at a movie theater. Find the number of ways they can sit, the number with Anna and Bea next to each other, and the number with Anna and Bea apart.

$$
P_6 = 6! = 720
$$
$$
\text{Anna and Bea adjacent} = 5! \cdot 2! = 120 \cdot 2 = 240
$$
$$
\text{Anna and Bea not adjacent} = 720 - 240 = 480
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A seating is an ordering of the $6$ friends in the $6$ seats, that is, a **permutation** of $6$ distinct people. By the counting formula for permutations, the number of orderings is $6! = 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 720$.

When Anna and Bea sit together, treat the pair as a single block. The objects to arrange are the block and the other $4$ friends, giving $5$ objects that can be arranged in $5! = 120$ ways. Within the block, Anna may sit to Bea’s left or right, so the block can be arranged in $2! = 2$ ways regardless of the order of the others. By the **fundamental counting principle** the number of seatings is $120 \cdot 2 = 240$.

In every ordering Anna and Bea are either together or apart, and the two cases are mutually exclusive. By the addition principle, the number of seatings with Anna and Bea apart is therefore the total minus the number with them together: $720 - 240 = 480$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the number of anagrams of the word $CASSETTA$, including arrangements that are not words, and how many start with the letter $C$.

$$
C: 1, \qquad A: 2, \qquad S: 2, \qquad E: 1, \qquad T: 2, \qquad 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 2 = 8
$$
$$
P_8^{(2, 2, 2)} = \frac{8!}{2! \cdot 2! \cdot 2!} = \frac{40\,320}{8} = 5040
$$
$$
\text{anagrams with } C \text{ as the first letter} = \frac{7!}{2! \cdot 2! \cdot 2!} = \frac{5040}{8} = 630
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The word has $8$ letters, and $A$, $S$, $T$ each occur twice. Interchanging the two occurrences of $A$, the two of $S$, or the two of $T$ does not give a new anagram, so the anagrams are **permutations with repeated elements**. The letters $C$ and $E$ occur once, contributing a factor of $1! = 1$ to the denominator, which does not change the result. By the formula for permutations with repeated elements the number of anagrams is $\dfrac{8!}{2! \cdot 2! \cdot 2!}$, and since $8! = 40\,320$ and $2! \cdot 2! \cdot 2! = 8$ this gives $5040$.

In anagrams beginning with $C$, the first letter is fixed and the remaining $7$ letters are to be arranged: $A$, $A$, $S$, $S$, $E$, $T$, $T$. Among these, $A$, $S$, $T$ still occur twice each, so the number of anagrams is $\dfrac{7!}{2! \cdot 2! \cdot 2!} = \dfrac{5040}{8} = 630$.

The second result is $\dfrac{1}{8}$ of the first. The letter $C$ occurs just once among the $8$ letters, and by symmetry it occupies each of the $8$ positions in the same number of anagrams, namely $\dfrac{5040}{8} = 630$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Six people, including Aldo and Bruno, sit around a round table with six seats. Arrangements differing by a rotation are considered the same. Find the total number of arrangements and the number in which Aldo and Bruno are next to each other.

$$
\text{seating arrangements} = (6 - 1)! = 5! = 120
$$
$$
\text{Aldo and Bruno adjacent} = (5 - 1)! \cdot 2! = 24 \cdot 2 = 48
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Arrangements around a round table, with rotations considered equivalent, are **circular permutations**. Fix one person’s seat. The other $5$ people can take the $5$ remaining seats in $5!$ ways, and each seating around the table is obtained exactly once. The number of arrangements is therefore $(6 - 1)! = 120$.

When Aldo and Bruno sit together, treat the pair as a single block. The objects to arrange around the table are then the block and the other $4$ people, giving $5$ objects, with $(5 - 1)! = 4! = 24$ circular permutations. Within the block, Bruno may sit to Aldo’s right or left, so the block can be ordered in $2$ ways regardless of the arrangement of the others. By the **fundamental counting principle** there are $24 \cdot 2 = 48$ arrangements with Aldo and Bruno together.

Fixing Aldo’s seat gives the same count. Bruno chooses one of the $2$ seats next to Aldo, and the other $4$ people take the $4$ remaining seats in $4! = 24$ ways, so there are $2 \cdot 24 = 48$ arrangements.

</div>
