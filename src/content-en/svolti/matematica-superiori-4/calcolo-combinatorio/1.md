## Exercise 1

A code consists of a letter chosen from $A$ and $B$ followed by a digit chosen from $1$, $2$, and $3$. Draw a tree diagram of the codes, find their number, and find how many end in an odd digit.

$$
\text{codes} = 2 \cdot 3 = 6
$$
$$
A1, \quad A2, \quad A3, \quad B1, \quad B2, \quad B3
$$
$$
\text{codes with an odd digit} = 2 \cdot 2 = 4
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 legenda=0 linee="0,0 3,1.6;0,0 3,-1.6;3,1.6 6,2.6;3,1.6 6,1.6;3,1.6 6,0.6;3,-1.6 6,-0.6;3,-1.6 6,-1.6;3,-1.6 6,-2.6" punti="0,0,pieno;3,1.6,pieno;3,-1.6,pieno;6,2.6,pieno;6,1.6,pieno;6,0.6,pieno;6,-0.6,pieno;6,-1.6,pieno;6,-2.6,pieno" etichette="2.8,2.05,A;2.8,-1.15,B;6.5,2.6,A1;6.5,1.6,A2;6.5,0.6,A3;6.5,-0.6,B1;6.5,-1.6,B2;6.5,-2.6,B3" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7.5 ymin=-3.2 ymax=3.2 titolo="Tree diagram of the codes: $2$ branches for the letter and $3$ branches for the digit, giving $2 \cdot 3 = 6$ leaves"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The code is formed in two stages: first choose the letter, then the digit. In the **tree diagram**, $2$ branches leave the root, one per letter. From the end of each of these, $3$ branches extend, one per digit. Every path from the root to a leaf corresponds to one code, so there are as many codes as leaves.

The second stage has $3$ outcomes regardless of the letter, so the **fundamental counting principle** applies and there are $2 \cdot 3 = 6$ codes. Listing the codes at the leaves confirms the count.

For codes ending in an odd digit, the second stage uses only the digits $1$ and $3$, so there are $2$ choices regardless of the letter. By the fundamental counting principle the number of such codes is $2 \cdot 2 = 4$, namely $A1$, $A3$, $B1$, $B3$. There are therefore $6$ codes, of which $4$ end in an odd digit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find how many three-digit natural numbers have only odd digits and how many three-digit natural numbers are even.

$$
\text{odd digits} = \{1, 3, 5, 7, 9\}
$$
$$
\text{numbers with all digits odd} = 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 = 125
$$
$$
\text{even numbers} = 9 \cdot 10 \cdot 5 = 450
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A three-digit number is formed in three stages: choosing its hundreds digit, its tens digit, and its units digit. Digits may repeat, since the problem does not require them to be distinct.

In the first question each digit is chosen from the $5$ odd digits. The hundreds digit cannot be $0$, but this restriction is already satisfied since $0$ is even. Each stage has $5$ outcomes regardless of the preceding digits, so by the **fundamental counting principle** the total is $5^3 = 125$.

In the second question the hundreds digit differs from $0$, since otherwise the number would have fewer than three digits, so there are $9$ choices. The tens digit is unrestricted, with $10$ choices. A number is even when its units digit is even, so the last digit is chosen from $0$, $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ in $5$ ways. By the fundamental counting principle there are $9 \cdot 10 \cdot 5 = 450$ three-digit even numbers.

The second result can also be checked as follows. The three-digit numbers run from $100$ to $999$, with $900$ numbers in total. Even and odd numbers alternate starting at $100$, which is even, so half are even, namely $450$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Roll a die. If the result is even, toss a coin. If it is odd, roll the die again. Find the number of possible outcomes of the experiment, expressed as ordered pairs (first result, second result).

$$
\text{even first roll} = \{2, 4, 6\}, \qquad \text{odd first roll} = \{1, 3, 5\}
$$
$$
\text{outcomes with an even first roll} = 3 \cdot 2 = 6
$$
$$
\text{outcomes with an odd first roll} = 3 \cdot 6 = 18
$$
$$
\text{outcomes} = 6 + 18 = 24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of outcomes at the second stage depends on the first: after an even number the coin has $2$ outcomes, heads or tails, while after an odd number the die has $6$ outcomes. The fundamental counting principle applies only when the second stage has the same number of outcomes for every first-stage outcome, so the product does *not* give the number of outcomes for the entire experiment.

Split the outcomes into two groups according to whether the first roll is even or odd. Within each group, the second stage always has the same number of outcomes. In the first group the first roll has $3$ possibilities and the coin has $2$, so by the **fundamental counting principle** there are $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ outcomes. In the second group the first roll has $3$ possibilities and the second roll has $6$, giving $3 \cdot 6 = 18$ outcomes.

The groups have no common outcomes, since the first roll is even in one group and odd in the other. By the **addition principle** the experiment has $6 + 18 = 24$ outcomes. In the corresponding tree diagram each of the three even branches splits into $2$ branches and each of the three odd branches into $6$.

</div>
