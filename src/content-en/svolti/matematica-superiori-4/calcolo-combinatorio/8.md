## Exercise 1

On a street grid, travel from intersection $O$ to intersection $P$, which is $5$ blocks to the right and $3$ blocks up, moving only right or up. Intersection $Q$ is $2$ blocks to the right and $1$ block up from $O$.

a) Find the number of paths from $O$ to $P$.

b) Find how many paths from $O$ to $P$ pass through $Q$ and how many do not.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad \frac{8!}{5! \cdot 3!} = \frac{8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 56
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad \text{from } O \text{ to } Q: \ \frac{3!}{2! \cdot 1!} = 3,
\qquad
\text{from } Q \text{ to } P: \ \frac{5!}{3! \cdot 2!} = 10
$$
$$
\text{paths through } Q: \ 3 \cdot 10 = 30,
\qquad
\text{paths that avoid } Q: \ 56 - 30 = 26
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 legenda=0 linee="0,0 0,3;1,0 1,3;2,0 2,3;3,0 3,3;4,0 4,3;5,0 5,3;0,0 5,0;0,1 5,1;0,2 5,2;0,3 5,3" punti="0,0,pieno,O;2,1,pieno,Q;5,3,pieno,P" xmin=-0.8 xmax=5.8 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.6 titolo="Grid from $O$ to $P$. Each path has $5$ steps to the right and $3$ up. Paths through $Q$ consist of one stretch from $O$ to $Q$ and another from $Q$ to $P$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each shortest path from $O$ to $P$ is a sequence of $8$ steps, with $5$ steps to the right ($D$) and $3$ steps up ($A$), and every such sequence is a path. The paths are therefore the **permutations with repeated elements** of $8$ letters containing $5$ occurrences of $D$ and $3$ occurrences of $A$, giving $\dfrac{8!}{5! \cdot 3!}$. Simplifying $5!$ leaves $\dfrac{8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6}{6} = 56$ paths.

A path through $Q$ is formed in two stages: one stretch from $O$ to $Q$, with $2$ steps labeled $D$ and $1$ step labeled $A$, and one from $Q$ to $P$, with $5 - 2 = 3$ steps labeled $D$ and $3 - 1 = 2$ steps labeled $A$. The first stretch can be chosen in $\dfrac{3!}{2! \cdot 1!} = 3$ ways and the second in $\dfrac{5!}{3! \cdot 2!} = 10$ ways regardless of the first. By the **fundamental counting principle** the number of paths through $Q$ is $3 \cdot 10 = 30$.

A path from $O$ to $P$ either passes through $Q$ or does not, and the two cases are mutually exclusive. By the addition principle the number of paths that do not pass through $Q$ is $56 - 30 = 26$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A code consists of $3$ distinct letters chosen from $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$ followed by $2$ digits, which may repeat.

a) Find the number of codes.

b) Find how many codes have their three letters in alphabetical order.

c) Find how many codes contain the letter $A$.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad D_{5,3} \cdot 10^2 = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 100 = 6000
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad \binom{5}{3} \cdot 10^2 = 10 \cdot 100 = 1000
$$
$$
\text{c)} \qquad \big( D_{5,3} - D_{4,3} \big) \cdot 10^2 = (60 - 24) \cdot 100 = 3600
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The code is formed in two stages, first letters and then digits. The letters form an ordered sequence of $3$ distinct objects chosen from $5$, an **ordered selection without repetition**, and there are $D_{5,3} = 60$ such sequences. The digits form an ordered sequence of $2$ objects chosen from $10$ with repetition, an ordered selection with repetition, and there are $10^2 = 100$ such sequences. By the fundamental counting principle the number of codes is $60 \cdot 100 = 6000$.

If the letters are in alphabetical order, a triple of letters is determined by the set of its three letters. For instance, the set $\{E, A, C\}$ gives only the sequence $ACE$. The number of letter sequences therefore equals the number of **combinations** from $5$ letters taken $3$ at a time, namely $\dbinom{5}{3} = 10$, and the number of codes is $10 \cdot 100 = 1000$. As a check, among the $3! = 6$ sequences formed with the same three letters, just one is in alphabetical order, and $\dfrac{6000}{6} = 1000$.

For part c), count the complementary case. The letter sequences without $A$ contain $3$ letters chosen from $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$, and there are $D_{4,3} = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 = 24$ such sequences. The number of letter sequences containing $A$ is therefore $60 - 24 = 36$, and the number of codes containing $A$ is $36 \cdot 100 = 3600$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On line $r$ mark $n$ distinct points, and on line $s$, parallel to $r$, mark $4$ distinct points.

a) Find $n$ if the number of triangles whose vertices are among the marked points is $96$.

b) For the value of $n$ found, determine how many quadrilaterals have two vertices on $r$ and two on $s$.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad \binom{n}{2} \cdot 4 + n \cdot \binom{4}{2} = 96
$$
$$
\frac{n \, (n - 1)}{2} \cdot 4 + 6n = 96
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2n^2 - 2n + 6n - 96 = 0
$$
$$
n^2 + 2n - 48 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
(n + 8)(n - 6) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
n = 6
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad \binom{6}{2} \cdot \binom{4}{2} = 15 \cdot 6 = 90
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 legenda=0 linee="-0.5,2 6.5,2;-0.5,0 6.5,0" poligoni="1,2 4,2 3,0 1,0" punti="0,2,pieno;1,2,pieno;2,2,pieno;3,2,pieno;4,2,pieno;5,2,pieno;1,0,pieno;2,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;4,0,pieno" etichette="6.6,2.15,r;6.6,0.15,s" xmin=-1 xmax=7.2 ymin=-0.7 ymax=2.7 titolo="With $n = 6$ points on $r$ and $4$ on $s$, a quadrilateral is formed by choosing $2$ points on $r$ and $2$ on $s$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A triangle has three noncollinear vertices. Three points on the same line are collinear, so a triangle has *two* vertices on one line and the third on the other. The triangles therefore fall into two disjoint groups. Those with two vertices on $r$ are formed by choosing $2$ points from the $n$ on $r$ in $\dbinom{n}{2}$ ways and $1$ point from the $4$ on $s$ in $4$ ways. Those with two vertices on $s$ are formed by choosing $1$ point on $r$ in $n$ ways and $2$ points on $s$ in $\dbinom{4}{2} = 6$ ways. By the fundamental counting principle and the **addition principle**, the number of triangles is $4 \dbinom{n}{2} + 6n$.

The equation is defined for $n \geq 2$, since $\dbinom{n}{2}$ requires at least $2$ points. Substituting $\dbinom{n}{2} = \dfrac{n \, (n - 1)}{2}$ gives $2n \, (n - 1) + 6n = 96$, or $2n^2 + 4n - 96 = 0$. Dividing by $2$ gives $n^2 + 2n - 48 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $2$ and product $-48$ are $8$ and $-6$, so the solutions are $n = -8$, discarded since it is not a natural number, and $n = 6$. Checking gives $4 \cdot 15 + 6 \cdot 6 = 60 + 36 = 96$.

With the lines parallel, two points on $r$ and two on $s$ are the vertices of a trapezoid. The quadrilateral is therefore determined by choosing $2$ points from the $6$ on $r$ and $2$ points from the $4$ on $s$. By the fundamental counting principle the number of quadrilaterals is $\dbinom{6}{2} \cdot \dbinom{4}{2} = 15 \cdot 6 = 90$.

</div>
