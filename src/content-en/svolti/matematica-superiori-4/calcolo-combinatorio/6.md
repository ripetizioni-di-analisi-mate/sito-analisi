## Exercise 1

Calculate $\dbinom{10}{3}$ and $\dbinom{10}{7}$, and verify Pascal’s identity $\dbinom{10}{3} = \dbinom{9}{2} + \dbinom{9}{3}$.

$$
\binom{10}{3} = \frac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = \frac{720}{6} = 120
$$
$$
\binom{10}{7} = \binom{10}{3} = 120
$$
$$
\binom{9}{2} = \frac{9 \cdot 8}{2} = 36,
\qquad
\binom{9}{3} = \frac{9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7}{6} = 84
$$
$$
\binom{9}{2} + \binom{9}{3} = 36 + 84 = 120 = \binom{10}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By definition, $\dbinom{10}{3} = \dfrac{10!}{3! \, 7!}$. The factor $7!$ in the denominator cancels with the corresponding part of $10! = 10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7!$, leaving the three factors $10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 = 720$ in the numerator and $3! = 6$ in the denominator. The result is $120$.

For $\dbinom{10}{7}$ apply the **symmetry of binomial coefficients**. Since $10 - 7 = 3$, we have $\dbinom{10}{7} = \dbinom{10}{3} = 120$, making the calculation with seven numerator factors unnecessary. In terms of subsets, choosing the $7$ elements to include from $10$ is equivalent to choosing the $3$ elements to leave out.

To verify **Pascal’s identity**, calculate the two terms with upper index $9$. The first has two factors in the numerator, $9 \cdot 8 = 72$, divided by $2! = 2$, and equals $36$. The second has three factors, $9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 504$, divided by $3! = 6$, and equals $84$. Their sum is $120$, equal to $\dbinom{10}{3}$, confirming the identity. In Pascal’s triangle the entries $36$ and $84$ lie next to each other in row $9$, and their sum $120$ lies below them in row $10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Prove that for every pair of natural numbers $n$ and $k$ with $1 \leq k \leq n$,
$$
k \binom{n}{k} = n \binom{n - 1}{k - 1} .
$$

$$
k \binom{n}{k} = k \cdot \frac{n!}{k! \, (n - k)!} = \frac{k \cdot n!}{k \, (k - 1)! \, (n - k)!} = \frac{n!}{(k - 1)! \, (n - k)!}
$$
$$
n \binom{n - 1}{k - 1} = n \cdot \frac{(n - 1)!}{(k - 1)! \, \big( (n - 1) - (k - 1) \big)!} = \frac{n!}{(k - 1)! \, (n - k)!}
$$
$$
k \binom{n}{k} = n \binom{n - 1}{k - 1}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the definition of a **binomial coefficient** separately to both sides until they yield the same expression. The assumptions $1 \leq k \leq n$ ensure that both coefficients are defined: $\dbinom{n}{k}$ requires $0 \leq k \leq n$, and $\dbinom{n - 1}{k - 1}$ requires $0 \leq k - 1 \leq n - 1$, which is precisely $1 \leq k \leq n$.

On the left write $k! = k \cdot (k - 1)!$, valid since $k \geq 1$. The factor $k$ in the numerator cancels with the factor $k$ in the denominator, leaving $\dfrac{n!}{(k - 1)! \, (n - k)!}$.

On the right, the second factorial in the denominator is $\big( (n - 1) - (k - 1) \big)! = (n - k)!$. The factor $n$ multiplies $(n - 1)!$, and by the definition of factorial $n \cdot (n - 1)! = n!$. The right-hand side therefore becomes $\dfrac{n!}{(k - 1)! \, (n - k)!}$.

Both sides equal the same expression, so the identity holds for every $n$ and $k$ with $1 \leq k \leq n$. For example, with $n = 5$ and $k = 2$ we have $2 \cdot 10 = 20$ and $5 \cdot 4 = 20$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write rows $7$ and $8$ of Pascal’s triangle starting from row $6$ and use them to calculate $\dbinom{9}{4}$ without factorials. Verify that the entries in row $8$ add up to $2^8$.

$$
\text{row } 6: \quad 1, \ 6, \ 15, \ 20, \ 15, \ 6, \ 1
$$
$$
\text{row } 7: \quad 1, \ 7, \ 21, \ 35, \ 35, \ 21, \ 7, \ 1
$$
$$
\text{row } 8: \quad 1, \ 8, \ 28, \ 56, \ 70, \ 56, \ 28, \ 8, \ 1
$$
$$
\binom{9}{4} = \binom{8}{3} + \binom{8}{4} = 56 + 70 = 126
$$
$$
1 + 8 + 28 + 56 + 70 + 56 + 28 + 8 + 1 = 256 = 2^8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each row of **Pascal’s triangle** begins and ends with $1$. By Pascal’s identity, every interior entry is the sum of the two entries immediately above it in the preceding row. Row $6$ gives the interior entries of row $7$: $1 + 6 = 7$, $6 + 15 = 21$, $15 + 20 = 35$, and then the same entries in reverse order, since row $6$ is symmetric. Row $7$ similarly gives $1 + 7 = 8$, $7 + 21 = 28$, $21 + 35 = 56$, and $35 + 35 = 70$, and the second half of row $8$ mirrors the first.

The number $\dbinom{9}{4}$ is the fifth entry in row $9$, since the row starts at $\dbinom{9}{0}$. By **Pascal’s identity** with $n = 9$ and $k = 4$, we have $\dbinom{9}{4} = \dbinom{8}{3} + \dbinom{8}{4}$, the sum of the fourth and fifth entries of row $8$, so $\dbinom{9}{4} = 56 + 70 = 126$.

Add the symmetric pairs to find the sum of row $8$: $2 \cdot (1 + 8 + 28 + 56) + 70 = 2 \cdot 93 + 70 = 256$. Since $2^8 = 256$, the sum is $2^8$, in agreement with the identity for the sum of binomial coefficients.

</div>
