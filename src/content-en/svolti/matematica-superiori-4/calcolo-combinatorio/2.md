## Exercise 1

A class of $12$ students elects a representative and a deputy, who must be different people. Find how many ways the two offices can be assigned and in how many of these Luca holds one of the offices.

$$
D_{12,2} = 12 \cdot 11 = 132
$$
$$
\text{Luca is the representative} = 11,
\qquad
\text{Luca is the deputy} = 11
$$
$$
\text{Luca holds an office} = 11 + 11 = 22
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An assignment of the offices is an ordered pair of students: the first is the representative and the second is the deputy. Order matters because (Anna, Luca) and (Luca, Anna) assign different offices to the same people. The students must be different, so the assignments are **ordered selections without repetition** from $12$ objects taken $2$ at a time. By the formula for ordered selections without repetition, multiply $2$ consecutive decreasing numbers starting with $12$ to obtain $D_{12,2} = 12 \cdot 11 = 132$.

For the second question, split the assignments in which Luca holds an office into two groups. If Luca is the representative, the deputy is chosen from the other $11$ students, giving $11$ assignments. If Luca is the deputy, the representative is chosen from the other $11$, again giving $11$ assignments. Luca cannot hold both offices, so the groups are disjoint and by the **addition principle** there are $11 + 11 = 22$ assignments.

The offices can be assigned in $132$ ways, and in $22$ of them Luca holds an office.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A phone unlock code consists of $5$ digits, and $0$ is allowed even in the first position. Find how many codes there are, how many have all digits distinct, and how many have at least one repeated digit.

$$
D'_{10,5} = 10^5 = 100\,000
$$
$$
D_{10,5} = 10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6 = 30\,240
$$
$$
\text{codes with at least one repetition} = 100\,000 - 30\,240 = 69\,760
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A code is an ordered sequence of $5$ digits chosen from the $10$ digits from $0$ to $9$, and a digit may appear more than once. The codes are therefore **ordered selections with repetition** from $10$ objects taken $5$ at a time. By the formula $D'_{n,k} = n^k$, there are $10^5 = 100\,000$ codes.

Codes with all digits distinct are **ordered selections without repetition** from $10$ objects taken $5$ at a time. The product contains $5$ consecutive decreasing factors starting with $10$ and ending with $10 - 5 + 1 = 6$. The calculation gives $10 \cdot 9 = 90$, then $90 \cdot 8 = 720$, $720 \cdot 7 = 5040$, and $5040 \cdot 6 = 30\,240$.

A code either has all digits distinct or contains at least one repeated digit, and the two cases are mutually exclusive. By the addition principle, the number of codes with at least one repeated digit is therefore the total number of codes minus the number with all digits distinct, namely $100\,000 - 30\,240 = 69\,760$. A direct count would require many cases, depending on how many and which digits repeat. Using the complementary case requires only one subtraction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find how many four-digit even natural numbers have all digits distinct.

$$
\text{units digit} = 0: \qquad 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 504
$$
$$
\text{units digit} \in \{2, 4, 6, 8\}: \qquad 4 \cdot 8 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 1792
$$
$$
\text{numbers} = 504 + 1792 = 2296
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number satisfies two restrictions: the thousands digit differs from $0$ and the units digit is even. Since $0$ is relevant to both restrictions, split the count into two cases according to whether the units digit is $0$.

In the first case the units digit is $0$. The other three digits are distinct and are chosen from the $9$ digits from $1$ to $9$. Since $0$ has already been used, the restriction on the thousands digit is satisfied. The thousands, hundreds, and tens digits therefore form an ordered selection without repetition, counted by $D_{9,3} = 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 504$.

In the second case the units digit is chosen from $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ in $4$ ways. The thousands digit differs from $0$ and from the units digit, so it is chosen from $8$ digits. The hundreds digit differs from the two already used and may be $0$, so it is chosen from $8$ digits, and the tens digit from the $7$ remaining. The number of outcomes at each stage is independent of previous choices, so by the **fundamental counting principle** the total is $4 \cdot 8 \cdot 8 \cdot 7 = 1792$.

The two cases are disjoint, so by the **addition principle** there are $504 + 1792 = 2296$ four-digit even numbers with distinct digits.

</div>
