## Exercise 1

An aqueous solution has a hydrogen ion concentration of $\left[ \mathrm{H}^+ \right] = 4 \cdot 10^{-5}$ mol/L.

a) Find the pH of the solution and determine whether it is acidic or basic.

b) The solution is diluted with water until its hydrogen ion concentration is $\dfrac{1}{100}$ of the initial concentration. Find the new pH.

c) Find the hydrogen ion concentration of a solution with pH $8.5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{pH}
&= -\log_{10} \left( 4 \cdot 10^{-5} \right) \\[6pt]
&= -\left( \log_{10} 4 + \log_{10} 10^{-5} \right) \\[6pt]
&= 5 - \log_{10} 4 \approx 5 - 0.602 \approx 4.40
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\mathrm{pH}' = -\log_{10} \left( \frac{4 \cdot 10^{-5}}{100} \right) = -\log_{10} \left( 4 \cdot 10^{-5} \right) + \log_{10} 100 \approx 4.40 + 2 = 6.40
$$
$$
\left[ \mathrm{H}^+ \right] = 10^{-8.5} = 10^{-9} \cdot 10^{0.5} = \sqrt{10} \cdot 10^{-9} \approx 3.16 \cdot 10^{-9} \ \text{mol/L}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pH is the negative of the common logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration. In part a) the argument is the product $4 \cdot 10^{-5}$, so by the rule for the **logarithm of a product**, its logarithm is $\log_{10} 4 + \log_{10} 10^{-5}$. The second term equals $-5$ by the equality $\log_a \left( a^x \right) = x$, so changing the sign gives $5 - \log_{10} 4$. A calculator gives $\log_{10} 4 \approx 0.602$, and the pH is approximately $4.40$. The value is less than $7$, so the solution is **acidic**.

In part b) the concentration is divided by $100$. By the quotient rule for logarithms, the logarithm of the new concentration equals the logarithm of the initial concentration minus $\log_{10} 100 = 2$. The pH is the negative of this logarithm, so it increases by $2$ to approximately $6.40$. The diluted solution is still acidic, though less acidic than before.

In part c), use the inverse formula $\left[ \mathrm{H}^+ \right] = 10^{-\mathrm{pH}}$, which follows from the definition of a logarithm. Write the exponent $-8.5$ as $-9 + 0.5$, and the product rule for powers gives $10^{-8.5} = 10^{-9} \cdot 10^{0.5}$. Since $10^{0.5} = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$, the concentration is approximately $3.16 \cdot 10^{-9}$ mol/L. The pH exceeds $7$, so this solution is basic.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The sound intensity level of a sound of intensity $I$ is $L = 10 \log_{10} \dfrac{I}{I_0}$, with $I_0 = 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$. At a point in a room a loudspeaker produces a sound of intensity $I = 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$.

a) Find the sound intensity level at the point.

b) A second identical loudspeaker is turned on, producing the same intensity at that point. The intensities add. Find the new level.

c) Find the smallest number of identical loudspeakers needed to produce a level of at least $80$ dB at the point.

$$
L = 10 \log_{10} \frac{10^{-5}}{10^{-12}} = 10 \log_{10} 10^{7} = 70 \ \text{dB}
$$
$$
L_2 = 10 \log_{10} \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-5}}{10^{-12}} = 10 \log_{10} 2 + 70 \approx 3.0 + 70 = 73.0 \ \text{dB}
$$
$$
10 \log_{10} \frac{n \cdot 10^{-5}}{10^{-12}} \geq 80
\iff
\log_{10} \left( n \cdot 10^{7} \right) \geq 8
\iff
n \cdot 10^{7} \geq 10^{8}
\iff
n \geq 10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In part a) the ratio of the intensity to the reference intensity is a quotient of powers with the same base $10$. Subtract their exponents. Since $-5 - (-12) = 7$, the ratio is $10^7$. Its common logarithm is $7$, and multiplying by $10$ gives a level of **70 dB**.

In part b) the intensities add, so the total intensity is $2 \cdot 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$. By the product rule for logarithms, $\log_{10} \left( 2 \cdot 10^7 \right) = \log_{10} 2 + 7$. Multiplying by $10$ gives $10 \log_{10} 2 + 70$. Since $\log_{10} 2 \approx 0.301$, the level increases by about $3$ dB to approximately $73$ dB. Thus doubling the intensity raises the level by about $3$ dB.

In part c), with $n$ loudspeakers the intensity is $n \cdot 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$. Require the level to be at least $80$ dB and divide both sides by $10$. Write $8 = \log_{10} 10^8$. The base $10$ exceeds $1$, so the **direction is preserved** and the inequality is equivalent to $n \cdot 10^7 \geq 10^8$, that is, $n \geq 10$. Therefore at least $10$ loudspeakers are needed. This agrees with the fact that an increase of $10$ dB corresponds to an intensity $10$ times as large.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider the equation in the unknown $x$
$$
\log_2 x + \log_2 (4 - x) = k ,
$$
where $k$ is a real parameter.

a) Find the domain conditions of the equation.

b) Solve the equation for $k = 1$.

c) Determine for which values of $k$ the equation has solutions and how many it has.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\[4pt]
4 - x > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
0 < x < 4
$$
$$
\log_2 \big( x (4 - x) \big) = k
\iff
x (4 - x) = 2^k
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad
4x - x^2 = 2
\iff
x^2 - 4x + 2 = 0
\iff
x = 2 \pm \sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\text{c)} \qquad
y = 4x - x^2 , \quad 0 < x < 4 :
\qquad
0 < y \leq 4
$$
$$
k < 2 : \ \text{two solutions} ;
\qquad
k = 2 : \ \text{one solution, } x = 2 ;
\qquad
k > 2 : \ \text{no solution}
$$

::grafico{f="4*x-x^2+0*sqrt(x*(4-x));2" punti="0.586,2,pieno;3.414,2,pieno;2,4,pieno;0,0,vuoto;4,0,vuoto" segmenti="0,4,2,4" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5 legenda="y = 4x - x^2;y = 2^k \text{ with } k = 1" titolo="The solutions are the horizontal coordinates of the points common to the parabolic arc $y = 4x - x^2$ for $0 < x < 4$ and the line $y = 2^k$. For $k = 1$ the solutions are $2 \pm \sqrt{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In part a) the arguments $x$ and $4 - x$ of the two logarithms must both be positive, and the system gives $0 < x < 4$.

On the domain both arguments are positive, so the rule for the **logarithm of a product** gives $\log_2 \big( x (4 - x) \big)$ on the left. By the definition of a logarithm, the equation is equivalent to $x (4 - x) = 2^k$, and every solution satisfies $x (4 - x) > 0$ because $2^k > 0$. Only the conditions $0 < x < 4$ remain to be imposed.

In part b) we have $2^1 = 2$, giving $x^2 - 4x + 2 = 0$. The simplified form of the quadratic formula gives $x = 2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{2}$. Since $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$, the solutions are approximately $0.59$ and $3.41$, both in the interval $(0, 4)$.

In part c) the solutions are the horizontal coordinates of the points common to the parabolic arc $y = 4x - x^2$ for $0 < x < 4$ and the horizontal line $y = 2^k$. The parabola opens downward and has its vertex at $(2, 4)$. At the excluded endpoints $x = 0$ and $x = 4$ it takes the value $0$. Therefore the arc takes the values $0 < y \leq 4$, and it takes each value less than $4$ twice. Since $2^k$ is always positive and $2^k$ is increasing, compare $2^k$ with $4 = 2^2$. The equation has **two solutions** for $k < 2$, a single solution, $x = 2$, for $k = 2$, and no solutions for $k > 2$.

</div>
