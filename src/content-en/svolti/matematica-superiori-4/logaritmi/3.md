## Exercise 1

Solve the equations:

a) $\log_3 (2x - 1) = 2$;

b) $\ln \left( x^2 - 8 \right) = 0$.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad
2x - 1 > 0
\iff
x > \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\log_3 (2x - 1) = 2
\iff
2x - 1 = 3^2
\iff
2x = 10
\iff
x = 5
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad
x^2 - 8 > 0
$$
$$
\ln \left( x^2 - 8 \right) = 0
\iff
x^2 - 8 = e^0 = 1
\iff
x^2 = 9
\iff
x = -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both are **elementary logarithmic equations** of the form $\log_a f(x) = c$. By the definition of a logarithm, $\log_a f(x) = c$ is equivalent to $f(x) = a^c$.

In part a) the argument $2x - 1$ must be positive, so $x > \dfrac{1}{2}$. The base is $3$ and the right-hand side is $2$, so the argument must equal $3^2 = 9$. The linear equation $2x - 1 = 9$ gives $x = 5$, which satisfies $x > \dfrac{1}{2}$. Indeed, $\log_3 9 = 2$. Every solution of $f(x) = a^c$ satisfies the domain condition automatically because $a^c$ is positive.

In part b) the base is $e$ and the right-hand side is $0$. Since $e^0 = 1$, the argument $x^2 - 8$ must equal $1$, giving $x^2 = 9$. The solutions are $x = -3$ and $x = 3$.

At both values the argument equals $9 - 8 = 1$, which is positive, so the condition $x^2 - 8 > 0$ holds. There is no need to solve the inequality $x^2 - 8 > 0$ in this exercise, since the solutions of $x^2 - 8 = 1$ make the argument equal to $1$. Thus equation b) has **two solutions**, $x = -3$ and $x = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $\ln x + \ln (x - 3) = \ln 4$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\[4pt]
x - 3 > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
x > 3
$$
$$
\ln \big( x (x - 3) \big) = \ln 4
$$
$$
x (x - 3) = 4
\iff
x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0
\iff
(x - 4)(x + 1) = 0
$$
$$
x = 4 \ \text{admissible},
\qquad
x = -1 \ \text{inadmissible}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains two logarithms, and their arguments $x$ and $x - 3$ must be positive *simultaneously*. Therefore write the two conditions as a system. The second condition, $x > 3$, implies the first, so the domain of the equation is $x > 3$.

For $x > 3$ both arguments are positive, so the rule for the **logarithm of a product** applies and the left-hand side becomes $\ln \big( x (x - 3) \big)$. The equation now has the form $\ln A(x) = \ln B(x)$ with the same base. The natural logarithm is strictly increasing, so two natural logarithms are equal only if their arguments are equal. This gives the quadratic equation $x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0$.

To factor the trinomial, find two numbers with sum $3$ and product $-4$, namely $4$ and $-1$. This gives $(x - 4)(x + 1) = 0$, with solutions $x = 4$ and $x = -1$.

The value $x = 4$ satisfies $x > 3$. The value $x = -1$ falls outside the domain, because at $x = -1$ the logarithms $\ln (-1)$ and $\ln (-4)$ are undefined. The value $-1$ arose because the product $x (x - 3)$ is positive for $x < 0$ as well. The equation therefore has the **unique solution** $x = 4$, and indeed $\ln 4 + \ln 1 = \ln 4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $(\ln x)^2 - \ln \left( x^2 \right) - 3 = 0$.

$$
x > 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\ln \left( x^2 \right) = 2 \ln x
$$
$$
(\ln x)^2 - 2 \ln x - 3 = 0
$$
$$
t = \ln x :
\qquad
t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0
\iff
(t - 3)(t + 1) = 0
$$
$$
\ln x = 3 \iff x = e^3 ,
\qquad
\ln x = -1 \iff x = e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains $\ln x$ and $\ln \left( x^2 \right)$. The first is defined for $x > 0$ and the second for $x \neq 0$, so the domain of the equation is $x > 0$.

On the domain we have $x > 0$, so the rule for the **logarithm of a power** gives $\ln \left( x^2 \right) = 2 \ln x$. Using the rule loses no solutions because the argument $x$ is positive throughout the domain of the equation.

After this transformation the unknown appears only through $\ln x$, raised to the second and first powers. Set $t = \ln x$ to obtain the quadratic equation $t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0$. The trinomial factors as $(t - 3)(t + 1)$ because $3$ and $-1$ have sum $2$ and product $-3$. The solutions are $t = 3$ and $t = -1$.

Return to the variable $x$ using the definition of the natural logarithm. From $\ln x = 3$ we obtain $x = e^3$, and from $\ln x = -1$ we obtain $x = e^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{e}$. Both values are positive, so they are in the domain, and the equation has **two solutions**, $x = e^3$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{e}$.

</div>
