## Exercise 1

Evaluate the expression $2 \log_3 6 - \log_3 4 + \log_3 \sqrt{3}$.

$$
2 \log_3 6 = \log_3 6^2 = \log_3 36
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_3 36 - \log_3 4 + \log_3 \sqrt{3}
&= \log_3 \frac{36}{4} + \log_3 \sqrt{3} \\[6pt]
&= \log_3 9 + \log_3 \sqrt{3} \\[6pt]
&= \log_3 \left( 9 \sqrt{3} \right)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
9 \sqrt{3} = 3^2 \cdot 3^{\frac{1}{2}} = 3^{\frac{5}{2}}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\log_3 \left( 9 \sqrt{3} \right) = \frac{5}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the arguments, namely $6$, $4$ and $\sqrt{3}$, are positive, and the base $3$ is positive and different from $1$, so the logarithm rules apply. The first two logarithms cannot be evaluated mentally because $6$ and $4$ are not powers of $3$ with rational exponents. For this reason combine the three terms into one logarithm.

The coefficient $2$ multiplying the first logarithm becomes an exponent of its argument by the rule for the **logarithm of a power**. This gives $\log_3 36$.

The difference $\log_3 36 - \log_3 4$ is the logarithm of the quotient $\dfrac{36}{4} = 9$ by the rule for the **logarithm of a quotient**. The sum $\log_3 9 + \log_3 \sqrt{3}$ is the logarithm of the product $9 \sqrt{3}$ by the product rule for logarithms.

It remains to evaluate $\log_3 \left( 9 \sqrt{3} \right)$ from its definition. Write $9 = 3^2$ and $\sqrt{3} = 3^{\frac{1}{2}}$. When powers with the same base are multiplied, their exponents add, and since $2 + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2}$, we obtain $9 \sqrt{3} = 3^{\frac{5}{2}}$. Thus the expression equals $\dfrac{5}{2}$ by the equality $\log_a \left( a^x \right) = x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x > 0$ and $y > 0$, use the logarithm rules to expand $\log_2 \dfrac{8 x^3}{\sqrt{y}}$. Then evaluate it for $x = 4$ and $y = 16$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2 \frac{8 x^3}{\sqrt{y}}
&= \log_2 \left( 8 x^3 \right) - \log_2 \sqrt{y} \\[6pt]
&= \log_2 8 + \log_2 x^3 - \log_2 y^{\frac{1}{2}} \\[6pt]
&= 3 + 3 \log_2 x - \frac{1}{2} \log_2 y
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = 4, \ y = 16:
\qquad
3 + 3 \cdot 2 - \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 = 3 + 6 - 2 = 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The assumptions $x > 0$ and $y > 0$ ensure that every argument appearing in the expansion, namely $8 x^3$, $\sqrt{y}$, $x$ and $y$, is positive. Therefore the rules apply without changing the domain of the expression.

The argument is a quotient, so the rule for the **logarithm of a quotient** gives the difference between the logarithm of the numerator $8 x^3$ and the logarithm of the denominator $\sqrt{y}$. The numerator is a product, and by the product rule its logarithm is $\log_2 8 + \log_2 x^3$. Write the square root as a power with exponent $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

By the rule for the **logarithm of a power**, the exponents $3$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ become coefficients. Finally, $\log_2 8 = 3$ because $2^3 = 8$.

For $x = 4$ and $y = 16$ we have $\log_2 4 = 2$ and $\log_2 16 = 4$. Substitution in the expansion gives $3 + 6 - 2 = 7$. Evaluating the argument directly gives the same value because $\dfrac{8 \cdot 4^3}{\sqrt{16}} = \dfrac{8 \cdot 64}{4} = 128 = 2^7$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given $\log_2 3 = a$ and $\log_2 5 = b$, express the following in terms of $a$ and $b$:

a) $\log_2 \dfrac{45}{4}$;

b) $\log_{15} 8$.

$$
\frac{45}{4} = \frac{3^2 \cdot 5}{2^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2 \frac{45}{4}
&= \log_2 3^2 + \log_2 5 - \log_2 2^2 \\[6pt]
&= 2 \log_2 3 + \log_2 5 - 2 \\[6pt]
&= 2a + b - 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\log_{15} 8 = \frac{\log_2 8}{\log_2 15} = \frac{3}{\log_2 3 + \log_2 5} = \frac{3}{a + b}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In part a), express the argument in prime factors. The numerator is $45 = 9 \cdot 5 = 3^2 \cdot 5$ and the denominator is $4 = 2^2$. By the product and quotient rules, the logarithm of $\dfrac{3^2 \cdot 5}{2^2}$ is the sum of the logarithms of the factors in the numerator minus the logarithm of the denominator. By the power rule, $\log_2 3^2 = 2 \log_2 3 = 2a$. Also, $\log_2 2^2 = 2$. Therefore we obtain $\log_2 \dfrac{45}{4} = 2a + b - 2$.

In part b) the base is $15$, different from the base $2$ used in the given logarithms. By the **change-of-base formula** with $c = 2$, the logarithm $\log_{15} 8$ is the quotient $\dfrac{\log_2 8}{\log_2 15}$. The formula applies because $15$ and $2$ are positive and different from $1$, and the argument $8$ is positive.

The numerator equals $\log_2 8 = 3$. The denominator is the logarithm of the product $15 = 3 \cdot 5$, so it equals $\log_2 3 + \log_2 5 = a + b$. Therefore $\log_{15} 8 = \dfrac{3}{a + b}$. A calculator gives $a \approx 1.585$ and $b \approx 2.322$, and the result is approximately $0.768$.

</div>
