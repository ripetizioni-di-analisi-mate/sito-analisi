## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality $\log_3 (2x + 1) < 2$.

$$
2 = \log_3 9
$$
$$
\log_3 (2x + 1) < \log_3 9
\iff
\begin{cases}
2x + 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
2x + 1 < 9
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x > -\dfrac{1}{2} \\[8pt]
x < 4
\end{cases}
$$
$$
-\frac{1}{2} < x < 4
$$

::grafico{f="log(2*x+1)/log(3);2" punti="4,2,pieno;4,0,vuoto;-0.5,0,vuoto" segmenti="4,0,4,2" asintoti="x=-0.5" xmin=-2 xmax=8 ymin=-3 ymax=4 legenda="y = \log_3 (2x + 1);y = 2" titolo="The graph of $\log_3 (2x + 1)$ lies below the line $y = 2$ for $-\dfrac{1}{2} < x < 4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare the two sides, write the right-hand side as a logarithm to base $3$ as well. Since $3^2 = 9$, we have $2 = \log_3 9$ and the inequality becomes a comparison between two logarithms with the same base.

The base $3$ exceeds $1$, so the function $\log_3$ is strictly increasing and the **direction is preserved**. For the values of $x$ for which the logarithm is defined, the inequality is equivalent to $2x + 1 < 9$.

The domain condition for the logarithm and the inequality between the arguments must hold simultaneously, so write them as a system. The first, $2x + 1 > 0$, gives $x > -\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the second, $2x < 8$, gives $x < 4$.

The solution set is the intersection of the two rays, that is, the interval $-\dfrac{1}{2} < x < 4$. The endpoint $4$ is excluded because the inequality is strict and at $x = 4$ the left-hand side equals $2$ exactly. The endpoint $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ is excluded because there the argument equals $0$ and the logarithm is undefined. On the graph the curve lies below the line $y = 2$ precisely between the asymptote $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the point with horizontal coordinate $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality $\log_{\frac{1}{2}} (x + 3) \geq \log_{\frac{1}{2}} (2x - 1)$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 3 > 0 \\[4pt]
2x - 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
x + 3 \leq 2x - 1
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x > -3 \\[4pt]
x > \dfrac{1}{2} \\[8pt]
x \geq 4
\end{cases}
\iff
x \geq 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both sides are logarithms with base $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Their arguments must be positive, and $x + 3 > 0$ and $2x - 1 > 0$ give the conditions $x > -3$ and $x > \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is less than $1$, so the logarithmic function is **strictly decreasing**. One logarithm to this base is greater than or equal to another only if its argument is less than or equal to the other argument. Therefore the inequality between the arguments has the **direction reversed**, giving $x + 3 \leq 2x - 1$.

Solve the linear inequality by moving the terms involving $x$ to the right and the constants to the left. This gives $3 + 1 \leq 2x - x$, that is, $x \geq 4$.

The system requires all three conditions to hold simultaneously. The condition $x \geq 4$ implies both $x > \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $x > -3$, so the solution set is the ray $x \geq 4$. At $x = 4$ both arguments equal $7$, and the inequality holds with equality. At $x = 5$, on the other hand, we have $\log_{\frac{1}{2}} 8 = -3$ and $\log_{\frac{1}{2}} 9 \approx -3.17$, and the first value exceeds the second, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality $2^x > 3^{x - 1}$.

$$
\ln 2^x > \ln 3^{x - 1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x \ln 2 &> (x - 1) \ln 3 \\[6pt]
x \ln 2 - x \ln 3 &> -\ln 3 \\[6pt]
x \left( \ln 2 - \ln 3 \right) &> -\ln 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\ln 2 - \ln 3 = \ln \frac{2}{3} < 0
$$
$$
x < \frac{-\ln 3}{\ln 2 - \ln 3} = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 3 - \ln 2} = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln \frac{3}{2}} \approx 2.710
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both sides are exponentials with different bases, $2$ and $3$, and are positive for every $x$. The natural logarithm is **strictly increasing**, so taking it on both sides preserves the direction and gives an equivalent inequality.

By the power rule for logarithms, the exponents become coefficients and we obtain the linear inequality $x \ln 2 > (x - 1) \ln 3$. Expand the right-hand side, $(x - 1) \ln 3 = x \ln 3 - \ln 3$, and move the terms involving $x$ to the left. Factoring out $x$ gives the coefficient $\ln 2 - \ln 3$.

The coefficient is **negative** because it equals $\ln \dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2}{3} < 1$. Dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number reverses the direction, so $>$ becomes $<$.

Finally, multiply the numerator and denominator of the quotient by $-1$ so that both are positive, and write $\ln 3 - \ln 2 = \ln \dfrac{3}{2}$. The solutions are therefore $x < \dfrac{\ln 3}{\ln \frac{3}{2}}$, approximately $x < 2.710$. For example, $x = 0$ is a solution because $2^0 = 1$ is greater than $3^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

</div>
