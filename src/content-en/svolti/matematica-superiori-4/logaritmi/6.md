## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $3^{x + 1} = 7$ and give an approximate value of the solution.

$$
3^{x + 1} = 7
\iff
x + 1 = \log_3 7
\iff
x = \log_3 7 - 1
$$
$$
x = \log_3 7 - \log_3 3 = \log_3 \frac{7}{3}
$$
$$
x = \frac{\ln \frac{7}{3}}{\ln 3} \approx \frac{0.8473}{1.0986} \approx 0.771
$$

::grafico{f="3^(x+1);7" punti="0.771,7,pieno" segmenti="0.771,0,0.771,7" xmin=-3 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=10 legenda="y = 3^{x + 1};y = 7" titolo="The graph of $3^{x + 1}$ meets the line $y = 7$ at the point with horizontal coordinate $\log_3 \dfrac{7}{3} \approx 0.771$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side $7$ is not an integer power of $3$, so the equation cannot be solved by expressing both sides with the same base. The base $3$ is positive and different from $1$ and the right-hand side $7$ is positive, so the equation has exactly one solution, which can be written using a **logarithm**.

By the definition of a logarithm, $3^{x + 1} = 7$ means that the exponent $x + 1$ is the logarithm to base $3$ of $7$. From $x + 1 = \log_3 7$ we obtain $x = \log_3 7 - 1$.

The solution can be written as a single logarithm. Since $1 = \log_3 3$, the rule for the **logarithm of a quotient** gives $\log_3 7 - \log_3 3 = \log_3 \dfrac{7}{3}$.

For the approximate value, use the change-of-base formula with base $e$ because the calculator has a natural logarithm key. The quotient $\dfrac{\ln \frac{7}{3}}{\ln 3}$ is approximately $0.771$.

The result agrees with the figure. At $x = 0$ the left-hand side equals $3$ and at $x = 1$ it equals $9$, so the solution lies between $0$ and $1$. The function $3^{x + 1}$ is increasing, so its graph intersects the line $y = 7$ only once.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $5^x = 3^{2x - 1}$.

$$
\ln 5^x = \ln 3^{2x - 1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x \ln 5 &= (2x - 1) \ln 3 \\[6pt]
x \ln 5 &= 2x \ln 3 - \ln 3 \\[6pt]
2x \ln 3 - x \ln 5 &= \ln 3 \\[6pt]
x \left( \ln 9 - \ln 5 \right) &= \ln 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 9 - \ln 5} = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln \frac{9}{5}} \approx 1.869
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two sides are powers with **different bases**, $5$ and $3$, which cannot be written as powers of the same base. Both sides are positive for every $x$, so at any solution their natural logarithms are equal, and the original equation is equivalent to the one obtained by applying $\ln$ to both sides. This equivalence holds because the natural logarithm is strictly increasing and therefore takes equal values only at equal inputs.

By the rule for the **logarithm of a power**, the exponents become coefficients, giving the linear equation $x \ln 5 = (2x - 1) \ln 3$. The numbers $\ln 5$ and $\ln 3$ are constants, and the equation is solved like any other linear equation.

Expand the product on the right and move the terms involving $x$ to the left, changing their signs so that the coefficient of $x$ is positive. Factor out $x$. By the power rule, $2 \ln 3 = \ln 9$, so the coefficient is $\ln 9 - \ln 5$.

By the quotient rule, the coefficient is $\ln \dfrac{9}{5}$, which is positive because $\dfrac{9}{5} > 1$. In particular it differs from $0$, so division is valid. The solution is $x = \dfrac{\ln 3}{\ln \frac{9}{5}}$, approximately $1.869$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $4^x - 2^{x + 1} - 3 = 0$.

$$
4^x = \left( 2^2 \right)^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2 ,
\qquad
2^{x + 1} = 2 \cdot 2^x
$$
$$
t = 2^x, \quad t > 0 :
\qquad
t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0
\iff
(t - 3)(t + 1) = 0
$$
$$
t = 3 \ \text{admissible},
\qquad
t = -1 \ \text{inadmissible}
$$
$$
2^x = 3
\iff
x = \log_2 3 = \frac{\ln 3}{\ln 2} \approx 1.585
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The powers $4^x$ and $2^{x + 1}$ have different bases, but both can be expressed in terms of $2^x$. Since $4 = 2^2$, the power-of-a-power rule gives $4^x = 2^{2x} = \left( 2^x \right)^2$. By the product rule for powers, $2^{x + 1} = 2^x \cdot 2$.

The unknown therefore appears only through $2^x$, so make the **substitution** $t = 2^x$. Since an exponential is always positive, the new variable must satisfy $t > 0$. The equation becomes $t^2 - 2t - 3 = 0$, and the trinomial factors as $(t - 3)(t + 1)$ because $-3$ and $1$ have sum $-2$ and product $-3$.

The solution $t = -1$ fails the condition $t > 0$, and indeed the equation $2^x = -1$ has no solutions. This leaves $t = 3$, that is, $2^x = 3$. The number $3$ is not an integer power of $2$, so write the solution as a logarithm, $x = \log_2 3$.

The change-of-base formula gives the approximate value $\dfrac{\ln 3}{\ln 2} \approx 1.585$. The equation therefore has the **unique solution** $x = \log_2 3$. Indeed, substitution gives $4^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2 = 9$ and $2^{x + 1} = 6$, and $9 - 6 - 3 = 0$.

</div>
