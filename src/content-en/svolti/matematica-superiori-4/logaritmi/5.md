## Exercise 1

Sketch the graph of $f(x) = \ln (x - 2) + 1$, giving its domain, asymptote and intercepts.

$$
x - 2 > 0
\iff
x > 2
\qquad
D = (2, +\infty)
$$
$$
\ln (x - 2) + 1 = 0
\iff
\ln (x - 2) = -1
\iff
x - 2 = e^{-1}
\iff
x = 2 + \frac{1}{e} \approx 2.37
$$
$$
f(3) = \ln 1 + 1 = 1
$$

::grafico{f="log(x-2)+1;log(x)" punti="2.368,0,pieno;3,1,pieno" asintoti="x=2" xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-4 ymax=4 legenda="y = \ln (x - 2) + 1;y = \ln x" titolo="The graph of $\ln (x - 2) + 1$ is obtained from that of $\ln x$ by shifting $2$ units to the right and $1$ unit up"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph is obtained from that of $y = \ln x$ by two **shifts**. Replacing $x$ with $x - 2$ shifts the graph $2$ units to the right, and adding $1$ to the function shifts it $1$ unit up.

The domain of $\ln x$ is $x > 0$, so the domain of $\ln (x - 2)$ is $x - 2 > 0$, that is, $x > 2$. For the same reason the vertical asymptote $x = 0$ of $\ln x$ becomes the line $x = 2$. Indeed, as $x$ approaches $2$ from the right, the argument $x - 2$ approaches $0$ and the function tends to $-\infty$.

The graph does not intersect the $y$-axis because $x = 0$ is outside the domain. To find its intersection with the $x$-axis, solve $f(x) = 0$. The equation $\ln (x - 2) = -1$ is elementary and by the definition of the natural logarithm is equivalent to $x - 2 = e^{-1}$. This gives $x = 2 + \dfrac{1}{e}$, approximately $2.37$, which is in the domain.

The point $(1, 0)$ on the graph of $\ln x$ moves to $(3, 1)$, and indeed $f(3) = \ln 1 + 1 = 1$. The function is **increasing** like $\ln x$ because shifts preserve monotonicity. It is negative for $2 < x < 2 + \dfrac{1}{e}$ and positive for $x > 2 + \dfrac{1}{e}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Sketch the graph of $f(x) = 2 - \log_3 x$ and determine where the function is positive.

$$
f(x) = -\log_3 x + 2 ,
\qquad
D = (0, +\infty)
$$
$$
f(1) = 2 ,
\qquad
f(3) = 1 ,
\qquad
f(9) = 0
$$
$$
2 - \log_3 x > 0
\iff
\log_3 x < 2
\iff
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\[4pt]
x < 9
\end{cases}
\iff
0 < x < 9
$$

::grafico{f="2-log(x)/log(3);log(x)/log(3)" punti="1,2,pieno;3,1,pieno;9,0,pieno" asintoti="x=0" xmin=-1 xmax=12 ymin=-3 ymax=5 legenda="y = 2 - \log_3 x;y = \log_3 x" titolo="The graph of $2 - \log_3 x$ is the reflection of the graph of $\log_3 x$ across the $x$-axis, shifted $2$ units up. The function vanishes at $x = 9$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the function as $-\log_3 x + 2$. Its graph is obtained from that of $\log_3 x$ by a **reflection** across the $x$-axis, which reverses the signs of its values, followed by a shift of $2$ units up. The domain remains $(0, +\infty)$, and the $y$-axis remains the vertical asymptote.

The base $3$ is greater than $1$, so $\log_3 x$ is increasing. Its negative $-\log_3 x$ is decreasing, and a shift preserves monotonicity, so $f$ is **decreasing**. As $x$ approaches $0$ from the right, $\log_3 x$ tends to $-\infty$, so $f(x)$ tends to $+\infty$.

To sketch the graph, calculate some points whose arguments are powers of $3$. This gives $f(1) = 2 - 0 = 2$, $f(3) = 2 - 1 = 1$ and $f(9) = 2 - 2 = 0$. The last value gives the intersection with the $x$-axis.

To find where the function is positive, solve $2 - \log_3 x > 0$, that is, $\log_3 x < 2$. Write $2 = \log_3 9$. Since the base $3$ is greater than $1$, the direction is preserved, so $x < 9$. Imposing the domain condition $x > 0$ shows that the function is **positive** for $0 < x < 9$ and negative for $x > 9$, as the graph also shows.

</div>

## Exercise 3

a) Sketch the graph of $f(x) = |\ln (x + 1)|$.

b) As the real number $k$ varies, determine how many solutions the equation $|\ln (x + 1)| = k$ has, and solve it for $k = 1$.

$$
D = (-1, +\infty),
\qquad
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
\ln (x + 1) & \text{if } x \geq 0 \\[4pt]
-\ln (x + 1) & \text{if } -1 < x < 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
k < 0: \ \text{no solution};
\qquad
k = 0: \ x = 0;
\qquad
k > 0: \ \text{two solutions}
$$
$$
k = 1:
\qquad
\ln (x + 1) = 1 \iff x = e - 1 ,
\qquad
\ln (x + 1) = -1 \iff x = \frac{1}{e} - 1
$$

::grafico{f="abs(log(x+1));1" punti="0,0,pieno;1.718,1,pieno;-0.632,1,pieno" asintoti="x=-1" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=4 legenda="y = |\ln (x + 1)|;y = 1" titolo="The line $y = 1$ meets the graph of $|\ln (x + 1)|$ at two points with horizontal coordinates $\dfrac{1}{e} - 1$ and $e - 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph of $\ln (x + 1)$ is the graph of $\ln x$ shifted $1$ unit to the left, with domain $x > -1$ and asymptote $x = -1$, and it passes through the origin. The **absolute value** leaves the part of the graph with nonnegative vertical coordinates unchanged, that is, the part with $x \geq 0$, because there $x + 1 \geq 1$ and $\ln (x + 1) \geq 0$. The part with $-1 < x < 0$ has negative vertical coordinates and is reflected upward across the $x$-axis.

The solutions of $|\ln (x + 1)| = k$ are the horizontal coordinates of the points common to the graph and the horizontal line $y = k$. An absolute value is never negative, so for $k < 0$ the equation has no solutions. For $k = 0$ the line is the $x$-axis, which touches the graph only at the origin, and the solution is $x = 0$. For $k > 0$ the line intersects the left branch once as it falls from $+\infty$ to $0$, and intersects the right branch once as it rises from $0$ to $+\infty$. Thus there are **two** solutions.

For $k = 1$ the equation $|\ln (x + 1)| = 1$ is equivalent to $\ln (x + 1) = 1$ or $\ln (x + 1) = -1$. The first gives $x + 1 = e$, that is, $x = e - 1 \approx 1.72$, and the second gives $x + 1 = e^{-1}$, that is, $x = \dfrac{1}{e} - 1 \approx -0.63$. Both values exceed $-1$ and are in the domain.

</div>
