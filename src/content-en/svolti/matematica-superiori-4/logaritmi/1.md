## Exercise 1

Use the definition to evaluate $\log_4 32$ and $\log_{\frac{1}{9}} 27$.

$$
\log_4 32 = x
\iff
4^x = 32
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left( 2^2 \right)^x &= 2^5 \\[6pt]
2^{2x} &= 2^5 \\[6pt]
2x &= 5
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\log_4 32 = \frac{5}{2}
$$
$$
\log_{\frac{1}{9}} 27 = y
\iff
\left( \frac{1}{9} \right)^y = 27
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left( 3^{-2} \right)^y &= 3^3 \\[6pt]
3^{-2y} &= 3^3 \\[6pt]
-2y &= 3
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\log_{\frac{1}{9}} 27 = -\frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both logarithms are defined because their bases $4$ and $\dfrac{1}{9}$ are positive and different from $1$ and their arguments $32$ and $27$ are positive. By the **definition of a logarithm**, $\log_4 32$ is the exponent $x$ to which $4$ must be raised to obtain $32$, so solve the equation $4^x = 32$.

The numbers $4$ and $32$ are powers of $2$ because $4 = 2^2$ and $32 = 2^5$. By the power-of-a-power rule, $\left( 2^2 \right)^x = 2^{2x}$, and the equation becomes $2^{2x} = 2^5$. Two powers with the same positive base different from $1$ are equal only if their exponents are equal, so $2x = 5$ and $x = \dfrac{5}{2}$. Indeed, $4^{\frac{5}{2}} = \left( \sqrt{4} \right)^5 = 2^5 = 32$.

For the second logarithm write its base and its argument as powers of $3$. Since $9 = 3^2$, its reciprocal is $\dfrac{1}{9} = 3^{-2}$. Also, $27 = 3^3$. The equation $\left( \dfrac{1}{9} \right)^y = 27$ becomes $3^{-2y} = 3^3$, so $-2y = 3$ and $y = -\dfrac{3}{2}$.

The second logarithm is **negative**. This agrees with the fact that the base is less than $1$, because to obtain a number greater than $1$ by raising $\dfrac{1}{9}$ to a power, the exponent must be negative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Use the definition to evaluate $\log_{\sqrt{3}} \left( 9 \sqrt[3]{3} \right)$.

$$
\sqrt{3} = 3^{\frac{1}{2}} ,
\qquad
9 \sqrt[3]{3} = 3^2 \cdot 3^{\frac{1}{3}} = 3^{\frac{7}{3}}
$$
$$
\left( 3^{\frac{1}{2}} \right)^x = 3^{\frac{7}{3}}
\iff
3^{\frac{x}{2}} = 3^{\frac{7}{3}}
\iff
\frac{x}{2} = \frac{7}{3}
$$
$$
\log_{\sqrt{3}} \left( 9 \sqrt[3]{3} \right) = \frac{14}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base $\sqrt{3}$ is positive and different from $1$, and the argument $9 \sqrt[3]{3}$ is positive, so the logarithm is defined. By the definition, we seek the exponent $x$ for which $\left( \sqrt{3} \right)^x = 9 \sqrt[3]{3}$.

Write the radicals as powers with fractional exponents. The square root of $3$ is $3^{\frac{1}{2}}$ and the cube root of $3$ is $3^{\frac{1}{3}}$. The argument is the product of $9 = 3^2$ and $3^{\frac{1}{3}}$. By the product rule for powers with the same base, add their exponents. Since $2 + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{7}{3}$, the argument is $3^{\frac{7}{3}}$.

The left-hand side $\left( 3^{\frac{1}{2}} \right)^x$ equals $3^{\frac{x}{2}}$ by the power-of-a-power rule. Thus the equation sets two powers of $3$ equal, so their exponents must be equal, that is, $\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{7}{3}$. Multiplying both sides by $2$ gives $x = \dfrac{14}{3}$.

The required logarithm is therefore $\dfrac{14}{3}$. The value exceeds $4$, as expected because the argument $9 \sqrt[3]{3} \approx 12.98$ exceeds $\left( \sqrt{3} \right)^4 = 9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

a) Find the real number $x$ such that $\log_x 8 = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

b) Use the value of $x$ to calculate $\log_x \dfrac{1}{2}$ and simplify $x^{\log_x 5}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x > 0 \\[4pt]
x \neq 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
x^{\frac{3}{2}} = 8
$$
$$
x = 8^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left( \sqrt[3]{8} \right)^2 = 2^2 = 4
$$
$$
\log_4 \frac{1}{2} = y
\iff
4^y = \frac{1}{2}
\iff
2^{2y} = 2^{-1}
\iff
y = -\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
4^{\log_4 5} = 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In part a) the unknown is the base of the logarithm. A logarithm's base must be positive and different from $1$, so write these conditions as a system before solving. By the **definition of a logarithm**, $\log_x 8 = \dfrac{3}{2}$ means that $x^{\frac{3}{2}} = 8$.

To find $x$, raise both sides to the exponent $\dfrac{2}{3}$, the reciprocal of $\dfrac{3}{2}$. This is valid because $x > 0$, and the power-of-a-power rule gives $x^{\frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3}} = x$ on the left. We obtain $x = 8^{\frac{2}{3}}$, that is, the square of the cube root of $8$, which equals $2^2 = 4$. The value $4$ satisfies $x > 0$ and $x \neq 1$, so the required base is $x = 4$.

In part b) calculate $\log_4 \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, the exponent $y$ for which $4^y = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Writing $4 = 2^2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2} = 2^{-1}$ gives $2^{2y} = 2^{-1}$, so $2y = -1$ and $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The expression $4^{\log_4 5}$ is a power with base $4$, and its exponent is the logarithm to base $4$ of $5$. By definition, $\log_4 5$ is precisely the exponent to which $4$ must be raised to obtain $5$, so $4^{\log_4 5} = 5$ without calculating the logarithm.

</div>
