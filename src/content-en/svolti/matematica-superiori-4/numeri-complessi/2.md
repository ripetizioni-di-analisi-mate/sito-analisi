## Exercise 1

Calculate the quotient $\dfrac{4 + 7i}{2 + i}$ and write it in algebraic form.

$$
\frac{4 + 7i}{2 + i} = \frac{(4 + 7i)(2 - i)}{(2 + i)(2 - i)}
$$
$$
(2 + i)(2 - i) = 4 - i^2 = 4 + 1 = 5
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(4 + 7i)(2 - i) &= 8 - 4i + 14i - 7i^2 \\[6pt]
&= 8 + 10i + 7 \\[6pt]
&= 15 + 10i
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{4 + 7i}{2 + i} = \frac{15 + 10i}{5} = 3 + 2i
$$

The quotient is $3 + 2i$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $2 + i$ is different from $0$, so the quotient exists. To write it in algebraic form, we need a real denominator. By the conjugate property, the product of a number and its **conjugate** is the real number $a^2 + b^2$. Thus we multiply the numerator and denominator by $2 - i$, the conjugate of $2 + i$. The fraction is unchanged because this amounts to multiplying by $\dfrac{2 - i}{2 - i} = 1$.

The difference-of-squares identity gives a denominator of $2^2 + 1^2 = 5$. Expand the numerator using the distributive property. The term $-7i^2$ equals $+7$ because $i^2 = -1$, so the real part of the numerator is $8 + 7 = 15$ and the imaginary part is $-4 + 14 = 10$.

Finally, divide both the real and imaginary parts by $5$. This gives $\dfrac{15}{5} = 3$ and $\dfrac{10}{5} = 2$, so the quotient in algebraic form is **$3 + 2i$**. The same result can also be written as the equality $(3 + 2i)(2 + i) = 4 + 7i$, which is the definition of the quotient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the real and imaginary parts of $z = \dfrac{1 + 3i}{1 - i} + \dfrac{2 - i}{i}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1 + 3i}{1 - i} &= \frac{(1 + 3i)(1 + i)}{(1 - i)(1 + i)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1 + i + 3i + 3i^2}{1 + 1} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{-2 + 4i}{2} = -1 + 2i
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{2 - i}{i} = \frac{(2 - i)(-i)}{i \cdot (-i)} = \frac{-2i + i^2}{1} = -1 - 2i
$$
$$
z = (-1 + 2i) + (-1 - 2i) = -2
$$

Thus $\operatorname{Re} z = -2$ and $\operatorname{Im} z = 0$, so the number $z$ is real.

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression is the sum of two quotients, each with a denominator different from $0$. Calculate each quotient in algebraic form, then add the results.

In the first quotient, the denominator is $1 - i$, with conjugate $1 + i$. The denominator becomes $(1 - i)(1 + i) = 1 - i^2 = 2$. In the numerator, the term $3i^2$ equals $-3$, so the real part is $1 - 3 = -2$ and the imaginary part is $1 + 3 = 4$. Dividing by $2$ gives $-1 + 2i$.

In the second quotient, the denominator is the purely imaginary number $i$, whose conjugate is $-i$. Multiply the numerator and denominator by $-i$. The denominator becomes $-i^2 = 1$. The numerator is $(2 - i)(-i) = -2i + i^2 = -1 - 2i$, so the second quotient equals $-1 - 2i$.

Add the real parts, $-1 - 1 = -2$, and the imaginary parts, $2 - 2 = 0$, separately. The imaginary part of $z$ is zero, so **$z = -2$ is a real number**. Indeed, the two quotients have the same real part and opposite imaginary parts.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $(2 - i)\,z + 3i = 1 + 2i$ and verify that the solution has modulus $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5}$.

$$
(2 - i)\,z = 1 + 2i - 3i = 1 - i
$$
$$
z = \frac{1 - i}{2 - i}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
z &= \frac{(1 - i)(2 + i)}{(2 - i)(2 + i)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2 + i - 2i - i^2}{4 + 1} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3 - i}{5} = \frac{3}{5} - \frac{1}{5}\,i
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
|z| = \sqrt{\frac{9}{25} + \frac{1}{25}} = \sqrt{\frac{10}{25}} = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{5}
$$

The solution is $z = \dfrac{3}{5} - \dfrac{1}{5}\,i$, with modulus $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is **linear** in the complex unknown $z$ and is solved by the same rules as linear equations in $\mathbb{R}$. First subtract $3i$ from both sides so that only the term containing $z$ remains on the left. The right-hand side becomes $1 + 2i - 3i = 1 - i$.

The coefficient $2 - i$ is different from $0$, so we can divide both sides by $2 - i$. The solution is the quotient $\dfrac{1 - i}{2 - i}$. To write it in algebraic form, multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator, $2 + i$. The denominator becomes $2^2 + 1^2 = 5$. In the numerator, the term $-i^2$ equals $+1$, so the real part is $2 + 1 = 3$ and the imaginary part is $1 - 2 = -1$.

The modulus of $z = a + bi$ is $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$. With $a = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $b = -\dfrac{1}{5}$, the sum of the squares is $\dfrac{10}{25}$ and its square root is $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5}$. The rule for the modulus of a quotient gives the same value because $\dfrac{|1 - i|}{|2 - i|} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{5}}$, which equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{5}$ after multiplying the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{5}$.

</div>
