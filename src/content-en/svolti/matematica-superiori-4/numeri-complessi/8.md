## Exercise 1

Let $z = (2 + k\,i)(1 - 3i)$, with $k$ a real parameter.

a) Find $k$ so that $z$ is real.

b) Find $k$ so that $z$ is purely imaginary.

c) Find $k$ so that $|z| = 10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
z &= 2 - 6i + k\,i - 3k\,i^2 \\[6pt]
&= (2 + 3k) + (k - 6)\,i
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{a)} \qquad k - 6 = 0
\iff
k = 6 ,
\qquad
z = 20
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad
\begin{cases}
2 + 3k = 0 \\[4pt]
k - 6 \neq 0
\end{cases}
\iff
k = -\frac{2}{3} ,
\qquad
z = -\frac{20}{3}\,i
$$
$$
\text{c)} \qquad |z| = |2 + k\,i| \cdot |1 - 3i| = \sqrt{4 + k^2} \cdot \sqrt{10}
$$
$$
\sqrt{10\left( 4 + k^2 \right)} = 10
\iff
10\left( 4 + k^2 \right) = 100
\iff
k^2 = 6
\iff
k = \pm\sqrt{6}
$$

Thus a) $k = 6$, b) $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, and c) $k = \sqrt{6}$ or $k = -\sqrt{6}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three questions concern the real part, imaginary part, and modulus of $z$. Thus we first write $z$ in algebraic form. The distributive property gives four terms. The term $-3k\,i^2$ equals $+3k$ because $i^2 = -1$. Since $k$ is real, the real part of $z$ is $2 + 3k$ and the imaginary part is $k - 6$.

a) A complex number is **real** when its imaginary part is zero. The condition $k - 6 = 0$ gives $k = 6$, and substituting into the real part gives $z = 2 + 18 = 20$.

b) A number is **purely imaginary** when its real part is zero and its imaginary part is different from $0$. The two conditions must hold simultaneously, so we write them as a system. The first gives $k = -\dfrac{2}{3}$, and for this value the imaginary part is $-\dfrac{2}{3} - 6 = -\dfrac{20}{3} \neq 0$, so the second condition is also satisfied.

c) By the **product rule for moduli**, $|z|$ is the product of the moduli of the two factors. This avoids squaring the algebraic form. The first modulus is $\sqrt{4 + k^2}$ and the second is $\sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10}$. Both sides of the equation are positive, so squaring gives an equivalent equation, $40 + 10k^2 = 100$, from which $k^2 = 6$ follows. Both values $\pm\sqrt{6}$ are admissible because $k$ can be any real number.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the complex plane, the points $A$ and $B$ represent the numbers $a = 1 + i$ and $b = 4 + 2i$ and are consecutive vertices of the square $ABCD$ when traversed counterclockwise.

a) Find the numbers $c$ and $d$ representing the other two vertices.

b) Calculate the area of the square.

$$
b - a = 3 + i
$$
$$
i\,(b - a) = 3i + i^2 = -1 + 3i
$$
$$
c = b + i\,(b - a) = 4 + 2i - 1 + 3i = 3 + 5i
$$
$$
d = a + i\,(b - a) = 1 + i - 1 + 3i = 4i
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad A = |b - a|^2 = 3^2 + 1^2 = 10
$$

::grafico{poligoni="1,1 4,2 3,5 0,4" vettori="1,1,4,2;4,2,3,5" punti="1,1,pieno,A;4,2,pieno,B;3,5,pieno,C;0,4,pieno,D" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=6 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" titolo="The side $BC$ is obtained by a rotation through $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ of the side $AB$, that is, by multiplying $b - a = 3 + i$ by $i$"}

Thus a) $c = 3 + 5i$ and $d = 4i$, and b) the area is $10$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The side $AB$ is represented by the vector from $A$ to $B$, that is, by the number $b - a = 3 + i$. When we traverse a square counterclockwise, the side $BC$ is obtained by rotating the side $AB$ counterclockwise through a right angle without changing its length.

The number $i$ has modulus $1$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. By the **product rule in trigonometric form**, multiplying by $i$ preserves the modulus and adds $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to the argument, which is exactly this rotation. The vector $BC$ is therefore $i\,(b - a) = -1 + 3i$.

a) To obtain the vertex $C$, translate $b$ by the vector $BC$, because adding a vector to a point translates that point. This gives $c = 3 + 5i$. The side $AD$ is parallel and equal in length to $BC$, so $d = a + i\,(b - a) = 4i$. Also, $c - d = 3 + i = b - a$, so the side $DC$ is parallel and equal in length to $AB$, and the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is a square.

b) The side length of the square is the distance between $A$ and $B$, that is, the modulus $|b - a| = \sqrt{9 + 1} = \sqrt{10}$. The area is the square of the side length, so **the area is $10$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider the polynomial $p(z) = z^3 + k z - 10$, with $k$ a real parameter.

a) Find $k$ so that $z = 2$ is a root of $p(z)$.

b) For this value, solve the equation $p(z) = 0$ in $\mathbb{C}$.

c) Verify that the three solutions are the vertices of an isosceles triangle and calculate its area.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad p(2) = 8 + 2k - 10 = 0
\iff
k = 1
$$
$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & 0 & 1 & -10 \\
2 & & 2 & 4 & 10 \\
\hline
 & 1 & 2 & 5 & 0
\end{array}
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad z^3 + z - 10 = (z - 2)\left( z^2 + 2z + 5 \right) ,
\qquad
\Delta = 4 - 20 = -16 ,
\qquad
z = \frac{-2 \pm 4i}{2} = -1 \pm 2i
$$
$$
\text{c)} \qquad |2 - (-1 + 2i)| = |3 - 2i| = \sqrt{13} ,
\qquad
|2 - (-1 - 2i)| = |3 + 2i| = \sqrt{13}
$$
$$
\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 6
$$

::grafico{poligoni="2,0 -1,2 -1,-2" punti="2,0,pieno,2;-1,2,pieno,−1 + 2i;-1,-2,pieno,−1 − 2i" segmenti="-1,0,2,0" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" titolo="The roots of $z^3 + z - 10$ form an isosceles triangle with base $4$ on the line $x = -1$ and height $3$"}

Thus a) $k = 1$, b) the solutions are $2$, $-1 + 2i$, and $-1 - 2i$, and c) the triangle is isosceles and has area $6$.

<div class="spiegazione">

a) A number is a root of a polynomial if the value of the polynomial at that number is $0$. Substituting $z = 2$ gives $8 + 2k - 10 = 2k - 2$, which vanishes at $k = 1$.

b) With $k = 1$, the polynomial is $z^3 + z - 10$ and has the root $2$. By the factor theorem it is therefore divisible by $z - 2$. In **synthetic division**, the coefficient of $z^2$ is $0$ and must be included. The quotient is $z^2 + 2z + 5$ with remainder $0$. By the zero-product property, the other solutions are those of $z^2 + 2z + 5 = 0$, whose discriminant is $-16$. The formula for equations with a negative discriminant gives $z = -1 \pm 2i$, two **conjugate** numbers, as expected for a polynomial with real coefficients.

c) A side length is the distance between two vertices, that is, the modulus of the difference of the corresponding numbers. The sides meeting at the vertex $2$ both have length $\sqrt{9 + 4} = \sqrt{13}$, so the triangle is **isosceles** with apex $2$. The symmetry follows because the two nonreal vertices are conjugates and therefore reflections of one another across the real axis, while the third vertex lies on that axis.

The base is the vertical segment between $-1 + 2i$ and $-1 - 2i$, of length $|4i| = 4$. The height relative to this base is the distance from the point $(2, 0)$ to the line $x = -1$, namely $3$. Thus the area is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 3 = 6$.

</div>
