## Exercise 1

Over $\mathbb{C}$, solve the equation $2z + i\,\bar z = 3 + 3i$.

$$
z = x + yi ,
\qquad
\bar z = x - yi ,
\qquad
x, y \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2z + i\,\bar z &= 2x + 2yi + i\,(x - yi) \\[6pt]
&= 2x + 2yi + xi - y\,i^2 \\[6pt]
&= (2x + y) + (x + 2y)\,i
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y = 3 \\[4pt]
x + 2y = 3
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
y = 3 - 2x \\[4pt]
x + 6 - 4x = 3
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x = 1 \\[4pt]
y = 1
\end{cases}
$$

The equation has the unique solution $z = 1 + i$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains both $z$ and its conjugate $\bar z$. The conjugate cannot be obtained from $z$ using algebraic operations, so the equation cannot be solved by isolating $z$ as in a linear equation. Instead, write the unknown in **algebraic form**, $z = x + yi$, with $x$ and $y$ real. Its conjugate is then $x - yi$.

After substitution, the left-hand side becomes an expression in $x$ and $y$. Expanding the product $i\,(x - yi)$ by the distributive property gives $xi - y\,i^2 = xi + y$ because $i^2 = -1$. Collecting the real terms and the coefficients of $i$ gives real part $2x + y$ and imaginary part $x + 2y$ on the left-hand side.

Two complex numbers are equal if and only if their real parts are equal and their imaginary parts are equal. The equation is therefore equivalent to a **system** of two linear equations in the real unknowns $x$ and $y$ that must hold simultaneously. To solve by substitution, the first equation gives $y = 3 - 2x$. Substituting into the second gives $-3x + 6 = 3$, so $x = 1$. Consequently, $y = 3 - 2 = 1$.

The system has only one solution, so the equation also has just one solution, $z = 1 + i$. Substituting gives $2(1 + i) + i\,(1 - i) = 2 + 2i + i + 1 = 3 + 3i$, which is the right-hand side.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Over $\mathbb{C}$, solve the equation $z^2 = \bar z$ and plot the solutions in the complex plane.

$$
(x + yi)^2 = x^2 - y^2 + 2xy\,i
$$
$$
x^2 - y^2 + 2xy\,i = x - yi
\iff
\begin{cases}
x^2 - y^2 = x \\[4pt]
2xy = -y
\end{cases}
$$
$$
2xy + y = 0
\iff
y\,(2x + 1) = 0
\iff
y = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
y = 0 :
\qquad
x^2 = x
\iff
x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 1
$$
$$
x = -\frac{1}{2} :
\qquad
\frac{1}{4} - y^2 = -\frac{1}{2}
\iff
y^2 = \frac{3}{4}
\iff
y = \pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno;1,0,pieno;-0.5,0.866,pieno;-0.5,-0.866,pieno" linee="1,0 -0.5,0.866 -0.5,-0.866 1,0" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2 ymax=2 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" legenda="|z| = 1" titolo="The solutions of $z^2 = \bar z$ are the origin and the three vertices of an equilateral triangle inscribed in the circle of radius $1$"}

The solutions are $0$, $1$, $-\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,i$, and $-\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,i$.

<div class="spiegazione">

As in the preceding exercise, set $z = x + yi$. Expanding the square of the binomial and using $(yi)^2 = -y^2$ shows that $z^2$ has real part $x^2 - y^2$ and imaginary part $2xy$. Equating the real and imaginary parts of the two sides gives a **nonlinear system**.

Solve the second equation first because it factors. Moving all terms to the left and factoring out $y$ gives $y\,(2x + 1) = 0$, and by the zero-product property $y = 0$ or $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Substitute each case into the first equation.

If $y = 0$, the first equation becomes $x^2 = x$, that is, $x\,(x - 1) = 0$, which gives $x = 0$ or $x = 1$. This yields the real numbers $0$ and $1$.

If $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, the first equation becomes $\dfrac{1}{4} - y^2 = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $y^2 = \dfrac{3}{4}$ and $y = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. This yields two **conjugate** numbers.

Thus there are **four** solutions. The three numbers different from $0$ have modulus $1$ and arguments $0$, $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, so they are the cube roots of unity and form an equilateral triangle. To see why, multiply both sides by $z$. For $z \neq 0$ this gives $z^3 = z \bar z = |z|^2$. Equating the moduli of the two sides gives $|z|^3 = |z|^2$, so $|z| = 1$ and hence $z^3 = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and plot in the complex plane the set of complex numbers $z$ satisfying $|z - 2| = |z + 2i|$.

$$
z - 2 = (x - 2) + yi ,
\qquad
z + 2i = x + (y + 2)\,i
$$
$$
\sqrt{(x - 2)^2 + y^2} = \sqrt{x^2 + (y + 2)^2}
$$
$$
(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = x^2 + (y + 2)^2
$$
$$
x^2 - 4x + 4 + y^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 4y + 4
$$
$$
-4x = 4y
\iff
y = -x
$$

::grafico{f="-x" punti="2,0,pieno,2;0,-2,pieno,−2i;1,-1,pieno" segmenti="2,0,0,-2" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" legenda="y = -x" titolo="The points equidistant from $2$ and $-2i$ form the line $y = -x$, the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them"}

The required set is the line with equation $y = -x$, the bisector of the second and fourth quadrants.

<div class="spiegazione">

The modulus of a difference is the **distance** between two points in the complex plane. The left-hand side $|z - 2|$ is the distance from $z$ to the point $(2, 0)$. The right-hand side can be written as $|z - (-2i)|$, so it is the distance from $z$ to the point $(0, -2)$. Thus the equation describes the points **equidistant** from $(2, 0)$ and $(0, -2)$, which form the perpendicular bisector of the segment with those endpoints.

To find the equation, set $z = x + yi$ and write out the real and imaginary parts of $z - 2$ and $z + 2i$. Each modulus is the square root of the sum of the squares of the two parts. Both sides are nonnegative, so squaring gives an equivalent equation.

On expanding the squares, the terms $x^2$, $y^2$, and $4$ appear on both sides and cancel. This leaves $-4x = 4y$, that is, $y = -x$. The equation is linear, so the set is a **straight line**.

This result agrees with the geometry of the problem. The midpoint of the segment is $(1, -1)$, which satisfies $y = -x$. The segment has slope $\dfrac{-2 - 0}{0 - 2} = 1$, and the line $y = -x$ has slope $-1$. The product of the two slopes is $-1$, so the line is perpendicular to the segment.

</div>
