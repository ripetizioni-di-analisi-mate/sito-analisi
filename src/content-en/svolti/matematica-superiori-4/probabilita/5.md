## Exercise 1

Two fair dice are rolled and the sum of the two numbers is known to be $8$. Given this information, find the probability that at least one die shows $6$ and the probability that the two numbers are equal.

$$
B = \text{"sum } 8\text{"} = \{ (2, 6), (3, 5), (4, 4), (5, 3), (6, 2) \}
$$
$$
A = \text{"at least one } 6\text{"} ,
\qquad
A \cap B = \{ (2, 6), (6, 2) \}
$$
$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|B|} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
C = \text{"equal numbers"} ,
\qquad
C \cap B = \{ (4, 4) \}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
P(C \mid B) = \frac{1}{5}
$$

::grafico{punti="1,1,vuoto;1,2,vuoto;1,3,vuoto;1,4,vuoto;1,5,vuoto;1,6,vuoto;2,1,vuoto;2,2,vuoto;2,3,vuoto;2,4,vuoto;2,5,vuoto;2,6,pieno;3,1,vuoto;3,2,vuoto;3,3,vuoto;3,4,vuoto;3,5,pieno;3,6,vuoto;4,1,vuoto;4,2,vuoto;4,3,vuoto;4,4,pieno;4,5,vuoto;4,6,vuoto;5,1,vuoto;5,2,vuoto;5,3,pieno;5,4,vuoto;5,5,vuoto;5,6,vuoto;6,1,vuoto;6,2,pieno;6,3,vuoto;6,4,vuoto;6,5,vuoto;6,6,vuoto" f="8-x" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=7 uguale=1 nomi="a,b" titolo="Given that the sum is $8$, the possible outcomes reduce to the $5$ solid points on the line $a + b = 8$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The information “the sum is $8$” means that event $B$ has occurred. The outcomes of rolling two dice are equally likely, so we can calculate **conditional probability** by counting outcomes. The new sample space is $B$, and for each event we count only its outcomes that lie in $B$.

The event $B$ contains $5$ pairs, shown as solid points in the figure. Among these, the pairs with at least one $6$ are $(2, 6)$ and $(6, 2)$, so $P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{2}{5}$. Without information about the sum, the probability of at least one $6$ would be $\dfrac{11}{36}$, since the number of pairs with at least one $6$ is $6 + 6 - 1 = 11$. The information therefore makes the event more likely.

The pairs $(1, 1), \ldots, (6, 6)$ have equal numbers, and among them only $(4, 4)$ has sum $8$. Therefore $P(C \mid B) = \dfrac{1}{5}$, whereas without this information the probability would be $P(C) = \dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At a school, $200$ students are surveyed. Of these, $120$ play a sport, and of those, $45$ play an instrument. Of the $80$ students who do not play a sport, $35$ play an instrument. One of the students surveyed is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student plays an instrument given that they play a sport, and the probability that they play a sport given that they play an instrument. Determine whether the two events are independent.

| | instrument | no instrument | total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sport | $45$ | $75$ | $120$ |
| no sport | $35$ | $45$ | $80$ |
| total | $80$ | $120$ | $200$ |

$$
P(M \mid S) = \frac{45}{120} = \frac{3}{8} ,
\qquad
P(S \mid M) = \frac{45}{80} = \frac{9}{16}
$$
$$
P(M) = \frac{80}{200} = \frac{2}{5} \neq \frac{3}{8} = P(M \mid S)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $S$ be the event “the student plays a sport” and $M$ the event “the student plays an instrument.” A **two-way table** organizes the data. Subtraction gives the missing entries. Of the students who play a sport, $120 - 45 = 75$ do not play an instrument. Of those who do not play a sport, $80 - 35 = 45$ do not play an instrument, and the total number who play an instrument is $45 + 35 = 80$.

The student is chosen at random, so the outcomes are equally likely and the conditional probability is a ratio of counts. For $P(M \mid S)$, consider only the $120$ students who play a sport. Of these, $45$ play an instrument, so $P(M \mid S) = \dfrac{3}{8}$. For $P(S \mid M)$, consider only the $80$ students who play an instrument. Of these, $45$ play a sport, so $P(S \mid M) = \dfrac{9}{16}$. The two probabilities differ because their denominators are different.

Two events are independent if the conditional probability equals the unconditional probability. Here $P(M) = \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{16}{40}$ and $P(M \mid S) = \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{15}{40}$. The two probabilities differ, so events $S$ and $M$ are **dependent**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For two events $A$ and $B$, suppose $P(A) = 0.6$, $P(B) = 0.5$, and $P(A \mid B) = 0.4$. Find $P(A \cap B)$, $P(B \mid A)$, $P(A \cup B)$, and the probability of $A$ given that $B$ did not occur.

$$
P(A \cap B) = P(B) \cdot P(A \mid B) = 0.5 \cdot 0.4 = 0.2
$$
$$
P(B \mid A) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)} = \frac{0.2}{0.6} = \frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
P(A \cup B) = 0.6 + 0.5 - 0.2 = 0.9
$$
$$
P\left( A \mid \overline{B} \right) = \frac{P\left( A \cap \overline{B} \right)}{P\left( \overline{B} \right)} = \frac{0.6 - 0.2}{1 - 0.5} = \frac{0.4}{0.5} = 0.8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Starting from the definition $P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$, multiply both sides by $P(B)$ to obtain $P(A \cap B) = 0.2$. Once the intersection probability is known, calculate $P(B \mid A)$, this time dividing by $P(A)$, to obtain $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

For the union, the **addition rule** gives $P(A \cup B) = 0.9$.

The last probability is conditional on the complement $\overline{B}$. The definition calls for $P\left( A \cap \overline{B} \right)$, the probability of the part of $A$ outside $B$. The event $A$ is the union of two mutually exclusive parts, $A \cap B$ and $A \cap \overline{B}$, so $P\left( A \cap \overline{B} \right) = 0.6 - 0.2 = 0.4$. By the complement rule, $P\left( \overline{B} \right) = 0.5$. The quotient is $0.8$.

The results $P(A \mid B) = 0.4$ and $P\left( A \mid \overline{B} \right) = 0.8$ differ from $P(A) = 0.6$, so $A$ and $B$ are dependent. In particular, when $B$ occurs, the probability of $A$ is lower than when $B$ does not occur.

</div>
