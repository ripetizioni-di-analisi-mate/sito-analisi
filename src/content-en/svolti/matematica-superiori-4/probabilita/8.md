## Exercise 1

A test consists of $5$ questions, each with $4$ possible answers, only one of which is correct. A student guesses at random on every question. Find the probability that the student answers exactly $3$ questions correctly and the probability that they answer at least $4$ correctly.

$$
n = 5 ,
\qquad
p = \frac{1}{4} ,
\qquad
1 - p = \frac{3}{4}
$$
$$
P(3) = \binom{5}{3} \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^3 \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^2 = 10 \cdot \frac{1}{64} \cdot \frac{9}{16} = \frac{90}{1024} = \frac{45}{512}
$$
$$
P(4) = \binom{5}{4} \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^4 \cdot \frac{3}{4} = 5 \cdot \frac{1}{256} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \frac{15}{1024}
$$
$$
P(5) = \left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^5 = \frac{1}{1024}
$$
$$
P(\text{at least } 4) = \frac{15}{1024} + \frac{1}{1024} = \frac{16}{1024} = \frac{1}{64}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each question is a trial with two outcomes, a correct answer (success) or a wrong answer (failure). The student randomly chooses one of the $4$ answers, so the probability of success on each trial is $\dfrac{1}{4}$. The answers to different questions are chosen independently. The conditions of the **Bernoulli model** are therefore satisfied, with $n = 5$ and $p = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

By the formula for repeated trials, the probability of $3$ successes is the product of $\dbinom{5}{3} = 10$, the number of ways to choose the three questions answered correctly, and $\left( \dfrac{1}{4} \right)^3 \left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^2$, the probability of each sequence. This gives $\dfrac{90}{1024}$, which simplifies on dividing by $2$ to give $\dfrac{45}{512} \approx 0.088$.

At least $4$ correct answers means $4$ or $5$. The two cases are mutually exclusive, so calculate their probabilities separately using the same formula and add. For $k = 5$, the binomial coefficient is $1$ and the factor $\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^0$ equals $1$. The sum is $\dfrac{16}{1024} = \dfrac{1}{64}$, or about $1.6\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An urn contains $4$ red balls and $n$ blue balls, with $n \geq 1$. Two balls are drawn without replacement. Find $n$ so that the probability of drawing two red balls is $\dfrac{2}{7}$. Using that value, find the probability of drawing two balls of different colors.

$$
P(RR) = \frac{4}{n + 4} \cdot \frac{3}{n + 3} = \frac{12}{(n + 4)(n + 3)}
$$
$$
\frac{12}{(n + 4)(n + 3)} = \frac{2}{7}
\iff
(n + 4)(n + 3) = 42
$$
$$
n^2 + 7n + 12 = 42
\iff
n^2 + 7n - 30 = 0
\iff
(n + 10)(n - 3) = 0
$$
$$
n = 3
$$
$$
P(\text{different colors}) = \frac{4}{7} \cdot \frac{3}{6} + \frac{3}{7} \cdot \frac{4}{6} = \frac{12}{42} + \frac{12}{42} = \frac{4}{7}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $n + 4$ balls in the urn altogether. The first is red with probability $\dfrac{4}{n + 4}$. If the first ball is red and is not returned to the urn, $3$ of the remaining $n + 3$ balls are red. By the **multiplication rule**, the probability of two red balls is the product of the two fractions.

Set this probability equal to $\dfrac{2}{7}$. The denominators are positive, so we can cross-multiply to obtain $(n + 4)(n + 3) = 42$. Expanding the product gives the **quadratic equation** $n^2 + 7n - 30 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $7$ and product $-30$ are $10$ and $-3$, so the trinomial factors as $(n + 10)(n - 3)$. The solutions are $n = -10$ and $n = 3$. The number of blue balls is a positive integer, so the valid solution is $n = 3$.

With $n = 3$, the urn contains $4$ red balls and $3$ blue balls, for a total of $7$ balls. Draws of different colors give the sequences $RB$ and $BR$, which are mutually exclusive. By the multiplication rule each has probability $\dfrac{12}{42}$, and their probabilities sum to $\dfrac{24}{42} = \dfrac{4}{7}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A biased coin lands heads with probability $p$.

a) Find $p$ given that the probability of getting two heads when the coin is tossed twice is $\dfrac{4}{9}$.

b) Using the value of $p$ found in part a), find the probability of getting exactly two heads in four tosses.

c) A coin is chosen at random, with equal probability, from the biased coin and a fair coin. The chosen coin is tossed twice, and both tosses give heads. Find the probability that the biased coin was chosen.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad p^2 = \frac{4}{9} , \quad 0 \leq p \leq 1
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
p = \frac{2}{3}
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad \binom{4}{2} \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^2 \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^2 = 6 \cdot \frac{4}{9} \cdot \frac{1}{9} = \frac{24}{81} = \frac{8}{27}
$$
$$
\text{c)} \qquad P(TT) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{9} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{9} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{16 + 9}{72} = \frac{25}{72}
$$
$$
P(\text{biased} \mid TT) = \frac{\frac{2}{9}}{\frac{25}{72}} = \frac{2}{9} \cdot \frac{72}{25} = \frac{16}{25}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3,1.6;0,0 3,-1.6;3,1.6 6,2.4;3,1.6 6,0.8;3,-1.6 6,-0.8;3,-1.6 6,-2.4" punti="0,0,pieno;3,1.6,pieno;3,-1.6,pieno;6,2.4,pieno;6,0.8,pieno;6,-0.8,pieno;6,-2.4,pieno" etichette="2.4,2.05,biased;2.5,-2.1,fair;6.2,2.4,TT;6.2,0.8,other;6.2,-0.8,TT;6.2,-2.4,other;1.2,1.15,½;1.2,-1.25,½;4.2,2.4,(⅔)²;4.3,-0.65,(½)²" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7.5 ymin=-3 ymax=3 titolo="Choosing the coin and tossing it twice. The paths ending in two heads have probabilities $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{4}{9}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

a) The two tosses are **independent**, so the probability of two heads is $p \cdot p = p^2$. The equation $p^2 = \dfrac{4}{9}$ has solutions $p = \pm \dfrac{2}{3}$. A probability is between $0$ and $1$, so the only valid solution is $p = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

b) Four tosses of the same coin are independent trials with a constant probability of success $\dfrac{2}{3}$, so the **Bernoulli model** applies with $n = 4$ and $k = 2$. The positions of the two heads can be chosen in $\dbinom{4}{2} = 6$ ways, and each sequence of two heads and two tails has probability $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{81}$. The required probability is $\dfrac{24}{81} = \dfrac{8}{27}$.

c) The two choices of coin form a partition, each with probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. With the biased coin, two heads have probability $\dfrac{4}{9}$ by part a), and with the fair coin they have probability $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$. By the **law of total probability**, add the probabilities of the two paths on the tree ending in $TT$ to obtain $\dfrac{25}{72}$, writing the fractions with the common denominator $72$. By Bayes’ theorem, the probability of the biased coin is the probability of its path divided by $P(TT)$, which gives $\dfrac{16}{25}$. The probability of the biased coin is therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}$ before the tosses and $\dfrac{16}{25} = 0.64$ given that the two tosses gave heads.

</div>
