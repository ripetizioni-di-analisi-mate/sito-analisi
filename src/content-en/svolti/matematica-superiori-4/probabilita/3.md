## Exercise 1

An urn contains $4$ white balls and $2$ black balls. Two balls are drawn one after the other without returning the first ball to the urn. Find the probability that both are white, the probability that both are black, and the probability that they are different colors.

$$
P(BB) = \frac{4}{6} \cdot \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
P(NN) = \frac{2}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{1}{15}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\text{different colors}) &= P(BN) + P(NB) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{5} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{15} + \frac{4}{15} = \frac{8}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A **tree diagram** represents the draws. On the first draw there are $6$ balls, so the probability of drawing a white ball is $\dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ and the probability of drawing a black ball is $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

On the second draw there are $5$ balls, and the number of each color left depends on the first draw. If the first ball is white, $3$ white balls and $2$ black balls remain, so the conditional probabilities are $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{2}{5}$. If the first ball is black, $4$ white balls and $1$ black ball remain, so the probabilities are $\dfrac{4}{5}$ and $\dfrac{1}{5}$.

By the **multiplication rule**, the probability of a path is the product of the probabilities on its branches. This gives $P(BB) = \dfrac{2}{5}$ and $P(NN) = \dfrac{1}{15}$.

The colors are different if the sequence is $BN$ or $NB$. The two sequences are mutually exclusive, so their probabilities add, and the probability of different colors is $\dfrac{8}{15}$. The three results sum to $\dfrac{6}{15} + \dfrac{1}{15} + \dfrac{8}{15} = 1$ because the three events are mutually exclusive and cover all possible outcomes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using the same urn as in the preceding exercise, with $4$ white balls and $2$ black balls, draw two balls, returning the first to the urn before drawing the second. Find the probability that both are white and the probability that they are different colors, and compare the results with those for draws without replacement.

$$
P(B) = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3} ,
\qquad
P(N) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
P(BB) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{9}
$$
$$
P(\text{different colors}) = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{9} + \frac{2}{9} = \frac{4}{9}
$$
$$
\frac{4}{9} > \frac{2}{5} ,
\qquad
\frac{4}{9} < \frac{8}{15}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With **replacement**, the ball drawn goes back into the urn, so on the second draw there are again $4$ white balls and $2$ black balls. The probabilities on the second draw are the same as on the first, whatever color came first, so the two draws are **independent**.

For independent events, the probability that both occur is the product of their probabilities. The probability of two white balls is therefore $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^2 = \dfrac{4}{9}$.

The sequences with different colors are $BN$ and $NB$. They are mutually exclusive, and each has probability $\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{9}$. Their probabilities sum to $\dfrac{4}{9}$.

To compare the fractions, give them a common denominator. Since $\dfrac{4}{9} = \dfrac{20}{45}$ and $\dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{18}{45}$, drawing two white balls is more likely with replacement. Also, $\dfrac{4}{9} = \dfrac{20}{45}$ and $\dfrac{8}{15} = \dfrac{24}{45}$, so different colors are more likely without replacement. Indeed, without replacement, drawing a white ball reduces the number of white balls left and makes a black ball more likely on the second draw.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An urn contains $5$ red balls, $3$ green balls, and $2$ yellow balls. A group of $3$ balls is drawn at once. Find the probability that they are three different colors and the probability that they are all the same color.

$$
\binom{10}{3} = \frac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 120
$$
$$
P(\text{three colors}) = \frac{\dbinom{5}{1} \dbinom{3}{1} \dbinom{2}{1}}{\dbinom{10}{3}} = \frac{5 \cdot 3 \cdot 2}{120} = \frac{30}{120} = \frac{1}{4}
$$
$$
\binom{5}{3} = 10 ,
\qquad
\binom{3}{3} = 1
$$
$$
P(\text{same color}) = \frac{10 + 1}{120} = \frac{11}{120}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Drawing $3$ balls at once is equivalent to drawing them without replacement, and order does not matter. The possible outcomes are therefore groups of $3$ balls chosen from $10$, giving $\dbinom{10}{3} = 120$ equally likely **combinations**.

The three balls are different colors if there is one of each color. There are $5$ choices for the red ball, $3$ for the green ball, and $2$ for the yellow ball, so by the fundamental counting principle there are $30$ favorable groups, and the probability is $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

The balls are the same color if all three are red or all three are green. Three yellow balls are impossible since there are only $2$ yellow balls. There are $\dbinom{5}{3} = 10$ groups of three red balls and only one group of three green balls. The two cases are mutually exclusive, so there are $11$ favorable outcomes and the probability is $\dfrac{11}{120}$.

The multiplication rule also gives the first result. The sequence red, green, yellow has probability $\dfrac{5}{10} \cdot \dfrac{3}{9} \cdot \dfrac{2}{8} = \dfrac{1}{24}$, and the three colors can occur in $3! = 6$ orders, each with the same probability. This again gives $6 \cdot \dfrac{1}{24} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>
