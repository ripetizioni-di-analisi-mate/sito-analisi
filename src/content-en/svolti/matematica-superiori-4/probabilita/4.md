## Exercise 1

From a deck of $40$ Neapolitan cards, draw $3$ cards at once. Find the probability that they all belong to the suit of coins and the probability that exactly one is an ace.

$$
\binom{40}{3} = \frac{40 \cdot 39 \cdot 38}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 9880
$$
$$
\binom{10}{3} = \frac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8}{6} = 120
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
P(\text{three cards from the suit of coins}) = \frac{120}{9880} = \frac{3}{247}
$$
$$
\binom{4}{1} \binom{36}{2} = 4 \cdot \frac{36 \cdot 35}{2} = 4 \cdot 630 = 2520
$$
$$
P(\text{exactly one ace}) = \frac{2520}{9880} = \frac{63}{247}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $3$ cards are drawn together, so an outcome is a group of $3$ cards in which order does not matter. The possible outcomes are the **combinations** of $40$ cards taken $3$ at a time. Each group of $3$ cards is equally likely, and there are $9880$ such groups.

There are $10$ cards in the suit of coins, so the number of groups containing only cards of that suit is $\dbinom{10}{3} = 120$. To simplify the fraction $\dfrac{120}{9880}$, divide by $40$ to obtain $\dfrac{3}{247}$.

A group with exactly one ace contains $1$ of the $4$ aces and $2$ of the $36$ other cards. The ace can be chosen in $\dbinom{4}{1} = 4$ ways and the other two cards in $\dbinom{36}{2} = 630$ ways. By the fundamental counting principle there are $2520$ favorable groups. The fraction $\dfrac{2520}{9880}$ simplifies by dividing by $40$, and the probability of exactly one ace is $\dfrac{63}{247} \approx 0.26$.

We multiply the numbers of choices because each ace can go with every pair of cards that are not aces. The second choice excludes the remaining aces, since otherwise groups with two or three aces would also count.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A class has $7$ girls and $5$ boys. A group of $4$ students is chosen at random to form a committee. Find the probability that the committee consists of $2$ girls and $2$ boys and the probability that it contains at least one girl.

$$
\binom{12}{4} = \frac{12 \cdot 11 \cdot 10 \cdot 9}{4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 495
$$
$$
\binom{7}{2} \binom{5}{2} = 21 \cdot 10 = 210
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
P(2F, 2M) = \frac{210}{495} = \frac{14}{33}
$$
$$
\binom{5}{4} = 5
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
P(\text{no girls}) = \frac{5}{495} = \frac{1}{99}
$$
$$
P(\text{at least one girl}) = 1 - \frac{1}{99} = \frac{98}{99}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A committee is a group of $4$ students in which order does not matter, so the possible committees are the combinations of $12$ students taken $4$ at a time. Each committee of $4$ students counts once, giving $495$ committees. The random selection makes all committees equally likely.

To form a committee with $2$ girls and $2$ boys, choose $2$ girls from $7$ in $\dbinom{7}{2} = 21$ ways and $2$ boys from $5$ in $\dbinom{5}{2} = 10$ ways. There are therefore $210$ favorable committees. Dividing the numerator and denominator by $15$ gives a probability of $\dfrac{14}{33}$.

The event “at least one girl” includes committees with $1$, $2$, $3$, or $4$ girls. Its **complement** is “no girls,” a committee of boys only, which is easier to count. The number of committees with $4$ boys chosen from $5$ is $\dbinom{5}{4} = 5$, so the probability of no girls is $\dfrac{1}{99}$. By the complement rule, the probability of at least one girl is $\dfrac{98}{99}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the Italian Lotto, $5$ numbers are drawn without replacement from the numbers $1$ to $90$. One player bets on a particular number and another on a particular pair of numbers (a two-number bet). Find the probability that the first player’s number is drawn and the probability that both numbers in the second player’s pair are drawn.

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = \binom{90}{5}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\text{chosen number drawn}) &= \frac{\dbinom{89}{4}}{\dbinom{90}{5}} = \frac{\dfrac{89!}{4! \, 85!}}{\dfrac{90!}{5! \, 85!}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{89! \cdot 5!}{90! \cdot 4!} = \frac{5}{90} = \frac{1}{18}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\text{both chosen numbers drawn}) &= \frac{\dbinom{88}{3}}{\dbinom{90}{5}} = \frac{88! \cdot 5!}{90! \cdot 3!} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{5 \cdot 4}{90 \cdot 89} = \frac{20}{8010} = \frac{2}{801}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A Lotto draw is a group of $5$ numbers chosen from $90$, and order does not matter. There are therefore $\dbinom{90}{5}$ possible outcomes, all equally likely.

To form a set of five containing the chosen number, add $4$ other numbers chosen from the remaining $89$, so there are $\dbinom{89}{4}$ such sets. To calculate the ratio, write the binomial coefficients in terms of factorials. The factor $85!$ occurs in both and cancels. Since $90! = 90 \cdot 89!$ and $5! = 5 \cdot 4!$, the ratio becomes $\dfrac{5}{90} = \dfrac{1}{18}$. The result also has a direct interpretation. The draw picks $5$ numbers out of $90$, and each number has the same chance of being among them, so the probability is $\dfrac{5}{90}$.

To form a set of five containing both numbers in the bet, add $3$ numbers chosen from the remaining $88$, giving $\dbinom{88}{3}$ such sets. Again $85!$ cancels. Since $90! = 90 \cdot 89 \cdot 88!$ and $5! = 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3!$, the ratio is $\dfrac{5 \cdot 4}{90 \cdot 89} = \dfrac{2}{801}$, or approximately $0.0025$.

</div>
