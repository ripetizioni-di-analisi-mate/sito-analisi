## Exercise 1

A card is drawn from a deck of $40$ Neapolitan cards. Find the probability that it is a face card (jack, knight, or king) or a card from the suit of cups.

$$
P(F) = \frac{12}{40} ,
\qquad
P(C) = \frac{10}{40} ,
\qquad
P(F \cap C) = \frac{3}{40}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(F \cup C) &= P(F) + P(C) - P(F \cap C) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{12}{40} + \frac{10}{40} - \frac{3}{40} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{19}{40}
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{zone="a,ab,b" nomi="figure,coppe" elementi="a:9;ab:3;b:7" titolo="The $9$ face cards outside the suit of cups, the $3$ face cards in cups, and the $7$ other cards in cups give $19$ favorable cards in all"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $F$ be the event “drawing a face card” and $C$ the event “drawing a card from the suit of cups.” The deck has $4$ suits and each suit contains $3$ face cards, so there are $12$ face cards. There are $10$ cards in the suit of cups. The card is drawn at random, so the $40$ cards are equally likely and each probability is the number of favorable cards divided by $40$.

The two events **overlap**, since the jack, knight, and king of cups belong to both. Their intersection $F \cap C$ therefore contains $3$ cards. Simply adding $12$ and $10$ would count these three cards twice.

By the **addition rule** for arbitrary events, subtract the probability of the intersection once to obtain $P(F \cup C) = \dfrac{19}{40}$. The Venn diagram confirms the count, since the three regions of the union contain $9$, $3$, and $7$ cards, and $9 + 3 + 7 = 19$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An integer from $1$ to $100$ is chosen at random. Find the probability that it is divisible by $4$ or $6$ and the probability that it is divisible by neither $4$ nor $6$.

$$
\text{multiples of } 4 : \ 4, 8, \ldots, 100 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad 25
$$
$$
\text{multiples of } 6 : \ 6, 12, \ldots, 96 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad 16
$$
$$
\text{multiples of } 12 : \ 12, 24, \ldots, 96 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad 8
$$
$$
P(A \cup B) = \frac{25}{100} + \frac{16}{100} - \frac{8}{100} = \frac{33}{100}
$$
$$
P\left( \overline{A \cup B} \right) = 1 - \frac{33}{100} = \frac{67}{100}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $A$ be the event “the number is divisible by $4$” and $B$ the event “the number is divisible by $6$.” The $100$ numbers are equally likely, so we count the favorable outcomes.

The multiples of $4$ up to $100$ are $4 \cdot 1, 4 \cdot 2, \ldots, 4 \cdot 25$, a total of $25$. The multiples of $6$ are $6 \cdot 1, \ldots, 6 \cdot 16$, a total of $16$, since $6 \cdot 16 = 96$ and $6 \cdot 17 = 102$ exceeds $100$. A number is divisible by both $4$ and $6$ if and only if it is divisible by their least common multiple, which is $12$. The number of multiples of $12$ up to $100$ is $8$, since $12 \cdot 8 = 96$.

By the **addition rule**, the probability of the union is $\dfrac{25 + 16 - 8}{100} = \dfrac{33}{100}$.

A number is divisible by neither $4$ nor $6$ precisely when it does not belong to $A \cup B$, so the second event is the complement of the union. By the **complement** rule, its probability is $\dfrac{67}{100}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For two events $A$ and $B$, suppose $P(A) = 0.5$, $P(B) = 0.4$, and $P(A \cup B) = 0.7$. Find $P(A \cap B)$, the probability that neither event occurs, and the probability that exactly one event occurs.

$$
P(A \cap B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cup B) = 0.5 + 0.4 - 0.7 = 0.2
$$
$$
P\left( \overline{A} \cap \overline{B} \right) = 1 - P(A \cup B) = 1 - 0.7 = 0.3
$$
$$
P\left( A \cap \overline{B} \right) = 0.5 - 0.2 = 0.3 ,
\qquad
P\left( \overline{A} \cap B \right) = 0.4 - 0.2 = 0.2
$$
$$
P(\text{exactly one}) = 0.3 + 0.2 = 0.5
$$

::venn{zone="a,b" nomi="A,B" universo=1 ambiente="Ω" elementi="a:30%;ab:20%;b:20%;u:30%" titolo="Probabilities of the four regions as percentages. The shaded regions form the event “exactly one of the two events occurs”"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **addition rule** relates the four probabilities $P(A)$, $P(B)$, $P(A \cap B)$, and $P(A \cup B)$. Three are known, so we can solve for the fourth to obtain $P(A \cap B) = 0.2$.

Neither event occurs when the outcome lies outside both $A$ and $B$, that is, outside their union. The event $\overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$ is therefore the complement of $A \cup B$, and its probability is $0.3$.

For the last question, split $A$ into the two mutually exclusive parts $A \cap B$ and $A \cap \overline{B}$. Since $P(A) = P(A \cap B) + P\left( A \cap \overline{B} \right)$, the part of $A$ outside $B$ has probability $0.5 - 0.2 = 0.3$. Similarly, the part of $B$ outside $A$ has probability $0.2$. Exactly one event occurs when the outcome lies in one of these two disjoint regions, so the probability is their sum, $0.5$. As a check, the probabilities of the four regions in the diagram satisfy $0.3 + 0.2 + 0.2 + 0.3 = 1$.

</div>
