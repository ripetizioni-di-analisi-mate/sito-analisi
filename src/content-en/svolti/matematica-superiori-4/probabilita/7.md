## Exercise 1

A fair coin is tossed. If it lands heads, a ball is drawn from urn $U_1$, which contains $3$ red balls and $2$ blue balls. If it lands tails, a ball is drawn from urn $U_2$, which contains $1$ red ball and $4$ blue balls. Find the probability of drawing a red ball and, given that the ball drawn is red, the probability that it came from urn $U_1$.

$$
P(U_1) = P(U_2) = \frac{1}{2} ,
\qquad
P(R \mid U_1) = \frac{3}{5} ,
\qquad
P(R \mid U_2) = \frac{1}{5}
$$
$$
P(R) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{5} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{3}{10} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
P(U_1 \mid R) = \frac{P(U_1) \cdot P(R \mid U_1)}{P(R)} = \frac{\frac{3}{10}}{\frac{2}{5}} = \frac{3}{10} \cdot \frac{5}{2} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3,1.6;0,0 3,-1.6;3,1.6 6,2.4;3,1.6 6,0.8;3,-1.6 6,-0.8;3,-1.6 6,-2.4" punti="0,0,pieno;3,1.6,pieno;3,-1.6,pieno;6,2.4,pieno;6,0.8,pieno;6,-0.8,pieno;6,-2.4,pieno" etichette="3.1,1.95,U₁;3.1,-1.25,U₂;6.2,2.4,R;6.2,0.8,B;6.2,-0.8,R;6.2,-2.4,B;1.2,1.15,½;1.2,-1.25,½;4.4,2.35,⅗;4.4,0.85,⅖;4.4,-0.85,⅕;4.4,-2.35,⅘" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7.5 ymin=-3 ymax=3 titolo="Tree for the choice of urn and the draw. The paths ending in $R$ have probabilities $\dfrac{3}{10}$ and $\dfrac{1}{10}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The color of the ball depends on the urn it is drawn from, so split the problem into the two cases $U_1$ and $U_2$. The two events are mutually exclusive and one of them always occurs, so they form a **partition** of the sample space. The coin is fair, so each urn has probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$. For each urn, the probability of drawing a red ball is the number of red balls divided by the total number of balls.

By the **law of total probability**, for each urn multiply the probability of choosing that urn by the probability of drawing a red ball from it, then add the two products. On the tree, the products are the probabilities of the paths ending in $R$. This gives $P(R) = \dfrac{2}{5}$.

The second question works backward, since the color is known and we want to identify the urn. By **Bayes’ theorem**, the posterior probability of $U_1$ is the ratio of the probability of path $U_1 R$ to the total probability of $R$. The ratio is $\dfrac{3}{4}$. The prior probability of $U_1$ was $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and becomes $\dfrac{3}{4}$ because urn $U_1$ contains more red balls.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A company buys parts from three suppliers. Supplier $A$ provides $50\%$ of the parts, $B$ provides $30\%$, and $C$ provides $20\%$. Defective parts account for $2\%$ of the parts from $A$, $3\%$ of those from $B$, and $5\%$ of those from $C$. One part is chosen at random. Find the probability that it is defective and, given that it is defective, the probability that it came from each supplier.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(D) &= P(A) \cdot P(D \mid A) + P(B) \cdot P(D \mid B) + P(C) \cdot P(D \mid C) \\[6pt]
&= 0.5 \cdot 0.02 + 0.3 \cdot 0.03 + 0.2 \cdot 0.05 \\[6pt]
&= 0.010 + 0.009 + 0.010 = 0.029
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
P(A \mid D) = \frac{0.010}{0.029} = \frac{10}{29} ,
\qquad
P(B \mid D) = \frac{0.009}{0.029} = \frac{9}{29} ,
\qquad
P(C \mid D) = \frac{0.010}{0.029} = \frac{10}{29}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each part comes from exactly one supplier, so events $A$, $B$, and $C$ form a **partition**. The percentages of parts supplied are the prior probabilities $0.5$, $0.3$, and $0.2$. The percentages of defective parts are conditional probabilities, since each refers only to parts from one supplier, and they are $P(D \mid A) = 0.02$, $P(D \mid B) = 0.03$, and $P(D \mid C) = 0.05$.

By the **law of total probability**, the probability that a part is defective is the sum of the three products, which is $0.029$, almost $3\%$.

By **Bayes’ theorem**, the probability that a defective part came from a particular supplier is the product for that supplier divided by $P(D)$. Multiplying the numerator and denominator by $1000$ gives the fractions $\dfrac{10}{29}$, $\dfrac{9}{29}$, and $\dfrac{10}{29}$, whose sum is $1$.

Supplier $C$ provides only $20\%$ of the parts, yet among the defective parts its probability rises to $\dfrac{10}{29} \approx 0.34$, the same as for supplier $A$. This is because its percentage of defective parts is the highest, $2.5$ times that of supplier $A$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A filter sorts an office’s email. Suppose $20\%$ of the messages are spam. The filter flags $90\%$ of the spam messages and also flags $5\%$ of the legitimate messages by mistake. Find the probability that a message is flagged, the probability that a flagged message really is spam, and the probability that an unflagged message is spam.

$$
P(I) = 0.2 ,
\quad
P\left( \overline{I} \right) = 0.8 ,
\quad
P(S \mid I) = 0.9 ,
\quad
P\left( S \mid \overline{I} \right) = 0.05
$$
$$
P(S) = 0.2 \cdot 0.9 + 0.8 \cdot 0.05 = 0.18 + 0.04 = 0.22
$$
$$
P(I \mid S) = \frac{0.18}{0.22} = \frac{9}{11}
$$
$$
P\left( I \mid \overline{S} \right) = \frac{P(I) \cdot P\left( \overline{S} \mid I \right)}{P\left( \overline{S} \right)} = \frac{0.2 \cdot 0.1}{1 - 0.22} = \frac{0.02}{0.78} = \frac{1}{39}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $I$ be the event “the message is spam” and $S$ the event “the message is flagged.” Events $I$ and $\overline{I}$ form a partition, and by the complement rule $P\left( \overline{I} \right) = 0.8$.

By the **law of total probability**, the probability that a message is flagged is $0.22$. By **Bayes’ theorem**, the probability that a flagged message is spam is the ratio of $0.18$, the probability of “spam and flagged,” to $0.22$. The result is $\dfrac{9}{11} \approx 0.82$. Therefore about $2$ out of every $11$ flagged messages are legitimate, even though the filter makes errors on only $5\%$ of the legitimate messages, because legitimate messages are much more numerous.

For the last question we need the probability that a spam message goes unflagged. By the complement rule, $P\left( \overline{S} \mid I \right) = 1 - 0.9 = 0.1$, and the denominator is $P\left( \overline{S} \right) = 1 - 0.22 = 0.78$. Bayes’ theorem gives $\dfrac{0.02}{0.78}$. Multiplying the numerator and denominator by $50$ gives $\dfrac{1}{39}$, or about $2.6\%$.

</div>
