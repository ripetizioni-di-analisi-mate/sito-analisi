## Exercise 1

Two fair dice are rolled. Find the probability that the sum of the two numbers is $8$ and the probability that the sum is at least $10$.

$$
|\Omega| = 6 \cdot 6 = 36
$$
$$
\text{sum } 8 : \quad (2, 6), \ (3, 5), \ (4, 4), \ (5, 3), \ (6, 2)
$$
$$
P(\text{sum } 8) = \frac{5}{36}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{sum } 10 &: \quad (4, 6), \ (5, 5), \ (6, 4) \\[4pt]
\text{sum } 11 &: \quad (5, 6), \ (6, 5) \\[4pt]
\text{sum } 12 &: \quad (6, 6)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
P(\text{sum} \geq 10) = \frac{3 + 2 + 1}{36} = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outcomes of rolling two dice are the **ordered pairs** $(a, b)$, where $a$ is the number on the first die and $b$ is the number on the second. Each die has $6$ faces, so there are $36$ pairs. The dice are fair, so the $36$ pairs are equally likely and the **classical definition** applies.

For a sum of $8$, let the first number $a$ range from $1$ to $6$ and find $b = 8 - a$. For $a = 1$ this would give $b = 7$, which is not a possible roll. For $a$ from $2$ to $6$, the value of $b$ lies between $1$ and $6$. There are therefore $5$ favorable pairs, and the probability is $\dfrac{5}{36}$. The pairs $(2, 6)$ and $(6, 2)$ both count because they are distinct outcomes.

The event “the sum is at least $10$” occurs when the sum is $10$, $11$, or $12$. The same method gives $3$, $2$, and $1$ pairs, respectively. There are $6$ favorable outcomes in all, so the probability that the sum is at least $10$ is $\dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A fair coin is tossed four times. Find the probability of getting exactly two heads and the probability of getting more heads than tails.

$$
|\Omega| = 2^4 = 16
$$
$$
TTCC, \ TCTC, \ TCCT, \ CTTC, \ CTCT, \ CCTT
$$
$$
\text{favorable outcomes} = \binom{4}{2} = 6
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
P(\text{two heads}) = \frac{6}{16} = \frac{3}{8}
$$
$$
\text{three heads: } \binom{4}{3} = 4 ,
\qquad
\text{four heads: } \binom{4}{4} = 1
$$
$$
P(\text{more heads than tails}) = \frac{4 + 1}{16} = \frac{5}{16}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each outcome is a sequence of four symbols, each either $T$ or $C$. Each toss has $2$ possible outcomes, so by the fundamental counting principle there are $2^4 = 16$ sequences. The coin is fair, so the sequences are equally likely.

The positions of the two heads among the four tosses determine a sequence with exactly two heads. Choosing two positions out of four gives a combination from $4$ elements taken $2$ at a time, with $2$ elements in each choice, so there are $\dbinom{4}{2} = 6$ sequences. The list above shows them all. The probability of two heads is therefore $\dfrac{6}{16} = \dfrac{3}{8}$.

There are more heads than tails when the number of heads is $3$ or $4$, since two heads and two tails give equal counts. By the same reasoning, there are $\dbinom{4}{3} = 4$ sequences with three heads and only one with four heads. The two cases are mutually exclusive, so their counts add and the probability is $\dfrac{5}{16}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two fair dice are rolled. Find the probability that the product of the two numbers is even and the probability that the difference between the larger number and the smaller number is $2$.

$$
\overline{E} = \text{"the product is odd"} = \text{"both numbers are odd"}
$$
$$
P\left( \overline{E} \right) = \frac{3 \cdot 3}{36} = \frac{9}{36} = \frac{1}{4}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
P(E) = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}
$$
$$
|a - b| = 2 : \quad (1, 3), \ (2, 4), \ (3, 5), \ (4, 6), \ (3, 1), \ (4, 2), \ (5, 3), \ (6, 4)
$$
$$
P(|a - b| = 2) = \frac{8}{36} = \frac{2}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $E$ be the event “the product is even.” The product of two integers is odd if and only if both factors are odd, so the complement $\overline{E}$ is “both numbers are odd.” Its outcomes are easier to count. Each die has $3$ odd faces, so there are $3 \cdot 3 = 9$ pairs of odd numbers and $P\left( \overline{E} \right) = \dfrac{1}{4}$. By the **complement** rule, $P(E) = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

The difference between the larger number and the smaller one is the absolute value $|a - b|$. In pairs with $b = a + 2$, the value of $a$ ranges from $1$ to $4$, since $b$ cannot exceed $6$, so there are $4$ such pairs. Interchanging the two numbers gives the pairs with $b = a - 2$, for another $4$ pairs. In the figure, the favorable outcomes are the solid points on the two lines parallel to the angle bisector. There are $8$ favorable outcomes out of $36$, so the probability is $\dfrac{2}{9}$.

</div>
