## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $9^x - 10 \cdot 3^x + 9 = 0$.

$$
9^x = \left( 3^2 \right)^x = \left( 3^x \right)^2 ,
\qquad
t = 3^x , \quad t > 0
$$
$$
t^2 - 10t + 9 = 0
\iff
(t - 1)(t - 9) = 0
\iff
t = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad t = 9
$$
$$
3^x = 1 = 3^0 \iff x = 0 ,
\qquad
3^x = 9 = 3^2 \iff x = 2
$$

The solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.

::grafico{f="9^x-10*3^x+9" punti="0,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-17 ymax=10 legenda="y = 9^x - 10 \cdot 3^x + 9" titolo="The graph of $y = 9^x - 10 \cdot 3^x + 9$ intersects the $x$-axis at the points with horizontal coordinates $0$ and $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains the powers $9^x$ and $3^x$. Since $9 = 3^2$, we have $9^x = 3^{2x} = \left( 3^x \right)^2$, so the first term is the square of $3^x$. For this reason introduce the **auxiliary variable** $t = 3^x$. An exponential is always positive, so impose the condition $t > 0$ on the new variable.

The substitution gives the quadratic equation $t^2 - 10t + 9 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $10$ and product $9$ are $1$ and $9$, so the trinomial factors as $(t - 1)(t - 9)$. A product is zero when at least one factor is zero, so $t = 1$ or $t = 9$. Both values are positive and satisfy the condition $t > 0$.

For each value of $t$, recover the original unknown $x$ by solving an elementary equation. Write the right-hand side as a power of $3$ and apply the **theorem on equations with the same base**. From $3^x = 3^0$ we obtain $x = 0$, and from $3^x = 3^2$ we obtain $x = 2$. The figure shows that the left-hand side, viewed as a function of $x$, vanishes at precisely these two points.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $4^x - 2^{x + 2} - 32 = 0$.

$$
4^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2 ,
\qquad
2^{x + 2} = 2^2 \cdot 2^x = 4 \cdot 2^x
$$
$$
t = 2^x , \quad t > 0 :
\qquad
t^2 - 4t - 32 = 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 128}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm 12}{2}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t_1 = -4 ,
\quad
t_2 = 8
$$
$$
t_1 = -4 < 0 \quad \text{inadmissible},
\qquad
2^x = 8 = 2^3 \iff x = 3
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponent of $2^{x + 2}$ contains both the unknown and a constant. By the product rule for powers with the same base, write $2^{x + 2} = 2^x \cdot 2^2 = 4 \cdot 2^x$. Also, $4^x = \left( 2^2 \right)^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2$. After these two transformations the equation contains only $2^x$ and its square.

Setting $t = 2^x$ and imposing $t > 0$ gives $t^2 - 4t - 32 = 0$. The discriminant is $16 + 4 \cdot 32 = 144$ and its square root is $12$, so the solutions in $t$ are $-4$ and $8$.

The solution $t = -4$ **is inadmissible**. Indeed, the range of $2^x$ is $(0, +\infty)$, so the equation $2^x = -4$ has no solutions. This leaves $2^x = 8$. Since $8 = 2^3$ and the base $2$ differs from $1$, the exponents are equal and $x = 3$.

Substitution in the original equation gives $4^3 - 2^5 - 32 = 64 - 32 - 32 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $3^{x + 1} + 3^{1 - x} = 10$.

$$
3^{x + 1} = 3 \cdot 3^x ,
\qquad
3^{1 - x} = \frac{3}{3^x}
$$
$$
t = 3^x , \quad t > 0 :
\qquad
3t + \frac{3}{t} = 10
$$
$$
3t^2 + 3 = 10t
\iff
3t^2 - 10t + 3 = 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{10 \pm \sqrt{100 - 36}}{6} = \frac{10 \pm 8}{6}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t_1 = \frac{1}{3} ,
\quad
t_2 = 3
$$
$$
3^x = \frac{1}{3} = 3^{-1} \iff x = -1 ,
\qquad
3^x = 3 \iff x = 1
$$

The solutions are $x = -1$ and $x = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponents $x + 1$ and $1 - x$ contain the unknown with opposite signs. By the exponent laws, write $3^{x + 1} = 3 \cdot 3^x$ and $3^{1 - x} = \dfrac{3^1}{3^x}$, since the exponent of a quotient of powers with the same base is the difference of their exponents. Setting $t = 3^x$ gives the equation $3t + \dfrac{3}{t} = 10$.

Multiply both sides by $t$. This gives an equivalent equation because $t > 0$ and hence $t \neq 0$. The result is $3t^2 - 10t + 3 = 0$, with discriminant $100 - 36 = 64$. The solutions are $t = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $t = \dfrac{18}{6} = 3$, both positive.

From $3^x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ we obtain $x = -1$ because $\dfrac{1}{3} = 3^{-1}$, and from $3^x = 3$ we obtain $x = 1$. The two solutions are opposites, which agrees with the form of the equation. Indeed, interchanging $x$ and $-x$ interchanges the two terms on the left and leaves the equation unchanged.

</div>
