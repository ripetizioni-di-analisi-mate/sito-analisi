## Exercise 1

Sketch the graph of the function $f(x) = 2 - \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^x$ starting from the graph of $y = \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^x$. Give its range, asymptote and intercepts, and describe its monotonicity.

$$
y = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^x
\;\longrightarrow\;
y = -\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^x
\;\longrightarrow\;
y = 2 - \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^x
$$
$$
\text{range: } (-\infty, 2) ,
\qquad
\text{asymptote: } y = 2
$$
$$
f(0) = 2 - 1 = 1 ,
\qquad
2 - \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^x = 0
\iff
\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^x = 2 = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{-1}
\iff
x = -1
$$

The function is increasing, has range $(-\infty, 2)$ and asymptote $y = 2$, and intersects the axes at $(0, 1)$ and $(-1, 0)$.

::grafico{f="0.5^x;2-0.5^x" punti="0,1,pieno;-1,0,pieno" asintoti="y=2" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-5 ymax=6 legenda="y = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^x;y = 2 - \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^x" titolo="The graph of $2 - \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^x$ is obtained from that of $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^x$ by reflecting across the $x$-axis and shifting $2$ units up"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The starting graph, $y = \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^x$, has a base between $0$ and $1$. It is therefore decreasing, passes through $(0, 1)$, has range $(0, +\infty)$ and approaches the $x$-axis as $x \to +\infty$.

The first step changes the sign. The graph of $y = -\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^x$ is the **reflection** of the previous graph across the $x$-axis because each vertical coordinate changes sign. Thus the range becomes $(-\infty, 0)$ and the function becomes increasing.

The second step adds $2$, shifting the graph $2$ units up. The range becomes $(-\infty, 2)$ and the horizontal asymptote becomes the line $y = 2$, which the graph approaches as $x \to +\infty$. A vertical shift preserves monotonicity, so the function is **increasing**.

For the intersection with the $y$-axis evaluate $f(0) = 2 - 1 = 1$. For the intersection with the $x$-axis solve $f(x) = 0$. The equation is equivalent to $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^x = 2$. Since $2 = \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{-1}$, the exponents are equal and $x = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Sketch the graph of $f(x) = \left| 3^x - 3 \right|$ and, as the real parameter $k$ varies, determine the number of solutions of $\left| 3^x - 3 \right| = k$.

$$
3^x - 3 \geq 0 \iff 3^x \geq 3 \iff x \geq 1
$$
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
3^x - 3 & \text{if } x \geq 1 \\
3 - 3^x & \text{if } x < 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f(0) = 2 ,
\qquad
f(1) = 0 ,
\qquad
\text{asymptote for } x \to -\infty : \; y = 3
$$

Number of solutions:
- for $k < 0$, no solutions;
- for $k = 0$, one solution, $x = 1$;
- for $0 < k < 3$, two solutions;
- for $k \geq 3$, one solution.

::grafico{f="abs(3^x-3);1.5" punti="1,0,pieno;0,2,pieno" asintoti="y=3" xmin=-5 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=8 legenda="y = |3^x - 3|;y = k" titolo="A horizontal line $y = k$ with $0 < k < 3$ intersects the graph of $|3^x - 3|$ at two points"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph of $y = 3^x - 3$ is the graph of $3^x$ shifted $3$ units down. It has asymptote $y = -3$ as $x \to -\infty$ and vanishes where $3^x = 3$, that is, at $x = 1$. For $x \geq 1$ the expression $3^x - 3$ is greater than or equal to $0$ because the base $3$ is greater than $1$, and taking its absolute value leaves it unchanged. For $x < 1$ the expression is negative and taking its absolute value reverses its sign.

Therefore the part of the graph with $x < 1$ is **reflected** upward across the $x$-axis. The asymptote $y = -3$ becomes $y = 3$, and the graph passes through $(0, 2)$ because $|1 - 3| = 2$. For $x < 1$ the function $3 - 3^x$ is decreasing and takes every value in the interval $(0, 3)$. For $x \geq 1$ the function $3^x - 3$ is increasing and takes every value from $0$ upward.

The solutions of $\left| 3^x - 3 \right| = k$ are the horizontal coordinates of the points common to the graph and the horizontal line $y = k$. An absolute value is never negative, so there are no solutions for $k < 0$. For $k = 0$ the line is the $x$-axis, which touches the graph only at $x = 1$. For $0 < k < 3$ the line intersects both the left branch and the right branch, giving **two** solutions. For $k \geq 3$ the line lies above the asymptote or coincides with it and intersects only the right branch.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Use a graph to determine the number of solutions of the equation $2^x = x^2$. Find the integer solutions and state between which consecutive integers the other solutions lie.

$$
x = 2 : \quad 2^2 = 4 = 2^2 ,
\qquad
x = 4 : \quad 2^4 = 16 = 4^2
$$
$$
x = -1 : \quad 2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2} < 1 = (-1)^2 ,
\qquad
x = 0 : \quad 2^0 = 1 > 0 = 0^2
$$

The equation has three solutions: $x = 2$, $x = 4$ and a solution between $-1$ and $0$.

::grafico{f="2^x;x^2" punti="2,4,pieno;4,16,pieno;-0.767,0.588,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=20 legenda="y = 2^x;y = x^2" titolo="The graphs of $2^x$ and $x^2$ intersect at points with horizontal coordinates $2$ and $4$, and at a point with horizontal coordinate between $-1$ and $0$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains an exponential and a power with the unknown in its base, and cannot be solved by algebraic methods. By the **graphical method**, its solutions are the horizontal coordinates of the points where the graphs of $y = 2^x$ and $y = x^2$ intersect.

For integer inputs, compare the values. At $x = 2$ and $x = 4$ the two sides are equal, so these numbers are solutions. Between $0$ and $2$ the exponential graph is above the parabola. For example, $2^1 = 2 > 1 = 1^2$. At $x = 3$, however, we have $2^3 = 8 < 9 = 3^2$, so between $2$ and $4$ the parabola lies above the exponential graph, and the two graphs have no other points in common in this interval. For $x > 4$ the exponential exceeds the power and the distance between the graphs increases, so there are no solutions greater than $4$.

For $x < 0$ the function $x^2$ is decreasing and takes arbitrarily large values, whereas $2^x$ is between $0$ and $1$. At $x = -1$ we have $2^x < x^2$, and at $x = 0$ we have $2^x > x^2$. Thus the difference $2^x - x^2$ changes sign between $-1$ and $0$, so the equation has a **third solution** in this interval. On the interval $(-1, 0)$ the function $2^x$ is increasing and $x^2$ is decreasing, so their difference $2^x - x^2$ is increasing and the solution is unique. For $x < -1$ we have $x^2 > 1 > 2^x$, so there are no other negative solutions. Successive trials give an approximate value of $-0.77$ for the negative solution.

</div>
