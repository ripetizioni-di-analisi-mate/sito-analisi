## Exercise 1

A principal of $5000$ euros is invested at an annual compound interest rate of $2\%$.

a) Find the accumulated amount after $3$ years and compare it with the amount obtained at the same rate under simple interest.

b) After how many whole years does the accumulated amount first exceed $5500$ euros?

$$
M(n) = 5000 \cdot 1.02^{n}
$$
$$
\text{a)} \quad M(3) = 5000 \cdot 1.02^3 = 5000 \cdot 1.061208 = 5306.04
$$
$$
M_{\text{simple}} = 5000 \cdot (1 + 3 \cdot 0.02) = 5000 \cdot 1.06 = 5300
$$
$$
\text{b)} \quad 5000 \cdot 1.02^{n} > 5500
\iff
1.02^{n} > 1.1
\iff
n > \log_{1.02} 1.1 \approx 4.81
$$
$$
M(4) \approx 5412.16 ,
\qquad
M(5) \approx 5520.40
$$

a) After $3$ years the accumulated amount is $5306.04$ euros, which is $6.04$ euros more than with simple interest. b) The accumulated amount exceeds $5500$ euros after $5$ years.

<div class="spiegazione">

With **compound interest**, each year's interest is added to the principal, so the balance is multiplied by $1 + i = 1 + 0.02 = 1.02$ each year. After $n$ years the accumulated amount is $M(n) = 5000 \cdot 1.02^n$, which gives an exponential growth model.

For part a), calculate $1.02^3 = 1.02 \cdot 1.02 \cdot 1.02 = 1.061208$ to obtain a balance of $5306.04$ euros. With simple interest, the interest is always calculated on the initial $5000$ euros, so it is $100$ euros each year and after three years the balance is $5300$ euros. The difference of $6.04$ euros comes from the interest earned on previous years' interest.

For part b), require $M(n) > 5500$ and divide both sides by $5000$ to obtain the exponential inequality $1.02^n > 1.1$. The base $1.02$ is greater than $1$, so the function is increasing and the inequality is equivalent to $n > \log_{1.02} 1.1$. The logarithm is approximately $4.81$, calculated using the formula $\dfrac{\ln 1.1}{\ln 1.02}$ from the topic on logarithms. The number of years is a whole number, so the first value satisfying the inequality is $n = 5$. The table of accumulated amounts confirms this: after $4$ years the balance is still below $5500$ euros, and after $5$ years it is above that amount.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A radioactive isotope has a half-life of $8$ days. A sample initially contains $240$ mg of the isotope.

a) Write the formula for the mass $m(t)$ of the isotope remaining after $t$ days.

b) Find the mass after $24$ days and after $12$ days.

c) Find the time in days when the mass falls to $15$ mg.

$$
\text{a)} \quad m(t) = 240 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{t}{8}}
$$
$$
\text{b)} \quad m(24) = 240 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{3} = 30 ,
\qquad
m(12) = 240 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{240}{2\sqrt{2}} = 60\sqrt{2} \approx 84.9
$$
$$
\text{c)} \quad 240 \cdot \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{t}{8}} = 15
\iff
\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{t}{8}} = \frac{1}{16} = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^4
\iff
\frac{t}{8} = 4
\iff
t = 32
$$

a) $m(t) = 240 \cdot \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{t}{8}}$. b) After $24$ days, $30$ mg remain, and after $12$ days, about $84.9$ mg remain. c) The mass is $15$ mg after $32$ days.

::grafico{f="240*0.5^(x/8)" punti="0,240,pieno;12,84.85,pieno;24,30,pieno;32,15,pieno" segmenti="24,0,24,30;32,0,32,15" xmin=0 xmax=40 ymin=0 ymax=250 nomi="t,m" legenda="m = 240 \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{t}{8}}" titolo="The mass of the isotope is halved every $8$ days, leaving $30$ mg after $24$ days and $15$ mg after $32$ days"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For part a), use the law of **radioactive decay**. After $t$ days the number of half-lives elapsed is $\dfrac{t}{8}$ because each lasts $8$ days, and each multiplies the mass by $\dfrac{1}{2}$. With an initial mass of $240$ mg, the formula is $m(t) = 240 \cdot \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{t}{8}}$.

For part b), at $t = 24$ the exponent is $3$, so the mass is $\dfrac{240}{8} = 30$ mg. At $t = 12$ the exponent is $\dfrac{3}{2}$, which is not an integer. By the definition of a power with a rational exponent, $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{3}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2^3}} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}$. Rationalizing gives $\dfrac{240}{2\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{120}{\sqrt{2}} = 60\sqrt{2}$. The result is more than a quarter and less than half of the initial mass because $12$ days is between one and two half-lives.

For part c), divide the equation by $240$ to obtain $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^{\frac{t}{8}} = \dfrac{1}{16}$. Since $16 = 2^4$, the right-hand side is $\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right)^4$, and by the **theorem on equations with the same base** the exponents are equal. Thus $\dfrac{t}{8} = 4$ and the time is $t = 32$ days, that is, four half-lives.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two bacterial cultures are observed in a laboratory. At time $t = 0$ culture $A$ contains $1000$ bacteria and doubles every $3$ hours. Culture $B$ contains $8000$ bacteria and doubles every $6$ hours.

a) Write the formulas $A(t)$ and $B(t)$, with $t$ measured in hours, and calculate the two populations after $6$ hours.

b) Find the time in hours when the two cultures contain the same number of bacteria.

c) Determine the values of $t$ for which the population in culture $A$ exceeds that in culture $B$.

d) The experiment is repeated with culture $B$ starting with $1000k$ bacteria, where $k > 0$, and culture $A$ unchanged. Find $k$ such that the cultures are equal in size after $12$ hours.

$$
\text{a)} \quad A(t) = 1000 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{3}} ,
\qquad
B(t) = 8000 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{6}} ,
\qquad
A(6) = 4000 ,
\quad
B(6) = 16\,000
$$
$$
\text{b)} \quad 1000 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{3}} = 8000 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{6}}
\iff
2^{\frac{t}{3}} = 2^{3 + \frac{t}{6}}
\iff
\frac{t}{3} = 3 + \frac{t}{6}
\iff
\frac{t}{6} = 3
\iff
t = 18
$$
$$
\text{c)} \quad 2^{\frac{t}{3}} > 2^{3 + \frac{t}{6}}
\iff
\frac{t}{3} > 3 + \frac{t}{6}
\iff
t > 18
$$
$$
\text{d)} \quad 1000 \cdot 2^{\frac{12}{3}} = 1000k \cdot 2^{\frac{12}{6}}
\iff
2^4 = k \cdot 2^2
\iff
k = 4
$$

a) After $6$ hours, culture $A$ contains $4000$ bacteria and culture $B$ contains $16\,000$. b) The cultures are equal in size after $18$ hours. c) Culture $A$ exceeds culture $B$ in size for $t > 18$. d) $k = 4$.

::grafico{f="2^(x/3);8*2^(x/6)" punti="18,64,pieno;6,4,pieno;6,16,pieno" segmenti="18,0,18,64" xmin=0 xmax=21 ymin=0 ymax=150 nomi="t,N" legenda="A(t) \text{ in thousands};B(t) \text{ in thousands}" titolo="The cultures, measured in thousands of bacteria, are equal in size after $18$ hours. After that, culture $A$ exceeds culture $B$ in size"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For part a), use the **exponential growth** model. Culture $A$ doubles every $3$ hours, so after $t$ hours the number of doublings is $\dfrac{t}{3}$ and the number of bacteria is $1000 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{3}}$. Similarly, for culture $B$ the number of doublings is $\dfrac{t}{6}$. After $6$ hours culture $A$ has doubled twice and culture $B$ once, so their populations are $4000$ and $16\,000$.

For part b), equate the two formulas and divide both sides by $1000$. The right-hand side becomes $8 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{6}} = 2^3 \cdot 2^{\frac{t}{6}} = 2^{3 + \frac{t}{6}}$ by the product rule for powers with the same base. Both sides are powers with base $2$, so their exponents are equal. The equation $\dfrac{t}{3} - \dfrac{t}{6} = 3$ gives $\dfrac{t}{6} = 3$, that is, $t = 18$. At that time both cultures contain $1000 \cdot 2^6 = 64\,000$ bacteria.

For part c), write the inequality $A(t) > B(t)$ and apply the same transformations. The base $2$ is greater than $1$, so by the **theorem on inequalities with the same base** the direction is preserved, giving $t > 18$.

For part d), substitute $t = 12$ into both formulas, with initial population $1000k$ for culture $B$. After $12$ hours, culture $A$ has doubled $4$ times and culture $B$ has doubled $2$ times, so $16 = 4k$ and $k = 4$. Hence culture $B$ must start with $4000$ bacteria.

</div>
