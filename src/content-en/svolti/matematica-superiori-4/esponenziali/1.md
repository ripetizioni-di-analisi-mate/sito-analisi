## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $8^{x - 1} = \left( \dfrac{1}{4} \right)^{x - 3}$.

$$
8 = 2^3 ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{4} = 2^{-2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
8^{x - 1} &= \left( 2^3 \right)^{x - 1} = 2^{3x - 3} \\[6pt]
\left( \frac{1}{4} \right)^{x - 3} &= \left( 2^{-2} \right)^{x - 3} = 2^{-2x + 6}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
2^{3x - 3} = 2^{-2x + 6}
\iff
3x - 3 = -2x + 6
\iff
5x = 9
\iff
x = \frac{9}{5}
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = \dfrac{9}{5}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $8$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ are both powers of $2$ because $8 = 2^3$ and $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2^2} = 2^{-2}$. The first step, therefore, is to write both sides as powers with base $2$.

By the power-of-a-power rule $\left( a^m \right)^n = a^{mn}$, the exponents are multiplied. On the left-hand side this gives $3\,(x - 1) = 3x - 3$. On the right it gives $-2\,(x - 3) = -2x + 6$, where multiplying $-2$ by $-3$ gives $+6$.

Both sides are now powers with the same base $2$, which is positive and different from $1$. By the **theorem on equations with the same base**, the equation is equivalent to equating the exponents, $3x - 3 = -2x + 6$. The exponents are polynomials, so they are defined for every $x$ and there are no domain restrictions.

To solve the linear equation, move the terms in $x$ to the left and the constants to the right. This gives $5x = 9$, that is, $x = \dfrac{9}{5}$. As a check, at $x = \dfrac{9}{5}$ both exponents equal $\dfrac{12}{5}$ because $3 \cdot \dfrac{9}{5} - 3 = \dfrac{27 - 15}{5}$ and $-2 \cdot \dfrac{9}{5} + 6 = \dfrac{-18 + 30}{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $\sqrt[3]{9^{x + 1}} = \dfrac{27^{x}}{3}$.

$$
\sqrt[3]{9^{x + 1}} = \left( 3^{2x + 2} \right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = 3^{\frac{2x + 2}{3}}
$$
$$
\frac{27^{x}}{3} = \frac{3^{3x}}{3^{1}} = 3^{3x - 1}
$$
$$
3^{\frac{2x + 2}{3}} = 3^{3x - 1}
\iff
\frac{2x + 2}{3} = 3x - 1
$$
$$
2x + 2 = 9x - 3
\iff
7x = 5
\iff
x = \frac{5}{7}
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = \dfrac{5}{7}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $9$ and $27$ and the denominator $3$ are powers of $3$ because $9 = 3^2$ and $27 = 3^3$. Therefore write each side as a single power with base $3$.

On the left the radicand is $9^{x + 1} = \left( 3^2 \right)^{x + 1} = 3^{2x + 2}$. A cube root is a power with exponent $\dfrac{1}{3}$ by the **definition of a power with a rational exponent**, which applies because the base $3^{2x + 2}$ is positive. Multiplying the exponents gives $3^{\frac{2x + 2}{3}}$.

On the right the numerator is $27^{x} = 3^{3x}$. Dividing two powers with the same base gives a power whose exponent is the difference of their exponents, so $\dfrac{3^{3x}}{3} = 3^{3x - 1}$.

Both sides are now powers with the same base $3$, which is greater than $0$ and different from $1$. Thus the equation is equivalent to $\dfrac{2x + 2}{3} = 3x - 1$. Multiplying both sides by $3$ clears the fractions and gives $2x + 2 = 9x - 3$, that is, $7x = 5$. The solution is $x = \dfrac{5}{7}$, at which both exponents equal $\dfrac{8}{7}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $4^{\frac{x}{x - 2}} = 8^{x - 1}$.

$$
\text{Domain condition:} \quad x - 2 \neq 0 \iff x \neq 2
$$
$$
2^{\frac{2x}{x - 2}} = 2^{3x - 3}
\iff
\frac{2x}{x - 2} = 3x - 3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= (3x - 3)(x - 2) \\[6pt]
2x &= 3x^2 - 9x + 6 \\[6pt]
0 &= 3x^2 - 11x + 6
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = \frac{11 \pm \sqrt{121 - 72}}{6} = \frac{11 \pm 7}{6}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x_1 = \frac{2}{3} ,
\quad
x_2 = 3
$$

The solutions are $x = \dfrac{2}{3}$ and $x = 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponent on the left is a fraction with the unknown in the denominator. The fraction is defined only when the denominator differs from $0$, so the **domain condition** is $x \neq 2$.

The bases $4 = 2^2$ and $8 = 2^3$ are powers of $2$. By the power-of-a-power rule, the left-hand side becomes $2^{\frac{2x}{x - 2}}$ and the right-hand side becomes $2^{3\,(x - 1)} = 2^{3x - 3}$. The common base $2$ is positive and different from $1$, so the equation is equivalent to equating the exponents.

For $x \neq 2$ we can multiply both sides by $x - 2$. Expanding the product $(3x - 3)(x - 2) = 3x^2 - 6x - 3x + 6$ and moving all terms to the right gives the quadratic equation $3x^2 - 11x + 6 = 0$. The discriminant $121 - 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 6 = 49$ is positive, so there are two solutions, $x = \dfrac{11 - 7}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ and $x = \dfrac{11 + 7}{6} = 3$.

Both solutions differ from $2$, so they satisfy the domain condition and are admissible. At $x = 3$ the left-hand side is $4^{3} = 64$ and the right-hand side is $8^{2} = 64$.

</div>
