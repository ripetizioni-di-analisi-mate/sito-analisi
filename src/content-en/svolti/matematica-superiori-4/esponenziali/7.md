## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality $9^x - 4 \cdot 3^x + 3 \leq 0$.

$$
t = 3^x , \quad t > 0 :
\qquad
t^2 - 4t + 3 \leq 0
$$
$$
t^2 - 4t + 3 = (t - 1)(t - 3) \leq 0
\iff
1 \leq t \leq 3
$$
$$
1 \leq 3^x \leq 3
\iff
3^0 \leq 3^x \leq 3^1
\iff
0 \leq x \leq 1
$$

The inequality holds for $0 \leq x \leq 1$.

::grafico{f="9^x-4*3^x+3" punti="0,0,pieno;1,0,pieno" segmenti="0,0,1,0" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2 ymax=6 legenda="y = 9^x - 4 \cdot 3^x + 3" titolo="The left-hand side, viewed as a function of $x$, is nonpositive for $0 \leq x \leq 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $9^x = \left( 3^x \right)^2$, the inequality contains only $3^x$ and its square. Therefore set $t = 3^x$ and impose the condition $t > 0$, which holds for every exponential.

The inequality in $t$ is quadratic. The roots of the trinomial $t^2 - 4t + 3$ are $1$ and $3$ because their sum is $4$ and their product is $3$. The coefficient of $t^2$ is positive, so the trinomial is nonpositive between its roots, that is, for $1 \leq t \leq 3$. All these values are positive, so the condition $t > 0$ excludes none of them.

Return to the original unknown $x$. Write the double inequality $1 \leq 3^x \leq 3$ using powers with base $3$, giving $3^0 \leq 3^x \leq 3^1$. The base $3$ exceeds $1$, so by the **theorem on inequalities with the same base**, the direction is preserved and we obtain $0 \leq x \leq 1$.

The figure confirms the result. The graph of the left-hand side has zeros at $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ and lies below the $x$-axis between those points. Its minimum value, $-1$, corresponds to $t = 2$, that is, the point at which $3^x = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality $4^x - 2^{x + 1} - 8 > 0$.

$$
4^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2 ,
\qquad
2^{x + 1} = 2 \cdot 2^x
$$
$$
t = 2^x , \quad t > 0 :
\qquad
t^2 - 2t - 8 > 0
\iff
(t - 4)(t + 2) > 0
$$
$$
t < -2 \quad \text{or} \quad t > 4
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
t > 0 \\[4pt]
t < -2 \;\text{ or }\; t > 4
\end{cases}
\iff
t > 4
$$
$$
2^x > 4 = 2^2
\iff
x > 2
$$

The inequality holds for $x > 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the exponent laws to write $4^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2$ and $2^{x + 1} = 2 \cdot 2^x$. The inequality then contains only $2^x$, and with the **substitution** $t = 2^x$, $t > 0$, it becomes $t^2 - 2t - 8 > 0$.

The trinomial has roots $-2$ and $4$ because $(-2) \cdot 4 = -8$ and $-2 + 4 = 2$. The parabola $y = t^2 - 2t - 8$ opens upward, so the trinomial is positive outside the interval between the roots, that is, for $t < -2$ or $t > 4$.

The solutions in $t$ must also satisfy $t > 0$, and both requirements must hold simultaneously. The interval $t < -2$ has no points in common with $t > 0$, so only $t > 4$ remains.

Return to the unknown $x$ through the inequality $2^x > 2^2$. The base $2$ is greater than $1$, so the direction is preserved and the solution is $x > 2$. For example, at $x = 3$ the left-hand side equals $64 - 16 - 8 = 40 > 0$, whereas at $x = 2$ it equals $16 - 8 - 8 = 0$. The value $2$ is excluded because the inequality is strict.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality $\dfrac{9^x - 3^{x + 1}}{2^x - 4} \geq 0$.

$$
\text{Domain condition:} \quad 2^x - 4 \neq 0 \iff x \neq 2
$$
$$
N(x) = 9^x - 3^{x + 1} = 3^x \left( 3^x - 3 \right)
$$
$$
N(x) \geq 0
\iff
3^x - 3 \geq 0
\iff
x \geq 1 ,
\qquad
D(x) = 2^x - 4 > 0
\iff
x > 2
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
x & & 1 & & 2 & \\ \hline
N(x) & - & 0 & + & + & + \\
D(x) & - & - & - & 0 & + \\ \hline
\dfrac{N(x)}{D(x)} & + & 0 & - & \nexists & +
\end{array}
$$

The inequality holds for $x \leq 1$ or $x > 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is defined when its denominator differs from $0$. The equation $2^x = 4$ has the solution $x = 2$, so the **domain condition** is $x \neq 2$.

Examine the sign of the numerator. Since $9^x = \left( 3^x \right)^2$ and $3^{x + 1} = 3 \cdot 3^x$, factor out $3^x$ to obtain $N(x) = 3^x \left( 3^x - 3 \right)$. The factor $3^x$ is always positive, so $N(x)$ has the sign of $3^x - 3$. This factor is greater than or equal to $0$ for $3^x \geq 3^1$, that is, for $x \geq 1$, because the base $3$ is greater than $1$.

The denominator is positive for $2^x > 2^2$, that is, for $x > 2$, and negative for $x < 2$.

In the sign chart the quotient is positive when the numerator and denominator have the same sign, that is, for $x < 1$ and for $x > 2$, and is negative for $1 < x < 2$. The quotient vanishes at $x = 1$, which is included because the inequality allows equality. The value $x = 2$ is excluded by the domain condition.

</div>
