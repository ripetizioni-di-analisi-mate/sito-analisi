## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $2^{x + 2} + 2^{x - 1} = 36$.

$$
2^{x + 2} = 2^2 \cdot 2^x = 4 \cdot 2^x ,
\qquad
2^{x - 1} = \frac{2^x}{2}
$$
$$
4 \cdot 2^x + \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2^x = 36
\iff
2^x \left( 4 + \frac{1}{2} \right) = 36
\iff
\frac{9}{2} \cdot 2^x = 36
$$
$$
2^x = 36 \cdot \frac{2}{9} = 8 = 2^3
\iff
x = 3
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two powers have the same base $2$, and the exponents $x + 2$ and $x - 1$ differ by a constant. To rewrite a sum of this kind, **factor out a common factor**.

By the product rule for powers with the same base, $2^{x + 2} = 2^x \cdot 2^2 = 4 \cdot 2^x$. By the quotient rule, $2^{x - 1} = \dfrac{2^x}{2^1} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2^x$. Hence both terms contain the factor $2^x$, which can be factored out. The remaining expression is the sum of their coefficients, $4 + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{9}{2}$.

The equation becomes $\dfrac{9}{2} \cdot 2^x = 36$. Divide both sides by $\dfrac{9}{2}$, that is, multiply by $\dfrac{2}{9}$, to obtain the elementary equation $2^x = 8$. Since $8 = 2^3$, the **theorem on equations with the same base** gives equal exponents and hence $x = 3$.

Substitution in the original equation gives $2^5 + 2^2 = 32 + 4 = 36$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $3^{x + 1} + 3^{x} = 2^{x + 3} - 2^{x + 1}$.

$$
3^{x + 1} + 3^{x} = 3^x\,(3 + 1) = 4 \cdot 3^x
$$
$$
2^{x + 3} - 2^{x + 1} = 2^x\,(8 - 2) = 6 \cdot 2^x
$$
$$
4 \cdot 3^x = 6 \cdot 2^x
\iff
\frac{3^x}{2^x} = \frac{6}{4}
\iff
\left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^x = \frac{3}{2}
\iff
x = 1
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side contains powers with base $3$ and the right-hand side contains powers with base $2$. The numbers $2$ and $3$ are not powers of a common base, so first simplify each side by factoring. On the left factor out $3^x$ because $3^{x + 1} = 3 \cdot 3^x$. On the right factor out $2^x$ because $2^{x + 3} = 8 \cdot 2^x$ and $2^{x + 1} = 2 \cdot 2^x$.

This gives $4 \cdot 3^x = 6 \cdot 2^x$. Divide both sides by $4 \cdot 2^x$, which is positive and therefore different from $0$. By the rule for the power of a quotient, the quotient $\dfrac{3^x}{2^x}$ can be written $\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^x$, and the number $\dfrac{6}{4}$ simplifies to $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

The equation $\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^x = \left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^1$ has base $\dfrac{3}{2}$, positive and different from $1$, so it is equivalent to equating the exponents, $x = 1$.

At $x = 1$ the left-hand side equals $9 + 3 = 12$ and the right-hand side equals $16 - 4 = 12$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $2 \cdot 9^x - 5 \cdot 6^x + 3 \cdot 4^x = 0$.

$$
9^x = 3^{2x} ,
\qquad
6^x = 3^x \cdot 2^x ,
\qquad
4^x = 2^{2x}
$$
$$
2 \cdot \frac{9^x}{4^x} - 5 \cdot \frac{6^x}{4^x} + 3 = 0
\iff
2 \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{2x} - 5 \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^{x} + 3 = 0
$$
$$
t = \left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^x , \quad t > 0 :
\qquad
2t^2 - 5t + 3 = 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{25 - 24}}{4} = \frac{5 \pm 1}{4}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t_1 = 1 ,
\quad
t_2 = \frac{3}{2}
$$
$$
\left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^x = 1 \iff x = 0 ,
\qquad
\left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^x = \frac{3}{2} \iff x = 1
$$

The solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $9 = 3^2$, $6 = 3 \cdot 2$ and $4 = 2^2$ are built from only the numbers $2$ and $3$. In each term the exponents of $2$ and $3$ sum to $2x$. An equation with this structure is called **homogeneous** and can be solved by dividing by one of the highest-degree powers.

Divide both sides by the positive quantity $4^x$ to obtain an equivalent equation. The quotients become $\dfrac{9^x}{4^x} = \left( \dfrac{9}{4} \right)^x = \left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^{2x}$ and $\dfrac{6^x}{4^x} = \left( \dfrac{6}{4} \right)^x = \left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^x$, while the third term becomes $3$.

The equation now contains only $\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^x$ and its square. The substitution $t = \left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^x$ with the condition $t > 0$ gives $2t^2 - 5t + 3 = 0$, which has discriminant $1$ and solutions $t = 1$ and $t = \dfrac{3}{2}$, both positive.

Return to the original unknown $x$. Since $1 = \left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^0$, the first equation gives $x = 0$ and the second gives $x = 1$. At $x = 1$ the check gives $18 - 30 + 12 = 0$.

</div>
