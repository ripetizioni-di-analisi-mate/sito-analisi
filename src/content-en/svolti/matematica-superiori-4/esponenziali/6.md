## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $3 \cdot 2^x - 5 = 16$ and state between which consecutive integers the solution lies.

$$
3 \cdot 2^x = 21
\iff
2^x = 7
$$
$$
x = \log_2 7
$$
$$
2^2 = 4 < 7 < 8 = 2^3
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2 < \log_2 7 < 3 ,
\qquad
\log_2 7 \approx 2.81
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = \log_2 7$, between $2$ and $3$.

::grafico{f="2^x;7" punti="2.807,7,pieno" segmenti="2.807,0,2.807,7" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=10 legenda="y = 2^x;y = 7" titolo="The line $y = 7$ intersects the graph of $2^x$ at exactly one point, whose horizontal coordinate $\log_2 7$ lies between $2$ and $3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step is to isolate the power. Adding $5$ to both sides gives $3 \cdot 2^x = 21$. Dividing both sides by $3$ then gives the **elementary equation** $2^x = 7$.

The number $7$ is positive, so it belongs to the range $(0, +\infty)$ of $2^x$. The function is one-to-one, so the equation has a unique solution. The number $7$ is not a power of $2$ with rational exponent, so the solution is written using the **logarithm** to base $2$, namely $x = \log_2 7$, which by definition is the exponent to which $2$ must be raised to obtain $7$.

To locate the solution between two integers, find powers of $2$ close to $7$. We have $4 < 7 < 8$, that is, $2^2 < 2^x < 2^3$. The base $2$ is greater than $1$, so by the theorem on comparing powers with the same base, the exponent $x$ lies between $2$ and $3$. The approximate value $2.81$ can be found with a calculator using the formula $\log_2 7 = \dfrac{\ln 7}{\ln 2}$ proved in the topic on logarithms. The figure shows the unique intersection of the graph of $2^x$ with the line $y = 7$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $4^x - 2^x - 6 = 0$.

$$
t = 2^x , \quad t > 0 :
\qquad
t^2 - t - 6 = 0
\iff
(t - 3)(t + 2) = 0
$$
$$
t = -2 < 0 \quad \text{inadmissible} ,
\qquad
t = 3
$$
$$
2^x = 3
\iff
x = \log_2 3 \approx 1.58
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = \log_2 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $4^x = \left( 2^2 \right)^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2$, the equation contains only $2^x$ and its square. Therefore set $t = 2^x$, with the condition $t > 0$, to obtain the quadratic equation $t^2 - t - 6 = 0$.

Two numbers with sum $1$ and product $-6$ are $3$ and $-2$, so the trinomial factors as $(t - 3)(t + 2)$ and its solutions are $t = 3$ and $t = -2$. The negative solution **is inadmissible** because it fails the condition $t > 0$, which follows from the fact that the function $2^x$ takes only positive values.

This leaves the elementary equation $2^x = 3$. The number $3$ is positive but is not an integer power of $2$, so the solution is written as a logarithm, $x = \log_2 3$. Since $2^1 = 2 < 3 < 4 = 2^2$, the solution lies between $1$ and $2$, and its approximate value is $1.58$.

As a check, at $2^x = 3$ we have $4^x = \left( 2^x \right)^2 = 9$ and the left-hand side equals $9 - 3 - 6 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $2^{x + 1} = 3^x$ and write the solution both as a logarithm to base $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and in terms of natural logarithms.

$$
2 \cdot 2^x = 3^x
\iff
\frac{3^x}{2^x} = 2
\iff
\left( \frac{3}{2} \right)^x = 2
\iff
x = \log_{\frac{3}{2}} 2
$$
$$
\ln 2^{x + 1} = \ln 3^x
\iff
(x + 1) \ln 2 = x \ln 3
\iff
x \left( \ln 3 - \ln 2 \right) = \ln 2
$$
$$
x = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 3 - \ln 2} \approx 1.71
$$

The equation has the unique solution $x = \log_{\frac{3}{2}} 2 = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\ln 3 - \ln 2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $2$ and $3$ are not powers of a common base. In the first method, write $2^{x + 1} = 2 \cdot 2^x$ and divide both sides by the positive quantity $2^x$. The quotient $\dfrac{3^x}{2^x}$ is the power $\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^x$, which gives the elementary equation $\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^x = 2$. The base $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is positive and different from $1$, and the right-hand side is positive, so the solution is unique and equals $x = \log_{\frac{3}{2}} 2$.

In the second method take the **natural logarithm** of both sides. Both sides are positive, and taking logarithms gives an equivalent equality because the logarithm is one-to-one. By the rule $\ln a^u = u \ln a$, proved in the topic on logarithms, the exponents become factors and we obtain the linear equation $(x + 1) \ln 2 = x \ln 3$.

Move the terms involving $x$ to the right and factor out $x$ to obtain $x \left( \ln 3 - \ln 2 \right) = \ln 2$. The coefficient $\ln 3 - \ln 2$ is positive because the natural logarithm is increasing and $3 > 2$, so we can divide by it. The approximate value is $x \approx 1.71$. Indeed, $2^{2.71} \approx 6.54$ and $3^{1.71} \approx 6.54$.

</div>
