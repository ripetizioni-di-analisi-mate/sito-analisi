## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality $\sin x\,(2\cos x + 1) < 0$.

$$
\sin x > 0 \iff 0 < x < \pi \qquad \text{in } [0, 2\pi)
$$
$$
2\cos x + 1 > 0 \iff \cos x > -\frac{1}{2} \iff 0 \leq x < \frac{2\pi}{3} \ \ \text{or} \ \ \frac{4\pi}{3} < x < 2\pi
$$

| | $\left( 0, \dfrac{2\pi}{3} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \pi \right)$ | $\left( \pi, \dfrac{4\pi}{3} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{4\pi}{3}, 2\pi \right)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\sin x$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ |
| $2\cos x + 1$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| product | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ |

$$
\frac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < \pi + 2k\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
\frac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x < 2\pi + 2k\pi , \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

::grafico{f="sin(x)*(2*cos(x)+1)" area="0;sin(x)*(2*cos(x)+1);2.0944;3.1416|0;sin(x)*(2*cos(x)+1);4.1888;6.2832" punti="2.0944,0,vuoto;3.1416,0,vuoto;4.1888,0,vuoto;6.2832,0,vuoto" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7 ymin=-2 ymax=2 titolo="In $[0, 2\pi)$, the graph of $\sin x\,(2\cos x + 1)$ lies below the $x$-axis for $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} < x < \pi$ and for $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} < x < 2\pi$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side is a **product** of two factors, and a product is negative when its factors have opposite signs. Both factors have period $2\pi$, so study their signs in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$. The sine is positive for $0 < x < \pi$, where the points on the circle have positive vertical coordinates. The factor $2\cos x + 1$ is positive when $\cos x > -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The cosine equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ at $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, and the points with horizontal coordinate greater than $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ lie outside the arc between these two angles.

The zeros of the two factors, $0$, $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, $\pi$, $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, divide $[0, 2\pi)$ into four intervals. In the **sign chart**, enter the sign of each factor on each interval and apply the sign rules to find the sign of their product. The product is negative on the second and fourth intervals.

The zeros are excluded because the product equals $0$ there and the inequality is strict. Adding integer multiples of the period $2\pi$ gives the solutions on $\mathbb{R}$. Checking $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ gives $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \big( 1 - \sqrt{3} \big) < 0$, and indeed $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ lies in the second interval.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality $\dfrac{2\sin x - 1}{\cos x} \geq 0$.

$$
N(x) = 2\sin x - 1 > 0 \iff \frac{\pi}{6} < x < \frac{5\pi}{6} \qquad \text{in } [0, 2\pi)
$$
$$
D(x) = \cos x > 0 \iff 0 \leq x < \frac{\pi}{2} \ \ \text{or} \ \ \frac{3\pi}{2} < x < 2\pi
$$

| | $\left( 0, \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{3\pi}{2}, 2\pi \right)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $2\sin x - 1$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ |
| $\cos x$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| quotient | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ |

$$
\frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \leq x < \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
\frac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \leq x < \frac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi , \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is **rational** and is defined for $\cos x \neq 0$, that is, for $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. A quotient is positive when its numerator and denominator have the same sign. Study the sign of each in $[0, 2\pi)$. The numerator is positive for $\sin x > \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, between $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, and vanishes at those two angles. The denominator is positive in the first and fourth quadrants and vanishes at $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$.

The sign chart shows that the quotient is positive on $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ and on $\left( \dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right)$. Next, examine the endpoints. The zeros of the numerator, $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, make the quotient equal to $0$, and the inequality allows equality, so they are **included**. The zeros of the denominator, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, are **excluded** because the quotient is undefined there.

The solutions on $\mathbb{R}$ are obtained by adding $2k\pi$, since the common period of the numerator and denominator is $2\pi$. For $x = \pi$ we have $\dfrac{0 - 1}{-1} = 1 \geq 0$, and indeed $\pi$ lies in the second interval.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality $\dfrac{1 - 2\cos x}{\sin 2x} > 0$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$.

$$
\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\frac{1 - 2\cos x}{2\sin x\cos x} > 0
$$
$$
1 - 2\cos x > 0 \iff \cos x < \frac{1}{2} \iff \frac{\pi}{3} < x < \frac{5\pi}{3}
$$

| | $\left( 0, \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pi \right)$ | $\left( \pi, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{3\pi}{2}, \dfrac{5\pi}{3} \right)$ | $\left( \dfrac{5\pi}{3}, 2\pi \right)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1 - 2\cos x$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ |
| $\sin x$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $-$ |
| $\cos x$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| quotient | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
\frac{\pi}{3} < x < \frac{\pi}{2}
\quad \text{or} \quad
\pi < x < \frac{3\pi}{2}
\quad \text{or} \quad
\frac{5\pi}{3} < x < 2\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator contains the angle $2x$ and the numerator the angle $x$. By the **double-angle identity** $\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x$, the denominator becomes a product of factors in the angle $x$. The factor $2$ is positive, so it does not affect the sign. The inequality is defined for $\sin 2x \neq 0$, that is, for $\sin x \neq 0$ and $\cos x \neq 0$. In $[0, 2\pi)$, the excluded values are therefore $0$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\pi$, and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$.

The numerator is positive when $\cos x < \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, on the arc from $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ through $\pi$. Sine is positive on $(0, \pi)$, and cosine is positive in the first and fourth quadrants. The zeros of the three factors divide $[0, 2\pi)$ into six intervals. In each interval, the sign of the quotient is the product of the three signs because the quotient of two numbers has the same sign as their product.

The quotient is positive on the second, fourth, and sixth intervals. All endpoints are excluded. At $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$, the quotient equals $0$, which is not positive, and the denominator vanishes at the other endpoints. For example, with $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ the numerator equals $1 + \sqrt{2} > 0$ and $\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{2} = 1 > 0$, so $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ is a **solution**.

</div>
