## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality $2\sin^2 x - \sin x - 1 < 0$.

$$
t = \sin x ,
\qquad
2t^2 - t - 1 < 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 8}}{4} = \frac{1 \pm 3}{4}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t_1 = -\frac{1}{2} , \quad t_2 = 1
$$
$$
-\frac{1}{2} < t < 1
\iff
\begin{cases}
\sin x > -\dfrac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
\sin x < 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
-\frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi < x < \frac{7\pi}{6} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad
x \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),-0.5236,1.5708;cos(t),sin(t),3.6652,5.7596;t,-0.5,-1.4,1.4;cos(t),sin(t),1.5708,3.6652" punti="0.866,-0.5,vuoto,−π/6;-0.866,-0.5,vuoto,7π/6;0,1,vuoto,π/2" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The solutions are the angles with sine greater than $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, shown by the solid arc from $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, excluding the point $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ where $\sin x = 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality contains only $\sin x$, so the **substitution** $t = \sin x$ gives a quadratic inequality in the variable $t$. The trinomial $2t^2 - t - 1$ has discriminant $1 + 8 = 9$ and roots $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $1$. The coefficient of $t^2$ is positive, so the trinomial is negative between the roots, that is, for $-\dfrac{1}{2} < t < 1$.

Return to the variable $x$. The double inequality is equivalent to the system of conditions $\sin x > -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin x < 1$, which must hold simultaneously. The sine equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ at the angles $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, and the points with vertical coordinate greater than $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the arc from $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ through $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The second condition excludes only the angles at which the sine equals $1$, namely $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, since the sine never exceeds $1$.

The intersection is therefore the arc from the first condition with the point $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ removed. The inequality is **satisfied** for $-\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{7\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ with $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. Indeed, for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the result is $2 - 1 - 1 = 0$, which is not negative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality $2\cos^2 x + 3\cos x - 2 \geq 0$.

$$
t = \cos x ,
\qquad
2t^2 + 3t - 2 \geq 0
$$
$$
t = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 16}}{4} = \frac{-3 \pm 5}{4}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t_1 = -2 , \quad t_2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
t \leq -2 \quad \text{or} \quad t \geq \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\cos x \leq -2 \quad \text{has no solutions} ,
\qquad
\cos x \geq \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
-\frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi , \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the substitution $t = \cos x$, the inequality becomes quadratic in $t$. The discriminant of the trinomial is $9 + 16 = 25$, so its roots are $\dfrac{-3 - 5}{4} = -2$ and $\dfrac{-3 + 5}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The coefficient of $t^2$ is positive, hence the trinomial is greater than or equal to $0$ for values of $t$ at or below the smaller root or at or above the larger root.

Return to the variable $x$ and compare the two conditions with the range of cosine, the interval $[-1, 1]$. Since $\cos x \geq -1 > -2$ for every $x$, the condition $\cos x \leq -2$ **has no solutions**. This leaves the condition $\cos x \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$. The cosine equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at the angles $\pm\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and the points on the circle with horizontal coordinate greater than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the arc from $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ through $(1, 0)$.

The endpoints are included because the trinomial equals $0$ there. The solutions are therefore $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. Checking $x = 0$ gives $2 + 3 - 2 = 3 \geq 0$, whereas $x = \pi$ gives $2 - 3 - 2 = -3$, and indeed $\pi$ is not a solution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality $\cos 2x + 3\sin x - 2 \geq 0$.

$$
\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x
$$
$$
1 - 2\sin^2 x + 3\sin x - 2 \geq 0
\iff
-2\sin^2 x + 3\sin x - 1 \geq 0
\iff
2\sin^2 x - 3\sin x + 1 \leq 0
$$
$$
t = \sin x ,
\qquad
2t^2 - 3t + 1 \leq 0
\iff
(2t - 1)(t - 1) \leq 0
\iff
\frac{1}{2} \leq t \leq 1
$$
$$
\sin x \geq \frac{1}{2}
\iff
\frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi , \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality contains two different arguments, $2x$ and $x$. To obtain a single function with the same argument, use the **double-angle identity** for cosine in the form $\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x$, which involves only sine. This form is chosen because the inequality contains $\sin x$.

After substitution, combine the constant terms, $1 - 2 = -1$, and multiply both sides by $-1$. Multiplying by a negative number reverses the inequality sign, so $\geq$ becomes $\leq$. With $t = \sin x$, the trinomial $2t^2 - 3t + 1$ has roots $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $1$ because $2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{3}{2} + 1 = 0$ and $2 - 3 + 1 = 0$, and it factors as $(2t - 1)(t - 1)$. The trinomial is less than or equal to $0$ between the roots, including the roots themselves.

The condition $\sin x \leq 1$ holds for every $x$, so this leaves $\sin x \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$. The points on the circle with vertical coordinate greater than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the arc from $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, including both endpoints. The inequality is therefore **satisfied** for $\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, we have $\cos\pi + 3 - 2 = -1 + 1 = 0 \geq 0$.

</div>
