## Exercise 1

Consider the inequality $2\sin x \geq m$, where $m$ is a real parameter.

a) Find the values of $m$ for which the inequality holds for every real $x$.

b) Find the values of $m$ for which the inequality has no solutions.

c) For $m = -1$, solve the inequality in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$.

$$
-1 \leq \sin x \leq 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
-2 \leq 2\sin x \leq 2
$$
$$
\text{a)} \quad m \leq -2
\qquad\qquad
\text{b)} \quad m > 2
$$
$$
\text{c)} \quad 2\sin x \geq -1
\iff
\sin x \geq -\frac{1}{2}
\iff
-\frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \leq x \leq \frac{7\pi}{6} + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\text{in } [0, 2\pi] : \qquad 0 \leq x \leq \frac{7\pi}{6} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{11\pi}{6} \leq x \leq 2\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sine takes every value in the interval $[-1, 1]$, so $2\sin x$ takes every value in $[-2, 2]$. The minimum value $-2$ occurs for $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, and the maximum value $2$ for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$.

a) The inequality holds for every $x$ if even the smallest value on the left-hand side is greater than or equal to $m$, that is, if $-2 \geq m$. If instead $m > -2$, setting $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ gives $-2 \geq m$, which is false. The answer is therefore $m \leq -2$.

b) The inequality has no solutions if even the largest value on the left-hand side is less than $m$, that is, if $2 < m$. For $m = 2$, the inequality still has the solutions $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, at which equality holds. The answer is $m > 2$.

c) With $m = -1$, divide by $2$ to obtain $\sin x \geq -\dfrac{1}{2}$. The sine equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ at $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6} = -\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2\pi$. The points on the circle with vertical coordinate greater than or equal to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the arc from $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ through $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. In $[0, 2\pi]$, the interval with $k = 0$ gives the part from $0$ to $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$, and the interval with $k = 1$ gives the part from $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ to $2\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isosceles triangle $ABC$ has equal sides $AB = AC = 2$ and vertex angle $B\widehat{A}C = x$, with $0 < x < \pi$.

a) Express the area and perimeter of the triangle in terms of $x$.

b) Find the values of $x$ for which the area is greater than or equal to $\sqrt{3}$.

c) Find the values of $x$ for which the perimeter is greater than $4 + 2\sqrt{2}$, and the values for which both conditions are satisfied.

$$
\text{a)} \quad
\mathcal{A}(x) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot \sin x = 2\sin x ,
\qquad
BC = 2 \cdot 2\sin\frac{x}{2} = 4\sin\frac{x}{2}
$$
$$
2p(x) = 4 + 4\sin\frac{x}{2}
$$
$$
\text{b)} \quad 2\sin x \geq \sqrt{3}
\iff
\sin x \geq \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\iff
\frac{\pi}{3} \leq x \leq \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$
$$
\text{c)} \quad 4 + 4\sin\frac{x}{2} > 4 + 2\sqrt{2}
\iff
\sin\frac{x}{2} > \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\iff
\frac{\pi}{4} < \frac{x}{2} < \frac{\pi}{2}
\iff
\frac{\pi}{2} < x < \pi
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\pi}{3} \leq x \leq \dfrac{2\pi}{3} \\[6pt]
\dfrac{\pi}{2} < x < \pi
\end{cases}
\iff
\frac{\pi}{2} < x \leq \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,2 -1.4142,0.5858 1.4142,0.5858 0,2" segmenti="0,2,0,0.5858" etichette="0,2.25,A;-1.6,0.45,B;1.6,0.45,C;-0.85,1.45,2;0.85,1.45,2;0.18,1.55,x;0.6,0.4,2 sin(x/2)" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=0.2 ymax=2.4 titolo="The isosceles triangle with equal sides of length $2$ and vertex angle $x$. The altitude from $A$ divides the base into two segments of length $2\sin\dfrac{x}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

a) The area of a triangle is half the product of two sides and the sine of the included angle, as shown in the topic on trigonometry. With sides $AB = AC = 2$ and angle $x$, this gives $2\sin x$. In an isosceles triangle, the altitude from $A$ is also an angle bisector and a median, so it divides the triangle into two right triangles with hypotenuse $2$ and angle $\dfrac{x}{2}$ at $A$. The leg opposite this angle is half the base and equals $2\sin\dfrac{x}{2}$ by the right-triangle theorem. The perimeter is therefore $2 + 2 + 4\sin\dfrac{x}{2}$.

b) Divide by $2$. The sine equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and is greater between these two angles. The intervals for $k \neq 0$ lie outside $(0, \pi)$, so the area is at least $\sqrt{3}$ for $\dfrac{\pi}{3} \leq x \leq \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

c) Subtracting $4$ and dividing by $4$ gives $\sin\dfrac{x}{2} > \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. Since $0 < x < \pi$, the angle $\dfrac{x}{2}$ lies between $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, where sine is increasing and equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Thus $\dfrac{x}{2} > \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, that is, $x > \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and the perimeter exceeds $4 + 2\sqrt{2}$ exactly when the vertex angle is obtuse. Both conditions must hold, and the intersection of the intervals is $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < x \leq \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The height above the ground of a Ferris wheel cabin is
$$
h(t) = 20 - 18\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{10}\,t \right) ,
$$
with $h$ in meters and $t$ in minutes, $0 \leq t \leq 20$.

a) Find the times when the cabin is at a height greater than $29\,\mathrm{m}$, and determine how long it stays above that height.

b) Find the times when the cabin is at a height no greater than $11\,\mathrm{m}$.

c) Calculate the fraction of one revolution that the cabin spends between $11\,\mathrm{m}$ and $29\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\text{a)} \quad
20 - 18\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{10}\,t \right) > 29
\iff
\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{10}\,t \right) < -\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{2\pi}{3} < \frac{\pi}{10}\,t < \frac{4\pi}{3}
\iff
\frac{20}{3} < t < \frac{40}{3} ,
\qquad
\frac{40}{3} - \frac{20}{3} = \frac{20}{3}\,\mathrm{min}
$$
$$
\text{b)} \quad
20 - 18\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{10}\,t \right) \leq 11
\iff
\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{10}\,t \right) \geq \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
0 \leq \frac{\pi}{10}\,t \leq \frac{\pi}{3} \ \ \text{or} \ \ \frac{5\pi}{3} \leq \frac{\pi}{10}\,t \leq 2\pi
\iff
0 \leq t \leq \frac{10}{3} \ \ \text{or} \ \ \frac{50}{3} \leq t \leq 20
$$
$$
\text{c)} \quad
20 - \frac{20}{3} - \frac{10}{3} - \frac{10}{3} = \frac{20}{3} ,
\qquad
\frac{20}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{20} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

::grafico{f="20-18*cos(pi*x/10);29;11" area="20-18*cos(pi*x/10);29;6.6667;13.3333" punti="6.6667,29,vuoto;13.3333,29,vuoto;3.3333,11,pieno;16.6667,11,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=20 ymin=0 ymax=40 nomi="t,h" legenda="h = 20 - 18\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{10}\,t \right);h = 29;h = 11" titolo="During one revolution, the cabin is above $29\,\mathrm{m}$ for $\dfrac{20}{3} < t < \dfrac{40}{3}$ and below $11\,\mathrm{m}$ at the beginning and end of the revolution"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument of the cosine is $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{10}\,t$, which for $0 \leq t \leq 20$ varies from $0$ to $2\pi$, so the time interval corresponds to one full revolution. At time $t = 0$, the cabin is at its lowest point, at $20 - 18 = 2\,\mathrm{m}$.

a) Subtract $20$ and divide by $-18$, which is negative, so the inequality reverses and $-18\cos\theta > 9$ becomes $\cos\theta < -\dfrac{1}{2}$. In $[0, 2\pi]$, this happens between $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$. Multiplying by $\dfrac{10}{\pi} > 0$ gives $\dfrac{20}{3} < t < \dfrac{40}{3}$. The duration is $\dfrac{20}{3}\,\mathrm{min}$, that is, **6 minutes and 40 seconds**.

b) Similarly, $-18\cos\theta \leq -9$ becomes $\cos\theta \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$, which in $[0, 2\pi]$ holds near $0$ and near $2\pi$. Multiplying by $\dfrac{10}{\pi}$ gives the two time intervals at the beginning and end of the revolution.

c) The times when the cabin is between $11\,\mathrm{m}$ and $29\,\mathrm{m}$ are those remaining after the two preceding cases have been removed. Their total duration is $20 - \dfrac{20}{3} - \dfrac{20}{3} = \dfrac{20}{3}\,\mathrm{min}$, that is, **one-third** of a revolution.

</div>
