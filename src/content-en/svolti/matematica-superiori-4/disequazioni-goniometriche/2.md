## Exercise 1

Solve the inequality $2\sin(2x) \geq 1$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$.

$$
2\sin(2x) \geq 1
\iff
\sin(2x) \geq \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \leq 2x \leq \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{12} + k\pi \leq x \leq \frac{5\pi}{12} + k\pi , \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
k = 0 &: \quad \frac{\pi}{12} \leq x \leq \frac{5\pi}{12} \\[6pt]
k = 1 &: \quad \frac{13\pi}{12} \leq x \leq \frac{17\pi}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sin(2*x);0.5" area="sin(2*x);0.5;0.2618;1.309|sin(2*x);0.5;3.4034;4.4506" punti="0.2618,0.5,pieno;1.309,0.5,pieno;3.4034,0.5,pieno;4.4506,0.5,pieno" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 legenda="y = \sin(2x);y = \dfrac{1}{2}" titolo="In $[0, 2\pi]$, the graph of $\sin(2x)$ lies above the line $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ on two intervals because the period of $\sin(2x)$ is $\pi$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing by $2$, which is positive, gives $\sin(2x) \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$. The argument of the sine is $2x$, so set $t = 2x$ and solve the **basic inequality** $\sin t \geq \dfrac{1}{2}$. The sine equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at the angles $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and is greater than $\dfrac{1}{2}$ between them, where the points on the circle lie above the line $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The endpoints are included because the sign is $\geq$.

Return to the variable $x$ by substituting $2x$ for $t$ and dividing all three parts of the double inequality by $2$. The term $2k\pi$ is also divided by $2$ and becomes $k\pi$, so the solutions repeat with period $\pi$. This agrees with the fact that the function $\sin(2x)$ has period $\dfrac{2\pi}{2} = \pi$.

To stay in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$, give $k$ the values $0$ and $1$. For $k = 1$, add $\pi = \dfrac{12\pi}{12}$ to the endpoints to obtain the interval from $\dfrac{13\pi}{12}$ to $\dfrac{17\pi}{12}$. With $k = 2$, the left endpoint is $\dfrac{25\pi}{12} > 2\pi$, and with $k = -1$ the right endpoint is negative, so these values give no solutions. The solutions are $\dfrac{\pi}{12} \leq x \leq \dfrac{5\pi}{12}$ or $\dfrac{13\pi}{12} \leq x \leq \dfrac{17\pi}{12}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the inequality $\cos\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) < \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$$
t = x - \frac{\pi}{4} ,
\qquad
\cos t < \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < t < \frac{5\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi < x - \frac{\pi}{4} < \frac{5\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi < x &< \frac{5\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi \\[6pt]
\frac{7\pi}{12} + 2k\pi < x &< \frac{23\pi}{12} + 2k\pi , \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality contains the cosine of the expression $x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so set $t = x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. The cosine is the horizontal coordinate of the point corresponding to the angle $t$. Since $\arccos\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, the points with horizontal coordinate less than $\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the arc from $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to $2\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ through $\pi$. The endpoints are excluded because the inequality is strict.

Substitute $x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ for $t$ and add $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ to all three parts. Adding the same number preserves the direction of the inequalities. Calculate the sums using the common denominator $12$:
$$
\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{4\pi + 3\pi}{12} = \frac{7\pi}{12} ,
\qquad
\frac{5\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{20\pi + 3\pi}{12} = \frac{23\pi}{12} .
$$
The coefficient of $x$ is $1$, so the period remains $2\pi$. The inequality is **satisfied** for $\dfrac{7\pi}{12} + 2k\pi < x < \dfrac{23\pi}{12} + 2k\pi$. Checking $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, which lies in the interval for $k = 0$, gives $\cos\pi = -1 < \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the inequality $\tan\left( 3x + \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) > \sqrt{3}$ in the interval $[0, \pi]$.

$$
t = 3x + \frac{\pi}{6} ,
\qquad
\tan t > \sqrt{3}
\iff
\frac{\pi}{3} + k\pi < t < \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{6} + k\pi < 3x < \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{6} + k\pi
\iff
\frac{\pi}{6} + k\pi < 3x < \frac{\pi}{3} + k\pi
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{18} + \frac{k\pi}{3} < x < \frac{\pi}{9} + \frac{k\pi}{3} , \qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
k = 0 &: \quad \frac{\pi}{18} < x < \frac{\pi}{9} \\[6pt]
k = 1 &: \quad \frac{7\pi}{18} < x < \frac{4\pi}{9} \\[6pt]
k = 2 &: \quad \frac{13\pi}{18} < x < \frac{7\pi}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set $t = 3x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Tangent is increasing on each branch and equals $\sqrt{3}$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so $\tan t > \sqrt{3}$ for $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi < t < \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. The right endpoint is excluded because tangent is undefined at $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. Thus the domain restriction $t \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ is already incorporated into the solutions.

Substitute $3x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ for $t$ and subtract $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ from all three parts. Since $\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, this gives a double inequality for $3x$. Divide by $3$, which is positive, so the direction is unchanged. The numbers $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ become $\dfrac{\pi}{18}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{9}$, and the period $k\pi$ becomes $\dfrac{k\pi}{3}$.

In the interval $[0, \pi]$, give $k$ the values $0$, $1$, $2$, adding $\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{6\pi}{18}$ to the endpoints each time. For $k = 3$ the left endpoint is $\dfrac{19\pi}{18} > \pi$, and for $k = -1$ the right endpoint is negative. The solutions therefore form **three intervals** of length $\dfrac{\pi}{18}$. For example, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{12}$ lies in the first, and indeed $\tan\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) = \tan\dfrac{5\pi}{12} = 2 + \sqrt{3} > \sqrt{3}$.

</div>
