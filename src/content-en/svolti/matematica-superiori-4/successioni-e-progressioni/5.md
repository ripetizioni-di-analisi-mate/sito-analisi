## Exercise 1

Insert five numbers between $3$ and $27$ so that the seven numbers form an arithmetic progression.

$$
a_1 = 3, \qquad a_7 = 27
$$
$$
a_7 = a_1 + 6d
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
27 = 3 + 6d
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
d = \frac{27 - 3}{6} = 4
$$
$$
3, \quad 7, \quad 11, \quad 15, \quad 19, \quad 23, \quad 27
$$

The five arithmetic means are $7$, $11$, $15$, $19$, and $23$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The five numbers to be inserted, together with $3$ and $27$, form a progression of $7$ terms, in which $3$ is the first term and $27$ is the seventh. Going from the first term to the seventh takes $6$ steps, one more than the number of inserted terms. This is the case $k = 5$ of the formula $d = \dfrac{B - A}{k + 1}$ for **arithmetic means**.

The general term gives $a_7 = a_1 + 6d$, so $6d = 24$ and $d = 4$. To find the inserted numbers, add $4$ repeatedly starting from $3$. The final step, from $23$ to $27$, confirms that the common difference is correct.

The middle term of the progression is $15$, which is also the arithmetic mean of the two end terms, $\dfrac{3 + 27}{2} = 15$, because the fourth term is three steps from either end. The property of terms equidistant from the ends also gives $7 + 23 = 11 + 19 = 3 + 27 = 30$. The sum of the seven numbers is therefore $\dfrac{7 \cdot 30}{2} = 105$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Insert three positive numbers between $2$ and $162$ so that the five numbers form a geometric progression. What changes if the numbers need not be positive?

$$
a_1 = 2, \qquad a_5 = 162
$$
$$
a_5 = a_1\,q^4
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q^4 = \frac{162}{2} = 81
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad q = -3
$$
$$
q = 3: \qquad 2, \quad 6, \quad 18, \quad 54, \quad 162
$$
$$
q = -3: \qquad 2, \quad -6, \quad 18, \quad -54, \quad 162
$$

The three positive geometric means are $6$, $18$, and $54$. Without the positivity condition there is also the solution consisting of $-6$, $18$, and $-54$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The five numbers form a geometric progression with first term $2$ and fifth term $162$. There are $4$ steps from the first term to the fifth, so the **general term** gives $162 = 2\,q^4$, that is, $q^4 = 81$.

The exponent is even, so the equation $q^4 = 81$ has two real solutions, $q = 3$ and $q = -3$. If the numbers must be positive, the common ratio must be positive because a negative common ratio makes the terms alternate in sign. Therefore only $q = 3$ is possible, and the **geometric means** are $6$, $18$, and $54$. This is the case $k = 3$ of the formula $q = \sqrt[k+1]{\dfrac{B}{A}} = \sqrt[4]{81} = 3$.

If positivity is not required, $q = -3$ is also possible, and the inserted numbers are $-6$, $18$, and $-54$. The middle term $18$ is the same in both cases because $a_3 = 2\,q^2$ and $q^2 = 9$ for both common ratios. The number $18$ is also the geometric mean of the two end terms because $\sqrt{2 \cdot 162} = \sqrt{324} = 18$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the real values of $x$ for which the numbers $x - 1$, $x + 3$, $3x + 1$, in that order, are consecutive terms of a geometric progression, and write down the resulting progressions.

$$
(x + 3)^2 = (x - 1)(3x + 1)
$$
$$
x^2 + 6x + 9 = 3x^2 - 2x - 1
$$
$$
2x^2 - 8x - 10 = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
(x - 5)(x + 1) = 0
$$
$$
x = 5: \qquad 4, \quad 8, \quad 16 \qquad (q = 2)
$$
$$
x = -1: \qquad -2, \quad 2, \quad -2 \qquad (q = -1)
$$

The values are $x = 5$ and $x = -1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Three numbers $a$, $b$, $c$, each different from $0$, are consecutive terms of a geometric progression if the quotients $\dfrac{b}{a}$ and $\dfrac{c}{b}$ are equal. This condition is equivalent to $b^2 = ac$, obtained by multiplying both sides by $ab$. Therefore set the square of the middle term $x + 3$ equal to the product of the other two.

Expand the square of the binomial $x + 3$ and the product $(x - 1)(3x + 1) = 3x^2 + x - 3x - 1 = 3x^2 - 2x - 1$. Moving all terms to the right-hand side gives $2x^2 - 8x - 10 = 0$, and division by $2$ then gives $x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0$. Two numbers with sum $4$ and product $-5$ are $5$ and $-1$, so the quadratic factors as $(x - 5)(x + 1)$ and the solutions are $x = 5$ and $x = -1$.

The condition $b^2 = ac$ was derived under the assumption that all three numbers differ from $0$, so check each solution. For $x = 5$ the numbers are $4$, $8$, $16$, all different from $0$, with common ratio $2$. For $x = -1$ the numbers are $-2$, $2$, $-2$, also different from $0$, with common ratio $-1$. Both solutions are therefore **valid**.

</div>
