## Exercise 1

Find the arithmetic progressions in which $a_2 + a_5 = 20$ and $a_3 \cdot a_4 = 99$.

$$
\begin{cases}
(a_1 + d) + (a_1 + 4d) = 20 \\[4pt]
(a_1 + 2d)(a_1 + 3d) = 99
\end{cases}
$$
$$
2a_1 + 5d = 20
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
a_1 = 10 - \frac{5}{2}\,d
$$
$$
a_3 = 10 - \frac{d}{2},
\qquad
a_4 = 10 + \frac{d}{2}
$$
$$
\left( 10 - \frac{d}{2} \right)\left( 10 + \frac{d}{2} \right) = 100 - \frac{d^2}{4} = 99
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
d^2 = 4
$$
$$
d = 2, \ a_1 = 5: \qquad 5, \ 7, \ 9, \ 11, \ 13, \ \ldots
$$
$$
d = -2, \ a_1 = 15: \qquad 15, \ 13, \ 11, \ 9, \ 7, \ \ldots
$$

There are two progressions, one with $a_1 = 5$ and $d = 2$ and the other with $a_1 = 15$ and $d = -2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknowns are the first term $a_1$ and the common difference $d$. Write each term using the **general term** $a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)\,d$. The two conditions must both hold, so they form a system of equations.

The first equation is linear and gives $2a_1 = 20 - 5d$, so $a_1 = 10 - \dfrac{5}{2}\,d$. Substituting into $a_3 = a_1 + 2d$ and $a_4 = a_1 + 3d$ gives $10 - \dfrac{d}{2}$ and $10 + \dfrac{d}{2}$. The number $10$ is the mean of the two middle terms $a_3$ and $a_4$, by the property of **equidistant terms**, which gives $a_2 + a_5 = a_3 + a_4 = 20$.

The difference-of-squares identity gives the product in the second equation as $100 - \dfrac{d^2}{4}$. Setting it equal to $99$ gives $\dfrac{d^2}{4} = 1$, so $d^2 = 4$ and $d = \pm 2$.

With $d = 2$ we have $a_1 = 10 - 5 = 5$, and with $d = -2$ we have $a_1 = 10 + 5 = 15$. In the first case $a_2 + a_5 = 7 + 13 = 20$ and $a_3 a_4 = 9 \cdot 11 = 99$. In the second, the same terms appear in reverse order, so both cases satisfy the conditions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a geometric progression with positive terms, the first term is $2$ and the seventh is $128$. Calculate the common ratio and the product of the first seven terms.

$$
128 = 2\,q^6
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q^6 = 64
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q = 2
$$
$$
P_7 = \sqrt{(a_1 \cdot a_7)^7} = \sqrt{(2 \cdot 128)^7} = \sqrt{256^7}
$$
$$
\sqrt{256^7} = \left( \sqrt{256} \right)^7 = 16^7 = 2^{28}
$$

The common ratio is $q = 2$ and the product of the first seven terms is $P_7 = 2^{28} = 268\,435\,456$.

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $6$ steps from the first term to the seventh, so the **general term** gives $a_7 = a_1\,q^6$ and $q^6 = 64$. The real solutions are $q = 2$ and $q = -2$ because the exponent is even. The terms are positive, so the common ratio is positive and only $q = 2$ is possible. The terms are $2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128$.

For the product, use the formula for the **product of the first n terms**, which for positive terms is $P_n = \sqrt{(a_1 a_n)^n}$. With $n = 7$ the product of the two end terms is $2 \cdot 128 = 256$. The square root of a power can be found by raising the square root of the base to that power, and $\sqrt{256} = 16$, so $P_7 = 16^7$. Since $16 = 2^4$, we have $16^7 = 2^{28}$.

To check the result, write every term as a power of $2$. The terms are $2^1, 2^2, \ldots, 2^7$, and when powers with the same base are multiplied, their exponents add. The sum $1 + 2 + \dots + 7$ equals $\dfrac{7 \cdot 8}{2} = 28$ by the formula for the sum of the first $n$ natural numbers, so the product is $2^{28}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three numbers are consecutive terms of a geometric progression. Their sum is $21$ and their product is $216$. Find the three numbers.

$$
\frac{b}{q}, \quad b, \quad b\,q
$$
$$
\frac{b}{q} \cdot b \cdot b\,q = b^3 = 216
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
b = 6
$$
$$
a + c = 21 - 6 = 15,
\qquad
a \cdot c = b^2 = 36
$$
$$
t^2 - 15t + 36 = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
(t - 3)(t - 12) = 0
$$

The numbers are $3$, $6$, $12$, with common ratio $2$, or $12$, $6$, $3$, with common ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The product is given, so denote the middle term by $b$ and write the other two as $\dfrac{b}{q}$ and $b\,q$, where $q$ is the common ratio. With this choice the ratio cancels from the product of the three numbers, leaving $b^3 = 216$. The equation $b^3 = 216$ has only one real solution, $b = 6$, because the exponent is odd.

The first number $a$ and the third number $c$ satisfy two conditions. The sum of the three numbers is $21$, so $a + c = 15$. Also, the middle term is the **geometric mean** of the other two, so $a \cdot c = b^2 = 36$. Two numbers with sum $15$ and product $36$ are the solutions of the equation $t^2 - 15t + 36 = 0$, which factors as $(t - 3)(t - 12) = 0$.

The solutions are $3$ and $12$, and there are two ways to assign them to $a$ and $c$. With $a = 3$ and $c = 12$, the progression is $3, 6, 12$, with common ratio $2$. With $a = 12$ and $c = 3$, the progression is $12, 6, 3$, with common ratio $\dfrac{1}{2}$. In both cases the sum is $21$ and the product is $3 \cdot 6 \cdot 12 = 216$.

</div>
