## Exercise 1

Given the sequence $a_n = \dfrac{3n - 1}{n + 2}$, with $n \geq 1$, calculate its first four terms, determine whether it is monotone, and prove that it is bounded.

$$
a_1 = \frac{2}{3}, \qquad a_2 = \frac{5}{4}, \qquad a_3 = \frac{8}{5}, \qquad a_4 = \frac{11}{6}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n+1} - a_n &= \frac{3n + 2}{n + 3} - \frac{3n - 1}{n + 2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(3n + 2)(n + 2) - (3n - 1)(n + 3)}{(n + 2)(n + 3)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3n^2 + 8n + 4 - \left( 3n^2 + 8n - 3 \right)}{(n + 2)(n + 3)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{7}{(n + 2)(n + 3)} > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
3 - a_n = \frac{3(n + 2) - (3n - 1)}{n + 2} = \frac{7}{n + 2} > 0
$$
$$
\frac{2}{3} \leq a_n < 3 \qquad \text{for every } n \geq 1
$$

::grafico{punti="1,0.6667,pieno;2,1.25,pieno;3,1.6,pieno;4,1.8333,pieno;5,2,pieno;6,2.125,pieno;7,2.2222,pieno;8,2.3,pieno;9,2.3636,pieno" asintoti="y=3" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=4 nomi="n,a" titolo="The terms of $a_n = \dfrac{3n - 1}{n + 2}$ increase from $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and stay below the line $y = 3$"}

The sequence is **strictly increasing** and **bounded**, with $\dfrac{2}{3} \leq a_n < 3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Substitute $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$ into the general term to obtain the first few terms. For example, $a_3 = \dfrac{9 - 1}{3 + 2} = \dfrac{8}{5}$.

To study monotonicity, calculate the difference $a_{n+1} - a_n$. To find $a_{n+1}$, write $n + 1$ in place of $n$, so the numerator becomes $3(n + 1) - 1 = 3n + 2$ and the denominator becomes $n + 3$. Write the two fractions with the common denominator $(n + 2)(n + 3)$ and expand the products in the numerator. The terms $3n^2$ and $8n$ cancel, leaving $4 + 3 = 7$. The denominator is positive for every $n \geq 1$, so the difference is positive and the sequence is **strictly increasing**.

The first term of an increasing sequence is its smallest, so $a_n \geq a_1 = \dfrac{2}{3}$ for every $n$, and the sequence is bounded below. To find an upper bound, note that the numerator $3n - 1$ is less than $3(n + 2) = 3n + 6$, and calculate the difference $3 - a_n$, which equals $\dfrac{7}{n + 2}$. This fraction is positive, so $a_n < 3$ for every $n$. Together, the two inequalities show that the sequence is **bounded**. The graph shows the points rising toward the line $y = 3$ without reaching it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the first five terms of the sequence defined recursively by
$$
\begin{cases}
a_1 = 2 \\[4pt]
a_{n+1} = 2a_n - 1
\end{cases}
$$
and verify that the general term $b_n = 2^{n-1} + 1$ satisfies the same two conditions.

$$
a_2 = 2 \cdot 2 - 1 = 3, \qquad a_3 = 2 \cdot 3 - 1 = 5, \qquad a_4 = 2 \cdot 5 - 1 = 9, \qquad a_5 = 2 \cdot 9 - 1 = 17
$$
$$
b_1 = 2^0 + 1 = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2b_n - 1 &= 2\left( 2^{n-1} + 1 \right) - 1 \\[6pt]
&= 2^n + 2 - 1 \\[6pt]
&= 2^n + 1 = b_{n+1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The first five terms are $2, 3, 5, 9, 17$, and the sequence has general term $b_n = 2^{n-1} + 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In a sequence **defined recursively**, each term is calculated from the preceding one. Start with $a_1 = 2$ and apply the rule $a_{n+1} = 2a_n - 1$ once for each new term. That is, double the term just found and subtract $1$.

The numbers $2, 3, 5, 9, 17$ are each $1$ greater than the corresponding numbers $1, 2, 4, 8, 16$, which are the powers $2^0, 2^1, 2^2, 2^3, 2^4$. This suggests the general term $b_n = 2^{n-1} + 1$, where the exponent is $n - 1$ because the first term corresponds to $2^0$.

To verify the proposed formula, check the two conditions in the system. The first holds because $b_1 = 1 + 1 = 2$. For the second, calculate $2b_n - 1$. Distribute the factor $2$ and use $2 \cdot 2^{n-1} = 2^n$, by the rule for multiplying powers with the same base. This gives $2^n + 1$, which is precisely $b_{n+1}$. The two sequences therefore have the same first term and follow the same rule, so all their terms agree. The **principle of induction** proves this rigorously, since the first condition gives the base case and the second gives the inductive step. The general term gives, for example, $a_{11} = 2^{10} + 1 = 1025$ directly, without calculating the ten preceding terms.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given the sequence $a_n = n^2 - 8n + 10$, with $n \geq 1$, study the sign of $a_{n+1} - a_n$, determine the indices for which the sequence increases and those for which it decreases, and find its minimum term.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n+1} - a_n &= (n + 1)^2 - 8(n + 1) + 10 - \left( n^2 - 8n + 10 \right) \\[6pt]
&= n^2 + 2n + 1 - 8n - 8 + 10 - n^2 + 8n - 10 \\[6pt]
&= 2n - 7
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
2n - 7 < 0 \iff n \leq 3 ,
\qquad
2n - 7 > 0 \iff n \geq 4
$$
$$
a_1 = 3, \quad a_2 = -2, \quad a_3 = -5, \quad a_4 = -6, \quad a_5 = -5, \quad a_6 = -2, \quad a_7 = 3
$$

The sequence decreases from $a_1$ to $a_4$, increases from $a_4$ onward, and has **minimum** $a_4 = -6$.

<div class="spiegazione">

To determine when the sequence increases, examine the sign of the difference between a term and its predecessor. Write $a_{n+1}$ by substituting $n + 1$ for $n$. Expand $(n + 1)^2 = n^2 + 2n + 1$, subtract the expression for $a_n$ by changing the sign of each of its terms, and combine like terms. The terms $n^2$, $8n$, and $10$ cancel, leaving $2n - 7$.

The expression $2n - 7$ vanishes at $n = \dfrac{7}{2}$, which is not a natural number. Therefore the difference is negative for $n = 1, 2, 3$ and positive for every $n \geq 4$. The inequality $a_{n+1} < a_n$ for $n \leq 3$ means that $a_1 > a_2 > a_3 > a_4$, so the sequence decreases up to $a_4$. The inequality $a_{n+1} > a_n$ for $n \geq 4$ means that from $a_4$ onward each term exceeds the preceding one, so the sequence is **eventually increasing**.

The term $a_4$ is less than every term before it and every term after it, so it is the minimum and equals $16 - 32 + 10 = -6$. The sequence is therefore bounded below by $-6$. It is unbounded above because $a_n = (n - 4)^2 - 6$ exceeds any given number for sufficiently large $n$. The same result follows by considering the parabola $y = x^2 - 8x + 10$, whose vertex lies at $x = 4$.

</div>
