## Exercise 1

In an arithmetic progression, the third term is $a_3 = 7$ and the tenth term is $a_{10} = 28$. Find the common difference, the first term, the general term, and the sum of the first $20$ terms.

$$
a_{10} = a_3 + 7d
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
28 = 7 + 7d
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
d = 3
$$
$$
a_3 = a_1 + 2d
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
7 = a_1 + 6
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
a_1 = 1
$$
$$
a_n = 1 + 3(n - 1) = 3n - 2
$$
$$
a_{20} = 3 \cdot 20 - 2 = 58,
\qquad
S_{20} = \frac{20\,(1 + 58)}{2} = 590
$$

::grafico{f="3*x-2" punti="1,1,pieno;2,4,pieno;3,7,pieno,a₃;4,10,pieno;5,13,pieno;6,16,pieno;7,19,pieno;8,22,pieno;9,25,pieno;10,28,pieno,a₁₀" xmin=0 xmax=11 ymin=-2 ymax=32 nomi="n,a" legenda="y = 3x - 2" titolo="The terms of $a_n = 3n - 2$ lie on the line with slope $d = 3$ passing through $(3, 7)$ and $(10, 28)$"}

The common difference is $d = 3$, the first term is $a_1 = 1$, the general term is $a_n = 3n - 2$, and the sum of the first $20$ terms is $S_{20} = 590$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Two nonconsecutive terms are known, so use the formula $a_n = a_k + (n - k)\,d$ for the **general term**. There are $10 - 3 = 7$ steps from the third term to the tenth, and we add $d$ at each step. Therefore $a_{10} = a_3 + 7d$, that is, $28 = 7 + 7d$, which gives $7d = 21$ and $d = 3$.

To find the first term, go back two steps from the third term. The formula $a_3 = a_1 + 2d$ gives $7 = a_1 + 6$, so $a_1 = 1$. Substituting $a_1 = 1$ and $d = 3$ into $a_n = a_1 + (n - 1)\,d$ and expanding gives $a_n = 3n - 2$. The given terms are easy to check because $a_3 = 9 - 2 = 7$ and $a_{10} = 30 - 2 = 28$.

For the sum, use the formula $S_n = \dfrac{n\,(a_1 + a_n)}{2}$ for the **sum of the first n terms**, which requires the first and last terms being added. The last term is $a_{20} = 58$, so $S_{20} = 10 \cdot 59 = 590$. The second form of the formula gives the same result, $S_{20} = \dfrac{20\,(2 \cdot 1 + 19 \cdot 3)}{2} = 10 \cdot 59 = 590$. In the graph, the points $(n, a_n)$ lie on the line $y = 3x - 2$, as they do for every arithmetic progression.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the sum of all the multiples of $7$ between $100$ and $500$.

$$
7 \cdot 14 = 98 < 100 < 105 = 7 \cdot 15,
\qquad
7 \cdot 71 = 497 \leq 500 < 504 = 7 \cdot 72
$$
$$
a_1 = 105, \qquad a_n = 497, \qquad d = 7
$$
$$
n = 71 - 15 + 1 = 57
$$
$$
S_{57} = \frac{57\,(105 + 497)}{2} = \frac{57 \cdot 602}{2} = 57 \cdot 301 = 17157
$$

The sum of the multiples of $7$ between $100$ and $500$ is $17157$.

<div class="spiegazione">

When the multiples of $7$ are listed in increasing order, consecutive multiples differ by $7$, so they form an **arithmetic progression** with common difference $d = 7$. To use the sum formula, we need the first term, the last term, and the number of terms.

To find the first multiple of $7$ greater than $100$, divide $100$ by $7$. The integer quotient is $14$, and $7 \cdot 14 = 98$ is still less than $100$. Therefore the first multiple is $7 \cdot 15 = 105$. Similarly, dividing $500$ by $7$ gives integer quotient $71$, so the last multiple no greater than $500$ is $7 \cdot 71 = 497$.

The desired multiples are $7 \cdot 15, 7 \cdot 16, \ldots, 7 \cdot 71$, so there are as many of them as there are integers from $15$ to $71$. Their number is $71 - 15 + 1 = 57$, where we add $1$ because both endpoints are counted. The general term gives the same count because $497 = 105 + (n - 1) \cdot 7$ yields $n - 1 = 56$.

Apply the formula $S_n = \dfrac{n\,(a_1 + a_n)}{2}$ with $n = 57$. The sum of the first and last terms is $602$, which is even, so first divide it by $2$ to obtain $57 \cdot 301 = 17157$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In an arithmetic progression, the first term is $5$ and the common difference is $4$. How many terms, starting with the first, must be added to obtain $860$?

$$
S_n = \frac{n\,\big( 2 \cdot 5 + (n - 1) \cdot 4 \big)}{2} = \frac{n\,(4n + 6)}{2} = 2n^2 + 3n
$$
$$
2n^2 + 3n = 860
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
2n^2 + 3n - 860 = 0
$$
$$
\Delta = 3^2 + 4 \cdot 2 \cdot 860 = 9 + 6880 = 6889 = 83^2
$$
$$
n = \frac{-3 \pm 83}{4}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
n = 20 \quad \text{or} \quad n = -\frac{43}{2}
$$

The first $20$ terms must be added.

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown is the number $n$ of terms, and the given values are $a_1 = 5$ and $d = 4$. For this reason, use the second formula for the **sum of the first n terms**, $S_n = \dfrac{n\,\big( 2a_1 + (n - 1)\,d \big)}{2}$, which does not involve the last term. Expanding $10 + 4n - 4 = 4n + 6$ and dividing by $2$ gives $S_n = n\,(2n + 3) = 2n^2 + 3n$.

Setting the sum equal to $860$ gives a quadratic equation in $n$. The discriminant is $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ with $a = 2$, $b = 3$, and $c = -860$, so $\Delta = 9 + 6880 = 6889$. The number $6889$ is the square of $83$, and the two solutions are $\dfrac{-3 + 83}{4} = 20$ and $\dfrac{-3 - 83}{4} = -\dfrac{43}{2}$.

The number of terms must be a natural number, so discard the negative, noninteger solution and take $n = 20$. The first form of the sum formula confirms the result because the last term is $a_{20} = 5 + 19 \cdot 4 = 81$ and $S_{20} = \dfrac{20\,(5 + 81)}{2} = 10 \cdot 86 = 860$. If the discriminant were not a perfect square, no sum of consecutive terms starting with the first would give exactly the required value.

</div>
