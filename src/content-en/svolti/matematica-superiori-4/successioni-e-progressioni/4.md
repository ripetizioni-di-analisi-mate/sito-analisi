## Exercise 1

In a geometric progression, $a_2 = 6$ and $a_5 = 162$. Find the common ratio, the first term, and the sum of the first $6$ terms.

$$
a_5 = a_2 \cdot q^3
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
162 = 6\,q^3
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q^3 = 27
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q = 3
$$
$$
a_1 = \frac{a_2}{q} = \frac{6}{3} = 2
$$
$$
S_6 = 2 \cdot \frac{1 - 3^6}{1 - 3} = 2 \cdot \frac{1 - 729}{-2} = 728
$$

::grafico{punti="1,2,pieno;2,6,pieno,a₂;3,18,pieno;4,54,pieno;5,162,pieno,a₅" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=-20 ymax=180 nomi="n,a" titolo="The first five terms of the geometric progression with $a_1 = 2$ and $q = 3$. Each term is three times the preceding one"}

The common ratio is $q = 3$, the first term is $a_1 = 2$, and the sum of the first $6$ terms is $S_6 = 728$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two known terms are nonconsecutive, so use the formula $a_n = a_k\,q^{n-k}$ for the **general term**. There are $5 - 2 = 3$ steps from the second term to the fifth, and we multiply by $q$ at each step, so $a_5 = a_2\,q^3$. Dividing $162$ by $6$ gives $q^3 = 27$. The exponent $3$ is odd, so the equation has only one real solution, $q = \sqrt[3]{27} = 3$.

The first term is one step before the second, so divide $a_2$ by the common ratio to obtain $a_1 = 2$. The progression is $2, 6, 18, 54, 162, 486, \ldots$, and the known terms agree with the given values.

For the **sum**, apply the formula $S_n = a_1 \cdot \dfrac{1 - q^n}{1 - q}$, which holds because $q \neq 1$. With $q = 3$ and $n = 6$, the numerator is $1 - 729 = -728$ and the denominator is $-2$, so the fraction equals $364$ and the sum is $728$. Adding the six terms confirms the result because $2 + 6 + 18 + 54 + 162 + 486 = 728$. The graph shows how fast the terms grow, as is typical of geometric progressions with common ratio greater than $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A geometric progression has first term $a_1 = 48$ and common ratio $q = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Calculate the sixth term and the sum of the first six terms.

$$
a_6 = 48 \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right)^5 = 48 \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{32} \right) = -\frac{3}{2}
$$
$$
\left( -\frac{1}{2} \right)^6 = \frac{1}{64}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_6 &= 48 \cdot \frac{1 - \frac{1}{64}}{1 + \frac{1}{2}} \\[6pt]
&= 48 \cdot \frac{63}{64} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{63}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The sixth term is $a_6 = -\dfrac{3}{2}$ and the sum of the first six terms is $S_6 = \dfrac{63}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

For the sixth term, use $a_n = a_1\,q^{n-1}$ with $n = 6$, so the exponent is $5$. An odd power of a negative number is negative, so $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right)^5 = -\dfrac{1}{32}$. Multiplying by $48$ and simplifying $\dfrac{48}{32} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ gives $a_6 = -\dfrac{3}{2}$. The terms of the progression are $48, -24, 12, -6, 3, -\dfrac{3}{2}$ and alternate in sign because the common ratio is negative.

The common ratio is different from $1$, so the formula for the **sum** applies. The numerator contains $q^6$, which has an even exponent, so the power is positive and equals $\dfrac{1}{64}$, and $1 - \dfrac{1}{64} = \dfrac{63}{64}$. The denominator is $1 - q = 1 + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$, and dividing by $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Canceling common factors in $48$ and $64$ gives $\dfrac{3}{4}$, and then $\dfrac{3}{4} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Therefore $S_6 = \dfrac{63}{2}$.

Adding the six terms directly confirms the result because $48 - 24 + 12 - 6 + 3 - \dfrac{3}{2} = 33 - \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{63}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a geometric progression, $a_3 = 12$ and $a_7 = 192$. Find the possible common ratios and, for each one, write down the first five terms and their sum.

$$
a_7 = a_3\,q^4
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q^4 = \frac{192}{12} = 16
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
q = 2 \quad \text{or} \quad q = -2
$$
$$
a_1 = \frac{a_3}{q^2} = \frac{12}{4} = 3
$$
$$
q = 2: \qquad 3, \ 6, \ 12, \ 24, \ 48,
\qquad
S_5 = 3 \cdot \frac{1 - 32}{1 - 2} = 93
$$
$$
q = -2: \qquad 3, \ -6, \ 12, \ -24, \ 48,
\qquad
S_5 = 3 \cdot \frac{1 + 32}{1 + 2} = 33
$$

There are **two** progressions. With $q = 2$ the sum of the first five terms is $93$, and with $q = -2$ it is $33$.

<div class="spiegazione">

There are $4$ steps from the third term to the seventh, so the formula for the **general term** gives $a_7 = a_3\,q^4$ and $q^4 = 16$. The exponent is even, so the equation has two real solutions that are opposites, $q = 2$ and $q = -2$, since $2^4 = (-2)^4 = 16$. The given information does not say whether the terms are positive, so both common ratios are possible.

The first term is two steps before the third, so $a_1 = \dfrac{a_3}{q^2}$. In both cases $q^2 = 4$, so $a_1 = 3$ for both progressions. The third, fifth, and seventh terms also agree because they contain even powers of $q$. The even-indexed terms, however, have opposite signs.

For the **sum**, apply $S_5 = a_1 \cdot \dfrac{1 - q^5}{1 - q}$. With $q = 2$ we have $q^5 = 32$, so $S_5 = 3 \cdot \dfrac{-31}{-1} = 93$. With $q = -2$ we have $q^5 = -32$ because the exponent is odd, so the numerator is $1 + 32 = 33$ and the denominator is $1 + 2 = 3$, giving $S_5 = 33$. Adding the terms directly gives $3 + 6 + 12 + 24 + 48 = 93$ and $3 - 6 + 12 - 24 + 48 = 33$, confirming the results.

</div>
