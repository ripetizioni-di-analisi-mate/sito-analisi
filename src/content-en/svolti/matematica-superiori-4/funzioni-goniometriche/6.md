## Exercise 1

Given the function $y = 3\sin(2x)$, determine its amplitude, period, and range, find its zeros, maxima, and minima, and draw its graph.

$$
A = 3 ,
\qquad
\omega = 2 ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi}{2} = \pi ,
\qquad
\text{range } [-3, 3]
$$
$$
3\sin(2x) = 0
\iff
2x = k\pi
\iff
x = k\,\frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
\text{maxima: } 2x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
\iff
x = \frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi ,
\qquad
y = 3
$$
$$
\text{minima: } 2x = \frac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
\iff
x = \frac{3\pi}{4} + k\pi ,
\qquad
y = -3
$$

::grafico{f="3*sin(2*x)" punti="0.785,3,pieno;2.356,-3,pieno;1.571,0,pieno;3.1416,0,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 legenda="y = 3\sin(2x)" titolo="The function $y = 3\sin(2x)$ has period $\pi$, a maximum of $3$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, a zero at $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and a minimum of $-3$ at $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$"}

The function has amplitude $3$, period $\pi$, and range $[-3, 3]$. It is zero at $x = k\,\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, reaches its maximum $3$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$, and reaches its minimum $-3$ at $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function has the form $y = A\sin(\omega x + \varphi) + b$ with $A = 3$, $\omega = 2$, $\varphi = 0$, and $b = 0$. By the theorem on **sinusoids**, its period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} = \pi$ and its range is $[b - A, b + A] = [-3, 3]$.

To find the zeros, set the sine equal to $0$ because the factor $3$ differs from $0$. Sine is zero when its argument is a multiple of $\pi$, so $2x = k\pi$, and dividing by $2$ gives $x = k\,\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

The maximum of $\sin t$ is $1$ and occurs at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. With $t = 2x$ this gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$, and at those points $y = 3 \cdot 1 = 3$. Similarly, the minimum $-1$ of sine occurs at $t = \dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, giving $x = \dfrac{3\pi}{4} + k\pi$ and $y = -3$. Dividing by $2$ also changes the term $2k\pi$ into $k\pi$, as expected from the period $\pi$.

To draw the graph, sketch one period from $0$ to $\pi$. The graph starts at $(0, 0)$, rises to the maximum at $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, returns to $0$ at $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, falls to the minimum at $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, and returns to $0$ at $\pi$. The same section then repeats.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given the function $y = 2\cos\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) + 1$, determine the period, range, and phase shift, find its intersection with the $y$-axis and its maximum and minimum points, and draw its graph.

$$
A = 2 ,
\qquad
T = 2\pi ,
\qquad
\text{range } [1 - 2, 1 + 2] = [-1, 3]
$$
$$
y(0) = 2\cos\left( -\frac{\pi}{3} \right) + 1 = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + 1 = 2
$$
$$
\text{maxima: } x - \frac{\pi}{3} = 2k\pi
\iff
x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad
y = 3
$$
$$
\text{minima: } x - \frac{\pi}{3} = \pi + 2k\pi
\iff
x = \frac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad
y = -1
$$

::grafico{f="2*cos(x-pi/3)+1;2*cos(x)+1" punti="1.047,3,pieno;4.189,-1,pieno;0,2,pieno" segmenti="-4,1,7,1" xmin=-4 xmax=7 ymin=-2 ymax=4 legenda="y = 2\cos\left(x - \frac{\pi}{3}\right) + 1;y = 2\cos x + 1" titolo="The graph of $y = 2\cos\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) + 1$ is the graph of $y = 2\cos x + 1$ shifted $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to the right"}

The function has period $2\pi$, range $[-1, 3]$, and phase shift $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The graph intersects the $y$-axis at $(0, 2)$, reaches its maximum $3$ at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$, and reaches its minimum $-1$ at $x = \dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $x$ is $1$, so the period is the same as that of cosine, $2\pi$. The amplitude is $2$ and the function oscillates about the line $y = 1$, so its range is $[-1, 3]$. Cosine has argument $x - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so the graph is that of $y = 2\cos x + 1$ shifted $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to the **right**, as the figure shows.

The intersection with the $y$-axis is obtained by setting $x = 0$. Cosine is even, so $\cos\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and $y(0) = 2$.

Cosine has maximum $1$ when its argument is a multiple of $2\pi$ and minimum $-1$ when its argument is $\pi + 2k\pi$. Apply these conditions to the argument $x - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and add $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ to both sides. This gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ for the maximum and $x = \pi + \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ for the minimum. The corresponding values are $2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3$ and $2 \cdot (-1) + 1 = -1$, the endpoints of the range.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the numbers $A > 0$, $\omega > 0$, and $b$ for which the function $y = A\sin(\omega x) + b$ has range $[-1, 5]$ and period $4\pi$. For the resulting function, find $y\left( \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$ and the first maximum point with positive horizontal coordinate.

$$
\begin{cases}
b - A = -1 \\[4pt]
b + A = 5
\end{cases}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
b = \frac{-1 + 5}{2} = 2 ,
\qquad
A = \frac{5 - (-1)}{2} = 3
$$
$$
\frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 4\pi
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{4\pi} = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
y = 3\sin\frac{x}{2} + 2
$$
$$
y\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = 3\sin\frac{\pi}{6} + 2 = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + 2 = \frac{7}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{x}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
x = \pi ,
\qquad
y(\pi) = 3 + 2 = 5
$$

::grafico{f="3*sin(x/2)+2" punti="3.1416,5,pieno;9.425,-1,pieno;1.047,3.5,pieno" segmenti="-2,2,13,2" xmin=-2 xmax=13 ymin=-2 ymax=6 legenda="y = 3\sin\frac{x}{2} + 2" titolo="The function $y = 3\sin\dfrac{x}{2} + 2$ has period $4\pi$, oscillates between $-1$ and $5$, and reaches its first maximum to the right of the origin at $x = \pi$"}

The function is $y = 3\sin\dfrac{x}{2} + 2$. We have $y\left( \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = \dfrac{7}{2}$, and the first maximum point with positive horizontal coordinate is $(\pi, 5)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on sinusoids, when $A > 0$ the range of $y = A\sin(\omega x) + b$ is $[b - A, b + A]$. Setting this interval equal to $[-1, 5]$ gives two conditions that must hold simultaneously, hence a system of equations. Adding the equations gives $2b = 4$, and subtracting the first from the second gives $2A = 6$. In other words, $b$ is the midpoint between the minimum and maximum, and $A$ is half the distance between them.

The period is $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$. Setting it equal to $4\pi$ gives $\omega = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which is positive as required. Writing $\omega x$ with $\omega = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is equivalent to writing $\dfrac{x}{2}$.

Substituting $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ gives the sine argument $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, whose sine is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The result is $\dfrac{3}{2} + 2 = \dfrac{7}{2}$.

Sine equals $1$ when its argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, so maxima occur when $\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, that is, at $x = \pi + 4k\pi$. The smallest positive value occurs for $k = 0$, so the maximum point is $(\pi, 5)$.

</div>
