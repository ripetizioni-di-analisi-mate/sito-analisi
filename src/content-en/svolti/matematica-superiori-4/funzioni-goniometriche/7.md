## Exercise 1

Evaluate the expression
$$
E = \arcsin\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) + \arccos\left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) + \arctan\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} .
$$

$$
\arcsin\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = -\frac{\pi}{3} ,
\qquad
\text{because } \sin\left( -\frac{\pi}{3} \right) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \text{ and } -\frac{\pi}{3} \in \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right]
$$
$$
\arccos\left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) = \frac{3\pi}{4} ,
\qquad
\text{because } \cos\frac{3\pi}{4} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \text{ and } \frac{3\pi}{4} \in [0, \pi]
$$
$$
\arctan\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{\pi}{6} ,
\qquad
\text{because } \tan\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \text{ and } \frac{\pi}{6} \in \left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right)
$$
$$
E = -\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{3\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{-4\pi + 9\pi + 2\pi}{12} = \frac{7\pi}{12}
$$

The expression equals $\dfrac{7\pi}{12}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The **arcsine** of a number $x$ is the unique angle in the interval $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ whose sine is $x$. The sine of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and sine is odd, so $\sin\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The angle $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ belongs to the interval and is therefore the required arcsine.

The **arccosine** is the unique angle in $[0, \pi]$ with the given cosine. The cosine is negative, so the angle lies in the second quadrant. By the supplementary-angle identity, $\cos\left( \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = -\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and the angle $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ belongs to $[0, \pi]$. Equivalently, $\arccos(-x) = \pi - \arccos x$.

The **arctangent** is the unique angle in $\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ with the given tangent, and the special-angle table gives $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ as the tangent of $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

To add the three angles, use the common denominator $12$ and write $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{4\pi}{12}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{9\pi}{12}$, and $\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{2\pi}{12}$. The sum of the numerators is $7\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given the function $f(x) = 2\arcsin(2x - 1)$, determine its domain and range, calculate $f\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)$, and find the value of $x$ for which $f(x) = 0$.

$$
-1 \leq 2x - 1 \leq 1
\iff
0 \leq 2x \leq 2
\iff
0 \leq x \leq 1
\qquad
D = [0, 1]
$$
$$
-\frac{\pi}{2} \leq \arcsin(2x - 1) \leq \frac{\pi}{2}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
-\pi \leq f(x) \leq \pi
$$
$$
f\left( \frac{3}{4} \right) = 2\arcsin\left( \frac{3}{2} - 1 \right) = 2\arcsin\frac{1}{2} = 2 \cdot \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
f(x) = 0
\iff
\arcsin(2x - 1) = 0
\iff
2x - 1 = 0
\iff
x = \frac{1}{2}
$$

::grafico{f="2*asin(2*x-1)" punti="0,-3.1416,pieno;1,3.1416,pieno;0.75,1.047,pieno;0.5,0,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=2 ymin=-4 ymax=4 legenda="y = 2\arcsin(2x - 1)" titolo="The function $f(x) = 2\arcsin(2x - 1)$ is defined on $[0, 1]$, has range $[-\pi, \pi]$, and is zero at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$"}

The domain is $[0, 1]$ and the range is $[-\pi, \pi]$. We have $f\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right) = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and the function is zero at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Arcsine is defined only for arguments between $-1$ and $1$, so the argument $2x - 1$ must satisfy the double inequality $-1 \leq 2x - 1 \leq 1$. Adding $1$ throughout and then dividing by $2$, which is positive, gives $0 \leq x \leq 1$.

As $x$ runs through $[0, 1]$, the argument $2x - 1$ runs through the entire interval $[-1, 1]$, so the arcsine takes all values between $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Multiplying by $2$ gives the range $[-\pi, \pi]$. The endpoints are attained at $x = 0$, where $f(0) = 2\arcsin(-1) = -\pi$, and at $x = 1$, where $f(1) = 2\arcsin 1 = \pi$.

At $x = \dfrac{3}{4}$ the argument is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and $\arcsin\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, because $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ is the angle in $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$ whose sine is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Arcsine is increasing, hence one-to-one, and is zero only for the argument $0$, since $\sin 0 = 0$ and $0$ belongs to $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$. Therefore $f(x) = 0$ only when $2x - 1 = 0$, that is, at $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which belongs to the domain.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $\arccos\left( \cos\dfrac{7\pi}{6} \right)$, $\sin\left( \arccos\left( -\dfrac{1}{3} \right) \right)$, and $\tan\left( \arcsin\left( -\dfrac{3}{5} \right) \right)$.

$$
\arccos\left( \cos\frac{7\pi}{6} \right) = \arccos\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = \frac{5\pi}{6}
$$
$$
\beta = \arccos\left( -\frac{1}{3} \right) \in [0, \pi]
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\sin\beta \geq 0
$$
$$
\sin\beta = \sqrt{1 - \cos^2\beta} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{9}} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
$$
$$
\gamma = \arcsin\left( -\frac{3}{5} \right) \in \left[ -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right]
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\cos\gamma \geq 0
$$
$$
\cos\gamma = \sqrt{1 - \frac{9}{25}} = \frac{4}{5} ,
\qquad
\tan\gamma = \frac{-\frac{3}{5}}{\frac{4}{5}} = -\frac{3}{4}
$$

The three values are $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$, and $-\dfrac{3}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ does not belong to $[0, \pi]$, so the arccosine of its cosine is a different angle. The identity for angles that differ by $\pi$ gives $\cos\dfrac{7\pi}{6} = -\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. We then seek the angle in $[0, \pi]$ with this cosine. By the supplementary-angle identity, it is $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

In the second calculation, set $\beta = \arccos\left( -\dfrac{1}{3} \right)$. By definition, $\cos\beta = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\beta \in [0, \pi]$. On this interval the corresponding point lies in the half-plane $y \geq 0$, so the sine is nonnegative. By the **first Pythagorean identity**, $\sin^2\beta = \dfrac{8}{9}$, and we choose the positive root $\dfrac{\sqrt{8}}{3} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$.

In the third calculation, $\gamma$ is an angle in $\left[ -\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, where cosine is nonnegative, with $\sin\gamma = -\dfrac{3}{5}$. The first Pythagorean identity gives $\cos\gamma = \dfrac{4}{5}$, which differs from $0$. Therefore the tangent is defined and equals $-\dfrac{3}{4}$. The sign is negative because $\gamma$ is a fourth-quadrant angle.

</div>
