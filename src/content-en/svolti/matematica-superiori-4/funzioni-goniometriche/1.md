## Exercise 1

Express the angles $210^\circ$ and $72^\circ$ in radians, and express the angles $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$ and $2$ radians in degrees.

$$
210^\circ \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{\pi}{180} \cdot 210 = \frac{7\pi}{6}
$$
$$
72^\circ \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{\pi}{180} \cdot 72 = \frac{2\pi}{5}
$$
$$
\frac{7\pi}{4} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{180}{\pi} \cdot \frac{7\pi}{4} = 315^\circ
$$
$$
2 \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{180}{\pi} \cdot 2 = \frac{360^\circ}{\pi} \approx 114.6^\circ
$$

The required measures are $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$ radians, and $315^\circ$ and approximately $114.6^\circ$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Degree and radian measures are proportional, and a straight angle measures $180^\circ$ or $\pi$ radians. We therefore apply the **conversion formula**. To convert from degrees to radians, multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$. To convert from radians to degrees, multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$.

For $210^\circ$, simplify the fraction $\dfrac{210}{180}$ by dividing its numerator and denominator by $30$ to obtain $\dfrac{7}{6}$. Thus $210^\circ$ corresponds to $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$. For $72^\circ$, divide by $36$ because $72 = 2 \cdot 36$ and $180 = 5 \cdot 36$, giving $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$.

In the third calculation the factor $\pi$ appears in both the numerator and denominator, so it cancels, leaving $\dfrac{180 \cdot 7}{4} = 45 \cdot 7 = 315$. In the last calculation the measure $2$ does not contain $\pi$, so the factor $\pi$ remains in the denominator. The exact result is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{\pi}$, and its approximate value is obtained by dividing $360$ by $3.1416$.

An angle of $2$ radians is slightly larger than a right angle because a right angle measures $\dfrac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.57$ radians. The result $114.6^\circ$ is **consistent** with this observation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a circle of radius $9\ \text{cm}$, a central angle measures $140^\circ$. Find the length of the corresponding arc and the area of its sector.

$$
\alpha = \frac{\pi}{180} \cdot 140 = \frac{7\pi}{9}
$$
$$
s = r\,\alpha = 9 \cdot \frac{7\pi}{9} = 7\pi\ \text{cm} \approx 22.0\ \text{cm}
$$
$$
A = \frac{1}{2}\,r^2 \alpha = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 81 \cdot \frac{7\pi}{9} = \frac{63\pi}{2}\ \text{cm}^2 \approx 99.0\ \text{cm}^2
$$

::grafico{curve="9*cos(t),9*sin(t),0,6.29;9*cos(t),9*sin(t),0,2.443" vettori="0,0,9,0;0,0,-6.894,5.785" etichette="4,-1.2,r = 9;1.2,1.2,140°" xmin=-12 xmax=12 ymin=-12 ymax=12 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="For a central angle of $140^\circ$, or $\dfrac{7\pi}{9}$, in a circle of radius $9$, the intercepted arc has length $7\pi$"}

The arc has length $7\pi\ \text{cm}$ and the sector has area $\dfrac{63\pi}{2}\ \text{cm}^2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The formulas $s = r\alpha$ and $A = \dfrac{1}{2}\,r^2\alpha$ apply when the angle is measured in radians, so the first step is the conversion. Dividing $140$ and $180$ by $20$ gives $\dfrac{140}{180} = \dfrac{7}{9}$, hence $\alpha = \dfrac{7\pi}{9}$.

In the arc length, the radius $9$ cancels with the denominator $9$, leaving $7\pi$. Multiplying $7$ by $3.1416$ gives approximately $22.0\ \text{cm}$.

In the sector area formula, square the radius to obtain $9^2 = 81$, then cancel $81$ with the denominator $9$ to obtain $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 9 \cdot 7\pi = \dfrac{63\pi}{2}$. The area is measured in square centimeters because the formula contains the square of the radius.

As a check, the sector is a fraction $\dfrac{140}{360} = \dfrac{7}{18}$ of the disk, whose area is $81\pi$. Indeed, $81\pi \cdot \dfrac{7}{18} = \dfrac{567\pi}{18} = \dfrac{63\pi}{2}$, and this **agrees** with the result obtained.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular sector has perimeter $30\ \text{cm}$ and area $50\ \text{cm}^2$. Find its radius and its central angle in radians.

$$
\begin{cases}
2r + r\alpha = 30 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,r^2 \alpha = 50
\end{cases}
\qquad r > 0 ,\quad 0 < \alpha \leq 2\pi
$$
$$
r\alpha = 30 - 2r
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,r\,(30 - 2r) = 50
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
15r - r^2 = 50
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
r^2 - 15r + 50 = 0
$$
$$
r = \frac{15 \pm \sqrt{225 - 200}}{2} = \frac{15 \pm 5}{2}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
r = 5 \quad \text{or} \quad r = 10
$$
$$
r = 5 : \quad \alpha = \frac{30 - 10}{5} = 4 ,
\qquad
r = 10 : \quad \alpha = \frac{30 - 20}{10} = 1
$$

The problem has two solutions, the sector of radius $5\ \text{cm}$ and angle $4$ radians, and the sector of radius $10\ \text{cm}$ and angle $1$ radian.

<div class="spiegazione">

The boundary of a circular sector consists of two radii and an arc, so the perimeter is $2r + s$, and the arc length formula gives $s = r\alpha$. The area is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,r^2\alpha$. Both conditions must hold, so we write a **system of equations** in the unknowns $r$ and $\alpha$.

The first equation gives the product $r\alpha = 30 - 2r$. In the second, write $r^2\alpha = r \cdot (r\alpha)$ and substitute for this product, leaving an equation in the single unknown $r$. Expanding $\dfrac{1}{2}\,r\,(30 - 2r) = 15r - r^2$ and moving all the terms to the left gives a quadratic equation with discriminant $225 - 200 = 25$.

The two roots $r = 10$ and $r = 5$ are positive. For each root, find the angle using $\alpha = \dfrac{30 - 2r}{r}$. The angle of $4$ radians is less than $2\pi \approx 6.28$, and the angle of $1$ radian is positive, so both pairs satisfy the conditions $r > 0$ and $0 < \alpha \leq 2\pi$.

As a check, with $r = 5$ and $\alpha = 4$ the perimeter is $10 + 20 = 30$ and the area is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 25 \cdot 4 = 50$. With $r = 10$ and $\alpha = 1$ the perimeter is $20 + 10 = 30$ and the area is $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot 1 = 50$. Therefore the problem has **two solutions**.

</div>
