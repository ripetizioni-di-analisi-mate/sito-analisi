## Exercise 1

Given that $\sin\alpha = -\dfrac{8}{17}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} < \alpha < 2\pi$, find $\cos\alpha$, $\tan\alpha$, and $\cot\alpha$.

$$
\cos^2\alpha = 1 - \sin^2\alpha = 1 - \frac{64}{289} = \frac{225}{289}
$$
$$
\cos\alpha > 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\cos\alpha = \sqrt{\frac{225}{289}} = \frac{15}{17}
$$
$$
\tan\alpha = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha} = \frac{-\frac{8}{17}}{\frac{15}{17}} = -\frac{8}{15} ,
\qquad
\cot\alpha = \frac{1}{\tan\alpha} = -\frac{15}{8}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="0,0,0.882,-0.471" segmenti="0.882,0,0.882,-0.471" punti="0.882,-0.471,pieno,P" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The point $P = \left( \dfrac{15}{17}, -\dfrac{8}{17} \right)$ corresponding to $\alpha$ lies in the fourth quadrant"}

This gives $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{15}{17}$, $\tan\alpha = -\dfrac{8}{15}$, and $\cot\alpha = -\dfrac{15}{8}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The **first Pythagorean identity** $\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1$ holds for every angle, so the square of the cosine is obtained by subtracting from $1$ the square of the sine. The square of $-\dfrac{8}{17}$ is $\dfrac{64}{289}$ because the square of a negative number is positive, and $1 - \dfrac{64}{289} = \dfrac{289 - 64}{289} = \dfrac{225}{289}$.

The equation $\cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{225}{289}$ has two solutions, $\dfrac{15}{17}$ and $-\dfrac{15}{17}$. The angle lies between $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ and $2\pi$, so the corresponding point $P$ is in the fourth quadrant, where the horizontal coordinate is positive. Therefore $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{15}{17}$.

Tangent is the ratio of sine to cosine, and the cosine differs from $0$, so the tangent is defined. In the quotient of the two fractions, the denominator $17$ cancels, leaving $-\dfrac{8}{15}$. The tangent is **negative**, as expected since sine and cosine have opposite signs in the fourth quadrant. Cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent because both sine and cosine differ from $0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given that $\tan\alpha = -3$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2} < \alpha < \pi$, find $\cos\alpha$, $\sin\alpha$, and $\cot\alpha$.

$$
\frac{1}{\cos^2\alpha} = 1 + \tan^2\alpha = 1 + 9 = 10
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\cos^2\alpha = \frac{1}{10}
$$
$$
\cos\alpha < 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\cos\alpha = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}} = -\frac{\sqrt{10}}{10}
$$
$$
\sin\alpha = \tan\alpha \cdot \cos\alpha = (-3) \cdot \left( -\frac{\sqrt{10}}{10} \right) = \frac{3\sqrt{10}}{10}
$$
$$
\cot\alpha = \frac{1}{\tan\alpha} = -\frac{1}{3}
$$

This gives $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{10}$, $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{3\sqrt{10}}{10}$, and $\cot\alpha = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The given value is the tangent, so we use the **second Pythagorean identity** $1 + \tan^2\alpha = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2\alpha}$, which holds for angles with $\cos\alpha \neq 0$. The angle lies in the second quadrant, where cosine differs from $0$, so the identity applies. With $\tan^2\alpha = 9$ we obtain $\dfrac{1}{\cos^2\alpha} = 10$, and taking reciprocals gives $\cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{1}{10}$.

In the second quadrant the horizontal coordinate of $P$ is negative, so choose the negative root. To write the result with a rational denominator, multiply the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$ by $\sqrt{10}$ to obtain $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{10}$.

From the definition $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}$ it follows that $\sin\alpha = \tan\alpha \cdot \cos\alpha$. The product of two negative numbers is positive, and indeed sine is positive in the second quadrant. As a check, $\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = \dfrac{90}{100} + \dfrac{10}{100} = 1$. Finally, cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent, that is, $-\dfrac{1}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given that $\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\pi < \alpha < \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, evaluate the expression
$$
E = \frac{\sin\alpha + \tan\alpha}{1 + \cos\alpha} .
$$

$$
\sin^2\alpha = 1 - \frac{1}{9} = \frac{8}{9} ,
\qquad
\sin\alpha < 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\sin\alpha = -\frac{\sqrt{8}}{3} = -\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
$$
$$
\tan\alpha = \frac{-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}}{-\frac{1}{3}} = 2\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= \frac{-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} + 2\sqrt{2}}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\frac{-2\sqrt{2} + 6\sqrt{2}}{3}}{\frac{2}{3}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \\[6pt]
&= 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The expression equals $2\sqrt{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

To evaluate $E$ we need the sine and tangent of $\alpha$. By the first Pythagorean identity, $\sin^2\alpha = 1 - \cos^2\alpha = 1 - \dfrac{1}{9} = \dfrac{8}{9}$. The angle lies in the third quadrant, where the vertical coordinate of $P$ is negative, so choose the negative root. Since $\sqrt{8} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 2} = 2\sqrt{2}$, we have $\sin\alpha = -\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$.

The tangent is the quotient of two negative numbers, so it is positive, as expected in the third quadrant. The factor $\dfrac{1}{3}$ appears in both the numerator and denominator and cancels, leaving $2\sqrt{2}$.

In the expression $E$ the numerator is $-\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} + 2\sqrt{2}$. Writing $2\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{6\sqrt{2}}{3}$ allows us to combine like terms and obtain $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}$. The denominator is $1 - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}$, which differs from $0$, so the expression is defined. Dividing by $\dfrac{2}{3}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $\dfrac{3}{2}$, and the product $\dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{2}$ equals $2\sqrt{2}$.

</div>
