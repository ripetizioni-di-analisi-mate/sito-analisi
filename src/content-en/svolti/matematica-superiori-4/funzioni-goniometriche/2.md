## Exercise 1

Evaluate the expression
$$
E = 4\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\cos\frac{\pi}{3} - \tan\frac{\pi}{4} + 2\cos^2\frac{\pi}{6} .
$$

$$
\sin\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{2} ,
\qquad
\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{2} ,
\qquad
\tan\frac{\pi}{4} = 1 ,
\qquad
\cos\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} - 1 + 2 \left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)^2 \\[6pt]
&= 1 - 1 + 2 \cdot \frac{3}{4} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The expression equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

All the angles that occur are special angles in the first quadrant, so their values are given in the **special-angle table**. The sine of $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and the cosine of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ both equal $\dfrac{1}{2}$ because the angles are complementary. The tangent of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ equals $1$ because sine and cosine are equal at this angle.

The notation $\cos^2\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ denotes the square of the cosine, so we square $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The square of $\sqrt{3}$ is $3$ and the square of $2$ is $4$, hence $\cos^2\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

Now perform the multiplications before adding and subtracting the terms. The first term is $4 \cdot \dfrac{1}{4} = 1$ and the last is $2 \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. The sum of the first two terms is $1 - 1 = 0$, so the expression equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate the expression
$$
E = \frac{\sin\frac{\pi}{3} - \cos\frac{\pi}{3}}{\sin\frac{\pi}{3} + \cos\frac{\pi}{3}}
$$
and write it with a rational denominator.

$$
E = \frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{1}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{1}{2}} = \frac{\frac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{\sqrt{3} + 1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= \frac{\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)}{\left( \sqrt{3} + 1 \right)\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3 - 2\sqrt{3} + 1}{3 - 1} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4 - 2\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[6pt]
&= 2 - \sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

The expression equals $2 - \sqrt{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The special-angle table gives $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The numerator and denominator of the fraction therefore both have denominator $2$ and can be written as $\dfrac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2}$. In the quotient of two fractions with the same denominator, the factor $2$ cancels.

The denominator $\sqrt{3} + 1$ is irrational. To **rationalize** it, multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3} - 1$, the factor that gives a difference of squares. This yields $\left( \sqrt{3} \right)^2 - 1^2 = 2$ in the denominator and the square of the binomial $\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)^2 = 3 - 2\sqrt{3} + 1 = 4 - 2\sqrt{3}$ in the numerator.

Finally, divide each term of the numerator by $2$. The result $2 - \sqrt{3}$ is approximately $0.27$ and is positive, as expected since $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} > \cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and the original fraction therefore has a positive numerator.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given the function $f(x) = 2\sin x + \cos(2x)$, find $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right)$, $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right)$, and $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$.

$$
f\left( \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = 2\sin\frac{\pi}{6} + \cos\frac{\pi}{3} = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}
$$
$$
f\left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = 2\sin\frac{\pi}{4} + \cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} + 0 = \sqrt{2}
$$
$$
f\left( \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = 2\sin\frac{\pi}{2} + \cos\pi = 2 \cdot 1 + (-1) = 1
$$

::grafico{f="2*sin(x)+cos(2*x)" punti="0.524,1.5,pieno;0.785,1.414,pieno;1.571,1,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=3 legenda="y = 2\sin x + \cos(2x)" titolo="The graph of $f(x) = 2\sin x + \cos(2x)$ passes through the points $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{3}{2} \right)$, $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4}, \sqrt{2} \right)$, and $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}, 1 \right)$"}

The three values are $\dfrac{3}{2}$, $\sqrt{2}$, and $1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

To evaluate $f$ at a point, replace $x$ with the given value at *every* occurrence. In the second term, $x$ is multiplied by $2$, so the angle in the cosine is twice the given value. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ the angle is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ it is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ it is $\pi$.

The sine and cosine values at $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ are given in the special-angle table. The angles $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pi$ correspond to the points $(0, 1)$ and $(-1, 0)$ on the unit circle, so $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$, $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$, and $\cos\pi = -1$.

In the second value, $2 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$ because the factor $2$ cancels. The three results give the points marked on the graph in the figure. The value $f\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) = \dfrac{3}{2}$ is the greatest of the three. Indeed, the graph is higher at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ than at the other two points.

</div>
