## Exercise 1

Evaluate the expression
$$
E = \sin\frac{5\pi}{4}\cos\frac{3\pi}{4} + \tan\frac{11\pi}{6}\tan\frac{2\pi}{3} .
$$

$$
\sin\frac{5\pi}{4} = \sin\left( \pi + \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = -\sin\frac{\pi}{4} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$
$$
\cos\frac{3\pi}{4} = \cos\left( \pi - \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = -\cos\frac{\pi}{4} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$
$$
\tan\frac{11\pi}{6} = \tan\left( 2\pi - \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = \tan\left( -\frac{\pi}{6} \right) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
$$
$$
\tan\frac{2\pi}{3} = \tan\left( \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = -\tan\frac{\pi}{3} = -\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= \left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)\left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) + \left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \right)\left( -\sqrt{3} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{4} + \frac{3}{3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The expression equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

None of the four angles lies in the first quadrant, so write each as a sum or difference of a multiple of $\pi$ and a special angle and apply the corresponding **related-angle** identity.

The angle $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ exceeds $\pi$ by $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, so we use the identity for angles that differ by $\pi$, which reverses the sign of the sine. The angle $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ is supplementary to $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, and the supplementary-angle identity reverses the sign of the cosine. The angle $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ differs by $2\pi$ from $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, so by periodicity it has the same tangent as $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Tangent is odd, so its value is $-\tan\dfrac{\pi}{6} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$. Finally, $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is supplementary to $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and the tangents of supplementary angles have opposite signs.

Each sign agrees with the one predicted by the quadrant. For example, $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ lies in the third quadrant, where sine is negative, and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ in the fourth, where tangent is negative.

In each product both factors are negative, so the products are positive. They are $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \cdot \sqrt{3} = \dfrac{3}{3} = 1$, and the sum is $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find $\cos\dfrac{23\pi}{6}$, $\sin\left( -\dfrac{19\pi}{4} \right)$, and $\tan\left( -\dfrac{10\pi}{3} \right)$.

$$
\cos\frac{23\pi}{6} = \cos\left( \frac{23\pi}{6} - 4\pi \right) = \cos\left( -\frac{\pi}{6} \right) = \cos\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\left( -\frac{19\pi}{4} \right) &= -\sin\frac{19\pi}{4} \\[6pt]
&= -\sin\left( \frac{19\pi}{4} - 4\pi \right) \\[6pt]
&= -\sin\frac{3\pi}{4} \\[6pt]
&= -\sin\frac{\pi}{4} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\tan\left( -\frac{10\pi}{3} \right) = -\tan\frac{10\pi}{3} = -\tan\left( \frac{10\pi}{3} - 3\pi \right) = -\tan\frac{\pi}{3} = -\sqrt{3}
$$

The three values are $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and $-\sqrt{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

For an angle greater than $2\pi$, subtract the largest possible multiple of $2\pi$ because sine and cosine have period $2\pi$. We have $\dfrac{23\pi}{6} = 4\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ since $4\pi = \dfrac{24\pi}{6}$. Subtracting two turns leaves $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Cosine is even, so $\cos\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{6} \right) = \cos\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

In the second calculation, first apply the negative-angle identity, which moves the minus sign outside the sine function. Subtracting $4\pi = \dfrac{16\pi}{4}$ from $\dfrac{19\pi}{4}$ then leaves $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, the supplement of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Supplementary angles have the same sine, so $\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and the result is $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

Tangent has period $\pi$, so multiples of $\pi$ can be subtracted. We have $\dfrac{10\pi}{3} = 3\pi + \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ because $3\pi = \dfrac{9\pi}{3}$, and therefore $\tan\dfrac{10\pi}{3} = \tan\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \sqrt{3}$. Tangent is odd, so the required value is $-\sqrt{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify the expression
$$
E(x) = \sin(\pi + x)\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - x \right) - \cos(\pi - x)\sin\left( \frac{\pi}{2} + x \right) + \tan(\pi + x)\tan\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - x \right) ,
$$
defined for $x \neq k\,\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, and calculate $E\left( \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$.

$$
\sin(\pi + x) = -\sin x ,
\qquad
\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - x \right) = \sin x
$$
$$
\cos(\pi - x) = -\cos x ,
\qquad
\sin\left( \frac{\pi}{2} + x \right) = \cos x
$$
$$
\tan(\pi + x) = \tan x ,
\qquad
\tan\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - x \right) = \cot x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E(x) &= (-\sin x)(\sin x) - (-\cos x)(\cos x) + \tan x \cot x \\[6pt]
&= -\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x + 1 \\[6pt]
&= \cos^2 x + \left( 1 - \sin^2 x \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2\cos^2 x
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
E\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = 2\cos^2\frac{\pi}{3} = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

The expression simplifies to $E(x) = 2\cos^2 x$, and $E\left( \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each factor involves an angle related to $x$ and can therefore be rewritten using the corresponding identity. The identities for angles that differ by $\pi$ reverse the sign of the sine and leave the tangent unchanged. The supplementary-angle identities reverse the sign of the cosine. The complementary-angle identities interchange sine and cosine and turn tangent into cotangent. For $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + x$ the sine becomes $\cos x$.

The third term is $\tan x \cdot \cot x$. For $x \neq k\,\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ both tangent and cotangent are defined, and each is the reciprocal of the other, so their product is $1$. The second term has a minus sign before it and is the product of $-\cos x$ and $\cos x$, so it becomes $+\cos^2 x$.

By the **first Pythagorean identity**, $1 - \sin^2 x = \cos^2 x$, so the sum $-\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x + 1$ equals $2\cos^2 x$. Finally, $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and its square is $\dfrac{1}{4}$. The value $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is not a multiple of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so it belongs to the domain of the expression.

</div>
