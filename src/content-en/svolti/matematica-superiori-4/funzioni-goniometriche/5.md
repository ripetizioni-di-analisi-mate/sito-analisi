## Exercise 1

Prove that for every real number $x$ the following identity holds:
$$
(\sin x + \cos x)^2 + (\sin x - \cos x)^2 = 2 .
$$

$$
(\sin x + \cos x)^2 = \sin^2 x + 2\sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x
$$
$$
(\sin x - \cos x)^2 = \sin^2 x - 2\sin x\cos x + \cos^2 x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sin x + \cos x)^2 + (\sin x - \cos x)^2 &= 2\sin^2 x + 2\cos^2 x \\[6pt]
&= 2\left( \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2 \cdot 1 = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

The left-hand side equals $2$ for every $x$, so the identity is proved.

<div class="spiegazione">

An **identity** is an equality that holds for all values of the variable for which both sides are defined. Only sine and cosine appear here, and both are defined for every real number, so there are no restrictions. To prove the identity, transform the left-hand side into the right-hand side.

Expand the two squared binomials using the formula $(a \pm b)^2 = a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2$, with $a = \sin x$ and $b = \cos x$. The cross terms $2\sin x\cos x$ and $-2\sin x\cos x$ are opposites, so they cancel in the sum, leaving twice $\sin^2 x$ and twice $\cos^2 x$.

Factoring out $2$ gives $2\left( \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x \right)$. By the **first Pythagorean identity**, the sum of the squares of sine and cosine is $1$ for every $x$, so the left-hand side equals $2$. For example, with $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ we have $(1 + 0)^2 + (1 - 0)^2 = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the values of $x$ for which both sides are defined and prove the identity
$$
\tan x + \cot x = \frac{1}{\sin x\cos x} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
\cos x \neq 0 \\[4pt]
\sin x \neq 0
\end{cases}
\iff
x \neq k\,\frac{\pi}{2} , \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan x + \cot x &= \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} + \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x}{\sin x\cos x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\sin x\cos x}
\end{aligned}
$$

For $x \neq k\,\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ both sides are defined and equal, so the identity is proved.

<div class="spiegazione">

Tangent requires $\cos x \neq 0$, cotangent requires $\sin x \neq 0$, and the right-hand side requires the product $\sin x\cos x$ to differ from $0$. All conditions must hold together, so we write a system. Cosine is zero at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ and sine at $x = k\pi$. Together these values are all multiples of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which must be excluded.

Write tangent and cotangent as ratios of sine and cosine using their definitions. The denominators of the two fractions are $\cos x$ and $\sin x$, so their common denominator is the product $\sin x\cos x$. The numerator becomes $\sin x \cdot \sin x + \cos x \cdot \cos x = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x$.

By the **first Pythagorean identity**, the numerator equals $1$, giving the right-hand side. For example, with $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ the left-hand side is $1 + 1 = 2$ and the right-hand side is $\dfrac{1}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{2}} = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Prove that for $x \neq k\pi$ with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ the following identity holds:
$$
\frac{1 - \cos x}{\sin x} = \frac{\sin x}{1 + \cos x} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
\sin x \neq 0 \\[4pt]
1 + \cos x \neq 0
\end{cases}
\iff
x \neq k\pi , \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1 - \cos x}{\sin x} &= \frac{(1 - \cos x)(1 + \cos x)}{\sin x\,(1 + \cos x)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1 - \cos^2 x}{\sin x\,(1 + \cos x)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sin^2 x}{\sin x\,(1 + \cos x)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sin x}{1 + \cos x}
\end{aligned}
$$

For $x \neq k\pi$ the left-hand side reduces to the right-hand side, so the identity is proved.

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side requires $\sin x \neq 0$, that is, $x \neq k\pi$. The right-hand side requires $1 + \cos x \neq 0$, that is, $\cos x \neq -1$, which excludes the values $x = \pi + 2k\pi$. These values are already multiples of $\pi$, so the combined conditions reduce to $x \neq k\pi$.

The right-hand side has denominator $1 + \cos x$, and the left-hand side has numerator $1 - \cos x$. Multiplying the numerator and denominator of the left-hand side by $1 + \cos x$, which differs from $0$, leaves the fraction unchanged. The numerator becomes the product of a sum and a difference, $1 - \cos^2 x$.

By the **first Pythagorean identity**, $1 - \cos^2 x = \sin^2 x$. The factor $\sin x$, which differs from $0$ by assumption, cancels from the numerator and denominator, leaving the right-hand side.

For example, with $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ the left-hand side equals $\dfrac{1 - \frac{1}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ and the right-hand side equals $\dfrac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{\frac{3}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.

</div>
