## Exercise 1

A point undergoes simple harmonic motion along a line according to $x(t) = 0.04\cos(5\pi t)$, with $x$ in meters and $t$ in seconds. Determine the amplitude, angular frequency, period, and frequency, and find the position of the point at times $t = 0.1\ \text{s}$, $t = 0.2\ \text{s}$, and $t = \dfrac{1}{15}\ \text{s}$.

$$
A = 0.04\ \text{m} ,
\qquad
\omega = 5\pi\ \text{rad/s}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{5\pi} = \frac{2}{5}\ \text{s} = 0.4\ \text{s} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{5}{2}\ \text{Hz} = 2.5\ \text{Hz}
$$
$$
x(0.1) = 0.04\cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 0
$$
$$
x(0.2) = 0.04\cos\pi = -0.04\ \text{m}
$$
$$
x\left( \frac{1}{15} \right) = 0.04\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = 0.04 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 0.02\ \text{m}
$$

The motion has amplitude $0.04\ \text{m}$, angular frequency $5\pi\ \text{rad/s}$, period $0.4\ \text{s}$, and frequency $2.5\ \text{Hz}$. At the three times, the point is at $x = 0$, $x = -0.04\ \text{m}$, and $x = 0.02\ \text{m}$, respectively.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$ for **simple harmonic motion** has parameters $A = 0.04$, $\omega = 5\pi$, and $\varphi = 0$ in this case. The amplitude is the maximum distance from the center of oscillation, since cosine takes values between $-1$ and $1$ and therefore $x$ varies between $-0.04$ and $0.04$.

The period is the time for one complete oscillation and equals $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$ because the argument $5\pi t$ must increase by $2\pi$. In the quotient $\dfrac{2\pi}{5\pi}$, the factor $\pi$ cancels, leaving $\dfrac{2}{5}$. The frequency is the reciprocal of the period, so the point completes $2.5$ oscillations per second.

To find each position, substitute the time into the argument of the cosine. When $t = 0.1$, the argument is $5\pi \cdot 0.1 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the cosine of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is $0$, so the point passes through the **center** of oscillation. When $t = 0.2$, the argument is $\pi$, the cosine is $-1$, and the point is at the negative extreme. This time is half the period, and in half a period the point indeed goes from one extreme to the other. When $t = \dfrac{1}{15}$, the argument is $\dfrac{5\pi}{15} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, whose cosine is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the point is halfway between the center and the positive extreme.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass attached to a spring undergoes simple harmonic motion with amplitude $6\ \text{cm}$ and frequency $0.25\ \text{Hz}$. At time $t = 0$ the mass passes through the center of oscillation in the positive $x$ direction. Write the equation of motion in the form $x(t) = A\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, with $-\pi < \varphi \leq \pi$, and calculate the position at $t = \dfrac{1}{3}\ \text{s}$ and the first time the mass reaches the position $x = 6\ \text{cm}$.

$$
T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{0.25} = 4\ \text{s} ,
\qquad
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{\pi}{2}\ \text{rad/s}
$$
$$
x(0) = 6\cos\varphi = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad \varphi = -\frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
\text{motion toward positive } x \text{ values}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\varphi = -\frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
x(t) = 6\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{2}\,t - \frac{\pi}{2} \right)
$$
$$
x\left( \frac{1}{3} \right) = 6\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{6} - \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = 6\cos\left( -\frac{\pi}{3} \right) = 6 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 3\ \text{cm}
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{2}\,t - \frac{\pi}{2} = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
t = 1\ \text{s}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,0,-1" punti="0,-1,pieno,Q" curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29;1.2*cos(t),1.2*sin(t),-1.571,-0.785" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="With $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the point $Q$ starts at the bottom and rotates counterclockwise, so its projection onto the $x$-axis starts at $0$ and moves in the positive $x$ direction"}

The equation of motion is $x(t) = 6\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}\,t - \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$, with $x$ in centimeters. At $t = \dfrac{1}{3}\ \text{s}$ the mass is at $x = 3\ \text{cm}$, and it first reaches $x = 6\ \text{cm}$ at time $t = 1\ \text{s}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Frequency is the reciprocal of the period, so $T = 4\ \text{s}$, and the angular frequency is $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The amplitude is $A = 6$.

Use the initial position to find the phase. The mass is at the center at time $0$, so $\cos\varphi = 0$. On the interval $(-\pi, \pi]$, this condition has two solutions, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. To choose between them, use the relationship between **simple harmonic motion and uniform circular motion**. The mass is the projection onto the $x$-axis of a point $Q$ rotating counterclockwise on a circle of radius $6$ from the initial angle $\varphi$. With $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the point $Q$ starts from $(0, -6)$ and moves toward the fourth quadrant, where its horizontal coordinate is positive. With $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ it would start from $(0, 6)$ and move toward the second quadrant, where its horizontal coordinate is negative. The problem states that the mass moves in the positive $x$ direction, so $\varphi = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

At $t = \dfrac{1}{3}$ the argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{6} - \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \dfrac{\pi - 3\pi}{6} = -\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Cosine is even, so $\cos\left( -\dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the position is $3\ \text{cm}$.

The mass reaches $x = 6$ when the cosine equals $1$, that is, when the argument is a multiple of $2\pi$. At $t \geq 0$ the argument starts at $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and increases, so the first multiple of $2\pi$ it reaches is $0$. This happens at $t = 1\ \text{s}$, that is, after a quarter of a period.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A Ferris wheel has radius $20\ \text{m}$ and its center is $22\ \text{m}$ above the ground. The wheel completes a turn in $8$ minutes at constant speed, and a cabin starts at the lowest point at time $t = 0$.

a) Show that the height of the cabin above the ground, in meters, at time $t$ in minutes is $h(t) = 22 - 20\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t \right)$, and write it in the form $A\cos(\omega t + \varphi) + b$.

b) Find the maximum and minimum heights and the height of the cabin after $2$ minutes and after $\dfrac{8}{3}$ minutes.

c) Determine how long during one turn the cabin is more than $32\ \text{m}$ above the ground.

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{8} = \frac{\pi}{4}\ \text{rad/min}
$$
$$
h(t) = 22 - 20\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{4}\,t \right) = 20\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{4}\,t + \pi \right) + 22
$$
$$
h_{\min} = 22 - 20 = 2\ \text{m} ,
\qquad
h_{\max} = 22 + 20 = 42\ \text{m}
$$
$$
h(2) = 22 - 20\cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 22\ \text{m} ,
\qquad
h\left( \frac{8}{3} \right) = 22 - 20\cos\frac{2\pi}{3} = 22 + 10 = 32\ \text{m}
$$
$$
h(t) > 32
\iff
-20\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{4}\,t \right) > 10
\iff
\cos\left( \frac{\pi}{4}\,t \right) < -\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{2\pi}{3} < \frac{\pi}{4}\,t < \frac{4\pi}{3}
\iff
\frac{8}{3} < t < \frac{16}{3}
$$
$$
\Delta t = \frac{16}{3} - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{8}{3}\ \text{min} = 2\ \text{min}\ 40\ \text{s}
$$

::grafico{f="22-20*cos(pi*x/4)" segmenti="0,32,9,32" punti="0,2,pieno;4,42,pieno;2.667,32,pieno;5.333,32,pieno;2,22,pieno" nomi="t,h" xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=45 legenda="h(t) = 22 - 20\cos\frac{\pi t}{4}" titolo="The height of the cabin exceeds $32\ \text{m}$ between $t = \dfrac{8}{3}$ and $t = \dfrac{16}{3}$ minutes"}

The equation is $h(t) = 20\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t + \pi \right) + 22$. The height varies between $2\ \text{m}$ and $42\ \text{m}$. It is $22\ \text{m}$ after $2$ minutes and $32\ \text{m}$ after $\dfrac{8}{3}$ minutes. The cabin stays above $32\ \text{m}$ for $\dfrac{8}{3}$ minutes, that is, $2$ minutes and $40$ seconds.

<div class="spiegazione">

a) Choose a coordinate system with its origin at the center of the wheel and its $y$-axis vertical. The cabin travels around the circle of radius $20$ at constant angular velocity. One turn, or $2\pi$ radians, takes $8$ minutes, so the angular velocity is $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ radians per minute. The cabin starts at the lowest point, corresponding to the angle $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, so at time $t$ the radius makes an angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t - \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ with the positive $x$-axis. The height of the cabin relative to the center is $20\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t - \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$. Sine is odd, so $\sin\left( \theta - \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = -\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta \right)$. By the complementary-angle identity, $\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta \right) = \cos\theta$, so with $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t$ the relative height is $-20\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t \right)$. The center is $22\ \text{m}$ above the ground, and adding gives $h(t)$. Finally, by the identity for angles that differ by $\pi$, we have $-\cos\theta = \cos(\theta + \pi)$, giving the required form with $A = 20$, $\omega = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\varphi = \pi$, and $b = 22$.

b) By the theorem on sinusoids, the height varies between $b - A = 2$ and $b + A = 42$. After $2$ minutes the argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the cosine equals $0$, and the cabin is level with the center. After $\dfrac{8}{3}$ minutes the argument is $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, whose cosine is $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ by the supplementary-angle identity, so $h = 22 + 10 = 32$.

c) We need the times when $h(t) > 32$. Subtracting $22$ gives $-20\cos\theta > 10$, with $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t$. Dividing by $-20$, which is negative, reverses the inequality. During one turn $\theta$ goes from $0$ to $2\pi$. On the unit circle, the points with horizontal coordinate less than $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ form the arc between $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ because $\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \cos\dfrac{4\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Multiplying by $\dfrac{4}{\pi}$ converts angles into times. The required interval lasts $\dfrac{8}{3}$ minutes, one-third of the turn.

</div>
