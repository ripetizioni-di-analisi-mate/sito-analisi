## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $\sqrt{3}\tan x + 1 = 0$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$.

$$
\text{Domain:} \qquad x \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
$$
$$
\tan x = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
$$
$$
x = \arctan\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \right) + k\pi = -\frac{\pi}{6} + k\pi ,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad x = -\frac{\pi}{6} + \pi = \frac{5\pi}{6} , \quad x = -\frac{\pi}{6} + 2\pi = \frac{11\pi}{6}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" linee="1,-1.8 1,1.8" segmenti="-1.6,0.924,1.6,-0.924" punti="0.866,-0.5,pieno,P₁;-0.866,0.5,pieno,P₂;1,-0.577,pieno,T" xmin=-1.8 xmax=1.8 ymin=-1.8 ymax=1.8 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The point $T\left(1, -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\right)$ lies on the line $x = 1$. The line $OT$ intersects the circle at $P_2$, corresponding to the angle $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, and at $P_1$, corresponding to the angle $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains a tangent, which is defined only where $\cos x \neq 0$. The **domain restrictions** are therefore $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$.

Subtracting $1$ and dividing by $\sqrt{3}$ gives $\tan x = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Rationalizing, that is, multiplying the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3}$, gives $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$.

Tangent takes every real value, so the equation has solutions. The special-angle values give $\tan\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$. Tangent is odd, so $\tan\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, and $-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ lies in the range $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ of the inverse tangent. By the theorem on the **basic tangent equation**, the solutions are $-\dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$, with period $\pi$.

All these solutions satisfy the domain restrictions because the tangent equals $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ at these points and is therefore defined.

In the interval $[0, 2\pi)$, the value for $k = 0$ is negative. Setting $k = 1$ gives $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and setting $k = 2$ gives $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$. With $k = 3$, the result exceeds $2\pi$. The solutions are $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$, corresponding to the two diametrically opposite points in the figure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $\cos\left(3x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, \pi]$.

$$
3x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \pm\frac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi
&\implies 3x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
\implies x = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{2k\pi}{3} \\[6pt]
3x + \frac{\pi}{4} = -\frac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi
&\implies 3x = -\pi + 2k\pi
\implies x = -\frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{2k\pi}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x \in [0, \pi] : \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{6} , \quad x = \frac{\pi}{3} , \quad x = \frac{5\pi}{6} , \quad x = \pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument of the cosine is the linear expression $3x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. First consider the basic equation $\cos t = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ in the unknown $t = 3x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Since $\arccos\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right) = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, its solutions are $t = \pm\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$.

Each family gives a linear equation in $x$. In the first, subtracting $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ gives $3x = \dfrac{3\pi - \pi}{4} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$. In the second, the result is $3x = \dfrac{-3\pi - \pi}{4} + 2k\pi = -\pi + 2k\pi$. Then divide each term by $3$, including $2k\pi$, which becomes $\dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$. The period of the solutions is therefore $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

For the interval $[0, \pi]$, test the values of $k$. The first family gives $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ for $k = 0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{4\pi}{6} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ for $k = 1$. For $k = 2$, it gives $\dfrac{3\pi}{2} > \pi$. The second family gives a negative value for $k = 0$, then $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ for $k = 1$ and $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{4\pi}{3} = \pi$ for $k = 2$. The endpoint $\pi$ belongs to the closed interval, so there are **four** solutions in the interval, namely $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, and $\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $\tan\left(\dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \sqrt{3}$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, 4\pi)$.

$$
\text{Domain:} \qquad \frac{x}{2} - \frac{\pi}{6} \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
\iff
x \neq \frac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\frac{x}{2} - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{3} + k\pi
\implies
\frac{x}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
\implies
x = \pi + 2k\pi
$$
$$
x \in [0, 4\pi) : \qquad x = \pi , \quad x = 3\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Tangent is defined when its argument differs from $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. To express the condition on $x$, add $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ to obtain $\dfrac{x}{2} \neq \dfrac{3\pi + \pi}{6} + k\pi = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + k\pi$, and multiply by $2$. The **domain restrictions** are therefore $x \neq \dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$.

Since $\arctan\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, the argument must equal $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + k\pi$. Adding $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ gives $\dfrac{x}{2} = \dfrac{2\pi + \pi}{6} + k\pi = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. Multiplying every term by $2$, including $k\pi$, gives $x = \pi + 2k\pi$. The period in $x$ is $2\pi$, that is, the period $\pi$ of tangent divided by the coefficient $\dfrac{1}{2}$ of $x$.

The difference between a number of the form $\pi + 2k\pi$ and a number of the form $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 2h\pi$ is $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ plus a multiple of $2\pi$, so it is never zero. Therefore the solutions found satisfy the domain restrictions.

In the interval $[0, 4\pi)$, we obtain $\pi$ for $k = 0$ and $3\pi$ for $k = 1$. With $k = 2$, the result is $5\pi$, which is outside the interval. The solutions are $\pi$ and $3\pi$.

</div>
