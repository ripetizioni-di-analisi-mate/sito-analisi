## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $\sin (2x) = \sqrt{3}\cos x$.

$$
2\sin x\cos x = \sqrt{3}\cos x
$$
$$
2\sin x\cos x - \sqrt{3}\cos x = 0
\iff
\cos x\,\left( 2\sin x - \sqrt{3} \right) = 0
$$
$$
\cos x = 0 \implies x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
$$
$$
\sin x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \implies x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \ \text{ or } \ x = \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains the angles $2x$ and $x$. Using the **double-angle identity** $\sin (2x) = 2\sin x\cos x$, every term involves only the angle $x$.

Move all terms to the left-hand side. The two terms have a common factor $\cos x$. Factoring it out gives the product $\cos x\,\left(2\sin x - \sqrt{3}\right)$. By the **zero-product property**, the product is zero if and only if at least one of its two factors is zero, so the equation splits into two basic equations.

The first factor gives $\cos x = 0$, a special case with solutions $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. The second factor gives $2\sin x = \sqrt{3}$, that is, $\sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, with $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and second family $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

Dividing both sides by $\cos x$ would give only the equation $2\sin x = \sqrt{3}$ and discard the family $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. These values are solutions, however, since for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ we have $\sin\pi = 0$ and $\sqrt{3}\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$. The solutions therefore consist of the **three families** found.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $\cos (2x) + 3\cos x + 2 = 0$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$.

$$
\cos (2x) = 2\cos^2 x - 1
$$
$$
2\cos^2 x - 1 + 3\cos x + 2 = 0
\iff
2\cos^2 x + 3\cos x + 1 = 0
$$
$$
(2\cos x + 1)(\cos x + 1) = 0
$$
$$
\cos x = -\frac{1}{2} \implies x = \pm\frac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad
\cos x = -1 \implies x = \pi + 2k\pi
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad x = \frac{2\pi}{3} , \quad x = \pi , \quad x = \frac{4\pi}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains $\cos (2x)$ and $\cos x$. The cosine of the double angle can be written in three ways. Choose the form $2\cos^2 x - 1$ because it involves only the cosine of $x$, the other function in the equation.

After substitution, combining the constant terms, $-1 + 2 = 1$, gives a quadratic equation in $\cos x$. The trinomial $2t^2 + 3t + 1$, with $t = \cos x$, has discriminant $9 - 8 = 1$ and roots $t = \dfrac{-3 \pm 1}{4}$, that is, $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $-1$. It therefore factors as $(2t + 1)(t + 1)$, and expanding recovers $2t^2 + 2t + t + 1$.

Both values lie between $-1$ and $1$. For the equation $\cos x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, we have $\arccos\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, hence the solutions $\pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. The equation $\cos x = -1$ is a special case with the single family $\pi + 2k\pi$.

In $[0, 2\pi)$, we obtain $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, then $-\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2\pi = \dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, and finally $\pi$. In increasing order, the solutions are $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, $\pi$, and $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$. They all lie in the second or third quadrant or on the negative horizontal axis, where cosine is negative.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $\sin (5x) - \sin x = \cos (3x)$.

$$
\sin (5x) - \sin x = 2\cos\frac{5x + x}{2}\sin\frac{5x - x}{2} = 2\cos (3x)\sin (2x)
$$
$$
2\cos (3x)\sin (2x) - \cos (3x) = 0
\iff
\cos (3x)\,\big( 2\sin (2x) - 1 \big) = 0
$$
$$
\cos (3x) = 0 \implies 3x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi \implies x = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{k\pi}{3}
$$
$$
\sin (2x) = \frac{1}{2} \implies
\begin{aligned}
2x &= \frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \implies x = \frac{\pi}{12} + k\pi \\[6pt]
2x &= \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi \implies x = \frac{5\pi}{12} + k\pi
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side is the difference of two sines. The **sum-to-product identity** $\sin p - \sin q = 2\cos\dfrac{p + q}{2}\sin\dfrac{p - q}{2}$, with $p = 5x$ and $q = x$, transforms it into a product. Half the sum of the angles is $\dfrac{6x}{2} = 3x$ and half their difference is $\dfrac{4x}{2} = 2x$, so the left-hand side becomes $2\cos (3x)\sin (2x)$.

After the transformation, the factor $\cos (3x)$ appears on both sides. Move all terms to the left-hand side and factor out $\cos (3x)$. By the zero-product property, the equation is equivalent to $\cos (3x) = 0$ or $2\sin (2x) - 1 = 0$.

The first equation is a special case with argument $3x$. The angles with zero cosine are $\dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, so $3x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$, and dividing each term by $3$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{k\pi}{3}$.

The second equation is $\sin (2x) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The angle $2x$ belongs to one of the two families $\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$, and dividing by $2$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{12} + k\pi$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{12} + k\pi$. The general solution is the union of the **three families**.

</div>
