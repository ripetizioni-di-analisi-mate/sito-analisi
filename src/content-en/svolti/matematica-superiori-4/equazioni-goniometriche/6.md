## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $\sqrt{3}\sin x + \cos x = 1$ using the auxiliary-angle method.

$$
a = \sqrt{3} , \quad b = 1 , \quad c = 1 ,
\qquad
r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{3 + 1} = 2
$$
$$
\cos\varphi = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} , \quad \sin\varphi = \frac{1}{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\varphi = \frac{\pi}{6}
$$
$$
2\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = 1
\iff
\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi &\implies x = 2k\pi \\[6pt]
x + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi &\implies x = \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation has the form $a\sin x + b\cos x = c$, with $a$ and $b$ both different from $0$, so it is **linear** in sine and cosine. With the auxiliary-angle method, write the left-hand side as $r\sin(x + \varphi)$, with $r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = 2$.

The angle $\varphi$ has cosine $\dfrac{a}{r} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and sine $\dfrac{b}{r} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Both are positive, so $\varphi$ lies in the first quadrant, and the special-angle values give $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Indeed, the sine addition formula gives $2\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = 2\sin x \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + 2\cos x \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \sqrt{3}\sin x + \cos x$.

Before solving, check that solutions exist. The condition is $c^2 \leq a^2 + b^2$, and here $1 \leq 4$, so the equation has solutions.

The equation becomes $\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$, a basic equation in the angle $x + \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The angles whose sine is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ are $\dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$. Subtracting $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ gives $x = 2k\pi$ and $x = \dfrac{4\pi}{6} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$.

For $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, the left-hand side equals $\sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{3}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$, in agreement with the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $3\sin x + \cos x = -1$ using the tangent half-angle substitution. Give the solutions in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ to two decimal places.

$$
x = \pi + 2k\pi : \qquad 3 \cdot 0 + (-1) = -1
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x = \pi + 2k\pi \ \text{ is a solution}
$$
$$
x \neq \pi + 2k\pi , \quad t = \tan\frac{x}{2} :
\qquad
3 \cdot \frac{2t}{1 + t^2} + \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2} = -1
$$
$$
6t + 1 - t^2 = -1 - t^2
\iff
6t = -2
\iff
t = -\frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
\tan\frac{x}{2} = -\frac{1}{3}
\implies
\frac{x}{2} = -\arctan\frac{1}{3} + k\pi
\implies
x = -2\arctan\frac{1}{3} + 2k\pi
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad x = \pi \approx 3.14 , \quad x = 2\pi - 2\arctan\frac{1}{3} \approx 5.64
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The auxiliary-angle method would give an angle $\varphi$ with $\cos\varphi = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{10}}$. This is not the cosine of a special angle. Using the **tangent half-angle formulas** instead gives an algebraic equation in $t = \tan\dfrac{x}{2}$.

The tangent half-angle formulas hold only for $x \neq \pi + 2k\pi$, where $\tan\dfrac{x}{2}$ is defined. The values $x = \pi + 2k\pi$ must therefore be checked separately. At these points $\sin x = 0$ and $\cos x = -1$, so the left-hand side equals $-1$, the same as the right-hand side, and all members of the family $x = \pi + 2k\pi$ are solutions.

For the other values, substitute $\sin x = \dfrac{2t}{1 + t^2}$ and $\cos x = \dfrac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}$. Multiplying both sides by $1 + t^2$, which is always positive, gives $6t + 1 - t^2 = -1 - t^2$. The terms $-t^2$ cancel, leaving the linear equation $6t = -2$, that is, $t = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.

From the equation $\tan\dfrac{x}{2} = -\dfrac{1}{3}$ it follows that $\dfrac{x}{2} = \arctan\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) + k\pi$. Since the inverse tangent is odd, $\arctan\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) = -\arctan\dfrac{1}{3}$. Multiplying each term by $2$ gives $x = -2\arctan\dfrac{1}{3} + 2k\pi$.

A calculator gives $\arctan\dfrac{1}{3} \approx 0.3218$, so $-2\arctan\dfrac{1}{3} \approx -0.6435$, and with $k = 1$ we obtain $2\pi - 0.6435 \approx 5.64$. The solutions in $[0, 2\pi)$ are therefore $\pi \approx 3.14$ and approximately $5.64$. Without the separate check, the solution $\pi$ would be lost.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $\sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x = \sqrt{3}$ graphically.

$$
X = \cos x , \quad Y = \sin x
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\sqrt{3}\,X + Y = \sqrt{3} \\[4pt]
X^2 + Y^2 = 1
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
Y = \sqrt{3}\,(1 - X)
$$
$$
X^2 + 3\,(1 - X)^2 = 1
\iff
4X^2 - 6X + 2 = 0
\iff
2X^2 - 3X + 1 = 0
$$
$$
X = \frac{3 \pm 1}{4}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
X = 1 , \ Y = 0
\quad \text{or} \quad
X = \frac{1}{2} , \ Y = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
x = 2k\pi
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="-0.15,1.992,1.3,-0.520" punti="1,0,pieno,A;0.5,0.866,pieno,B" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=2.2 uguale=1 nomi="X,Y" legenda=0 titolo="The line $\sqrt{3}\,X + Y = \sqrt{3}$ intersects the unit circle at $A(1, 0)$ and $B\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$, corresponding to the angles $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the graphical method, let $X$ denote the cosine and $Y$ the sine of the unknown angle. The point $(X, Y)$ lies on the unit circle, so it satisfies $X^2 + Y^2 = 1$, and the given equation becomes the equation of the line $\sqrt{3}\,X + Y = \sqrt{3}$. The two conditions must hold simultaneously, so we write them as a **system**.

The first equation gives $Y = \sqrt{3} - \sqrt{3}\,X = \sqrt{3}\,(1 - X)$. Substitute this into the second equation. The square of $\sqrt{3}\,(1 - X)$ is $3\,(1 - 2X + X^2)$, so this gives $X^2 + 3 - 6X + 3X^2 = 1$, that is, $4X^2 - 6X + 2 = 0$. Dividing by $2$ gives $2X^2 - 3X + 1 = 0$, with discriminant $9 - 8 = 1$ and solutions $X = 1$ and $X = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

For each value of $X$, calculate $Y$ using the equation of the line. For $X = 1$, we have $Y = 0$, and the point $A(1, 0)$ corresponds to the angles $2k\pi$. For $X = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we have $Y = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and the point $B$ has cosine $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and sine $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so it corresponds to the angles $\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$.

The line is a **secant** of the circle, so the equation has two families of solutions. The auxiliary-angle method gives the same result, since the left-hand side is $2\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$.

</div>
