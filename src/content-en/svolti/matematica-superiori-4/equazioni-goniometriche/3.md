## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $\sin (2x) = \sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{3}\right)$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$.

$$
2x = x + \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
2x = \pi - \left( x + \frac{\pi}{3} \right) + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2x = x + \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
&\implies x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi \\[6pt]
2x = \pi - x - \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
&\implies 3x = \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
\implies x = \frac{2\pi}{9} + \frac{2k\pi}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad x = \frac{2\pi}{9} , \quad x = \frac{\pi}{3} , \quad x = \frac{8\pi}{9} , \quad x = \frac{14\pi}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two sides are the sines of different angles, $2x$ and $x + \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. By the theorem on equations of the form $\sin f(x) = \sin g(x)$, two angles have the same sine if and only if they differ by a multiple of $2\pi$ or their sum is $\pi$ plus a multiple of $2\pi$. This gives two linear equations in $x$.

In the first, subtracting $x$ from both sides immediately gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. In the second, the minus sign in front of $x + \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ changes the signs of both terms. Moving $-x$ to the left-hand side gives $3x = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. Dividing every term by $3$ gives $x = \dfrac{2\pi}{9} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$, with period $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

In the interval $[0, 2\pi)$, the first family gives only $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, for $k = 0$. The second family gives three values because $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ fits into $2\pi$ three times. With $k = 0, 1, 2$, the results are $\dfrac{2\pi}{9}$, $\dfrac{2\pi}{9} + \dfrac{6\pi}{9} = \dfrac{8\pi}{9}$, and $\dfrac{2\pi}{9} + \dfrac{12\pi}{9} = \dfrac{14\pi}{9}$.

For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, both sides equal $\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, in agreement with the calculation. There are therefore **four** solutions in $[0, 2\pi)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $\cos (3x) = \cos\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, \pi)$.

$$
3x = x - \frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
3x = -\left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right) + 2k\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2x = -\frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi
&\implies x = -\frac{\pi}{8} + k\pi \\[6pt]
4x = \frac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi
&\implies x = \frac{\pi}{16} + \frac{k\pi}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x \in [0, \pi) : \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{16} , \quad x = \frac{9\pi}{16} , \quad x = \frac{7\pi}{8}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two angles have the same cosine if and only if they differ by a multiple of $2\pi$ or are negatives of one another up to a multiple of $2\pi$, since points on the circle with the same horizontal coordinate are reflections of one another across the $x$-axis. By the theorem on equations of the form $\cos f(x) = \cos g(x)$, we therefore write the two equations $3x = g(x) + 2k\pi$ and $3x = -g(x) + 2k\pi$, with $g(x) = x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

In the first equation, subtracting $x$ gives $2x = -\dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$, and dividing by $2$ gives $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{8} + k\pi$. In the second, the negative of $x - \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is $-x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Adding $x$ to both sides gives $4x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$, and dividing by $4$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{16} + \dfrac{k\pi}{2}$.

In the interval $[0, \pi)$, the first family gives a negative value for $k = 0$ and $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{8} = \dfrac{7\pi}{8}$ for $k = 1$. The second gives $\dfrac{\pi}{16}$ for $k = 0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{16} + \dfrac{8\pi}{16} = \dfrac{9\pi}{16}$ for $k = 1$. With $k = 2$, it gives $\dfrac{17\pi}{16}$, which exceeds $\pi$. There are therefore **three** solutions in the interval, namely $\dfrac{\pi}{16}$, $\dfrac{9\pi}{16}$, and $\dfrac{7\pi}{8}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $\sin x = \cos (2x)$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$.

$$
\cos (2x) = \sin\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - 2x \right)
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin x = \sin\left( \frac{\pi}{2} - 2x \right)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = \frac{\pi}{2} - 2x + 2k\pi
&\implies 3x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
\implies x = \frac{\pi}{6} + \frac{2k\pi}{3} \\[6pt]
x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{2} + 2x + 2k\pi
&\implies -x = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi
\implies x = -\frac{\pi}{2} - 2k\pi
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{6} , \quad x = \frac{5\pi}{6} , \quad x = \frac{3\pi}{2}
$$

::grafico{f="sin(x);cos(2*x)" punti="0.5236,0.5,pieno;2.6180,0.5,pieno;4.7124,-1,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 legenda="y = \sin x;y = \cos 2x" titolo="In $[0, 2\pi)$, the graphs of $\sin x$ and $\cos 2x$ intersect at the points with horizontal coordinates $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two sides contain different functions. By the identity for complementary angles, $\cos\alpha = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \alpha\right)$, and with $\alpha = 2x$ the right-hand side becomes a sine. The equation now has the form $\sin f(x) = \sin g(x)$, with $f(x) = x$ and $g(x) = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - 2x$.

The first family follows from $x = g(x) + 2k\pi$. Adding $2x$ gives $3x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, and dividing by $3$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$.

The second family follows from $x = \pi - g(x) + 2k\pi = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2x + 2k\pi$. Subtracting $2x$ gives $-x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$, so $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2} - 2k\pi$. As $k$ ranges over the integers, $-k$ also takes on every integer value, so this family coincides with $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} + 2k\pi$.

The second family is contained in the first. Indeed, for $k = 2$ the first family gives $\dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{4\pi}{3} = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, which differs from $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ by $2\pi$. The general solution is therefore $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{2k\pi}{3}$.

In $[0, 2\pi)$, choose $k = 0, 1, 2$ to obtain $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, the horizontal coordinates of the three intersections of the graphs in the figure.

</div>
