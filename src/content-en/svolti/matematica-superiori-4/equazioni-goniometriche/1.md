## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $2\sin x - \sqrt{3} = 0$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$.

$$
2\sin x = \sqrt{3}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin x = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
\alpha = \arcsin\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi = \frac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{3} , \quad x = \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="0,0,0.5,0.866,P₁;0,0,-0.5,0.866,P₂" segmenti="-1.4,0.866,1.4,0.866" punti="0.5,0.866,pieno;-0.5,0.866,pieno" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The line $y = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ intersects the unit circle at $P_1$, corresponding to the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and at $P_2$, corresponding to the angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step is to isolate the sine. Adding $\sqrt{3}$ to both sides and dividing by $2$ gives the basic equation $\sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

The number $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ lies between $-1$ and $1$, so the theorem on the **basic sine equation** applies and the equation has solutions. We need an angle whose sine is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The special-angle values give $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ lies in the interval $\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$, so $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

The theorem gives two families. The first is $\alpha + 2k\pi$. The second is $\pi - \alpha + 2k\pi$, corresponding to the reflection across the $y$-axis, that is, the point in the second quadrant with the same vertical coordinate. The calculation $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{3\pi - \pi}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ gives the second family.

For the interval $[0, 2\pi)$, choose $k = 0$ in both families. With $k = 1$, the results are $\dfrac{7\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{8\pi}{3}$, both greater than $2\pi$. With $k = -1$, the results are negative. The solutions in the interval are therefore $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, corresponding to the two points in the figure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $2\cos x + \sqrt{2} = 0$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$.

$$
2\cos x = -\sqrt{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\cos x = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$
$$
\beta = \arccos\left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) = \pi - \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{3\pi}{4}
$$
$$
x = \pm\frac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad x = \frac{3\pi}{4} , \quad x = -\frac{3\pi}{4} + 2\pi = \frac{5\pi}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $\sqrt{2}$ from both sides and dividing by $2$ gives $\cos x = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The value is between $-1$ and $1$, so the theorem on the **basic cosine equation** applies.

The inverse cosine of a negative number is an angle in the second quadrant because the range of the inverse cosine is $[0, \pi]$. The cosine of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. By the identity for supplementary angles, $\cos\left(\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. Therefore $\beta = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.

The solutions of the cosine equation are $\beta + 2k\pi$ and $-\beta + 2k\pi$, since the two points on the circle with horizontal coordinate $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ are reflections of each other across the $x$-axis. The two families can be written together as $\pm\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2k\pi$.

In the interval $[0, 2\pi)$, the first family gives $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ for $k = 0$. The second gives a negative number for $k = 0$, so choose $k = 1$ to obtain $-\dfrac{3\pi}{4} + 2\pi = \dfrac{-3\pi + 8\pi}{4} = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$. The solutions in the interval are $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, in the second and third quadrants, where cosine is negative.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $4\sin x + 1 = 0$. Give the solutions in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ to two decimal places.

$$
\sin x = -\frac{1}{4}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\alpha = \arcsin\left( -\frac{1}{4} \right) = -\arcsin\frac{1}{4}
$$
$$
x = -\arcsin\frac{1}{4} + 2k\pi
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
x = \pi + \arcsin\frac{1}{4} + 2k\pi ,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$
$$
\arcsin\frac{1}{4} \approx 0.25
$$
$$
x \in [0, 2\pi) : \qquad
\begin{aligned}
x &= \pi + \arcsin\frac{1}{4} \approx 3.39 , \\[6pt]
x &= 2\pi - \arcsin\frac{1}{4} \approx 6.03
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $1$ and dividing by $4$ gives $\sin x = -\dfrac{1}{4}$. The value $-\dfrac{1}{4}$ lies between $-1$ and $1$, so the equation has solutions, but it is not the sine of a special angle. For this reason, the angle $\alpha$ is written using the inverse sine.

The inverse sine is an odd function, that is, $\arcsin(-m) = -\arcsin m$, since sine is odd. Therefore $\alpha = -\arcsin\dfrac{1}{4}$, an angle in the fourth quadrant close to $0$.

The second family is $\pi - \alpha + 2k\pi$. Substituting $\alpha = -\arcsin\dfrac{1}{4}$ gives $\pi - \alpha = \pi + \arcsin\dfrac{1}{4}$, an angle in the third quadrant.

A calculator in radian mode gives $\arcsin\dfrac{1}{4} \approx 0.2527$. In the interval $[0, 2\pi)$, the second family with $k = 0$ gives $\pi + 0.2527 \approx 3.39$. The first family with $k = 0$ gives a negative number, so choose $k = 1$ to obtain $2\pi - 0.2527 \approx 6.03$. The two solutions are approximately $3.39$ and $6.03$, in the third and fourth quadrants, where sine is negative.

</div>
