## Exercise 1

Solve the equation $\sin^2 x - 4\sin x\cos x + 3\cos^2 x = 0$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right)$.

$$
\cos x = 0 \implies \sin^2 x = 1 \implies 1 - 0 + 0 = 1 \neq 0
$$
$$
\frac{\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} - 4\,\frac{\sin x\cos x}{\cos^2 x} + 3\,\frac{\cos^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = 0
\iff
\tan^2 x - 4\tan x + 3 = 0
$$
$$
(\tan x - 1)(\tan x - 3) = 0
$$
$$
\tan x = 1 \implies x = \frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi ,
\qquad
\tan x = 3 \implies x = \arctan 3 + k\pi
$$
$$
x \in \left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2} \right) : \qquad
x = \frac{\pi}{4} , \quad x = \arctan 3 , \quad x = \frac{5\pi}{4} , \quad x = \pi + \arctan 3
$$

::grafico{f="tan(x);1;3" punti="0.7854,1,pieno;1.2490,3,pieno;3.9270,1,pieno;4.3906,3,pieno" asintoti="x=1.5708" xmin=-1.6 xmax=4.8 ymin=-2 ymax=5 legenda="y = \tan x;y = 1;y = 3" titolo="In the interval $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right)$, the graph of $\tan x$ intersects each of the lines $y = 1$ and $y = 3$ twice, with the intersections $\pi$ apart"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Every term has total degree $2$ in $\sin x$ and $\cos x$, so the equation is **homogeneous and quadratic**. The coefficient of $\sin^2 x$ is $1 \neq 0$.

Before dividing by $\cos^2 x$, check that points with $\cos x = 0$ are not solutions. At these points $\sin^2 x = 1$, and the left-hand side equals $1$, which differs from $0$. Dividing by $\cos^2 x$ therefore does not discard any solutions.

Dividing each term by $\cos^2 x$ gives $\dfrac{\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \tan^2 x$, then $\dfrac{\sin x\cos x}{\cos^2 x} = \tan x$, and finally $\dfrac{\cos^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = 1$. The resulting equation is $\tan^2 x - 4\tan x + 3 = 0$. Two numbers whose sum is $4$ and whose product is $3$ are $1$ and $3$, so the trinomial factors as $(\tan x - 1)(\tan x - 3)$.

The basic equations $\tan x = 1$ and $\tan x = 3$ give $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$ and $x = \arctan 3 + k\pi$, where $\arctan 3 \approx 1.25$ is an angle in the first quadrant.

The given interval has length $2\pi$, equal to two periods of the tangent, so each family gives two values in it, for $k = 0$ and $k = 1$. The solutions are $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\arctan 3$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, and $\pi + \arctan 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the equation $2\sin^2 x - 3\sin x\cos x + 3\cos^2 x = 1$.

$$
2\sin^2 x - 3\sin x\cos x + 3\cos^2 x = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x
$$
$$
\sin^2 x - 3\sin x\cos x + 2\cos^2 x = 0
$$
$$
\cos x = 0 \implies 1 \neq 0
$$
$$
\tan^2 x - 3\tan x + 2 = 0
\iff
(\tan x - 1)(\tan x - 2) = 0
$$
$$
x = \frac{\pi}{4} + k\pi
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
x = \arctan 2 + k\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side is homogeneous and quadratic, but the right-hand side is the number $1$, which has degree $0$. By the **Pythagorean identity**, $1 = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x$ for every $x$, so we can replace the constant term by this quadratic expression without changing the solutions.

Move all terms to the left-hand side and subtract the coefficients of like terms. The coefficient of $\sin^2 x$ becomes $2 - 1 = 1$ and that of $\cos^2 x$ becomes $3 - 1 = 2$, while the term in $\sin x\cos x$ stays the same. The equation is now **homogeneous**.

The coefficient of $\sin^2 x$ is $1 \neq 0$, so points with $\cos x = 0$ are not solutions. Indeed, at these points $\sin^2 x = 1$ and the left-hand side equals $1$. We can therefore divide by $\cos^2 x$ to obtain $\tan^2 x - 3\tan x + 2 = 0$.

The roots of the trinomial have sum $3$ and product $2$, so they are $1$ and $2$. The equation $\tan x = 1$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$, and the equation $\tan x = 2$ gives $x = \arctan 2 + k\pi$, with $\arctan 2 \approx 1.11$.

For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, we have $\sin^2 x = \cos^2 x = \sin x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the original left-hand side equals $1 - \dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{3}{2} = 1$, in agreement with the result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the equation $\sin^2 x + \sqrt{3}\sin x\cos x = 1$ and give the solutions that lie in the interval $[0, \pi)$.

$$
\sin^2 x + \sqrt{3}\sin x\cos x = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x
\iff
\sqrt{3}\sin x\cos x - \cos^2 x = 0
$$
$$
\cos x\,\left( \sqrt{3}\sin x - \cos x \right) = 0
$$
$$
\cos x = 0 \implies x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
$$
$$
\sqrt{3}\sin x - \cos x = 0
\implies
\tan x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
\implies
x = \frac{\pi}{6} + k\pi
$$
$$
x \in [0, \pi) : \qquad x = \frac{\pi}{6} , \quad x = \frac{\pi}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the constant term as $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x$ and move all terms to the left-hand side. The two terms $\sin^2 x$ cancel, leaving the homogeneous equation $\sqrt{3}\sin x\cos x - \cos^2 x = 0$.

In this equation, the coefficient of $\sin^2 x$ is $0$. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the left-hand side equals $\sqrt{3} \cdot 1 \cdot 0 - 0 = 0$, so the points with $\cos x = 0$ **are solutions**, and dividing by $\cos^2 x$ would discard them. For this reason, factor out the common factor $\cos x$.

By the zero-product property, the equation splits into two. The factor $\cos x$ vanishes for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$. The factor $\sqrt{3}\sin x - \cos x$ gives a first-degree homogeneous equation. At a point with $\cos x = 0$, it would give $\sqrt{3}\sin x = 0$, which is impossible because $\sin x = \pm 1$. We can therefore divide by $\cos x$ to obtain $\sqrt{3}\tan x = 1$, that is, $\tan x = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, with solutions $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + k\pi$.

In the interval $[0, \pi)$, each family has period $\pi$ and gives only one value, for $k = 0$. The solutions are $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, the original left-hand side equals $\dfrac{1}{4} + \sqrt{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{3}{4} = 1$.

</div>
