## Exercise 1

Find the solutions of the equation $2\cos\dfrac{x}{2} + 1 = 0$ that lie in the interval $[-2\pi, 4\pi]$.

$$
\cos\frac{x}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}
\implies
\frac{x}{2} = \pm\frac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
\implies
x = \pm\frac{4\pi}{3} + 4k\pi
$$
$$
-2\pi \leq \frac{4\pi}{3} + 4k\pi \leq 4\pi
\iff
-\frac{10\pi}{3} \leq 4k\pi \leq \frac{8\pi}{3}
\iff
-\frac{5}{6} \leq k \leq \frac{2}{3}
\implies
k = 0
$$
$$
-2\pi \leq -\frac{4\pi}{3} + 4k\pi \leq 4\pi
\iff
-\frac{2\pi}{3} \leq 4k\pi \leq \frac{16\pi}{3}
\iff
-\frac{1}{6} \leq k \leq \frac{4}{3}
\implies
k = 0 , \ k = 1
$$
$$
x = -\frac{4\pi}{3} , \quad x = \frac{4\pi}{3} , \quad x = \frac{8\pi}{3}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(x/2);-0.5" punti="-4.1888,-0.5,pieno;4.1888,-0.5,pieno;8.3776,-0.5,pieno" segmenti="-6.2832,-1.5,-6.2832,1.5;12.5664,-1.5,12.5664,1.5" xmin=-7 xmax=13 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 legenda="y = \cos\dfrac{x}{2};y = -\dfrac{1}{2}" titolo="Between the vertical lines $x = -2\pi$ and $x = 4\pi$, the graph of $\cos\dfrac{x}{2}$ intersects the line $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ at three points with horizontal coordinates $-\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, and $\dfrac{8\pi}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolating the cosine gives $\cos\dfrac{x}{2} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $\arccos\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, the angle $\dfrac{x}{2}$ equals $\pm\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$. Multiplying every term by $2$ gives $x = \pm\dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 4k\pi$, and the period of the solutions is $4\pi$.

To choose the values of $k$, write for each family the double inequality expressing membership in the interval. In the first family, subtracting $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ from all three parts gives $-\dfrac{10\pi}{3} \leq 4k\pi \leq \dfrac{8\pi}{3}$, then divide by $4\pi > 0$, which leaves the inequality signs unchanged. The result is $-\dfrac{5}{6} \leq k \leq \dfrac{2}{3}$, and the only integer is $k = 0$.

In the second family, adding $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ gives $-\dfrac{2\pi}{3} \leq 4k\pi \leq \dfrac{16\pi}{3}$. Dividing by $4\pi$ gives $-\dfrac{1}{6} \leq k \leq \dfrac{4}{3}$, which contains the integers $0$ and $1$. Setting $k = 0$ gives $-\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, and setting $k = 1$ gives $-\dfrac{4\pi}{3} + 4\pi = \dfrac{8\pi}{3}$.

There are therefore **three** solutions in the interval, as shown in the graph.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the values of the real parameter $m$ for which the equation $2\sin x - 1 = m$ has solutions in the interval $[0, \pi]$, and determine how many solutions there are.

$$
\sin x = \frac{m + 1}{2}
$$
$$
x \in [0, \pi] \implies 0 \leq \sin x \leq 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
0 \leq \frac{m + 1}{2} < 1 &\iff -1 \leq m < 1 &&\text{two solutions} \\[6pt]
\frac{m + 1}{2} = 1 &\iff m = 1 &&\text{one solution} \\[6pt]
m < -1 \ \text{ or } \ m > 1 & &&\text{no solution}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*sin(x)-1" segmenti="0,0,3.1416,0;0,1,3.1416,1" punti="0,-1,pieno;3.1416,-1,pieno;1.5708,1,pieno;0.5236,0,pieno;2.6180,0,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=3.2 ymin=-2 ymax=2 legenda="y = 2\sin x - 1" titolo="In $[0, \pi]$, the graph of $2\sin x - 1$ goes from $-1$ to $1$ and back to $-1$, so the line $y = m$ intersects it at two points for $-1 \leq m < 1$ and at one point for $m = 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolate the sine by adding $1$ and dividing by $2$ to obtain the basic equation $\sin x = \dfrac{m + 1}{2}$. The number of solutions depends on the value of the right-hand side.

In the interval $[0, \pi]$, the point corresponding to the angle $x$ moves along the upper semicircle, where the sine lies between $0$ and $1$. Sine increases from $0$ to $1$ between $0$ and $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and decreases from $1$ to $0$ between $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pi$. A horizontal line $Y = s$ with $0 \leq s < 1$ therefore meets the semicircle at two points. For $s = 0$, the two points are the endpoints $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$, corresponding to the angles $0$ and $\pi$, which belong to the closed interval. For $s = 1$, the line touches the semicircle only at $(0, 1)$. For $s < 0$ or $s > 1$, there are no intersections.

Express these conditions in terms of the parameter. The inequality $0 \leq \dfrac{m + 1}{2} < 1$ is equivalent to $0 \leq m + 1 < 2$, that is, to $-1 \leq m < 1$. The equality $\dfrac{m + 1}{2} = 1$ is equivalent to $m = 1$.

The equation therefore has **two solutions** for $-1 \leq m < 1$, **one solution** for $m = 1$, namely $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and no solutions for $m < -1$ or $m > 1$. In the figure, the line $y = 0$, which corresponds to $m = 0$, intersects the graph at $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The segment $AB$ is the diameter of a semicircle of radius $1$, and $P$ is a point on the semicircle other than $A$ or $B$. Let $x = P\widehat{A}B$.

a) Express the perimeter $2p(x)$ of triangle $APB$ as a function of $x$, giving the admissible values of $x$.

b) Find $x$ such that the perimeter is $3 + \sqrt{3}$.

c) Determine whether there is a position of $P$ for which the perimeter is $5$.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad A\widehat{P}B = \frac{\pi}{2} , \quad AP = 2\cos x , \quad PB = 2\sin x , \qquad 0 < x < \frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
2p(x) = 2 + 2\cos x + 2\sin x
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad 2 + 2\sin x + 2\cos x = 3 + \sqrt{3}
\iff
\sin x + \cos x = \frac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
\sqrt{2}\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{2}
\iff
\sin\left( x + \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6}}{4} = \sin\frac{5\pi}{12}
$$
$$
x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{5\pi}{12} \implies x = \frac{\pi}{6} ,
\qquad
x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{7\pi}{12} \implies x = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
\text{c)} \qquad \sin x + \cos x = \frac{3}{2} , \qquad \frac{3}{2} > \sqrt{2}
\implies
\text{no solution}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,3.1416" linee="-1,0 0.5,0.866 1,0 -1,0" punti="-1,0,pieno,A;1,0,pieno,B;0.5,0.866,pieno,P;0,0,pieno,O" xmin=-1.4 xmax=1.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.3 uguale=1 assi=0 legenda=0 titolo="Triangle $APB$ inscribed in the semicircle with diameter $AB = 2$, in the position $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

a) A triangle inscribed in a semicircle with the diameter as one side is a right triangle, with its right angle at vertex $P$. In right triangle $APB$, the hypotenuse is $AB = 2$. The leg $AP$ is adjacent to the angle $x$, so $AP = AB\cos x = 2\cos x$. The leg $PB$ is opposite the angle $x$, so $PB = 2\sin x$. The point $P$ differs from $A$ and $B$, so the triangle exists for $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The perimeter is $2p(x) = 2 + 2\cos x + 2\sin x$.

b) Setting the perimeter equal to $3 + \sqrt{3}$, subtracting $2$, and dividing by $2$ gives a linear equation in sine and cosine. With the **auxiliary-angle method**, choosing $a = b = 1$ gives $r = \sqrt{2}$ and $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. Dividing by $\sqrt{2}$ and rationalizing gives $\dfrac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{6}}{4}$, which is the sine of $\dfrac{5\pi}{12}$, that is, of $75^\circ$, as the addition formula $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{6}\right)$ shows. The angles with this sine are $\dfrac{5\pi}{12} + 2k\pi$ and $\pi - \dfrac{5\pi}{12} + 2k\pi = \dfrac{7\pi}{12} + 2k\pi$. The condition $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is equivalent to $\dfrac{\pi}{4} < x + \dfrac{\pi}{4} < \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, so retain only $k = 0$. There are **two positions** of $P$, with $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and with $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, symmetric about the perpendicular bisector of $AB$.

c) The perimeter $5$ leads to $\sin x + \cos x = \dfrac{3}{2}$. A linear equation has solutions only if $c^2 \leq a^2 + b^2$, and here $\dfrac{9}{4} > 2$. Therefore **no position** of $P$ gives perimeter $5$. Since $\sin x + \cos x = \sqrt{2}\sin\left(x + \dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) \leq \sqrt{2}$, the maximum perimeter is $2 + 2\sqrt{2}$, attained for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

</div>
