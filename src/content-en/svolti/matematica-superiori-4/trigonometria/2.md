## Exercise 1

Find the area of triangle $ABC$ with $a = 12$, $b = 7$, and $\gamma = 150^\circ$.

$$
\sin 150^\circ = \sin\left( 180^\circ - 30^\circ \right) = \sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
S = \frac{1}{2}\,ab\sin\gamma = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 12 \cdot 7 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 21
$$

The area of the triangle is $21$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Angle $\gamma$ has its vertex at $C$, so it is included between sides $a = BC$ and $b = CA$, precisely the two known sides. We therefore apply the formula for the **area from two sides and the included angle**, $S = \dfrac{1}{2}\,ab\sin\gamma$.

The angle $150^\circ$ is obtuse. We find its sine using supplementary angles. Since $150^\circ = 180^\circ - 30^\circ$ and $\sin(180^\circ - x) = \sin x$, we have $\sin 150^\circ = \sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The sine of an obtuse angle is positive, so the area is also positive.

Substitution gives $S = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 84 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 21$. The formula holds for every measure of $\gamma$ between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. For an obtuse angle, the altitude to side $a$ falls on the extension of $a$. Using the sine therefore avoids having to construct the altitude. In this triangle, the altitude to $a$ has length $b\sin\gamma = \dfrac{7}{2}$, and indeed $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 12 \cdot \dfrac{7}{2} = 21$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A triangle has sides $b = 8$ and $c = 9$ and area $S = 18\sqrt{3}$. Find the angle $\alpha$ between the two sides and the third side $a$ for each angle obtained.

$$
\frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 9 \cdot \sin\alpha = 18\sqrt{3}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
36\sin\alpha = 18\sqrt{3}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
\alpha = 60^\circ \qquad \text{or} \qquad \alpha = 120^\circ
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha = 60^\circ : \quad a^2 &= 64 + 81 - 2 \cdot 8 \cdot 9 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 145 - 72 = 73 \\[6pt]
\alpha = 120^\circ : \quad a^2 &= 64 + 81 - 2 \cdot 8 \cdot 9 \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right) = 145 + 72 = 217
\end{aligned}
$$

There are two triangles. For $\alpha = 60^\circ$, the third side is $a = \sqrt{73}$. For $\alpha = 120^\circ$, it is $a = \sqrt{217}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Angle $\alpha$ is included between sides $b$ and $c$, so the area is $S = \dfrac{1}{2}\,bc\sin\alpha$. Equating this expression to the given value yields an equation in $\sin\alpha$, and dividing by $36$ gives $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

An angle of a triangle lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. On this interval, the sine equals $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ at two supplementary angles, $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$. Both angles are admissible because, for each one, we can construct a triangle with sides $8$ and $9$ and that included angle. The problem therefore has **two solutions**.

For each angle, find the third side using the **law of cosines**, since two sides and the included angle are known. With $\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we subtract the cross term, giving $a^2 = 73$. With $\cos 120^\circ = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, we add its magnitude, giving $a^2 = 217$. As expected, the triangle with the obtuse angle has the longer third side.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In quadrilateral $ABCD$, we have $AB = 5$, $BC = 3$, $CD = 5$, $DA = 8$, and $\widehat{ABC} = 120^\circ$. Find diagonal $AC$, angle $\delta = \widehat{CDA}$, and the area of the quadrilateral.

$$
AC^2 = 5^2 + 3^2 - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 3 \cos 120^\circ = 25 + 9 + 15 = 49
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
AC = 7
$$
$$
\cos\delta = \frac{DA^2 + CD^2 - AC^2}{2 \cdot DA \cdot CD} = \frac{64 + 25 - 49}{2 \cdot 8 \cdot 5} = \frac{40}{80} = \frac{1}{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\delta = 60^\circ
$$
$$
S_{ABC} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 5 \cdot 3 \cdot \sin 120^\circ = \frac{15\sqrt{3}}{4}
$$
$$
S_{ACD} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 5 \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 10\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
S = \frac{15\sqrt{3}}{4} + 10\sqrt{3} = \frac{55\sqrt{3}}{4} \approx 23.82
$$

The diagonal has length $7$, the angle at $D$ is $60^\circ$, and the area of the quadrilateral is $\dfrac{55\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Diagonal $AC$ divides the quadrilateral into two triangles, $ABC$ and $ACD$. In triangle $ABC$, we know sides $AB$ and $BC$ and the included angle at $B$, so we find $AC$ using the **law of cosines**. Since $\cos 120^\circ = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, the term $-2 \cdot 5 \cdot 3\cos 120^\circ$ equals $+15$, giving $AC = 7$.

In triangle $ACD$, all three sides $AC = 7$, $CD = 5$, and $DA = 8$ are now known. Angle $\delta$ is opposite side $AC$, so solving the law of cosines for its cosine gives $\cos\delta = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $\delta$ lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, the only value is $\delta = 60^\circ$.

The area of the quadrilateral is the sum of the areas of the two triangles. For each triangle, apply the formula for two sides and the included angle. In $ABC$, use sides $5$ and $3$ with the angle of $120^\circ$. In $ACD$, use sides $8$ and $5$ with the angle of $60^\circ$. Both sines equal $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and the two areas add up to $\dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{4} + \dfrac{40\sqrt{3}}{4} = \dfrac{55\sqrt{3}}{4}$.

The angles at $B$ and $D$ are supplementary, so the quadrilateral can be inscribed in a circle.

</div>
