## Exercise 1

An object of mass $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest on a frictionless inclined plane, held by a force parallel to the plane of magnitude $39.2\,\mathrm{N}$. Find the angle of inclination and the magnitude of the normal force exerted by the plane. Use $g = 9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
P = mg = 8 \cdot 9.8 = 78.4\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
P\sin\alpha = 39.2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin\alpha = \frac{39.2}{78.4} = \frac{1}{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\alpha = 30^\circ
$$
$$
N = P\cos\alpha = 78.4 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 39.2\sqrt{3} \approx 67.9\,\mathrm{N}
$$

The plane is inclined at $30^\circ$ and the normal force has magnitude $39.2\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$, approximately $67.9\,\mathrm{N}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces acting on the object are its weight, the normal force from the plane, perpendicular to the plane because there is no friction, and the force holding the object in place, parallel to the plane. Resolve the weight into the component parallel to the plane, $P_\parallel = P\sin\alpha$, and the perpendicular component, $P_\perp = P\cos\alpha$, as for the **object on an inclined plane**. The two formulas follow from the first right-triangle theorem because the weight is the hypotenuse of the right triangle whose legs are the two components, and the angle between the weight and its perpendicular component equals $\alpha$.

The object is at rest, so the forces along the plane balance. The force holding it therefore has the same magnitude as $P_\parallel$, giving the equation $78.4\sin\alpha = 39.2$. The angle of inclination is acute and $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $\alpha = 30^\circ$.

The forces perpendicular to the plane also balance, so the normal force $N$ equals the magnitude of the perpendicular component of the weight. With $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, we obtain $N = 39.2\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two forces of magnitudes $F_1 = 6\,\mathrm{N}$ and $F_2 = 10\,\mathrm{N}$ act at the same point and have a resultant of magnitude $R = 14\,\mathrm{N}$. Find the angle $\theta$ between the forces and the angle $\varphi$ that the resultant makes with $\vec F_1$.

$$
R^2 = F_1^2 + F_2^2 + 2F_1F_2\cos\theta
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
196 = 36 + 100 + 120\cos\theta
$$
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{196 - 136}{120} = \frac{1}{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\theta = 60^\circ
$$
$$
\sin\varphi = \frac{F_2\sin\left( 180^\circ - \theta \right)}{R} = \frac{10 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{14} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{14}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\varphi \approx 38.21^\circ
$$

The forces make an angle of $60^\circ$, and the resultant makes an angle with $\vec F_1$ of approximately $38.21^\circ$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnitude of the resultant of two forces making an angle $\theta$ is given by the formula for the **resultant of two forces**, which follows from applying the law of cosines to the triangle with sides $F_1$, $F_2$, and $R$. Here the unknown is $\theta$. Substituting the data gives $120\cos\theta = 60$, so $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The angle between two forces lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, and the only angle in that interval with cosine $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is $60^\circ$.

In the force triangle, side $F_2$ is opposite angle $\varphi$, and side $R$ is opposite angle $180^\circ - \theta = 120^\circ$. By the **law of sines**, $\dfrac{F_2}{\sin\varphi} = \dfrac{R}{\sin 120^\circ}$, giving $\sin\varphi = \dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{14}$.

The value of $\sin\varphi$ corresponds to two supplementary angles. Angle $\varphi$ is opposite side $F_2 = 10$, which is shorter than side $R = 14$ opposite the $120^\circ$ angle. Therefore $\varphi < 120^\circ$, and since the triangle already contains an obtuse angle, $\varphi$ is acute. Thus $\varphi = \arcsin\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{14} \approx 38.21^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A lamp of weight $P = 40\,\mathrm{N}$ hangs at point $L$ from two strings attached to the ceiling. With the horizontal line through $L$, the strings make angles of $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. Find the tensions $T_1$ and $T_2$ in the two strings.

$$
\begin{cases}
T_1\cos 30^\circ = T_2\cos 60^\circ \\[6pt]
T_1\sin 30^\circ + T_2\sin 60^\circ = 40
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,T_1 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,T_2 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,T_1 + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,T_2 = 40
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T_2 = \sqrt{3}\,T_1
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{1}{2}\,T_1 + \frac{3}{2}\,T_1 = 40
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
T_1 = 20\,\mathrm{N} ,
\quad
T_2 = 20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 34.6\,\mathrm{N}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 curve="0+0.5*cos(t),0+0.5*sin(t),2.618,3.142;0+0.5*cos(t),0+0.5*sin(t),0,1.047" linee="-4.2,2 2,2;-3.464,2 0,0;0,0 1.155,2" segmenti="-2,0,2,0" vettori="0,0,-0.866,0.5,T₁;0,0,0.866,1.5,T₂;0,0,0,-2,P" etichette="-1.3,0.15,30°;0.866,0.5,60°;0.3,-0.3,L" xmin=-4.3 xmax=2.3 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.5 uguale=1 titolo="The tensions in the two strings and the weight of the lamp, all acting at point $L$"}

The tensions are $T_1 = 20\,\mathrm{N}$ in the string inclined at $30^\circ$ and $T_2 = 20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$ in the string inclined at $60^\circ$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The lamp is in equilibrium, so the forces acting at $L$ add up to zero. Resolve each tension into its horizontal and vertical **components** using $F_x = F\cos\theta$ and $F_y = F\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle the string makes with the horizontal. The weight has only a vertical component, directed downward.

The horizontal components of the tensions point in opposite directions and must cancel, and their vertical components must balance the weight. Both conditions must hold, so they form a system with unknowns $T_1$ and $T_2$.

Multiplying the first equation by $2$ gives $T_2 = \sqrt{3}\,T_1$. On substituting into the second, the term $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \sqrt{3}\,T_1$ equals $\dfrac{3}{2}\,T_1$, so $2T_1 = 40$ and $T_1 = 20\,\mathrm{N}$.

The two strings make an angle of $180^\circ - 30^\circ - 60^\circ = 90^\circ$ with each other. Therefore the force triangle has a right angle and hypotenuse $P$, and the first right-triangle theorem gives the same result, namely $T_1 = 40\sin 30^\circ$ and $T_2 = 40\cos 30^\circ$. The string closer to vertical, inclined at $60^\circ$, has the greater tension.

</div>
