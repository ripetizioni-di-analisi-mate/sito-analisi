## Exercise 1

Solve triangle $ABC$, which has a right angle at $A$, given that its hypotenuse has length $a = 20$ and $\beta = 30^\circ$. Then find the area of the triangle.

$$
\gamma = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ
$$
$$
b = a\sin\beta = 20 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 10
$$
$$
c = a\cos\beta = 20 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 10\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
S = \frac{1}{2}\,bc = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 10\sqrt{3} = 50\sqrt{3}
$$

The triangle has angles $90^\circ$, $30^\circ$, and $60^\circ$, legs $b = 10$ and $c = 10\sqrt{3}$, and area $50\sqrt{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The hypotenuse and an acute angle are known, so this is the first case of **solving right triangles**. The acute angles of a right triangle are complementary because the angles add up to $180^\circ$ and the angle at $A$ is a right angle. Therefore $\gamma = 60^\circ$.

Leg $b = CA$ is opposite angle $\beta$. By the **first right-triangle theorem**, it therefore equals the hypotenuse times the sine of $\beta$, and since $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we obtain $b = 10$. Leg $c = AB$ is adjacent to angle $\beta$, so it equals the hypotenuse times the cosine of $\beta$. With $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, we obtain $c = 10\sqrt{3}$.

Apply the Pythagorean theorem as a check. We find $b^2 + c^2 = 100 + 300 = 400 = a^2$, so the three side lengths are consistent.

In a right triangle, one leg serves as the base and the other as the height, so the area is half the product of the legs, $S = 50\sqrt{3}$. The formula $S = \dfrac{1}{2}\,bc\sin\alpha$ gives the same value, since $\sin 90^\circ = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve triangle $ABC$, which has a right angle at $A$, given that leg $b = CA$ has length $6$ and $\beta = 40^\circ$. Round the results to the nearest hundredth.

$$
\gamma = 90^\circ - 40^\circ = 50^\circ
$$
$$
c = b\cot\beta = \frac{b}{\tan\beta} = \frac{6}{\tan 40^\circ} \approx \frac{6}{0.8391} \approx 7.15
$$
$$
a = \frac{b}{\sin\beta} = \frac{6}{\sin 40^\circ} \approx \frac{6}{0.6428} \approx 9.33
$$

The triangle has $\gamma = 50^\circ$, $c \approx 7.15$, and hypotenuse $a \approx 9.33$.

<div class="spiegazione">

One leg and one acute angle are known, so this is the second case for solving right triangles. Angle $\gamma$ is the complement of $\beta$, so $\gamma = 50^\circ$.

The known leg $b$ is opposite $\beta$, and the unknown leg $c$ is adjacent to $\beta$. By the **second right-triangle theorem**, $b = c\tan\beta$. Dividing by $\tan\beta$, which is nonzero because $\beta$ is acute, gives $c = \dfrac{b}{\tan\beta}$, that is, $c = b\cot\beta$. The angle $40^\circ$ is not a special angle, so we use a calculator in degree mode to find $\tan 40^\circ$.

To find the hypotenuse, use the **first theorem**, which states that leg $b$ equals the hypotenuse times the sine of the opposite angle, namely $b = a\sin\beta$. Dividing by $\sin\beta$ gives $a = \dfrac{b}{\sin\beta} \approx 9.33$.

Checking with the Pythagorean theorem gives $6^2 + 7.15^2 \approx 36 + 51.12 = 87.12$ and $9.33^2 \approx 87.05$. The small difference is due to rounding to the nearest hundredth. Moreover, hypotenuse $a$ is longer than both legs.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Triangle $ABC$ has a right angle at $A$, hypotenuse $a = 25$, and leg $b = 7$. Find leg $c$, the acute angles in degrees to the nearest hundredth, and the altitude $AH$ to the hypotenuse.

$$
c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{625 - 49} = \sqrt{576} = 24
$$
$$
\sin\beta = \frac{b}{a} = \frac{7}{25}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\beta = \arcsin\frac{7}{25} \approx 16.26^\circ
$$
$$
\gamma = 90^\circ - \beta \approx 73.74^\circ
$$
$$
AH = c\sin\beta = 24 \cdot \frac{7}{25} = \frac{168}{25} = 6.72
$$

The leg is $c = 24$, the acute angles are $\beta \approx 16.26^\circ$ and $\gamma \approx 73.74^\circ$, and the altitude to the hypotenuse is $\dfrac{168}{25}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The hypotenuse and one leg are known, so the **Pythagorean theorem** gives the missing leg through $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$, yielding $c = 24$.

Leg $b$ is opposite angle $\beta$. By the first right-triangle theorem, $b = a\sin\beta$, so $\sin\beta = \dfrac{7}{25}$. Angle $\beta$ is acute, and the arcsine of a number between $0$ and $1$ is an angle between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$. Thus $\beta$ is precisely $\arcsin\dfrac{7}{25}$, which a calculator gives as approximately $16.26^\circ$. Angle $\gamma$ is its complement.

Altitude $AH$ divides the triangle into two right triangles. Triangle $ABH$ has a right angle at $H$, hypotenuse $AB = c = 24$, and angle $\beta$ at vertex $B$. Leg $AH$ is opposite $\beta$. The first theorem therefore gives $AH = c\sin\beta$, with $\sin\beta = \dfrac{7}{25}$ already known, and the exact result is $AH = \dfrac{168}{25}$.

The same result follows by equating two expressions for the area, $\dfrac{1}{2}\,bc = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a \cdot AH$, which gives $AH = \dfrac{bc}{a} = \dfrac{7 \cdot 24}{25}$.

</div>
