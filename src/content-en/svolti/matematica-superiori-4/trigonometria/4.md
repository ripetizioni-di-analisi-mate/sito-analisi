## Exercise 1

Solve triangle $ABC$ given $a = 3$, $b = 8$, and $\gamma = 60^\circ$. Round the angles to the nearest hundredth of a degree.

$$
c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos\gamma = 9 + 64 - 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 8 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 73 - 24 = 49
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
c = 7
$$
$$
\cos\beta = \frac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac} = \frac{9 + 49 - 64}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7} = -\frac{6}{42} = -\frac{1}{7}
$$
$$
\beta = \arccos\left( -\frac{1}{7} \right) \approx 98.21^\circ
$$
$$
\alpha = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - \beta \approx 21.79^\circ
$$

The third side is $c = 7$. The angles are $\alpha \approx 21.79^\circ$, $\beta \approx 98.21^\circ$, and $\gamma = 60^\circ$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Two sides and the included angle are known, since the vertex of $\gamma$ is $C$, where sides $a = BC$ and $b = CA$ meet. This is therefore the second of the four cases for solving triangles. Find the third side using the form of the **law of cosines** that contains $\cos\gamma$. The result $c^2 = 49$ gives $c = 7$, since a length is positive.

The longest side is $b = 8$, so the largest angle is $\beta$, the one that may be obtuse. For this reason, we find $\beta$ from its cosine given all three sides, which distinguishes acute from obtuse angles. The numerator $9 + 49 - 64$ is negative, that is, $b^2 > a^2 + c^2$, so $\beta$ is **obtuse**. The arccosine of $-\dfrac{1}{7}$ lies between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$ and is approximately $98.21^\circ$.

Find the third angle by subtraction, since the angles add up to $180^\circ$. The angle measuring $\alpha \approx 21.79^\circ$ is the smallest and is opposite the shortest side $a = 3$. If we had used the law of sines to find $\beta$, the calculator would have returned the acute angle $\arcsin\dfrac{8\sin 60^\circ}{7} \approx 81.79^\circ$, the supplement of the correct angle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that $5$, $6$, and $7$ are the side lengths of a triangle. Find the angles to the nearest hundredth of a degree and calculate the area.

$$
7 < 5 + 6
$$
$$
\cos\gamma = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab} = \frac{25 + 36 - 49}{2 \cdot 5 \cdot 6} = \frac{12}{60} = \frac{1}{5}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\gamma \approx 78.46^\circ
$$
$$
\cos\alpha = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} = \frac{36 + 49 - 25}{2 \cdot 6 \cdot 7} = \frac{60}{84} = \frac{5}{7}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\alpha \approx 44.42^\circ
$$
$$
\beta = 180^\circ - \alpha - \gamma \approx 57.12^\circ
$$
$$
\sin\gamma = \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{25}} = \sqrt{\frac{24}{25}} = \frac{2\sqrt{6}}{5}
$$
$$
S = \frac{1}{2}\,ab\sin\gamma = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 5 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{2\sqrt{6}}{5} = 6\sqrt{6} \approx 14.70
$$

The angles are $\alpha \approx 44.42^\circ$, $\beta \approx 57.12^\circ$, and $\gamma \approx 78.46^\circ$, and the area is $6\sqrt{6}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

For the **triangle inequality**, it suffices to check the longest side. The side of length $7$ is shorter than the sum $5 + 6 = 11$, so the three numbers are the sides of a triangle. Set $a = 5$, $b = 6$, and $c = 7$.

First find angle $\gamma$, opposite the longest side, using the formula for the **cosine of an angle given the three sides**. Its cosine is positive, so $\gamma$ is acute. Since it is the largest angle, the triangle is acute. The same method gives $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{5}{7}$. Find the third angle by subtraction.

For the area, we need the sine of one angle. Angle $\gamma$ lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, so its sine is positive and can be found using the fundamental identity $\sin^2\gamma + \cos^2\gamma = 1$. The exact value $\sin\gamma = \dfrac{2\sqrt{6}}{5}$ gives the exact area without using the rounded angle.

Angle $\gamma$ is included between sides $a$ and $b$, so the area is $S = \dfrac{1}{2}\,ab\sin\gamma = 6\sqrt{6}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Parallelogram $ABCD$ has sides $AB = 5$ and $AD = 8$ and angle $\widehat{DAB} = 60^\circ$. Find the lengths of both diagonals.

$$
BD^2 = AB^2 + AD^2 - 2 \cdot AB \cdot AD \cos 60^\circ = 25 + 64 - 40 = 49
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
BD = 7
$$
$$
\widehat{ABC} = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ ,
\qquad
BC = AD = 8
$$
$$
AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 - 2 \cdot AB \cdot BC \cos 120^\circ = 25 + 64 + 40 = 129
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
AC = \sqrt{129} \approx 11.36
$$

::grafico{assi=0 curve="0+0.5*cos(t),0+0.5*sin(t),0,1.047;2.5+0.4*cos(t),0+0.4*sin(t),1.047,3.142" linee="0,0 2.5,0 4.5,3.464 2,3.464 0,0" segmenti="2.5,0,2,3.464;0,0,4.5,3.464" etichette="0.86,0.28,60°;1.93,0.48,120°;-0.357,-0.274,A;2.564,-0.445,B;4.857,3.739,C;1.936,3.909,D;1.05,-0.346,5;0.6,1.732,8" xmin=-1 xmax=5.5 ymin=-0.8 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="The diagonals $BD$ and $AC$ of the parallelogram are the third sides of triangles $ABD$ and $ABC$"}

The diagonal lengths are $BD = 7$ and $AC = \sqrt{129}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Diagonal $BD$ is the third side of triangle $ABD$, whose sides $AB$ and $AD$ and included angle at $A$ are known. By the **law of cosines**, $BD^2 = 25 + 64 - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 8 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 49$, so $BD = 7$.

Diagonal $AC$ is the third side of triangle $ABC$. Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal, so $BC = AD = 8$, and the angles adjacent to the same side are supplementary, so the angle at $B$ equals $120^\circ$. The law of cosines now involves $\cos 120^\circ = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the cross term is added and $AC^2 = 129$.

Diagonal $AC$ is opposite the obtuse angle and is the longer diagonal, while $BD$ is opposite the acute angle. The sum of the squares of the diagonals is $49 + 129 = 178$, which equals $2\left( 5^2 + 8^2 \right)$ because the two terms involving the cosine are opposites and cancel.

The same construction gives the magnitude of the resultant of two forces, represented by the diagonal of the parallelogram formed by the forces.

</div>
