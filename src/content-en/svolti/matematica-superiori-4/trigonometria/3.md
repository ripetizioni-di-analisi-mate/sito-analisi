## Exercise 1

Solve triangle $ABC$ given $c = 6$, $\alpha = 45^\circ$, and $\beta = 105^\circ$. Also calculate the radius $R$ of its circumcircle.

$$
\gamma = 180^\circ - 45^\circ - 105^\circ = 30^\circ
$$
$$
a = \frac{c\sin\alpha}{\sin\gamma} = \frac{6 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\dfrac{1}{2}} = 6\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\sin 105^\circ = \sin 75^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}
$$
$$
b = \frac{c\sin\beta}{\sin\gamma} = 12 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} = 3\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right) \approx 11.59
$$
$$
2R = \frac{c}{\sin\gamma} = \frac{6}{\dfrac{1}{2}} = 12
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
R = 6
$$

The triangle has $\gamma = 30^\circ$, $a = 6\sqrt{2}$, and $b = 3\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right)$, and its circumcircle has radius $6$.

<div class="spiegazione">

One side and two angles are known, so this is the first of the **four cases for solving triangles**. Subtracting the known angles from $180^\circ$ gives the third angle, $\gamma = 30^\circ$. The known side $c$ is opposite $\gamma$, so the side and opposite angle $c$ and $\gamma$ are both known, and we can apply the **law of sines**.

The proportion $\dfrac{a}{\sin\alpha} = \dfrac{c}{\sin\gamma}$ gives $a = \dfrac{c\sin\alpha}{\sin\gamma}$. The quotient $\dfrac{6}{\dfrac{1}{2}}$ equals $12$, so $a = 12 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 6\sqrt{2}$.

To find side $b$, we need $\sin 105^\circ$. Angles $105^\circ$ and $75^\circ$ are supplementary, so they have the same sine, and $\sin 75^\circ = \sin\left( 45^\circ + 30^\circ \right)$ can be found using the addition formula, as shown in the chapter on trigonometric identities. This gives $b \approx 11.59$. Side $b$ is the longest and is opposite the largest angle $\beta$, in agreement with the ordering of the sides.

The common ratio in the law of sines is the diameter $2R$ of the circumcircle. Since this ratio equals $12$, the radius is $R = 6$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a circle of radius $6$, chord $AB$ has length $6\sqrt{3}$. Find the inscribed angles subtended by $AB$, the corresponding central angle, and the distance from the chord to the center $O$.

$$
AB = 2r\sin\alpha
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin\alpha = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{12} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
\alpha = 60^\circ \qquad \text{or} \qquad \alpha = 120^\circ
$$
$$
\widehat{AOB} = 2 \cdot 60^\circ = 120^\circ
$$
$$
OH = r\cos 60^\circ = 6 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 3
$$

The inscribed angles are $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$, the central angle is $120^\circ$, and the chord is at a distance of $3$ from the center.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **chord length theorem**, a chord length equals the diameter times the sine of an inscribed angle subtended by the chord. The diameter is $12$, so $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

The inscribed angles subtended by the chord have their vertices on one of the two arcs $AB$. The angles with vertices on the major arc are acute, those with vertices on the minor arc are obtuse, and the two values are supplementary. Therefore the angles are $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$, both with sine $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

The central angle that intercepts the minor arc $AB$ is twice the acute inscribed angle that intercepts the same arc, so it equals $120^\circ$.

Let $H$ be the midpoint of $AB$. Triangle $AOB$ is isosceles because $OA = OB = 6$, so its median $OH$ is also an altitude and an angle bisector. Thus triangle $AOH$ has a right angle at $H$, hypotenuse $OA = 6$, and an angle at $O$ of measure $60^\circ$. By the first right-triangle theorem, leg $OH$, adjacent to the $60^\circ$ angle, equals $6\cos 60^\circ = 3$. As a check, $AH = 6\sin 60^\circ = 3\sqrt{3}$, which is half the chord length.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Triangle $ABC$ is inscribed in a circle of radius $R = 5$, with $\alpha = 30^\circ$ and $\beta = 45^\circ$. Find its side lengths, perimeter, and area.

$$
a = 2R\sin\alpha = 10 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 5
$$
$$
b = 2R\sin\beta = 10 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 5\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\gamma = 180^\circ - 30^\circ - 45^\circ = 105^\circ ,
\qquad
c = 2R\sin\gamma = 10 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} = \frac{5\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right)}{2}
$$
$$
2p = 5 + 5\sqrt{2} + \frac{5\sqrt{6} + 5\sqrt{2}}{2} = 5 + \frac{15\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{5\sqrt{6}}{2} \approx 21.73
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S = \frac{abc}{4R} &= \frac{5 \cdot 5\sqrt{2} \cdot 5\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right)}{2 \cdot 20} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{25\left( \sqrt{12} + 2 \right)}{8} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{25\left( 2\sqrt{3} + 2 \right)}{8} = \frac{25\left( \sqrt{3} + 1 \right)}{4} \approx 17.08
\end{aligned}
$$

The sides are $a = 5$, $b = 5\sqrt{2}$, and $c = \dfrac{5\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right)}{2}$. The perimeter is approximately $21.73$ and the area is $\dfrac{25\left( \sqrt{3} + 1 \right)}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The **law of sines** states that each side equals $2R$ times the sine of the opposite angle. The radius is known, so we can find each side directly from its opposite angle, $a$ from $\alpha$ and $b$ from $\beta$.

The third angle is $\gamma = 105^\circ$, which is supplementary to $75^\circ$. Therefore $\sin 105^\circ = \sin 75^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$, and side $c$ is the longest, as expected from the ordering of the sides.

The perimeter is the sum of the three sides. Write $c$ as $\dfrac{5\sqrt{6}}{2} + \dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and combine the terms involving $\sqrt{2}$, that is, $5\sqrt{2} + \dfrac{5\sqrt{2}}{2} = \dfrac{15\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

For the area, use the formula for the **radius of the circumcircle**, $R = \dfrac{abc}{4S}$, solved for $S$. The product $\sqrt{2}\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right)$ equals $\sqrt{12} + 2 = 2\sqrt{3} + 2$, and canceling the factor $2$ gives $S = \dfrac{25\left( \sqrt{3} + 1 \right)}{4}$. The area formula using two sides and the included angle gives the same value, since $\dfrac{1}{2}\,ab\sin\gamma = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 25\sqrt{2} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} = \dfrac{25\left( \sqrt{12} + 2 \right)}{8}$.

</div>
