## Exercise 1

In triangle $ABC$, we have $AB = 6$, $AC = 4$, and $\widehat{BAC} = x$. Find $x$ such that $BC = 2\sqrt{7}$ and calculate the area of the corresponding triangle.

$$
0 < x < \pi
$$
$$
BC^2 = 36 + 16 - 2 \cdot 6 \cdot 4\cos x = 52 - 48\cos x
$$
$$
52 - 48\cos x = 28
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\cos x = \frac{24}{48} = \frac{1}{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
S = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 6 \cdot 4 \cdot \sin\frac{\pi}{3} = 12 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 6\sqrt{3}
$$

The condition holds for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and the triangle has area $6\sqrt{3}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown is the angle at $A$ in a triangle, so the **restrictions** are $0 < x < \pi$. At the endpoints the triangle degenerates into a line segment.

Side $BC$ is opposite angle $x$, which is included between the known sides $AB$ and $AC$. The **law of cosines** therefore gives $BC^2 = 52 - 48\cos x$, which determines the side length in terms of $x$. The condition $BC = 2\sqrt{7}$ is equivalent to $BC^2 = 28$ because $BC$ is positive, and gives the elementary trigonometric equation $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The solutions of $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ in $\mathbb{R}$ are $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. On the interval $(0, \pi)$, the cosine is strictly decreasing, so it takes the value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ only once, at $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The solution satisfies the restrictions and is unique.

Use the formula for the **area from two sides and the included angle** to obtain $6\sqrt{3}$. The problem has only one solution because the three side lengths $6$, $4$, and $2\sqrt{7}$ determine a unique triangle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A semicircle has diameter $AB = 2r$. From a point $P$ on the semicircle, draw the perpendicular $PH$ to the diameter. Set $x = \widehat{PAB}$ and find $x$ such that $AH + PH = 2r$.

$$
0 < x < \frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
AP = 2r\cos x ,
\qquad
AH = AP\cos x = 2r\cos^2 x ,
\qquad
PH = AP\sin x = 2r\sin x\cos x
$$
$$
2r\cos^2 x + 2r\sin x\cos x = 2r
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\cos^2 x + \sin x\cos x = \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x
$$
$$
\sin x\cos x - \sin^2 x = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin x\,(\cos x - \sin x) = 0
$$
$$
\sin x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad \tan x = 1
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x = \frac{\pi}{4}
$$

The condition holds when $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, that is, when $P$ is the midpoint of the semicircular arc.

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $P$ must be distinct from $A$ and $B$ for triangle $ABP$ to exist, so angle $x$ satisfies the restrictions $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Triangle $ABP$ has a right angle at $P$ because the angle at $P$ intercepts a semicircle, as in the case of the **triangle inscribed in a semicircle**. Leg $AP$ is adjacent to angle $x$, so the first right-triangle theorem gives $AP = 2r\cos x$. Triangle $APH$ also has a right angle, at $H$, with hypotenuse $AP$ and angle $x$ at $A$. By the same theorem, the adjacent leg is $AH = AP\cos x$ and the opposite leg is $PH = AP\sin x$.

The condition gives an equation in $\sin x$ and $\cos x$. Divide by $2r$ and write the right-hand side $1$ as $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x$. The $\cos^2 x$ terms cancel, leaving $\sin x\cos x - \sin^2 x = 0$. Factoring out $\sin x$ gives a product equal to zero.

The factor $\sin x$ cannot vanish under the restrictions because $x$ is acute. The second factor vanishes when $\sin x = \cos x$. Since $\cos x \neq 0$ for acute $x$, we can divide by $\cos x$ to obtain $\tan x = 1$, that is, $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + k\pi$, with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. The only value within the restrictions is $x = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. In this case $AH = PH = r$, and indeed $H$ coincides with the center.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In triangle $ABC$, side $BC$ has length $2$ and the angle at $A$ measures $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$. Set $x = \widehat{ABC}$.

a) Write down the restrictions on $x$.

b) Express sides $AC$ and $AB$ in terms of $x$.

c) Prove that the perimeter is $2p(x) = 2 + 4\cos\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$.

d) Find $x$ such that the perimeter equals $2 + 2\sqrt{3}$.

e) Find the maximum perimeter and the corresponding triangle.

$$
\text{a)} \qquad \widehat{ACB} = \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} - x = \frac{2\pi}{3} - x ,
\qquad
0 < x < \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$
$$
\text{b)} \qquad 2R = \frac{BC}{\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}} = \frac{2}{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}} ,
\qquad
AC = \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\sin x ,
\qquad
AB = \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\sin\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} - x \right)
$$
$$
\text{c)} \qquad \sin\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} - x \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x + \frac{1}{2}\sin x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2p(x) &= 2 + \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\left( \sin x + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x + \frac{1}{2}\sin x \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2 + \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}\left( \frac{3}{2}\sin x + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cos x \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2 + 2\sqrt{3}\sin x + 2\cos x \\[6pt]
&= 2 + 4\left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin x + \frac{1}{2}\cos x \right) = 2 + 4\cos\left( x - \frac{\pi}{3} \right)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{d)} \qquad 2 + 4\cos\left( x - \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = 2 + 2\sqrt{3}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\cos\left( x - \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$
$$
x - \frac{\pi}{3} = \pm\frac{\pi}{6}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x = \frac{\pi}{6} \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
\text{e)} \qquad 2p(x) \leq 2 + 4 = 6 ,
\qquad
2p = 6 \iff x = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$

The restrictions are $0 < x < \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. The perimeter equals $2 + 2\sqrt{3}$ for $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Its maximum value is $6$, for the equilateral triangle of side $2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

a) The angles add up to $\pi$, so the angle at $C$ is $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - x$. The angles at $B$ and $C$ must both be positive, hence $x > 0$ and $x < \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

b) Side $BC$ and the opposite angle $A$ are known, so the ratio in the **law of sines** is known and equals $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{3}}$. Side $AC$ is opposite angle $x$ and side $AB$ is opposite the angle at $C$. Each side equals $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{3}}$ times the sine of its opposite angle.

c) Expand the sine of $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} - x$ with the angle difference formula, using $\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos\dfrac{2\pi}{3} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Collect the terms in $\sin x$ and multiply by $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{3}}$ to obtain $2\sqrt{3}\sin x + 2\cos x$. Finally, factor out $4$. The coefficients $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ are $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and by the cosine difference formula, $\cos x\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} + \sin x\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \cos\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right)$.

d) The equation reduces to $\cos t = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ with $t = x - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. The restrictions give $-\dfrac{\pi}{3} < t < \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, and in this interval the solutions are $t = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Both values of $x$ are **admissible**. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the triangle has a right angle at $B$. For $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, it has a right angle at $C$.

e) The cosine is at most $1$, so $2p(x) \leq 6$. The value $6$ is attained when $\cos\left( x - \dfrac{\pi}{3} \right) = 1$, that is, when $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, which satisfies the restrictions. All angles then equal $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and the triangle is **equilateral**, with side $2$.

</div>
