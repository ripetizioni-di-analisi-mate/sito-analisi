## Exercise 1

A person whose eyes are $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground stands $15\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base of a building on level ground. The angle of elevation to the top of the building is $60^\circ$. Find the height of the building.

$$
x = 15\tan 60^\circ = 15\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
h = 1.5 + 15\sqrt{3} \approx 1.5 + 25.98 = 27.48
$$

The height of the building is $1.5 + 15\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$, or approximately $27.48\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle of elevation is measured from the horizontal line through the observer's eyes. This line meets the wall of the building at a point $K$ located $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Consider the triangle whose vertices are the observer's eyes, point $K$, and the top of the building. It has a right angle at $K$ because the wall is vertical and the reference line of sight is horizontal. The horizontal leg is the distance to the building, $15\,\mathrm{m}$, and the angle opposite the vertical leg is the angle of elevation.

The vertical leg $x$ is the part of the building above eye level. By the **second right-triangle theorem**, it equals the other leg times the tangent of the opposite angle, giving $x = 15\tan 60^\circ = 15\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$.

The building's height is the sum of $x$ and the observer's eye height above the ground, since point $K$ lies $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ above the base. Neglecting the observer's eye height would give an error of exactly $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

From the top $T$ of a lighthouse of height $40\,\mathrm{m}$, two boats $M$ and $N$, in line with the base $O$ of the lighthouse and on the same side of it, are seen at angles of depression of $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. Find the distance between the boats.

$$
OM = \frac{OT}{\tan 45^\circ} = \frac{40}{1} = 40
$$
$$
ON = \frac{OT}{\tan 30^\circ} = \frac{40}{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}} = \frac{120}{\sqrt{3}} = 40\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
MN = ON - OM = 40\sqrt{3} - 40 = 40\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right) \approx 29.28
$$

::grafico{assi=0 curve="0+0.35*cos(t),2+0.35*sin(t),-0.785,0;0+1.1*cos(t),2+1.1*sin(t),-0.524,0" linee="-0.5,0 4,0;0,0 0,2;0,2 2,0;0,2 3.464,0;0.2,0 0.2,0.2 0,0.2" segmenti="0,2,4,2" punti="2,0,pieno;3.464,0,pieno" etichette="0.744,1.692,45°;1.7,1.544,30°;-0.3,-0.3,O;-0.3,2.1,T;2,-0.35,M;3.464,-0.35,N;-0.45,1,40 m" xmin=-1 xmax=4.2 ymin=-0.7 ymax=2.6 uguale=1 titolo="From the top $T$ of the lighthouse, boats $M$ and $N$ are seen at angles of depression of $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$"}

The distance between the boats is $40\left( \sqrt{3} - 1 \right)\,\mathrm{m}$, or approximately $29.28\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle of depression of a boat is the angle between the horizontal line through $T$ and the line of sight to the boat. The horizontal line through $T$ is parallel to line $OM$ at sea level, and the line of sight $TM$ is a transversal, so the angle of depression equals angle $\widehat{TMO}$ because these are alternate interior angles. The same holds for $N$.

Triangle $TOM$ has a right angle at $O$ because the lighthouse is vertical. Leg $OT$ is opposite the angle at $M$, so by the **second right-triangle theorem**, $OT = OM\tan 45^\circ$. Dividing by $\tan 45^\circ = 1$ gives $OM = 40\,\mathrm{m}$.

Similarly, in triangle $TON$, we have $OT = ON\tan 30^\circ$, so $ON = \dfrac{40}{\tan 30^\circ}$. Since $\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, the quotient equals $\dfrac{120}{\sqrt{3}}$, and rationalizing the denominator gives $40\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$.

The boats are on the same side of the lighthouse, so the distance between them is the difference of their distances from its base. The boat seen at the smaller angle of depression is farther away.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two points $P$ and $Q$ lie on the opposite bank of a river. On the near bank, a baseline $AB = 60\,\mathrm{m}$ parallel to the bank is established, and the angles $\widehat{PAB} = 75^\circ$, $\widehat{QAB} = 45^\circ$, $\widehat{PBA} = 45^\circ$, and $\widehat{QBA} = 75^\circ$ are measured. Find $AP$, $AQ$, and the distance $PQ$.

$$
\widehat{APB} = 180^\circ - 75^\circ - 45^\circ = 60^\circ ,
\qquad
AP = \frac{AB\sin 45^\circ}{\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{60 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} = \frac{60\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}} = 20\sqrt{6}
$$
$$
\widehat{AQB} = 180^\circ - 45^\circ - 75^\circ = 60^\circ ,
\qquad
AQ = \frac{AB\sin 75^\circ}{\sin 60^\circ} = 60 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = 30\sqrt{2} + 10\sqrt{6}
$$
$$
\widehat{PAQ} = 75^\circ - 45^\circ = 30^\circ
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ^2 &= AP^2 + AQ^2 - 2 \cdot AP \cdot AQ\cos 30^\circ \\[6pt]
&= 2400 + \left( 2400 + 1200\sqrt{3} \right) - 2 \cdot 20\sqrt{6}\left( 30\sqrt{2} + 10\sqrt{6} \right) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\[6pt]
&= 4800 + 1200\sqrt{3} - \left( 3600 + 1200\sqrt{3} \right) = 1200
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
PQ = \sqrt{1200} = 20\sqrt{3} \approx 34.64
$$

We obtain $AP = 20\sqrt{6} \approx 48.99\,\mathrm{m}$, $AQ = 30\sqrt{2} + 10\sqrt{6} \approx 66.92\,\mathrm{m}$, and $PQ = 20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 34.64\,\mathrm{m}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In triangles $ABP$ and $ABQ$, one side, the baseline $AB$, and both adjacent angles are known. Find the third angle by subtraction, giving $60^\circ$ in both cases, then apply the **law of sines**. In triangle $ABP$, side $AP$ is opposite the angle at $B$, which measures $45^\circ$. In triangle $ABQ$, side $AQ$ is opposite the angle at $B$, which measures $75^\circ$. For $\sin 75^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2}}{4}$, see the chapter on trigonometric identities. The product $\dfrac{30\left( \sqrt{6} + \sqrt{2} \right)}{\sqrt{3}}$ simplifies on dividing each term by $\sqrt{3}$, giving $30\sqrt{2} + 10\sqrt{6}$.

In triangle $APQ$, two sides and the included angle $\widehat{PAQ}$ are now known. This angle is the difference between $\widehat{PAB}$ and $\widehat{QAB}$ because ray $AQ$ lies inside angle $\widehat{PAB}$. Apply the **law of cosines**. The squares are $AP^2 = 400 \cdot 6 = 2400$ and $AQ^2 = 1800 + 600 + 2 \cdot 300\sqrt{12} = 2400 + 1200\sqrt{3}$. For the cross term, calculate $20\sqrt{6} \cdot 30\sqrt{2} = 600\sqrt{12} = 1200\sqrt{3}$ and $20\sqrt{6} \cdot 10\sqrt{6} = 1200$. Multiplying their sum by $2 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$ gives $3600 + 1200\sqrt{3}$. The terms involving $\sqrt{3}$ cancel, and $PQ^2 = 1200$.

The figure is symmetric about the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ because the angles at $A$ and $B$ are interchanged. Therefore $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$.

</div>
