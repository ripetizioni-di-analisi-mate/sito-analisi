## Exercise 1

In $60$ independent draws, three categories occur $18$, $24$, and $18$ times. Use Pearson's test for equal category probabilities at level $0.05$. Use $\chi^2_{2,0.95}=5.991$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1=E_2=E_3&=n\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{60}{3}=20,\\[6pt]
Q&=\frac{(18-20)^2}{20}+\frac{(24-20)^2}{20}
    +\frac{(18-20)^2}{20}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{4}{20}+\frac{16}{20}+\frac{4}{20}\\[6pt]
 &=0.2+0.8+0.2=1.2,\\[6pt]
\nu&=k-1=3-1=2,\\[6pt]
Q&=1.2<5.991
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{failure to reject }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The dashed line marks the expected count of 20 in each category. The second bar shows the greatest deviation."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The null hypothesis assigns probability one-third to each category. All expected counts equal $20$ and sum to the observed total $60$. Each is at least five, so the chi-square approximation for **Pearson's statistic** is adequate.

The observed-minus-expected differences are $-2$, $4$, and $-2$. Divide their squares $4$, $16$, and $4$ by expected count $20$. The three contributions sum to $1.2$.

The fixed total count leaves two degrees of freedom. No parameters are estimated because the probabilities are fully specified. Pearson's test rejects in the upper tail and $1.2$ is well below $5.991$. Therefore **fail to reject**. The observed counts are compatible with equal probabilities at level $0.05$.

The second category makes the largest contribution, $0.8$, because its deviation is twice as large and all categories have the same denominator.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A regular multinomial model for four categories has one parameter estimated by maximum likelihood from the counts. For $80$ units the observed counts are $16$, $34$, $10$, and $20$. The fitted model gives expected count $20$ in each category. Calculate the statistic and decide at level $0.05$. Use $\chi^2_{2,0.95}=5.991$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
O_j-E_j&=-4,\ 14,\ -10,\ 0,\\[6pt]
Q&=\frac{(-4)^2+14^2+(-10)^2+0^2}{20}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{16+196+100+0}{20}=\frac{312}{20}=15.6,\\[6pt]
\nu&=k-1-d=4-1-1=2,\\[6pt]
Q&=15.6>5.991\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{rejection of }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The deviations are $-4$, $14$, $-10$, and zero. All expected counts equal twenty, so sum the squared deviations and divide once by twenty. The category contributions are $0.8$, $9.8$, $5$, and $0$, with sum $15.6$.

The fixed total uses one degree of freedom and estimation of a parameter uses another under the stated regularity conditions, so $\nu=4-1-1=2$. All expected counts are twenty, making the chi-square approximation adequate.

The statistic exceeds upper quantile $5.991$, so **reject** the model's fit at level $0.05$. The second category, with $34$ observations versus $20$ expected, contributes $9.8$, which on its own exceeds the cutoff.

</div>
