## Exercise 1

Draw $10$ independent observations from a normal population. The unbiased sample variance is $s^2=9$. Calculate the statistic for $H_0:\sigma^2=4$ and decide for the upper-tailed alternative at level $0.05$. Use $\chi^2_{9,0.95}=16.919$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_\sigma&=\frac{(n-1)s^2}{\sigma_0^2}=\frac{(10-1)\cdot9}{4}=\frac{81}{4}=20.25,\\[6pt]
\nu&=n-1=9,\\[6pt]
C&=\{\,q\ge\chi^2_{9,0.95}\,\}=\{\,q\ge16.919\,\},\\[6pt]
Q_\sigma&=20.25>16.919\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{rejection of }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
s^2\ge\frac{\sigma_0^2\,\chi^2_{9,0.95}}{n-1}=\frac{4\cdot16.919}{9}\approx7.52
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Independence and normality justify the **chi-square test for variance**. The unbiased sample variance uses divisor $n-1$. Multiplying it by nine recovers the sum of squared deviations from the sample mean, here $81$.

Under normality the ratio of this sum to the null variance $4$ has a chi-square distribution with nine degrees of freedom. The alternative specifies an increase, so large values give evidence against $H_0$ and the critical region is the right tail of area $0.05$.

The observed statistic $20.25$ exceeds quantile $16.919$, so at level $0.05$ **reject** $H_0$ in favor of a variance above $4$. The p-value is $P(Q_\sigma\ge20.25)\approx0.016$.

On the original scale the quantile gives a cutoff for $s^2$ of approximately $\dfrac{4\cdot16.919}{9}=7.52$, below the observed variance $9$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Ten independent observations from a normal population give $s^2=1$. Test $H_0:\sigma^2=4$ against $H_1:\sigma^2\ne4$ at level $0.05$ using tails of equal probability. Use $\chi^2_{9,0.025}=2.700$ and $\chi^2_{9,0.975}=19.023$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_\sigma&=\frac{(10-1)\cdot1}{4}=\frac{9}{4}=2.25,\\[6pt]
C&=\{\,q\le2.700\,\}\cup\{\,q\ge19.023\,\},\\[6pt]
Q_\sigma&=2.25<2.700
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{rejection of }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
s^2\le\frac{4\cdot2.700}{9}=1.2
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
s^2\ge\frac{4\cdot19.023}{9}\approx8.45
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The population is normal and the observations are independent, so under $H_0$ the statistic $Q_\sigma=\dfrac{9S^2}{4}$ has a chi-square distribution with nine degrees of freedom.

A two-sided alternative regards sample dispersion both too small and too large relative to the null variance as evidence against it. Split level $0.05$ between two tails of area $0.025$ and use the chi-square quantiles. The distribution is asymmetric, so quantiles $2.700$ and $19.023$ lie at different distances from its mean $9$.

The observed statistic is below the lower quantile, so the left tail determines rejection. On the sample-variance scale reject for $s^2\le1.2$ or $s^2\ge8.45$, and the observed value $s^2=1$ satisfies the first condition.

Thus at level $0.05$ **reject** $H_0$. The data indicate a variance below $4$. The equal-tailed two-sided p-value is $2\,P(Q_\sigma\le2.25)\approx0.026$.

</div>
