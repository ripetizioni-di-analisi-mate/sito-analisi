## Exercise 1

A statistic that is standard normal under $H_0$ has observed value $z_{\mathrm{obs}}=-1.50$. For a symmetric two-sided test calculate the p-value and decide at level $0.10$. Use $\Phi(1.50)=0.9332$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=P_{H_0}(|Z|\ge1.50)=P_{H_0}(Z\le-1.50)+P_{H_0}(Z\ge1.50),\\[6pt]
P_{H_0}(Z\ge1.50)&=1-\Phi(1.50)=1-0.9332=0.0668,\\[6pt]
P_{H_0}(Z\le-1.50)&=\Phi(-1.50)=1-\Phi(1.50)=0.0668,\\[6pt]
p&=2\cdot0.0668=0.1336,\\[6pt]
p&>0.10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{failure to reject }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **p-value** is the probability, under $H_0$, of a result at least as far from zero as the observation. A statistic equal to $-1.50$ is as far from zero as one equal to $1.50$. Include both tails starting at these cutoffs.

The area to the right of $1.50$ is the complement of the given $\Phi$. The standard normal is symmetric about zero, so $\Phi(-1.50)=1-\Phi(1.50)$ and the area to the left of $-1.50$ is the same. The p-value is therefore twice $0.0668$.

At level $0.10$ each critical tail would have area $0.05$ and the corresponding cutoff is $z_{0.95}\approx1.645$. The observed point marks a tail of area $0.0668$, larger than $0.05$. Its magnitude is therefore below the rejection cutoff, as confirmed by $1.50<1.645$.

Since $p=0.1336$ exceeds $0.10$, at level $0.10$ **fail to reject** $H_0$.

## Exercise 2

A two-sided t test for a normal population mean gives $t_{\mathrm{obs}}=2.25$ with $19$ degrees of freedom. Calculate the p-value and decide at levels $0.05$ and $0.02$. The distribution function of $t_{19}$ at $2.25$ is approximately $0.98175$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{H_0}(T\ge2.25)&\approx1-0.98175=0.01825,\\[6pt]
P_{H_0}(T\le-2.25)&=P_{H_0}(T\ge2.25)\approx0.01825,\\[6pt]
p&\approx2\cdot0.01825=0.03650,\\[6pt]
0.02&<0.03650<0.05.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
t_{19,0.975}\approx2.093<2.25<t_{19,0.99}\approx2.539
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The population is normal and its standard deviation is estimated from the data, so under the null the statistic has a t distribution with nineteen degrees of freedom. Symmetry about zero makes the lower tail beyond $-2.25$ as probable as the upper tail beyond $2.25$. Double the upper-tail probability, the complement of the given distribution function value.

Reject at level $\alpha$ exactly when $p\le\alpha$. The same p-value is below $0.05$ and above $0.02$, so **reject** at the first level and **fail to reject** at the second.

Comparing cutoffs gives the same decisions. At level $0.05$ each tail has area $0.025$ and cutoff $t_{19,0.975}\approx2.093$, which $2.25$ exceeds. At level $0.02$ each tail has area $0.01$ and cutoff $t_{19,0.99}\approx2.539$ exceeds $2.25$.

</div>
