## Exercise 1

A normal sample mean has standard error $2$. To test $H_0:\mu\le20$ against $H_1:\mu>20$ the rule rejects if $\bar X\ge24$. Find power and Type II error probability at $\mu=26$. Use $\Phi(-1)=0.1587$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar X&\sim N(26,\,2^2)\quad\text{if }\mu=26,\\[6pt]
\frac{24-26}{2}&=-1,\\[6pt]
\beta(26)&=P_{26}(\bar X<24)=P\!\left(Z<\frac{24-26}{2}\right)=\Phi(-1)\approx0.1587,\\[6pt]
\pi(26)&=P_{26}(\bar X\ge24)=1-\beta(26)\approx1-0.1587=0.8413.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\pi(20)=P_{20}(\bar X\ge24)=1-\Phi\!\left(\frac{24-20}{2}\right)=1-\Phi(2)\approx0.0228
$$

The area under the same curve to the right of the cutoff at 24 is the power."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the sample is normal, its mean has a normal distribution with mean $\mu$ and standard error $2$. Under $\mu=26$ standardize using center $26$.

The cutoff stays at $24$ when computing probabilities under the alternative. The center of the sample mean distribution changes to $26$. The cutoff is one standard error below the center because $\dfrac{24-26}{2}=-1$.

A **Type II error** is nonrejection when $\mu=26$, the event $\bar X<24$ with probability $\Phi(-1)\approx0.1587$. **Power** is the probability of its complement $\bar X\ge24$, approximately $0.8413$.

Using center $20$ gives the rejection risk at the null boundary. With center $20$ the cutoff is two standard errors away, so that risk is $1-\Phi(2)\approx0.0228$ and the rule has level approximately $0.023$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Plan an upper-tailed z test at level $0.05$ with known standard deviation $6$. Require power at least $0.80$ for a mean increase of $3$. Find the minimum sample size using $z_{0.95}\approx1.645$ and $z_{0.80}\approx0.842$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi(\mu_0+\delta)&=1-\Phi\!\left(z_{0.95}-\frac{\delta\sqrt n}{\sigma}\right)\ge0.80,\\[6pt]
z_{0.95}-\frac{\delta\sqrt n}{\sigma}&\le z_{0.20}=-z_{0.80},\\[6pt]
\frac{3\sqrt n}{6}&\ge 1.645+0.842=2.487,\\[6pt]
\sqrt n&\ge2\cdot2.487=4.974,\\[6pt]
n&\ge 4.974^2\approx24.74
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n_{\min}=25.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n=25:&\quad 1-\Phi\!\left(1.645-\frac{3\cdot5}{6}\right)=1-\Phi(-0.855)\approx0.804,\\[6pt]
n=24:&\quad 1-\Phi\!\left(1.645-\frac{3\sqrt{24}}{6}\right)=1-\Phi(-0.804)\approx0.789.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Start from the **power formula** for the upper-tailed z test, evaluated at the alternative $\mu_0+\delta$ with $\delta=3$. The level fixes the test cutoff, while the required power constrains where that same cutoff lies under the alternative.

The condition $1-\Phi(a)\ge0.80$ is equivalent to $\Phi(a)\le0.20$, or $a\le z_{0.20}$. By normal symmetry, $z_{0.20}=-z_{0.80}=-0.842$. Moving the standardized distance to the other side gives the inequality linking the two quantiles to the distance between the means in standard-error units.

Since $\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac12$, multiply by two to obtain $\sqrt n\ge4.974$ and hence $n\ge24.74$. The requirement is a lower bound, so round the sample size up.

A direct check confirms the result. With $25$ observations power is about $0.804$, while with $24$ it is about $0.789$, below the target. The **minimum sample size** is therefore $25$ observations.

</div>
