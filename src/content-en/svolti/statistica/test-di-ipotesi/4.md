## Exercise 1

A normal variable is observed on $25$ independent units. The sample mean is $48$ and the sample standard deviation $5$. Test $H_0:\mu=50$ against $H_1:\mu\ne50$ at level $0.05$. Use $t_{24,0.975}=2.064$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SE}&=\frac{s}{\sqrt n}=\frac{5}{\sqrt{25}}=\frac{5}{5}=1,\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{obs}}&=\frac{\bar x-\mu_0}{\mathrm{SE}}=\frac{48-50}{1}=-2,\qquad \nu=n-1=24,\\[6pt]
C&=\{\,|t|\ge2.064\,\},\\[6pt]
|t_{\mathrm{obs}}|&=2<2.064
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{failure to reject }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The population standard deviation is unknown and estimated from the data by $s=5$. Under normality and independence the **t test** applies, and its statistic has a t distribution with $25-1=24$ degrees of freedom.

The estimated standard error of the mean is $\dfrac{5}{5}=1$, so the statistic equals the difference between observed and null means. For a two-sided alternative divide level $0.05$ into two tails of area $0.025$, giving the quantile of order $0.975$.

The statistic is $2$ from zero, below the two-sided critical value $2.064$. Thus at five percent **fail to reject**. The data are insufficient to conclude that the mean differs from $50$. The p-value is $2\,P(T\le-2)\approx0.057$, just above $0.05$.

The normal cutoff $1.960$ with the same estimated denominator would give rejection because $2>1.960$. That cutoff disregards the variability of $s$, which the $t_{24}$ distribution accounts for through heavier tails.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $16$ independent pairs of measurements, assume that after-minus-before differences are normal. Observe $\bar d=-1.2$ and $s_D=2.4$. Test $H_0:\mu_D\ge0$ against $H_1:\mu_D<0$ at level $0.05$. Use $t_{15,0.05}=-1.753$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SE}_D&=\frac{s_D}{\sqrt n}=\frac{2.4}{\sqrt{16}}=\frac{2.4}{4}=0.6,\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{obs}}&=\frac{\bar d-0}{\mathrm{SE}_D}=\frac{-1.2-0}{0.6}=-2,\qquad \nu=16-1=15,\\[6pt]
C&=\{\,t\le-1.753\,\},\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{obs}}&=-2<-1.753
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{rejection of }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two measurements within each pair come from the same unit and are dependent. Use one difference per pair and apply the **paired t test** to the sixteen independent normal differences. The relevant standard deviation is that of the differences.

Dividing by $\sqrt{16}=4$ converts the standard deviation of one difference to the standard error of the mean difference. The degrees of freedom are the number of pairs minus one, $15$.

With differences defined as after minus before, a reduction corresponds to negative values, so the critical region is in the left tail. The value $0$ is the null boundary where rejection is most probable. Use $\mu_D=0$ to determine the cutoff.

The statistic lies to the left of the given quantile and is significant in the specified direction. At level $0.05$ **reject** $H_0$ and conclude that the after measurement is lower on average than the before measurement. The p-value is $P(T\le-2)\approx0.032$ with $T\sim t_{15}$.

</div>
