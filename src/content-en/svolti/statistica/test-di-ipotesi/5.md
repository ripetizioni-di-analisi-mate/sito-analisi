## Exercise 1

In $100$ independent Bernoulli trials, $61$ successes are observed. Calculate the score statistic for $H_0:p=\dfrac12$ and decide for a two-sided test at level $0.05$. Use $z_{0.975}=1.960$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
np_0&=100\cdot0.5=50,\qquad n(1-p_0)=100\cdot0.5=50,\\[6pt]
\widehat p&=\frac{61}{100}=0.61,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{SE}_0&=\sqrt{\frac{p_0(1-p_0)}{n}}=\sqrt{\frac{0.5\cdot0.5}{100}}
=\sqrt{0.0025}=0.05,\\[6pt]
z_{\mathrm{obs}}&=\frac{\widehat p-p_0}{\mathrm{SE}_0}=\frac{0.61-0.50}{0.05}=2.2,\\[6pt]
|z_{\mathrm{obs}}|&=2.2>1.960
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{rejection of }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The success count in a hundred independent trials is binomial. The **score test** calculates the standard error using the proportion specified by $H_0$ because the statistic is meant to be approximately standard normal when $H_0$ is true.

The expected counts under $H_0$ are fifty successes and fifty failures. Both are large, making the normal approximation adequate for this exercise.

The observed proportion is $0.61$ and differs from its null value by $0.11$. Dividing by standard error $0.05$ gives $2.2$. The observed count exceeds expectation by eleven successes, or $2.2$ standard deviations of the count under $H_0$, with standard deviation $\sqrt{100\cdot0.5\cdot0.5}=5$.

For a two-sided alternative use the quantile of order $0.975$. The magnitude of the statistic exceeds quantile $1.960$, so at level $0.05$ **reject** the null hypothesis $p=\dfrac12$. The approximate p-value is $2[1-\Phi(2.2)]\approx0.028$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two independent samples of $100$ trials give $60$ and $40$ successes, respectively. Calculate the score statistic for equality of the proportions and decide at two-sided level $0.05$. Use $z_{0.975}=1.960$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat p_1&=\frac{60}{100}=0.60,\qquad \widehat p_2=\frac{40}{100}=0.40,\\[6pt]
\widehat p_{\mathrm c}&=\frac{60+40}{100+100}=\frac{100}{200}=0.50,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{SE}_0&=\sqrt{0.50\cdot0.50
 \left(\frac{1}{100}+\frac{1}{100}\right)}
 =\sqrt{\frac{0.25\cdot2}{100}}
 =\frac{1}{\sqrt{200}}\approx0.0707,\\[6pt]
z_{\mathrm{obs}}&=\frac{0.60-0.40}{\mathrm{SE}_0}
=0.20\sqrt{200}=2\sqrt{2}\approx2.828,\\[6pt]
|z_{\mathrm{obs}}|&\approx2.828>1.960
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{rejection of }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Under equality both success probabilities equal one common parameter. Estimate it by dividing total successes by total trials, giving the **pooled proportion** $0.50$.

At the pooled proportion each sample has fifty expected successes and fifty expected failures, so the normal approximation is adequate.

The sample proportions are independent, so the variance of their difference equals the sum of their variances. Under $H_0$ each variance equals $\dfrac{0.25}{100}$, giving sum $\dfrac{0.5}{100}=\dfrac{1}{200}$ and standard error $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{200}}$.

Dividing by $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{200}}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $\sqrt{200}=10\sqrt2$, giving $z_{\mathrm{obs}}=0.20\cdot10\sqrt2=2\sqrt2$. Since $2.828$ exceeds cutoff $1.960$ in absolute value, at level $0.05$ **reject** equality of the two proportions.

</div>
