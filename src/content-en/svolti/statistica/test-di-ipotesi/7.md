## Exercise 1

In a table of $120$ units, the first row totals $40$ and the first column $30$. The first cell has $16$ observations. Calculate its expected count under independence and its contribution to Pearson's statistic.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{n_{1\cdot}}{n}&=\frac{40}{120}=\frac13,\qquad \frac{n_{\cdot1}}{n}=\frac{30}{120}=\frac14,\\[6pt]
E_{11}&=n\cdot\frac{n_{1\cdot}}{n}\cdot\frac{n_{\cdot1}}{n}=120\cdot\frac13\cdot\frac14=10,\\[6pt]
E_{11}&=\frac{n_{1\cdot}\,n_{\cdot1}}{n}=\frac{40\cdot30}{120}=10,\\[6pt]
Q_{11}&=\frac{(16-10)^2}{10}=\frac{36}{10}=3.6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Under independence a cell's probability is the product of its row and column marginal probabilities. Estimate these with sample proportions $\dfrac13$ and $\dfrac14$. The expected cell count is $n$ times their product. The totals formula $\dfrac{n_{1\cdot}n_{\cdot1}}{n}$ gives the same result in one step.

Alternatively use the column proportion. The first column's proportion is $\dfrac{30}{120}=\dfrac14$. Under independence it also applies within the first row, so a quarter of its forty units are expected in the cell, giving ten.

The observed deviation is $16-10=6$ and the **Pearson contribution** is its square divided by the expected count, $3.6$. The standardized residual $\dfrac{16-10}{\sqrt{10}}\approx1.90$ is positive, indicating more observations in the cell than independence predicts.

To test independence over the whole table, add this value to the contributions from the other cells.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three groups of $20$ independent units have $15$, $10$, and $5$ successes, respectively. Use Pearson's test for independence between group and binary outcome at level $0.05$. Use $\chi^2_{2,0.95}=5.991$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
 & \text{success} & \text{failure} & \text{total}\\ \hline
\text{group }1 & 15 & 5 & 20\\
\text{group }2 & 10 & 10 & 20\\
\text{group }3 & 5 & 15 & 20\\ \hline
\text{total} & 30 & 30 & 60
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{i1}=E_{i2}&=\frac{20\cdot30}{60}=10\quad(i=1,2,3),\\[6pt]
Q&=\frac{5^2+5^2+0^2+0^2+5^2+5^2}{10}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{25+25+0+0+25+25}{10}=10,\\[6pt]
\nu&=(3-1)(2-1)=2,\\[6pt]
Q&=10>5.991\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{rejection of }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The rejection region begins at $5.991$ and has area $0.05$. The observed value $Q=10$ lies inside the rejection region."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The thirty successes and thirty failures give two columns of equal size. Under independence each group of twenty contributes an average of ten observations to each column, so all six expected counts are ten. Each is at least five, making the chi-square approximation adequate.

In the middle group the observed and expected counts agree and both contributions vanish. In each of the other two groups every cell is five from its expected count. Four equal contributions of $\dfrac{25}{10}=2.5$ sum to $Q=10$.

The table has three rows and two columns, giving $(3-1)(2-1)=2$ degrees of freedom. Since $Q=10$ exceeds quantile $5.991$, at level $0.05$ **reject independence**. The data indicate that the success proportion differs by group. With two degrees of freedom the upper-tail probability is $e^{-\frac{q}{2}}$, giving p-value $e^{-5}\approx0.0067$.

</div>
