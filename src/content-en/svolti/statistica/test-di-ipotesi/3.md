## Exercise 1

An observed z statistic for a lower-tailed alternative is $z_{\mathrm{obs}}=1.28$. Calculate the p-value and decide at level $0.10$. Use $\Phi(1.28)=0.8997$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=P_{H_0}(Z\le z_{\mathrm{obs}})=P_{H_0}(Z\le1.28)=\Phi(1.28)=0.8997,\\[6pt]
p&>0.10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{failure to reject }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C=\{\,z\le z_{0.10}\,\}=\{\,z\le-1.28\,\},\qquad z_{\mathrm{obs}}=1.28\notin C
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a lower-tailed alternative, small statistic values count as evidence against $H_0$. The required probability includes all values to the left of $1.28$. For a statistic standard normal under $H_0$ this probability is $\Phi(1.28)$.

The observed value is positive, so the tail also contains the center of the distribution. Its large probability $p=0.8997$ exceeds level $0.10$. The test therefore **fails to reject** $H_0$.

The critical region gives the same decision. At level $0.10$ the lower cutoff is quantile $z_{0.10}$. By normal symmetry, $z_{0.10}=-z_{0.90}$. Since $\Phi(1.28)\approx0.90$, the cutoff is approximately $-1.28$. The observation $1.28$ is on the opposite side and lies outside the critical region.

The right tail would measure evidence for an increase, a different question. Its probability would be $1-0.8997=0.1003$, and comparison with $0.10$ would again give nonrejection.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four successes are observed in five independent Bernoulli trials. Test $H_0:p\le\dfrac12$ against $H_1:p>\dfrac12$ using the exact binomial test at level $0.10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\frac12}(X\ge4)
  &=P_{\frac12}(X=4)+P_{\frac12}(X=5)\\[6pt]
  &=\binom54\left(\frac12\right)^5+
     \binom55\left(\frac12\right)^5\\[6pt]
  &=5\cdot\frac{1}{32}+1\cdot\frac{1}{32}\\[6pt]
  &=\frac{6}{32}=\frac{3}{16}=0.1875,\\[6pt]
p&=0.1875>0.10
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{failure to reject }H_0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of successes $X$ in five independent trials with common success probability $p$ has binomial distribution $\operatorname{Bin}(5,p)$. The exact test therefore uses binomial probabilities directly.

The probability of at least four successes increases with the success probability, reaching its maximum over null values at $p=\dfrac12$. Calculate the p-value at this null boundary.

For the upper-tailed alternative, the outcomes at least as extreme against $H_0$ as the observation are four and five successes. Under the null value each five-outcome sequence has probability $\left(\dfrac12\right)^5=\dfrac{1}{32}$. There are $\dbinom54=5$ sequences with four successes and one with five, giving six qualifying sequences.

The p-value $0.1875$ exceeds $0.10$, so at level $0.10$ **fail to reject** $H_0$. Five successes would give a tail of area $\dfrac{1}{32}=0.03125$, enough for rejection.

</div>
