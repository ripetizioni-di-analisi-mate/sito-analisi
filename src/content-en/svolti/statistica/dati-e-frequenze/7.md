## Exercise 1

Find the midpoints of the length classes $[10,14)$, $[14,20)$, and $[20,30]$ centimeters. Determine which classes contain measurements of $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $30\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_1&=(14-10)\,\mathrm{cm}=4\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
h_2&=(20-14)\,\mathrm{cm}=6\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
h_3&=(30-20)\,\mathrm{cm}=10\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1&=\frac{10+14}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=12\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
c_2&=\frac{14+20}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=17\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
c_3&=\frac{20+30}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=25\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
14\in[14,20),\qquad 30\in[20,30].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The midpoint lies halfway between the endpoints, so add them and divide by two. Each midpoint is half a class width from its left endpoint. For example, the second class has width $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, so $c_2=14+\dfrac62=17$ centimeters.

The class widths differ, so the distances between consecutive midpoints also vary. The distance between $c_1$ and $c_2$ is $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, the sum of the half-widths 2 and 3. The distance between $c_2$ and $c_3$ is $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, or $3+5$.

The measurement $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the common endpoint of the first two classes. The first excludes its right endpoint, and the second includes its left endpoint, so $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ belongs to the **second class**. The last class includes its right endpoint, so $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ belongs to the **third class**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass class $[a,b)$ has width $8\,\mathrm{g}$ and midpoint $36\,\mathrm{g}$. Three adjacent classes $[24,a)$, $[a,b)$, and $[b,50]$, in grams, contain 7, 11, and 12 observations. Find the unknown endpoints and the possible numbers of observations strictly below $36\,\mathrm{g}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
a+b=72\\[4pt]
b-a=8
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
a=36-\frac82=32,\quad b=36+\frac82=40
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{[24,32)}&=7\quad\text{below }36\\[6pt]
n_{[40,50]}&=12\quad\text{above }36\\[6pt]
7&\le n_{<36}\le7+11=18.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The midpoint is $\dfrac{a+b}{2}=36$ and the width is $b-a=8$, so the endpoints satisfy both equations of the system. Adding the equations gives $2b=80$, and subtracting them gives $2a=64$. Each endpoint is therefore half a width, or $4\,\mathrm{g}$, from the midpoint. The endpoints are $a=32\,\mathrm{g}$ and $b=40\,\mathrm{g}$. The three classes contain $7+11+12=30$ observations in all.

All 7 observations in the first class are less than $32\,\mathrm{g}$ and therefore below the threshold. All 12 in the last class are at least $40\,\mathrm{g}$ and therefore above it. The threshold $36\,\mathrm{g}$ cuts through the middle class, and the table reports only its count.

The 11 middle observations could all be above the threshold, all below, or distributed between the two sides. The number strictly below $36\,\mathrm{g}$ is therefore 7 in the first case, 18 in the second, and an integer between them in the others. Every integer count **from 7 through 18** is compatible with the table.

</div>
