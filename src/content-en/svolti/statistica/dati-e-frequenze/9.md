## Exercise 1

In a course, 24 of the 30 students in the morning session and 35 of the 50 in the afternoon session pass a test. Find the proportion who passed among morning students and the proportion who attended the morning session among those who passed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{mat}}&=30,\qquad n_{\mathrm{id}}=24+35=59\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{id}\mid\mathrm{mat}}&=\frac{24}{30}=\frac45=0.80\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{mat}\mid\mathrm{id}}&=\frac{24}{59}\approx0.4068.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The data cross-classify two variables recorded for each student, the session attended and the test outcome. There are $30+50=80$ students, of whom $24+35=59$ passed.

The 24 morning students who passed meet both conditions and form the numerator of both ratios. For the first question, the reference group is all morning students, so the denominator is the row total of 30. The resulting proportion is $\dfrac45$: $80\%$ of morning students passed the test.

For the second question the reference group includes all students who passed, including afternoon students, so the denominator is the column total of 59. The proportion is approximately $0.4068$. Thus about $40.7\%$ of those who passed attended in the morning. Since 59 is prime and does not divide 24, $\dfrac{24}{59}$ is in lowest terms, and the decimal is rounded to four places.

The first proportion describes outcomes in the morning session. The second describes the composition of the group that passed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two teams handle routine and complex requests. Team A closes 9 of 10 routine requests and 20 of 100 complex ones the same day. Team B closes 80 of 100 routine requests and 1 of 10 complex ones the same day. Compare the proportions by request type and then overall.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{A,ord}}&=\frac9{10}=0.90,\qquad
f_{\mathrm{B,ord}}=\frac{80}{100}=0.80\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{A,comp}}&=\frac{20}{100}=0.20,\qquad
f_{\mathrm{B,comp}}=\frac1{10}=0.10
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{A,tot}}&=\frac{9+20}{10+100}=\frac{29}{110}\approx0.2636\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{B,tot}}&=\frac{80+1}{100+10}=\frac{81}{110}\approx0.7364
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{A,tot}}&=\frac{10}{110}\cdot0.90+\frac{100}{110}\cdot0.20\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{B,tot}}&=\frac{100}{110}\cdot0.80+\frac{10}{110}\cdot0.10.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Team A's proportion exceeds B's for each type: $0.90$ versus $0.80$ for routine requests and $0.20$ versus $0.10$ for complex requests. Each team's overall proportion uses all the requests it closed out of the 110 it handled.

The overall comparison reverses because A handles mainly complex requests and B mainly routine ones. By the formula for the **relative frequency of pooled groups**, each team's overall rate is the weighted mean of its two rates, with weights equal to the fractions of requests of each type. For A, the complex-request rate $0.20$ has weight $\dfrac{100}{110}$, so the total stays close to $0.20$. For B, the routine-request rate $0.80$ has weight $\dfrac{100}{110}$, so the total stays close to $0.80$.

For both teams the same-day closure rate is higher for routine requests. As a result, team B, which handles one hundred routine requests, has the higher overall rate. This reversal between the comparisons within groups and the pooled comparison is known as **Simpson's paradox**.
