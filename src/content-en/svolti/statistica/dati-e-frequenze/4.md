## Exercise 1

The observed values are $2,2,4,5,8,8$. Find $F_6(2)$, $F_6(3)$, and $F_6(8)$, where $F_6$ is the empirical distribution function.

$$
F_6(x)=\frac{n_{\le x}}{6}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\le2}&=2,\qquad F_6(2)=\frac26=\frac13\\[6pt]
n_{\le3}&=2,\qquad F_6(3)=\frac26=\frac13\\[6pt]
n_{\le8}&=6,\qquad F_6(8)=\frac66=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-1 xmax=10 ymin=-0.12 ymax=1.22 nomi="x,F_6(x)" linee="-0.6,0 2,0;2,0.3333 4,0.3333;4,0.5 5,0.5;5,0.6667 8,0.6667;8,1 9.6,1" punti="2,0,vuoto;2,0.3333,pieno;4,0.3333,vuoto;4,0.5,pieno;5,0.5,vuoto;5,0.6667,pieno;8,0.6667,vuoto;8,1,pieno;3,0.3333,pieno" segmenti="3,0,3,0.3333" etichette="-0.65,0.31,⅓;-0.65,0.48,½;-0.65,0.65,⅔" titolo="Empirical distribution function for $2,2,4,5,8,8$. The step heights are $\dfrac13$, $\dfrac12$, $\dfrac23$, and 1. At $x=3$ the function is still equal to $\dfrac13$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The empirical distribution function is $F_6(x)=\dfrac{n_{\le x}}{6}$, where $n_{\le x}$ counts observations less than or equal to the threshold $x$, including repeats. At the threshold 2, both observations equal to 2 are counted because the defining inequality includes equality. Thus $F_6(2)=\dfrac13$.

The threshold 3 lies between the observed values 2 and 4, and there are no observations in $(2,3]$. The count therefore stays the same and $F_6(3)=\dfrac13$. This explains why the graph is horizontal between consecutive distinct observed values.

The threshold 8 includes all six observations, including both occurrences of the maximum, so the distribution function reaches 1. Its jumps occur at the observed values $2,4,5,8$ and have sizes $\dfrac26$, $\dfrac16$, $\dfrac16$, and $\dfrac26$. Each jump is therefore the relative frequency of the value at which it occurs, and the jumps sum to 1.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a sample of 50 observations, $F_{50}(4^-)=0.18$, $F_{50}(4)=0.30$ and $F_{50}(7)=0.74$. Calculate the number of observations equal to 4 and the number of observations in $[4,7]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{<4}&=50\cdot0.18=9\\[6pt]
n_{\le4}&=50\cdot0.30=15\\[6pt]
n_{\le7}&=50\cdot0.74=37
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\{4\}}&=50\bigl(F_{50}(4)-F_{50}(4^-)\bigr)\\[6pt]
&=15-9=6\\[6pt]
n_{[4,7]}&=50\bigl(F_{50}(7)-F_{50}(4^-)\bigr)\\[6pt]
&=37-9=28.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbol $F_{50}(4^-)$ denotes the value of the distribution function just before the jump at 4. It is the proportion of observations strictly less than 4. Multiplying the three proportions by 50 gives counts of 9, 15, and 37.

Observations less than or equal to 4 comprise the two disjoint groups of observations strictly below 4 and observations equal to 4. Thus $15-9$ counts the observations exactly equal to 4, namely **6**. This count is the jump size at 4, $0.12$, multiplied by 50.

For the closed interval $[4,7]$, start with the observations up to 7 and remove only those strictly below 4, since 4 is included. This leaves **28 observations**. Using $F_{50}(4)$ instead of $F_{50}(4^-)$ would count $37-15=22$ observations, those in $(4,7]$, excluding the six equal to 4.

</div>
