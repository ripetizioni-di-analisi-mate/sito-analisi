## Exercise 1

Of 30 processing times, 6 fall in the class $[4,8)$, measured in minutes. Find the rectangle's height in a relative frequency density histogram and in a frequency density histogram based on counts.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=(8-4)\,\mathrm{min}=4\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
f&=\frac{6}{30}=0.20\\[6pt]
d_{\mathrm{rel}}&=\frac{f}{h}=\frac{0.20}{4\,\mathrm{min}}=0.05\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}\\[6pt]
d_{\mathrm{ass}}&=\frac{n_i}{h}=\frac{6}{4\,\mathrm{min}}=1.5\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}\\[6pt]
\frac{d_{\mathrm{ass}}}{d_{\mathrm{rel}}}&=\frac{1.5}{0.05}=30.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Class $[4,8)$ has endpoints of 4 and 8 minutes, so the width of its rectangle is $4\,\mathrm{min}$. The relative frequency of the class is its count divided by the total number of observations, $\dfrac{6}{30}=0.20$.

In a **relative frequency density** histogram, the rectangle's area is the relative frequency $0.20$. Since area equals width times height, dividing $0.20$ by four minutes gives the height $0.05\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}$. The unit $\mathrm{min}^{-1}$ indicates relative frequency per minute.

In a histogram based on **absolute frequency density**, the area represents the 6 observations, so the numerator is the count. The height is $1.5$ observations per minute. The ratio of the two heights is 30, the number of observations, since $\dfrac{n_i}{h}=n\cdot\dfrac{f_i}{h}$. In either case, height times width gives the quantity represented by the rectangle: $0.20$ in the first case and 6 in the second.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The waiting times of 40 people are grouped into $[0,2)$, $[2,6)$, and $[6,14)$ minutes, with 10, 12, and 18 observations, respectively. Calculate the relative frequency density heights and identify the class with the tallest rectangle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{10}{40}=0.25,\qquad h_1=2\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
f_2&=\frac{12}{40}=0.30,\qquad h_2=4\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
f_3&=\frac{18}{40}=0.45,\qquad h_3=8\,\mathrm{min}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_1&=\frac{0.25}{2}\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}
=0.125\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}\\[6pt]
d_2&=\frac{0.30}{4}\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}
=0.075\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}\\[6pt]
d_3&=\frac{0.45}{8}\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}
=0.05625\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three class widths are $2-0=2$, $6-2=4$, and $14-6=8$ minutes. Dividing the counts by 40 gives relative frequencies of $0.25$, $0.30$, and $0.45$. They sum to 1 because all the waiting times are in these classes. To find each height, divide the relative frequency by its class width.

The **first class has the tallest rectangle** because its proportion is spread over just two minutes. The third class has the highest frequency, with 18 observations, but its proportion is spread over eight minutes and its rectangle is the shortest. Multiplying its height $0.05625\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}$ by its width $8\,\mathrm{min}$ recovers its area $0.45$.

The height therefore measures the concentration of observations per minute within the class, while the area measures their proportion.

</div>
