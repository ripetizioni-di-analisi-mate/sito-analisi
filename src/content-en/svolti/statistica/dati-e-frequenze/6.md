## Exercise 1

In a relative frequency density histogram for 80 response times, the classes $[0,2)$ and $[2,5)$ seconds have heights $0.15\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $0.10\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. Find the number of observations below $5\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=d_1h_1=0.15\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\cdot2\,\mathrm{s}=0.30\\[6pt]
f_2&=d_2h_2=0.10\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\cdot3\,\mathrm{s}=0.30\\[6pt]
f_1+f_2&=0.30+0.30=0.60\\[6pt]
n_{<5}&=80\cdot0.60=48.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In this histogram the area of each rectangle equals its class's relative frequency, since $d_ih_i=f_i$. To obtain a frequency, multiply the height by the class width, $2\,\mathrm{s}$ for the first class and $5-2=3\,\mathrm{s}$ for the second. The units $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $\mathrm{s}$ cancel, so the frequencies are dimensionless.

The two classes have the same area, $0.30$. The second is wider by the same factor, $\dfrac32$, by which its height is smaller. Thus each contains $80\cdot0.30=24$ observations.

Response times are nonnegative, so the first two classes cover all of $[0,5)$, that is, all times below $5\,\mathrm{s}$. The classes are disjoint, so their relative frequencies add to $0.60$. Of the 80 observations, this gives **48 times** below $5\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A relative frequency density histogram summarizes 200 times in the three classes $[0,2)$, $[2,6)$, and $[6,11]$ seconds. The first two heights are $0.12\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $0.09\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. Find the height and count for the third class.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=2\cdot0.12=0.24\\[6pt]
f_2&=4\cdot0.09=0.36\\[6pt]
f_3&=1-(0.24+0.36)=0.40\\[6pt]
h_3&=(11-6)\,\mathrm{s}=5\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
d_3&=\frac{0.40}{5}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}
=0.08\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\\[6pt]
n_3&=200\cdot0.40=80.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two classes have widths $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and $6-2=4\,\mathrm{s}$. Multiplying each height by its width gives relative frequencies of $0.24$ and $0.36$, corresponding to 48 and 72 of the 200 times.

Since the three classes are disjoint and cover all the times, the three rectangle areas sum to 1. The first two areas total $0.60$, leaving an area of $0.40$ for the last class.

The last class $[6,11]$ has width $5\,\mathrm{s}$, since including its right endpoint does not affect its width. Dividing area by width gives a height of $0.08\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, less than that of the second class even though the third contains more observations. Its count depends only on its relative frequency. It is $40\%$ of 200, or **80 times**. The three counts, 48, 72, and 80, sum to 200.
