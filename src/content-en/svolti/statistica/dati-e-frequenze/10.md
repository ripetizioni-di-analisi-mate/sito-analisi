## Exercise 1

For 80 waiting times, $F_{80}(12)=0.65$ and $F_{80}(12^-)=0.60$ are known. Determine how many times strictly exceed 12 minutes and how many are at least 12 minutes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\le12}&=80\cdot0.65=52\\[6pt]
n_{<12}&=80\cdot0.60=48
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{>12}&=80-n_{\le12}=80(1-0.65)=28\\[6pt]
n_{\ge12}&=80-n_{<12}=80(1-0.60)=32\\[6pt]
n_{\{12\}}&=n_{\ge12}-n_{>12}=32-28=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The empirical distribution function at 12 gives the proportion of times less than or equal to 12 minutes, whereas $F_{80}(12^-)$ gives the proportion strictly below 12. Multiplying by 80 gives 52 and 48 times, respectively.

The complement of “strictly above 12” includes all times up to 12, including times exactly at the threshold. Using the cumulative frequency at 12 therefore gives **28** times above the threshold.

The complement of “at least 12” includes only times strictly less than 12, so use the cumulative frequency before the jump. There are **32** waits of at least 12 minutes.

The two counts differ by four, which is the number of waits exactly at the threshold. The jump size at 12 gives the same count because $80(0.65-0.60)=4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Of 120 people enrolled in a service, 96 answer a question about waiting time. Of the respondents, 18 report waiting more than 20 minutes. Find the proportion among respondents and the smallest and largest proportions among all enrollees that are compatible with the data.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{risp}}&=\frac{18}{96}=\frac{3}{16}=0.1875\\[6pt]
n_{\mathrm{missing}}&=120-96=24\\[6pt]
f_{\min}&=\frac{18+0}{120}=0.15\\[6pt]
f_{\max}&=\frac{18+24}{120}=\frac{42}{120}=0.35.
\end{aligned}
$$

The remaining 24 did not respond. The proportion among all participants ranges from $\dfrac{18}{120}$ to $\dfrac{42}{120}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator for the proportion among respondents is 96 because it includes only people who reported a waiting time. Dividing the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{18}{96}$ by 6 gives $\dfrac{3}{16}=0.1875$. The response rate is $\dfrac{96}{120}=0.80$.

For all enrollees combined, the unknown quantity is how many of the 24 nonrespondents waited longer than the threshold. It can be any integer from 0 to 24 because the missing responses give no information about their waiting times.

If none of the 24 unreported waits exceeds the threshold, there are 18 cases out of 120 and the minimum proportion is $0.15$. If all exceed it, there are 42 out of 120 and the maximum proportion is $0.35$. The proportion among all enrollees is therefore between $15\%$ and $35\%$, whereas $18.75\%$ describes only the respondents.

</div>
