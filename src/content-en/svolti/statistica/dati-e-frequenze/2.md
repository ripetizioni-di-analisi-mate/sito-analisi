## Exercise 1

An archive contains 160 cases. Of these, $12.5\%$ are under review and $32.5\%$ are awaiting documents. The three categories are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. The third category is “closed.” Find the count in each category.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{rev}}&=\frac{12.5}{100}=0.125\\[6pt]
n_{\mathrm{rev}}&=0.125\cdot160=20\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{att}}&=\frac{32.5}{100}=0.325\\[6pt]
n_{\mathrm{att}}&=0.325\cdot160=52\\[6pt]
n_{\mathrm{closed}}&=160-20-52=88\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{closed}}&=\frac{88}{160}=0.55.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The percentages use all 160 cases as their base. Dividing a percentage by 100 gives its relative frequency, so $12.5\%$ corresponds to $0.125$ and $32.5\%$ to $0.325$.

Since $f_i=\dfrac{n_i}{n}$, multiply the relative frequency by $n=160$ to obtain the count. This gives 20 cases under review and 52 awaiting documents. Both results are integers, so the stated percentages are compatible with a total of 160 cases.

Each case belongs to exactly one of the three categories, so the counts sum to 160. Subtract the 72 cases already counted from the total. The remaining **88 cases are closed**. Their relative frequency is $\dfrac{88}{160}=0.55$, or $55\%$, and the three percentages sum to $12.5\%+32.5\%+55\%=100\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A category accounts for $43\%$ of 250 records after its percentage is rounded to the nearest integer, with halves rounded up. Find all possible counts for the category.

$$
\begin{aligned}
42.5&\le 100\frac{n_i}{250}<43.5\\[6pt]
42.5\cdot\frac{250}{100}&\le n_i<43.5\cdot\frac{250}{100}\\[6pt]
106.25&\le n_i<108.75\\[6pt]
n_i&\in\{107,108\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
100\cdot\frac{107}{250}=42.8,\qquad 100\cdot\frac{108}{250}=43.2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exact percentage in the category is $100\dfrac{n_i}{250}$. Under the stated rule, it rounds to 43 if it is less than half a point from 43 or equals $42.5$. An exact percentage of $42.5\%$ rounds to $43\%$, so the lower bound is included. An exact percentage of $43.5\%$ rounds to $44\%$, so the upper bound is excluded.

Since $\dfrac{250}{100}=2.5$ is positive, multiplying all three parts of the inequality by it preserves the directions of the inequalities. The resulting interval of counts runs from $106.25$ inclusive to $108.75$ exclusive.

The count must be an integer, so the only possibilities are **107 and 108**. The value 106 lies below the lower bound, and 109 exceeds the upper bound. The two admissible counts give exact percentages of $42.8\%$ and $43.2\%$, both of which round to $43\%$.

</div>
