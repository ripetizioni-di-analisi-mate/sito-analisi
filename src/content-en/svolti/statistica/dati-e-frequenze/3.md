## Exercise 1

In a group of students, the numbers of absences $0,1,2,3$ have absolute frequencies $5,9,4,2$. Construct the cumulative frequency table and find the number of students with at least two absences.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_1&=n_1=5\\[6pt]
N_2&=N_1+n_2=5+9=14\\[6pt]
N_3&=N_2+n_3=14+4=18\\[6pt]
N_4&=N_3+n_4=18+2=20\\[6pt]
n_{\ge2}&=N_4-N_2=20-14=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

The difference between 14 and the total of 20 gives the six students with at least two absences."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The values $0,1,2,3$ are numbers of absences. They have a natural ordering, so cumulative counts are meaningful. Set $x_1=0$, $x_2=1$, $x_3=2$, and $x_4=3$. The cumulative frequency $N_i$ counts students with at most $x_i$ absences. Since $N_i=N_{i-1}+n_i$, each cumulative count is obtained by adding the count for the next value to the previous total.

The first cumulative count includes the five students with no absences. The second adds the nine students with one absence, giving 14. Adding the counts for two and three absences in turn gives 18 and 20. The final cumulative count equals the total number of students because nobody has more than three absences.

“At least two” includes two and three absences. Its complement includes zero and one absence and is counted by $N_2=14$. Thus the number of students with at least two absences is $20-14=6$. Adding the two counts $n_3=4$ and $n_4=2$ directly gives the same number.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A review assigns each case to one of four ordered levels: low, medium, high, and urgent. The cumulative counts are $8,20,35,40$. Recover the absolute frequencies and find the percentage of cases at the medium or high level.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_1&=N_1=8\\[6pt]
n_2&=N_2-N_1=20-8=12\\[6pt]
n_3&=N_3-N_2=35-20=15\\[6pt]
n_4&=N_4-N_3=40-35=5
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_2+n_3&=12+15=27=N_3-N_1\\[6pt]
\frac{27}{40}\cdot100\,\%&=0.675\cdot100\,\%=67.5\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The levels low, medium, high, and urgent are ordered, so the cumulative counts $N_1,\ldots,N_4$ include cases up to and including each level. The final count is 40, the total number of cases.

The count of 20 includes both low and medium levels. To isolate the medium level, subtract the 8 low-level cases using $n_i=N_i-N_{i-1}$. Applying the same reasoning to each consecutive pair of cumulative counts gives absolute frequencies of 15 and 5 for the high and urgent levels. The sum $8+12+15+5$ equals 40 because the four levels cover all cases.

The question excludes both low and urgent cases. Adding the two middle counts gives 27, with 40 as the denominator. The count 27 can also be obtained as $N_3-N_1=35-8$: removing the low-level cases from all cases up to the high level leaves exactly the medium- and high-level cases. Their proportion is $0.675$, so $67.5\%$ of the cases are medium or high.

</div>
