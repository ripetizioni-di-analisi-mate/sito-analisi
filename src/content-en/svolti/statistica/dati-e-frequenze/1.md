## Exercise 1

In a batch of 24 lamps, 9 were repaired during testing. Calculate the relative frequency and percentage of repaired lamps.

$$
n=24,\qquad n_{\mathrm{rip}}=9
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{\mathrm{rip}}&=\frac{n_{\mathrm{rip}}}{n}=\frac{9}{24}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3\cdot3}{3\cdot8}=\frac38\\[6pt]
&=0.375\\[6pt]
100f_{\mathrm{rip}}\,\%&=100\cdot0.375\,\%=37.5\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The statistical units are the lamps in the batch, and the recorded variable is the test outcome, with categories “repaired” and “not repaired.” The denominator of the relative frequency is therefore the number of units, $n=24$. The repaired category contains 9 lamps, so 9 is the numerator.

The numerator and denominator are both multiples of 3. Dividing each by 3 reduces the fraction to $\dfrac38$. Dividing 3 by 8 gives $0.375$ because $8\cdot0.375=3$. The fraction $\dfrac38$ and the decimal $0.375$ therefore express the same proportion.

The percentage frequency is $100f_i\,\%$, so multiply the relative frequency by 100. Repaired lamps account for $37.5\%$ of the batch. There are $24-9=15$ lamps that were not repaired, with relative frequency $\dfrac{15}{24}=0.625$. The two relative frequencies sum to 1 because the categories are disjoint and together cover the entire batch.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two service departments resolve 15 of 60 requests and 14 of 40 requests, respectively, within an hour. Identify the department with the larger proportion and calculate the overall relative frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{15}{60}=\frac14=0.25\\[6pt]
f_2&=\frac{14}{40}=\frac{7}{20}=0.35\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\frac{15+14}{60+40}=\frac{29}{100}=0.29.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_1&=\frac{60}{100}=0.6,\qquad w_2=\frac{40}{100}=0.4\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{tot}}&=0.6\cdot0.25+0.4\cdot0.35\\[6pt]
&=0.15+0.14=0.29.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first department resolves one more request within an hour but also receives twenty more. To compare proportions, divide each department's count by its own total. In the first department, dividing the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{15}{60}$ by 15 gives $0.25$. In the second, dividing the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{14}{40}$ by 2 gives $0.35$. The **second department** therefore has the higher proportion, $35\%$.

Overall there are $15+14=29$ favorable cases among $60+40=100$ requests, so the pooled relative frequency is $0.29$, or $29\%$.

The same result follows from the **relative frequency for pooled groups**: weight each proportion by the fraction of requests belonging to its department. The departments are disjoint, and their weights are $0.6$ and $0.4$, so the overall proportion is $0.15+0.14=0.29$. It lies closer to $25\%$ because the first department has greater weight. The unweighted arithmetic mean of the proportions is $0.30$ and would give equal weight to departments of different sizes.

</div>
