## Exercise 1

Let $X\sim U(4,10)$. Calculate the variance and the threshold leaving $25\%$ of the probability to its right.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}=\frac{(10-4)^2}{12}=\frac{36}{12}=3,\\[6pt]
P(X>q)&=\frac14 \ \Longrightarrow\ F_X(q)=1-\frac14=\frac34,\\[6pt]
F_X(q)&=\frac{q-4}{10-4}=\frac34
\ \Longrightarrow\ q-4=\frac34\cdot6=\frac92,\\[6pt]
q&=4+\frac92=\frac{17}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on **quantiles and moments of the uniform distribution**, with $a=4$ and $b=10$ the variance is $\dfrac{(b-a)^2}{12}$. The support has length $6$, so the variance is $\dfrac{36}{12}=3$ and the standard deviation is $\sqrt3$.

A threshold leaving one quarter of the area on the right leaves three quarters on the left, so $F_X(q)=\dfrac34$. The required threshold is therefore the quantile $q_{\frac34}$. Between $4$ and $10$, the cumulative distribution function is $\dfrac{x-4}{6}$ because the density is constant and the accumulated area grows in proportion to the distance from $4$.

The equation $\dfrac{q-4}{6}=\dfrac34$ gives $q-4=\dfrac92$. Starting at the lower endpoint $4$ and adding $\dfrac92$ units gives the threshold $\dfrac{17}{2}=8.5$. The formula $q_p=a+p(b-a)$ with $p=\dfrac34$ gives the same result.

As a check, the segment from $\dfrac{17}{2}$ to $10$ has length $\dfrac32$, one quarter of the six available units.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform variable has mean $13$ and variance $12$. Find the support endpoints and the quantile of order $\dfrac16$.

$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{a+b}{2}=13\\[8pt]
\dfrac{(b-a)^2}{12}=12\\[8pt]
a<b
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{cases}
a+b=26\\[4pt]
b-a=12
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{26-12}{2}=7,\qquad b=\frac{26+12}{2}=19,\\[6pt]
q_{\frac16}&=a+\frac16(b-a)=7+\frac16(19-7)=7+2=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean and variance formulas for the uniform distribution give two equations in the unknowns $a$ and $b$, subject to $a<b$. The mean identifies the midpoint, so the sum of the endpoints is $26$.

The variance gives the squared length, $(b-a)^2=144$. The equation has solutions $b-a=12$ and $b-a=-12$. Since $b>a$, take the positive root. Adding the two linear equations gives $2b=38$, hence $b=19$. Subtracting them gives $2a=14$, hence $a=7$. The support is therefore $(7,19)$. Equivalently, half the length gives the distance $6$ on either side of the center $13$.

Use $q_p=a+p(b-a)$ for the quantile. Add to $a$ one sixth of the width $12$, namely $2$. The quantile of order $\dfrac16$ is $9$.

At $9$ the cumulative distribution function equals $\dfrac{9-7}{19-7}=\dfrac2{12}=\dfrac16$, as required. The original data are also satisfied because $\dfrac{7+19}{2}=13$ and $\dfrac{12^2}{12}=12$.

</div>
