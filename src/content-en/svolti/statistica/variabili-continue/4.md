## Exercise 1

A waiting time $T$ is exponentially distributed with mean $5\,\mathrm{min}$. Calculate the probability of waiting between $5$ and $15\,\mathrm{min}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[T]&=\frac1\lambda=5\,\mathrm{min}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda=\frac15\,\mathrm{min}^{-1},\\[6pt]
P(T>t)&=e^{-\frac{t}{5}}\qquad (t\ge0),\\[6pt]
P(5<T<15)&=P(T>5)-P(T>15)\\[6pt]
         &=e^{-\frac55}-e^{-\frac{15}{5}}\\[6pt]
         &=e^{-1}-e^{-3}\approx0.3679-0.0498=0.3181.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the **exponential distribution**, the mean is the reciprocal of the rate, so $\lambda=\dfrac15\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}$. The rate has units $\mathrm{min}^{-1}$, making the product of rate and time in minutes dimensionless, as an exponent must be.

The survival function $P(T>t)=e^{-\lambda t}$ directly gives the probability of exceeding a threshold. Event $\{T>5\}$ includes all waits longer than five minutes, including those longer than fifteen. Subtract the second tail to isolate the required interval.

With $\lambda=\dfrac15$ the exponents are $-1$ and $-3$. Each individual endpoint has probability zero, so the difference also gives the probability of the open interval. The probability of waiting between $5$ and $15$ minutes is $e^{-1}-e^{-3}$, approximately $0.318$.

Integrating the density $\dfrac15e^{-\frac t5}$ between $5$ and $15$ gives the same value because an antiderivative is $-e^{-\frac t5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A component has an exponential lifetime with mean $8\,\mathrm{h}$. Given that it has already operated for $12\,\mathrm{h}$, calculate the probability that it fails between $4$ and $8\,\mathrm{h}$ after that time.

$$
\lambda=\frac18\,\mathrm{h}^{-1},\qquad P(T>t)=e^{-\frac t8}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(16<T<20\mid T>12)
 &=\frac{P(\{16<T<20\}\cap\{T>12\})}{P(T>12)}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{P(T>16)-P(T>20)}{P(T>12)}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{e^{-\frac{16}{8}}-e^{-\frac{20}{8}}}{e^{-\frac{12}{8}}}
 =\frac{e^{-2}-e^{-\frac52}}{e^{-\frac32}}\\[6pt]
 &=e^{-2+\frac32}-e^{-\frac52+\frac32}\\[6pt]
 &=e^{-\frac12}-e^{-1}\approx0.6065-0.3679=0.2386.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean $8\,\mathrm{h}$ gives rate $\lambda=\dfrac18\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ and survival function $e^{-\frac t8}$. Four and eight hours are additional durations, so the corresponding total times to failure are $16$ and $20$.

The failure interval is contained in the event of survival through the first twelve hours, so the intersection in the numerator equals $\{16<T<20\}$. In the numerator subtract the two tail probabilities, then divide by the probability of having reached twelve hours.

Dividing by $e^{-\frac32}$ is equivalent to adding $\dfrac32$ to each exponent. This leaves precisely the two tails for the additional durations, $e^{-\frac48}$ and $e^{-\frac88}$. The required probability is $e^{-\frac12}-e^{-1}$, approximately $0.239$.

The **memoryless property** applied to each tail gives the same result. Indeed, $P(T>12+4\mid T>12)=P(T>4)$ and $P(T>12+8\mid T>12)=P(T>8)$, so the probability equals $P(4<T<8)$ for a new component.

</div>
