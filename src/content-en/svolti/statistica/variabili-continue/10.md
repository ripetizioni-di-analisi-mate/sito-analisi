## Exercise 1

The density of $X$ is $3x^2$ for $0<x<1$ and zero elsewhere. Calculate the mean and variance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X]&=\int_0^1 x\cdot3x^2\,dx=\int_0^1 3x^3\,dx=\left[\frac{3x^4}{4}\right]_0^1=\frac34,\\[6pt]
E[X^2]&=\int_0^1 x^2\cdot3x^2\,dx=\int_0^1 3x^4\,dx=\left[\frac{3x^5}{5}\right]_0^1=\frac35,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=E[X^2]-E[X]^2=\frac35-\left(\frac34\right)^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac35-\frac9{16}=\frac{48-45}{80}=\frac3{80}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,3*t*t,0,1" linee="-0.3,0 0,0;1,0 1.35,0" segmenti="1,0,1,3;0.75,0,0.75,1.6875;0.5,0,0.5,0.75" etichette="0.55,1.95,mean 0.75;0.22,0.95,midpoint" xmin=-0.3 xmax=1.4 ymin=-0.25 ymax=3.4 nomi="x,f_X" legenda="f_X(x)=3x^2" titolo="The density $3x^2$ increases from left to right, so the mean $\dfrac34$ lies above the midpoint $\dfrac12$ of the support."}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of the **moments of a distribution with a density**, the mean is the integral of $x f_X(x)$ and the second moment is the integral of $x^2 f_X(x)$. The density is nonzero only between $0$ and $1$, so both integrals are restricted to this interval. They are finite because their integrands are bounded on a bounded interval.

For the mean, multiply the density by $x$ to obtain $3x^3$, with antiderivative $\dfrac{3x^4}{4}$. For the second moment, multiply by $x^2$ to obtain $3x^4$, with antiderivative $\dfrac{3x^5}{5}$. Both antiderivatives equal zero at $0$.

Finally, subtract the squared mean from the second moment. The common denominator of $\dfrac35$ and $\dfrac9{16}$ is $80$, so the difference is $\dfrac{3}{80}$, or $0.0375$. The standard deviation is approximately $0.194$.

The increasing density gives more weight to the right side of the support, so the mean $\dfrac34$ exceeds the midpoint $\dfrac12$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The density of $X$ is $\dfrac12$ on $(-1,0)$, $\dfrac x4$ on $(0,2)$ and zero elsewhere. Calculate the mean and variance.

$$
\int_{-1}^{0}\frac12\,dx+\int_0^2\frac{x}{4}\,dx=\frac12+\left[\frac{x^2}{8}\right]_0^2=\frac12+\frac12=1.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X]&=\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{x}{2}\,dx+\int_0^2\frac{x^2}{4}\,dx\\[6pt]
    &=\left[\frac{x^2}{4}\right]_{-1}^{0}+\left[\frac{x^3}{12}\right]_0^2
     =-\frac14+\frac8{12}\\[6pt]
    &=-\frac14+\frac23=\frac{-3+8}{12}=\frac5{12},\\[6pt]
E[X^2]&=\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{x^2}{2}\,dx+\int_0^2\frac{x^3}{4}\,dx\\[6pt]
      &=\left[\frac{x^3}{6}\right]_{-1}^{0}+\left[\frac{x^4}{16}\right]_0^2
       =\frac16+1=\frac76,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=\frac76-\left(\frac5{12}\right)^2=\frac{168}{144}-\frac{25}{144}=\frac{143}{144}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before calculating moments, check that the function is a **density**. It is nonnegative, and the two areas sum to one: $\dfrac12$ for the rectangle and $\dfrac12$ for the triangle with base $2$ and height $\dfrac12$.

The integrand changes at zero, so calculate each moment using two integrals. The first contribution to the mean is negative because $x$ is negative on the left segment. The antiderivative $\dfrac{x^2}{4}$ equals $0$ at $0$ and $\dfrac14$ at $-1$, giving contribution $-\dfrac14$. The second contribution, $\dfrac23$, is positive and larger in absolute value, so the mean is positive.

Both contributions to the second moment contain $x^2$ and are positive. The antiderivative $\dfrac{x^3}{6}$ equals $-\dfrac16$ at $-1$, so the first contribution is $\dfrac16$ and the second is $\dfrac{16}{16}=1$.

Subtract the squared mean only after adding the contributions to the second moment. Using the common denominator $144$ gives variance $\dfrac{143}{144}$, just under one.

</div>
