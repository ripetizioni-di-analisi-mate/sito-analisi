## Exercise 1

A waiting time $T$ is exponential with mean $6\,\mathrm{min}$. Find the threshold exceeded in $10\%$ of waits.

$$
\lambda=\frac16\,\mathrm{min}^{-1},\qquad P(T>q)=e^{-\frac{q}{6}}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
e^{-\frac{q}{6}}&=\frac1{10},\\[6pt]
-\frac{q}{6}&=\log\frac1{10}=-\log 10,\\[6pt]
q&=6\log 10\,\mathrm{min}\approx6\cdot2.3026\,\mathrm{min}\approx13.82\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,exp(-t/6)/6,0,30" area="exp(-x/6)/6;0;13.8155;30" segmenti="13.8155,0,13.8155,0.016667" etichette="13.8,0.04,q" xmin=-0.9 xmax=30.9 ymin=-0.02 ymax=0.2 nomi="t,f_T" legenda="f_T(t)=\dfrac16e^{-t/6}" titolo="The shaded tail beyond $q=6\log 10\approx13.8$ minutes has area $0.10$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For the **exponential distribution**, the rate is the reciprocal of the mean. It is therefore $\dfrac16\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}$ and the survival function is $e^{-\frac q6}$. The stated ten percent is the probability to the right of the threshold, so set the survival function equal to $\dfrac1{10}$.

The natural logarithm is strictly increasing, so applying it to both sides preserves equality and brings the unknown out of the exponent. Since $\log(\dfrac1{10})=-\log 10$, multiplication by $-6$ gives a positive result. It is measured in minutes because $6$ is a duration in minutes.

The quantile has order $0.90$ because ninety percent of the probability is on the left. The formula $q_p=-\dfrac{\log(1-p)}{\lambda}$ with $p=0.90$ gives the same value because $-\log 0.1=\log 10$.

The threshold exceeded in $10\%$ of waits is approximately $13.8$ minutes, just over twice the mean.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A time $T$ has an exponential distribution with median $8\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the rate and the quantile of order $\dfrac78$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(T>8)=e^{-8\lambda}&=\frac12,\\[6pt]
-8\lambda&=-\log 2
  \ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda=\frac{\log 2}{8}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\approx0.0866\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(T>q_{\frac78})&=1-\frac78=\frac18=\left(\frac12\right)^3,\\[6pt]
e^{-\lambda q_{\frac78}}&=\frac18
\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda q_{\frac78}=\log 8=3\log 2,\\[6pt]
q_{\frac78}&=\frac{3\log 2}{\lambda}=3\log 2\cdot\frac{8}{\log 2}=24\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The median divides the area into two equal parts, so the tail beyond $8\,\mathrm{s}$ has probability $\dfrac12$. The exponential survival function equals $e^{-8\lambda}$ at that point. Taking natural logarithms gives $8\lambda=\log 2$ and hence the rate $\dfrac{\log 2}{8}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.

At the quantile of order $\dfrac78$, the probability to the right is $1-\dfrac78=\dfrac18$, the cube of $\dfrac12$. Setting the survival function equal to $\dfrac18$ and taking logarithms gives $\lambda q=\log 8$, with $\log 8=3\log 2$ by the logarithm power rule.

Since the tail exponent is proportional to time, tripling the median threshold changes the tail probability to $\left(\dfrac12\right)^3=\dfrac18$. Substituting the rate cancels $\log 2$ and gives the same value.

The rate is approximately $0.0866\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, and the quantile of order $\dfrac78$ is $24\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
