## Exercise 1

A duration $X$ has distribution $N(50,36)$ in minutes. Find the threshold exceeded by $5\%$ of durations. Use $z_{0.95}\approx1.645$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X>q)&=0.05\ \Longrightarrow\ P(X\le q)=1-0.05=0.95,\\[6pt]
P\!\left(Z\le\frac{q-50}{6}\right)&=0.95
\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{q-50}{6}=z_{0.95},\\[6pt]
q_{0.95}&=50+\sqrt{36}\,z_{0.95}\\[6pt]
          &\approx 50+6\cdot1.645=50+9.87=59.87\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The shaded tail has area $0.05$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The threshold marks off the upper five percent, so the cumulative probability is $0.95$ and the threshold is the quantile of order $0.95$. The variance is $36\,\mathrm{min}^2$, so the standard deviation is $6\,\mathrm{min}$.

Standardizing transforms event $\{X\le q\}$ into $\{Z\le\dfrac{q-50}{6}\}$, which has probability $0.95$ only if $\dfrac{q-50}{6}=z_{0.95}$. This gives the quantile formula $q_p=\mu+\sigma z_p$ for the **normal distribution**.

The standard normal quantile gives the number of standard deviations between the threshold and the mean. The product $6\cdot1.645$ is approximately $9.87\,\mathrm{min}$. Add this distance to the mean to return to the original time scale.

The threshold exceeded by $5\%$ of durations is approximately $59.9$ minutes. The standard quantile is positive because the threshold lies in the right tail, above the mean.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A normal distribution with mean $100$ assigns probability $0.90$ to the central interval $(90,110)$. Using $z_{0.95}\approx1.645$, estimate the standard deviation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X<90)+P(X>110)&=1-0.90=0.10,\\[6pt]
P(X>110)&=\frac{1-0.90}{2}=0.05,\\[6pt]
P(X\le110)&=0.95\ \Longrightarrow\ 110=q_{0.95},\\[6pt]
110&=100+\sigma z_{0.95},\\[6pt]
\sigma&=\frac{110-100}{z_{0.95}}\approx\frac{10}{1.645}
       \approx 6.08.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Ten percent of the probability lies outside the central interval. The interval is symmetric about the mean and the normal density is symmetric about $\mu=100$, so the outer ten percent splits into two equal tails of $0.05$.

The upper limit leaves $0.95$ to its left and is therefore the quantile of order $0.95$. By the formula $q_p=\mu+\sigma z_p$, the ten units from the mean to this limit represent approximately $1.645$ standard deviations.

Divide the distance by the standard quantile because the unknown here is the scale of the distribution. The standard deviation is approximately $6.08$, in the same units as $X$, and the variance is approximately $36.95$.

The lower limit gives the same equation because $90=100+\sigma z_{0.05}$ with $z_{0.05}=-z_{0.95}$.

</div>
