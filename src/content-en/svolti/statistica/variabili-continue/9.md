## Exercise 1

Let $X\sim U(2,5)$ and $Y=12-2X$. Find the density of $Y$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_X(x)&=\frac{1}{5-2}=\frac13\qquad\text{for }2<x<5,\\[6pt]
g(x)&=12-2x,\qquad g'(x)=-2<0,\\[6pt]
g(2)&=8,\qquad g(5)=2,\\[6pt]
y=12-2x\ &\Longrightarrow\ g^{-1}(y)=\frac{12-y}{2},\qquad
\left|\frac{d}{dy}g^{-1}(y)\right|=\left|-\frac12\right|=\frac12,\\[6pt]
f_Y(y)&=f_X\!\left(\frac{12-y}{2}\right)\cdot\frac12=\frac13\cdot\frac12=\frac16
\qquad\text{for }2<y<8.
\end{aligned}
$$

The density is zero outside $(2,8)$.

::grafico{linee="0,0 2,0;2,0.333333 5,0.333333;5,0 9.4,0" segmenti="2,0,2,0.333333;5,0,5,0.333333;2,0.166667,8,0.166667;8,0,8,0.166667" etichette="3.5,0.37,f_X;6.5,0.2,f_Y" xmin=-0.4 xmax=9.6 ymin=-0.04 ymax=0.43 nomi="x,densità" legenda=0 titolo="The density of $X$ has height $\dfrac13$ on $(2,5)$. The dashed density of $Y$ has height $\dfrac16$ on $(2,8)$, an interval twice as long."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $g(x)=12-2x$ is of class $C^1$ and has a constant, nonzero derivative $-2$. It is therefore strictly monotone, so the **density formula for a monotone transformation** applies.

The transformation is decreasing, so the right endpoint for $X$ gives the left endpoint for $Y$. The endpoints $2$ and $5$ map to $8$ and $2$, respectively. The support of $Y$ is therefore $(2,8)$.

Solving $y=12-2x$ for $x$ gives the inverse $\dfrac{12-y}{2}$, whose derivative is $-\dfrac12$. Its absolute value, $\dfrac12$, is the reciprocal of the magnitude of the slope of $g$. For every $y$ in $(2,8)$, the point $\dfrac{12-y}{2}$ belongs to $(2,5)$, where $f_X$ equals $\dfrac13$.

Multiplying the original density by this factor gives a constant density $\dfrac16$ on an interval of length six, so $Y\sim U(2,8)$. The resulting rectangle has area $6\cdot\dfrac16=1$, satisfying normalization.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $X\sim U(-3,3)$ and $Y=X^2$. Calculate the cumulative distribution function and density of $Y$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_Y(y)&=P(X^2\le y)=P(-\sqrt y\le X\le\sqrt y)\\[6pt]
     &=\frac{\sqrt y-(-\sqrt y)}{3-(-3)}=\frac{2\sqrt y}{6}=\frac{\sqrt y}{3}
       &&\text{for }0<y<9,\\[6pt]
f_Y(y)&=F_Y'(y)=\frac13\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt y}=\frac1{6\sqrt y}&&\text{for }0<y<9.
\end{aligned}
$$

The cumulative distribution function equals $0$ for $y\le0$ and $1$ for $y\ge9$. The density is zero outside $(0,9)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Squaring gives values between $0$ and $9$ because $X$ ranges over $(-3,3)$. The function $x^2$ is decreasing for $x<0$ and increasing for $x>0$, so it is not monotone on the support and the monotone transformation formula does not apply directly. Use the **cumulative distribution function**.

For an interior threshold $0<y<9$, event $X^2\le y$ requires $X$ to lie between the two roots $-\sqrt y$ and $\sqrt y$ of $x^2=y$. Both roots lie in $(-3,3)$ because $\sqrt y<3$. The relevant interval has length $2\sqrt y$. Dividing by the original length $6$ gives the cumulative distribution function $\dfrac{\sqrt y}{3}$.

Differentiate on the interior to find the density, since $\dfrac{d}{dy}\sqrt y=\dfrac1{2\sqrt y}$. The density $\dfrac1{6\sqrt y}$ tends to $+\infty$ as $y\to0^+$, yet its area over $(0,9)$ is finite and equals $F_Y(9)=1$.

The cumulative distribution function method accounts for both roots from the outset. For $y<0$, event $X^2\le y$ is impossible. For $y=0$ it equals $\{X=0\}$, which has probability zero. For $y\ge9$ it is certain.

</div>
