## Exercise 1

A laboratory examines a component. Event $A$ denotes a seal defect and $B$ a dimensional defect. Given $P(A)=0.18$, $P(B)=0.12$ and $P(A\cap B)=0.05$, calculate the probability that the component has at least one defect and the probability that it has neither.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A\cup B)&=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\\[6pt]
&=0.18+0.12-0.05=0.25.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A\setminus B)&=0.18-0.05=0.13,\\[6pt]
P(B\setminus A)&=0.12-0.05=0.07,\\[6pt]
P(A\cup B)&=0.13+0.05+0.07=0.25.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P((A\cup B)^c)=1-0.25=0.75.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The event “at least one defect” is the union $A\cup B$. The two events are not mutually exclusive because $P(A\cap B)=0.05>0$, so we cannot simply add their probabilities. Apply the **inclusion–exclusion formula**. Adding the marginal probabilities counts components with both defects twice, so subtract $0.05$ once.

We get the same result by splitting the union into three pairwise disjoint events: a seal defect only, both defects, and a dimensional defect only. Since $A$ is the disjoint union of $A\setminus B$ and $A\cap B$, we have $P(A\setminus B)=0.18-0.05=0.13$. Likewise, $P(B\setminus A)=0.07$. By additivity, the three probabilities sum to $0.25$.

The event “neither defect” is the complement of the union: every component either has at least one defect or has none. By the complement rule, the **probability of no defect is $0.75$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Of $80$ applications, $34$ require a technical check, $28$ an administrative check and $12$ both. One application is selected uniformly at random. Calculate the probability that it requires exactly one check and the probability that it requires neither.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{technical\ only}}&=34-12=22,\\[6pt]
N_{\mathrm{administrative\ only}}&=28-12=16,\\[6pt]
N_{\mathrm{one}}&=22+16=38.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{at\ least\ one}}&=34+28-12=50,\\[6pt]
N_{\mathrm{none}}&=80-50=30.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P(\text{exactly one check})=\frac{38}{80}=\frac{19}{40}.
$$

$$
P(\text{no checks})=\frac{30}{80}=\frac{3}{8}.
$$

::venn{zone="a,b" nomi="T,A" elementi="a:22;ab:12;b:16;u:30" universo=1 ambiente="Ω" titolo="Cases receiving a technical check T or an administrative check A. The shaded regions contain the 38 cases receiving exactly one check."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each initial count includes the $12$ applications that require both checks. Subtracting these from each count leaves $22$ applications requiring only a technical check and $16$ requiring only an administrative check. These regions are disjoint, so $38$ applications require exactly one check.

For the second probability, count all applications that require at least one check. By inclusion–exclusion for set sizes, the common group must be counted only once. The union therefore contains $34+28-12=50$ applications and leaves $80-50=30$ outside. The four regions of the diagram contain $22+12+16+30=80$ applications, accounting for all of them.

Uniform selection gives each application probability $\dfrac{1}{80}$, so the probability of an event is the number of applications it contains divided by $80$. Thus **the probability of exactly one check is $\dfrac{19}{40}$** and **the probability of neither check is $\dfrac{3}{8}$**.

</div>
