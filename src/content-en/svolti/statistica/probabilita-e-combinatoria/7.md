## Exercise 1

A chair and a deputy are chosen from eight people. The positions must go to different people. Count the assignments in which Lucia holds one of the two positions.

$$
N_{\mathrm{Lucia\ as\ supervisor}}=7,\qquad
N_{\mathrm{Lucia\ as\ deputy}}=7.
$$

$$
N=7+7=14.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{total}}&=D_{8,2}=8\cdot7=56,\\[6pt]
N_{\mathrm{without\ Lucia}}&=D_{7,2}=7\cdot6=42,\\[6pt]
N&=56-42=14.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The chair and deputy have different duties, so exchanging the same two people gives a different assignment. If Lucia is chair, there are seven possible deputies. If Lucia is deputy, there are seven possible chairs. The two cases are mutually exclusive because the positions go to different people and Lucia cannot hold both. They also cover every assignment requested, so we add the counts to obtain **$14$ assignments**.

We can obtain the same number using the complement. All unrestricted assignments are **ordered selections** of two distinct people from eight, giving $D_{8,2}=8\cdot7=56$. The assignments in which Lucia holds neither position use only the other seven people, giving $D_{7,2}=7\cdot6=42$. Every assignment belongs to just one of the two groups, “Lucia holds a position” and “Lucia holds neither position,” so the difference $56-42$ counts the first group.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A shift has three distinct duties to assign to three different people chosen from four operators and three technicians. The first duty requires a technician, and at least one of the other two must be assigned to an operator. Count the possible assignments.

$$
N_{\mathrm{first\ technician}}=3\cdot6\cdot5=90.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{all\ technicians}}=3\cdot2\cdot1=6.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{allowed}}=90-6=84.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{(O,O)}&=3\cdot4\cdot3=36,\\[6pt]
N_{(O,T)}&=3\cdot4\cdot2=24,\\[6pt]
N_{(T,O)}&=3\cdot2\cdot4=24,\\[6pt]
N_{\mathrm{allowed}}&=36+24+24=84.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three technicians to choose from for the first duty. After choosing one, assign the other two duties in order from the six available people, without repetition. This count includes assignments using only technicians, in which the three duties go to all three technicians. There are $3!=6$ such assignments, so remove them from the initial count. Every remaining assignment includes an operator in at least one of the last two roles. The number of permitted assignments is therefore **$84$**.

A direct count distinguishes who fills the second and third roles, using $O$ for an operator and $T$ for a technician. In case $(O,O)$, choose two distinct operators in order, in $4\cdot3$ ways. In case $(O,T)$, the third role goes to one of the two technicians left after the first role is filled. In case $(T,O)$, the second role goes to one of those two technicians and the third to one of the four operators. The three cases are mutually exclusive and exhaust the permitted assignments, so adding their counts again gives $84$.

</div>
