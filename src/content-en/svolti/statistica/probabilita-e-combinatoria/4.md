## Exercise 1

An identifier consists of one of the letters $A,B,C,D$ followed by two digits from $0$ to $9$. The digits may be the same. Count the identifiers whose last digit is even.

$$
N_{\mathrm{letters}}=4,\qquad N_{\mathrm{first\ digit}}=10.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{last\ digits}}=|\{0,2,4,6,8\}|=5.
$$

$$
N=4\cdot 10\cdot 5=200.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{unrestricted}}=4\cdot10\cdot10=400,\qquad \frac{200}{400}=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Construct the identifier in three stages: the letter, the middle digit and the final digit. To apply the **multiplication principle**, the number of choices at each stage must be the same for every choice made at the preceding stages.

There are four choices for the first position, the letter. The middle digit is unrestricted, giving ten choices regardless of the letter. The final digit must be even and may equal the middle digit because repetitions are allowed. For each opening pair there are exactly five possible final digits, namely $0,2,4,6,8$. The product $4\cdot10\cdot5$ gives **$200$ identifiers**.

Without the restriction, there would be ten choices for the final digit and $400$ identifiers. Five of the ten digits are even, so the restriction halves the total. This works because it affects only the last stage and leaves the choices at earlier stages unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using the digits $0,1,2,3,4,5$, form four-digit numbers with distinct digits and an odd final digit. How many such numbers are there?

$$
N_{\mathrm{last}}=|\{1,3,5\}|=3.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{before}}=|\{1,2,3,4,5\}|-1=4.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{second}}=6-2=4,\qquad N_{\mathrm{third}}=6-3=3.
$$

$$
N=3\cdot4\cdot4\cdot3=144.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the final digit first, since this is the position that must be odd. The available odd digits are $1$, $3$ and $5$, so there are three choices.

The first digit must be nonzero and different from the final digit. There are five nonzero digits, one of which is already in the final position. That leaves four choices for the first digit, whichever odd digit was chosen. The number of available choices is therefore constant.

Two of the six digits have now been used, and zero is still available. There are $6-2=4$ choices for the second digit and $6-3=3$ for the third because each digit can appear at most once.

At each stage the number of choices is independent of the preceding choices, so the multiplication principle applies. This gives **$144$ numbers**.

If we filled the positions from left to right, the multiplication principle would not apply directly. If the first digit is odd, there are two choices for the last digit. If it is even, there are three. Filling the restricted position first keeps the number of choices at each stage constant.

</div>
