## Exercise 1

Form three-letter codes from $A,B,C,D$, allowing repetitions. Count the codes that contain exactly two As.

$$
N_{\mathrm{positions\ of\ A}}=\binom{3}{2}=3.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{remaining\ letters}}=3.
$$

$$
N=3\cdot3=9.
$$

$$
AAx,\qquad AxA,\qquad xAA,\qquad x\in\{B,C,D\}.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{total}}=4^3=64.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A code with exactly two As is determined by the positions of the As and the remaining letter. The two A positions form a two-element subset of the three positions, giving $\binom{3}{2}=3$ choices. The three patterns are $AAx$, $AxA$ and $xAA$, where $x$ denotes the letter in the only position without an A.

The free position cannot contain an A, since that would give three As. It can contain B, C or D. Permitting repetitions gives no further choices because the only letter different from A occurs once.

Choosing the A positions first gives three disjoint cases, since each code has a unique position without an A. Each case allows three letters in that position. By the **multiplication principle**, the three equal counts give **$9$ codes**. There are $4^3=64$ codes in total when repetitions are allowed, since each position allows four letters.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using the digits $0,1,2,3,4,5$, form three-digit numbers with distinct digits that are divisible by $5$. How many such numbers are there?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{ultima\ cifra\ }0}&=N_{\mathrm{before}}\cdot N_{\mathrm{central}}\\[6pt]
&=5\cdot4=20.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{ultima\ cifra\ }5}&=N_{\mathrm{before}}\cdot N_{\mathrm{central}}\\[6pt]
&=4\cdot4=16.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
N=20+16=36.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A number divisible by $5$ ends in $0$ or $5$. The first digit must be nonzero, so the number of choices for the first digit depends on the last digit. The multiplication principle therefore does not apply to all the numbers at once. Count the two cases separately.

If the number ends in $0$, zero has been used and any of the five remaining digits $1,2,3,4,5$ can come first. If it ends in $5$, the first digit can be neither zero nor $5$, leaving only $1$, $2$, $3$ or $4$.

In either case, fixing the two end positions uses two of the six digits and leaves four for the middle position. In the second case these four include zero, which is allowed in the middle.

The cases are disjoint because a number has only one final digit. Adding their counts gives **$36$ numbers**.

</div>
