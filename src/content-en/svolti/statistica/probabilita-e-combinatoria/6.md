## Exercise 1

Count the distinct strings that can be formed by rearranging all the letters of TATTO.

$$
n=5,\qquad n_{\mathrm{T}}=3.
$$

$$
N=\frac{5!}{3!\,1!\,1!}=\frac{120}{6}=20.
$$

$$
N=\binom{5}{3}\cdot2!=10\cdot2=20.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

TATTO has five letters: three Ts, one A and one O. Apply the formula for **permutations with indistinguishable objects**, with $n=5$ and group sizes $3$, $1$ and $1$.

If the three Ts were given distinct labels, $T_1,T_2,T_3$, the five symbols could be arranged in $5!=120$ ways. In any visible string the positions of A and O are fixed, and the three T positions can receive the labels in $3!=6$ orders. All these orders give the same visible string, so each string occurs $6$ times and we divide by $3!$.

Alternatively, choose the three positions for the Ts and then the order of A and O. The T positions form a three-element subset of five positions, giving $\binom{5}{3}=10$ choices. The distinct letters A and O can occupy the remaining two positions in $2!=2$ ways. Both counts give **$20$ distinct strings**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rearrange all the letters of LETTERA. Count the distinct strings that begin with E and end with T.

$$
\mathrm{LETTERA}\longrightarrow
\mathrm{A},\mathrm{E},\mathrm{L},\mathrm{R},\mathrm{T}
\quad\text{in the interior positions}.
$$

$$
N=5!=120.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{unrestricted}}=\frac{7!}{2!\,2!}=\frac{5040}{4}=1260.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The word contains two Es and two Ts. Fixing one E at the beginning and one T at the end leaves one E, one T and the letters A, L and R for the five inner positions. These five letters are all distinct, so each permitted string has a unique inner arrangement. The Es are indistinguishable, as are the Ts. Choosing which copy goes at each end therefore adds no extra factor. This gives **$120$ strings**.

Without the restrictions, the formula for permutations with indistinguishable objects, applied with $n=7$ and two pairs of identical letters, gives $1260$ strings. Once an E and a T are fixed at the ends, only one copy of each letter remains for the inner positions. This is why the inner count involves no division by factorials.

</div>
