## Exercise 1

An experiment records the first two calls of the morning, distinguishing between support $A$ and information $I$. Let $E$ be the event “the first call is about support” and $F$ the event “both calls concern the same service.” Find $E\cap F$ and $E\setminus F$.

$$
\Omega=\{(A,A),(A,I),(I,A),(I,I)\},\qquad |\Omega|=2\cdot2=4.
$$

$$
E=\{(A,A),(A,I)\},\qquad F=\{(A,A),(I,I)\}.
$$

$$
E\cap F=\{(A,A)\},\qquad E\setminus F=\{(A,I)\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each call concerns one of two services, so the multiplication principle gives $2\cdot2=4$ ordered pairs. The pair records the order of the calls. Thus $(A,I)$ and $(I,A)$ are different outcomes: in the first, the support call comes before the information call, and in the second it comes after.

To form $E$, put $A$ first and leave the second position unrestricted. Thus $E$ contains two outcomes. To form $F$, keep the pairs with two identical symbols, namely $(A,A)$ and $(I,I)$.

The intersection contains the outcomes that belong to both events. The only pair with a support call first and two identical symbols is $(A,A)$, so the intersection contains just one outcome. The pair $(I,I)$ belongs to $F$, but its first call concerns information, so it does not belong to $E$ and is outside the intersection.

The difference $E\setminus F$ contains the outcomes in $E$ that lie outside $F$. The pair $(A,I)$ belongs to $E$ and has two different symbols, so it is the only outcome in the difference. We conclude that **$E\cap F=\{(A,A)\}$ and $E\setminus F=\{(A,I)\}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A box contains eight cards numbered from $1$ to $8$. One card is drawn. Let $M$ be the event “the number is a multiple of $3$” and $D$ the event “the number is odd.” Find the outcomes in which exactly one event occurs and those in which neither occurs.

$$
\Omega=\{1,2,\ldots,8\},\qquad M=\{3,6\},\qquad D=\{1,3,5,7\}.
$$

$$
M\cap D=\{3\}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(M\setminus D)\cup(D\setminus M)&=\{6\}\cup\{1,5,7\}\\[6pt]
&=\{1,5,6,7\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(M\cup D)^c=\{1,3,5,6,7\}^c=\{2,4,8\}.
$$

::venn{zone="a,b" nomi="M,D" elementi="a:6;ab:3;b:1,5,7;u:2,4,8" universo=1 ambiente="Ω" titolo="The shaded regions contain the outcomes satisfying exactly one condition. The values 2, 4, and 8 lie outside both circles."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Between $1$ and $8$, the multiples of $3$ are $3$ and $6$, while the odd numbers are $1,3,5,7$. The number $3$ is the only outcome common to the two events, so $M\cap D=\{3\}$.

The event “exactly one of the two” occurs when the outcome belongs to one event and not the other. Take the union of the two differences $M\setminus D=\{6\}$ and $D\setminus M=\{1,5,7\}$, both of which exclude $3$.

To find the outcomes that satisfy neither condition, start with the full sample space and remove all the elements of the union $M\cup D=\{1,3,5,6,7\}$. The number $3$ belongs to the union, so it is excluded from this second result as well. This leaves **$2$, $4$ and $8$**.

The three sets $\{3\}$, $\{1,5,6,7\}$ and $\{2,4,8\}$ are pairwise disjoint and contain $1+4+3=8$ outcomes altogether. They therefore form a partition of $\Omega$, as the three regions of the diagram show.

</div>
