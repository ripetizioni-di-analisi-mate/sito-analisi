## Exercise 1

A batch contains nine distinct items, three of them defective. Two items are drawn uniformly at random without replacement. Calculate the probability of drawing exactly one defective item.

$$
|\Omega|=\binom{9}{2}=36.
$$

$$
|A|=\binom{3}{1}\binom{6}{1}=18.
$$

$$
P(A)=\frac{18}{36}=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

$$
P(D,I)+P(I,D)=\frac{3}{9}\cdot\frac{6}{8}+\frac{6}{9}\cdot\frac{3}{8}=\frac{18}{72}+\frac{18}{72}=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each two-item subset has the same probability because the two ordered draws producing it are each as likely as any other ordered draw of distinct items. The denominator therefore counts all unordered pairs. A favorable pair is determined by choosing one of the three defective items and one of the six nondefective items. Changing the order leaves the pair unchanged, so the product directly counts the eighteen favorable outcomes. This calculation uses the formula for **uniform sampling without replacement** with $N=9$, $R=3$, $n=2$ and $k=1$.

The same result follows by taking the order of the draws into account, using $D$ for a defective item and $I$ for a nondefective one. For sequence $(D,I)$, the first draw has probability $\dfrac{3}{9}$ and the second has probability $\dfrac{6}{8}$ because eight items remain after the first draw, six of them nondefective. Sequence $(I,D)$ has the same probability, and the two sequences are mutually exclusive. The **probability of exactly one defective item is therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A batch contains five type X items, four type Y items and three type Z items. Three items are drawn uniformly at random without replacement. Calculate the probability that exactly two types are represented.

$$
|\Omega|=\binom{12}{3}=\frac{12\cdot11\cdot10}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=220.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{one\ type}}&=\binom{5}{3}+\binom{4}{3}+\binom{3}{3}\\[6pt]
&=10+4+1=15,\\[6pt]
N_{\mathrm{three\ types}}&=5\cdot4\cdot3=60.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P(\text{exactly two types})=\frac{220-15-60}{220}=\frac{145}{220}=\frac{29}{44}.
$$

The shaded group of $145$ samples is the event in question."}

<div class="spiegazione">

A sample of three items represents one, two or three types, and these events are mutually exclusive. Count the first case by selecting three items of a single type and adding the counts for the three types. In the third case each type must occur once, so multiply the three group sizes. Subtracting both counts from the total number of samples gives $220-15-60=145$ samples with exactly two types. Uniform sampling makes all three-item subsets equally likely, so dividing by the total $220$ gives **$\dfrac{29}{44}$**.

A direct count confirms the figure of $145$. For types X and Y, choose two items of one type and one of the other, giving $\binom{5}{2}\cdot4+5\cdot\binom{4}{2}=40+30=70$ samples. The same reasoning gives $30+15=45$ samples for types X and Z and $18+12=30$ for types Y and Z, with $70+45+30=145$.

</div>
