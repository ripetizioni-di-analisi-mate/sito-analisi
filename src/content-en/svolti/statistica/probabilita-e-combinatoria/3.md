## Exercise 1

Two fair dice are rolled independently. Calculate the probability that the sum is $9$.

$$
\Omega=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}^2,\qquad |\Omega|=36.
$$

$$
P(\{(i,j)\})=\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{6}=\frac{1}{36}.
$$

$$
j=9-i,\qquad 1\le 9-i\le 6\iff 3\le i\le 6.
$$

$$
A=\{(3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3)\}.
$$

$$
P(A)=\frac{|A|}{|\Omega|}=\frac{4}{36}=\frac{1}{9}.
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=7 ymin=-0.4 ymax=7 nomi="i,j" segmenti="1,1,6,1;1,2,6,2;1,3,6,3;1,4,6,4;1,5,6,5;1,6,6,6;1,1,1,6;2,1,2,6;3,1,3,6;4,1,4,6;5,1,5,6;6,1,6,6" punti="1,1,vuoto;1,2,vuoto;1,3,vuoto;1,4,vuoto;1,5,vuoto;1,6,vuoto;2,1,vuoto;2,2,vuoto;2,3,vuoto;2,4,vuoto;2,5,vuoto;2,6,vuoto;3,1,vuoto;3,2,vuoto;3,3,vuoto;3,4,vuoto;3,5,vuoto;3,6,pieno;4,1,vuoto;4,2,vuoto;4,3,vuoto;4,4,vuoto;4,5,pieno;4,6,vuoto;5,1,vuoto;5,2,vuoto;5,3,vuoto;5,4,pieno;5,5,vuoto;5,6,vuoto;6,1,vuoto;6,2,vuoto;6,3,pieno;6,4,vuoto;6,5,vuoto;6,6,vuoto" titolo="The grid shows outcomes by the value i of the first die and j of the second. The four filled points have a sum of 9."}

<div class="spiegazione">

We distinguish the first die from the second, so $(3,6)$ and $(6,3)$ are different outcomes. Each die has six faces, so by the multiplication principle there are $6\cdot6=36$ ordered pairs.

The dice are fair, so each face has probability $\dfrac{1}{6}$. Since the rolls are independent, each pair has probability $\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{1}{6}=\dfrac{1}{36}$. All $36$ pairs are therefore equally likely, and we can use the **finite model with equally likely outcomes**, $P(A)=\dfrac{|A|}{|\Omega|}$.

Once the first value $i$ is fixed, the second must equal $j=9-i$. Also, $j$ must lie between $1$ and $6$, and the inequality $9-i\le6$ is equivalent to $i\ge3$. The values $i=1$ and $i=2$ would give $j=8$ and $j=7$, neither of which appears on a die. This leaves four pairs, shown as solid points on the grid.

Dividing the number of favorable pairs by $36$ gives the **probability of a sum of $9$ as $\dfrac{1}{9}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One token is drawn uniformly at random from a bag containing three yellow tokens numbered $1,2,3$ and five green tokens numbered $1,2,3,4,5$. Calculate the probability that the token is green or has an even number.

$$
\Omega=\{g_1,g_2,g_3,v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4,v_5\},\qquad N=8.
$$

$$
V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_5\},\qquad Q=\{g_2,v_2,v_4\},\qquad V\cap Q=\{v_2,v_4\}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{even}}&=1+2=3,\\[6pt]
N_{\mathrm{green\ and\ even}}&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(V\cup Q)&=P(V)+P(Q)-P(V\cap Q)\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5}{8}+\frac{3}{8}-\frac{2}{8}=\frac{6}{8}=\frac{3}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(V\cup Q)^c=\{g_1,g_3\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An elementary outcome is a single token, identified by its color and number. For example, $g_2$ is the yellow token numbered $2$, and $v_4$ is the green token numbered $4$. Tokens of different colors bearing the same number are therefore distinct outcomes. The draw is uniform over the eight tokens, so each has probability $\dfrac{1}{8}$.

The event $V$, “green,” contains five tokens. An even number appears on yellow token $g_2$ and green tokens $v_2$ and $v_4$, so event $Q$, “even,” contains three tokens. Both even-numbered green tokens belong to both events, so $V$ and $Q$ are not mutually exclusive.

By the **inclusion–exclusion formula**, subtract the probability of the intersection once so that the union counts $v_2$ and $v_4$ just once each. Thus **the probability of a green or even-numbered token is $\dfrac{3}{4}$**. The excluded outcomes are the odd-numbered yellow tokens $g_1$ and $g_3$, whose combined probability is $\dfrac{2}{8}=\dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>
