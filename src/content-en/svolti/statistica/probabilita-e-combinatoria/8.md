## Exercise 1

Four of ten distinct projects are selected for inspection. One particular project must be included. Count the possible selections.

$$
N=\binom{9}{3}.
$$

$$
\binom{9}{3}=\frac{9\cdot8\cdot7}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=\frac{504}{6}=84.
$$

$$
\binom{10}{4}=\frac{10\cdot9\cdot8\cdot7}{4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1}=210,\qquad \frac{84}{210}=\frac{2}{5}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required project is already selected, leaving three places to fill from the other nine projects. A selection is identified by the subset of projects chosen. Removing the required project gives a one-to-one correspondence between permitted selections and three-element subsets of the other nine projects. The inspection order plays no part in the outcome, so use the binomial coefficient. The product $9\cdot8\cdot7=504$ would count every selection $3!=6$ times, once for each ordering of the three freely chosen projects. Dividing $504$ by $6$ gives **$84$ selections**.

Without the restriction there are $\binom{10}{4}=210$ ways to select four projects from ten. The particular project therefore appears in $\dfrac{84}{210}=\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the selections. The proportion equals $\dfrac{4}{10}$ because, under uniform selection, each of the ten projects has the same chance of occupying one of the four places.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Form a group of four by choosing exactly two of five trainees and two of six employees. Count the groups if one particular trainee and one particular employee cannot both be included.

$$
\binom{5}{2}=\frac{5\cdot4}{2}=10,\qquad \binom{6}{2}=\frac{6\cdot5}{2}=15.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{total}}=\binom{5}{2}\binom{6}{2}=10\cdot15=150.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{excluded}}=\binom{4}{1}\binom{5}{1}=4\cdot5=20.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{allowed}}=150-20=130.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To determine the group, choose two trainees and two employees separately. The choices can be combined because the two sets are distinct. To count the forbidden groups, include both people who cannot serve together and complete the group with one of the four other trainees and one of the five other employees. The specified pair appears exactly once in each forbidden group, so subtract exactly twenty groups.

The forbidden and permitted groups are disjoint and together account for all $150$ groups with the required composition. We therefore have **$130$ permitted groups**. A group containing just one member of the specified pair, or neither, satisfies the restriction and stays in the count.

</div>
