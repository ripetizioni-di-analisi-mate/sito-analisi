## Exercise 1

Six distinct panels are displayed in a row. Two particular panels must be next to each other. Count the possible displays.

$$
N_{\mathrm{unit}}=5,\qquad N_{\mathrm{internal\ orderings}}=2.
$$

$$
5!=5\cdot4\cdot3\cdot2\cdot1=120.
$$

$$
N=5!\cdot2=120\cdot2=240.
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{unrestricted}}=6!=720,\qquad \frac{240}{720}=\frac{1}{3}.
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=0 xmax=9.4 ymin=0.5 ymax=5.2 poligoni="4.62,3.92 6.38,3.92 6.38,4.64 4.62,4.64;4.62,1.42 6.38,1.42 6.38,2.14 4.62,2.14" vettori="5.5,3.62,5.5,2.42" etichette="0.18,4.18,6 panels;2.83,4.18,U;3.83,4.18,V;4.83,4.18,A;5.83,4.18,B;6.83,4.18,W;7.83,4.18,X;0.18,1.68,5 units;2.83,1.68,U;3.83,1.68,V;5.15,1.68,AB;6.83,1.68,W;7.83,1.68,X" titolo="Each admissible row counts as an arrangement of five units. The two panels in the block can appear as AB or BA."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $A$ and $B$ be the panels that must be adjacent. In every permitted display they occupy consecutive positions, so we can treat them as a single block. This leaves five distinct units: the block and the four individual panels $U,V,W,X$.

All five distinct units must be arranged, giving $5!=120$ **permutations**. For each permutation, the block can be expanded in two ways, $AB$ or $BA$, since either panel can come first. By the **multiplication principle**, the total is $120\cdot2=240$.

The product counts every display once. In any permitted display, we can read off both the order of the five units and the order of the panels within the block. Exactly one pair of choices produces that display.

Without the restriction, the six panels can be arranged in $6!=720$ ways. We obtain **$240$ displays**, one third of all possible displays.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Seven distinct books are arranged on a shelf. The dictionary must be at the far left and two particular manuals must not be adjacent. Count the permitted arrangements.

$$
N_{\mathrm{with\ the\ dictionary\ on\ the\ left}}=6!=720.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{adjacent\ textbooks}}&=5!\cdot2\\[6pt]
&=120\cdot2=240.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{allowed}}=720-240=480.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The dictionary is fixed in place, so there is no choice to make for it. The other six books are distinct and occupy the six remaining positions. There are therefore $6!=720$ arrangements with the dictionary on the left.

It is easier to count the arrangements with nonadjacent manuals using the complement. In a forbidden arrangement the two manuals occupy consecutive positions between the second and seventh positions. Treating the manuals as one block leaves five distinct units, the block and the other four books, to arrange after the dictionary. The units can be arranged in $5!=120$ ways. For each arrangement there are two orders within the block, giving $240$ forbidden arrangements.

The arrangements with the dictionary on the left fall into two disjoint groups: those with adjacent manuals and those with separated manuals. Subtract the first group from the total to obtain **$480$ permitted arrangements**, two thirds of the $720$ arrangements.

</div>
