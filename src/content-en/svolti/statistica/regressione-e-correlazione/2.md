## Exercise 1

Find the least-squares line with an intercept for $(0,1)$, $(1,2)$, and $(2,6)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{0+1+2}{3}=1,\qquad \bar y=\frac{1+2+6}{3}=3,\\[6pt]
(x_i-\bar x)&=(-1,\;0,\;1),\qquad (y_i-\bar y)=(-2,\;-1,\;3),\\[6pt]
S_{xx}&=(-1)^2+0^2+1^2=2,\\[6pt]
S_{xy}&=(-1)(-2)+0(-1)+1\cdot3=5.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
b&=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}=\frac52,\\[6pt]
a&=\bar y-b\bar x=3-\frac52\cdot1=\frac12,\\[6pt]
\hat y(x)&=\frac12+\frac52x.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.5+2.5*x" xmin=-0.35 xmax=2.65 ymin=-0.5 ymax=7.3 punti="0,1;1,2;2,6;1,3,vuoto,C" segmenti="1,0,1,3;0,3,1,3" legenda="\hat y(x)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{2}x" titolo="The fitted line passes through C, the point of means. The projections give its coordinates (1, 3)."}

<div class="spiegazione">

First calculate the means and their deviations for the centered sums. The predictor values differ, so $S_{xx}=2$ is positive. This meets assumption $S_{xx}>0$ of the **least-squares line** theorem, giving a unique slope.

The two extreme observations have same-sign deviations and contribute $2$ and $3$ to $S_{xy}$. The central observation has zero deviation in $x$ and contributes zero. Thus $S_{xy}=5$ and the slope is $b=\dfrac52$.

Find the intercept using $a=\bar y-b\bar x$, equivalent to requiring the line to pass through the point of means $(1,3)$. Substitution gives $a=\dfrac12$. As a check, $\hat y(1)=\dfrac12+\dfrac52=3=\bar y$. The positive slope means the fitted response **increases by $2.5$ units** when the predictor increases by one unit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four measurements give the pairs $(1,8)$, $(2,5)$, $(3,7)$, and $(4,4)$. Find the fitted line with an intercept and evaluate it at $x=5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{1+2+3+4}{4}=\frac52,\qquad \bar y=\frac{8+5+7+4}{4}=6,\\[6pt]
(x_i-\bar x)&=\left(-\frac32,\;-\frac12,\;\frac12,\;\frac32\right),\\[6pt]
(y_i-\bar y)&=(2,\;-1,\;1,\;-2),\\[6pt]
S_{xx}&=\frac94+\frac14+\frac14+\frac94=5,\\[6pt]
S_{xy}&=-3+\frac12+\frac12-3=-5.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
b&=\frac{-5}{5}=-1,\\[6pt]
a&=6-(-1)\cdot\frac52=\frac{17}{2},\\[6pt]
\hat y(x)&=\frac{17}{2}-x,\\[6pt]
\hat y(5)&=\frac{17}{2}-5=\frac72.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The predictor values are distinct, so $S_{xx}=5>0$ and the least-squares line is unique. The first pair contributes $\left(-\dfrac32\right)\cdot2=-3$ and the last $\dfrac32\cdot(-2)=-3$ to the centered sum of cross-products. The two middle pairs contribute $\left(-\dfrac12\right)(-1)=\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac12\cdot1=\dfrac12$.

The negative contributions of the two extreme pairs outweigh the two middle contributions. Thus the slope is negative even though the second and third observations alone suggest an increase. The slope is $b=-1$, so the fitted response decreases by one unit for each additional unit of $x$.

The intercept follows from $\hat y(\bar x)=\bar y$, or $a=6+\dfrac52=\dfrac{17}{2}$. The fitted value at $x=5$ is $\dfrac72$. Since the observed predictor values extend only to $4$, interpreting this value as a prediction requires extending the model beyond the sample.

</div>
