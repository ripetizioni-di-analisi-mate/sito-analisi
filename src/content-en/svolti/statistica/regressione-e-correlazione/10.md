## Exercise 1

At predictor values $1,2,4$ two residuals per value were observed, respectively $(-1,1)$, $(-2,2)$, and $(-5,5)$. Calculate the vertical range of the residuals at each predictor value and interpret the pattern.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar e_1&=\frac{-1+1}{2}=0,\qquad \bar e_2=\frac{-2+2}{2}=0,\qquad \bar e_4=\frac{-5+5}{2}=0,\\[6pt]
A_1&=1-(-1)=2,\\[6pt]
A_2&=2-(-2)=4,\\[6pt]
A_4&=5-(-5)=10.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_1^2&=\frac{(-1)^2+1^2}{2-1}=2,\qquad s_2^2=\frac{(-2)^2+2^2}{2-1}=8,\qquad s_4^2=\frac{(-5)^2+5^2}{2-1}=50,\\[6pt]
\frac{s_4^2}{s_1^2}&=\frac{50}{2}=25.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0" xmin=-0.4 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=6 nomi="x,e" punti="1,-1;1,1;2,-2;2,2;4,-5;4,5" legenda="e=0" titolo="The vertical spread of the residuals increases with the horizontal coordinate."}

<div class="spiegazione">

At each predictor value the two residuals are negatives of one another, so their mean is zero and the **residual plot** shows no shift in center. These data therefore do not show a curved pattern of the kind produced by positive residuals at the extremes and negative residuals in the middle.

The vertical range is the largest residual minus the smallest at the same predictor value. It increases from $2$ to $4$ and then $10$, so the residual band widens as $x$ increases. The sample variances of the two residuals at each predictor value, using denominator $2-1=1$, are $2$, $8$, and $50$. At the last predictor value the variance is **twenty-five times** that at the first.

This suggests **heteroscedasticity**, or nonconstant error variance. The interval model assumes common variance $\sigma^2$. For these data, intervals using a single $s_e$ would be too wide at small $x$ and too narrow at large $x$. The few observations reveal a possible pattern, while a firmer diagnosis requires more observations.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An observational study uses $x$ values between $20$ and $30$ and estimates $\hat y(x)=40-0.5x$ with $R^2=0.92$. Calculate the estimates at $25$ and $60$, identify the extrapolation, and discuss whether the slope has a causal interpretation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat y(25)&=40-0.5\cdot25=40-12.5=27.5,\\[6pt]
\hat y(60)&=40-0.5\cdot60=40-30=10,\\[6pt]
\hat y(x)&=0\iff x=\frac{40}{0.5}=80.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
20\le25\le30,\qquad 60>30.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value $25$ belongs to the observed range $[20,30]$, so the fitted value $27.5$ is an **interpolation** based on predictor values like those in the sample.

The predictor value $60$ lies $30$ units above the largest observation, making the estimate $10$ an **extrapolation**. It assumes the linear relationship holds beyond $30$, which the data cannot check. The same line reaches zero at $x=80$ and becomes negative beyond that point, which would be meaningless if, for example, $y$ represented a nonnegative quantity.

The value $R^2=0.92$ describes the fit to the observed data, the fraction of variability in $y$ explained by the line between $20$ and $30$. By itself it leaves the relationship's shape at $60$ undetermined.

The slope $-0.5$ means that in these data, units differing by one unit in $x$ have an average $y$ lower by $0.5$ for the higher predictor value. An observational design allows other variables to be associated with both $x$ and $y$, so the slope describes an **association**. It cannot by itself establish the effect of externally imposing a change in $x$.

</div>
