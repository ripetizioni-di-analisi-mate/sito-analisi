## Exercise 1

Suppose $S_{xx}=16$, $S_{yy}=25$, and $S_{xy}=-12$. Calculate $r$ and the coefficient of determination for the line with an intercept.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}&=\sqrt{16\cdot25}=\sqrt{400}=20,\\[6pt]
r&=\frac{S_{xy}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}}=\frac{-12}{20}=-\frac35,\\[6pt]
R^2&=r^2=\left(-\frac35\right)^2=\frac9{25}=0.36.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
b&=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}=\frac{-12}{16}=-\frac34,\\[6pt]
SSR&=b^2S_{xx}=\frac{9}{16}\cdot16=9,\\[6pt]
\frac{SSR}{SST}&=\frac{9}{25}=0.36.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both sums of squares are positive, so **Pearson's correlation coefficient** is defined. Its denominator is the square root of their product, $20$. Thus $r$ has the sign of the centered sum of cross-products, which is negative. The observed linear association is **negative**.

For a line with an intercept, $R^2=r^2$. Squaring Pearson's correlation gives the fraction of variability explained by the line. The slope provides another calculation. For the regression of $y$ on $x$ the slope is $-\dfrac34$, the explained sum of squares is $SSR=b^2S_{xx}=9$, and the total response sum of squares is $SST=S_{yy}=25$.

Thus $r=-0.6$ and $R^2=0.36$. The direction of the relationship is given by $r$, which has the sign of slope $b$. The fraction $R^2$ is always nonnegative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate Spearman's correlation for $(10,1)$, $(10,3)$, $(30,2)$, and $(40,4)$, assigning average ranks to ties.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x&=\left(\frac{1+2}{2},\frac{1+2}{2},3,4\right)=\left(\frac32,\frac32,3,4\right),\\[6pt]
R_y&=(1,3,2,4),\qquad \bar R_x=\bar R_y=\frac{1+2+3+4}{4}=\frac52,\\[6pt]
R_x-\bar R_x&=\left(-1,\;-1,\;\frac12,\;\frac32\right),\\[6pt]
R_y-\bar R_y&=\left(-\frac32,\;\frac12,\;-\frac12,\;\frac32\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{R_xR_x}&=1+1+\frac14+\frac94=\frac92,\\[6pt]
S_{R_yR_y}&=\frac94+\frac14+\frac14+\frac94=5,\\[6pt]
S_{R_xR_y}&=\frac32-\frac12-\frac14+\frac94=3,\\[6pt]
r_S&=\frac{3}{\sqrt{\frac92\cdot5}}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{\frac{45}{2}}}=\sqrt{\frac{9\cdot2}{45}}=\sqrt{\frac25}\approx0.632.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.35 xmax=4.9 ymin=-0.45 ymax=4.9 nomi="R_x,R_y" punti="1.5,1;1.5,3;3,2;4,4" segmenti="1.5,0,1.5,3;0,2.5,4.6,2.5" titolo="Rank plot. The two tied observations have the same horizontal coordinate, 1.5. The horizontal line marks the mean of the ranks on the vertical axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two predictor values equal to $10$ occupy first and second positions and each receives average rank $\dfrac32$. Values $30$ and $40$ have ranks $3$ and $4$. The responses have distinct ranks, which must be recorded in the original pair order, giving $R_y=(1,3,2,4)$.

Both columns have rank sum $10$ even with the tie, giving mean rank $\dfrac52$. Center the ranks and apply Pearson's formula to them. The four centered cross-products are $\dfrac32$, $-\dfrac12$, $-\dfrac14$, and $\dfrac94$, with sum $3$.

The result is $r_S=\sqrt{\dfrac25}\approx0.63$. Its positive sign reflects the **overall agreement** of the ranks, even though the second observation has a smaller predictor value and a higher response than the third.

</div>
