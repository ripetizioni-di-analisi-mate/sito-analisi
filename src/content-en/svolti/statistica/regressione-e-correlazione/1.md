## Exercise 1

For the pairs $(1,2)$, $(2,4)$, and $(3,3)$, calculate the means, $S_{xx}$, $S_{yy}$, and $S_{xy}$.

$$
\bar x=\frac{1+2+3}{3}=2,
\qquad \bar y=\frac{2+4+3}{3}=3.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_i-\bar x)&=(1-2,\;2-2,\;3-2)=(-1,\;0,\;1),\\[6pt]
(y_i-\bar y)&=(2-3,\;4-3,\;3-3)=(-1,\;1,\;0).
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xx}&=(-1)^2+0^2+1^2=2,\\[6pt]
S_{yy}&=(-1)^2+1^2+0^2=2,\\[6pt]
S_{xy}&=(-1)(-1)+0\cdot1+1\cdot0=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.3 xmax=4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=5 punti="1,2;2,4;3,3;2,3,vuoto,C" segmenti="2,0,2,4.6;0,3,3.7,3" titolo="The dashed lines intersect at the point of means $C=(2,3)$. Only the first point has two nonzero deviations, and these have the same sign."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Add each column and divide by $n=3$ to obtain $\bar x=2$ and $\bar y=3$. In the original pair order the predictor deviations are $-1,0,1$ and the response deviations $-1,1,0$. For $S_{xx}$ and $S_{yy}$, sum each variable's squared deviations separately.

For the cross-product retain the two deviations from the same unit, as required for **paired data**. The first observation has both measurements below their respective means, so its cross-product is positive and equals $1$. The second has predictor equal to its mean and the third has response equal to its mean, so each contributes zero. Consequently $S_{xy}=1$.

In the figure the dashed lines divide the plane into quadrants about the point of means. A point in the lower-left or upper-right quadrant contributes positively to $S_{xy}$, while points on the dashed lines contribute zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A table of four observations reports $\sum_i x_i=10$, $\sum_i y_i=18$, $\sum_i x_i^2=30$, $\sum_i y_i^2=94$, and $\sum_i x_i y_i=52$. Recover the three centered sums without reconstructing the pairs.

$$
\bar x=\frac{10}{4}=\frac52,
\qquad \bar y=\frac{18}{4}=\frac92.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xx}&=\sum_i x_i^2-n\bar x^2=30-4\cdot\frac{25}{4}=30-25=5,\\[6pt]
S_{yy}&=\sum_i y_i^2-n\bar y^2=94-4\cdot\frac{81}{4}=94-81=13,\\[6pt]
S_{xy}&=\sum_i x_iy_i-n\bar x\bar y=52-4\cdot\frac{45}{4}=52-45=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The column totals give the means even without the individual data because $\bar x=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_i x_i$ and $\bar y=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_i y_i$ with $n=4$.

Use the identities $S_{xx}=\sum_i x_i^2-n\bar x^2$ and $S_{xy}=\sum_i x_iy_i-n\bar x\bar y$ obtained by expanding the squares and products of deviations. From the sum of squares of $x$ subtract $4\cdot\left(\dfrac52\right)^2=25$, the contribution four values equal to $\bar x$ would make. Likewise subtract $4\cdot\left(\dfrac92\right)^2=81$ for $y$. From the sum of cross-products subtract $n\bar x\bar y=4\cdot\dfrac52\cdot\dfrac92=45$, the combined contribution of the means in the expansion.

The results $S_{xx}=5$ and $S_{yy}=13$ are positive because each sums squared deviations that are not all zero. The result $S_{xy}=7>0$ shows that products of same-sign deviations outweigh the others in the four pairs. The observed linear association is **positive**.

</div>
