## Exercise 1

A regression with an intercept has $SST=36$ and $SSE=9$. Calculate $SSR$, $R^2$, and the residual fraction of the total sum of squares.

$$
\begin{aligned}
SST&=SSR+SSE,\\[6pt]
SSR&=SST-SSE=36-9=27.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R^2&=\frac{SSR}{SST}=\frac{27}{36}=\frac34=0.75,\\[6pt]
\frac{SSE}{SST}&=\frac9{36}=\frac14=0.25,\\[6pt]
R^2+\frac{SSE}{SST}&=\frac34+\frac14=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-2 xmax=38 ymin=-1 ymax=2 poligoni="0,0 27,0 27,1 0,1" linee="0,0 36,0 36,1 0,1 0,0;27,0 27,1" etichette="10.5,0.43,SSR = 27;28.1,0.43,SSE = 9" titolo="The whole bar represents a total of 36. The shaded portion has length 27 and represents the 75% explained by regression."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The least-squares regression has an intercept, so the **decomposition of the total sum of squares** $SST=SSR+SSE$ applies. Subtract the residual component from the total to find $SSR=27$.

The coefficient of determination is the explained component divided by the total. Dividing each component by $36$ gives comparable fractions $\dfrac34$ and $\dfrac14$. They sum to $1$, as follows directly by dividing the decomposition by $SST$.

Thus $R^2=0.75$. The line explains **three-quarters** of the sum of squared deviations from the mean and one-quarter remains in the residuals. The second form of the definition, $R^2=1-\dfrac{SSE}{SST}=1-\dfrac14$, gives the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the data $(-1,1)$, $(0,6)$, and $(1,7)$, find $R^2$ and verify the decomposition by calculating $SSE$ directly from the residuals.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=0,\qquad \bar y=\frac{1+6+7}{3}=\frac{14}{3},\\[6pt]
(y_i-\bar y)&=\left(-\frac{11}{3},\;\frac43,\;\frac73\right),\\[6pt]
S_{xx}&=2,\qquad S_{xy}=(-1)\left(-\frac{11}{3}\right)+0\cdot\frac43+1\cdot\frac73=6,\\[6pt]
b&=\frac62=3,\qquad a=\frac{14}{3}-3\cdot0=\frac{14}{3},\\[6pt]
\hat y(x)&=\frac{14}{3}+3x.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
SST&=\frac{121+16+49}{9}=\frac{186}{9}=\frac{62}{3},\\[6pt]
(\hat y_1,\hat y_2,\hat y_3)&=\left(\frac53,\;\frac{14}{3},\;\frac{23}{3}\right),\\[6pt]
(e_1,e_2,e_3)&=\left(-\frac23,\;\frac43,\;-\frac23\right),\\[6pt]
SSE&=\frac{4+16+4}{9}=\frac83,\\[6pt]
SSR&=b^2S_{xx}=3^2\cdot2=18,\\[6pt]
SSR+SSE&=18+\frac83=\frac{62}{3}=SST,\\[6pt]
R^2&=\frac{18}{\frac{62}{3}}=\frac{54}{62}=\frac{27}{31}\approx0.871.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The predictor values $-1,0,1$ have mean zero. They are already centered and equal their own deviations. Since $\bar x=0$, the intercept $a=\bar y-b\bar x$ equals the mean response $\dfrac{14}{3}$, and the slope is $b=\dfrac{6}{2}=3$.

Find the total sum $SST$ by squaring the three deviations from the mean, whose numerators are $-11$, $4$, and $7$. Subtract the fitted values from the observed responses to obtain three residuals. Summing their squares gives $SSE=\dfrac83$ without using the decomposition.

Each fitted value differs from the mean by $3x_i$, giving the explained component $SSR=9\sum_i x_i^2=18$. The sum $18+\dfrac83$ equals $SST=\dfrac{62}{3}$ and verifies the decomposition. Thus $R^2=\dfrac{27}{31}$, meaning the line accounts for approximately $87\%$ of the response variability.

</div>
