## Exercise 1

From a normal population with known $\sigma=9\,\mathrm{g}$, observe $81$ independent weights with mean $120\,\mathrm{g}$. Calculate the one-sided $95\,\%$ upper limit using $z_{0.95}=1.645$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=0.05,\qquad q=z_{1-\alpha}=z_{0.95}=1.645,\\[6pt]
e&=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}=\frac9{\sqrt{81}}=\frac99=1\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
U&=\bar x+q\,e=(120+1.645\cdot1)\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=121.645\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
I&=(-\infty,121.645]\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The left-pointing arrow shows that the set has no lower bound."}

<div class="spiegazione">

An upper limit gives a confidence set containing all means up to $U$. The population is normal and its standard deviation known, so the standardized mean $Z=\dfrac{\bar X-\mu}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}}$ has the standard normal distribution.

The event $\mu\leq U$ is equivalent to $Z\geq-z_{0.95}$ and has probability $0.95$. The entire excluded $5\,\%$ lies in one tail, so use the quantile of order $0.95$, namely $1.645$.

The standard error is $\dfrac{9}{\sqrt{81}}=1\,\mathrm{g}$, so the margin equals the quantile, $1.645\,\mathrm{g}$. Add the margin to the observed mean.

The $95\,\%$ **upper limit** is therefore $121.645\,\mathrm{g}$. The confidence set is the half-line of values up to $121.645\,\mathrm{g}$, as the figure shows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample of $25$ independent normal measurements has mean $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and sample standard deviation $5\,\mathrm{mm}$. Calculate the one-sided $95\,\%$ lower limit and determine whether the confidence set contains $38\,\mathrm{mm}$. Use $t_{24,0.95}=1.711$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nu&=25-1=24,\qquad q=t_{24,0.95}=1.711,\\[6pt]
e&=\frac{s}{\sqrt n}=\frac5{\sqrt{25}}=\frac55=1\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
L&=\bar x-q\,e=(40-1.711\cdot1)\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
&=38.289\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
I&=[38.289,+\infty)\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
38\,\mathrm{mm}&<38.289\,\mathrm{mm}=L.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use Student's t distribution because the population standard deviation is estimated from the data. Twenty-five measurements give twenty-four degrees of freedom. The one-sided $95\,\%$ level uses the quantile of order $0.95$, namely $1.711$.

The estimated standard error is $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{25}}=1\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the margin is $1.711\,\mathrm{mm}$. For a **lower limit** subtract the margin from the observed mean to obtain $L=38.289\,\mathrm{mm}$. The confidence set includes all mean values greater than or equal to $L$.

The comparison value $38\,\mathrm{mm}$ is less than $38.289\,\mathrm{mm}$ and lies outside the set. At confidence level $95\,\%$, the value $38\,\mathrm{mm}$ is therefore **incompatible** with the data as a population mean. This comparison also corresponds to a one-sided test on the mean.

</div>
