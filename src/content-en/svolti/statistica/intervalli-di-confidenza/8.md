## Exercise 1

For $16$ independent pairs, the after-minus-before differences are normal with sample mean $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and sample standard deviation $4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Calculate the two-sided $95\,\%$ interval for the mean difference using $t_{15,0.975}=2.131$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nu&=16-1=15,\qquad q=t_{15,0.975}=2.131,\\[6pt]
e_D&=\frac{s_D}{\sqrt n}=\frac4{\sqrt{16}}=\frac44=1\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
h&=2.131\cdot1=2.131\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
I&=[5-2.131,\,5+2.131]\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
&=[2.869,7.131]\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each pair produces one difference and the pairs are independent. The sixteen differences therefore form a sample of sixteen independent normal observations. Apply the **one-sample t interval** to the differences. Measurements within a pair may be correlated, and the standard error based on differences accounts for that covariance.

The standard deviation of the differences is estimated from the data, so use a t quantile with $16-1=15$ degrees of freedom. A two-sided $95\,\%$ interval leaves $0.025$ in each tail, yielding the quantile of order $0.975$, namely $2.131$.

Divide the sample standard deviation of the differences by the square root of the sample size. Since $\sqrt{16}=4$, the estimated standard error is $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the margin equals the quantile, $2.131\,\mathrm{mm}$.

The $95\,\%$ interval for the mean after-minus-before change runs from $2.869$ to $7.131\,\mathrm{mm}$. Every value is positive, indicating a **mean increase** in the measurements under this model.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For five independent units, before measurements are $(11,12,13,14,15)$ and the corresponding after measurements are $(13,12,17,16,17)$, in millimeters. Assuming normal differences, calculate the $95\,\%$ interval for the mean after-minus-before difference. Use $t_{4,0.975}=2.776$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(d_1,\ldots,d_5)&=(13-11,\,12-12,\,17-13,\,16-14,\,17-15)\\[6pt]
&=(2,0,4,2,2)\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
\bar d&=\frac{2+0+4+2+2}{5}=\frac{10}{5}=2\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
d_i-\bar d&=0,\ -2,\ 2,\ 0,\ 0\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
s_D^2&=\frac{0+4+4+0+0}{4}
=2\,\mathrm{mm}^2,\\[6pt]
e_D&=\sqrt{\frac{s_D^2}{n}}=\sqrt{\frac25}\,\mathrm{mm}\approx0.6325\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
h&=2.776\sqrt{\frac25}\,\mathrm{mm}
\approx1.756\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
I&\approx[2-1.756,\,2+1.756]\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
&=[0.244,3.756]\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract each unit's before measurement from its after measurement so each difference belongs to one unit. The five differences are $2$, $0$, $4$, $2$, and $2\,\mathrm{mm}$, with mean $2\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Calculate dispersion using the five differences. Two lie $2\,\mathrm{mm}$ from their mean and the other three equal it, giving sum of squared deviations $8\,\mathrm{mm}^2$. Divisor $5-1=4$ gives unbiased sample variance $2\,\mathrm{mm}^2$.

The standard error is the square root of $\dfrac{2}{5}$, approximately $0.6325\,\mathrm{mm}$. The differences are normal and their variance is estimated, so use a **t interval for the differences** with four degrees of freedom and quantile $2.776$. The margin is approximately $1.756\,\mathrm{mm}$.

The t margin is below the observed mean difference, giving a positive lower limit. The $95\,\%$ interval for the mean after-minus-before change runs from approximately $0.244$ to $3.756\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>
