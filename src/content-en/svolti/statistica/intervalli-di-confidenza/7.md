## Exercise 1

Two independent normal samples have $n_X=n_Y=12$, $s_X^2=s_Y^2=24$, and an observed difference in means of $5$. Calculate the $95\,\%$ Welch interval using $t_{22,0.975}=2.074$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_X=v_Y&=\frac{24}{12}=2,\\[6pt]
e_W&=\sqrt{v_X+v_Y}=\sqrt{2+2}=2,\\[6pt]
\frac{v_X^2}{n_X-1}=\frac{v_Y^2}{n_Y-1}&=\frac{4}{11},\\[6pt]
\nu_W&=\frac{(2+2)^2}{\frac4{11}+\frac4{11}}=\frac{16}{\frac{8}{11}}=16\cdot\frac{11}{8}=22,\\[6pt]
h&=2.074\cdot2=4.148,\\[6pt]
I_W&=[5-4.148,\,5+4.148]=[0.852,9.148].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The population variances are unknown and need not be equal, so use **Welch's interval**. It requires independent groups and, for this small-sample approximation, normal populations.

Divide each sample variance by its sample size to obtain its contribution to the estimated variance. Each contribution is $\dfrac{24}{12}=2$, giving estimated difference variance $4$ and standard error $2$.

The numerator of the degrees-of-freedom formula is the square of the estimated difference variance, $16$. In the denominator each squared contribution is divided by its group's degrees of freedom and their sum is $\dfrac{8}{11}$. The contributions are equal here, and Welch's formula gives twenty-two degrees of freedom, $n_X+n_Y-2$.

The quantile $2.074$ gives margin $4.148$. The positive observed difference exceeds the margin, so the **lower limit is positive** as well. The $95\,\%$ interval for $\mu_X-\mu_Y$ runs from $0.852$ to $9.148$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two independent samples from normal populations have $n_X=10$, $n_Y=6$, $s_X^2=40$, $s_Y^2=6$, $\bar x=15$, and $\bar y=11$. Calculate the $95\,\%$ Welch interval, rounding the degrees of freedom down. Use $t_{12,0.975}=2.179$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=15-11=4,\\[6pt]
v_X&=\frac{40}{10}=4,\qquad v_Y=\frac66=1,\\[6pt]
e_W&=\sqrt{4+1}=\sqrt5\approx2.236,\\[6pt]
\frac{v_X^2}{n_X-1}&=\frac{16}{9},\qquad \frac{v_Y^2}{n_Y-1}=\frac15,\\[6pt]
\nu_W&=\frac{(4+1)^2}{\frac{16}{9}+\frac15}
=\frac{25}{\frac{89}{45}}
=\frac{1125}{89}\approx12.64,\\[6pt]
\lfloor\nu_W\rfloor&=12,\qquad q=t_{12,0.975}=2.179,\\[6pt]
h&=2.179\sqrt5\approx4.872,\\[6pt]
I_W&\approx[4-4.872,\,4+4.872]\\[6pt]
&=[-0.872,8.872].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first group makes the larger contribution to the estimated difference variance, $4$ versus $1$, because its sample variance $40$ is much greater than $6$. The contributions sum to $5$ and the standard error is $\sqrt5$.

Welch's formula uses each contribution separately with its group's degrees of freedom. The two denominator terms are $\dfrac{16}{9}$ and $\dfrac15$, which sum to $\dfrac{80+9}{45}=\dfrac{89}{45}$. Dividing $25$ by this sum gives $\dfrac{1125}{89}$, approximately $12.64$.

Round the fractional result down as requested. The quantile with twelve degrees of freedom exceeds the one with $12.64$, slightly widening the interval. Multiplying this t quantile by $\sqrt5$ gives margin $4.872$.

The $95\,\%$ interval for $\mu_X-\mu_Y$ runs from approximately $-0.872$ to $8.872$. It **contains zero** because the margin exceeds the observed difference $15-11=4$.

</div>
