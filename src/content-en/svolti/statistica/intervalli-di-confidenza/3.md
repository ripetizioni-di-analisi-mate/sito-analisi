## Exercise 1

Of $100$ independent units, $50$ have the characteristic of interest. Calculate the $95\,\%$ Wald interval with $z_{0.975}=1.96$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat p&=\frac{50}{100}=0.50,\qquad 1-\widehat p=0.50,\\[6pt]
\frac{\widehat p(1-\widehat p)}{n}&=\frac{0.50\cdot0.50}{100}=\frac{0.25}{100}=0.0025,\\[6pt]
e&=\sqrt{0.0025}=0.05,\\[6pt]
h&=1.96\cdot0.05=0.098,\\[6pt]
I&=[0.50-0.098,\,0.50+0.098]\\[6pt]
 &=[0.402,0.598].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each unit gives a Bernoulli outcome equal to $1$ if it has the characteristic and $0$ otherwise. The observed proportion is the mean of the hundred indicators, $0.50$.

The success count has a binomial distribution. A normal approximation is plausible since the fifty successes and fifty failures both exceed the rule-of-thumb threshold of ten.

An indicator has variance $p(1-p)$. The **Wald interval** substitutes $0.50$ for the unknown proportion, giving estimated indicator variance $0.25$. Dividing by $n=100$ gives estimated sample-proportion variance $0.0025$. Its square root is standard error $0.05$.

The $95\,\%$ level calls for quantile $1.96$, yielding margin $0.098$. Subtract and add it at $0.50$. The interval for the population proportion runs from $0.402$ to $0.598$, or $40.2\,\%$ to $59.8\,\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An inspection of $144$ independent parts finds $36$ defective. Compare the widths of the $90\,\%$ and $95\,\%$ Wald intervals. Use $z_{0.95}=1.645$ and $z_{0.975}=1.96$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat p&=\frac{36}{144}=\frac14,\qquad 1-\widehat p=\frac34,\\[6pt]
e&=\sqrt{\frac{\frac14\cdot\frac34}{144}}
=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2304}}=\frac{\sqrt3}{48}\approx0.03608,\\[6pt]
h_{90}&=1.645\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{48}\approx0.05936,\\[6pt]
h_{95}&=1.96\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{48}\approx0.07073,\\[6pt]
A_{90}&=2h_{90}\approx0.1187,\\[6pt]
A_{95}&=2h_{95}\approx0.1415,\\[6pt]
A_{95}-A_{90}&\approx0.0227.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both intervals use the same outcomes, so they share observed proportion $\dfrac14$ and standard error. The product $\dfrac14\cdot\dfrac34$ equals $\dfrac{3}{16}$. Dividing by $144$ gives $\dfrac{3}{2304}$. Since $2304=48^2$, the square root is $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{48}$, approximately $0.03608$.

The quantiles change with the requested coverage. At $90\,\%$ each tail has area $0.05$ and the quantile is $1.645$. At $95\,\%$ each tail has area $0.025$ and the quantile is $1.96$.

The width from lower to upper endpoint contains two margins. It is approximately $0.1187$ at $90\,\%$ and $0.1415$ at $95\,\%$. Both counts, $36$ and $108$, exceed ten, making the normal approximation plausible.

The higher level produces an interval **wider** by about $2.27$ percentage points, obtained by subtracting the widths before rounding.

</div>
