## Exercise 1

Two independent normal samples of sizes $25$ and $16$ have means $22$ and $19$. Their population standard deviations are $5$ and $4$. Calculate the two-sided interval for $\mu_X-\mu_Y$ using $z=1.96$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\bar x-\bar y=22-19=3,\\[6pt]
\frac{\sigma_X^2}{n_X}&=\frac{25}{25}=1,\qquad \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{n_Y}=\frac{16}{16}=1,\\[6pt]
e&=\sqrt{1+1}=\sqrt2\approx1.4142,\\[6pt]
h&=1.96\sqrt2\approx2.772,\\[6pt]
I&\approx[3-2.772,\,3+2.772]\\[6pt]
&=[0.228,5.772].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interval is centered on the observed difference of the means in the specified order $X-Y$, giving $3$.

The first sample mean has variance $\dfrac{\sigma_X^2}{n_X}=\dfrac{5^2}{25}=1$ and the second $\dfrac{4^2}{16}=1$. The two sample means are independent, so their covariance is zero and the variance of their difference is the sum, $2$. Its square root is the standard error $\sqrt2$.

Both populations are normal, so the difference of their means is normal. Their standard deviations are known, so use the **comparison with known variances** and the normal quantile $1.96$. The margin is $1.96\sqrt2$, approximately $2.772$.

Subtracting and adding the margin at the observed difference gives the interval for $\mu_X-\mu_Y$, from approximately $0.228$ to $5.772$. Since the margin is less than $3$, the interval is **entirely positive**. Every value in it implies a larger mean in the first group.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two independent samples from normal populations have $n_X=25$, $n_Y=16$, $\bar x=18$, $\bar y=22$, $\sigma_X=15$, and $\sigma_Y=16$. Calculate the interval for $\mu_X-\mu_Y$ and derive the interval for $\mu_Y-\mu_X$. Use the two-sided critical value $z=2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{X-Y}&=18-22=-4,\\[6pt]
\frac{\sigma_X^2}{n_X}&=\frac{225}{25}=9,\qquad \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{n_Y}=\frac{256}{16}=16,\\[6pt]
e&=\sqrt{9+16}=\sqrt{25}=5,\\[6pt]
h&=2\cdot5=10,\\[6pt]
I_{X-Y}&=[-4-10,\,-4+10]=[-14,6],\\[6pt]
I_{Y-X}&=[-6,14].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the order $X-Y$ the center is $18-22=-4$. The population variances are $15^2=225$ and $16^2=256$, so the two means contribute $9$ and $16$ to the variance of their difference. Independence gives variance $25$ and standard error $5$. With quantile $2$ the margin is $10$ and the interval for $\mu_X-\mu_Y$ is $[-14,6]$.

Swapping the groups reverses the center's sign, giving $4$. The variance of the difference still sums the same contributions, so the standard error and margin stay the same. The interval for $\mu_Y-\mu_X$ is therefore $[4-10,\,4+10]=[-6,14]$.

Alternatively, reflect the first interval about zero by negating the endpoints and reversing their order. Reflection maps the previous upper endpoint $6$ to the new lower endpoint $-6$. Both intervals **contain zero**, so the data are compatible with equal means at the chosen level.

</div>
