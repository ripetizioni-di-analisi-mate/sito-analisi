## Exercise 1

From a normal population draw $16$ independent times with $\bar x=50\,\mathrm{min}$ and $s=8\,\mathrm{min}$. Calculate the two-sided $95\,\%$ interval using $t_{15,0.975}=2.131$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nu&=n-1=16-1=15,\\[6pt]
q&=t_{15,0.975}=2.131,\\[6pt]
e&=\frac{s}{\sqrt n}=\frac{8}{\sqrt{16}}=\frac{8}{4}=2\,\mathrm{min},\\[6pt]
h&=q\,e=2.131\cdot2=4.262\,\mathrm{min},\\[6pt]
I&=[50-4.262,\,50+4.262]\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
 &=[45.738,54.262]\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value $8\,\mathrm{min}$ is a sample standard deviation calculated from the same sixteen times. The population standard deviation is unknown, so use a **t interval**. Under normality, standardizing the mean using $S$ gives Student's t distribution with $n-1=15$ degrees of freedom.

A two-sided $95\,\%$ interval leaves $0.025$ in each tail. Use the t quantile of order $0.975$ with $15$ degrees of freedom, namely $2.131$.

Dividing the sample standard deviation by $\sqrt{16}=4$ gives estimated standard error $2\,\mathrm{min}$ for the mean. Multiplication by the quantile gives margin $2.131\cdot2=4.262\,\mathrm{min}$.

Subtracting and adding the margin at the observed mean $50\,\mathrm{min}$ gives the $95\,\%$ interval for the population mean, from $45.738$ to $54.262$ minutes. The normal quantile $1.96$ would give margin $3.92\,\mathrm{min}$. The larger t quantile gives a wider interval to account for estimation of $\sigma$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four independent lengths from a normal population are $7$, $9$, $9$, and $11\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the two-sided $95\,\%$ interval for the mean. Use $t_{3,0.975}=3.182$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{7+9+9+11}{4}=\frac{36}{4}=9\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
x_i-\bar x&=-2,\ 0,\ 0,\ 2\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\sum_{i=1}^4(x_i-\bar x)^2&=4+0+0+4=8\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[6pt]
s^2&=\frac{8}{4-1}=\frac83\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[6pt]
e&=\frac{s}{\sqrt4}=\frac{\sqrt{\frac83}}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}
=\sqrt{\frac23}\,\mathrm{cm}\approx0.8165\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
h&=3.182\sqrt{\frac23}\,\mathrm{cm}
\approx2.598\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
I&\approx[9-2.598,\,9+2.598]\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
 &=[6.402,11.598]\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The four individual observations are given, so first calculate the center and the sample standard deviation. The mean is $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the deviations are $-2$, $0$, $0$, and $2\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The unbiased variance estimate divides the sum of squared deviations $8\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ by $3$ because the mean was estimated from the same data. Taking the square root of $s^2$ restores the length units.

Divide the sample standard deviation by $\sqrt4=2$ for the standard error. Simplifying gives $\sqrt{\dfrac23}\,\mathrm{cm}$, approximately $0.8165\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The population variance is unknown, so use **Student's t interval** with three degrees of freedom. With only four observations the quantile $3.182$ is much larger than $1.96$ because the estimate of $\sigma$ based on three degrees of freedom is imprecise. Retain the unrounded margin until calculating the endpoints.

The $95\,\%$ interval for the population mean length therefore runs from about $6.402$ to about $11.598\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
