## Exercise 1

The recorded consumption values are $3,4,4,6,8,9,11$ liters. Find the first and third quartiles using ranks rounded up, then find the interquartile range and the range.

$$
\begin{aligned}
7\cdot0.25&=1.75,\qquad 7\cdot0.75=5.25\\[6pt]
\lceil7\cdot0.25\rceil&=2,\qquad
\lceil7\cdot0.75\rceil=6\\[6pt]
Q_1&=4\,\mathrm{L},\qquad Q_3=9\,\mathrm{L}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{IQR}&=(9-4)\,\mathrm{L}=5\,\mathrm{L}\\[6pt]
R&=(11-3)\,\mathrm{L}=8\,\mathrm{L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The seven observations are already in increasing order, so their positions can be read directly. The theoretical quartile ranks are $1.75$ and $5.25$. Rounding each up to the next integer gives positions two and six. The value $4$ appears twice, with a separate position for each occurrence. The distance between the quartiles measures the spread of the central portion. The range instead uses the first and last observations and includes the distances from the extreme values to the quartiles. Both differences are in liters, the units of the data.

The range exceeds the interquartile range by $3\,\mathrm{L}$. This difference is the sum of the distances from the extremes to the quartiles, $4-3=1\,\mathrm{L}$ on the left and $11-9=2\,\mathrm{L}$ on the right. The quartiles are **$Q_1=4\,\mathrm{L}$** and **$Q_3=9\,\mathrm{L}$**, the interquartile range is $5\,\mathrm{L}$, and the range is $8\,\mathrm{L}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The ordered times $2,4,5,6,8,9,15$ are in minutes. The last time is corrected from $15$ to $29$. Determine how the mean, median, interquartile range, and range change. Round ranks up for the quartiles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x_{\rm prima}&=\frac{2+4+5+6+8+9+15}{7}\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{49}{7}\,\mathrm{min}=7\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
\bar x_{\rm dopo}&=\frac{49-15+29}{7}\,\mathrm{min}
=\frac{63}{7}\,\mathrm{min}=9\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Med}(x)&=x_{(4)},\qquad Q_1=x_{(2)},\qquad Q_3=x_{(6)}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Med}(x)&=6\,\mathrm{min},\qquad
\operatorname{IQR}=(9-4)\,\mathrm{min}=5\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
R_{\rm prima}&=13\,\mathrm{min},\qquad
R_{\rm dopo}=27\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial sum of the seven times is $49\,\mathrm{min}$. The replacement changes the sum by $29-15=14\,\mathrm{min}$ but leaves the count at seven. Spreading that increase across seven times raises the mean by $2\,\mathrm{min}$. The corrected value stays last, so the fourth, second, and sixth observations remain the same. Since those positions determine the median and quartiles, the median and interquartile range are unchanged. The range depends directly on the last value and increases by $14\,\mathrm{min}$. The example distinguishes measures sensitive to an extreme's magnitude from those determined by positions in the ordered list.

The mean changes **from $7\,\mathrm{min}$ to $9\,\mathrm{min}$**, the median remains $6\,\mathrm{min}$, the interquartile range remains $5\,\mathrm{min}$, and the range changes **from $13\,\mathrm{min}$ to $27\,\mathrm{min}$**.

</div>
