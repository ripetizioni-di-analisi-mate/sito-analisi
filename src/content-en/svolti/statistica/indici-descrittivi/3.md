## Exercise 1

Find the median and mode of the daily service-call counts recorded over six days, $8,3,5,12,4,5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{(1)},\ldots,x_{(6)})&=(3,4,5,5,8,12)\\[6pt]
n&=6=2m,\qquad m=3\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Med}(x)&=\frac{x_{(3)}+x_{(4)}}{2}
=\frac{5+5}{2}=5
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{value} & 3 & 4 & 5 & 8 & 12\\ \hline
\text{frequency} & 1 & 1 & 2 & 1 & 1
\end{array}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\operatorname{Mo}(x)=5.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The median depends on positions in the ordered list, so first sort the six observations. The value $5$ occurs twice and occupies the third and fourth positions.

There are six observations, an even number with $m=3$. The median is therefore the average of the third and fourth observations. They agree because $5$ service calls were recorded on two different days, so the median is $5$.

To find the mode, compare the frequencies in the table. The value $5$ occurs twice and every other value occurs once. Thus $5$ is the only value with maximum frequency.

In this sample the median and the mode coincide because the same number meets both criteria. The median daily number of service calls is **$5$** and the mode is **$5$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A frequency table lists values $2,4,7,10$ with respective counts $3,2,4,1$. Find the median without expanding the table and explain whether it is one of the observed values.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3+2+4+1=10=2m,\qquad m=5\\[6pt]
N_1&=3,\quad N_2=3+2=5,\quad N_3=5+4=9,\quad N_4=9+1=10\\[6pt]
x_{(5)}&=4\quad(N_2=5\ge5),\qquad x_{(6)}=7\quad(N_2<6\le N_3)\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Med}(x)&=\frac{4+7}{2}=5.5.
\end{aligned}
$$

The median is $5.5$, with $3+2=5$ observations to its left and $4+1=5$ to its right."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The sample size is ten, an even number with $m=5$. The median is the mean of the fifth and sixth values in the ordered list.

The cumulative counts $N_j$ give the last position occupied by each value. The value $2$ occupies positions one through three, $4$ positions four and five, and $7$ positions six through nine. The fifth position is the last occupied by $4$ because the cumulative count there reaches exactly five. The sixth is the first occupied by $7$ because the next cumulative count rises from five to nine.

The even-sample convention averages these two values, so the median is **$5.5$**.

The table contains five observations below $5.5$ and five above. None equals $5.5$, so the median **does not appear** in the data. This occurs whenever the sample size is even and the two middle values differ.

</div>
