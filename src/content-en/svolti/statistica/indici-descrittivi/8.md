## Exercise 1

Two suppliers deliver items with mean masses $80\,\mathrm{g}$ and $150\,\mathrm{g}$ and respective standard deviations $4\,\mathrm{g}$ and $6\,\mathrm{g}$. Identify the supplier with greater relative spread.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{CV}_A&=\frac{4\,\mathrm{g}}{80\,\mathrm{g}}
=0.05=5\,\%\\[6pt]
\operatorname{CV}_B&=\frac{6\,\mathrm{g}}{150\,\mathrm{g}}
=0.04=4\,\%\\[6pt]
s_B-s_A&=2\,\mathrm{g}>0,\qquad
\operatorname{CV}_A-\operatorname{CV}_B=0.01>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **coefficient of variation** is the ratio $\dfrac{s}{\bar x}$ of the standard deviation to the mean. Its use requires ratio-scale measurements with a positive mean. Both $80\,\mathrm{g}$ and $150\,\mathrm{g}$ are positive.

Mass has a meaningful zero, so the ratio of standard deviation to mean can be interpreted as relative spread. Grams cancel, making the coefficient dimensionless. The second supplier has the larger absolute standard deviation, but its mean mass is sufficiently higher to give it the smaller ratio. The last line compares the two rankings, which are reversed. The first supplier therefore has **greater relative spread** even though its standard deviation is $2\,\mathrm{g}$ smaller than the second supplier's. Its standard deviation is five percent of its mean, compared with four percent for the second supplier.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Each empty container weighs $200\,\mathrm{g}$. The gross mass of a group of full containers has mean $500\,\mathrm{g}$ and standard deviation $15\,\mathrm{g}$. Find the coefficients of variation for the contents alone and for gross mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_i&=x_i-200\,\mathrm{g}\qquad(a=-200\,\mathrm{g},\ b=1)\\[6pt]
\bar x_{\rm netto}&=(500-200)\,\mathrm{g}=300\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
s_{\rm netto}&=|1|\cdot15\,\mathrm{g}=15\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{CV}_{\rm netto}&=\frac{15}{300}=0.05\\[6pt]
\operatorname{CV}_{\rm lordo}&=\frac{15}{500}=0.03\\[6pt]
\frac{\operatorname{CV}_{\rm netto}}{\operatorname{CV}_{\rm lordo}}&=\frac{500}{300}=\frac{5}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Assume the same tare of $200\,\mathrm{g}$ for each container. Converting gross to net subtracts this constant from every measurement and from the mean. The transformation is $y=a+bx$ with $b=1$, so each deviation from the mean is unchanged and the standard deviation remains $15\,\mathrm{g}$. The net mean is lower, so the same absolute variability represents a larger share of it. The two coefficients describe different quantities: the mass of the contents and the mass of the full container. Comparing them shows how a fixed tare affects the ratio. Since the standard deviations are equal, the ratio of the coefficients is the inverse ratio of the means, $\dfrac{5}{3}$.

The coefficient of variation for the contents is **$0.05$**, or $5\,\%$. For gross mass it is **$0.03$**, or $3\,\%$.

</div>
