## Exercise 1

For four length measurements $1,3,3,5$ meters, find the descriptive variance and its corresponding standard deviation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{1+3+3+5}{4}\,\mathrm{m}=\frac{12}{4}\,\mathrm{m}=3\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
x_i-\bar x&=-2,\ 0,\ 0,\ 2\ \mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
D&=\bigl[(-2)^2+0^2+0^2+2^2\bigr]\,\mathrm{m^2}
=8\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
v&=\frac{8}{4}\,\mathrm{m^2}=2\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
\sqrt v&=\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m}\approx1.41\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.3 xmax=5.1 ymin=-0.3 ymax=6.1 nomi="i,xᵢ" punti="1,1;2,3;3,3;4,5" linee="0.4,3 4.6,3" segmenti="1,1,1,3;4,3,4,5" titolo="The horizontal line marks the mean. The vertical segments show deviations of −2 and 2. The other two deviations are zero."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean is the sum $12\,\mathrm{m}$ divided by four measurements, giving $3\,\mathrm{m}$.

Subtract the mean from each measurement because spread is measured relative to the group's center. The two values $3\,\mathrm{m}$ equal the mean and have zero deviations. The other two measurements are equidistant on opposite sides and each contributes $(\pm2)^2=4\,\mathrm{m^2}$ to the sum of squares. The sum of squared deviations is therefore $D=8\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

Because the aim is to describe the observed list, divide the sum by four, the number of measurements. This gives the **descriptive variance** $v=2\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The variance is in square meters because the deviations were squared.

Taking the square root gives a measure of spread in meters, comparable with the original distances. The descriptive standard deviation is **$\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m}$**, approximately $1.41\,\mathrm{m}$. It is smaller than the maximum absolute deviation $2\,\mathrm{m}$ because two of the four deviations are zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Six volume measurements expressed in liters have sum $30$ and sum of squares $174$. Find their descriptive variance without using the individual measurements.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{30}{6}\,\mathrm{L}=5\,\mathrm{L}\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{6}\sum_i x_i^2&=\frac{174}{6}\,\mathrm{L^2}
=29\,\mathrm{L^2}\\[6pt]
v&=\frac{1}{6}\sum_i x_i^2-\bar x^2\\[6pt]
&=(29-5^2)\,\mathrm{L^2}=(29-25)\,\mathrm{L^2}=4\,\mathrm{L^2}\\[6pt]
D&=6\cdot4\,\mathrm{L^2}=24\,\mathrm{L^2}=\bigl(174-6\cdot5^2\bigr)\,\mathrm{L^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the sum of the measurements by six gives the mean $\bar x=5\,\mathrm{L}$. Similarly, dividing the sum of squares by six gives the mean of the squares.

Expanding the squared deviations from the mean gives the variance formula $v=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_i x_i^2-\bar x^2$. It uses the **mean of the squares**, $29\,\mathrm{L^2}$, even if the individual deviations are unknown. The second term, the **square of the mean**, is $25\,\mathrm{L^2}$, obtained by squaring $5\,\mathrm{L}$. The two quantities coincide only when all observations agree. Here their positive difference shows that at least one measurement differs from the mean. The result is a descriptive variance because the denominator in the mean of the squares is the number of observations, six.

The final line also recovers the sum of squared deviations. Since $v=\dfrac{D}{6}$, it follows that $D=24\,\mathrm{L^2}$. The identity $D=\sum_i x_i^2-n\bar x^2=174-150$ gives the same value. The descriptive variance of the six volumes is **$4\,\mathrm{L^2}$**, and the corresponding standard deviation is $2\,\mathrm{L}$.

</div>
