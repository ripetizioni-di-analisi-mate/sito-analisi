## Exercise 1

A test has mean $40$ and standard deviation $5$. Find the standardized value of $32$ and the raw score corresponding to the standardized threshold $z=1.4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
z&=\frac{x-\bar x}{s}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x=\bar x+s\,z\\[6pt]
z(32)&=\frac{32-40}{5}=-\frac85=-1.6\\[6pt]
x(1.4)&=40+5\cdot1.4=40+7=47.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x/5" xmin=-10.3 xmax=9.8 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.3 nomi="d,z" legenda=0 punti="-8,-1.6;0,0;7,1.4" segmenti="-8,0,-8,-1.6;7,0,7,1.4" titolo="The horizontal axis measures deviation from the mean of 40. Deviations of −8 and 7 correspond to standardized scores of −1.6 and 1.4."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first line gives the definition of the **standardized score** and its inverse, obtained by multiplying by $s$ and adding $\bar x$. Since the standard deviation $5$ is positive, the division is defined.

The value $32$ is below the mean, so the numerator of its standardized score is negative. Dividing the eight-point difference by five puts it $1.6$ standard deviations below the center. To recover a raw score, reverse the operations. The product $5\cdot1.4$ converts the threshold to a seven-point deviation, which is added to the mean because the threshold is positive. The value $47$ is therefore seven points above the center.

The standardized value of $32$ is **$-1.6$**, meaning it is one and six-tenths standard deviations below the mean. The threshold $z=1.4$ corresponds to the raw score **$47$**. The first score is dimensionless because numerator and denominator are both measured in points. The second is measured in test points.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On two tests a student scores $74$ and $79$ points. The means are $66$ and $70$, and the standard deviations are $4$ and $6$. Determine on which test the score lies further above the mean in standardized units. Find the score on the second test that would give the same $z$ as on the first.

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_A&=\frac{74-66}{4}=2\\[6pt]
z_B&=\frac{79-70}{6}=\frac32\\[6pt]
z_A&>z_B\\[6pt]
x_B^\ast&=\bar x_B+s_B\,z_A=70+6\cdot2=82.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The raw deviations above the means are eight and nine points. The second test has greater spread, so compare the positions by dividing each deviation by its own test's standard deviation. The first result is two standard deviations above its mean, while the second is one and a half above. On the second test, two standard deviations equal twelve points, which when added to the mean give $82$. This uses the inverse formula with the second test's mean and standard deviation and the first test's standardized score. The equivalence concerns standardized distances. The percentiles can differ if the distributions have different shapes.

The result is **further above the mean on the first test**, with $z_A=2$ versus $z_B=1.5$. On the second test the same standardized value corresponds to **$82$** points.

</div>
