## Exercise 1

An index measured in a group has mean $7$ and descriptive variance $5$. The new scale is $y=30-2x$. Find the mean, descriptive variance, and descriptive standard deviation on the new scale.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar y&=30-2\bar x=30-2\cdot7=16\\[6pt]
y_i-\bar y&=(30-2x_i)-(30-2\bar x)=-2(x_i-\bar x)\\[6pt]
v_y&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i 4(x_i-\bar x)^2\\[6pt]
&=(-2)^2\cdot5=20\\[6pt]
\sqrt{v_y}&=\sqrt{20}=\sqrt{4\cdot5}=2\sqrt5\approx4.47.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Applying the same transformation to the old center gives the new mean. For the variance, consider deviations from the new mean. In $y_i-\bar y$ the additive constant $30$ cancels, and each original deviation is multiplied by $-2$. The negative sign exchanges values above and below the mean, then disappears on squaring. The variance is therefore multiplied by four and the standard deviation by two, the absolute value of the coefficient.

This is the rule $v_y=b^2v_x$ with $a=30$ and $b=-2$. Comparing the standard deviations gives the same factor $|b|=2$ because $\sqrt5\cdot2=2\sqrt5$. On the new scale, the mean is **$16$**, the descriptive variance is **$20$**, and the descriptive standard deviation is **$2\sqrt5$**, approximately $4.47$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $2,5,8,12$ be the ordered values of $x$. Apply $y=20-2x$ and find the median and first quartile of $y$, rounding the rank up for the quartile.

$$
\begin{aligned}
20-2\cdot2&=16,\qquad 20-2\cdot5=10\\[6pt]
20-2\cdot8&=4,\qquad 20-2\cdot12=-4\\[6pt]
(y_{(1)},y_{(2)},y_{(3)},y_{(4)})
&=(-4,4,10,16)\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Med}(y)&=\frac{4+10}{2}=7\\[6pt]
k&=\lceil4\cdot0.25\rceil=1\\[6pt]
Q_1(y)&=y_{(1)}=-4=20-2\cdot x_{(4)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The smallest observation $x=2$ has the largest image $16$, and the largest observation $x=12$ has the smallest image $-4$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $x$ is negative, so the transformation is decreasing and the largest original observation gives the smallest transformed value. The first line calculates the four images in the order of the $x$ values, so the results are in decreasing order. To find quantiles, first arrange the transformed values in increasing order. The first quartile is at position $\lceil4\cdot0.25\rceil=1$, so it is the smallest $y$ value. It comes from transforming $12$, the fourth entry in the ordered $x$ list, as prescribed by $y_{(k)}=a+bx_{(n+1-k)}$ with $k=1$ and $n=4$. For the median, the two middle positions exchange places, and the average of $4$ and $10$ is $7$. This can be checked by transforming the original median $\dfrac{5+8}{2}=6.5$ directly to obtain $20-2\cdot6.5=7$. The median of $y$ is **$7$** and the first quartile of $y$ is **$-4$**.

</div>
