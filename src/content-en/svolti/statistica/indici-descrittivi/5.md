## Exercise 1

A sample of five times has descriptive variance $8\,\mathrm{min^2}$. Find the corrected sample variance and its corresponding sample standard deviation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=\frac{D}{5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D=5v\\[6pt]
D&=5\cdot8\,\mathrm{min^2}=40\,\mathrm{min^2}\\[6pt]
s^2&=\frac{40}{5-1}\,\mathrm{min^2}=\frac{40}{4}\,\mathrm{min^2}=10\,\mathrm{min^2}\\[6pt]
s&=\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{min}\approx3.16\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
\frac{s^2}{v}&=\frac{10}{8}=\frac{5}{4}=\frac{n}{n-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The descriptive variance divides $D$ by the number of times. Multiplying by five therefore recovers the sum of the squared deviations. The corrected sample variance uses the same deviations from the same mean, changing only the divisor to four. Since $D$ is positive, reducing the denominator increases the variance. The variance is still measured in square minutes. Its square root, the standard deviation, is measured in minutes.

The last line compares the two variances. Both have numerator $D$, so their ratio is $\dfrac{n}{n-1}=\dfrac{5}{4}$ and $s^2$ is obtained by multiplying $v$ by this factor. For five observations the increase is $25\,\%$. For large samples the factor is close to $1$ and the two variances differ little.

The corrected sample variance is **$10\,\mathrm{min^2}$** and the sample standard deviation is **$\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{min}$**, approximately $3.16\,\mathrm{min}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two disjoint groups each contain three observations. Their means are $4$ and $8$. Each has corrected sample variance $1$. Find the corrected sample variance of the combined group.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{3\cdot4+3\cdot8}{6}=\frac{12+24}{6}=6\\[6pt]
s_1^2&=\frac{D_1}{3-1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_1=(3-1)\cdot1=2,\qquad D_2=2\\[6pt]
D&=D_1+D_2+n_1(\bar x_1-\bar x)^2+n_2(\bar x_2-\bar x)^2\\[6pt]
&=2+2+3(4-6)^2+3(8-6)^2\\[6pt]
&=4+12+12=28\\[6pt]
s^2&=\frac{28}{6-1}=\frac{28}{5}=5.6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The overall mean is the weighted mean of the group means with weights given by their sizes. Since the sizes are equal, $\bar x$ is the unweighted mean of $4$ and $8$.

Multiplying each corrected variance by its divisor $3-1=2$ gives the within-group sums of squared deviations $D_1$ and $D_2$. Once the groups are pooled, deviations must be measured from $6$, the overall mean. Each group mean is two units from $6$, adding $3\cdot2^2$ to each group's sum of squares. The cross terms vanish because deviations from each group's own mean sum to zero. The overall variance therefore reflects both within-group variation and the separation of the group centers.

In the sum $D=28$ the within-group contribution is $4$ and the contribution from the difference between the means is $24$. Divide by $6-1=5$ because the corrected sample variance for the pooled group uses the total number of observations minus one. The corrected sample variance of the six observations is **$\dfrac{28}{5}=5.6$**.

</div>
