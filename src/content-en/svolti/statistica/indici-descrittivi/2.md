## Exercise 1

Across twelve shifts, three shifts processed $4$ orders each, five processed $7$ each, and four processed $10$ each. Find the mean number of orders per shift.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=3+5+4=12\\[6pt]
T&=3\cdot4+5\cdot7+4\cdot10\\[6pt]
&=12+35+40=87\\[6pt]
\bar x&=\frac{87}{12}=\frac{29}{4}=7.25.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.6 xmax=12.3 ymin=-0.3 ymax=6.1 nomi="x,n" poligoni="3.55,0 4.45,0 4.45,3 3.55,3;6.55,0 7.45,0 7.45,5 6.55,5;9.55,0 10.45,0 10.45,4 9.55,4" segmenti="7.25,0,7.25,5.6" etichette="7.45,5.75,7.25" titolo="The horizontal axis shows orders per shift. The heights 3, 5, and 4 give the numbers of shifts with 4, 7, and 10 orders, respectively. The dashed vertical line marks the mean $\dfrac{29}{4}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The values $4$, $7$, and $10$ count orders per shift. The frequencies $3$, $5$, and $4$ count the shifts with each value. The frequencies sum to $12$, so the table accounts for all twelve shifts.

To recover the total, multiply each order count by the number of times it occurs. The three shifts with $4$ orders contribute $12$ orders, the five shifts with $7$ contribute $35$, and the four with $10$ contribute $40$. There are therefore $87$ orders in total.

The denominator is $12$ because the mean is per shift. This is the **weighted mean** with absolute frequencies as weights. Giving the three distinct values equal weight would yield $\dfrac{4+7+10}{3}=7$, ignoring that $7$ orders occur in five shifts and $10$ orders in four.

The mean number of orders per shift is **$7.25$**. The mean distributes a total across shifts, so it need not be an integer or a count actually observed on any shift.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A waiting-time table has three observations in $[0,10)$, five in $[10,20)$, and two in $[20,40)$, in minutes. Estimate the mean using class midpoints and find an interval containing the actual mean.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1&=\frac{0+10}{2}=5,\qquad
c_2=\frac{10+20}{2}=15,\qquad
c_3=\frac{20+40}{2}=30\\[6pt]
\bar x_{\rm stimata}
&=\frac{3\cdot5+5\cdot15+2\cdot30}{10}\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{15+75+60}{10}\,\mathrm{min}
=15\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
L&=\frac{3\cdot0+5\cdot10+2\cdot20}{10}\,\mathrm{min}
=\frac{90}{10}\,\mathrm{min}=9\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
U&=\frac{3\cdot10+5\cdot20+2\cdot40}{10}\,\mathrm{min}
=\frac{210}{10}\,\mathrm{min}=21\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sample size is $3+5+2=10$, the denominator of all three weighted means. The class midpoints are the averages of the endpoints of each interval, $5$, $15$, and $30$ minutes.

The midpoints replace the unknown values within each class, so $15\,\mathrm{min}$ is an estimate. It is the mean of the table obtained by placing every observation at its class midpoint.

For a lower bound, assign every observation its class's left endpoint, which belongs to its interval. Every actual observation is at least its left endpoint, so the actual total is at least $90\,\mathrm{min}$. The minimum mean compatible with the table is thus $9\,\mathrm{min}$.

Each observation is strictly below its class's right endpoint. The actual total is therefore less than $210\,\mathrm{min}$ and the mean is strictly below $21\,\mathrm{min}$.

The estimated mean is **$15\,\mathrm{min}$** and the actual mean belongs to **$[9,21)$** minutes. The width of this interval, $21-9=12\,\mathrm{min}$, is the weighted mean of the class widths $10$, $10$, and $20$, using the frequencies as weights.

</div>
