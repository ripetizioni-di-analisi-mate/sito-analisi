## Exercise 1

For $1,3,4,5,6,7,9,16$, find Tukey's fences, the whisker endpoints, and the observations beyond the fences. Round ranks up when finding the quartiles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lceil8\cdot0.25\rceil&=2,\qquad\lceil8\cdot0.75\rceil=6\\[6pt]
Q_1&=x_{(2)}=3,\qquad Q_3=x_{(6)}=7\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Med}(x)&=\frac{x_{(4)}+x_{(5)}}{2}=\frac{5+6}{2}=5.5\\[6pt]
\operatorname{IQR}&=7-3=4,\qquad 1.5\cdot4=6\\[6pt]
L&=3-6=-3,\qquad U=7+6=13\\[6pt]
\text{whiskers}&:\ \min\{x_i\ge-3\}=1,\qquad \max\{x_i\le13\}=9\\[6pt]
\text{outliers}&:\ 16>13.
\end{aligned}
$$

The whiskers end at 1 and 9. The observation at 16 is an outlier."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The quartiles occupy positions two and six because there are eight observations and both $8\cdot0.25=2$ and $8\cdot0.75=6$ are already integers. The median, used for the line inside the box, is the average of the fourth and fifth values, $5.5$.

The quartiles are four units apart. **Tukey's rule** places the fences one and a half times this distance beyond the quartiles, so subtract six from the first quartile and add six to the third. The minimum $1$ is inside the fences and forms the lower whisker endpoint. The maximum $16$ exceeds the upper fence and is plotted as an individual point. The upper whisker ends at $9$, the largest observed value inside the fences. The value $13$ is a comparison threshold and is not observed in this sample.

The whiskers extend from **$1$** to **$9$**, and the only observation beyond them is **$16$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The ordered observations are $1,4,5,6,7,8,14,20$. Round the quartile ranks up. Find the median and whisker endpoints, and determine which of $14$ and $20$ are plotted as points outside the fences.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_1&=x_{(2)}=4,\qquad Q_3=x_{(6)}=8,\qquad \operatorname{IQR}=4\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Med}(x)&=\frac{x_{(4)}+x_{(5)}}{2}=\frac{6+7}{2}=6.5\\[6pt]
L&=4-1.5\cdot4=4-6=-2\\[6pt]
U&=8+1.5\cdot4=8+6=14\\[6pt]
14&\le U,\qquad 20>U.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quartiles again occupy positions two and six because there are eight observations, and their distance is again four. The fences therefore lie six units beyond the quartiles. Equality matters for the whiskers because observations in the closed interval $[-2,14]$ lie inside the fences under Tukey's rule. The observation $14$ equals the upper fence and thus forms the upper whisker endpoint. The lower whisker reaches $1$, the sample minimum. The value $20$ strictly exceeds $U=14$ and is the only point plotted separately. The median differs from the quartiles and is marked at $6.5$, between the fourth and fifth observations.

The median is **$6.5$**, the whiskers extend from **$1$** to **$14$**, and of $14$ and $20$ only **$20$** is plotted outside.

</div>
