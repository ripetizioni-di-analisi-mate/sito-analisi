## Exercise 1

Let $X$ and $Y$ be independent. The mass function of $X$ gives $\dfrac13$ at $0$ and $\dfrac23$ at $1$. That of $Y$ gives $\dfrac12$ at $0$, $\dfrac13$ at $1$, and $\dfrac16$ at $2$. Find the distribution of the sum $S=X+Y$.

$$
p_S(s)=\sum_x p_X(x)\,p_Y(s-x)=p_X(0)\,p_Y(s)+p_X(1)\,p_Y(s-1)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_S(0)&=\frac13\cdot\frac12=\frac16,\\[6pt]
p_S(1)&=\frac13\cdot\frac13+\frac23\cdot\frac12=\frac19+\frac13=\frac49,\\[6pt]
p_S(2)&=\frac13\cdot\frac16+\frac23\cdot\frac13=\frac1{18}+\frac29=\frac5{18},\\[6pt]
p_S(3)&=\frac23\cdot\frac16=\frac19,\\[6pt]
\frac3{18}+\frac8{18}+\frac5{18}+\frac2{18}&=1,\\[6pt]
E[S]&=1\cdot\frac8{18}+2\cdot\frac5{18}+3\cdot\frac2{18}=\frac{24}{18}=\frac43.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Independence of $X$ and $Y$ allows the discrete **convolution** formula. The variable $X$ takes only $0$ and $1$, so the sum over $x$ has two terms. The sum $S$ takes integer values from $0$ to $3$.

The extreme sums $0$ and $3$ each come from a single pair, while $1$ and $2$ each have two representations. For $1$ the pairs are $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$. For $2$ they are $(0,2)$ and $(1,1)$ because $X$ takes only $0$ and $1$. Independence gives each pair mass equal to the product of its marginal masses. Add the products for all pairs with the same sum.

With common denominator $18$ the four masses sum to one. A second check uses linearity of expectation. Since $E[X]=\dfrac23$ and $E[Y]=\dfrac13+2\cdot\dfrac16=\dfrac23$, $E[S]$ must equal $\dfrac43$, as the computed distribution confirms.

The sum thus takes values $0$, $1$, $2$, and $3$ with respective probabilities $\dfrac16$, $\dfrac49$, $\dfrac5{18}$, and $\dfrac19$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $X$ be uniform on $(0,1)$ and $Y$ uniform on $(0,2)$, independently. Find the density of $S=X+Y$ on each part of the support.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_S(s)&=\int_{\mathbb R}f_X(x)f_Y(s-x)\,dx
=\int_{\mathbb R}1\cdot\frac12\,\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}(x)\,\mathbf 1_{(0,2)}(s-x)\,dx\\[6pt]
&=\frac12\operatorname{length}\big((0,1)\cap(s-2,s)\big),\\[6pt]
(0,1)\cap(s-2,s)&=(0,s) &&(0<s<1),\\[6pt]
(0,1)\cap(s-2,s)&=(0,1) &&(1\le s\le2),\\[6pt]
(0,1)\cap(s-2,s)&=(s-2,1) &&(2<s<3),\\[6pt]
f_S(s)&=\frac s2 &&(0<s<1),\\[6pt]
f_S(s)&=\frac12 &&(1\le s\le2),\\[6pt]
f_S(s)&=\frac{3-s}{2} &&(2<s<3),\\[6pt]
\int_0^3 f_S(s)\,ds&=\frac14+\frac12+\frac14=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The variables are independent and have densities, so the convolution formula for densities applies. The symbol $\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}(x)$ denotes the function equal to $1$ for $x\in(0,1)$ and $0$ elsewhere.

For fixed $s$ the product of the densities is $\dfrac12$ when $0<x<1$ and $0<s-x<2$ hold. The latter condition is equivalent to $s-2<x<s$. Below $1$ the common section has length $s$. Between $1$ and $2$ it contains the full interval $(0,1)$ and has length one. Above $2$ its lower limit is $s-2$ and the length is $3-s$. The density vanishes outside $(0,3)$.

The final calculation sums the areas under the three pieces of the graph, two triangles with base $1$ and height $\dfrac12$ and a rectangle with base $1$ and height $\dfrac12$. Their total is one, confirming a density. The graph is symmetric about $s=\dfrac32$, which equals $E[S]=\dfrac12+1$.

</div>
