## Exercise 1

For two indicators, $P(X=1)=\dfrac35$, $P(Y=1)=\dfrac12$, and $P(X=1,Y=1)=\dfrac3{10}$. Reconstruct their joint table and determine whether $X$ and $Y$ are independent.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{11}&=\frac3{10},\\[6pt]
p_{10}&=\frac35-\frac3{10}=\frac3{10},\\[6pt]
p_{01}&=\frac12-\frac3{10}=\frac15,\\[6pt]
p_{00}&=1-\frac3{10}-\frac3{10}-\frac15=\frac15.
\end{aligned}
$$

| $X$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | Total |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $\dfrac15$ | $\dfrac15$ | $\dfrac25$ |
| $1$ | $\dfrac3{10}$ | $\dfrac3{10}$ | $\dfrac35$ |
| Total | $\dfrac12$ | $\dfrac12$ | $1$ |

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_X(0)\,p_Y(0)&=\frac25\cdot\frac12=\frac15=p_{00},\qquad
p_X(0)\,p_Y(1)=\frac25\cdot\frac12=\frac15=p_{01},\\[6pt]
p_X(1)\,p_Y(0)&=\frac35\cdot\frac12=\frac3{10}=p_{10},\qquad
p_X(1)\,p_Y(1)=\frac35\cdot\frac12=\frac3{10}=p_{11}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the common mass at $(1,1)$ from each marginal total at $1$ to obtain the two cells with exactly one indicator equal to one. The fourth cell completes the total mass. Every cell is nonnegative, so the table gives a joint distribution.

By the **factorization criterion**, two discrete variables are independent exactly when each joint mass is the product of its marginal masses. The marginal of $Y$ assigns $\dfrac12$ to each value. The first row is its total $\dfrac25$ times each mass of $Y$, and the second row is $\dfrac35$ times the same masses. Factorization holds in every cell, so **the indicators are independent**.

For two indicators it suffices to check cell $(1,1)$ because that cell and the marginals determine the other three. The given $\dfrac3{10}$ equals $\dfrac35\cdot\dfrac12$, and the other equalities follow, as the calculations confirm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pair has density $3x$ for $0<y<x<1$ and zero elsewhere. Calculate the marginals and check independence.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_X(x)&=\int_0^x3x\,dy=3x\cdot x=3x^2 &&(0<x<1),\\[6pt]
f_Y(y)&=\int_y^1 3x\,dx=\left[\frac32x^2\right]_y^1=\frac32(1-y^2) &&(0<y<1),\\[6pt]
\int_0^1\frac32(1-y^2)\,dy&=\frac32\left(1-\frac13\right)=1,\\[6pt]
f_X\left(\frac14\right)f_Y\left(\frac34\right)
&=\frac3{16}\cdot\frac32\cdot\frac7{16}=\frac{63}{512}>0,\qquad
f_{X,Y}\left(\frac14,\frac34\right)=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The product of the marginal densities is positive throughout the square, including at $P=\left(\dfrac{1}{4},\dfrac{3}{4}\right)$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For fixed $x$ integrate the constant $3x$ over a vertical section of length $x$. For the marginal of $Y$ the horizontal section extends from $x=y$ to $x=1$. An antiderivative of $3x$ is $\dfrac32x^2$. Evaluating it between $1$ and $y$ gives $\dfrac32(1-y^2)$. The third calculation checks that this marginal integrates to one.

Both marginals are positive on $(0,1)$ and zero outside. For $0<x<y<1$ their product is positive but the joint density is zero throughout that region. At $P=(\dfrac14,\dfrac34)$, for example, the product equals $\dfrac{63}{512}$ while the joint density is zero.

Factorization requires equality almost everywhere, meaning outside a set of area zero. The region above the diagonal has area $\dfrac12$. Factorization fails on a region of positive area, so **the variables are dependent**.

</div>
