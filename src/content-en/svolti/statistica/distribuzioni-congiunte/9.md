## Exercise 1

Three lines make $\dfrac12$, $\dfrac13$, and $\dfrac16$ of all parts. Their mean inspection times per part are $4$, $7$, and $10$ minutes, respectively. Find the mean inspection time for a part selected at random from the entire output.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac12+\frac13+\frac16&=\frac{3+2+1}{6}=1,\\[6pt]
E[Y]&=E\big[E[Y\mid X]\big]
=\sum_{k=1}^3 P(X=k)\,E[Y\mid X=k]\\[6pt]
&=\frac12\cdot4+\frac13\cdot7+\frac16\cdot10\\[6pt]
&=2+\frac73+\frac53=2+\frac{12}{3}=6\,\mathrm{min},\\[6pt]
\frac{4+7+10}{3}&=7\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The production line of a randomly chosen part is itself random. Let $X\in\{1,2,3\}$ denote the line and $Y$ the inspection time in minutes. The three production shares sum to one and form the distribution of $X$. For a fixed line, its mean inspection time is the given conditional mean.

The mean inspection times are finite, so $Y$ is integrable and the **law of total expectation** $E[Y]=E[E[Y\mid X]]$ applies. The conditional mean $E[Y\mid X]$ is a random variable taking values $4$, $7$, and $10$ with probabilities $\dfrac12$, $\dfrac13$, and $\dfrac16$. Weight each mean by the probability of selecting a part made on that line.

The two terms with denominator $3$ sum to $\dfrac{12}{3}=4$. Adding the first term $2$ gives $6$. For comparison, the last line computes the unweighted average of the three mean times, $7$ minutes. The first line has a greater weight than the third, making the overall mean lower than the unweighted average. A randomly selected part therefore takes $6$ minutes to inspect on average.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An observation comes from group A with probability $\dfrac34$ and group B with probability $\dfrac14$. Their means are $2$ and $6$ and their variances are $1$ and $5$. Find the overall mean and variance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[Y]&=\frac34\cdot2+\frac14\cdot6=\frac32+\frac32=3,\\[6pt]
E[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)]&=\frac34\cdot1+\frac14\cdot5=\frac34+\frac54=2,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(E[Y\mid X])
&=\frac34(2-3)^2+\frac14(6-3)^2=\frac34+\frac94=3,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(Y)&=2+3=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[Y^2\mid X=\mathrm A]&=1+2^2=5,\qquad
E[Y^2\mid X=\mathrm B]=5+6^2=41,\\[6pt]
E[Y^2]&=\frac34\cdot5+\frac14\cdot41=\frac{15}{4}+\frac{41}{4}=14,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(Y)&=14-3^2=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $X$ denote the group. The group variances are finite, so $E[Y^2]<\infty$ and the **law of total variance** applies. Calculate the overall mean first because it provides the reference point for deviations of the group means.

The weighted mean of the individual group variances is $2$. The two group means have deviations $1$ and $3$ from the overall mean, so their weighted mean squared deviation is $3$. Total variance adds these contributions, which measure dispersion within groups and between their means, respectively.

The second calculation checks the result by another method. In each group the second moment equals the variance plus the squared mean, by $\operatorname{Var}=E[Y^2]-E[Y]^2$. Total expectation applied to $Y^2$ gives $E[Y^2]=14$. Subtracting the squared mean $3$ recovers variance $5$.

The observation thus has overall mean $3$ and overall variance $5$, of which $2$ comes from within-group variability and $3$ from differences between group means.

</div>
