## Exercise 1

Variables $X$ and $Y$ have standard deviations $3$ and $5$ and covariance $-6$. Find their correlation and the correlation between $2X+1$ and $3Y-4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)&=\frac{-6}{3\cdot5}=-\frac25,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Cov}(2X+1,3Y-4)&=2\cdot3(-6)=-36,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(2X+1)&=2^2\cdot3^2=36,\qquad
\sigma_{2X+1}=6,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(3Y-4)&=3^2\cdot5^2=225,\qquad
\sigma_{3Y-4}=15,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Corr}(2X+1,3Y-4)&=\frac{-36}{6\cdot15}=-\frac{36}{90}=-\frac25.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both standard deviations are positive, so correlation is defined. Normalize the covariance by dividing by their product. The value $-\dfrac25$ belongs to $[-1,1]$, as required by the bounds on correlation.

For the transformed pair, apply $\operatorname{Cov}(a+bX,c+dY)=bd\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$ with $b=2$ and $d=3$. The additive constants disappear on centering. The covariance is therefore multiplied by $6$.

The variance of $2X+1$ is $4$ times that of $X$ because its deviations are multiplied by $2$ and variance squares them. Likewise, the variance of $3Y-4$ is $9$ times that of $Y$. The standard deviations become $6$ and $15$, so their product is also multiplied by $6$. The ratio is unchanged.

Both coefficients $2$ and $3$ are positive, preserving the sign of the correlation. With just one negative coefficient, for example $-3Y$ instead of $3Y$, covariance would change sign and the standard deviations would stay the same, giving correlation $\dfrac25$. Thus both correlations are $-\dfrac25$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Suppose $E[X]=2$, $E[Y]=7$, $\sigma_X=3$, $\sigma_Y=6$, and $\operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)=-1$. Determine the affine relationship between $X$ and $Y$ that holds almost surely.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Y-7}{6}&=-\frac{X-2}{3},\\[6pt]
Y-7&=-\frac63(X-2)=-2(X-2),\\[6pt]
Y&=7-2X+4=11-2X.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(11-2X)&=(-2)^2\cdot3^2=36=\sigma_Y^2,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Cov}(X,11-2X)&=-2\operatorname{Var}(X)=-18,\\[6pt]
\frac{-18}{3\cdot6}&=-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The correlation is $-1$, so the variables centered and divided by their respective standard deviations are negatives of each other almost surely. This is the equality case of the correlation bound, with $V=\rho U$ and $\rho=-1$. Multiply by $6$ to return to the units of $Y$. The standard deviation ratio is $2$ and the negative sign gives a decreasing relationship.

Expanding $-2(X-2)=-2X+4$ and adding $7$ gives the line $Y=11-2X$. Its slope equals $\rho\dfrac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_X}=-1\cdot\dfrac63=-2$, consistent with the proof of the theorem.

Substituting $X=2$ gives $Y=7$, in agreement with the stated means. The second calculation checks the other data. The variable $11-2X$ has variance $36$ and standard deviation $6$. Its covariance with $X$ is $-18$, which divided by $3\cdot6$ gives correlation $-1$. Therefore $Y=11-2X$ almost surely.

</div>
