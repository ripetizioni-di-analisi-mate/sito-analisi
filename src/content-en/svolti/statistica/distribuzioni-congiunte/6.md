## Exercise 1

Two independent lifetimes exceed threshold $t$ with probabilities $\dfrac25$ and $\dfrac14$. Find the probability that the minimum exceeds $t$ and that the maximum exceeds $t$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X\le t)&=1-\frac25=\frac35,\qquad
P(Y\le t)=1-\frac14=\frac34,\\[6pt]
P(\min(X,Y)>t)&=P(X>t)\,P(Y>t)=\frac25\cdot\frac14=\frac1{10},\\[6pt]
P(\max(X,Y)\le t)&=P(X\le t)\,P(Y\le t)=\frac35\cdot\frac34=\frac9{20},\\[6pt]
P(\max(X,Y)>t)&=1-\frac9{20}=\frac{11}{20}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\max(X,Y)>t)
&=P(X>t)+P(Y>t)-P(X>t,\ Y>t)\\[6pt]
&=\frac8{20}+\frac5{20}-\frac2{20}=\frac{11}{20}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both lifetimes must exceed the threshold for their minimum to exceed it. Independence allows their survival probabilities to be multiplied. This applies the maximum and minimum theorem's formula $P(m>t)=\prod_i(1-F_i(t))$ to two variables.

The maximum exceeds the threshold as soon as either lifetime exceeds it. Take the complement of the event that both lifetimes are at most $t$, multiplying the two nonexceedance probabilities. These are $\dfrac35$ and $\dfrac34$, the complements of the given probabilities.

The second calculation uses inclusion–exclusion to confirm the result. The event $\max(X,Y)>t$ is the union of $X>t$ and $Y>t$. Adding their probabilities counts the intersection twice, so subtract $P(X>t,\ Y>t)=\dfrac1{10}=\dfrac2{20}$ once.

Thus the minimum exceeds $t$ with probability $\dfrac1{10}$ and the maximum exceeds $t$ with probability $\dfrac{11}{20}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three independent measurements uniform on $(0,2)$ have maximum $M$. Determine $F_M(t)$ for $0<t<2$ and calculate $P(1<M\le\dfrac32)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X_i\le t)&=\frac t2 &&(0<t<2),\\[6pt]
F_M(t)&=P(X_1\le t)\,P(X_2\le t)\,P(X_3\le t)=\left(\frac t2\right)^3=\frac{t^3}{8},\\[6pt]
F_M\left(\frac32\right)&=\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{27}{8}=\frac{27}{64},\qquad
F_M(1)=\frac18=\frac8{64},\\[6pt]
P\left(1<M\le\frac32\right)
&=F_M\left(\frac32\right)-F_M(1)\\[6pt]
&=\frac{27}{64}-\frac8{64}=\frac{19}{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,t^3/8,0,2" linee="-0.15,0 0,0;2,1 2.3,1" xmin=-0.18 xmax=2.4 ymin=-0.12 ymax=1.18 nomi="t,F" punti="1,0.125;1.5,0.421875" segmenti="1,0,1,0.125;1.5,0,1.5,0.421875;0,0.125,1,0.125;0,0.421875,1.5,0.421875" titolo="Cumulative distribution function of the maximum $F_M(t)=\dfrac{t^3}{8}$. The difference between its values at $t=\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $t=1$ is $P\left(1<M\le\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For one uniform measurement, the probability of being at most $t$ is the length $t$ divided by $2$. For the maximum to be at most $t$ all three measurements must satisfy the condition. Independence gives the cube of the individual probability. The distribution function is zero for $t\le0$ and one for $t\ge2$ because all measurements lie in $(0,2)$.

Subtracting the distribution function at $1$ removes from $M\le\dfrac32$ all outcomes for which the maximum is also at most $1$. Use $P(a<M\le b)=F_M(b)-F_M(a)$, valid for every distribution function. Cubing $\dfrac32$ gives $\dfrac{27}{8}$, so $F_M(\dfrac32)=\dfrac{27}{64}$. Write $F_M(1)=\dfrac18$ as $\dfrac8{64}$ for a common denominator.

The graph shows the required probability as the difference in height between the two dashed levels. Thus $F_M(t)=\dfrac{t^3}{8}$ for $0<t<2$, and $P(1<M\le\dfrac32)=\dfrac{19}{64}$, approximately $0.30$.

</div>
