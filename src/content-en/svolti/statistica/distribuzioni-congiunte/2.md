## Exercise 1

For $X\in\{0,1\}$ and $Y\in\{0,1,2\}$, the rows of the joint probability table are $(\dfrac1{12},\dfrac2{12},\dfrac3{12})$ and $(\dfrac2{12},\dfrac1{12},\dfrac3{12})$. Determine the distribution of $Y$ given $X=0$ and find its mean.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_X(0)&=\frac{1+2+3}{12}=\frac6{12}=\frac12,\\[6pt]
p_{Y\mid X}(0\mid0)&=\frac{\frac1{12}}{\frac12}=\frac2{12}=\frac16,\\[6pt]
p_{Y\mid X}(1\mid0)&=\frac{\frac2{12}}{\frac12}=\frac4{12}=\frac13,\\[6pt]
p_{Y\mid X}(2\mid0)&=\frac{\frac3{12}}{\frac12}=\frac6{12}=\frac12,\\[6pt]
\frac16+\frac13+\frac12&=\frac{1+2+3}{6}=1,\\[6pt]
E[Y\mid X=0]&=0\cdot\frac16+1\cdot\frac13+2\cdot\frac12=\frac43.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Given $X=0$, the relevant total is the mass of the first row. Divide by $\dfrac12$ because that row must have mass one after conditioning. Its mass $p_X(0)=\dfrac12$ is positive, so the definition of conditional probability mass applies.

Dividing by $\dfrac12$ is equivalent to multiplying by $2$, so each first-row mass doubles. The resulting distribution has three outcomes whose masses $\dfrac16$, $\dfrac13$, and $\dfrac12$ retain the ratio $1:2:3$ of the original joint masses. They sum to one as a probability distribution requires.

Multiply each value of $Y$ by its new mass and add the three products. The value $0$ contributes nothing, $1$ contributes $\dfrac13$, and $2$ contributes $1$. The result $\dfrac43$ is closer to $2$ than to $0$ because the conditional distribution assigns its largest mass to $2$.

Thus given $X=0$, the variable $Y$ takes values $0$, $1$, and $2$ with probabilities $\dfrac16$, $\dfrac13$, and $\dfrac12$, and has conditional mean $\dfrac43$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The joint density is $3y$ for $0<x<y<1$ and zero elsewhere. Find the density of $X$ given $Y=y$, with $0<y<1$, and calculate $P(X>\dfrac14\mid Y=\dfrac34)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_Y(y)&=\int_0^y 3y\,dx=3y\cdot y=3y^2,\\[6pt]
\int_0^1 3y^2\,dy&=\left[y^3\right]_0^1=1,\\[6pt]
f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)&=\frac{3y}{3y^2}=\frac1y
&& (0<x<y),\\[6pt]
f_{X\mid Y}\left(x\ \middle|\ \frac34\right)&=\frac43
&& \left(0<x<\frac34\right),\\[6pt]
P\left(X>\frac14\ \middle|\ Y=\frac34\right)
&=\int_{\frac14}^{\frac34}\frac43\,dx\\[6pt]
&=\frac43\left(\frac34-\frac14\right)=\frac43\cdot\frac12=\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Given $y$, the possible values of $x$ range from zero to $y$. Integrating the joint density over that section gives the denominator of the conditional density. The function $3y$ does not depend on the coordinate $x$, so the integral is $3y$ times the section length $y$. The marginal density $3y^2$ integrates to one on $(0,1)$ and is positive for $0<y<1$. The conditional-density definition therefore applies at each $y$ in that interval.

The resulting distribution is uniform on $(0,y)$, with density zero outside the interval. The constant $\dfrac1y$ is the reciprocal of the section length, so the integral over $(0,y)$ equals one.

At $y=\dfrac34$ divide the length of the portion above $\dfrac14$ by the full section length. The portion from $\dfrac14$ to $\dfrac34$ has length $\dfrac12$, so the probability is $\dfrac12$ divided by $\dfrac34$, or $\dfrac23$. In the graph this is the area of the shaded rectangle with base $\dfrac12$ and height $\dfrac43$.

</div>
