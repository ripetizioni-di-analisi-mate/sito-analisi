## Exercise 1

The pair $(X,Y)$ equals $(0,0)$ with probability $\dfrac14$, $(0,2)$ with probability $\dfrac14$, and $(2,2)$ with probability $\dfrac12$. Calculate $E[XY]$ and the covariance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X]&=0\cdot\frac14+0\cdot\frac14+2\cdot\frac12=1,\\[6pt]
E[Y]&=0\cdot\frac14+2\cdot\frac14+2\cdot\frac12=2\left(\frac14+\frac12\right)=\frac32,\\[6pt]
E[XY]&=(0\cdot0)\frac14+(0\cdot2)\frac14+(2\cdot2)\frac12=2,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=2-1\cdot\frac32=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[(X-1)(Y-\tfrac32)]
&=(-1)\left(-\frac32\right)\frac14+(-1)\left(\frac12\right)\frac14+(1)\left(\frac12\right)\frac12\\[6pt]
&=\frac38-\frac18+\frac14=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

The dashed lines pass through the means $E[X]=1$ and $E[Y]=\dfrac{3}{2}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Only the last pair contributes to the mean of $X$ because $X=0$ in the others. For the mean of $Y$, the two pairs with second coordinate $2$ contribute. The product $XY$ is nonzero only at $(2,2)$, where it equals $4$. Its mean is $2$. Subtracting the product of the means gives positive covariance.

The second calculation uses deviations from the means and confirms $\dfrac12$. Each pair contributes the product of its deviations weighted by its mass. In $(0,0)$ and $(2,2)$ the deviations share a sign and contribute positively. In $(0,2)$ they have opposite signs, giving negative contribution $-\dfrac18$. The positive contributions sum to $\dfrac58$ and outweigh it, so the covariance is positive.

In the graph the dashed lines divide the plane into four quadrants around the point of means. The pairs with combined mass $\dfrac34$ lie in the quadrants where the deviations share a sign. Thus $E[XY]=2$ and $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\dfrac12$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pair has density $2$ for $0<y<x<1$ and zero elsewhere. Find its covariance from the moments.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^x2x\,dy&=2x\cdot x=2x^2,\qquad
\int_0^x2y\,dy=\left[y^2\right]_0^x=x^2,\\[6pt]
\int_0^x2xy\,dy&=x\left[y^2\right]_0^x=x^3,\\[6pt]
E[X]&=\int_0^1\int_0^x2x\,dy\,dx=\int_0^1 2x^2\,dx=\frac23,\\[6pt]
E[Y]&=\int_0^1\int_0^x2y\,dy\,dx=\int_0^1 x^2\,dx=\frac13,\\[6pt]
E[XY]&=\int_0^1\int_0^x2xy\,dy\,dx=\int_0^1 x^3\,dx=\frac14,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=\frac14-\frac23\cdot\frac13
=\frac9{36}-\frac8{36}=\frac1{36}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expectation of a function $g(X,Y)$ is the integral of $g$ times the joint density over the region of positive density. All three integrals use the same triangle. For each $x$, coordinate $y$ ranges from zero to $x$. Integrate first with respect to $y$, holding $x$ constant.

The function weighted by the density changes in each calculation. For $E[X]$, the function $2x$ is constant along a vertical section and the inner integral gives $2x^2$. For $E[Y]$, integrating $2y$ gives $x^2$. For the product, integrating $2xy$ gives $x^3$. The outer integrals over $(0,1)$ are $\dfrac23$, $\dfrac13$, and $\dfrac14$, respectively.

Covariance compares the mixed moment with the product of the two separately calculated means. Their product is $\dfrac29$ and using common denominator $36$ gives difference $\dfrac9{36}-\dfrac8{36}$. The shape of the support explains its positive sign. For fixed $X=x$, $Y$ is uniform on $(0,x)$, so the conditional mean $\dfrac x2$ increases with $x$. The covariance is $\dfrac1{36}$.

</div>
