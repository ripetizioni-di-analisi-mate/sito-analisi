## Exercise 1

Three independent exponential waiting times of rate $\lambda$ are $2,4,9$ minutes. Find the method-of-moments estimates of the rate and the mean waiting time (scale).

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_\lambda[X]&=\frac1\lambda,\\[6pt]
\bar x&=\frac{2+4+9}{3}\,\mathrm{min}=\frac{15}{3}\,\mathrm{min}=5\,\mathrm{min},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1\lambda&=5\,\mathrm{min},\\[6pt]
\widehat\lambda&=\frac{1}{5}\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}=0.2\,\mathrm{min}^{-1},\\[6pt]
\widehat{\frac1\lambda}&=\frac{1}{\widehat\lambda}=5\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The model is parameterized by the rate, and its mean is the reciprocal, $E_\lambda[X]=\dfrac1\lambda$. The **method of moments** equates this theoretical mean to the observed mean of $\dfrac{15}{3}=5$ minutes. The equation $\dfrac1\lambda=5$ has unique positive solution $\lambda=\dfrac15$, giving an estimated rate of one-fifth per minute.

The scale parameter $\dfrac1\lambda$ measures time and equals the mean. Rate and scale are related one-to-one for positive values, so invert the rate estimate to obtain a scale of $5$ minutes. Even with the same data the units distinguish the parameters. Rate is measured in $\mathrm{min}^{-1}$ and scale in minutes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four independent exponential observations have sum $a=12\,\mathrm{min}$. Find the maximum likelihood estimate of the rate, the bias of its estimator, and the estimate given by an unbiased estimator.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(\lambda)&=\prod_{i=1}^4\lambda e^{-\lambda x_i}=\lambda^4e^{-\lambda a},\\[6pt]
\ell(\lambda)&=4\log\lambda-\lambda a,\\[6pt]
\ell'(\lambda)&=\frac4\lambda-a=\frac{4-a\lambda}{\lambda},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{ML}}&=\frac4{12}\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}
=\frac13\,\mathrm{min}^{-1},\\[6pt]
E_\lambda\!\left[\frac1A\right]&=\frac{\lambda}{4-1}=\frac{\lambda}{3},\\[6pt]
E_\lambda\!\left[\frac4A\right]&=\frac{4\lambda}{3},\\[6pt]
b_\lambda\!\left(\frac4A\right)&=\frac{4\lambda}{3}-\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{3},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_\lambda\!\left[\frac3A\right]&=3\cdot\frac{\lambda}{3}=\lambda,\\[6pt]
\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{corr}}&=\frac3{12}\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}
=\frac14\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

At the unbiased estimate of ¼ the derivative equals 4."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each observation contributes density $\lambda e^{-\lambda x_i}$, so the likelihood product depends on the data only through their sum $a$. With the data fixed, the log-likelihood derivative has the sign of $4-12\lambda$. It changes from positive to negative at $\lambda=\dfrac4a=\dfrac13$, the maximizer.

To assess bias consider the random sum $A$, which has a gamma distribution with shape $4$ and rate $\lambda$. By the result on **bias of the exponential rate estimator**, its reciprocal has expectation $\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1}=\dfrac{\lambda}{3}$. A numerator of four gives positive bias $\dfrac{\lambda}{3}$, one-third of the parameter. With numerator three the expectation equals the rate, so $\dfrac3A$ is unbiased. It gives an estimate of $\dfrac14$ per minute for this sample, below the maximum likelihood estimate.

</div>
