## Exercise 1

A normal population has known mean $\mu=4$ and unknown variance. The independent observations are $2,3,7$. Find the maximum likelihood estimate of the variance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(v)&=\prod_{i=1}^3\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi v}}\,e^{-\frac{(x_i-4)^2}{2v}},\qquad v=\sigma^2>0,\\[6pt]
\ell(v)&=-\frac32\log(2\pi v)-\frac{d}{2v},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
d=(2-4)^2+(3-4)^2+(7-4)^2=4+1+9=14,
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell'(v)&=-\frac{3}{2v}+\frac{14}{2v^2}
=\frac{14-3v}{2v^2},\\[6pt]
\ell'(v)>0 &\iff v<\frac{14}{3},\qquad
\ell'(v)<0 \iff v>\frac{14}{3},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\widehat{\sigma^2}=\frac{14}{3}\approx4.67.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The log-likelihood uses deviations from the known mean $\mu=4$. The log of the normal density contains $-\dfrac12\log(2\pi v)$ for each observation and the term $-\dfrac{(x_i-4)^2}{2v}$. Summing over the three observations gives the sum of squares $d=14$.

The term $-\dfrac{d}{2v}$ increases with $v$, while the normalizing term $-\dfrac32\log(2\pi v)$ decreases. The maximum occurs where these changes balance. With common positive denominator $2v^2$ the derivative has the sign of $14-3v$. It is positive before $\dfrac{14}{3}$ and negative afterward, so the numerator's zero is the maximum. The estimate is the mean squared deviation from the known mean, with divisor $n=3$, as in the treatment of **normal mean and variance** with known mean.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The observations $2,5,5,8$ come from a normal population with unknown mean and variance. Find both maximum likelihood estimates and compare the estimated variance with $s^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\mu&=\bar x=\frac{2+5+5+8}{4}=\frac{20}{4}=5,\\[6pt]
x_i-\bar x&:\quad -3,\ 0,\ 0,\ 3,\\[6pt]
d&=(2-5)^2+0^2+0^2+(8-5)^2=9+9=18,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\sigma^2}&=\frac{d}{n}=\frac{18}{4}=\frac92,\\[6pt]
s^2&=\frac{d}{n-1}=\frac{18}{3}=6,\\[6pt]
\frac{\widehat{\sigma^2}}{s^2}&=\frac{n-1}{n}=\frac34.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At each fixed variance the mean maximizing the likelihood minimizes the sum of squared deviations. By the decomposition $\sum_i(x_i-\mu)^2=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2+n(\bar x-\mu)^2$ it is $\bar x=5$. The deviations from the observed mean are $-3$, $0$, $0$, and $3$, with sum of squares $18$.

After substituting $\bar x$, maximizing over the variance divides the residual sum of squares by four, as in the previous exercise, giving $\dfrac92$. The unbiased estimator divides the same sum by three because the center was estimated, by **unbiasedness of the sample variance**, yielding $6$. The criteria give different estimates from the same sample, with ratio $\dfrac34$. The maximum likelihood estimate is smaller and its estimator has expectation $\dfrac34\sigma^2$ and bias $-\dfrac{\sigma^2}{4}$.

</div>
