## Exercise 1

Four readings of the same quantity are $1,3,5,7$. Calculate the unbiased sample variance and the descriptive variance with divisor $n$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{1+3+5+7}{4}=\frac{16}{4}=4,\\[6pt]
x_i-\bar x&:\quad -3,\ -1,\ 1,\ 3,\\[6pt]
d&=(-3)^2+(-1)^2+1^2+3^2\\[6pt]
&=9+1+1+9=20,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^2&=\frac{d}{n-1}=\frac{20}{4-1}=\frac{20}{3},\\[6pt]
v_n&=\frac{d}{n}=\frac{20}{4}=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.45 ymax=8.2 nomi="i,xᵢ" punti="1,1;2,3;3,5;4,7" linee="0.55,4 4.45,4" segmenti="1,1,1,4;2,3,2,4;3,4,3,5;4,4,4,7" titolo="The horizontal line marks the mean of 4. The segments show the deviations −3, −1, 1, and 3 used in both measures."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both measures use the sum of squared deviations from the observed mean. The mean is $4$, so the four deviations are $-3$, $-1$, $1$, and $3$. As for any data set, their sum is zero. The sum of their squares is $d=20$.

Divisor $n$ averages the four squared deviations to describe these data, giving $v_n=5$. Divisor $n-1$ gives an unbiased estimator of population variance for independent sampling from the same population, by **unbiasedness of the sample variance**. This gives $s^2=\dfrac{20}{3}\approx6.67$. Their ratio is $\dfrac{n}{n-1}=\dfrac43$ because the numerator is shared.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A population has known mean $\mu=4$ and unknown variance. For an independent sample with observations $2,4,9$, calculate the unbiased estimate based on deviations from the known mean and compare it with the one based on $S^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_i-4&:\quad -2,\ 0,\ 5,\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{3}\sum_i(x_i-4)^2&=\frac{4+0+25}{3}=\frac{29}{3},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{2+4+9}{3}=\frac{15}{3}=5,\\[6pt]
x_i-5&:\quad -3,\ -1,\ 4,\\[6pt]
s^2&=\frac{(2-5)^2+(4-5)^2+(9-5)^2}{2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{9+1+16}{2}=\frac{26}{2}=13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each squared deviation from the known mean has expectation $E[(X_i-\mu)^2]=\sigma^2$, so their mean is already unbiased and the divisor is $n=3$. The deviations $-2$, $0$, and $5$ give estimate $\dfrac{29}{3}\approx9.67$.

Using $\bar x=5$ estimates the center from the same observations and calls for divisor $n-1=2$, by **unbiasedness of the sample variance**. The observed mean minimizes the sum of squared deviations, reducing it from $29$ to $26$. Reducing the divisor from three to two more than offsets this decrease, so the second estimate is larger in this sample, $s^2=13$ versus $\dfrac{29}{3}$. Both rules are unbiased for $\sigma^2$, and the observed difference is due to sampling variability.

</div>
