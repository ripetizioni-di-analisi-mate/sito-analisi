## Exercise 1

A population mean $\mu$ is estimated from a measurement $X$ with expectation $\mu$. The instrument records $T=X+2$. Find its bias and give a correction yielding an unbiased estimator.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[T]&=E[X+2]\\[6pt]
&=E[X]+2\\[6pt]
&=\mu+2,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_\mu(T)&=E[T]-\mu\\[6pt]
&=(\mu+2)-\mu=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T'&=T-2,\\[6pt]
E[T']&=E[T]-2=\mu+2-2=\mu,\\[6pt]
b_\mu(T')&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
MSE_\mu(T)&=\operatorname{Var}(X)+2^2=\operatorname{Var}(X)+4,\\[6pt]
MSE_\mu(T')&=\operatorname{Var}(X)+0^2=\operatorname{Var}(X).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By linearity the expectation of $X+2$ is $E[X]=\mu$ plus the constant $2$. The additive shift moves every measurement equally, so it moves the expectation by two units. The bias is the difference between that expectation and the population mean, $2$ for every $\mu$.

Subtracting the known constant bias of two from each recorded value gives $T'=T-2$. Its expectation is $\mu$ for every parameter value, so it is **unbiased**. Subtracting a constant preserves variance, so $T$ and $T'$ have the same spread as the measurements. By the **mean squared error decomposition**, their mean squared errors differ only by the squared bias. The correction reduces mean squared error by $4$, namely $2^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two measurements $X_1,X_2$ have common expectation $\mu$. Consider $U_a=aX_1+2aX_2-1$. Determine whether a constant $a$ makes this rule unbiased for every $\mu\in\mathbb R$ and, if necessary, modify the constant term as well.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[U_a]&=aE[X_1]+2aE[X_2]-1\\[6pt]
&=a\mu+2a\mu-1\\[6pt]
&=3a\mu-1,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_\mu(U_a)&=E[U_a]-\mu\\[6pt]
&=(3a-1)\mu-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
b_0(U_a)=-1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{no value of } a \text{ makes } U_a \text{ unbiased}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[U_a+1]&=3a\mu,\\[6pt]
3a\mu=\mu\ \text{ for every }\mu
&\iff 3a=1
\iff a=\frac13.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_{\frac13}+1&=\frac13X_1+\frac23X_2=\frac{X_1+2X_2}{3},\\[6pt]
E\!\left[\frac{X_1+2X_2}{3}\right]&=\frac{\mu+2\mu}{3}=\mu.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By linearity, the coefficients $a$ and $2a$ multiply the common mean $\mu$, while the constant $-1$ passes unchanged through the expectation. Unbiasedness must hold for every possible mean. At mean zero any weights leave the fixed shift of minus one, so the bias at $\mu=0$ is $-1$ and no value of $a$ makes $U_a$ unbiased.

Adding $1$ removes that shift and gives expectation $3a\mu$. To equal $\mu$ for every $\mu$, the coefficient of $\mu$ must equal one, requiring $3a=1$, or $a=\dfrac13$. The resulting estimator is a weighted mean of $X_1$ and $X_2$ with weights $\dfrac13$ and $\dfrac23$ summing to one. This expectation argument also works for dependent measurements because linearity requires no independence.

</div>
