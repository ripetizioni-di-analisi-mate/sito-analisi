## Exercise 1

Five successes are observed in eight independent Bernoulli trials. Derive the maximum likelihood estimate of $p\in[0,1]$ from the log-likelihood.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(p)&=p^5(1-p)^3,\\[6pt]
\ell(p)&=5\log p+3\log(1-p),\qquad 0<p<1,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell'(p)&=\frac5p-\frac3{1-p}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5(1-p)-3p}{p(1-p)}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5-8p}{p(1-p)},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\ell'(p)>0 \iff p<\frac58,\qquad
\ell'(p)<0 \iff p>\frac58,
$$

$$
L(0)=L(1)=0,\qquad \widehat p=\frac58=0.625.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each success contributes $p$ and each failure $1-p$, giving likelihood $p^5(1-p)^3$. For $0<p<1$ it is positive, and taking its logarithm turns the product into a sum. The derivative has common denominator $p(1-p)$ and numerator $5(1-p)-3p$, which simplifies to $5-8p$.

Inside the interval the derivative's denominator is positive, so $\ell'$ has the sign of $5-8p$. The log-likelihood increases up to five-eighths and decreases afterward. The likelihood is zero at both endpoints because both outcomes occur in the sample, and positive in the interior. Thus $\dfrac58$ maximizes it on all of $[0,1]$. The estimate equals the observed success proportion, as the result on **Bernoulli maximum likelihood** states.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A model imposes $p\in[0.3,0.6]$. Eight successes are observed in ten independent Bernoulli trials. Find the maximum likelihood estimate on the specified domain.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell(p)&=8\log p+2\log(1-p),\\[6pt]
\ell'(p)&=\frac8p-\frac2{1-p}=\frac{8-10p}{p(1-p)},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p\le0.6
&\implies 8-10p\ge 8-10\cdot0.6=2>0,\\[6pt]
\ell'(p)&>0\quad\text{for } 0.3\le p\le0.6,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\widehat p=0.6,\qquad \ell(0.6)\approx-5.92.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The log-likelihood has the same form as in the previous exercise, with eight successes and two failures. The sample proportion $0.8$ sets the unconstrained derivative to zero. All admissible values in this model lie below it, and for $p\le0.6$ the numerator $8-10p$ is at least $2$.

The denominator $p(1-p)$ is also positive, so the derivative is positive throughout the interval and the log-likelihood strictly increases. Its maximizer is the upper endpoint $\widehat p=0.6$, which belongs to the domain. The equation $\ell'(p)=0$ has no solution in the permitted interval, leaving a boundary maximum. The admissible value closest to the observed proportion is $0.6$.

</div>
