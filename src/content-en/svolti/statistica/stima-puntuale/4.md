## Exercise 1

The counts of visits in four equal periods are $0,2,2,4$. Assume a Poisson model with the same parameter $\lambda$ in every period. Calculate the method-of-moments estimate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_1(\lambda)&=E_\lambda[X]=\lambda,\\[6pt]
\widehat m_1&=\bar x=\frac{0+2+2+4}{4}=\frac{8}{4}=2,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_1(\lambda)&=\widehat m_1\\[6pt]
\lambda&=2,\\[6pt]
\widehat\lambda&=2\ \text{ visits per period}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\widehat\lambda=2\in[0,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The model has one parameter, so the **method of moments** needs only the first-moment equation. The theoretical first moment is the mean number of visits per period, equal to $\lambda$ for a Poisson distribution. The corresponding sample moment is the mean of all four counts, including the period with no visits. Their sum is $8$ and their mean $2$.

Equating the moments identifies $\lambda$ directly because the Poisson parameter equals its expectation. The estimate is $\widehat\lambda=2$ visits per period. It is nonnegative and belongs to the parameter space. The estimator is $\bar X$, unbiased for $\lambda$ because the sample mean is unbiased for the population expectation. The period with zero visits counts equally. Omitting it would raise the mean to $\dfrac83$ and increase the estimate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a sample from $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ the first sample moment is $4$ and the second is $22$. Calculate both method-of-moments estimates and determine whether the resulting parameter values are admissible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_1(\mu,\sigma^2)&=E[X]=\mu,\\[6pt]
m_2(\mu,\sigma^2)&=E[X^2]=\operatorname{Var}(X)+E[X]^2=\mu^2+\sigma^2,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
\mu=4,\\[4pt]
\mu^2+\sigma^2=22,
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\mu&=4,\\[6pt]
\widehat{\sigma^2}&=22-\widehat\mu^{\,2}=22-16=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\widehat\mu=4\in\mathbb R,\qquad \widehat{\sigma^2}=6>0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are two unknown parameters, so equate the first two theoretical moments to their sample counterparts and solve. The first moment identifies the mean. The identity $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-E[X]^2$ gives the second moment and shows that it includes both the squared mean and the variance.

The first equation gives $\widehat\mu=4$. In the second subtract the squared estimate $16$, leaving $\widehat{\sigma^2}=6$. The mean is real and variance positive, so both belong to the parameter space of the nondegenerate normal distribution. The difference $\widehat m_2-\widehat m_1^{\,2}$ is the mean of squared deviations from the observed mean, a variance with divisor $n$. By **unbiasedness of the sample variance** the corresponding estimator has expectation $\dfrac{n-1}{n}\sigma^2$ and is biased. Converting to the estimate with divisor $n-1$ requires $n$.

</div>
