## Exercise 1

Three independent devices each pass a test with probability $\dfrac34$. Calculate the probability that exactly two pass.

$$
X\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left(3,\frac34\right)
$$

$$
P(\mathrm{RRF})=P(\mathrm{RFR})=P(\mathrm{FRR})=\frac34\cdot\frac34\cdot\frac14=\frac{9}{64}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=2)&=\binom32\left(\frac34\right)^2\frac14\\[6pt]
&=3\cdot\frac9{64}=\frac{27}{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.2 xmax=5.05 ymin=-0.15 ymax=3.9 legenda=0 linee="0.7,2.7 3.7,2.7;0.7,1.8 3.7,1.8;0.7,0.9 3.7,0.9" punti="0.7,2.7,pieno;2.2,2.7,pieno;3.7,2.7,pieno;0.7,1.8,pieno;2.2,1.8,pieno;3.7,1.8,pieno;0.7,0.9,pieno;2.2,0.9,pieno;3.7,0.9,pieno" etichette="0.7,3.6,trial 1;2.2,3.6,trial 2;3.7,3.6,trial 3;0.7,2.95,R;2.2,2.95,R;3.7,2.95,F;0.7,2.05,R;2.2,2.05,F;3.7,2.05,R;0.7,1.15,F;2.2,1.15,R;3.7,1.15,R" titolo="The three favorable sequences differ in the position of the failure. R denotes a success and F a failure."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each device gives a binary outcome. Since the three trials are independent with the same success probability, the count is binomial with $n=3$ and $p=\dfrac34$. A single device fails with probability $1-\dfrac34=\dfrac14$.

A sequence with two passes and one failure has probability $\dfrac9{64}$. By independence the sequence probability is the product of the three outcome probabilities, with two factors of $\dfrac34$ and one of $\dfrac14$ in any order. The failing device can occupy any of three positions, corresponding to disjoint events. Add three equal probabilities. The number of positions is the coefficient $\binom32=3$ in the binomial formula.

The other masses are $P(X=0)=\dfrac1{64}$, $P(X=1)=\dfrac9{64}$ and $P(X=3)=\left(\dfrac34\right)^3=\dfrac{27}{64}$, and all four sum to $1$. Thus two and three passes are tied as the most likely counts.

The probability that exactly two devices pass is therefore $\dfrac{27}{64}\approx0.42$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four independent checks succeed with probability $\dfrac23$. Calculate $P(X=2)$, where $X$ counts successes, and the probability of exactly two successes with the last check successful.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=2)&=\binom42\left(\frac23\right)^2\left(\frac13\right)^2\\[6pt]
&=6\cdot\frac49\cdot\frac19=\frac{24}{81}=\frac8{27}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=2,\ \text{last trial a success})
&=P(\text{one success in the first three trials})\cdot\frac23\\[6pt]
&=\binom31\frac23\left(\frac13\right)^2\cdot\frac23\\[6pt]
&=\frac29\cdot\frac23=\frac4{27}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The count $X$ is binomial with $n=4$ and $p=\dfrac23$ because the checks are independent with the same success probability. For the first probability, choose any two positions for the successes in $\binom42=6$ ways. Each sequence with two successes and two failures has probability $\dfrac49\cdot\dfrac19=\dfrac4{81}$, so $P(X=2)=\dfrac{24}{81}=\dfrac8{27}$.

In the second case, one position is fixed because the last check must succeed. Exactly one of the first three checks must therefore succeed. The count for those checks is binomial with $n=3$, and its mass at $1$ is $3\cdot\dfrac23\cdot\dfrac19=\dfrac29$. The last outcome is independent of the first three, so multiply the probability of their count by $\dfrac23$. The restriction leaves three of the six configurations with two successes and halves the probability.

The two requested probabilities are $\dfrac8{27}$ and $\dfrac4{27}$.

</div>
