## Exercise 1

Arrivals follow a homogeneous Poisson process at a rate of $8$ per hour. Calculate the probability of zero arrivals in $15\,\mathrm{min}$.

$$
t=15\,\mathrm{min}=\frac{15}{60}\,\mathrm{h}=\frac14\,\mathrm{h}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda&=8\,\mathrm{h^{-1}}\cdot\frac14\,\mathrm{h}=2\\[6pt]
P(X=0)&=e^{-2}\frac{2^0}{0!}=e^{-2}\approx0.135.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P(X\ge1)=1-e^{-2}\approx0.865
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate is per hour, so express the duration in hours before multiplying by the rate. Fifteen minutes is one quarter of an hour, giving expected count $2$. The product $rt$ is dimensionless because the hours in the rate and duration cancel.

Since the process is homogeneous Poisson, this value is the parameter of the count distribution over the interval, namely $X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(2)$. The Poisson mass at $k$ is $e^{-\lambda}\dfrac{\lambda^k}{k!}$. For a count of zero, the power and the factorial both equal $1$, leaving only the factor $e^{-2}$. Under this distribution, the mean and variance of the number of arrivals in a quarter hour both equal $2$.

The probability of no arrivals in fifteen minutes is therefore $e^{-2}\approx0.135$. Its complement, at least one arrival in the same quarter hour, has probability $1-e^{-2}\approx0.865$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous Poisson process has a rate of $3$ events per $10\,\mathrm{min}$. Calculate the probability of at least two events in $20\,\mathrm{min}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda&=3\cdot\frac{20}{10}=3\cdot 2=6\\[6pt]
P(X=0)&=e^{-6}\frac{6^0}{0!}=e^{-6}\\[6pt]
P(X=1)&=e^{-6}\frac{6^1}{1!}=6e^{-6}\\[6pt]
P(X\ge2)&=1-(e^{-6}+6e^{-6})\\[6pt]
&=1-7e^{-6}\approx0.983.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interval contains two ten-minute periods, so its parameter is twice the rate per period. In a homogeneous Poisson process the mean count is proportional to duration, so $\lambda=6$ and the count has distribution $\operatorname{Pois}(6)$.

“At least two” includes infinitely many counts, from $2$ onward. Its complement consists of the disjoint counts $0$ and $1$. Add their Poisson masses and subtract the sum from $1$. The mass at $1$ is $6e^{-6}$ because $\dfrac{6^1}{1!}=6$, and the two masses sum to $7e^{-6}\approx0.017$.

The probability of at least two events in twenty minutes is therefore $1-7e^{-6}\approx0.983$. It is close to $1$ because the mean $6$ is much larger than the threshold $2$, and the largest masses are at $5$ and $6$.
