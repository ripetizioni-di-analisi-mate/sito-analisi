## Exercise 1

Independent trials are repeated with success probability $\dfrac25$. Calculate the expected number of failures before the first success.

$$
T\sim\text{geometric with parameter } p=\frac25,\qquad q=\frac35
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[T]&=\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{\frac25}=\frac52\\[6pt]
E[T-1]&=E[T]-1=\frac52-1=\frac32\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(T-1)&=\operatorname{Var}(T)=\frac{q}{p^2}=\frac{\frac35}{\frac4{25}}=\frac{15}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The trial number $T$ of the first success is geometric because the trials are independent and have the same success probability $\dfrac25$. The geometric variable $T$ counts all trials up to and including the successful one. The number of failures is therefore $T-1$. The formula $E[T]=\dfrac1p$ gives $E[T]=\dfrac52$.

By linearity of expectation, subtract one trial from the expected value of $T$. The value $T-1=0$, meaning no failures, has probability $P(T=1)=\dfrac25$. The result $\dfrac32$ is a mean and need not be an integer, although every observed failure count is an integer.

Subtracting a constant shifts every value and the mean by the same amount, so the number of failures has the same variance as $T$. Using $q=\dfrac35$ and $p^2=\dfrac4{25}$ gives $\dfrac{15}4$.

The expected number of failures before the first success is therefore $\dfrac32$, or one and a half trials.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Each independent attempt succeeds with probability $\dfrac12$. Let $T_3$ be the trial number of the third success. Calculate $E[T_3]$ and $P(T_3=5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[T_3]&=\frac{r}{p}=3\cdot\frac{1}{\frac12}=6\\[6pt]
P(T_3=5)&=\binom{5-1}{3-1}p^3(1-p)^{5-3}\\[6pt]
&=\binom42\left(\frac12\right)^2
\left(\frac12\right)^2\frac12\\[6pt]
&=6\cdot\frac1{32}=\frac{6}{32}=\frac3{16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The variable $T_3$ is negative binomial with $r=3$ and $p=\dfrac12$ because it counts the independent trials needed to obtain three successes. The waiting time for the third success is the sum of the three waiting times between successive successes, each with mean $2$. This gives $E[T_3]=6$ trials. The three waiting times are independent geometric variables, each with variance $\dfrac{q}{p^2}=\dfrac{\frac12}{\frac14}=2$, so the variance of $T_3$ is $3\cdot2=6$.

To finish on exactly the fifth trial, the first four trials must contain two successes and the fifth must succeed. There are six ways to choose the two success positions among the first four because $\binom42=\dfrac{4\cdot3}{2}=6$. Each such five-outcome sequence has three successes and two failures, hence probability $\left(\dfrac12\right)^5=\dfrac1{32}$. Independence allows us to multiply the probability of the count in the first four trials by the probability of the final success.

The expected number of trials until the third success is $6$, and the probability that it occurs on exactly the fifth trial is $\dfrac3{16}$.

</div>
