## Exercise 1

A batch contains $4$ marked and $6$ unmarked items. A uniform sample of $3$ items is drawn without replacement. Calculate the probability of drawing exactly two marked items.

$$
N=10,\qquad R=4,\qquad n=3
$$

$$
\binom42=\frac{4\cdot3}{2}=6,\qquad \binom61=6,\qquad \binom{10}3=\frac{10\cdot9\cdot8}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=120
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=2)&=\frac{\binom42\binom61}{\binom{10}3}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{6\cdot6}{120}=\frac{36}{120}=\frac3{10}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $X$ of marked items has a hypergeometric distribution with $N=10$, $R=4$ and $n=3$ because the sample is a uniformly chosen subset drawn without replacement. The value $2$ belongs to the support because $\max(0,3-6)=0\le2\le\min(3,4)=3$.

Every three-item subset is equally likely, so count the favorable samples and all possible samples. A favorable sample contains two of the four marked items and one of the six unmarked items. These choices combine freely, giving the product of the two binomial coefficients, $36$. The denominator counts all ways to draw three distinct items, giving $120$.

The probability of drawing exactly two marked items is therefore $\dfrac3{10}$. With replacement, the count would be binomial and the same event would have a different probability, $\binom32\left(\dfrac25\right)^2\dfrac35=\dfrac{36}{125}=0.288$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

From $8$ units, of which $3$ have a characteristic of interest, $6$ are drawn uniformly without replacement. Determine the support, mean and variance of the count $X$ of units with that characteristic.

$$
N=8,\qquad R=3,\qquad N-R=5,\qquad n=6,\qquad p=\frac RN=\frac38
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max(0,6-5)&=1,\qquad \min(6,3)=3\\[6pt]
E[X]&=6\cdot\frac38=\frac94\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(X)
&=6\cdot\frac38\cdot\frac58\cdot\frac{8-6}{8-1}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{90}{64}\cdot\frac27=\frac{180}{448}=\frac{45}{112}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P(X=1)=\frac{\binom31\binom55}{\binom86}=\frac3{28},\qquad
P(X=2)=\frac{\binom32\binom54}{\binom86}=\frac{15}{28},\qquad
P(X=3)=\frac{\binom33\binom53}{\binom86}=\frac{10}{28}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every sample of six units contains at least one unit of interest because there are only five other units. It contains at most three because the population has only three units of interest. Every intermediate count is possible, so the support is $\{1,2,3\}$. The three masses, calculated from $\binom86=28$ possible samples, sum to $\dfrac{28}{28}=1$.

Expectation adds the marginal probabilities for the six sample positions, each equal to $\dfrac38$, giving $E[X]=\dfrac94$. The largest mass is at $2$, the integer nearest the mean. The table gives the same mean because $1\cdot\dfrac3{28}+2\cdot\dfrac{15}{28}+3\cdot\dfrac{10}{28}=\dfrac{63}{28}=\dfrac94$.

For the variance, use the finite population correction because sampling is without replacement. The factor is $\dfrac27$ and reduces the binomial variance $\dfrac{90}{64}$ to $\dfrac{45}{112}\approx0.40$. The count therefore has support $\{1,2,3\}$, mean $\dfrac94$ and variance $\dfrac{45}{112}$.

</div>
