## Exercise 1

A machine produces $0$, $1$ or $3$ rejected parts with respective probability masses $2c$, $c$ and $5c$. Find $c$ and the probability that the number of rejected parts lies between $1$ and $3$, inclusive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2c+c+5c&=1\\[6pt]
8c&=1\\[6pt]
c&=\frac18
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
p_X(0)=\frac28=\frac14,\qquad p_X(1)=\frac18,\qquad p_X(3)=\frac58
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(1\le X\le3)&=p_X(1)+p_X(3)\\[6pt]
&=\frac18+\frac58=\frac34.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P(1\le X\le 3)=1-p_X(0)=1-\frac14=\frac34
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three counts are mutually exclusive and exhaust the possible outcomes, so their masses must sum to $1$. Factoring out $c$ gives the equation $8c=1$, hence $c=\dfrac18$. The value $c=\dfrac18$ makes all masses positive, so the table defines a probability distribution. Substituting $c$ in the three expressions gives masses $\dfrac14$, $\dfrac18$ and $\dfrac58$, which indeed sum to $1$.

To find the requested probability, first identify the support points in the closed interval. The value $2$ lies in the interval but has mass zero. Add the masses at $1$ and $3$. The complement gives the same result because the only support point outside the interval $[1,3]$ is $0$.

The probability that the machine produces between one and three rejected parts is therefore $\dfrac34$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A random variable $X$ has support $\{-1,0,2\}$. Given that $F_X(-1)=\dfrac14$ and that $p_X(2)$ is twice $p_X(0)$, determine the probability masses and $P(-1<X\le1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_X(-1)&=F_X(-1)=\frac14\\[6pt]
p_X(0)+2p_X(0)&=1-\frac14=\frac34\\[6pt]
3p_X(0)&=\frac34\\[6pt]
p_X(0)&=\frac14,\qquad p_X(2)=2\cdot\frac14=\frac12
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_X(1)=p_X(-1)+p_X(0)=\frac14+\frac14=\frac12
$$

$$
P(-1<X\le1)=F_X(1)-F_X(-1)=\frac12-\frac14=\frac14.
$$

Their difference isolates the probability mass at 0."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The cutoff $-1$ is the smallest point in the support, so $F_X(-1)$ includes just one mass. The other two masses must account for the remaining probability. Their ratio gives an equation with one unknown. Substituting $p_X(2)=2p_X(0)$ in the normalization condition gives $3p_X(0)=\dfrac34$, so $p_X(0)=\dfrac14$ and $p_X(2)=\dfrac12$.

At the cutoff $1$, the cumulative distribution function already includes $-1$ and $0$. The point $2$ exceeds the cutoff, so its mass is excluded and $F_X(1)=\dfrac12$. Subtracting $F_X(-1)$ removes precisely the mass at the open endpoint. Only the support value $0$ lies in the interval $(-1,1]$, so the difference equals $p_X(0)$. The jumps at $-1$, $0$ and $2$ are $\dfrac14$, $\dfrac14$ and $\dfrac12$, respectively, the three masses found above.

The required probability is $\dfrac14$.

</div>
