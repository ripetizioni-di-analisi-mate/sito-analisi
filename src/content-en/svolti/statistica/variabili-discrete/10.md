## Exercise 1

An inspection is repeated independently three times. Each time it flags an anomaly with probability $\dfrac15$. Calculate the probability of at least one flag.

$$
X\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left(3,\frac15\right),\qquad 1-\frac15=\frac45
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=0)&=\binom30\left(\frac15\right)^0\left(\frac45\right)^3=\frac{64}{125}\\[6pt]
P(X\ge1)&=1-P(X=0)=1-\frac{64}{125}=\frac{61}{125}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P(X=1)+P(X=2)+P(X=3)=\frac{48}{125}+\frac{12}{125}+\frac1{125}=\frac{61}{125}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $X$ of flags is binomial with $n=3$ and $p=\dfrac15$ because the repetitions are independent with the same probability of a flag. “At least one” includes one, two and three flags. Its complement is no flags, requiring three trials without a detected anomaly.

By independence, multiply the three no-flag probabilities, each equal to $\dfrac45$. Subtracting this probability from $1$ gives the probability of meeting the threshold. The complement requires only one mass instead of three. Directly adding the masses at $1$, $2$ and $3$, with numerators $3\cdot1\cdot16=48$, $3\cdot1\cdot4=12$ and $1$, gives the same result. The mean number of flags is $np=\dfrac35$. For any nonnegative integer-valued count, $P(X\ge1)\le E[X]$ because each value $k\ge1$ contributes to the mean with factor $k\ge1$. Indeed, $\dfrac{61}{125}<\dfrac{75}{125}$.

The probability of at least one flag is therefore $\dfrac{61}{125}=0.488$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A system starts if at least two of its four modules respond. The modules respond independently with probability $\dfrac34$. Calculate the probability that the system starts.

$$
X\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left(4,\frac34\right),\qquad P(X\ge2)=1-P(X\le1)=1-F_X(1)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=0)&=\left(\frac14\right)^4=\frac1{256}\\[6pt]
P(X=1)&=\binom41\frac34\left(\frac14\right)^3=4\cdot\frac{3}{256}=\frac{12}{256}\\[6pt]
F_X(1)&=\frac1{256}+\frac{12}{256}=\frac{13}{256}\\[6pt]
P(X\ge2)&=1-\frac{13}{256}=\frac{243}{256}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of responding modules is binomial because the four outcomes are independent with the same response probability. The system stays off if at most one module responds, that is, on event $X\le1$. Its probability is the cumulative distribution function evaluated at $1$.

With no responses, each of the four modules fails to respond with probability $\dfrac14$. With one response, any of the four modules can be the one that responds. Each sequence with one response has probability $\dfrac34\cdot\left(\dfrac14\right)^3=\dfrac3{256}$, and there are four such sequences. The two masses for failure to start add because the cases are disjoint, giving $F_X(1)=\dfrac{13}{256}$. The required result is the probability of their complement. The mean number of responding modules is $np=3$, above the threshold $2$, and the mass of the distribution is concentrated on the counts that start the system.

The probability that the system starts is therefore $\dfrac{243}{256}\approx0.949$.

</div>
