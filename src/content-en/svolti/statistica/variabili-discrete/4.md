## Exercise 1

Four independent transmissions each fail with probability $\dfrac14$. Calculate the mean and variance of the number $X$ of failed transmissions.

$$
X=I_1+I_2+I_3+I_4,\qquad I_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\left(\frac14\right)
$$

$$
E[I_j]=\frac14,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(I_j)=\frac14\cdot\frac34=\frac3{16}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X]&=4\cdot\frac14=1\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=4\cdot\frac3{16}=4\cdot\frac14\cdot\frac34=\frac34\\[6pt]
\sigma_X&=\sqrt{\frac34}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\approx0.87.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For this count, a failed transmission is the outcome called a success in the binomial model. Let $I_j$ be the indicator equal to $1$ if transmission $j$ fails. Then $X$ is the sum of the four indicators and has distribution $\operatorname{Bin}\left(4,\dfrac14\right)$.

By the Bernoulli formulas, each failure indicator has mean $\dfrac14$ and variance $\dfrac14\cdot\dfrac34$. Linearity of expectation gives the mean of the sum as the sum of the means. Since the four transmissions are independent, the variance of the count is also the sum of the four indicator variances. This recovers the binomial formulas $np$ and $np(1-p)$.

The mean number of failed transmissions is therefore $1$ and the variance is $\dfrac34$. The mean $1$ describes the average count over many groups of four transmissions under the same conditions. The standard deviation is the square root of the variance and equals $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\approx0.87$ transmissions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A machine performs six independent checks. Each flags a defect with probability $\dfrac13$. If $X$ counts the flags, the cost is $C=12+3X$ euros. Calculate $E[C]$ and $\operatorname{Var}(C)$.

$$
X\sim\operatorname{Bin}\left(6,\frac13\right)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X]&=6\cdot\frac13=2\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=6\cdot\frac13\cdot\frac23=\frac{12}{9}=\frac43\\[6pt]
E[C]&=12+3\cdot2=18\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(C)&=3^2\cdot\frac43=9\cdot\frac43=12\\[6pt]
\sigma_C&=\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3\approx3.46.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The machine performs a fixed number of independent trials with constant success probability. The count is therefore binomial, with mean $2$ and variance $\dfrac43$ from the formulas $np$ and $np(1-p)$ with $n=6$ and $p=\dfrac13$.

Cost is an affine transformation of the count with $a=12$ and $b=3$, so apply the **theorem on moments of an affine transformation**. Each flag adds $3\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so the expected total includes $6\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in variable costs and $12\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in fixed costs. The mean cost is therefore $18\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The fixed fee leaves dispersion unchanged because it shifts each cost and its mean by the same amount. In the variable cost, each deviation of the count from its mean is multiplied by $3$, so the variance is multiplied by $9$. The cost variance is $12\,\mathrm{EUR}^2$ and its standard deviation is $2\sqrt3\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx3.46\,\mathrm{EUR}$, three times the standard deviation $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3}$ of the count.

</div>
