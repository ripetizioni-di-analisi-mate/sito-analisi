## Exercise 1

Let $K\sim\operatorname{Bin}(100,0.5)$. Approximate $P(K\le54)$ using the normal distribution with a continuity correction. Use $\Phi(0.9)\approx0.8159$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
np&=100\cdot0.5=50,\qquad n(1-p)=50,\\[6pt]
E[K]&=50,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(K)=100\cdot0.5\cdot0.5=25,\qquad SE(K)=5,\\[6pt]
\{K\le54\}&=\{K\in\{0,1,\ldots,54\}\}\ \longrightarrow\ Y\le54.5,\\[6pt]
z&=\frac{54.5-50}{5}=\frac{4.5}{5}=0.9,\\[6pt]
P(K\le54)&\approx\Phi(0.9)
\approx0.8159.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rule of thumb for a normal approximation to the binomial holds because $np$ and $n(1-p)$ both equal $50$, which is at least $10$. The count has mean $np=50$ and variance $np(1-p)=25$, so the approximating normal variable $Y$ has distribution $N(50,25)$ and standard deviation $5$.

The largest admissible count is $54$. With the **continuity correction**, the mass at $54$ corresponds to the area over the interval from $53.5$ to $54.5$, including the right half of the cell centered at $54$. The continuous cutoff is therefore $54.5$, which lies $\dfrac{4.5}{5}=0.9$ standard deviations above the center. Evaluate the distribution function at $0.9$ because the event includes all lower counts. The probability is **approximately $0.8159$**. The exact sum of binomial masses is also about $0.8159$, so the two probabilities agree to four decimal places.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $144$ independent Bernoulli trials with $p=0.5$, approximate the probability that the proportion $\hat p$ is strictly greater than $0.55$. Use $\Phi(1.25)\approx0.8944$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[K]&=144\cdot0.5=72,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(K)=144\cdot0.5\cdot0.5=36,\qquad SE(K)=6,\\[6pt]
144\cdot0.55&=79.2,\qquad
\{\hat p>0.55\}=\{K>79.2\}=\{K\ge80\},\\[6pt]
\{K\ge80\}&\ \longrightarrow\ Y\ge79.5,\\[6pt]
z&=\frac{79.5-72}{6}=\frac{7.5}{6}=1.25,\\[6pt]
P(\hat p>0.55)
&\approx1-\Phi(1.25)\approx1-0.8944=0.1056.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The proportion is $\hat p=\dfrac{K}{144}$, where $K\sim\operatorname{Bin}(144,0.5)$ counts successes. Since $np=n(1-p)=72$ exceeds $10$, the normal approximation is appropriate, with mean $72$ and standard deviation $\sqrt{36}=6$. Multiplying the proportion threshold by the sample size converts it to the count threshold $79.2$.

Counts are integers, so the first count strictly above $79.2$ is $80$. The **continuity correction** includes the entire continuous cell centered at $80$, starting at $79.5$ and extending the event to the right. With count standard deviation $6$, the corrected cutoff has standardized value $1.25$. The normal right tail gives probability **approximately $0.1056$**. Without the correction the cutoff would be $79.2$ with standardized value $1.2$, and the area would include part of the cell for count $79$, which is outside the event.

</div>
