## Exercise 1

A measurement has mean $12\,\mathrm{g}$ and variance $4\,\mathrm{g}^2$. Use Chebyshev's inequality to obtain a lower bound on $P(7<X<17)$, with $X$ expressed in grams.

$$
\begin{aligned}
7&=12-5,\qquad 17=12+5,\\[6pt]
\{7<X<17\}&=\{|X-12|<5\},\\[6pt]
P(|X-12|\ge5)&\le\frac{\sigma^2}{\varepsilon^2}=\frac4{5^2}=\frac{4}{25}=0.16,\\[6pt]
P(7<X<17)&=1-P(|X-12|\ge5)\\[6pt]
&\ge1-0.16=0.84.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both endpoints are 5 grams from the mean of 12 and belong to the complement."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both endpoints are $5\,\mathrm{g}$ from the mean, so the open interval corresponds to the event $|X-12|<5$. The measurement has finite variance $4\,\mathrm{g}^2$ and the tolerance $\varepsilon=5\,\mathrm{g}$ is positive. **Chebyshev's inequality** therefore applies to a deviation of at least $5\,\mathrm{g}$. The ratio $\dfrac{4}{25}$ is dimensionless because both numerator and denominator are in $\mathrm{g}^2$.

A deviation of at least $5\,\mathrm{g}$ is the complement of membership in the open interval. Subtracting its upper bound from one gives the required lower bound. The measurement lies between $7$ and $17$ grams with probability **at least $0.84$**. In standard deviation units, $\sigma=2\,\mathrm{g}$ means the tolerance is $k\sigma$ with $k=2.5$, so the bound $\dfrac{1}{k^2}=\dfrac{1}{6.25}$ again equals $0.16$. The bound uses only the mean and variance and holds for every distribution with these moments.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A population has standard deviation $9\,\mathrm{g}$. The measurements are i.i.d. Find the minimum $n$ for which Chebyshev's inequality guarantees $P(|\bar X_n-\mu|<2\,\mathrm{g})\ge0.95$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_n)&=\frac{9^2}{n}\,\mathrm{g}^2=\frac{81}{n}\,\mathrm{g}^2,\\[6pt]
P(|\bar X_n-\mu|\ge2\,\mathrm{g})
&\le\frac{\dfrac{81}{n}}{2^2}=\frac{81}{4n},\\[6pt]
P(|\bar X_n-\mu|<2\,\mathrm{g})&\ge1-\frac{81}{4n},\\[6pt]
1-\frac{81}{4n}\ge0.95
\iff\frac{81}{4n}\le0.05
&\iff n\ge\frac{81}{4\cdot0.05}=405,\\[6pt]
n=404&\implies\frac{81}{1616}\approx0.0501>0.05,\\[6pt]
n_{\min}&=405.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The statistic is the sample mean, so use its variance $\dfrac{81}{n}\,\mathrm{g}^2$ and the tolerance $\varepsilon=2\,\mathrm{g}$ in Chebyshev's inequality. Dividing by $\varepsilon^2=4\,\mathrm{g}^2$ gives the upper bound $\dfrac{81}{4n}$ on a deviation of at least $2\,\mathrm{g}$. By taking the complement, the probability of remaining within the tolerance is at least $1-\dfrac{81}{4n}$.

The probability outside the tolerance must be at most $0.05$. Requiring the upper bound to be at most $0.05$ gives a sufficient sample size condition. Multiplying by the positive quantity $\dfrac{4n}{0.05}$ yields $n\ge405$. With $404$ measurements the bound is just above $0.05$. Equality is allowed, so the minimum is **$n=405$**. For a normal population $78$ measurements would suffice because $1.96\cdot\dfrac{9}{\sqrt n}\le2$ is equivalent to $n\ge77.8$. The Chebyshev guarantee holds for every distribution with the stated standard deviation.

</div>
