## Exercise 1

Each of $100$ i.i.d. devices consumes an average of $2\,\mathrm{kWh}$ with standard deviation $0.5\,\mathrm{kWh}$. Approximate the probability that total consumption exceeds $205\,\mathrm{kWh}$. Use $\Phi(1)\approx0.8413$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[T_{100}]&=100\cdot2\,\mathrm{kWh}=200\,\mathrm{kWh},\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(T_{100})&=100\cdot0.5^2\,\mathrm{kWh}^2=25\,\mathrm{kWh}^2,\\[6pt]
SE(T_{100})&=\sqrt{25}\,\mathrm{kWh}=\sqrt{100}\cdot0.5\,\mathrm{kWh}
=5\,\mathrm{kWh},\\[6pt]
z&=\frac{205-200}{5}=1,\\[6pt]
P(T_{100}>205\,\mathrm{kWh})
&\approx1-\Phi(1)\approx1-0.8413=0.1587.
\end{aligned}
$$

The shaded area beyond $205$ is approximately $0.1587$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The threshold concerns the total, so its center is the sum of the expectations, $100\cdot2=200\,\mathrm{kWh}$. Independence allows the variances to be added, with each device contributing $0.25\,\mathrm{kWh}^2$. Taking the square root shows that the total has standard deviation $\sqrt{100}=10$ times that of one device, namely $5\,\mathrm{kWh}$.

The devices are i.i.d. with finite variance, so the **central limit theorem** approximates the distribution of the total by $N(200,25)$. The threshold $205\,\mathrm{kWh}$ is one standard deviation above the mean and has standardized value $1$. For an exceedance use the normal right tail, the complement of the distribution function. Total consumption exceeds $205\,\mathrm{kWh}$ with probability **approximately $0.1587$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A shipment contains $81$ i.i.d. items with mean mass $120\,\mathrm{g}$ and standard deviation $15\,\mathrm{g}$. Approximate the threshold $c$ that the total exceeds with probability $0.025$. Use $z_{0.975}\approx1.96$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[T_{81}]&=81\cdot120\,\mathrm{g}=9720\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(T_{81})&=81\cdot15^2\,\mathrm{g}^2=18225\,\mathrm{g}^2,\\[6pt]
SE(T_{81})&=\sqrt{18225}\,\mathrm{g}=9\cdot15\,\mathrm{g}=135\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
P(T_{81}>c)=0.025
&\iff P(T_{81}\le c)=0.975\\[6pt]
&\iff\frac{c-9720}{135}\approx z_{0.975}\approx1.96,\\[6pt]
c&\approx(9720+1.96\cdot135)\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=(9720+264.6)\,\mathrm{g}=9984.6\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum of the $81$ masses has expectation $81\cdot120\,\mathrm{g}$. The masses are independent, so their variances add and the standard deviation of the total is $\sqrt{81}=9$ times that of one item, namely $135\,\mathrm{g}$. By the **central limit theorem**, the total has approximate distribution $N(9720,\,135^2)$.

An exceedance probability of $0.025$ leaves probability $0.975$ to the left. The cutoff lies above the center and its standardized value is the positive standard normal quantile $1.96$. To return to the total scale, multiply by $135\,\mathrm{g}$ to obtain $264.6\,\mathrm{g}$ and add the mean of the total. This gives the approximate cutoff **$c\approx9984.6\,\mathrm{g}$**, or about $9.98\,\mathrm{kg}$. Dividing by $81$ shows that this is a mean mass of about $123.27\,\mathrm{g}$, or $1.96$ standard errors of size $\dfrac{15}{9}\,\mathrm{g}$ above $120\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>
