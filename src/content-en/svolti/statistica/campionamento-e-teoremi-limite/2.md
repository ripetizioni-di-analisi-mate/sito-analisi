## Exercise 1

Nine independent observations are drawn from a $N(20,16)$ population. Calculate $P(\bar X_9>22)$. Use $\Phi(1.5)\approx0.9332$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma&=\sqrt{16}=4,\\[6pt]
\bar X_9&\sim N\!\left(20,\frac{16}{9}\right),\\[6pt]
SE(\bar X_9)&=\frac{4}{\sqrt9}=\frac43,\\[6pt]
z&=\frac{22-20}{\frac43}=2\cdot\frac34=1.5,\\[6pt]
P(\bar X_9>22)&=P(Z>1.5)=1-\Phi(1.5)\\[6pt]
&\approx1-0.9332=0.0668.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the notation $N(20,16)$ the second parameter is the variance, so an individual observation has standard deviation $4$. The mean is normal because the observations are independent and normal. By the **sampling distribution of the mean for a normal sample**, its center is $20$ and variance $\dfrac{16}{9}$. The mean therefore has standard error $\dfrac43$, the standard deviation of an individual observation divided by $\sqrt9=3$.

Subtracting the center $20$ and dividing by the standard error changes the threshold $22$ to $1.5$, because dividing $2$ by $\dfrac43$ is equivalent to multiplying by $\dfrac34$. The event $\bar X_9>22$ is the event $Z>1.5$, where $Z$ is the standardized mean with distribution $N(0,1)$. Since $\Phi$ gives left-tail areas, take the complement for the required tail. The mean exceeds $22$ with probability **approximately $0.0668$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A concentration has distribution $N(72,36)$ and is measured in $\mathrm{mg/L}$. Draw $16$ independent observations. Find the cutoffs symmetric about $72\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ that enclose the central $90\,\%$ of the distribution of $\bar X_{16}$. Use $z_{0.95}\approx1.645$ and round to two decimal places.

$$
\begin{aligned}
SE(\bar X_{16})&=\frac{\sqrt{36}}{\sqrt{16}}\,\mathrm{mg/L}=\frac{6}{4}\,\mathrm{mg/L}
=1.5\,\mathrm{mg/L},\\[6pt]
P(a<\bar X_{16}<b)=0.90
&\iff P\!\left(-z_{0.95}<Z<z_{0.95}\right)=0.90,\\[6pt]
z_{0.95}\cdot SE&\approx1.645\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{mg/L}=2.4675\,\mathrm{mg/L},\\[6pt]
a&=(72-2.4675)\,\mathrm{mg/L}
\approx69.53\,\mathrm{mg/L},\\[6pt]
b&=(72+2.4675)\,\mathrm{mg/L}
\approx74.47\,\mathrm{mg/L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean of independent normal observations has distribution $N\!\left(72,\dfrac{36}{16}\right)$ and standard error $\dfrac64=1.5\,\mathrm{mg/L}$. Symmetry about $72\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ divides the $10\,\%$ outside the interval into equal tails. Each has probability $0.05$.

The upper cutoff therefore corresponds to the normal quantile with $95\,\%$ to its left. The lower quantile is its negative, $-1.645$, because $\Phi(-z)=1-\Phi(z)$. To return to the sample-mean scale, multiply the quantiles by the standard error to obtain $2.4675\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and add $72\,\mathrm{mg/L}$. The means of $16$ observations thus fall **between $69.53\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and $74.47\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ with probability $0.90$**.

</div>
