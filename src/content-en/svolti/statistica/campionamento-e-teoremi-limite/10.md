## Exercise 1

A population of $51$ units has variance $\sigma_N^2=50$ with divisor $N$. Draw $17$ units by simple random sampling without replacement. Calculate the variance of the sample mean.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sigma_N^2}{n}&=\frac{50}{17}\approx2.94,\\[6pt]
\frac{N-n}{N-1}&=\frac{51-17}{51-1}
=\frac{34}{50}=0.68,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_{17})
&=\frac{50}{17}\cdot\frac{34}{50}=\frac{34}{17}=2,\\[6pt]
SE(\bar X_{17})&=\sqrt2\approx1.41.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The draws are dependent because a selected unit cannot be drawn again. Apply the **finite population correction** for simple random sampling without replacement, using the population variance with divisor $N$. With independent draws the variance of the mean would be $\dfrac{50}{17}$, approximately $2.94$.

The variance correction is the ratio of the $N-n=34$ units outside the sample to $N-1=50$, namely $0.68$. Apply it to the variance of the mean under independent draws. The factor $50$ cancels, leaving $\dfrac{34}{17}$. The sample mean has **variance $2$** and standard error $\sqrt2$, approximately $1.41$. The sample contains one-third of the population, and its variance is $32\,\%$ lower than it would be with replacement.

</div>

## Exercise 2

From a population with four values $1$, $3$, $5$, and $7$, draw a simple random sample of two units without replacement. Find the standard error of the mean and verify its variance by enumerating samples.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_N&=\frac{1+3+5+7}{4}=4,\\[6pt]
\sigma_N^2&=\frac{(1-4)^2+(3-4)^2+(5-4)^2+(7-4)^2}{4}=\frac{9+1+1+9}{4}=5,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_2)
&=\frac{\sigma_N^2}{n}\frac{N-n}{N-1}=\frac52\cdot\frac23=\frac53,\\[6pt]
\{1,3\}\to2,\ \{1,5\}\to3,\ \{1,7\}&\to4,\ \{3,5\}\to4,\ \{3,7\}\to5,\ \{5,7\}\to6,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_2)&=\frac{(2-4)^2+(3-4)^2+2(4-4)^2+(5-4)^2+(6-4)^2}{6}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{4+1+0+1+4}{6}=\frac{10}{6}=\frac53,\\[6pt]
SE(\bar X_2)&=\sqrt{\frac53}\approx1.29.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The population mean is $4$ and the variance with divisor $N$ is $5$. With $N=4$ and $n=2$ the correction factor is $\dfrac{2}{3}$, so the finite population formula gives $\dfrac53$. There are $\binom42=6$ unordered samples and simple random sampling assigns each the same probability $\dfrac16$.

Their means are $2$, $3$, $4$, $4$, $5$, and $6$, with $4$ arising from two different pairs. Their average is $4$, equal to $\mu_N$ as the theorem predicts. Averaging the squared deviations from $4$, including both samples with zero deviation, gives $\dfrac{10}{6}=\dfrac53$. The variance agrees with the finite population correction, and its square root gives the standard error **$\sqrt{\dfrac53}\approx1.29$**.

</div>
