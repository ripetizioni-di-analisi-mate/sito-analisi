## Exercise 1

A service call has nonnegative cost with mean $18\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Use Markov's inequality to bound the probability that the cost reaches $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y&\ge0,\qquad E[Y]=18,\qquad a=60>0,\\[6pt]
P(Y\ge60)
&\le\frac{E[Y]}{60}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{18}{60}=\frac{3}{10}=0.30,\\[6pt]
P(Y<60)&=1-P(Y\ge60)\ge1-0.30=0.70.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&P(Y=0)=0.70,\quad P(Y=60)=0.30,\\[6pt]
&E[Y]=0\cdot0.70+60\cdot0.30=18,\qquad P(Y\ge60)=0.30.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cost $Y$ is nonnegative, measured in euros, and has finite expectation. The threshold $60$ is positive, so **Markov's inequality** applies. Dividing the expectation by the threshold, both in euros, gives a dimensionless upper bound. The probability that the cost reaches $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is **at most $0.30$**. Taking the complement, the cost stays below $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ with probability at least $0.70$.

The actual cost distribution could give a probability below this bound. However, $0.30$ is the best bound available from the mean alone. A cost of $0$ with probability $0.70$ and $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ with probability $0.30$ has mean $18\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and for this distribution $P(Y\ge60)$ is exactly $0.30$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Daily requests fall into four categories with means $3$, $2$, $1$, and $4$. The categories may be dependent. Use Markov's inequality to find the smallest integer $a$ for which it guarantees $P(Y\ge a)\le0.2$, where $Y$ is the total.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y&=Y_1+Y_2+Y_3+Y_4\ge0,\\[6pt]
E[Y]&=E[Y_1]+E[Y_2]+E[Y_3]+E[Y_4]\\[6pt]
&=3+2+1+4=10,\\[6pt]
P(Y\ge a)&\le\frac{10}{a}\qquad(a>0),\\[6pt]
\frac{10}{a}\le0.2
&\iff a\ge\frac{10}{0.2}=50,\\[6pt]
a=49&\implies\frac{10}{49}\approx0.204>0.2,\\[6pt]
a_{\min}&=50.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total $Y$ is the sum of the four counts $Y_1,\ldots,Y_4$. Linearity of expectation allows their four means to be added even when the counts are dependent. The total therefore has mean $10$. Each count and hence their total is nonnegative, so **Markov's inequality** bounds its tail probability by the mean divided by the threshold.

Find the first integer for which this ratio is at most $0.2$. Since $a$ is positive, $\dfrac{10}{a}\le0.2$ is equivalent to $10\le0.2\,a$, or $a\ge50$. At $a=49$ the bound is approximately $0.204$, exceeding $0.2$, so the smallest integer is **$a=50$**. Without the actual distribution of the total we cannot determine whether the same probability guarantee holds at smaller thresholds.

</div>
