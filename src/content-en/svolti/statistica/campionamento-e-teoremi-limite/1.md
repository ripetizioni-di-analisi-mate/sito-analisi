## Exercise 1

A machine produces parts whose diameters have standard deviation $6\,\mathrm{mm}$. The diameters of $16$ independently and identically distributed parts are measured. Calculate the variance and standard error of the sample mean.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X_i)&=6^2\,\mathrm{mm}^2=36\,\mathrm{mm}^2,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{16}X_i\Bigr)
&=16\cdot36\,\mathrm{mm}^2=576\,\mathrm{mm}^2,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_{16})
&=\frac{1}{16^2}\cdot576\,\mathrm{mm}^2
=\frac{576}{256}\,\mathrm{mm}^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac{6^2}{16}\,\mathrm{mm}^2
=2.25\,\mathrm{mm}^2,\\[6pt]
SE(\bar X_{16})
&=\sqrt{2.25}\,\mathrm{mm}
=1.5\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One diameter has variance equal to its squared standard deviation, $36\,\mathrm{mm}^2$. The sum has variance $16\cdot6^2\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ because the measurements are independent. Covariances between distinct parts are zero, leaving only the $16$ equal variances. The mean multiplies the sum by $\dfrac1{16}$. Multiplying a variable by a constant $c$ multiplies its variance by $c^2$, so divide the variance of the sum by $16^2=256$. This recovers the formula $\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}$ for the **sampling distribution of the mean**.

Taking the square root gives the standard deviation of sample means, in millimeters like the diameters. It measures how means of repeated samples vary about their expectation. The mean of $16$ parts therefore has **variance $2.25\,\mathrm{mm}^2$ and standard error $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$**. The standard error is one-quarter of the standard deviation of an individual part because $\sqrt{16}=4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A measurement method has standard deviation $11\,\mathrm{mg}$. The measurements are i.i.d. Find the smallest sample size for which the standard error of the mean is at most $2\,\mathrm{mg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
SE(\bar X_n)&=\frac{11}{\sqrt n}\,\mathrm{mg},\\[6pt]
SE(\bar X_n)\le2\,\mathrm{mg}
&\iff\frac{11}{\sqrt n}\le2\\[6pt]
&\iff\sqrt n\ge\frac{11}{2}=5.5\\[6pt]
&\iff n\ge\left(\frac{11}{2}\right)^2
=30.25,\\[6pt]
SE(\bar X_{30})&=\frac{11}{\sqrt{30}}\,\mathrm{mg}\approx2.008\,\mathrm{mg},\\[6pt]
SE(\bar X_{31})&=\frac{11}{\sqrt{31}}\,\mathrm{mg}\approx1.976\,\mathrm{mg},\\[6pt]
n_{\min}&=31.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For i.i.d. measurements the standard error of the mean is $\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}$ with $\sigma=11\,\mathrm{mg}$. To find the number of measurements, start with the standard error requirement and isolate $\sqrt n$ by multiplying both sides by the positive quantity $\dfrac{\sqrt n}{2}$. Both sides of $\sqrt n\ge5.5$ are positive, so squaring preserves the inequality.

Since $n$ counts observations, take the first integer reaching the threshold $30.25$, namely $31$. With $30$ measurements the standard error is approximately $2.008\,\mathrm{mg}$, still above $2\,\mathrm{mg}$, while with $31$ measurements it falls to about $1.976\,\mathrm{mg}$. The minimum sample size is **$n=31$**.

</div>
