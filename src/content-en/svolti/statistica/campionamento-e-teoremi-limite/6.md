## Exercise 1

Requests are closed the same day with probability $0.3$ independently of one another. Find the probability limit of the proportion closed and, for $n=700$, an upper bound on the probability of a deviation of at least $0.1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_i&\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.3),\qquad
E[X_i]=0.3,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=0.3\cdot0.7=0.21,\\[6pt]
\hat p_n&=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nX_i
\xrightarrow{P}0.3,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Var}(\hat p_{700})
&=\frac{0.21}{700}=0.0003,\\[6pt]
P(|\hat p_{700}-0.3|\ge0.1)
&\le\frac{0.0003}{0.1^2}=\frac{0.0003}{0.01}
=0.03,\\[6pt]
P(|\hat p_{700}-0.3|<0.1)&\ge1-0.03=0.97.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Represent each request by an indicator $X_i$ equal to $1$ if it closes that day and $0$ otherwise. The indicators are independent Bernoulli variables with common expectation $0.3$ and finite variance $p(1-p)=0.21$. Their mean is the relative frequency, so the **weak law of large numbers** gives convergence in probability to their expectation $0.3$.

For the fixed sample size, Chebyshev's inequality gives a numerical guarantee from the variance of the mean and the tolerance. The variance of the relative frequency is the variance of one indicator divided by $700$, namely $0.0003$. Dividing by the squared tolerance $0.01$ gives the upper bound. The probability of a deviation of at least $0.1$ is **at most $0.03$**, and the fraction closed lies between $0.2$ and $0.4$ with probability at least $0.97$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $Y$ be a variable with $P(Y=1)=P(Y=0)=\dfrac12$. Observe $X_i=Y$ for every $i$. Calculate $P(|\bar X_n-\dfrac12|>\dfrac14)$ and determine whether $\bar X_n$ converges in probability to $\dfrac12$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X_i]&=E[Y]=\frac12,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\frac14\quad(i\ne j),\\[6pt]
\bar X_n&=\frac{nY}{n}=Y,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_n)=\frac14\quad\text{for every }n,\\[6pt]
\left|Y-\frac12\right|&=\frac12
\qquad\text{for both values of }Y,\\[6pt]
P\left(\left|\bar X_n-\frac12\right|>\frac14\right)
&=P\left(\frac12>\frac14\right)=1\qquad\text{for every }n,\\[6pt]
\lim_{n\to\infty}P\left(\left|\bar X_n-\frac12\right|>\frac14\right)&=1\ne0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All observations have the same distribution with expectation $\dfrac12$, but they are dependent. Each repeats the same variable, so the covariance between distinct observations is the variance of $Y$, namely $\dfrac14$. The mean of $n$ copies of $Y$ is again $Y$, with variance $\dfrac14$ for every $n$.

If $Y=0$, the deviation from the center is $-\dfrac12$, and if $Y=1$ it is $\dfrac12$. In both cases the absolute value $\dfrac12$ exceeds tolerance $\dfrac14$, so the event has probability $1$. This probability tends to $1$, different from $0$. Thus **$\bar X_n$ does not converge in probability to $\dfrac12$**. The weak law for independent observations does not apply because independence fails.

</div>
