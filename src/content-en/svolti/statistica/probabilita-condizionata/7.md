## Exercise 1

An urn contains four white balls and three black balls. Two balls are drawn without replacement. Calculate the probability that the first is white and the second black, and the probability that the two balls have different colors.

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.8 xmax=12 ymin=-0.6 ymax=6.5 linee="0,3 4,4.7;0,3 4,1.3;4,4.7 8,5.9;4,4.7 8,3.8;4,1.3 8,2.1;4,1.3 8,0.1" punti="0,3,pieno;4,4.7,pieno;4,1.3,pieno;8,5.9,pieno;8,3.8,pieno;8,2.1,pieno;8,0.1,pieno" etichette="1.45,4.08,⁴⁄₇;1.45,1.62,³⁄₇;5.55,5.68,½;5.55,3.65,½;5.55,2.33,⅔;5.55,0.05,⅓;8.2,5.9,white / white;8.2,3.8,white / black;8.2,2.1,black / white;8.2,0.1,black / black" titolo="Tree for two draws without replacement from an urn holding four white and three black balls. The middle leaves correspond to draws of different colors."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(B_1\cap N_2)&=\frac{4}{7}\cdot\frac{3}{6}=\frac{12}{42}=\frac{2}{7},\\[6pt]
P(N_1\cap B_2)&=\frac{3}{7}\cdot\frac{4}{6}=\frac{12}{42}=\frac{2}{7},\\[6pt]
P(\text{different colors})&=\frac{2}{7}+\frac{2}{7}=\frac{4}{7}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(B_1\cap B_2)&=\frac{4}{7}\cdot\frac{3}{6}=\frac{2}{7},\\[6pt]
P(N_1\cap N_2)&=\frac{3}{7}\cdot\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{7},\\[6pt]
P(\text{different colors})&=1-\frac{2}{7}-\frac{1}{7}=\frac{4}{7}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Events $B_i$ and $N_i$ denote white and black, respectively, on draw $i$. All seven balls are equally likely on the first draw, so $P(B_1)=\dfrac47$ and $P(N_1)=\dfrac37$. After a white first draw, six balls remain, three of them black. In the reverse order, a black first draw leaves six balls, four of them white. By the **multiplication rule**, the probability of either order is the probability of the first draw times the conditional probability of the second given the first. The orders are mutually exclusive because their first colors differ, so add the probabilities. The products are equal because the numerators $4$ and $3$ exchange places while the denominators remain $7$ and $6$.

A second calculation uses the two leaves with matching colors. The four leaves are disjoint and their union is the whole sample space. The probability of different colors is therefore one minus the combined probability of the “white, white” and “black, black” leaves. Both methods give the same result.

The probability that the two balls have different colors is therefore **$\dfrac{4}{7}$**, and the probability of white followed by black is **$\dfrac{2}{7}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A box contains three red cards, two yellow cards and one green card. Three cards are drawn without replacement. Given that the second card is yellow, calculate the probability that all three colors appear.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R_1\cap G_2\cap V_3)&=\frac{3}{6}\cdot\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{4}
=\frac{1}{20},\\[6pt]
P(V_1\cap G_2\cap R_3)&=\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{3}{4}
=\frac{1}{20},\\[6pt]
P(G_2)&=P(G_1)P(G_2\mid G_1)+P(G_1^c)P(G_2\mid G_1^c)\\[6pt]
&=\frac{2}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{5}+\frac{4}{6}\cdot\frac{2}{5}
=\frac{2}{30}+\frac{8}{30}=\frac{1}{3},\\[6pt]
P(\text{three colors}\mid G_2)&=\frac{\tfrac{1}{20}+\tfrac{1}{20}}{\tfrac{1}{3}}
=\frac{1}{10}\cdot3=\frac{3}{10}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $R_i$, $G_i$ and $V_i$ denote red, yellow and green in position $i$, respectively. If the second card is yellow, the first and third must be red and green in either order for all three colors to appear. The two sequences are disjoint, and the **multiplication rule** for three events gives $\dfrac{1}{20}$ for each. For example, in the first sequence a red first card leaves five cards, two yellow. A red card followed by a yellow card leaves four cards, one green.

Calculate the denominator $P(G_2)$ using the **law of total probability** with the partition $G_1$, $G_1^c$. If the first card is yellow, one of the five remaining cards is yellow. Otherwise, two of the five are yellow. The two contributions sum to $\dfrac{10}{30}=\dfrac13$, the initial proportion of yellow cards. This follows because sampling without replacement is uniform over ordered triples of distinct cards, so each card is equally likely to be second.

The numerator sums the two sequences compatible with the condition. Given that the second card is yellow, all three colors therefore appear with probability **$\dfrac{3}{10}$**.

</div>
