## Exercise 1

A center receives $70\%$ of its applications online and the rest at the counter. Additional information is required for $10\%$ of online applications and $30\%$ of applications received at the counter. Calculate the probability that an application requires additional information.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(W)&=0.70,\qquad P(W^c)=1-0.70=0.30,\\[6pt]
P(I\mid W)&=0.10,\qquad P(I\mid W^c)=0.30,\\[6pt]
P(I)&=P(I\mid W)P(W)+P(I\mid W^c)P(W^c)\\[6pt]
&=0.10\cdot0.70+0.30\cdot0.30\\[6pt]
&=0.07+0.09=0.16.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $I$ denote “additional information required” and $W$ denote online submission. Applications not submitted online arrive at the counter, so the counter's share is the complement $1-0.70=0.30$. The two rates are measured within the corresponding submission channels, so they are the conditional probabilities $P(I\mid W)$ and $P(I\mid W^c)$.

Each application belongs to exactly one channel, and both channels have positive probability. They therefore form a partition and the **law of total probability** applies. The first product gives the proportion of all applications that were submitted online and require additional information. The second gives the corresponding proportion for the counter. The two sets are disjoint, so adding their contributions gives $16\%$. This is closer to the online rate because the online channel accounts for $70\%$ of applications and so has greater weight. Out of $100$ applications, we would expect $7$ online applications and $9$ counter applications to require additional information.

The probability that an application requires additional information is therefore **$0.16$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three warehouses prepare $50\%$, $30\%$ and $20\%$ of all shipments, respectively. The overall delay rate is $7.5\%$. The rate is $5\%$ for the first warehouse and $10\%$ for the second. Calculate the delay rate for the third warehouse.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R)&=P(R\mid B_1)P(B_1)+P(R\mid B_2)P(B_2)+P(R\mid B_3)P(B_3),\\[6pt]
0.075&=0.50\cdot0.05+0.30\cdot0.10+0.20p,\\[6pt]
0.075&=0.025+0.030+0.20p,\\[6pt]
0.20p&=0.075-0.025-0.030=0.020,\\[6pt]
p&=\frac{0.020}{0.20}=0.10.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $R$ denote a delay, $B_i$ denote shipment from warehouse $i$, and $p=P(R\mid B_3)$ denote the conditional probability of a delay given shipment from the third warehouse. The shares $0.50$, $0.30$ and $0.20$ are positive and sum to $1$. Every shipment comes from exactly one warehouse, so the three events $B_i$ form a partition. The **law of total probability** expresses the overall rate as the sum of three weighted contributions.

The first contribution is $0.50\cdot0.05=0.025$ and the second is $0.30\cdot0.10=0.030$. The unknown appears in only one term, so the equation is linear in $p$. Subtracting both known contributions leaves $0.020$, the proportion of all shipments that come from the third warehouse and are late. Since the third warehouse prepares only $20\%$ of all shipments, divide that proportion by $0.20$ to obtain the rate within the warehouse. Indeed, $10\%$ of a group accounting for $20\%$ of shipments is $2\%$ of all shipments.

The delay rate for the third warehouse is therefore **$10\%$**.

</div>
