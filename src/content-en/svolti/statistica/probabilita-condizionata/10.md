## Exercise 1

A measurement chain requires the sensor, transmitter and recorder all to work. Their operating probabilities are $0.9$, $0.8$ and $0.95$, respectively. The operating events are mutually independent. Calculate the reliability of the chain and the probability that it stops.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(F_1\cap F_2\cap F_3)&=0.9\cdot0.8\cdot0.95\\[6pt]
&=0.72\cdot0.95=0.684,\\[6pt]
P(\text{stopping})&=1-0.684=0.316.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $F_1$, $F_2$ and $F_3$ denote operation of the sensor, transmitter and recorder, respectively. The chain operates on the intersection of the three operating events. By **mutual independence**, calculate the probability of the intersection by multiplying the three individual probabilities. The first two factors give $0.72$, and multiplication by $0.95$ gives $0.684$. The chain stops when at least one component fails, the complement of the intersection just calculated. Subtract the reliability from one to find the probability of a stop. The reliability $0.684$ is below each individual reliability because whenever the chain works, every individual component must also work.

The probability that all three components fail is $0.1\cdot0.2\cdot0.05=0.001$. This event is contained in the event that the chain stops, which also includes cases with one or two failures. Its probability is therefore much smaller than $0.316$.

The reliability of the chain is therefore **$0.684$**, and the probability that it stops is **$0.316$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A system requires device $A$ and at least one of devices $B$ and $C$ to work. The three operating events are mutually independent with probabilities $0.8$, $0.7$ and $0.6$. Calculate the probability that the system operates.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A\cap B)&=0.8\cdot0.7=0.56,\\[6pt]
P(A\cap C)&=0.8\cdot0.6=0.48,\\[6pt]
P(A\cap B\cap C)&=0.8\cdot0.7\cdot0.6=0.336,\\[6pt]
P\bigl(A\cap(B\cup C)\bigr)&=0.56+0.48-0.336=0.704.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(B\cup C)&=1-(1-0.7)(1-0.6)=1-0.12=0.88,\\[6pt]
P\bigl(A\cap(B\cup C)\bigr)&=P(A)P(B\cup C)=0.8\cdot0.88=0.704.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use $A$, $B$ and $C$ also to denote the operating events for the devices with those names. The system works if $A$ and $B$ work or if $A$ and $C$ work. Both cases include outcomes in which all three work. By the **inclusion–exclusion formula**, add the two probabilities and subtract the common probability, which would otherwise be counted twice. Mutual independence gives both pairwise products and the triple product.

A second calculation follows the diagram. The parallel pair stops only if $B$ and $C$ both fail, an event of probability $0.3\cdot0.4=0.12$, so $P(B\cup C)=0.88$. Event $B\cup C$ depends only on devices $B$ and $C$. By mutual independence it is therefore independent of $A$, and the probability of the intersection is the product $0.8\cdot0.88$. Both methods give the same value.

The result is below $0.8$ because operation of $A$ is necessary. The probability that the system works is therefore **$0.704$**.

</div>
