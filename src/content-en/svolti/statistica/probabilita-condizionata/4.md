## Exercise 1

Two workshops supply $60\%$ and $40\%$ of all parts, respectively. Of the first workshop's parts, $5\%$ are rejected, as are $10\%$ of the second workshop's parts. Given that a part was rejected, calculate the probability that it came from the second workshop.

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.45 xmax=12 ymin=-0.5 ymax=6.55 linee="0,3 3.7,4.7;0,3 3.7,1.3;3.7,4.7 8,5.8;3.7,4.7 8,4.1;3.7,1.3 8,2.1;3.7,1.3 8,0.2" punti="0,3;3.7,4.7;3.7,1.3;8,5.8;8,4.1;8,2.1;8,0.2" etichette="1.1,4.1,60%;1.1,1.72,40%;5.4,5.57,5%;5.5,3.82,95%;5.4,1.99,10%;5.45,0.23,90%;8.28,5.76,rejected A;8.28,4.06,accepted A;8.28,2.06,rejected B;8.28,0.16,accepted B" titolo="Separate paths show the source and the outcome. The two branches ending in a rejected item give the denominator in Bayes’ formula."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(S\cap A)&=0.60\cdot0.05=0.03,\\[6pt]
P(S\cap B)&=0.40\cdot0.10=0.04,\\[6pt]
P(S)&=0.03+0.04=0.07,\\[6pt]
P(B\mid S)&=\frac{P(S\cap B)}{P(S)}=\frac{0.04}{0.07}=\frac{4}{7},\\[6pt]
P(A\mid S)&=1-\frac{4}{7}=\frac{3}{7}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $S$ denote rejection, $A$ supply by the first workshop and $B$ supply by the second. Each part comes from exactly one workshop, so $A$ and $B$ form a partition with positive probabilities. The rates $5\%$ and $10\%$ are measured within the individual workshops, so they are $P(S\mid A)$ and $P(S\mid B)$.

By the **multiplication rule**, each rejection rate times the workshop's production share gives the joint probability for the corresponding path in the tree. By the law of total probability, the two paths ending in rejection sum to $P(S)=0.07$. Since $P(S)>0$, **Bayes' formula** applies. We are given that the part was rejected, so the denominator includes rejections from both workshops and the numerator includes only those from the second.

The second workshop supplies $40\%$ of the parts, but its rejection rate exceeds the overall rate of $7\%$. Its share among rejected parts therefore rises to $\dfrac{4}{7}$. The two posterior probabilities have the same denominator and sum to $1$, so the first workshop has posterior probability $\dfrac37$.

A rejected part therefore comes from the second workshop with probability **$\dfrac{4}{7}$**, approximately $57\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three carriers handle shares $\dfrac{1}{2}$, $\dfrac{3}{10}$ and $\dfrac{1}{5}$ of all deliveries. Their on-time probabilities are $\dfrac{9}{10}$, $\dfrac{4}{5}$ and $\dfrac{3}{4}$, respectively. Given that a delivery was late, calculate the probability that it was handled by the first or third carrier.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R\mid B_1)&=1-\frac{9}{10}=\frac{1}{10},\\[6pt]
P(R\mid B_2)&=1-\frac{4}{5}=\frac{1}{5},\\[6pt]
P(R\mid B_3)&=1-\frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R\cap B_1)&=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{10}=\frac{1}{20},\\[6pt]
P(R\cap B_2)&=\frac{3}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{5}=\frac{3}{50},\\[6pt]
P(R\cap B_3)&=\frac{1}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{20},\\[6pt]
P(R)&=\frac{5}{100}+\frac{6}{100}+\frac{5}{100}=\frac{16}{100}=\frac{4}{25},\\[6pt]
P(B_1\cup B_3\mid R)&=\frac{\tfrac{1}{20}+\tfrac{1}{20}}{\tfrac{4}{25}}
=\frac{1}{10}\cdot\frac{25}{4}=\frac{5}{8}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $R$ denote a late delivery and $B_i$ assignment to carrier $i$. The shares $\dfrac12$, $\dfrac{3}{10}$ and $\dfrac15$ are positive and sum to $1$, so the three events $B_i$ form a partition. Within each carrier, being late is the complement of being on time, so subtract the on-time probability from one to find the delay rate.

By the **multiplication rule**, a carrier's delay rate times its share of deliveries gives the probability of a late delivery handled by that carrier. With common denominator $100$, the three contributions are $5$, $6$ and $5$ hundredths. By the law of total probability they sum to $P(R)=\dfrac{4}{25}$.

Event $B_1\cup B_3$ is the union of two disjoint groups, so **Bayes' formula** applies to each and we add the two posterior probabilities. The denominator sums all three contributions, and the numerator sums the first and third. The numerator contributions are equal because the first carrier handles more deliveries but has a lower delay rate. The second carrier has the complementary posterior probability, $1-\dfrac58=\dfrac38$.

The probability that a late delivery was handled by the first or third carrier is therefore **$\dfrac{5}{8}$**.

</div>
