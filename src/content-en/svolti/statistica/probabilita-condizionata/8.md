## Exercise 1

A hypothetical batch of $1000$ parts contains $40$ defective parts. An inspection flags $36$ defective parts and $48$ nondefective parts. Calculate sensitivity, specificity and positive predictive value.

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.15 xmax=8.8 ymin=-0.2 ymax=4.15 poligoni="2,0.25 4.85,0.25 4.85,2.6 2,2.6" linee="2,0.25 7.7,0.25 7.7,2.6 2,2.6 2,0.25;4.85,0.25 4.85,2.6;2,1.425 7.7,1.425" etichette="2.72,3,Flagged;5.3,3,Not flagged;0.08,1.98,Defective;0.08,0.81,Conforming;3.28,1.98,36;6.13,1.98,4;3.28,0.81,48;6.03,0.81,912" titolo="Inspection counts for 1,000 items. The highlighted column contains the 36 true positives and the 48 false positives."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
|D^c|&=1000-40=960,\\[6pt]
|D\cap T^c|&=40-36=4,\\[6pt]
|D^c\cap T^c|&=960-48=912,\\[6pt]
|T|&=36+48=84,\\[6pt]
P(T\mid D)&=\frac{36}{40}=\frac{9}{10},\\[6pt]
P(T^c\mid D^c)&=\frac{912}{960}=\frac{19}{20},\\[6pt]
P(D\mid T)&=\frac{36}{84}=\frac{3}{7}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $D$ denote a defect and $T$ a positive inspection result. The batch is described by counts, so each conditional probability is the number of favorable cases divided by the number of parts in the reference group. Find the missing table entries by subtraction. There are $1000-40=960$ nondefective parts, $40-36=4$ unflagged defective parts and $36+48=84$ flagged parts.

Sensitivity is calculated among the $40$ defective parts and counts the $36$ that were flagged. Specificity is calculated among the $960$ nondefective parts. Subtract the $48$ flagged parts to obtain $912$ true negatives. The fraction $\dfrac{912}{960}$ reduces to $\dfrac{19}{20}$ on division by $48$. Positive predictive value uses all $84$ flagged parts as its denominator. The fraction $\dfrac{36}{84}$ reduces on division by $12$.

Because nondefective parts greatly outnumber defective parts, false positives outnumber true positives even though specificity is $95\%$. The false positive rate is only $\dfrac{48}{960}=\dfrac{1}{20}$, but it applies to $960$ parts.

Sensitivity is therefore **$\dfrac{9}{10}$**, specificity is **$\dfrac{19}{20}$**, and positive predictive value is **$\dfrac{3}{7}$**, approximately $43\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A hypothetical inspection has sensitivity $0.8$ and specificity $0.9$. It is applied to a production run with defective fraction $0.1$. Among unflagged parts, calculate the probability that a part is defective and the probability that it is nondefective.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(D)&=0.1,\\[6pt]
P(D^c)&=1-0.1=0.9,\\[6pt]
P(T^c\mid D)&=1-0.8=0.2,\\[6pt]
P(T^c\mid D^c)&=0.9,\\[6pt]
P(D\cap T^c)&=0.1\cdot0.2=0.02,\\[6pt]
P(D^c\cap T^c)&=0.9\cdot0.9=0.81,\\[6pt]
P(T^c)&=0.02+0.81=0.83,\\[6pt]
P(D\mid T^c)&=\frac{0.02}{0.83}=\frac{2}{83},\\[6pt]
P(D^c\mid T^c)&=\frac{0.81}{0.83}=\frac{81}{83}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $D$ denote a defect and $T$ a positive inspection result. Sensitivity is $P(T\mid D)=0.8$ and specificity is $P(T^c\mid D^c)=0.9$. Among defective parts, an unflagged result is the complement of a flagged result, so the false negative probability is $1-0.8=0.2$. By the **multiplication rule**, the proportion of all parts that are defective and unflagged is $0.1\cdot0.2=0.02$. Among nondefective parts the probability of an unflagged result is the specificity, so the proportion of all parts that are nondefective and unflagged is $0.9\cdot0.9=0.81$.

Add the two proportions because each part is either defective or nondefective. Their sum $P(T^c)=0.83$ is positive, so **Bayes' formula** applies with condition $T^c$. Both answers have denominator $0.83$, the unflagged proportion. Out of $1000$ parts, we would expect $830$ to be unflagged, including $20$ defective and $810$ nondefective parts.

The second answer is the negative predictive value and the first is its complement within the same group. Among unflagged parts, the probability of a defect is therefore **$\dfrac{2}{83}$**, approximately $2.4\%$, and the probability of no defect is **$\dfrac{81}{83}$**.

</div>
