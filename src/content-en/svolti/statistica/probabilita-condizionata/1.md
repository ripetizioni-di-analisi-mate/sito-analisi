## Exercise 1

Of $120$ students enrolled in a course, $45$ attend the evening section. The first assignment was submitted by $30$ evening students and $42$ daytime students. A student is chosen at random. Given that the student attends the evening section, calculate the probability that they submitted the assignment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(S)&=\frac{45}{120},\\[6pt]
P(C\cap S)&=\frac{30}{120},\\[6pt]
P(C\mid S)&=\frac{P(C\cap S)}{P(S)}
=\frac{\tfrac{30}{120}}{\tfrac{45}{120}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{30}{120}\cdot\frac{120}{45}
=\frac{30}{45}
=\frac{2}{3},\\[6pt]
P(C)&=\frac{30+42}{120}=\frac{72}{120}=\frac{3}{5}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $S$ be the event “the student attends the evening section” and $C$ the event “the student submitted the assignment.” Students are equally likely to be selected, so each probability is a count divided by $120$. Since $P(S)=\dfrac{45}{120}>0$, the definition of **conditional probability** applies.

Knowing the section restricts the reference group to the $45$ evening students. The $30$ who submitted the assignment belong to both $C$ and $S$, so they are the only favorable cases within that group. In the ratio of probabilities the total $120$ appears in both numerator and denominator and cancels, leaving $30$ divided by $45$. The greatest common divisor of $30$ and $45$ is $15$, so the fraction reduces to $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

The $42$ assignments submitted by daytime students enter the calculation only when finding the submission probability for the whole course, which is $\dfrac{3}{5}$. Since $\dfrac{2}{3}>\dfrac{3}{5}$, submission is more common among evening students than in the course as a whole.

The probability that an evening student submitted the assignment is therefore **$\dfrac{2}{3}$**, approximately $67\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Of $160$ support requests, $64$ are urgent and $40$ were reopened. Among the urgent requests, $24$ were reopened. For a randomly chosen request, calculate the probability that it was reopened given that it is urgent, and the probability that it is urgent given that it was reopened.

::venn{nomi="U,R" ambiente="Ω" zone="ab" elementi="a:40;ab:24;b:16;u:80" titolo="U denotes urgent requests and R denotes reopened requests. The same 24 cases belong to both events in each conditional probability."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
|U\cap R^c|&=64-24=40,\\[6pt]
|U^c\cap R|&=40-24=16,\\[6pt]
|U^c\cap R^c|&=160-40-24-16=80,\\[6pt]
P(R\mid U)&=\frac{P(R\cap U)}{P(U)}=\frac{24}{64}=\frac{3}{8},\\[6pt]
P(U\mid R)&=\frac{P(U\cap R)}{P(R)}=\frac{24}{40}=\frac{3}{5}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $U$ and $R$ denote the events “urgent” and “reopened,” and let $|\cdot|$ denote the number of requests in a group. Find the counts in the four regions by subtraction. From the $64$ urgent requests, subtract the $24$ reopened ones to find the urgent requests that were not reopened. From the $40$ reopened requests, subtract the same $24$ to find the nonurgent ones. The remaining $80$ requests are neither urgent nor reopened.

The requests are equally likely, so the total $160$ cancels in each ratio, leaving the counts. The first calculation conditions on urgency, so its denominator is $64$. Of the $64$ urgent requests, the $24$ that were also reopened are favorable. The second calculation conditions on reopening, giving a reference group of $40$ requests. The number meeting both conditions is still $24$. Because the second group is smaller while the numerator is the same, the second probability is larger. The event after the vertical bar tells us which event is given.

An urgent request is therefore reopened with probability **$\dfrac{3}{8}$**, and a reopened request is urgent with probability **$\dfrac{3}{5}$**.

</div>
