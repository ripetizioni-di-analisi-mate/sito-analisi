## Exercise 1: Internal energy balance

A system absorbs $80\,\mathrm J$ of heat and does $30\,\mathrm J$ of work on its surroundings. Using $\Delta U=q+w$, where $w$ is work on the system, what is $\Delta U$?

- $+50\,\mathrm J$
- $+110\,\mathrm J$
- $-50\,\mathrm J$
- $-110\,\mathrm J$
- $+30\,\mathrm J$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $+50\,\mathrm J$

Heat enters the system, so $q=+80\,\mathrm J$. Work is done by the system on the surroundings, so work on the system is $w=-30\,\mathrm J$. The balance gives $\Delta U=80-30=50\,\mathrm J$. The sign convention is explicit because changing the definition of $w$ changes the equation's form.

$$
\begin{aligned}q&=+80\,\mathrm J,\qquad w=-30\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]\Delta U&=q+w=80-30=50\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

The balance follows energy crossing the system boundary. Heat entering increases its energy, while energy transferred to the surroundings as work decreases it. The positive difference means part of the absorbed heat remains as increased internal energy. Giving both contributions a positive sign would count outgoing work as incoming energy. Choosing signs therefore precedes the numerical addition.

</div>

## Exercise 2: Free energy and temperature

For a change at constant temperature and pressure, $\Delta H<0$ and $\Delta S>0$. What is the sign of $\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$ for $T>0$, assuming the signs remain unchanged?

- Negative
- Positive
- Always zero
- Negative only if $T=0$
- Determined by reaction rate

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** Negative

The enthalpy term is negative. Since absolute temperature and reaction entropy are positive, $-T\Delta S$ is also negative. Their sum is therefore negative. The change is thermodynamically favored in the direction considered, but this does not establish its rate: a kinetic barrier can make it slow.

$$
\Delta H<0,\quad -T\Delta S<0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\Delta G<0.
$$

The problem supplies signs only, so the conclusion follows without assigning arbitrary numerical values. At positive absolute temperature, $T$ times the entropy change is positive; the preceding minus sign makes it negative. The enthalpy change is also negative, so both contributions agree. This criterion describes thermodynamic tendency at constant temperature and pressure. Transformation time also depends on the activation barrier and is not determined by this result alone.

</div>

## Exercise 3: Reaction quotient and equilibrium constant

For $\mathrm{A\rightleftharpoons2B}$, $K_c=4$. In a mixture, concentrations expressed in the units adopted for $K_c$ are $[A]=1$ and $[B]=1$. Which direction is initially favored?

- Toward B, because $Q_c=1<K_c$
- Toward A, because $Q_c=1<K_c$
- Neither, because $[A]=[B]$
- Toward A, because $Q_c=4$
- Neither, because $K_c$ is positive

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** Toward B, because $Q_c=1<K_c$

The quotient follows the reaction coefficients: $Q_c=\dfrac{[B]^2}{[A]}=\dfrac{1^2}{1}=1$. It is below the equilibrium value four, so the net reaction produces B and consumes A until equilibrium is reached. Equal initial concentrations do not imply equal forward and reverse rates.

$$
Q_c=\frac{[B]^2}{[A]}=\frac{1^2}{1}=1<4=K_c.
$$

First construct the quotient from the balanced reaction: B's coefficient two becomes an exponent. Then insert current concentrations, which are not stated to be equilibrium values. Since the ratio is too small relative to $K$, the net process increases the numerator and decreases the denominator, producing B and consuming A. In the new state, concentrations need not become equal: they must make the quotient equal the constant at the specified temperature.

</div>
