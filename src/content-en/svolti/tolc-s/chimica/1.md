## Exercise 1: Neutrons in an isotope

A neutral atom has atomic number $8$ and mass number $18$. How many neutrons does it contain?

- $10$
- $8$
- $18$
- $26$
- $16$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $10$

The mass number includes protons and neutrons, whereas the atomic number counts only protons. Therefore $N=A-Z=18-8=10$. Neutrality also establishes that there are eight electrons, but these do not enter the mass number. Eighteen is the total number of nucleons, not just neutrons.

$$
N=A-Z=18-8=10,\qquad n_e=Z=8.
$$

The value identifying oxygen is eight, its proton number. Eighteen instead describes the total number of nucleons in this isotope. Reconstructing the nucleus requires subtracting the already counted protons from the total. Electrons can be determined separately using neutrality, but play a different role in the problem. The final check reconstructs mass number: eight protons plus ten neutrons give eighteen nucleons.

</div>

## Exercise 2: The shape of ammonia

In the Lewis structure of $\mathrm{NH_3}$, nitrogen has three bonds and one lone pair. What is the geometry of the nuclei according to VSEPR?

- Trigonal pyramidal
- Trigonal planar
- Linear
- Square planar
- Octahedral

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** Trigonal pyramidal

The four electron regions adopt an approximately tetrahedral arrangement. One region is a lone pair, however, so only three positions contain hydrogen nuclei. The nuclear geometry is trigonal pyramidal. Confusing electron geometry with molecular geometry would treat a position without a nucleus as a molecular vertex.

$$
N_{\mathrm{regions}}=3+1=4,\qquad N_{\mathrm{outer\ nuclei}}=3.
$$

The initial count includes both bonds and the lone pair. Four regions orient the electron distribution toward a tetrahedral arrangement. The lone pair, however, does not correspond to a fourth hydrogen. Molecular shape considers only nuclear positions: the three hydrogens form a base and nitrogen occupies the pyramid apex. Distinguishing the two counts resolves the apparent contrast between tetrahedron and pyramid.

</div>

## Exercise 3: Hybridization in ethyne

In ethyne, $\mathrm{HC\equiv CH}$, each carbon has two bonding regions and linear geometry. Which hybridization describes this model?

- $sp$
- $sp^2$
- $sp^3$
- $sp^3d$
- $sp^3d^2$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $sp$

The two bonding regions correspond, in the hybridization model, to two linearly oriented $sp$ orbitals. The triple bond counts as one region for geometry despite containing several bonding components. The $sp^2$ and $sp^3$ configurations describe three and four regions, respectively, with different geometries.

$$
N_{\mathrm{regions}}=2\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad sp,\quad180^\circ.
$$

Around each carbon, distinguish the bond toward hydrogen and that toward the other carbon. The triple bond involves several electron pairs but remains one bonding direction in the geometric count. The two directions are opposite, so the model is linear and uses two sp hybrid orbitals. The other two unhybridized p orbitals participate in the triple bond's additional components without introducing new neighboring nuclei.

</div>
