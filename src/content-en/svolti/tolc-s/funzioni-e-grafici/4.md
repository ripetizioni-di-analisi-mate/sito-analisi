## Exercise 1

For $f(x)=x^2$ and $g(x)=x+1$, find both compositions and the inputs where they agree.

$$
(f\circ g)(x)=(x+1)^2,\qquad(g\circ f)(x)=x^2+1.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+1)^2&=x^2+1,\\[6pt]
x^2+2x+1&=x^2+1,\\[6pt]
2x&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In $f\circ g$, first add $1$ and then square the result. In $g\circ f$, first square and then add $1$. Writing the formulas makes this difference in order explicit.

To find the inputs where the compositions give the same result, equate their formulas. Expanding the square produces the term $2x$, which occurs only in the first composition. Equality therefore holds only at zero, where both equal $1$. This shared point does not make the functions identical. For example, at $x=1$ their values are respectively $4$ and $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $f(u)=\ln u$ and $g(x)=4-x^2$, find the formula, domain and zeros of $f\circ g$.

$$
(f\circ g)(x)=\ln(4-x^2).
$$
$$
4-x^2>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2<4
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -2<x<2.
$$
$$
\ln(4-x^2)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad4-x^2=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=\pm\sqrt3.
$$
$$
D=(-2,2),\qquad Z=\{-\sqrt3,\sqrt3\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inner function $g$ is a polynomial, so it is defined for every real number. The outer logarithm, however, accepts only positive outputs. The composition's domain is therefore the set of inputs that $g$ sends into $(0,+\infty)$, found by solving $4-x^2>0$.

To find the zeros, use the fact that the natural logarithm vanishes only at argument $1$. Both roots $\pm\sqrt3$ have absolute value less than $2$, so both belong to the domain. Finding the domain and finding the zeros use different conditions: positivity for the former, equality to $1$ for the latter.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider $f(x)=(x-1)^2+2$ with domain $(-\infty,1]$. Find its inverse and the inverse's domain.

$$
y=(x-1)^2+2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y\ge2.
$$
$$
x\le1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x-1\le0.
$$
$$
(x-1)^2=y-2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x-1=-\sqrt{y-2}.
$$
$$
f^{-1}(y)=1-\sqrt{y-2},\qquad D_{f^{-1}}=[2,+\infty).
$$

::grafico{curve="t,(t-1)^2+2,-2,1" legenda="y = (x-1)^2+2" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=0 ymax=11 punti="1,2;0,3" titolo="The branch with x less than or equal to 1 is strictly decreasing."}

<div class="spiegazione">

On the assigned domain the parabola is **strictly decreasing**, so it is injective and has an inverse on its range. Its minimum value is $2$, attained at $x=1$; toward the left, the function takes every larger value. This range becomes the inverse's domain.

When solving the square, the sign of the root is determined by the original branch. Since $x-1\le0$, choose $-\sqrt{y-2}$, giving $x=1-\sqrt{y-2}$. Checking $y=3$ gives $f^{-1}(3)=0$, consistent with $f(0)=3$. For $y>2$, the positive branch would instead produce inputs greater than $1$, excluded from the given domain; at $y=2$, both signs give the vertex $x=1$.

</div>
