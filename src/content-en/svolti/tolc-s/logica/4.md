## Exercise 1

Five friends occupy a row. Gino is in the middle. Lia is immediately to Marco’s left. Nora is to Gino’s right. Omar is not at either end. Who occupies the ends?

- Lia and Nora
- Lia and Marco
- Omar and Nora
- Marco and Nora
- Gino and Omar

| Position | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Person | Lia | Marco | Gino | Omar | Nora |

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** Lia and Nora.

Gino occupies position $3$. The consecutive Lia-Marco pair can occupy $1,2$ or $4,5$. The latter would leave no position to Gino’s right for Nora, so the former holds. Of positions $4,5$, Omar must take $4$ because he cannot be at an end. Nora takes $5$.

The final table allows each constraint to be checked without relying only on the conclusion about the ends. Gino is central, Lia immediately precedes Marco, Nora is right of Gino and Omar is internal. Uniqueness was established by first ruling out the only other possible placement of the Lia-Marco block. Merely drawing a valid row, without that exclusion, would have shown only one possibility.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Exactly two proposals are chosen from A, B, C and D. Choosing A requires choosing B. C and D are incompatible. D is chosen. Which other proposal must be chosen?

- B
- A
- C
- A or C
- None: the conditions are impossible

$$
\begin{aligned}
D\text{ chosen}&\Longrightarrow C\text{ excluded},\\
A\text{ chosen}&\Longrightarrow A,B,D\text{ chosen},\\
3&>2,\\[6pt]
\text{allowed pair}&=\{B,D\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** B.

C is excluded because it is incompatible with D. A is excluded because it would also require B, bringing the total to three proposals. B remains, and BD satisfies every constraint: it has two proposals, contains no A and excludes C. This pair also confirms that the conditions are consistent.

Excluding A uses two constraints together: the implication from A to B and the exact limit of two proposals. If three proposals were permitted, ABD would be allowed. The required number is therefore part of the reasoning, rather than a textual detail. The final check of BD confirms that eliminating the alternatives has not left an inconsistent system.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A key is in exactly one of rooms A, B and C. Ada says “It is in A”, Bea says “It is not in A”, and Ciro says “It is in C”. Exactly two statements are true. Where is the key?

- In C
- In A
- In B
- In A or B
- The data are incompatible

| Key location | Ada | Bea | Ciro | True statements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | T | F | F | 1 |
| B | F | T | F | 1 |
| C | F | T | T | 2 |

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** In C.

Ada’s and Bea’s statements are negations of one another, so exactly one is true regardless of the key’s location. To have two true statements, Ciro’s must also be true, placing the key in C. In C, Bea and Ciro are truthful and Ada is not, confirming the count.

The table considers all three allowed locations without assigning anyone a fixed truthful or lying role beforehand. In each row, the actual content of the statements is evaluated and the truths counted. Only row C reaches two. Case analysis therefore gives both the answer and verification that no second location is compatible.

</div>
