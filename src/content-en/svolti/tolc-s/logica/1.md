## Exercise 1

What is the negation of “Every student has read at least one assigned text”?

- At least one student has read none of the assigned texts
- Every student has left one text unread
- No text has been read by everyone
- At least one student has read every text
- All students have read only one text

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\neg\bigl[\forall s\in S\;\exists t\in T:\ R(s,t)\bigr]\\[6pt]
&\qquad\iff\exists s\in S\;\forall t\in T:\ \neg R(s,t).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** At least one student has read none of the assigned texts.

The sentence requires at least one read text to exist for each student. Its negation requires a student for whom that existence fails. That particular student has read no assigned text. Thus the negation changes “every student” to “at least one student” and “at least one text” to “no text”.

In the formula, $S$ denotes students, $T$ texts and $R(s,t)$ the statement “student $s$ has read text $t$”. The symbols $\forall$ and $\exists$ mean “for every” and “there is at least one”, respectively.

To check the meaning, consider two students and two texts. If the first reads only A and the second only B, every student has read something: the original sentence is true. Yet each student has left one text unread. That observation is therefore not the required negation. What is needed is a student who has read neither A nor B. The example shows why the order of quantifiers and the reference to the same student must be preserved.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the negation of “If the caretaker opens the gate, the indicator goes off”?

- The caretaker opens the gate and the indicator does not go off
- The caretaker does not open the gate
- The indicator goes off and the gate stays closed
- If the indicator goes off, the caretaker opens the gate
- The caretaker does not open the gate or the indicator goes off

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\text{the caretaker opens the gate},\\
Q&=\text{the indicator goes off},\\[6pt]
\neg(P\Rightarrow Q)&\iff P\land\neg Q.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** The caretaker opens the gate and the indicator does not go off.

To refute a conditional promise, its initial condition must occur and its promised outcome must fail. Writing $P$ for opening and $Q$ for switching off gives $\neg(P\Rightarrow Q)\iff P\land\neg Q$. Failure to open alone does not violate the rule, because in that case the rule does not require the indicator to go off.

The four possible combinations check the result. Opening and switching off satisfy the promise. A closed gate with the indicator either on or off does not violate it, because opening has not occurred. Only an open gate with the indicator failing to go off remains. The answer must describe exactly this case: the contrapositive preserves the rule’s truth and therefore is not its negation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

When the pass is valid and the room is free, entry is allowed. The pass is valid, but entry is not allowed. What follows?

- The room is not free
- The pass is invalid
- The room is free
- Entry is allowed
- The pass expired yesterday

$$
\begin{aligned}
(P\land L)&\Rightarrow I,\\
P&=\text{true},\qquad I=\text{false},\\[6pt]
\neg(P\land L)&\iff\neg P\lor\neg L,\\
\neg L&=\text{true}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** The room is not free.

If the room were free, that fact together with the valid pass would satisfy both sufficient conditions for entry. Allowed entry would follow, contrary to the given fact. The room must therefore not be free. This reasoning uses the known validity of the pass; without it, forbidden entry would rule out only the conjunction of requirements.

Here P denotes a valid pass, L a free room and I allowed entry. The final step uses P being true: failure of the compound requirement through an invalid pass has already been ruled out. Only a room that is not free remains. The conclusion concerns the room’s status under the given information; it does not assign a specific cause such as a lesson or a repair.

</div>
