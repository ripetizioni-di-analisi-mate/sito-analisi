## Exercise 1

A double-stranded DNA fragment contains $200$ base pairs. Guanine makes up $20\%$ of all bases. Determine the total numbers of G, C, A and T in the fragment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\text{bases}}&=2\cdot200=400,\\
N_G=N_C&=0.20\cdot400=80,\\
N_A=N_T&=\frac{400-80-80}{2}=120.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each pair contains two bases, so the total is $400$, not $200$. Complementarity requires equal G and C counts and equal A and T counts in the entire duplex. **The result is G = 80, C = 80, A = 120, T = 120.** The sum returns $400$ and the G fraction is $\dfrac{80}{400}=20\%$.

The count does not automatically assign the same percentages to each individual strand. One strand may be richer in one base and the other in its complement. The double-stranded assumption is therefore essential to the reasoning.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The template strand of a gene, for the segment considered, is 3′–TAC GGA ATT–5′. Write the mRNA in the 5′→3′ direction and the resulting polypeptide, given that AUG means start/methionine, CCU proline and UAA stop. Ignore later protein modifications.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{template DNA:}&\quad 3'\text{–TAC GGA ATT–}5',\\
\text{mRNA:}&\quad 5'\text{–AUG CCU UAA–}3',\\
\text{polypeptide:}&\quad \text{Met–Pro}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Transcription uses complementarity and antiparallelism: template T corresponds to RNA A, A to U, G to C and C to G. The template is already written $3'\to5'$, so the product reads left to right in the required $5'\to3'$ direction.

The ribosome reads three codons. AUG and CCU supply **two amino acids**, while UAA signals termination and adds no third residue. The result depends on the reading frame and supplied code; the template DNA must not be translated directly as though it were mRNA.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A dominant-phenotype individual, denoted $A\_$, is crossed with $aa$. Among $100$ offspring, $48$ have the dominant phenotype and $52$ the recessive phenotype. Assuming complete dominance, Mendelian segregation and no mutations, infer the unknown genotype and calculate expected counts for the two groups.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AA\times aa&\Rightarrow \text{all }Aa,\\
Aa\times aa&\Rightarrow P(Aa)=\frac12,\quad P(aa)=\frac12,\\
N_{\text{expected per group}}&=100\cdot\frac12=50.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Recessive offspring must have received $a$ from both parents. The dominant parent therefore has both $A$ and $a$: **its genotype is $Aa$**. The $AA$ alternative could not produce $aa$ under the stated assumptions.

The model predicts $50$ dominant and $50$ recessive offspring as expected values. Counts of $48$ and $52$ need not match expectation exactly: segregation and fertilization produce probabilistic outcomes. Recessive offspring are decisive here for excluding $AA$, while proximity to a $1:1$ ratio is consistent with $Aa\times aa$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

An $AaBb$ individual is crossed with $aabb$. The two loci lie on different chromosomes, segregation is regular and gametes unite randomly. List possible offspring genotypes and probabilities, then calculate the probability of at least one dominant allele.

| Gamete from $AaBb$ | Gamete from $aabb$ | Offspring | Probability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $AB$ | $ab$ | $AaBb$ | $\dfrac14$ |
| $Ab$ | $ab$ | $Aabb$ | $\dfrac14$ |
| $aB$ | $ab$ | $aaBb$ | $\dfrac14$ |
| $ab$ | $ab$ | $aabb$ | $\dfrac14$ |

$$
P(\text{at least one dominant allele})
=1-P(aabb)=1-\frac14=\frac34.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Independent assortment makes the double heterozygote’s four gametes equally probable. The $aabb$ parent instead produces only $ab$ gametes, so each type from the first parent corresponds to one offspring genotype. “At least one” includes carriers of $A$, carriers of $B$ and carriers of both: it is useful to exclude the only case with neither, $aabb$.

**The four probabilities are one quarter each; the requested probability is three quarters.** Simply adding $P(A)$ and $P(B)$ without correcting their intersection would count $AaBb$ twice. If the loci were linked, allele arrangement and recombination would need to be known.

</div>
