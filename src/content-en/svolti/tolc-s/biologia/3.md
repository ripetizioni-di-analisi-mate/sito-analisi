## Exercise 1

A diploid cell has $2n=6$ and contains $2\,\mathrm{pg}$ of DNA in G1. The graph tracks DNA amount per cell: replication occurs between $t=1$ and $t=3$, and regular mitosis with cytokinesis ends at $t=4$. After that moment, only one daughter cell is followed. At $t=3.5$ the cell is still in G2. Give DNA, chromosome and chromatid counts at $t=0$, $t=3.5$ and $t=5$.

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=5.5 ymin=0 ymax=5 nomi="t,D" linee="0,2 1,2 3,4 4,4;4,2 5,2" punti="4,4,vuoto;4,2,pieno" titolo="Time in arbitrary units and DNA in pg per cell: only one daughter is followed after division"}

| Time | DNA per cell | Chromosomes | Chromatids |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $t=0$ | $2\,\mathrm{pg}$ | 6 | 6 |
| $t=3.5$ | $4\,\mathrm{pg}$ | 6 | 12 |
| $t=5$ | $2\,\mathrm{pg}$ | 6 | 6 |

<div class="spiegazione">

The rise represents S phase: DNA amount doubles, but sister chromatids remain joined and chromosome number is still six. At the end of mitosis each daughter receives one copy of every chromosome. **Each daughter returns to $2\,\mathrm{pg}$ and retains $2n=6$.** The graph’s jump does not mean half the DNA was destroyed: the reference changes from the mother cell to one of its two daughters.

These counts do not describe anaphase before cytoplasmic division, when separated chromatids are counted as chromosomes. Specifying that the cell is in G2 at $t=3.5$ excludes anaphase; the plateau DNA value alone would not distinguish these stages.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cell with $2n=6$ contains $2\,\mathrm{pg}$ DNA before S phase. It undergoes regular meiosis. Complete the balance for a single cell after S phase, meiosis I and meiosis II. There is no second replication between divisions.

| Stage | Ploidy | Chromosomes | Chromatids | DNA |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| After S | diploid | 6 | 12 | $4\,\mathrm{pg}$ |
| After meiosis I | haploid | 3 | 6 | $2\,\mathrm{pg}$ |
| After meiosis II | haploid | 3 | 3 | $1\,\mathrm{pg}$ |

$$
D_{\mathrm{S}}=2\cdot2=4\,\mathrm{pg},\qquad
D_{\mathrm{I}}=\frac42=2\,\mathrm{pg},\qquad
D_{\mathrm{II}}=\frac22=1\,\mathrm{pg}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first division separates homologs. Each cell receives three still-duplicated chromosomes: it is **haploid with six chromatids**. Its DNA amount is numerically equal to that of the initial G1 cell, but ploidy differs. This coincidence shows why DNA mass alone cannot define ploidy.

The second division separates chromatids and halves DNA per cell again. Final products have three chromosomes and one picogram each. Altogether the four products contain $4\cdot1=4\,\mathrm{pg}$, equal to DNA after replication: division distributes material already synthesized.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a model, oxygen partial pressure is $100$ units in alveoli and $40$ in blood arriving at pulmonary capillaries. In tissues, incoming blood has value $95$ and cells $20$. Determine diffusion direction at both sites and describe the role of blood between exchanges.

$$
\Delta p_{\text{lung}}=100-40=60>0,\qquad
\Delta p_{\text{tissues}}=95-20=75>0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At each site oxygen diffuses from higher to lower partial pressure. **In alveoli it passes from air into blood; in tissues from blood into cells.** The numbers describe gradients, not transferred oxygen quantities: calculating a flow would also require area, thickness and other barrier properties.

Blood connects exchange sites through circulation; much oxygen is carried by hemoglobin. Ventilation, diffusion and circulatory transport perform distinct tasks. Greater alveolar thickness could hinder exchange while initially maintaining the same gradient, whereas lower hemoglobin would reduce carrying capacity.

</div>

## Exercise 4

In a simplified daily kidney balance, $180\,\mathrm{L}$ water is filtered and $178.5\,\mathrm{L}$ reabsorbed. For glucose, $180\,\mathrm{g}$ is filtered and $180\,\mathrm{g}$ reabsorbed. There is no tubular secretion of either substance in the model. Calculate amounts eliminated in final urine and distinguish filtration from reabsorption.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{\text{urine}}&=180-178.5=1.5\,\mathrm{L},\\
m_{\text{glucose}}&=180-180=0\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Excreted amount equals filtered amount minus reabsorbed amount when secretion is zero. **The model gives $1.5\,\mathrm{L}$ water and no glucose in final urine.** Glucose crossed the initial filter but was fully recovered: its final absence does not show that it was never filtered.

The large difference between filtrate and urine results from selective recovery along the tubules. The values are an assigned teaching balance, not a universal prediction for every person. Adding tubular secretion would require a positive term in the excretion balance, explicitly excluded here.

</div>
