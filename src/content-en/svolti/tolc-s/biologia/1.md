## Exercise 1

A laboratory observes three cell samples. In the table, “present” means a structure was directly identified. Classify the samples by cell organization and explain whether absence of chloroplasts excludes plant origin. In sample A, capable of reproducing by binary fission, DNA occupies an unbounded cytoplasmic region and membrane-bounded organelles are absent.

| Sample | Bounded nucleus | Wall | Chloroplasts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | absent | present | absent |
| B | present | cellulose | present |
| C | present | cellulose | absent |

<div class="spiegazione">

**A has prokaryotic organization; B and C have eukaryotic organization.** A membrane-bounded nucleus identifies B and C as eukaryotic. For A, DNA location, absence of membrane-bounded organelles and binary fission are considered together; absence of a nucleus alone in a specialized cell would not be sufficient. For A, the data alone do not distinguish Bacteria from Archaea: additional molecular features would be required.

B is consistent with a photosynthetic plant cell. C can come from nonphotosynthetic plant tissue, such as a root. Its cellulose wall and nucleus are consistent with that interpretation, while the absence of chloroplasts does not exclude it. The conclusion does not require every cell in one organism to have the same organelles in equal quantities. Both plant samples can have mitochondria and respire.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A membrane allows water through but not the dissolved solute. It separates two compartments initially at equal pressure: L contains $100\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ solute and R contains $300\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$. Determine the direction of initial net water flow. Then predict the effect of a hypotonic external solution on an animal cell and on a plant cell with an intact wall.

::grafico{assi=0 griglia=0 xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=5 linee="5,0.7 5,4.3" punti="2,2,pieno;3,3,pieno;6,1.3,pieno;6,3.5,pieno;7.3,2,pieno;8.5,1.3,pieno;9,3.5,pieno;8,4,pieno" vettori="3.5,2.5,6.5,2.5" etichette="2.5,4.7,L: 100;7.5,4.7,R: 300;5,0.2,membrane" titolo="Initial concentrations in mmol/L; the arrow shows initial net water flow"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The impermeant solute concentration is higher in R. At equal initial pressure, osmosis causes net flow **from L toward R**. The solute cannot cross the membrane, so its transfer does not reduce the difference. Water enters the more concentrated compartment and dilutes it; pressure differences may also develop over time, so initial direction alone does not describe every later state.

In a hypotonic external solution, water enters the cell. An animal cell increases in volume and may lyse. A plant cell with an intact wall develops turgor: wall resistance opposes expansion. The wall does not prevent all water entry and does not replace the membrane in selecting solutes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The same enzyme is measured at equal temperature, substrate concentration and enzyme amount. Initial rates, in arbitrary units, are shown in the table. Identify the best pH among those tested and calculate the percentage rate increase from pH 5 to pH 7. Decide whether the data prove that the absolute optimum is exactly 7.

| pH | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Rate | 4 | 10 | 2 |

$$
\text{increase}=10-4=6,\qquad
\text{percentage increase}=\frac{6}{4}\cdot100\%=150\%.
$$

::grafico{assi=1 griglia=1 xmin=4 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=12 nomi="pH,v" punti="5,4,pieno;7,10,pieno;9,2,pieno" titolo="Initial rate in arbitrary units: points are measurements, not a complete curve"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**pH 7 gives the highest rate among the three measured values**, namely $10$. The percentage comparison uses the initial rate $4$ as its base; the final value is $250\%$ of the initial value, whereas the increase is $150\%$. These percentages answer different questions.

Because other conditions are controlled, the comparison supports an influence of pH on activity. It does not prove that the absolute maximum lies exactly at 7: an unmeasured intermediate value could produce a higher rate. Locating the optimum requires additional measurements, without inventing a unique curve from three points.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Brief amino acid labeling tracks a secreted protein. The signal first appears in rough endoplasmic reticulum, then in the Golgi and finally in vesicles near the membrane. Reconstruct the route and predict the immediate effect of blocking vesicle fusion with the membrane if synthesis and transport into vesicles continue.

<div class="spiegazione">

The observed route is **rough reticulum → Golgi → secretory vesicles → external environment**. Reticulum-associated ribosomes synthesize the chain destined for this pathway; the Golgi participates in modification and sorting. Final secretion requires exocytosis, meaning fusion of the vesicle membrane with the plasma membrane.

If only this fusion is blocked and protein continues arriving, accumulation in vesicles and reduced new secretion outside are expected. It does not follow that synthesis stops immediately, because the statement keeps it active. The tracer indicates a passage sequence, not that all organelles perform the same reaction.

</div>
