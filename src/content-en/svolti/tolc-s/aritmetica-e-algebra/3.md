## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x+2}{3}-\dfrac{x-1}{2}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x+2)-3(x-1)&=6,\\
2x+4-3x+3&=6,\\
-x&=-1,\\
x&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerical denominators impose no restrictions on the unknown. Multiplying the entire equation by six eliminates the fractions. The minus sign before the second fraction applies to both terms of $x-1$, so the expansion contains $-3x+3$. Combining like terms leaves $-x+7=6$, giving $x=1$. Substituting into the original equation makes the first ratio one and the second zero; their difference is exactly one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find all real values of $x$ satisfying $2x^2-7x+3=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&=(-7)^2-4\cdot2\cdot3=25,\\[4pt]
x&=\frac{7\pm5}{4},\\[4pt]
x_1&=\frac12,\qquad x_2=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quadratic coefficient is nonzero, so the quadratic formula applies. The positive discriminant shows that two distinct real solutions exist. The formula’s denominator is $2a=4$, and the two signs before the square root produce $\dfrac24$ and $\dfrac{12}{4}$, respectively. The factorization $(2x-1)(x-3)$ confirms both roots and gives the linear coefficient $-6-1=-7$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Plan A costs a fixed $6$ euros and $2$ euros per hour. Plan B costs $3.50$ euros per hour. Find the duration for which the costs are equal and the amount paid.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6+2h&=3.5h,\\
6&=1.5h,\\
h&=4,\\
C&=6+2\cdot4=14.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="6+2*x;3.5*x" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=25 punti="4,14,pieno" nomi="h,C" legenda="C_A=6+2h;C_B=3.5h" titolo="Duration in hours on the horizontal axis and cost in euros on the vertical axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The letter $h$ represents duration in hours, so $h\ge0$. The first plan starts at six euros and increases by two euros per hour; the second starts at zero and increases faster. Equating the costs gives a linear equation. The result, four hours, belongs to the physical domain and gives fourteen euros under either plan. In the graph, the lines intersect at $(4,14)$; their different slopes explain why equality occurs only once.

</div>
