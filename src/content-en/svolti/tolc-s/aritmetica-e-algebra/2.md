## Exercise 1

Simplify $\dfrac{(2a^3)^2a^{-1}}{4a^2}$ for $a\ne0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(2a^3)^2a^{-1}}{4a^2}
&=\frac{4a^6a^{-1}}{4a^2}\\[6pt]
&=a^{6-1-2}=a^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outer square applies to both factors in $2a^3$, producing $4a^6$. The factor $a^{-1}$ is defined only for $a\ne0$, a condition already required by the denominator. Exponents are added in the product and subtracted in the division. The numerical coefficients cancel because both equal four. The result is $a^3$ on the original domain, without restoring the excluded value $a=0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}$ and state the admissible values.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-2\ne0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\ne2,\\[6pt]
\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}&=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}=x+2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x+2" xmin=-4 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=7 punti="2,4,vuoto" titolo="The simplified formula describes the line with the point at horizontal coordinate two excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator determines the domain before any cancellation. The difference of squares then provides the common factor $x-2$, which is nonzero on that domain. Thus the fraction coincides with the line $y=x+2$ for every $x\ne2$. At horizontal coordinate two, the line would have vertical coordinate four, but the original expression is undefined. The open point in the graph records this exclusion and prevents simplification from being confused with an extension of the domain.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize $\dfrac4{\sqrt5-1}$ and simplify the result.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac4{\sqrt5-1}
&=\frac{4(\sqrt5+1)}{(\sqrt5-1)(\sqrt5+1)}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{4(\sqrt5+1)}{5-1}=\sqrt5+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is a difference of two terms. Multiplication by their sum, called the conjugate expression, gives the difference of their squares and makes the denominator rational. Multiplying top and bottom by $\sqrt5+1$ preserves the ratio because this factor is nonzero. The denominator becomes four, which cancels with the four in the numerator. The result is positive, like the original expression, since $\sqrt5>1$.

</div>
