## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{x-4}\le0$.

$$
x\ne4,\qquad x+1=0\Longleftrightarrow x=-1.
$$

| $x$ | $x<-1$ | $-1<x<4$ | $x>4$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x+1$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x-4$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $\dfrac{x+1}{x-4}$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
x\in[-1,4).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator excludes four. The numerator changes sign at minus one; the two values divide the number line into three intervals. The ratio is negative only in the middle interval, where the factors have opposite signs. At minus one, the ratio is defined and equals zero, so that point is included by the non-strict inequality. At four, the ratio does not exist and the point remains excluded. The solution therefore has one closed and one open endpoint.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the real numbers at most $3$ away from $2$ and strictly greater than zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x-2|\le3&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -3\le x-2\le3,\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -1\le x\le5,\\[4pt]
[-1,5]\cap(0,+\infty)&=(0,5].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Distance from the point with coordinate two is represented by the absolute value $|x-2|$. “At most three” includes points at distance exactly three, so both endpoints of the first interval are included. The second requirement must hold together with the first, corresponding to intersection with the positive real numbers. This excludes every point from minus one through zero. Five remains admissible because it is positive and its distance from two is exactly three.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\sqrt{2x+3}=x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&\ge0,\\
2x+3&=x^2,\\
(x-3)(x+1)&=0,\\
x&=3\quad\text{or}\quad x=-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\sqrt{2\cdot3+3}=3,\qquad S=\{3\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side is nonnegative, so every solution must satisfy $x\ge0$. This condition also guarantees that the square root exists. Squaring both sides produces a quadratic equation with two candidates. Minus one violates the sign restriction and is excluded; three is admissible and directly satisfies the equation. The solution set contains only three. Substitution into the original equation completes the check after squaring, a step that can introduce extraneous candidates.

</div>
