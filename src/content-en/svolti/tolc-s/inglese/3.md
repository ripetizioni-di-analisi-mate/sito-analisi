## Exercise 1: Articles and initial sounds

Complete: “She waited for ___ hour outside ___ university.”

- an / a
- a / an
- an / an
- a / a
- many / a

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: an / a**

*Hour* begins with a vowel sound because its *h* is silent, so it requires *an*. *University* begins with the consonant sound /j/, so it requires *a*. The choice depends on pronunciation, not the first written letter.

The complete sentence is “She waited for an hour outside a university.” Comparing the two words shows why spelling alone is insufficient. *Hour* has an unpronounced written consonant; *university* has a written vowel preceded in speech by /j/. The check concerns the first sound of the word immediately following the article, even when an adjective intervenes before the noun.

</div>

## Exercise 2: A possessive without a noun

Complete: “This bag belongs to Anna. It is ___.”

- hers
- her
- she
- herself
- she's

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: hers**

After *is*, the possessive replacing *her bag* is needed: *hers*. *Her* alone does not function as a possessive pronoun here, while *she* is a subject and *herself* is reflexive. Possessives such as *hers* do not take an apostrophe.

The complete sentence is “This bag belongs to Anna. It is hers.” *Hers* avoids repeating *her bag*, preserving the ownership expressed by *belongs to*. The word ends in *s*, but this ending is not plural: there is still one bag. The apostrophe used in *Anna's bag* is not added, because the possessive pronoun has its own form.

</div>

## Exercise 3: Few and insufficient

Read: “We have few chairs, so some people will have to stand.” Which interpretation is correct?

- There are not many chairs, and they are insufficient for everyone.
- There are more chairs than people.
- There are no people in the room.
- Everyone has been promised a chair.
- All the chairs are broken.

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: There are not many chairs, and they are insufficient for everyone.**

*Few*, without an article, emphasizes a small number. The consequence *some people will have to stand* establishes that seats are insufficient for everyone. The text does not say the chairs are broken or entirely absent: scarcity does not necessarily mean zero.

Two pieces of information must be preserved: chairs are few, and some people will stand. The second clause clarifies the negative force of *few* in this context. It does not, however, authorize calculating an exact number of chairs or people. An answer introducing a precise count would go beyond the text even if it retained the general idea of scarcity.

</div>
