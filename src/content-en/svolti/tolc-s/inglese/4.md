## Exercise 1: An explicit obligation

Complete by expressing the stated obligation: “You ___ wear a seat belt; it is compulsory.”

- must
- might
- would
- may
- could

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: must**

*Compulsory* specifies that wearing a seat belt is obligatory. *Must* directly expresses that obligation and takes base-form *wear*. *May*, *might* and *could* indicate permission or possibility in their relevant uses; *would* does not convey this obligation.

The complete sentence is “You must wear a seat belt; it is compulsory.” The decisive point is the meaning of *compulsory*, excluding mere possibility. The proposed forms after the subject could be grammatical elsewhere: *might wear*, for example, would describe possible behavior. Here grammar must be read together with the constraint expressed in the second clause.

</div>

## Exercise 2: Lack of obligation

Which sentence means that printing the form is optional?

- You don't have to print the form.
- You mustn't print the form.
- You must print the form.
- You have to print the form.
- You are forbidden to print the form.

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: You don't have to print the form.**

*Don't have to* denies obligation: one can choose not to print. *Mustn't* and *are forbidden to* instead express prohibition, while *must* and *have to* require printing. All are grammatical sentences, but only one preserves the meaning of optional.

The chosen sentence leaves two actions open: printing or not printing. The message removes the necessity of printing without introducing a penalty for someone who chooses to print. This distinction matters in forms and notices: an optional request should not be interpreted as forbidden. The meaning can be checked with the paraphrase “Printing is not required.”

</div>

## Exercise 3: An explicit prohibition

Complete to express the prohibition: “The sign says ‘No photography’. You ___ take photos here.”

- mustn't
- don't have to
- should
- might
- would like to

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: mustn't**

*No photography* prohibits taking photos. *Mustn't* communicates that prohibition. *Don't have to* would make photos optional without forbidding them; *should* would give contrary advice, while the other expressions do not convey the sign's rule.

The complete sentence is “You mustn't take photos here.” The sign excludes the action rather than merely calling it unnecessary or optional. Compared with the previous exercise, the position of *not* alone does not determine the same logic for all modals: *must not* prohibits the action, whereas *do not have to* denies only the obligation to perform it.

</div>
