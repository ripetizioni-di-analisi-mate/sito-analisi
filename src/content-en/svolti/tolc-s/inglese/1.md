## Exercise 1: Present habits

Complete the sentence about a current habit: “She ___ to university every morning.”

- goes
- go
- going
- gone
- are going

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: goes**

The subject *she* is third-person singular. To describe the current habit indicated by *every morning*, the present simple requires *goes*. *Go* lacks the ending, *going* and *gone* do not form a finite verb here, and *are* does not agree with the subject.

The complete sentence is “She goes to university every morning.” The final phrase describes repeated action, not one ongoing journey. Agreement can be checked by replacing *she* with *they*: the result would be *They go*. Subject and verb form change, while the habit remains the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2: Actions in progress

Complete: “Please be quiet. The children ___ an online lesson right now.”

- are attending
- attends
- is attending
- attending
- has attend

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: are attending**

The request for silence concerns an ongoing lesson, as *right now* specifies. *Children* is plural, so *are attending* is required. The progressive needs both an agreeing auxiliary and an *-ing* verb: none of the other options satisfies both requirements.

The complete sentence is “The children are attending an online lesson right now.” The request for silence has a present reason: the lesson is taking place. If the text said *every Tuesday* and described only a habit, it could use *attend*. Here the progressive presents an ongoing activity, and the plural auxiliary links that information to the subject.

</div>

## Exercise 3: A state verb

Complete: “I ___ the answer, so I can explain it to you.”

- know
- am knowing
- knows
- knowing
- have know

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: know**

*Know*, meaning to possess knowledge, normally uses the present simple. With *I*, the form is *know*. *Am knowing* inappropriately treats this state as a progressive activity in standard usage; *knows* does not agree, while the other sequences are incomplete or malformed.

The complete sentence is “I know the answer, so I can explain it to you.” Knowledge makes explanation possible, but is not presented as an action unfolding. This distinction prevents automatically choosing the progressive for everything true in the present. The same semantic check applies to state verbs such as *believe* and *understand* in their ordinary uses.

</div>
