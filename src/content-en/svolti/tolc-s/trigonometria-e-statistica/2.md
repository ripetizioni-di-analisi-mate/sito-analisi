## Exercise 1

Solve $2\sin^2x-\sin x=0$ on $[0,2\pi)$.

$$
\sin x(2\sin x-1)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sin x=0\ \text{or}\ \sin x=\frac12.
$$
$$
\sin x=0\Rightarrow x\in\{0,\pi\},\qquad
\sin x=\frac12\Rightarrow x\in\left\{\frac\pi6,\frac{5\pi}{6}\right\}.
$$
$$
S=\left\{0,\frac\pi6,\frac{5\pi}{6},\pi\right\}.
$$

::grafico{f="2*sin(x)^2-sin(x)" xmin=-0.2 xmax=6.5 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.5 punti="0,0,pieno;0.5235987756,0,pieno;2.617993878,0,pieno;3.1415926536,0,pieno;6.2831853072,0,vuoto" titolo="The four allowed zeros; the point at 2π on the right is excluded from the interval."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The common factor is $\sin x$: factoring it out produces a **zero product**, solved by setting each factor to zero. Dividing immediately by $\sin x$ would instead remove solutions where that factor is zero. Both resulting equations must be treated under the same original restriction.

The zeros of sine in the interval are $0$ and $\pi$. The level $\dfrac12$ is reached in the first and second quadrants, at $\dfrac\pi6$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. Their union contains four distinct values. The point $2\pi$ makes the expression zero but is not in the specified interval.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Triangle $ABC$ has $AB=8$, $AC=5$ and $\angle A=60^\circ$. Determine $BC$ and the area.

$$
BC^2=8^2+5^2-2\cdot8\cdot5\cos60^\circ
=64+25-40=49.
$$
$$
BC=7,\qquad
h=5\sin60^\circ=\frac{5\sqrt3}{2}.
$$
$$
\mathcal A=\frac12\cdot8\cdot\frac{5\sqrt3}{2}=10\sqrt3.
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 8,0 2.5,4.3301270189 0,0" segmenti="2.5,0,2.5,4.3301270189" assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-1 ymax=5.5 punti="0,0,pieno,A;8,0,pieno,B;2.5,4.3301270189,pieno,C" etichette="4,-0.5,8;0.7,2.5,5;5.5,2.5,7;1,0.45,60°" titolo="The altitude meets base AB; the side opposite angle A has length 7."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The data specify two sides and their **included angle**, so the cosine rule directly gives the square of the third side. The minus sign before the mixed term comes from the formula, and the cosine of $60^\circ$ is positive. Only the positive square root is chosen because the result is a length.

For the area, draw the altitude from C to base AB. In the resulting right triangle, AC is the hypotenuse and the altitude is opposite $60^\circ$, so sine applies. The area is half the product of base and altitude and is expressed in squared length units.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two points on the same side of a tower are aligned with its base and are $20\,\mathrm m$ apart. The elevation is $60^\circ$ from the nearer point and $30^\circ$ from the farther point. The ground is level and measurements start at ground level. Determine the tower height.

$$
\frac hd=\tan60^\circ=\sqrt3,\qquad
\frac h{d+20}=\tan30^\circ=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}.
$$
$$
d\sqrt3=(d+20)\frac{\sqrt3}{3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad3d=d+20\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d=10.
$$
$$
h=10\sqrt3\,\mathrm m.
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 0,17.3205080757 30,0 0,0;0,17.3205080757 10,0" assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-3 xmax=33 ymin=-4 ymax=20 etichette="-1.5,9,h;5,-1.8,d;20,-1.8,20 m;7,1.5,60°;25,1.2,30°" titolo="The two triangles have the same height, and their bases differ by 20 meters."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let the unknown distance from the nearer point be $d$, so the farther distance is $d+20$. The tower is the opposite leg in both triangles. **Tangent** connects its height to the horizontal distance without introducing the lengths of the lines of sight.

The two expressions for $h$ can be equated because they describe the same tower. After finding $d=10$, substitution into the first relation gives the height. Checking the second relation gives $30\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{3}=10\sqrt3$. No eye height is added because the problem places measurements at ground level.

</div>
