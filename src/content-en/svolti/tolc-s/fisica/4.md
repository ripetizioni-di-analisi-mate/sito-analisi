## Exercise 1

An ideal $12\,\mathrm{V}$ source supplies a $2\,\Omega$ resistor in series with two parallel $6\,\Omega$ resistors. Determine total current and current in each parallel branch.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_p&=\left(\frac16+\frac16\right)^{-1}=3\,\Omega,\\[6pt]
R_{\mathrm{eq}}&=2+3=5\,\Omega,\\[6pt]
I&=\frac{12}{5}=2.4\,\mathrm{A},\\[6pt]
V_p&=IR_p=7.2\,\mathrm{V},\\[6pt]
I_1=I_2&=\frac{7.2}{6}=1.2\,\mathrm{A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Circuit reduction starts with the parallel pair, because both resistors share the same nodes and voltage. The parallel equivalent resistance is then added to the series resistance. Total current crosses the $2\,\Omega$ resistor and divides only at the next junction. The branches are identical, so they carry equal currents. A junction check gives $1.2+1.2=2.4\,\mathrm{A}$; a voltage check gives $2\cdot2.4+7.2=12\,\mathrm{V}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A real object $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall is $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from a converging lens of focal length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine image position, orientation and height in the thin-lens model.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1q&=\frac1{10}-\frac1{30}=\frac1{15},\\[6pt]
q&=15\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
M&=-\frac{15}{30}=-\frac12,\\[6pt]
h_i&=Mh_o=-2\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Positive distances are used for real objects and real images. The result $q>0$ places the image on the opposite side of the lens from the object and allows it to be collected on a screen. Magnification compares heights and includes the orientation sign. The negative value means an inverted image; magnitude $\dfrac12$ means it is half the object's height. Geometric height is therefore $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, whereas $-2\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the signed coordinate relative to the optical axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A capacitor of capacitance $5\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is charged to $12\,\mathrm{V}$, then disconnected and isolated. Its capacitance is reduced to $2.5\,\mu\mathrm{F}$. Determine initial charge and final voltage, assuming no charge leakage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i&=C_iV_i=5\cdot12=60\,\mu\mathrm{C},\\[6pt]
Q_f&=Q_i,\\[6pt]
V_f&=\frac{Q_f}{C_f}=\frac{60}{2.5}=24\,\mathrm{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During charging, the source fixes voltage; after disconnection, isolation instead fixes charge. This distinction determines which quantity remains constant in the second phase. The relation $Q=CV$ applies in both states. Halving capacitance with unchanged charge doubles voltage. Microcoulombs divided by microfarads leave volts, so the prefixes cancel. The plates retain charges of equal magnitude and opposite signs.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A sample initially has $1600$ radioactive nuclei and a half-life of $2\,\mathrm{h}$. Determine the average number of original nuclei remaining after $6\,\mathrm{h}$ and the fraction that has decayed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=\frac62=3,\\[6pt]
N&=1600\left(\frac12\right)^3=200,\\[6pt]
\frac{N_0-N}{N_0}&=\frac{1600-200}{1600}=\frac78=87.5\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First count the elapsed half-life intervals. Each interval halves the number still present, so after three intervals one eighth of the initial sample remains. The result is an average value of the statistical model: an individual nucleus's decay is random. The decayed fraction is found by subtracting the remaining fraction from one. Transformed nuclei do not disappear as matter; they become daughter nuclei through the radioactive process.

</div>
