## Exercise 1

A sensor is $3\,\mathrm{m}$ below a lake's surface. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and atmospheric pressure $100000\,\mathrm{Pa}$, determine the pressure increase and absolute pressure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta p&=\rho gh=1000\cdot10\cdot3=30000\,\mathrm{Pa},\\[6pt]
p&=p_0+\Delta p=130000\,\mathrm{Pa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="100+10*x" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=145 nomi="h,p" punti="0,100,pieno;3,130,pieno" legenda="p=100+10h" titolo="Depth in meters and absolute pressure in kPa: surface pressure is 100 kPa"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The liquid is at rest, so the **hydrostatic pressure law** applies. Depth is measured downward from the surface and gives a positive pressure contribution. The first result measures only the water column's effect; the second also includes atmospheric pressure at the surface. The graph has intercept $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and slope $10\,\mathrm{kPa/m}$. At zero depth it correctly returns atmospheric pressure, while at three meters it adds $30\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of volume $500\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and average density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ floats in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Determine immersed volume and buoyancy, with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{\mathrm{imm}}&=\frac{\rho_c}{\rho_f}V
=\frac{600}{1000}\cdot500=300\,\mathrm{cm^3},\\[6pt]
m&=600\cdot500\cdot10^{-6}=0.3\,\mathrm{kg},\\[6pt]
F_A&=mg=3\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Floating equilibrium requires buoyancy and weight to be equal. Buoyancy uses displaced-water volume, which equals immersed volume. The body's mass instead uses total volume. The density ratio shows that $60\%$ of the volume must be immersed, while $40\%$ remains above water. A direct check with Archimedes' principle gives $1000\cdot10\cdot300\cdot10^{-6}=3\,\mathrm{N}$, equal to the weight calculated through body density.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A gas absorbs $500\,\mathrm{J}$ and expands its volume by $1\,\mathrm{L}$ against constant external pressure $2\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Determine work done and internal-energy change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V&=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3},\\[6pt]
W&=p_{\mathrm{est}}\Delta V=200\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\Delta U&=Q-W=500-200=300\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas is the system, and work is positive when it pushes the surroundings during expansion. Before multiplying pressure and volume, liters are converted to cubic meters; pascals times cubic meters have units of joules. The first law divides the incoming $500\,\mathrm{J}$ between delivered work and energy remaining in the system. Final temperature cannot be determined from the given data alone because gas amount and the relation between internal energy and temperature would be needed.

</div>
