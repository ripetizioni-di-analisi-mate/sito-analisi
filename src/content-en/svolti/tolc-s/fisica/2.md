## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ crate is pulled horizontally with $18\,\mathrm{N}$. Kinetic friction is $6\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate acceleration and velocity after $3\,\mathrm{s}$, starting from rest and assuming constant forces.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{ris}}&=18-6=12\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
a&=\frac{12}{4}=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
v&=0+3\cdot3=9\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,1 0,1" linee="-2,0 5,0" vettori="2,0.5,4.5,0.5,18 N;0,0.5,-0.83,0.5,6 N" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=2 uguale=1 titolo="Horizontal forces point in opposite directions; crate mass is 4 kg"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The selected system is the crate alone. Vertically, weight and the floor's normal force balance; horizontally, friction is subtracted from the pulling force. **Newton's second law** applies to the $12\,\mathrm{N}$ net force. Since forces are constant, acceleration is also constant and the velocity equation applies. Checking units gives $\mathrm{N/kg}=\mathrm{m/s^2}$; acceleration points along the pulling force, which has greater magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts from rest at height $5\,\mathrm{m}$ and descends along a frictionless guide to height zero. Taking $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, determine final kinetic energy and speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_i&=mgh=2\cdot10\cdot5=100\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
K_f&=U_i=100\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
\frac12\cdot2\,v_f^2&=100,\\[6pt]
v_f&=10\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final height sets the potential-energy reference. The body starts from rest, so initially it has only gravitational potential energy. The guide's normal force is perpendicular to motion and does zero work; with no friction, mechanical energy is conserved. At height zero all initial energy has become kinetic. Speed is the positive square root because it is a magnitude. The result depends on vertical drop and stays the same for differently shaped guides satisfying the same assumptions.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart moving at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ hits a stationary $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart and remains attached. Neglect external impulse. Determine their common velocity and the kinetic energy converted into other forms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_f&=\frac{2\cdot6+4\cdot0}{2+4}=2\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
K_i&=\frac12\cdot2\cdot6^2=36\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
K_f&=\frac12\cdot6\cdot2^2=12\,\mathrm{J},\\[6pt]
K_i-K_f&=24\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both carts form the system during the collision. Interaction forces are internal and do not change total momentum; negligible external impulse justifies momentum conservation. After attachment, the mass multiplying the common velocity is the sum of both masses. The collision is completely inelastic, so kinetic energy is not conserved. The missing $24\,\mathrm{J}$ becomes deformation, heat and other energy forms; final momentum remains $6\cdot2=12\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$, equal to the initial value.

</div>
