## Exercise 1

A fair six-sided die is rolled once. Let A be “an even number” and B be “a number greater than four.” List their union and intersection and calculate the probability of the union.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{2,4,6\},\qquad B=\{5,6\},\\
A\cap B&=\{6\},\qquad A\cup B=\{2,4,5,6\},\\[4pt]
P(A\cup B)&=\frac36+\frac26-\frac16=\frac46=\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{tipo="due" zone="a,ab,b" elementi="a:2,4;ab:6;b:5;u:1,3" titolo="The four outcomes in the union and the two excluded outcomes"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The union contains every face satisfying at least one condition, including six, which satisfies both. When the sizes of A and B are added, six is counted twice: subtracting the intersection removes that repetition. The diagram separates the two outcomes in A only, the one in B only, the common outcome, and the two outside. Since the die is fair, every face has weight one sixth, so counting the four union elements gives probability two thirds.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three members must be selected from five distinct people. Count the selections when the roles of chair, secretary, and treasurer are assigned, and then when only an unassigned group is formed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{roles}}&=5\cdot4\cdot3=60,\\[4pt]
N_{\mathrm{groups}}&=\frac{5\cdot4\cdot3}{3!}=\frac{60}{6}=10.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With distinct roles, three successive choices are made: one person for the first role, one of the remaining four for the second, and one of the remaining three for the third. Swapping chair and secretary produces a different assignment. In an unassigned group, however, the same three people represent one selection. Each group appears in the previous count in the six possible orders of its three members. Division by six removes this overcounting and gives ten groups.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An unordered group of three objects is selected uniformly from four red and three blue objects, all distinct. Calculate the probability that the group contains exactly two red objects.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=\binom73=35,\\[4pt]
N_A&=\binom42\binom31=6\cdot3=18,\\[4pt]
P(A)&=\frac{18}{35}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Selection is uniform among unordered groups, so numerator and denominator must both count that type of group. “Exactly two red objects” requires the third object to be blue. The red pair can be selected in six ways and the blue object in three; each red pair combines with every blue object. The ratio of eighteen favorable groups to thirty-five possible groups is the required probability. A group of three red objects is not in the event, even though it contains at least two reds.

</div>
