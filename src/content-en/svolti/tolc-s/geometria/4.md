## Exercise 1

Rotate $P=(4,1)$ through $90^\circ$ counterclockwise about $C=(1,2)$. Check the distance from the center before and after rotation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P-C&=(3,-1),\\[6pt]
(3,-1)&\longmapsto(1,3),\\[6pt]
P_1&=(1,2)+(1,3)=(2,5),\\[6pt]
CP&=\sqrt{3^2+(-1)^2}=\sqrt{10},\\[6pt]
CP_1&=\sqrt{1^2+3^2}=\sqrt{10}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rule $(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)$ applies to a counterclockwise quarter-turn about the origin. Here the center is $C$, so first consider the vector from $C$ to $P$. After rotation, return to the original reference by adding the center coordinates again. The image is $(2,5)$. The two calculated distances agree, as required for a rotation. The angle can also be checked: the dot product of vectors $(3,-1)$ and $(1,3)$ is $3-3=0$, so they are perpendicular. The chosen rule fixes the counterclockwise direction among the two possibilities.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Point $P=(2,1)$ is first translated by $(3,0)$ and then reflected in the $y$-axis. Find the result and compare it with the result obtained in the reverse order.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2,1)&\longmapsto(5,1)\longmapsto(-5,1),\\[6pt]
(2,1)&\longmapsto(-2,1)\longmapsto(1,1).
\end{aligned}
$$

::struttura{punti="2,1,P;5,1,Q;-5,1,R;1,1,S" frecce="0,0,6,0,x;0,0,0,3,y" riferimento=0 titolo="R is the result in the stated order; S corresponds to the reverse order"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Translation adds $3$ to the horizontal coordinate, while reflection changes the sign of the coordinate present when it is applied. In the stated order, it therefore changes the sign of $2+3$, giving $-5$. In reverse order, it first changes the sign of $2$ and then adds $3$, giving $1$. The operations produce different points even though each preserves distances. In general, the two compositions send $(x,y)$ to $(-x-3,y)$ and $(-x+3,y)$, respectively. This comparison shows why a description of successive transformations must retain their order.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the vertices, area, and perimeter of the region described by
$$
\begin{cases}
x\ge0,\\
y\ge0,\\
x+y\le6.
\end{cases}
$$

::grafico{f="6-x" poligoni="0,0 6,0 0,6" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=7 uguale=1 titolo="The region is the intersection of the three half-planes"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
O&=(0,0),\quad A=(6,0),\quad B=(0,6),\\[6pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{6\cdot6}{2}=18,\\[6pt]
AB&=\sqrt{6^2+6^2}=6\sqrt2,\\[6pt]
P&=12+6\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two conditions select the first quadrant, including its axes. The third selects the half-plane below $y=6-x$, which passes through $(6,0)$ and $(0,6)$. Their intersection is therefore the right triangle with these two vertices and the origin. Both legs are $6$, so the area is half the product $6\cdot6$, and the sloping side follows from the Pythagorean theorem. All inequalities are non-strict, so the three sides and vertices belong to the region. Including the boundaries does not change the area but does determine whether a boundary point satisfies the system.

</div>
