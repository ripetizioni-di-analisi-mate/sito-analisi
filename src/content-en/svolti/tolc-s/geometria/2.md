## Exercise 1

A figure consists of a square with side $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and an external semicircle whose diameter coincides with one square side. Find the area and outer perimeter.

::grafico{curve="3+3*cos(t),6+3*sin(t),0,3.141592653589793" linee="0,6 0,0 6,0 6,6" segmenti="0,6,6,6" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=10 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The shared diameter lies inside the composite figure"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=3\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
A&=6^2+\frac{\pi\cdot3^2}{2}=\left(36+\frac{9\pi}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{cm^2},\\[6pt]
P&=3\cdot6+\pi\cdot3=(18+3\pi)\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two parts have disjoint interiors, so their areas add. The square side is the semicircle’s diameter, so the radius used in the formula is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. For the perimeter, trace only the outer boundary: it includes three square sides and the semicircular arc. The fourth side coincides with the shared diameter and lies inside the figure, so it does not contribute to the boundary length. This distinction prevents simply adding the perimeters of the two separate figures, which would count the shared segment twice.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An open-top cylindrical container has internal radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. Ignoring thickness, find its capacity and the material area forming its bottom and wall.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\pi\cdot3^2\cdot8=72\pi\,\mathrm{cm^3},\\[6pt]
S_l&=2\pi\cdot3\cdot8=48\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2},\\[6pt]
S_b&=\pi\cdot3^2=9\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2},\\[6pt]
S&=48\pi+9\pi=57\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Capacity is a volume and is obtained by multiplying the base area by the height. The material area instead concerns the surface. Cutting the wall along a generator and unfolding it gives a rectangle with base equal to the circumference and height $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its area is added to only the circular bottom. The container is open, so there is no top surface. Units help keep the results distinct: cubic centimeters for capacity and square centimeters for covering. In liters, the capacity is exactly $\dfrac{72\pi}{1000}\,\mathrm{L}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

From a square sheet with side $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, four $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ squares are cut at the corners. Folding the four flaps makes an open-top box. Find its volume and the area of the remaining sheet.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b&=10-2\cdot2=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
V&=6^2\cdot2=72\,\mathrm{cm^3},\\[6pt]
S&=10^2-4\cdot2^2=84\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The box height is the side length of each removed square because that width is folded vertically. Each base side loses two $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ segments, one at each end. Thus the base is a square with side $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the volume follows by multiplying its area by the height. The remaining sheet includes the base and all four walls. A face-by-face check gives $6^2+4\cdot6\cdot2=36+48=84\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, equal to the initial area minus the four cutouts. Folding changes the sheet’s arrangement but preserves its area.

</div>
