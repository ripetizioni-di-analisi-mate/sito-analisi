## Exercise 1

A sample lies below the crust-mantle boundary and belongs to the rigid portion of a plate. Classify it by composition and mechanical behaviour. Another mantle volume deforms slowly over geological time: does this prove it is wholly liquid?

<div class="spiegazione">

Its position below the compositional boundary places the sample in the **mantle**. The rigid portion moving with the plate belongs to the **lithosphere**. These answers are compatible because the lithosphere includes crust and rigid uppermost mantle.

Slow deformation does not prove a wholly liquid state. A mostly solid material can deform under persistent stress for millions of years and transmit shear waves over short times. Classification therefore also depends on the behaviour and timescale observed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On both sides of a ridge, the seafloor moves away from the axis at $2\,\mathrm{cm/year}$ per side. Calculate the separation of two points initially at the axis after one million years, assuming constant speeds. State where the youngest rocks are found.

$$
v_{\mathrm{rel}}=2+2=4\,\mathrm{cm/year},
$$
$$
d=v_{\mathrm{rel}}t=4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{cm}
=\frac{4\cdot10^6}{10^5}\,\mathrm{km}=40\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The speeds add because the points move apart in opposite directions. One kilometre contains $10^5$ centimetres, so conversion gives **$40\,\mathrm{km}$**. Each point's distance from the axis would instead be $20\,\mathrm{km}$.

New crust forms near the axis, where the youngest basalts are found. More distant ones have had longer to travel. The numerical result matches this geometry and does not measure one plate's speed relative to a global reference frame.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compare two pathways: A, limestone recrystallises in the solid state near an intrusion; B, a rock melts and the melt cools. Classify the products and explain why heat alone does not justify giving both the same classification.

<div class="spiegazione">

Pathway A involves **contact metamorphism**: the rock may become marble while remaining solid during the stated change. In pathway B, solidification of a melt produces an **igneous rock**. The decisive distinction is whether melting occurred.

High temperature can operate in both cases. Classification therefore requires the full event sequence, distinguishing solid-state recrystallisation from crystallisation out of a liquid. Merely identifying a nearby heat source is insufficient.

</div>

## Exercise 4

In a cross-section with surface $z=0$, the hypocentre is $H=(18,-12)\,\mathrm{km}$ and a station is $P=(2,0)\,\mathrm{km}$. Find epicentre E and straight-line distance PH in the planar model.

$$
E=(18,0)\,\mathrm{km},\qquad
PH=\sqrt{(18-2)^2+(-12-0)^2}
=\sqrt{256+144}=20\,\mathrm{km}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Vertical projection keeps the horizontal coordinate and sets elevation to zero. The station is $16\,\mathrm{km}$ horizontally from the epicentre; depth is $12\,\mathrm{km}$. These segments are perpendicular, so Pythagoras gives **$PH=20\,\mathrm{km}$**. This hypocentral distance differs from the $16\,\mathrm{km}$ epicentral distance. Actual underground wave paths can curve; the question uses the stated straight-line model.
