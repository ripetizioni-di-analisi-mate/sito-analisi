## Exercise 1

An aquifer stores $30$ million cubic metres. Each year it receives $4$ million through recharge and loses $6$ million to pumping. Neglect other fluxes and keep these values constant. Determine storage after three years. Then distinguish infiltration from condensation.

$$
\Delta S=4-6=-2,\qquad S(3)=30+3(-2)=24.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All quantities in the equation are in millions of cubic metres, with changes referring to one year. Outputs are subtracted from inputs: **$24$ million cubic metres remain after three years**. The deficit is not $6$ million per year, because recharge offsets part of pumping. The linear relation describes only the interval over which the assumptions hold.

Infiltration is water entry into the ground and may contribute to recharge. Condensation instead changes vapour into liquid, as in cloud droplets. Movement and phase change describe different processes.

## Exercise 2

An observer climbs from sea level to a summit and measures lower pressure. Explain the role of the air column. Also explain why one cold week there does not by itself disprove a rise in multidecadal average temperature.

<div class="spiegazione">

Atmospheric pressure is related to the weight of overlying air per unit area. As the observer climbs, some atmosphere remains below: the upper column weighs less and pressure generally decreases. Gravity need not disappear and the air need not become pure oxygen.

The week describes an episode of **weather**. The multidecadal trend describes a statistical property of **climate**. A distribution can shift its mean while retaining variability and individual cold episodes; comparing trends requires representative time series.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Snow and dark ground each receive $100$ energy units. Snow reflects $80\%$ and ground $20\%$. Neglecting transmission, compare absorbed energy.

$$
E_{\mathrm{snow}}=100(1-0.80)=20,
\qquad E_{\mathrm{ground}}=100(1-0.20)=80,
$$
$$
E_{\mathrm{ground}}-E_{\mathrm{snow}}=80-20=60.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Albedo measures the reflected fraction. Under the stated conditions, the absorbed fraction is therefore one minus albedo. Dark ground absorbs **$60$ units more**, four times the snow's absorption. This compares the incident-radiation budget rather than directly calculating temperature: other energy exchanges would also be required for that.

</div>

## Exercise 4

An event has annual probability $2\%$ and, if it occurs, causes a loss of $300\,000$ euros; otherwise the loss is zero. Calculate expected annual loss. If strengthening buildings halves the event loss without changing its probability, what new expected loss results?

$$
L=0.02\cdot300\,000=6\,000\,\mathrm{euros},
$$
$$
L'=0.02\cdot150\,000=3\,000\,\mathrm{euros}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight the loss by the event probability. The zero loss in the other case contributes nothing to the average. Results are **$6\,000$ euros before and $3\,000$ euros after the measure**; these are not amounts necessarily paid every year.

Strengthening reduces building vulnerability. Hazard remains as assigned because probability and phenomenon have not changed. The model includes only one event type: a complete assessment may require different intensities and losses.

</div>
