## Exercise 1

Analyze the function $f(x) = x\sqrt{4 - x^2}$ and sketch its graph.

**Domain, symmetry, intercepts and sign**
$$
4 - x^2 \geq 0 \iff -2 \leq x \leq 2 ,
\qquad
D = [-2, 2]
$$
$$
f(-x) = -x\sqrt{4 - x^2} = -f(x) ,
\qquad
f(x) = 0 \iff x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = \pm 2
$$

**First derivative**, for $-2 < x < 2$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x)
&= \sqrt{4 - x^2} + x \cdot \frac{-2x}{2\sqrt{4 - x^2}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4 - x^2 - x^2}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2\,\left( 2 - x^2 \right)}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f'(x) > 0 \iff -\sqrt{2} < x < \sqrt{2} ,
\qquad
f\left( \sqrt{2} \right) = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 2 ,
\qquad
f\left( -\sqrt{2} \right) = -2
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 2^-} f'(x) = \lim_{x \to 2^-} \frac{2\,(2 - x^2)}{\sqrt{4 - x^2}} = -\infty
$$

**Second derivative**, for $-2 < x < 2$
$$
f''(x) = \frac{2x\,\left( x^2 - 6 \right)}{\left( 4 - x^2 \right)^{\frac{3}{2}}} ,
\qquad
f'(0) = 2
$$

The function has an absolute maximum of $2$ at $x = \sqrt{2}$ and an absolute minimum of $-2$ at $x = -\sqrt{2}$, an inflection point at the origin with tangent $y = 2x$, and vertical tangents at $(\pm 2, 0)$.

::grafico{f="x*sqrt(4-x^2);2*x" punti="1.414,2,pieno;-1.414,-2,pieno;0,0,vuoto;2,0,pieno;-2,0,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda="y = x\sqrt{4 - x^2};y = 2x" titolo="Graph of $f(x) = x\sqrt{4 - x^2}$ with a maximum at $\left( \sqrt{2}, 2 \right)$, a minimum at $\left( -\sqrt{2}, -2 \right)$ and an inflection point at the origin, where the tangent is $y = 2x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The square root is defined when the radicand is nonnegative, so the domain is the closed interval $[-2, 2]$. The domain is symmetric and $f(-x) = -f(x)$, so the function is **odd** and its graph is symmetric about the origin. The square root is positive for $|x| < 2$, so $f$ has the same sign as $x$. The domain is bounded and the function is continuous, so there are no asymptotes.

Use the product rule to find the first derivative. The derivative of $\sqrt{4 - x^2}$ is $\dfrac{-2x}{2\sqrt{4 - x^2}}$ by the chain rule. Combining the terms over a common denominator gives $4 - 2x^2$ in the numerator. The denominator is positive, so $f'$ has the sign of $2 - x^2$, which is positive for $|x| < \sqrt{2}$. The function decreases on $\left[ -2, -\sqrt{2} \right]$, increases on $\left[ -\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2} \right]$ and decreases on $\left[ \sqrt{2}, 2 \right]$.

The function is continuous on the closed interval $[-2, 2]$, so by the extreme value theorem it has an absolute maximum and minimum. Compare the values at the endpoints, $f(\pm 2) = 0$, with those at the stationary points, $f\left( \pm\sqrt{2} \right) = \pm 2$. The **absolute maximum** is therefore $2$ and the **absolute minimum** is $-2$.

As $x \to 2^-$, the numerator of $f'$ tends to $-4$ and the denominator tends to $0^+$, so $f'(x) \to -\infty$. By the differentiability criterion, the left-hand derivative at $2$ is $-\infty$, and the tangent at $(2, 0)$ is vertical. By symmetry, the same holds at $(-2, 0)$.

In the second derivative, the factor $x^2 - 6$ is negative throughout the domain because $x^2 \leq 4$. The denominator is positive, so $f''$ and $x$ have opposite signs. The graph is concave up for $x < 0$ and concave down for $x > 0$, and the origin is an **inflection point** with an oblique tangent of slope $f'(0) = \dfrac{2 \cdot 2}{2} = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Analyze the function $f(x) = x + 2\cos x$ on the interval $[0, 2\pi]$ and sketch its graph.

**Values at the endpoints**
$$
f(0) = 2 ,
\qquad
f(2\pi) = 2\pi + 2 \approx 8.28
$$

**First derivative**
$$
f'(x) = 1 - 2\sin x ,
\qquad
f'(x) = 0 \iff \sin x = \frac{1}{2} \iff x = \frac{\pi}{6} \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{5\pi}{6}
$$

| | $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{6} < x < \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ | $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} < x < 2\pi$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | increasing | decreasing | increasing |

$$
f\left( \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{\pi}{6} + \sqrt{3} \approx 2.26 ,
\qquad
f\left( \frac{5\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3} \approx 0.89
$$

**Second derivative**
$$
f''(x) = -2\cos x ,
\qquad
f''(x) > 0 \iff \cos x < 0 \iff \frac{\pi}{2} < x < \frac{3\pi}{2}
$$
$$
f\left( \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = \frac{\pi}{2} ,
\qquad
f\left( \frac{3\pi}{2} \right) = \frac{3\pi}{2}
$$

The function has a local maximum at $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \sqrt{3} \right)$ and a local minimum at $\left( \dfrac{5\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3} \right)$, which is also its absolute minimum. The absolute maximum is $2\pi + 2$ at the endpoint $x = 2\pi$. The inflection points are $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ and $\left( \dfrac{3\pi}{2}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right)$.

::grafico{f="x+2*cos(x)+0*sqrt(x*(6.2832-x))" punti="0,2,pieno;0.5236,2.2556,pieno;2.618,0.8859,pieno;1.5708,1.5708,vuoto;4.7124,4.7124,vuoto;6.2832,8.2832,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=9 titolo="Graph of $f(x) = x + 2\cos x$ on $[0, 2\pi]$ with a local maximum at horizontal coordinate $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, a minimum at horizontal coordinate $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and inflection points (open circles) with horizontal coordinates $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is continuous and differentiable on all of $\mathbb{R}$, and the interval under consideration is the closed interval $[0, 2\pi]$. The function is not periodic because $f(x + 2\pi) = f(x) + 2\pi$, and the exercise calls for its analysis only on $[0, 2\pi]$.

The first derivative vanishes when $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. On $[0, 2\pi]$ the solutions are $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. The derivative is positive when $\sin x < \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, outside the interval $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right)$. By the **first derivative test**, $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ is a point of local maximum and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ is a point of local minimum. To find their values, use $\cos \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

By the theorem on absolute maxima and minima on a closed interval, compare the values at the stationary points and the endpoints. These values are $2$, approximately $2.26$, approximately $0.89$ and approximately $8.28$. The **absolute maximum** is therefore $2\pi + 2$, attained at $x = 2\pi$, and the **absolute minimum** is $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} - \sqrt{3}$. Since the minimum is positive, the function is positive throughout the interval and its graph does not meet the $x$-axis.

The second derivative $-2\cos x$ is negative where the cosine is positive, that is, on $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$ and $\left( \dfrac{3\pi}{2}, 2\pi \right]$, and positive on $\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right)$. At $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ it changes sign, so these are **inflection points**. At both points the cosine equals $0$, so the inflection points lie on the angle bisector $y = x$. The tangents at these points have slopes $f'\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right) = -1$ and $f'\left( \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right) = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Analyze the function $f(x) = |x - 2|\,e^{x}$ and sketch its graph.

**Piecewise form**
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
(2 - x)\,e^{x} & \text{if } x < 2 \\
(x - 2)\,e^{x} & \text{if } x \geq 2
\end{cases}
$$

**Intercepts, sign and limits**
$$
f(0) = 2 ,
\qquad
f(x) = 0 \iff x = 2 ,
\qquad
f(x) \geq 0 \ \text{ for every } x
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} (2 - x)\,e^{x} = \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{2 - x}{e^{-x}} = 0 ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} (x - 2)\,e^{x} = +\infty
$$

**First derivative**
$$
f'(x) =
\begin{cases}
(1 - x)\,e^{x} & \text{if } x < 2 \\
(x - 1)\,e^{x} & \text{if } x > 2
\end{cases}
\qquad
f'_-(2) = -e^2 ,
\quad
f'_+(2) = e^2
$$
$$
f(1) = e \approx 2.72
$$

**Second derivative**
$$
f''(x) =
\begin{cases}
-x\,e^{x} & \text{if } x < 2 \\
x\,e^{x} & \text{if } x > 2
\end{cases}
\qquad
f'(0) = 1
$$

The function has horizontal asymptote $y = 0$ as $x \to -\infty$, a local maximum at $(1, e)$, an absolute minimum at the corner $(2, 0)$ and an inflection point at $(0, 2)$.

::grafico{f="abs(x-2)*exp(x)" punti="1,2.718,pieno;2,0,pieno;0,2,vuoto" asintoti="y=0" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=9 titolo="Graph of $f(x) = |x - 2|\,e^{x}$ with a local maximum at $(1, e)$, a corner at $(2, 0)$, an inflection point at $(0, 2)$ (open circle) and asymptote $y = 0$ as $x \to -\infty$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value $|x - 2|$ equals $2 - x$ for $x < 2$ and $x - 2$ for $x \geq 2$, so the function can be written in piecewise form. It is defined and continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and positive for $x \neq 2$ because it is the product of an absolute value and an exponential. The graph meets the $x$-axis only at $(2, 0)$ and the $y$-axis at $(0, 2)$.

As $x \to -\infty$, write $e^{x} = \dfrac{1}{e^{-x}}$. The denominator $e^{-x}$ grows faster than the numerator $2 - x$, so the limit is $0$ and the line $y = 0$ is a **horizontal asymptote** on the left. As $x \to +\infty$, the product tends to $+\infty$ and $\dfrac{f(x)}{x}$ also tends to $+\infty$, so there is no oblique asymptote.

On each piece, the product rule gives the derivative. For $x < 2$, the derivative is $-e^{x} + (2 - x)\,e^{x} = (1 - x)\,e^{x}$, which is positive for $x < 1$ and negative for $1 < x < 2$. For $x > 2$, we have $(x - 1)\,e^{x} > 0$. The function increases on $(-\infty, 1]$, decreases on $[1, 2]$ and increases on $[2, +\infty)$. Therefore, $x = 1$ is a point of **local maximum** and $x = 2$ is a point of minimum, which is absolute because $f(2) = 0$ and $f \geq 0$.

At $x = 2$ the function is continuous, and by the differentiability criterion the left-hand and right-hand derivatives are the limits of the two expressions for $f'$, namely $-e^2$ and $e^2$. These derivatives are finite and different, so $(2, 0)$ is a **corner**. This is the case described for functions of the form $|g(x)|$, because $g(x) = (x - 2)\,e^{x}$ vanishes at $2$ with $g'(2) = e^2 \neq 0$.

For $x < 2$, the second derivative $-x\,e^{x}$ is positive for $x < 0$ and negative for $0 < x < 2$, so it changes sign at $x = 0$ and the point $(0, 2)$ is an **inflection point** with a tangent of slope $f'(0) = 1$. For $x > 2$, the second derivative is positive and the graph is concave up. At $x = 2$ the concavity changes, but the function is not differentiable, so the point is a corner and is not an inflection point.

</div>
