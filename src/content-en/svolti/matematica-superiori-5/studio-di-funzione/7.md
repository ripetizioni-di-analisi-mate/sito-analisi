## Exercise 1

For each value of the real parameter $k$, find the number of real solutions of the equation $x^3 - 3x^2 = k$.

$$
f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 ,
\qquad
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x = 3x\,(x - 2)
$$

| | $x < 0$ | $0 < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | increasing | decreasing | increasing |

$$
f(0) = 0 ,
\qquad
f(2) = 8 - 12 = -4 ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = -\infty ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} f(x) = +\infty
$$

The equation has

- one solution if $k < -4$ or $k > 0$,
- two solutions if $k = -4$ or $k = 0$,
- three solutions if $-4 < k < 0$.

::grafico{f="x^3-3*x^2;-2" punti="0,0,pieno;2,-4,pieno" segmenti="-2,-4,4,-4" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-6 ymax=4 legenda="y = x^3 - 3x^2;y = -2" titolo="The line $y = k$ intersects the graph of $x^3 - 3x^2$ at three points for $-4 < k < 0$, as the line $y = -2$ illustrates, and at two points for $k = 0$ and for $k = -4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The solutions are the horizontal coordinates of the intersections of the graph of $y = x^3 - 3x^2$ with the horizontal line $y = k$. To count them, determine where $f$ increases and decreases. The sign of $f'$ gives the sign chart, and by the **first derivative test**, $f$ has a local maximum at $(0, 0)$ and a local minimum at $(2, -4)$.

The function increases from $-\infty$ to $0$ on $(-\infty, 0]$, decreases from $0$ to $-4$ on $[0, 2]$, and increases from $-4$ to $+\infty$ on $[2, +\infty)$. A horizontal line with $k$ strictly between $-4$ and $0$ therefore meets the graph once on each of the three intervals. If $k > 0$ or $k < -4$, the line meets only the increasing branch on the right or the left, respectively, so there is one solution. For $k = 0$, the line passes through the maximum and the distinct solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 3$ because $x^3 - 3x^2 = x^2\,(x - 3)$. For $k = -4$, the line passes through the minimum and the solutions are $x = 2$ and $x = -1$ because $x^3 - 3x^2 + 4 = (x - 2)^2 (x + 1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For each value of the real parameter $k$, find the number of solutions of the equation $e^{x} = kx$.

$$
x = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad e^{0} = 1 \neq 0
$$
$$
e^{x} = kx
\iff
\frac{e^{x}}{x} = k ,
\qquad
x \neq 0
$$
$$
g(x) = \frac{e^{x}}{x} ,
\qquad
g'(x) = \frac{e^{x}\,(x - 1)}{x^2}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} g(x) = 0^- ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to 0^-} g(x) = -\infty ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to 0^+} g(x) = +\infty ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} g(x) = +\infty ,
\qquad
g(1) = e
$$

The equation has

- one solution, which is negative, if $k < 0$,
- no solutions if $0 \leq k < e$,
- one solution, $x = 1$, if $k = e$,
- two solutions, both positive, if $k > e$.

::grafico{f="exp(x)/x;4" punti="1,2.718,pieno" asintoti="x=0;y=0" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-6 ymax=10 legenda="y = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x};y = 4" titolo="For $k > e$, for example $k = 4$, the line $y = k$ meets the graph of $\dfrac{e^{x}}{x}$ at two points with positive horizontal coordinates. For $k < 0$ it meets the graph at one point with a negative horizontal coordinate"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The value $x = 0$ is not a solution for any $k$ because the left-hand side equals $1$ and the right-hand side equals $0$. For $x \neq 0$, both sides can be divided by $x$, giving $g(x) = k$ with the parameter isolated on the right. The solutions are therefore the horizontal coordinates of the intersections of the graph of $g$ with the line $y = k$.

The function $g(x) = \dfrac{e^{x}}{x}$ was analyzed in the example on exponential functions. For $x < 0$ we have $g'(x) < 0$ because $x - 1 < 0$, so $g$ decreases from negative values close to $0$ to $-\infty$ and takes each negative value **exactly once**. For $x > 0$, the function decreases from $+\infty$ to $e$ on $(0, 1]$ and increases from $e$ to $+\infty$ on $[1, +\infty)$, so it attains its minimum value of $e$ at $x = 1$.

If $k < 0$, the line meets only the left branch, at one point. If $0 \leq k < e$, the line lies above the left branch, where the function is negative, and below the minimum of the right branch, so it does not meet the graph. If $k = e$, the line passes through the minimum and the solution is $x = 1$. In this case the line $y = ex$ is tangent to the graph of $e^{x}$ at $(1, e)$. If $k > e$, the line meets the right branch at two points, one with horizontal coordinate in $(0, 1)$ and the other in $(1, +\infty)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Prove that the equation $x^3 + x - 3 = 0$ has a unique real solution $\alpha$ and that $1 < \alpha < 2$. Narrow the interval using three steps of the bisection method, then approximate $\alpha$ using two steps of the tangent method.

$$
f(x) = x^3 + x - 3 ,
\qquad
f'(x) = 3x^2 + 1 > 0 ,
\qquad
f(1) = -1 ,
\qquad
f(2) = 7
$$

| step | interval | midpoint $m$ | $f(m)$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1$ | $[1, 2]$ | $1.5$ | $1.875 > 0$ |
| $2$ | $[1;\ 1.5]$ | $1.25$ | $0.203125 > 0$ |
| $3$ | $[1;\ 1.25]$ | $1.125$ | $\approx -0.451 < 0$ |

$$
1.125 < \alpha < 1.25
$$
$$
f''(x) = 6x > 0 ,
\qquad
x_0 = 1.25
$$
$$
x_1 = 1.25 - \frac{f(1.25)}{f'(1.25)} = 1.25 - \frac{0.203125}{5.6875} = 1.25 - \frac{1}{28} = \frac{17}{14} \approx 1.21429
$$
$$
x_2 = x_1 - \frac{f(x_1)}{f'(x_1)} \approx 1.21429 - \frac{0.00474}{5.42347} \approx 1.21341
$$

The equation has one solution between $1.125$ and $1.25$, and the tangent method gives $\alpha \approx 1.2134$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $f$ is a polynomial, so it is continuous on $[1, 2]$, and the values $f(1) = -1$ and $f(2) = 7$ have opposite signs. The derivative $3x^2 + 1$ is positive for every $x$. By the theorem on the **uniqueness of a zero**, the equation has exactly one solution in $(1, 2)$. Also, $f$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, so it has no other zeros outside this interval.

In the **bisection method**, evaluate $f$ at the midpoint and keep the half on which $f$ has opposite signs at the endpoints. Since $f(1) < 0$ and $f(1.5) > 0$, the solution lies in $[1;\ 1.5]$. Since $f(1.25) > 0$, it lies in $[1;\ 1.25]$. Finally, $f(1.125) = 1.423828\ldots + 1.125 - 3 < 0$, so the solution lies in $[1.125;\ 1.25]$, which has width $0.125$.

In the **tangent method**, start at the endpoint where $f$ and $f''$ have the same sign. On $[1.125;\ 1.25]$, the second derivative $6x$ is positive and $f(1.25) > 0$, so $x_0 = 1.25$. We have $f'(1.25) = 3 \cdot 1.5625 + 1 = 5.6875$, and the quotient $\dfrac{0.203125}{5.6875}$ equals exactly $\dfrac{1}{28}$. The second step, with $f(x_1) \approx 0.00474$ and $f'(x_1) \approx 5.42347$, gives $x_2 \approx 1.21341$. The value of $f(x_2)$ is less than $10^{-5}$. Also, $f(1.2134) < 0$ and $f(1.2135) > 0$, so by Bolzano's theorem $\alpha$ lies between $1.2134$ and $1.2135$, and its decimal expansion begins $1.2134$.

</div>
