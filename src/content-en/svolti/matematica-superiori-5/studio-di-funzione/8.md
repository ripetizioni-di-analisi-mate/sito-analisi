## Exercise 1

The figure shows the graph of the derivative $f'$ of a function $f$ that is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$. The graph of $f'$ is a parabola that meets the $x$-axis at the points with horizontal coordinates $-1$ and $3$ and the $y$-axis at $(0, -3)$.

::grafico{f="x^2-2*x-3" punti="-1,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;0,-3,pieno;1,-4,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=6 legenda="y = f'(x)" titolo="The graph of the derivative $f'$ is a parabola with zeros at $-1$ and $3$ that passes through $(0, -3)$ and has its vertex at $(1, -4)$"}

1. Use the graph to find the intervals on which $f$ increases or decreases and the horizontal coordinates of its local maxima and minima.
2. Use the graph to find the intervals of concavity of $f$ and the horizontal coordinate of its inflection point.
3. Given that $f$ is a cubic polynomial and $f(0) = 2$, find the expression for $f$ and the coordinates of the points identified.
4. Write the equation of the tangent at the inflection point and sketch the graph of $f$.

**Part 1**

| | $x < -1$ | $-1 < x < 3$ | $x > 3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | increasing | decreasing | increasing |

**Part 2**

| | $x < 1$ | $x > 1$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $f'$ | decreasing | increasing |
| concavity of $f$ | down | up |

**Part 3**
$$
f'(x) = (x + 1)(x - 3) = x^2 - 2x - 3
$$
$$
f(x) = ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d ,
\qquad
f'(x) = 3ax^2 + 2bx + c
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
3a = 1 \\
2b = -2 \\
c = -3 \\
d = f(0) = 2
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
f(x) = \frac{1}{3}\,x^3 - x^2 - 3x + 2
$$
$$
f(-1) = -\frac{1}{3} - 1 + 3 + 2 = \frac{11}{3} ,
\qquad
f(3) = 9 - 9 - 9 + 2 = -7 ,
\qquad
f(1) = \frac{1}{3} - 1 - 3 + 2 = -\frac{5}{3}
$$

**Part 4**
$$
f'(1) = -4 ,
\qquad
y + \frac{5}{3} = -4\,(x - 1)
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
y = -4x + \frac{7}{3}
$$

::grafico{f="x^3/3-x^2-3*x+2;-4*x+7/3" punti="-1,3.667,pieno;3,-7,pieno;1,-1.667,vuoto;0,2,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-10 ymax=10 legenda="y = \dfrac{1}{3}\,x^3 - x^2 - 3x + 2;y = -4x + \dfrac{7}{3}" titolo="Graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{3}\,x^3 - x^2 - 3x + 2$ with a maximum at $\left( -1, \dfrac{11}{3} \right)$, a minimum at $(3, -7)$ and an inflection point at $\left( 1, -\dfrac{5}{3} \right)$ with its tangent"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The graph of $f'$ lies above the $x$-axis for $x < -1$ and for $x > 3$ and below the $x$-axis between $-1$ and $3$. By the monotonicity test, $f$ increases on $(-\infty, -1]$ and $[3, +\infty)$ and decreases on $[-1, 3]$. By the **first derivative test**, $x = -1$ is a point of local maximum because $f'$ changes from positive to negative, and $x = 3$ is a point of local minimum.

**Part 2.** The concavity of $f$ depends on the sign of $f''$, the derivative of $f'$, and hence on whether $f'$ is increasing or decreasing. The vertex of the parabola lies at $x = 1$, so $f'$ decreases for $x < 1$ and increases for $x > 1$. The graph of $f$ is therefore concave down for $x < 1$ and concave up for $x > 1$. At $x = 1$ the derivative $f'$ has a minimum and the concavity changes, so $x = 1$ is the **inflection point** of $f$.

**Part 3.** The zeros of the parabola are $-1$ and $3$, so $f'(x) = a'\,(x + 1)(x - 3)$ for some number $a'$. Since the graph passes through $(0, -3)$, we have $a' \cdot 1 \cdot (-3) = -3$, that is, $a' = 1$. The derivative of the general cubic polynomial must equal $x^2 - 2x - 3$, so the coefficients of like powers of $x$ are equal. The constant term $d$ follows from $f(0) = 2$. The four conditions hold simultaneously and give $a = \dfrac{1}{3}$, $b = -1$, $c = -3$ and $d = 2$. Substituting $-1$, $3$ and $1$ into the function gives the vertical coordinates.

**Part 4.** The slope of the tangent at the inflection point is $f'(1)$, the vertical coordinate of the vertex of the parabola, namely $-4$. The tangent passes through $\left( 1, -\dfrac{5}{3} \right)$ and its constant term is $-\dfrac{5}{3} + 4 = \dfrac{7}{3}$. Sketch the graph of $f$ using the points found, the value $f(0) = 2$, and the limits $f(x) \to \pm\infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$, which follow because the coefficient of $x^3$ is positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Consider the function $f(x) = ax + \dfrac{b}{x}$, where $a$ and $b$ are real parameters.

1. Find $a$ and $b$ so that $f$ has a local minimum at $(1, 4)$.
2. Using these values, analyze $f$ and sketch its graph.
3. For each value of the real parameter $k$, find the number of solutions of the equation $f(x) = k$.
4. Find the points on the graph where the tangent is parallel to the line $y = -6x$ and write the equations of those tangents.

**Part 1**
$$
f'(x) = a - \frac{b}{x^2}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
f(1) = 4 \\
f'(1) = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\begin{cases}
a + b = 4 \\
a - b = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
a = b = 2
$$
$$
f(x) = 2x + \frac{2}{x} = \frac{2\,\left( x^2 + 1 \right)}{x}
$$

**Part 2**
$$
D = (-\infty, 0) \cup (0, +\infty) ,
\qquad
f(-x) = -f(x)
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0^{\pm}} f(x) = \pm\infty ,
\qquad
m = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{f(x)}{x} = 2 ,
\qquad
q = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \big( f(x) - 2x \big) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2}{x} = 0
$$
$$
f'(x) = 2 - \frac{2}{x^2} = \frac{2\,(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x^2} ,
\qquad
f(1) = 4 ,
\qquad
f(-1) = -4
$$
$$
f''(x) = \frac{4}{x^3}
$$

**Part 3**

- two solutions if $k < -4$ or $k > 4$,
- one solution if $k = -4$ or $k = 4$,
- no solutions if $-4 < k < 4$.

**Part 4**
$$
f'(x) = -6
\iff
2 - \frac{2}{x^2} = -6
\iff
x^2 = \frac{1}{4}
\iff
x = \pm\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
f\left( \frac{1}{2} \right) = 1 + 4 = 5 ,
\qquad
f\left( -\frac{1}{2} \right) = -5
$$
$$
y - 5 = -6\left( x - \frac{1}{2} \right) \ \Longrightarrow \ y = -6x + 8 ,
\qquad
y + 5 = -6\left( x + \frac{1}{2} \right) \ \Longrightarrow \ y = -6x - 8
$$

::grafico{f="2*x+2/x;2*x" punti="1,4,pieno;-1,-4,pieno" asintoti="x=0" xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-12 ymax=12 legenda="y = 2x + \dfrac{2}{x};y = 2x" titolo="Graph of $f(x) = 2x + \dfrac{2}{x}$ with vertical asymptote $x = 0$, oblique asymptote $y = 2x$, local maximum at $(-1, -4)$ and local minimum at $(1, 4)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** If $(1, 4)$ is a point of local minimum, then the graph passes through that point, so $f(1) = 4$. Also, $x = 1$ is an interior point of the domain where $f$ is differentiable, so Fermat's theorem gives $f'(1) = 0$. The two conditions hold simultaneously and form a system. Adding the equations gives $2a = 4$, that is, $a = 2$, and hence $b = 2$. These conditions are only necessary. The analysis in Part 2 confirms that $x = 1$ is indeed a point of minimum.

**Part 2.** The function is defined for $x \neq 0$ and is **odd** because both terms change sign when $x$ changes sign. The numerator $2\,\left( x^2 + 1 \right)$ is always positive, so $f$ has the sign of $x$ and the graph meets neither axis. As $x \to 0^+$, the term $\dfrac{2}{x}$ tends to $+\infty$, and as $x \to 0^-$ it tends to $-\infty$, so $x = 0$ is a vertical asymptote. The difference $f(x) - 2x = \dfrac{2}{x}$ tends to $0$, so $y = 2x$ is the oblique asymptote.

The first derivative has the sign of $x^2 - 1$ because the denominator $x^2$ is positive. The function increases for $x < -1$ and for $x > 1$ and decreases on $[-1, 0)$ and $(0, 1]$. By the first derivative test, $x = -1$ is a point of **local maximum** and $x = 1$ is a point of **local minimum**, as required. The second derivative $\dfrac{4}{x^3}$ has the same sign as $x$, so the graph is concave down for $x < 0$ and concave up for $x > 0$. There are no inflection points because $0$ lies outside the domain.

**Part 3.** The solutions are the horizontal coordinates of the intersections of the graph with the line $y = k$. For $x > 0$, the function decreases from $+\infty$ to $4$ and then increases from $4$ to $+\infty$, so it takes each value greater than $4$ twice and the value $4$ once. For $x < 0$, by symmetry, it takes each value less than $-4$ twice and the value $-4$ once. It never takes any value strictly between $-4$ and $4$, so for these values of $k$ the equation **has no solutions**.

**Part 4.** Two lines are parallel if they have the same slope, so we look for points where $f'(x) = -6$. The equation $2 - \dfrac{2}{x^2} = -6$ is equivalent to $\dfrac{2}{x^2} = 8$, that is, to $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The points of tangency are $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 5 \right)$ and $\left( -\dfrac{1}{2}, -5 \right)$. They are symmetric about the origin because the function is odd, and the constant terms in the two tangent line equations are opposites.

</div>
