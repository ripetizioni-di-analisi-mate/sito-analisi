## Exercise 1

Analyze the function $f(x) = x^4 - 2x^2$ and sketch its graph.

**Domain and symmetry**
$$
D = \mathbb{R} ,
\qquad
f(-x) = (-x)^4 - 2\,(-x)^2 = x^4 - 2x^2 = f(x)
$$

**Intercepts and sign**
$$
f(x) = x^2\,\left( x^2 - 2 \right) = 0
\iff
x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = \pm\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
f(x) > 0
\iff
x^2 - 2 > 0
\iff
x < -\sqrt{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x > \sqrt{2}
$$

**Limits**
$$
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \left( x^4 - 2x^2 \right) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} x^4 \left( 1 - \frac{2}{x^2} \right) = +\infty
$$

**First derivative**
$$
f'(x) = 4x^3 - 4x = 4x\,(x - 1)(x + 1)
$$

| | $x < -1$ | $-1 < x < 0$ | $0 < x < 1$ | $x > 1$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f'(x)$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | decreasing | increasing | decreasing | increasing |

$$
f(\pm 1) = 1 - 2 = -1 ,
\qquad
f(0) = 0
$$

**Second derivative**
$$
f''(x) = 12x^2 - 4 = 4\,\left( 3x^2 - 1 \right)
$$
$$
f''(x) > 0
\iff
x < -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad x > \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} ,
\qquad
f\left( \pm\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3} \right) = \frac{1}{9} - \frac{2}{3} = -\frac{5}{9}
$$

The function has absolute minima at $(\pm 1, -1)$, a local maximum at $(0, 0)$ and two inflection points at $\left( \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}, -\dfrac{5}{9} \right)$.

::grafico{f="x^4-2*x^2" punti="-1,-1,pieno;1,-1,pieno;0,0,pieno;0.577,-0.556,vuoto;-0.577,-0.556,vuoto;1.414,0,pieno;-1.414,0,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2 ymax=4 titolo="Graph of $f(x) = x^4 - 2x^2$ with minima at $(\pm 1, -1)$, a local maximum at $(0, 0)$ and inflection points (open circles) with horizontal coordinates $\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is defined, continuous and differentiable on all of $\mathbb{R}$. It contains only even powers of $x$, so it is **even** and its graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis. To find the intercepts with the $x$-axis, factor out $x^2$. The factor $x^2$ is positive for $x \neq 0$, so $f$ has the same sign as $x^2 - 2$. The function is positive for $|x| > \sqrt{2}$ and negative for $0 < |x| < \sqrt{2}$. At $x = 0$ the graph touches the $x$-axis without crossing it.

Factoring out the highest power gives the limits as $x \to \pm\infty$, both of which are $+\infty$. A polynomial of degree greater than one has neither horizontal nor oblique asymptotes because $\dfrac{f(x)}{x}$ also tends to infinity.

The first derivative factors as $4x\,\left( x^2 - 1 \right) = 4x\,(x - 1)(x + 1)$. The sign of the product of the three factors gives the sign chart. By the **first derivative test**, the function has local minima at $x = \pm 1$ and a local maximum at $x = 0$. The minima are also absolute because $f(x) = \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^2 - 1 \geq -1$ for every $x$.

The second derivative is a trinomial with roots $\pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, positive outside the interval between them. The graph is therefore concave up for $|x| > \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ and concave down for $|x| < \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$. At both points $f''$ changes sign, so they are **inflection points**. To find their vertical coordinate, use $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $x^4 = \dfrac{1}{9}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Analyze the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 3}{x - 2}$ and sketch its graph.

**Domain, symmetry and intercepts**
$$
D = (-\infty, 2) \cup (2, +\infty) ,
\qquad
f(0) = \frac{-3}{-2} = \frac{3}{2} ,
\qquad
f(x) = 0 \iff x = \pm\sqrt{3}
$$

**Sign**

| | $x < -\sqrt{3}$ | $-\sqrt{3} < x < \sqrt{3}$ | $\sqrt{3} < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x^2 - 3$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x - 2$ | $-$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f(x)$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

**Limits and asymptotes**
$$
\lim_{x \to 2^-} \frac{x^2 - 3}{x - 2} = -\infty ,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to 2^+} \frac{x^2 - 3}{x - 2} = +\infty
$$
$$
m = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x^2 - 3}{x\,(x - 2)} = 1 ,
\qquad
q = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \left( \frac{x^2 - 3}{x - 2} - x \right) = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{2x - 3}{x - 2} = 2
$$

**First derivative**
$$
f'(x) = \frac{2x\,(x - 2) - \left( x^2 - 3 \right)}{(x - 2)^2} = \frac{x^2 - 4x + 3}{(x - 2)^2} = \frac{(x - 1)(x - 3)}{(x - 2)^2}
$$

| | $x < 1$ | $1 < x < 2$ | $2 < x < 3$ | $x > 3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | increasing | decreasing | decreasing | increasing |

$$
f(1) = \frac{1 - 3}{1 - 2} = 2 ,
\qquad
f(3) = \frac{9 - 3}{3 - 2} = 6
$$

**Second derivative**
$$
f''(x) = \frac{2}{(x - 2)^3}
$$

The function has vertical asymptote $x = 2$, oblique asymptote $y = x + 2$, a local maximum at $(1, 2)$ and a local minimum at $(3, 6)$. The graph is concave down for $x < 2$ and concave up for $x > 2$, and there are no inflection points.

::grafico{f="(x^2-3)/(x-2);x+2" punti="1,2,pieno;3,6,pieno;0,1.5,pieno;1.732,0,pieno;-1.732,0,pieno" asintoti="x=2" xmin=-4 xmax=8 ymin=-6 ymax=12 legenda="y = \dfrac{x^2 - 3}{x - 2};y = x + 2" titolo="Graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 3}{x - 2}$ with vertical asymptote $x = 2$, oblique asymptote $y = x + 2$, local maximum at $(1, 2)$ and local minimum at $(3, 6)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator vanishes at $x = 2$, which is excluded from the domain. The domain is not symmetric about $0$, so the function is neither even nor odd. The graph meets the $y$-axis at $\left( 0, \dfrac{3}{2} \right)$ and the $x$-axis where the numerator is zero, that is, at $x = \pm\sqrt{3}$. The sign of $f$ follows from the sign chart for the numerator and denominator.

As $x \to 2$, the numerator tends to $4 - 3 = 1$ and the denominator tends to $0$ through negative values from the left and positive values from the right. Therefore, $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to 2^-$ and $f(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to 2^+$, and the line $x = 2$ is a **vertical asymptote**. The degree of the numerator exceeds that of the denominator by one, so use the formulas for $m$ and $q$ to find an oblique asymptote. To calculate $q$, combine the terms over a common denominator, leaving $x^2 - 3 - x^2 + 2x = 2x - 3$ in the numerator. The oblique asymptote is $y = x + 2$ on both the right and the left.

The **quotient rule** gives the first derivative. The denominator $(x - 2)^2$ is positive on the domain, so $f'$ has the same sign as the trinomial $(x - 1)(x - 3)$, positive outside the interval $[1, 3]$ and negative inside it. By the first derivative test, $x = 1$ is a point of local maximum and $x = 3$ is a point of local minimum.

Differentiating $f'(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4x + 3}{(x - 2)^2}$ by the quotient rule and canceling a factor of $x - 2$ leaves $(2x - 4)(x - 2) - 2\,\left( x^2 - 4x + 3 \right) = 2$ in the numerator. Therefore, $f''$ has the same sign as $(x - 2)^3$, that is, as $x - 2$. Concavity changes at $x = 2$, which is outside the domain, so the function **has no inflection points**. As a check, the graph lies below the oblique asymptote for $x < 2$ and above it for $x > 2$ because $f(x) - (x + 2) = \dfrac{1}{x - 2}$.

</div>
