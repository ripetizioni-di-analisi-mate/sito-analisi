## Exercise 1

Find the intervals on which the function $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 1$ is increasing and those on which it is decreasing.

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 3\left( x^2 - 2x - 3 \right) = 3\,(x + 1)(x - 3)
$$

| | $x < -1$ | $-1 < x < 3$ | $x > 3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | increasing | decreasing | increasing |

$$
f(-1) = -1 - 3 + 9 + 1 = 6,
\qquad
f(3) = 27 - 27 - 27 + 1 = -26
$$

The function is increasing on $(-\infty, -1]$ and on $[3, +\infty)$ and decreasing on $[-1, 3]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$, and its intervals of increase and decrease can be found from the sign of its derivative. The derivative is a quadratic trinomial. Factoring out $3$ leaves $x^2 - 2x - 3$, whose roots are $-1$ and $3$, so it factors as $(x + 1)(x - 3)$.

The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, so the trinomial is positive outside the interval between the roots and negative between the roots. By the **monotonicity test**, $f$ is increasing where $f' > 0$ and decreasing where $f' < 0$. The endpoints $-1$ and $3$ are included in the intervals because $f$ is continuous.

The function increases up to $x = -1$ and then decreases, so $(-1, 6)$ is a local maximum point. Likewise, $(3, -26)$ is a local minimum point because the function decreases to the left of $3$ and increases to its right.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the intervals on which the function $f(x) = x^2 e^{-x}$ increases or decreases.

$$
f'(x) = 2x\,e^{-x} + x^2 \cdot \left( -e^{-x} \right) = \left( 2x - x^2 \right) e^{-x} = x\,(2 - x)\,e^{-x}
$$

| | $x < 0$ | $0 < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $2 - x$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ |
| $f'(x)$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ |
| $f$ | decreasing | increasing | decreasing |

$$
f(0) = 0,
\qquad
f(2) = \frac{4}{e^2} \approx 0.54
$$

The function is decreasing on $(-\infty, 0]$ and on $[2, +\infty)$ and increasing on $[0, 2]$.

::grafico{f="x^2*exp(-x)" punti="0,0,pieno;2,0.5413,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,0.5413" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="The function $f(x) = x^2 e^{-x}$ decreases up to $x = 0$, increases on $[0, 2]$, and decreases for $x \geq 2$. The point $\left( 2, \dfrac{4}{e^2} \right)$ is a local maximum"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is the product of $x^2$ and $e^{-x}$, so differentiate it using the product rule. The derivative of $e^{-x}$ is $-e^{-x}$ by the chain rule. Factoring out $e^{-x}$ and then $x$ gives $f'(x) = x\,(2 - x)\,e^{-x}$.

The factor $e^{-x}$ is positive for every $x$, so the sign of $f'$ is the sign of the product $x\,(2 - x)$. The table gives the signs of the two factors and, by the sign rule, the sign of the product. By the **monotonicity test**, $f$ decreases on $(-\infty, 0]$, increases on $[0, 2]$, and decreases on $[2, +\infty)$.

It follows that $x = 0$ is a local minimum point and $x = 2$ is a local maximum point. The minimum $f(0) = 0$ is also the smallest value of $f$ because $x^2 e^{-x} \geq 0$ for every $x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the intervals on which the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{x - 1}$ increases or decreases.

$$
D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}
$$
$$
f'(x) = \frac{2x\,(x - 1) - x^2 \cdot 1}{(x - 1)^2} = \frac{x^2 - 2x}{(x - 1)^2} = \frac{x\,(x - 2)}{(x - 1)^2}
$$

| | $x < 0$ | $0 < x < 1$ | $1 < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f'(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f$ | increasing | decreasing | decreasing | increasing |

$$
f(0) = 0,
\qquad
f(2) = \frac{4}{1} = 4
$$

The function is increasing on $(-\infty, 0]$ and on $[2, +\infty)$ and decreasing on $[0, 1)$ and on $(1, 2]$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is zero at $x = 1$, so the domain is $\mathbb{R}$ with $1$ removed. Differentiate using the quotient rule. In the numerator, $2x\,(x - 1) - x^2 = x^2 - 2x = x\,(x - 2)$, and the denominator $(x - 1)^2$ is positive throughout the domain. Thus the sign of $f'$ is the sign of $x\,(x - 2)$, which is positive for $x < 0$ and for $x > 2$ and negative between $0$ and $2$, excluding $1$.

The domain is not an interval, so the **monotonicity test** applies separately on each interval in the domain. The function is decreasing on $[0, 1)$ and on $(1, 2]$, but it is *not* decreasing on their union. For example, $f\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $f\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right) = \dfrac{9}{2}$, and the value at $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is greater than the value at $\dfrac{1}{2}$. This happens because the function has a vertical asymptote at $x = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the values of the parameter $k$ for which the function $f(x) = x^3 + kx^2 + 3x + 1$ is increasing throughout $\mathbb{R}$.

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 + 2kx + 3
$$
$$
\frac{\Delta}{4} = k^2 - 9
$$
$$
f'(x) \geq 0 \text{ for every } x
\iff
\frac{\Delta}{4} \leq 0
\iff
k^2 \leq 9
\iff
-3 \leq k \leq 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$. Its derivative is a quadratic trinomial in which the coefficient of $x^2$ is positive and equals $3$. Such a trinomial is positive for every $x$ when its discriminant is negative. With $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = k^2 - 3 \cdot 3 = k^2 - 9$ this happens for $-3 < k < 3$, and by the **monotonicity test** $f$ is then increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.

For $k = 3$ and for $k = -3$ the discriminant is zero, and the derivative vanishes at a single point. For $k = 3$ we have $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 6x + 3 = 3\,(x + 1)^2$, which is zero only at $x = -1$ and positive elsewhere. The test still applies when the derivative is zero at finitely many points, so $f$ is increasing in this case as well. The same holds for $k = -3$.

For $k^2 > 9$ the trinomial has two distinct roots and is negative between them. On that interval $f$ is decreasing, so $f$ **is not increasing** on $\mathbb{R}$. The required values are therefore those satisfying $-3 \leq k \leq 3$.

</div>
