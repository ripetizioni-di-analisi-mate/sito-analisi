## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int 4x \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^3 dx$.

$$
g(x) = x^2 - 1,
\qquad
g'(x) = 2x,
\qquad
4x = 2 \cdot 2x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int 4x \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^3 dx
&= 2 \int \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^3 \cdot 2x\,dx \\[6pt]
&= 2 \cdot \frac{\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^4}{4} + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^4}{2} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand contains a power of $g(x) = x^2 - 1$, and the factor $4x$ is a multiple of the derivative $g'(x) = 2x$. We write $4x = 2 \cdot 2x$ and use linearity to take the constant $2$ outside the integral. This leaves the integral of $g(x)^3 \cdot g'(x)$, which by the rule for **powers of a composite function** is $\dfrac{g(x)^4}{4} + c$.

We check by differentiating. The derivative of $\dfrac{\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^4}{2}$ is $\dfrac{4 \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^3 \cdot 2x}{2} = 4x \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^3$, the integrand. Expanding the cube and integrating the polynomial would give the same result up to a constant, but the calculation would be longer.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^2}{x^3 + 8}\,dx$ for $x \neq -2$.

$$
g(x) = x^3 + 8,
\qquad
g'(x) = 3x^2
$$
$$
\int \frac{x^2}{x^3 + 8}\,dx = \frac{1}{3} \int \frac{3x^2}{x^3 + 8}\,dx = \frac{1}{3} \ln \left\lvert x^3 + 8 \right\rvert + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator $x^2$ is the derivative of the denominator $x^3 + 8$ up to the factor $3$. We multiply the numerator by $3$ and divide the integral by $3$ so that the value of the expression stays the same. The resulting integral has the form $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx$ and therefore equals $\ln \lvert g(x) \rvert + c$.

The absolute value is necessary because $x^3 + 8$ is negative for $x < -2$. The point $x = -2$ is excluded because the denominator vanishes there.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{(\ln x)^2}{x}\,dx$ for $x > 0$.

$$
g(x) = \ln x,
\qquad
g'(x) = \frac{1}{x}
$$
$$
\int \frac{(\ln x)^2}{x}\,dx = \int (\ln x)^2 \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \frac{(\ln x)^3}{3} + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Rewrite the fraction as the product of $(\ln x)^2$ and $\dfrac{1}{x}$. The factor $\dfrac{1}{x}$ is the derivative of $\ln x$, so the integral has the form $\displaystyle\int g(x)^2 \cdot g'(x)\,dx$ with $g(x) = \ln x$. By the power rule for integrating a composite function, the result is $\dfrac{(\ln x)^3}{3} + c$.

To check, the derivative of $\dfrac{(\ln x)^3}{3}$ is $\dfrac{3 (\ln x)^2}{3} \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{(\ln x)^2}{x}$ by the chain rule.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}\,dx$ for $-1 < x < 1$.

$$
x^4 = \left( x^2 \right)^2,
\qquad
g(x) = x^2,
\qquad
g'(x) = 2x
$$
$$
\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{2x}{\sqrt{1 - \left( x^2 \right)^2}}\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \arcsin \left( x^2 \right) + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root $\sqrt{1 - x^4}$ has the same form as the denominator in the derivative of the arcsine, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 - t^2}}$, because $x^4$ is the square of $x^2$. We therefore choose $g(x) = x^2$, whose derivative is $2x$. The numerator $x$ is half of $2x$, so we multiply and divide by $2$.

The resulting integral has the form $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{g'(x)}{\sqrt{1 - g(x)^2}}\,dx$, which equals $\arcsin g(x) + c$. The result is therefore $\dfrac{1}{2} \arcsin \left( x^2 \right) + c$. The condition $-1 < x < 1$ ensures $x^4 < 1$, which means that the radicand is positive.

</div>
