## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{5}{2x - 3}\,dx$ for $x \neq \dfrac{3}{2}$.

$$
\int \frac{5}{2x - 3}\,dx = \frac{5}{2} \int \frac{2}{2x - 3}\,dx = \frac{5}{2} \ln \lvert 2x - 3 \rvert + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is a constant, and the denominator is linear with derivative $2$. We take the factor $5$ outside, then multiply and divide by $2$ so that the numerator is the derivative of the denominator. The integral of $\dfrac{2}{2x - 3}$ is therefore $\ln \lvert 2x - 3 \rvert$, and the result is $\dfrac{5}{2} \ln \lvert 2x - 3 \rvert + c$.

The same result follows from the formula $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{k}{m x + q}\,dx = \dfrac{k}{m} \ln \lvert m x + q \rvert + c$ with $k = 5$, $m = 2$, and $q = -3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{4x - 1}{x + 2}\,dx$ for $x \neq -2$.

$$
4x - 1 = 4\,(x + 2) - 9
$$
$$
\frac{4x - 1}{x + 2} = \frac{4\,(x + 2) - 9}{x + 2} = 4 - \frac{9}{x + 2}
$$
$$
\int \frac{4x - 1}{x + 2}\,dx = \int \left( 4 - \frac{9}{x + 2} \right) dx = 4x - 9 \ln \lvert x + 2 \rvert + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator and denominator have the same degree, so we divide first. With two linear polynomials, we can do the division mentally. Write the numerator as a multiple of the denominator plus a constant. The coefficient of $x$ in the numerator is $4$, so we start with $4\,(x + 2) = 4x + 8$. To obtain $4x - 1$, subtract $9$.

The fraction then splits into the quotient $4$ and the term $-\dfrac{9}{x + 2}$. The integral of the quotient is $4x$, and the integral of $\dfrac{1}{x + 2}$ is $\ln \lvert x + 2 \rvert$ because the numerator $1$ is the derivative of the denominator.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 1}{x - 1}\,dx$ for $x \neq 1$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 & 1 & 2 & -1 & 1 \\
1 & & 1 & 3 & 2 \\
\hline
 & 1 & 3 & 2 & 3
\end{array}
\qquad
Q(x) = x^2 + 3x + 2,
\quad
R = 3
$$
$$
\frac{x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 1}{x - 1} = x^2 + 3x + 2 + \frac{3}{x - 1}
$$
$$
\int \frac{x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 1}{x - 1}\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{3}{2}\,x^2 + 2x + 3 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator has degree $3$ and the denominator has degree $1$, so we divide. The divisor has the form $x - 1$, so we use **synthetic division** with $1$. The coefficients of the numerator are $1$, $2$, $-1$, and $1$. Bring down the first coefficient $1$. Multiply it by $1$ and add the product to $2$ to obtain $3$. The next steps give $3 \cdot 1 - 1 = 2$ and finally $2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3$, the remainder.

The quotient is therefore $x^2 + 3x + 2$ and the remainder is $3$. As a check, the remainder on division by $x - 1$ is the value of the numerator at $x = 1$, namely $1 + 2 - 1 + 1 = 3$. Integrate the quotient term by term. The term $\dfrac{3}{x - 1}$ gives $3 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{2x^2 + 1}{2x + 1}\,dx$ for $x \neq -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

$$
\begin{array}{rrr|l}
2x^2 & & +1 & 2x + 1 \\
\hline
-2x^2 & -x & & x - \dfrac{1}{2} \\
 & -x & +1 & \\
 & x & +\dfrac{1}{2} & \\
\hline
 & & \dfrac{3}{2} &
\end{array}
$$
$$
\frac{2x^2 + 1}{2x + 1} = x - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{3}{2\,(2x + 1)}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \frac{2x^2 + 1}{2x + 1}\,dx
&= \int \left( x - \frac{1}{2} \right) dx + \frac{3}{2} \int \frac{1}{2x + 1}\,dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x}{2} + \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \ln \lvert 2x + 1 \rvert + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x}{2} + \frac{3}{4} \ln \lvert 2x + 1 \rvert + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator has degree $2$, higher than that of the denominator, so we perform polynomial division. The first term of the quotient is $\dfrac{2x^2}{2x} = x$. Multiplying $x$ by $2x + 1$ and subtracting gives the intermediate remainder $-x + 1$. The second term of the quotient is $\dfrac{-x}{2x} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Multiplying $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ by $2x + 1$ and subtracting gives the remainder $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

As a check, $(2x + 1)\left( x - \dfrac{1}{2} \right) + \dfrac{3}{2} = 2x^2 - x + x - \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{3}{2} = 2x^2 + 1$. Integrate the quotient as a polynomial. In the integral of $\dfrac{1}{2x + 1}$, the denominator has derivative $2$, so the antiderivative of the integrand is $\dfrac{1}{2} \ln \lvert 2x + 1 \rvert$ by the formula for a **linear inner function**.

</div>
