## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \left( 6x^2 - 4x + 3 \right) dx$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \left( 6x^2 - 4x + 3 \right) dx
&= 6 \int x^2\,dx - 4 \int x\,dx + \int 3\,dx \\[6pt]
&= 6 \cdot \frac{x^3}{3} - 4 \cdot \frac{x^2}{2} + 3x + c \\[6pt]
&= 2x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x + c
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\left( 2x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x + c \right)' = 6x^2 - 4x + 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By **linearity**, the integral of the sum is the sum of the integrals, and the constant factors $6$ and $-4$ can be taken outside the integral. We integrate each power using the formula $\displaystyle\int x^{a}\,dx = \dfrac{x^{a + 1}}{a + 1} + c$, which applies because the exponents $2$ and $1$ both differ from $-1$. The integral of the constant $3$ is $3x$. The constants of integration from the three terms combine into a single constant $c$.

We check by differentiating the result. The derivative of $2x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x + c$ is $6x^2 - 4x + 3$, the integrand, so the result is **correct**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{x^3 - 2x + 1}{x^2}\,dx$ for $x \neq 0$.

$$
\frac{x^3 - 2x + 1}{x^2} = \frac{x^3}{x^2} - \frac{2x}{x^2} + \frac{1}{x^2} = x - \frac{2}{x} + x^{-2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \frac{x^3 - 2x + 1}{x^2}\,dx
&= \int x\,dx - 2 \int \frac{1}{x}\,dx + \int x^{-2}\,dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x^2}{2} - 2 \ln \lvert x \rvert + \frac{x^{-1}}{-1} + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x^2}{2} - 2 \ln \lvert x \rvert - \frac{1}{x} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is a quotient, and there is no direct quotient rule for integration. The denominator is the single monomial $x^2$, so dividing each term of the numerator by $x^2$ gives a sum of powers. We write the term $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ as a power with a negative exponent, $x^{-2}$, so that the power rule applies.

The term $-\dfrac{2}{x}$ contains a power with exponent $-1$, for which the power rule does *not* apply. Its integral is $-2 \ln \lvert x \rvert$, with the absolute value because the function is also defined for $x < 0$. For the power $x^{-2}$, on the other hand, we obtain $\dfrac{x^{-2 + 1}}{-2 + 1} = -x^{-1} = -\dfrac{1}{x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{(x + 1)^2}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx$ for $x > 0$.

$$
\frac{(x + 1)^2}{\sqrt{x}} = \frac{x^2 + 2x + 1}{x^{\frac{1}{2}}} = x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2x^{\frac{1}{2}} + x^{-\frac{1}{2}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \frac{(x + 1)^2}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx
&= \frac{x^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\frac{5}{2}} + 2 \cdot \frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}} + \frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{2}} + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{5}\,x^{\frac{5}{2}} + \frac{4}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2\,x^{\frac{1}{2}} + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{5}\,x^2\sqrt{x} + \frac{4}{3}\,x\sqrt{x} + 2\sqrt{x} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First expand the square of the binomial, $(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$, and write the root as a power, $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$. Divide each term of the numerator by $x^{\frac{1}{2}}$ by subtracting the exponents. The resulting exponents are $2 - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$, $1 - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and $0 - \dfrac{1}{2} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

Apply the formula $\displaystyle\int x^{a}\,dx = \dfrac{x^{a + 1}}{a + 1} + c$ to each power. Dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal, so dividing by $\dfrac{5}{2}$ means multiplying by $\dfrac{2}{5}$. In the last line, rewrite the powers with fractional exponents as roots, for example, $x^{\frac{5}{2}} = x^2 \cdot x^{\frac{1}{2}} = x^2\sqrt{x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \tan^2 x\,dx$ for $x \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$.

$$
\tan^2 x = \frac{\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{1 - \cos^2 x}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{1}{\cos^2 x} - 1
$$
$$
\int \tan^2 x\,dx = \int \left( \frac{1}{\cos^2 x} - 1 \right) dx = \tan x - x + c
$$
$$
(\tan x - x)' = \frac{1}{\cos^2 x} - 1 = \tan^2 x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $\tan^2 x$ does not appear in the table of basic integrals, so we rewrite it using trigonometric identities. By the Pythagorean identity $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$, we replace $\sin^2 x$ with $1 - \cos^2 x$ and split the fraction into two terms.

The first term is $\dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$, whose integral is $\tan x$ because $(\tan x)' = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2 x}$. The second term is the constant $-1$, whose integral is $-x$. This gives $\displaystyle\int \tan^2 x\,dx = \tan x - x + c$, and the derivative of the result equals the integrand.

</div>
