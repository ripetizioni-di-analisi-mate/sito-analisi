## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{5x - 1}{x^2 - 1}\,dx$ for $x \neq \pm 1$.

$$
x^2 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)
$$
$$
\frac{5x - 1}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} = \frac{A}{x - 1} + \frac{B}{x + 1}
\iff
5x - 1 = A\,(x + 1) + B\,(x - 1)
$$
$$
x = 1: \quad 4 = 2A \;\Longrightarrow\; A = 2,
\qquad
x = -1: \quad -6 = -2B \;\Longrightarrow\; B = 3
$$
$$
\int \frac{5x - 1}{x^2 - 1}\,dx = \int \left( \frac{2}{x - 1} + \frac{3}{x + 1} \right) dx = 2 \ln \lvert x - 1 \rvert + 3 \ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert + c
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator has degree $1$, less than the degree of the denominator, so division is unnecessary. The denominator is a difference of squares with distinct roots $1$ and $-1$, and its discriminant is positive. We therefore seek two numbers $A$ and $B$ for the decomposition into **partial fractions**.

After we write the right-hand side over the common denominator $(x - 1)(x + 1)$, the numerators must be equal for every $x$. Substituting $x = 1$ makes the term containing $B$ vanish and gives $4 = 2A$. Substituting $x = -1$ makes the term containing $A$ vanish and gives $-6 = -2B$. With $A = 2$ and $B = 3$, each partial fraction has a logarithm as an antiderivative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{3}{2x^2 + x - 1}\,dx$ for $x \neq -1$ and $x \neq \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$$
\Delta = 1 + 8 = 9,
\qquad
x_{1,2} = \frac{-1 \pm 3}{4}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x_1 = -1, \quad x_2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
2x^2 + x - 1 = 2\,(x + 1)\left( x - \frac{1}{2} \right) = (x + 1)(2x - 1)
$$
$$
\frac{3}{(x + 1)(2x - 1)} = \frac{A}{x + 1} + \frac{B}{2x - 1}
\iff
3 = A\,(2x - 1) + B\,(x + 1)
$$
$$
x = -1: \quad 3 = -3A \;\Longrightarrow\; A = -1,
\qquad
x = \frac{1}{2}: \quad 3 = \frac{3}{2}\,B \;\Longrightarrow\; B = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \frac{3}{2x^2 + x - 1}\,dx
&= \int \left( -\frac{1}{x + 1} + \frac{2}{2x - 1} \right) dx \\[6pt]
&= -\ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert + \ln \lvert 2x - 1 \rvert + c \\[6pt]
&= \ln \left\lvert \frac{2x - 1}{x + 1} \right\rvert + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discriminant of the denominator is $9 > 0$, so there are two distinct real roots, $-1$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The leading coefficient is $2$, so the denominator factors as $2\,(x + 1)\left( x - \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$. We incorporate the factor $2$ into the second binomial, which becomes $2x - 1$.

Substitute the two roots into the partial fraction decomposition. For $x = -1$, we obtain $3 = A \cdot (-3)$, so $A = -1$. For $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, we obtain $3 = B \cdot \dfrac{3}{2}$, so $B = 2$. The second partial fraction has numerator $2$, which is exactly the derivative of $2x - 1$, so its antiderivative is $\ln \lvert 2x - 1 \rvert$. Finally, the quotient rule for logarithms combines the two terms into a single logarithm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{3x - 1}{x^2 + 2x + 1}\,dx$ for $x \neq -1$.

$$
x^2 + 2x + 1 = (x + 1)^2,
\qquad
3x - 1 = 3\,(x + 1) - 4
$$
$$
\frac{3x - 1}{(x + 1)^2} = \frac{3\,(x + 1)}{(x + 1)^2} - \frac{4}{(x + 1)^2} = \frac{3}{x + 1} - \frac{4}{(x + 1)^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \frac{3x - 1}{x^2 + 2x + 1}\,dx
&= 3 \int \frac{1}{x + 1}\,dx - 4 \int (x + 1)^{-2}\,dx \\[6pt]
&= 3 \ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert - 4 \cdot \frac{(x + 1)^{-1}}{-1} + c \\[6pt]
&= 3 \ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert + \frac{4}{x + 1} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is the square of the binomial $x + 1$, so its discriminant is **zero** and $-1$ is a double root. Rewrite the numerator in terms of $x + 1$. The coefficient of $x$ is $3$, and $3\,(x + 1) = 3x + 3$. To obtain $3x - 1$, subtract $4$.

Split the fraction into two terms. In the first term, a factor of $x + 1$ cancels, leaving $\dfrac{3}{x + 1}$, whose antiderivative is $3 \ln \lvert x + 1 \rvert$. The second is a power with exponent $-2$ and inner function $x + 1$, whose derivative is $1$, so we use the power rule to integrate it. The two minus signs, one before $4$ and the other from dividing by the new exponent $-1$, give a plus sign.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int \frac{x + 5}{x^2 - 4x + 13}\,dx$.

$$
\Delta = 16 - 52 = -36 < 0,
\qquad
x^2 - 4x + 13 = (x - 2)^2 + 9
$$
$$
\left( x^2 - 4x + 13 \right)' = 2x - 4,
\qquad
x + 5 = \frac{1}{2}\,(2x - 4) + 7
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \frac{x + 5}{x^2 - 4x + 13}\,dx
&= \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{2x - 4}{x^2 - 4x + 13}\,dx + 7 \int \frac{1}{(x - 2)^2 + 9}\,dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{2} \ln \left( x^2 - 4x + 13 \right) + 7 \cdot \frac{1}{3} \arctan \frac{x - 2}{3} + c \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{2} \ln \left( x^2 - 4x + 13 \right) + \frac{7}{3} \arctan \frac{x - 2}{3} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x+5)/(x^2-4*x+13);log(x^2-4*x+13)/2+7/3*atan((x-2)/3)" xmin=-4 xmax=8 ymin=-1 ymax=5 legenda="y = \dfrac{x + 5}{x^2 - 4x + 13};y = \dfrac{1}{2} \ln \left( x^2 - 4x + 13 \right) + \dfrac{7}{3} \arctan \dfrac{x - 2}{3}" titolo="The integrand is positive for $x > -5$, so the antiderivative with $c = 0$ is increasing throughout the interval shown"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The discriminant of the denominator is negative, so the denominator has no real roots. **Completing the square** gives $x^2 - 4x + 13 = x^2 - 4x + 4 + 9 = (x - 2)^2 + 9$, that is, $h = 2$ and $k = 3$. The numerator contains the variable, so we first rewrite it to bring out the derivative of the denominator, $2x - 4$. Since $\dfrac{1}{2}\,(2x - 4) = x - 2$, to obtain $x + 5$ we add $7$.

The first integral has a numerator equal to the derivative of the denominator, so it gives a logarithm. The absolute value is unnecessary because the denominator is always positive. We evaluate the second integral using the formula $\displaystyle\int \dfrac{1}{(x - h)^2 + k^2}\,dx = \dfrac{1}{k} \arctan \dfrac{x - h}{k} + c$ with $h = 2$ and $k = 3$. The result therefore contains a logarithm and an **arctangent**.

</div>
