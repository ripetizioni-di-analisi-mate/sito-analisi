## Exercise 1

Find the value of the constant $k$ for which the function equal to $k x^2$ for $0 \leq x \leq 3$ and $0$ elsewhere is a probability density. For the random variable $X$ with this density, calculate $P(1 \leq X \leq 2)$, its mean, and its variance.

$$
\int_0^3 k x^2\,dx = k \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^3 = 9k = 1
\iff
k = \frac{1}{9}
$$
$$
P(1 \leq X \leq 2) = \int_1^2 \frac{x^2}{9}\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{27} \right]_1^2 = \frac{8 - 1}{27} = \frac{7}{27}
$$
$$
M(X) = \int_0^3 x \cdot \frac{x^2}{9}\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^4}{36} \right]_0^3 = \frac{81}{36} = \frac{9}{4}
$$
$$
\int_0^3 x^2 \cdot \frac{x^2}{9}\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^5}{45} \right]_0^3 = \frac{243}{45} = \frac{27}{5}
$$
$$
\operatorname{var}(X) = \frac{27}{5} - \left( \frac{9}{4} \right)^2 = \frac{432}{80} - \frac{405}{80} = \frac{27}{80}
$$

::grafico{f="x<0||x>3?0:x^2/9" area="x^2/9;0;1;2" segmenti="3,0,3,1" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-0.2 ymax=1.3 legenda="y = f(x)" titolo="The density equals $\dfrac{x^2}{9}$ on $[0, 3]$ and $0$ elsewhere. The shaded area is $P(1 \leq X \leq 2) = \dfrac{7}{27}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A function is a **probability density** if it is never negative and its integral over the entire real line is $1$. For $k > 0$, the function $k x^2$ is nonnegative, and outside $[0, 3]$ it is zero, so its integral over the real line equals its integral over $[0, 3]$, which is $9k$. The condition $9k = 1$ gives $k = \dfrac{1}{9}$.

The probability that $X$ lies between $1$ and $2$ is the area under the density on $[1, 2]$. An antiderivative of $\dfrac{x^2}{9}$ is $\dfrac{x^3}{27}$, and the difference between its values at $2$ and $1$ is $\dfrac{7}{27} \approx 0.26$.

Integrating $x\,f(x) = \dfrac{x^3}{9}$ gives the mean $\dfrac{9}{4}$. It is greater than the midpoint $\dfrac{3}{2}$ of the interval because the density is higher near $3$. To obtain the variance, calculate the integral of $x^2 f(x)$ and subtract the square of the mean using the common denominator $80$. This gives $\dfrac{27}{80}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bus serves a stop every $15$ minutes, and a passenger arrives at the stop at a random time. The waiting time $X$, in minutes, is uniformly distributed on $[0, 15]$. Calculate the probability of waiting more than $10$ minutes, the probability of waiting between $3$ and $9$ minutes, the mean wait, and the standard deviation.

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{15} \quad \text{for } 0 \leq x \leq 15
$$
$$
P(X > 10) = \frac{15 - 10}{15} = \frac{1}{3},
\qquad
P(3 \leq X \leq 9) = \frac{9 - 3}{15} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
M(X) = \frac{0 + 15}{2} = 7.5,
\qquad
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{15^2}{12}} = \frac{15}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 4.33
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the **uniform distribution** on $[0, 15]$, the density is constant and equals $\dfrac{1}{15}$, so the probability of an interval contained in $[0, 15]$ is its length divided by $15$. The event “waiting more than $10$ minutes” corresponds to the interval from $10$ to $15$, of length $5$, and has probability $\dfrac{1}{3}$. The interval from $3$ to $9$ has length $6$ and probability $\dfrac{6}{15} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.

The mean is the midpoint of the interval, $7.5$ minutes. The variance is $\dfrac{(b - a)^2}{12} = \dfrac{225}{12}$. Writing $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$ under the square root and rationalizing gives a standard deviation of $\dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 4.33$ minutes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The lifetime $X$ of an electronic component, in years, has an exponential distribution with a mean of $4$ years. Calculate the probability that the component lasts more than $6$ years and the probability that it fails within $2$ years. Find the time $m$ such that $P(X \leq m) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and the probability that a component that has already worked for $3$ years will work for at least another $4$ years.

$$
M(X) = \frac{1}{\lambda} = 4
\iff
\lambda = \frac{1}{4},
\qquad
P(X > t) = e^{-\frac{t}{4}}
$$
$$
P(X > 6) = e^{-\frac{6}{4}} = e^{-\frac{3}{2}} \approx 0.223
$$
$$
P(X \leq 2) = 1 - e^{-\frac{2}{4}} = 1 - e^{-\frac{1}{2}} \approx 0.393
$$
$$
1 - e^{-\frac{m}{4}} = \frac{1}{2}
\iff
e^{-\frac{m}{4}} = \frac{1}{2}
\iff
-\frac{m}{4} = -\ln 2
\iff
m = 4 \ln 2 \approx 2.77
$$
$$
P(X > 7 \mid X > 3) = \frac{e^{-\frac{7}{4}}}{e^{-\frac{3}{4}}} = e^{-1} \approx 0.368
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the **exponential distribution**, the mean is $\dfrac{1}{\lambda}$, so a mean of $4$ years gives $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The probability of lasting longer than time $t$ is $e^{-\lambda t}$, and for $t = 6$ this equals $e^{-\frac{3}{2}} \approx 0.223$.

Failure within $2$ years is the complement of “lasting more than $2$ years” and has probability $1 - e^{-\frac{1}{2}} \approx 0.393$.

The time $m$ satisfying $P(X \leq m) = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is called the median. Using the cumulative distribution function $F(m) = 1 - e^{-\frac{m}{4}}$ gives an exponential equation that can be solved by taking logarithms because $\ln \dfrac{1}{2} = -\ln 2$. The result is $m = 4 \ln 2 \approx 2.77$ years. Half the components fail before this time, which is less than the mean.

A component that has operated for $3$ years and operates for another $4$ lasts more than $7$ years. The event $X > 7$ is contained in $X > 3$, so the conditional probability is the quotient $\dfrac{P(X > 7)}{P(X > 3)} = e^{-1}$. The result equals $P(X > 4)$, as predicted by the **memoryless property** of the exponential distribution.

</div>
