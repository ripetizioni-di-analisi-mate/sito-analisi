## Exercise 1

A random variable $X$ is normally distributed with $\mu = 50$ and $\sigma = 4$. Calculate $P(X \leq 56)$, $P(X > 46)$, and $P(46 \leq X \leq 58)$, given that $\Phi(1) = 0.8413$, $\Phi(1.5) = 0.9332$, and $\Phi(2) = 0.9772$.

$$
P(X \leq 56) = \Phi\left( \frac{56 - 50}{4} \right) = \Phi(1.5) = 0.9332
$$
$$
P(X > 46) = 1 - \Phi\left( \frac{46 - 50}{4} \right) = 1 - \Phi(-1) = 1 - \big( 1 - \Phi(1) \big) = \Phi(1) = 0.8413
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P(46 \leq X \leq 58)
&= \Phi\left( \frac{58 - 50}{4} \right) - \Phi\left( \frac{46 - 50}{4} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \Phi(2) - \Phi(-1) \\[6pt]
&= 0.9772 - 0.1587 \\[6pt]
&= 0.8185
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="exp(-x^2/2)/sqrt(2*pi)" area="exp(-x^2/2)/sqrt(2*pi);0;-1;2" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-0.05 ymax=0.5 nomi="z,y" legenda="y = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-\frac{z^2}{2}}" titolo="With $\mu = 50$ and $\sigma = 4$, the values $46$ and $58$ standardize to $z = -1$ and $z = 2$. The shaded area under the standard normal density is $\Phi(2) - \Phi(-1) = 0.8185$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **standardization** theorem, the probability $P(X \leq x)$ equals $\Phi(z)$, with $z = \dfrac{x - 50}{4}$. The value $56$ lies $1.5$ standard deviations above the mean, so $P(X \leq 56) = \Phi(1.5) = 0.9332$.

The value $46$ lies one standard deviation below the mean and corresponds to $z = -1$. The event $X > 46$ is the complement of $X \leq 46$, so its probability is $1 - \Phi(-1)$. The table contains only nonnegative values of $z$, and by the symmetry of the density, $\Phi(-1) = 1 - \Phi(1) = 0.1587$. This gives $P(X > 46) = \Phi(1) = 0.8413$.

For an interval, standardize the two endpoints to obtain $z = -1$ and $z = 2$, and subtract the values of $\Phi$. The probability is $0.8185$, the shaded area in the figure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Test scores are normally distributed with mean $100$ and standard deviation $15$. Calculate the percentage of people with scores between $85$ and $115$ and the percentage of people with scores above $130$, given that $\Phi(1) = 0.8413$ and $\Phi(2) = 0.9772$.

$$
z_1 = \frac{85 - 100}{15} = -1,
\qquad
z_2 = \frac{115 - 100}{15} = 1
$$
$$
P(85 \leq X \leq 115) = \Phi(1) - \Phi(-1) = 2\,\Phi(1) - 1 = 2 \cdot 0.8413 - 1 = 0.6826
$$
$$
P(X > 130) = 1 - \Phi\left( \frac{130 - 100}{15} \right) = 1 - \Phi(2) = 1 - 0.9772 = 0.0228
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interval from $85$ to $115$ is the interval from $\mu - \sigma$ to $\mu + \sigma$, which standardizes to $[-1, 1]$. By symmetry, $\Phi(-1) = 1 - \Phi(1)$, so the probability is $2\,\Phi(1) - 1 = 0.6826$. About $68\%$ of people have scores between $85$ and $115$, in agreement with the **three-sigma rule**.

The score $130$ lies two standard deviations above the mean. The probability of exceeding it is $1 - \Phi(2) = 0.0228$, so about $2.3\%$ of people have a score above $130$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A machine fills packages of coffee. The weight of the contents in grams is normally distributed with standard deviation $\sigma = 4$. Given that $\Phi(1.645) = 0.95$ and $\Phi(1.96) = 0.975$:

1. for a mean of $\mu = 250$, find the weight $x$ exceeded by only $5\%$ of the packages,
2. find the mean $\mu$ at which the machine must be set so that only $2.5\%$ of the packages contain less than $250$ grams.

**Part 1**
$$
P(X \leq x) = 0.95
\iff
\frac{x - 250}{4} = 1.645
\iff
x = 250 + 4 \cdot 1.645 = 256.58
$$

**Part 2**
$$
P(X < 250) = \Phi\left( \frac{250 - \mu}{4} \right) = 0.025
$$
$$
\Phi(-1.96) = 1 - 0.975 = 0.025
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\frac{250 - \mu}{4} = -1.96
\iff
\mu = 250 + 4 \cdot 1.96 = 257.84
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The weight exceeded by $5\%$ of the packages is the weight $x$ such that $95\%$ of the packages weigh at most $x$, that is, $P(X \leq x) = 0.95$. By standardization, this probability is $\Phi\left( \dfrac{x - 250}{4} \right)$, and the given data tell us that $\Phi(1.645) = 0.95$. The function $\Phi$ is increasing, so $\dfrac{x - 250}{4} = 1.645$, which gives $x \approx 256.6$ grams.

**Part 2.** This time the mean is unknown. The probability $0.025$ is less than $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the standardized value of $250$ is negative, meaning that $250$ lies below the mean. By symmetry, $\Phi(-1.96) = 1 - \Phi(1.96) = 0.025$, so $\dfrac{250 - \mu}{4} = -1.96$. This gives $\mu \approx 257.8$ grams, a mean weight that lies $1.96$ standard deviations above $250$ grams.

</div>
