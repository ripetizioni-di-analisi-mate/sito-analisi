## Exercise 1

Roll two dice. Given that the sum of the two numbers is $8$, calculate the probability that at least one die shows $6$. Then determine whether the events “the sum is $8$” and “the first die shows $3$” are independent.

$$
B = \{\text{sum } 8\} = \{(2, 6),\ (3, 5),\ (4, 4),\ (5, 3),\ (6, 2)\}
$$
$$
A = \{\text{at least one } 6\},
\qquad
A \cap B = \{(2, 6),\ (6, 2)\}
$$
$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} = \frac{\dfrac{2}{36}}{\dfrac{5}{36}} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
C = \{\text{first die shows } 3\},
\qquad
P(B \mid C) = \frac{P(\{(3, 5)\})}{P(C)} = \frac{\dfrac{1}{36}}{\dfrac{6}{36}} = \frac{1}{6}
\neq
\frac{5}{36} = P(B)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The possible outcomes are the $36$ ordered pairs, all equally likely. Knowing that the sum is $8$ restricts the possibilities to the $5$ pairs in $B$. Among these, the pairs containing at least one $6$ are $(2, 6)$ and $(6, 2)$, so by the definition of **conditional probability** we obtain $P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{2}{5}$. Without the information about the sum, the probability of at least one $6$ would be $\dfrac{11}{36}$.

If the first die shows $3$, the possible outcomes are the $6$ pairs with first entry $3$, and among these only $(3, 5)$ has sum $8$. Therefore $P(B \mid C) = \dfrac{1}{6}$. This differs from $P(B) = \dfrac{5}{36}$, so the events $B$ and $C$ **are not independent**. Indeed, if the first die shows $3$, the sum $8$ becomes a little more likely.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a school of $200$ students, $80$ are girls. Of the $120$ students who play a sport, $40$ are girls. Choose one student at random. Calculate the probability that the student plays a sport given that the student is a girl, and the probability that the student is a girl given that the student plays a sport. Determine whether the events “is a girl” and “plays a sport” are independent.

| | plays a sport | does not play a sport | total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| girls | $40$ | $40$ | $80$ |
| boys | $80$ | $40$ | $120$ |
| total | $120$ | $80$ | $200$ |

$$
P(S \mid R) = \frac{P(S \cap R)}{P(R)} = \frac{40}{80} = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
P(R \mid S) = \frac{P(R \cap S)}{P(S)} = \frac{40}{120} = \frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
P(S) = \frac{120}{200} = \frac{3}{5} \neq \frac{1}{2} = P(S \mid R)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Complete the two-way table by subtraction. The number of girls who do not play a sport is $80 - 40 = 40$, the number of boys who play a sport is $120 - 40 = 80$, and the total number of boys is $200 - 80 = 120$. Let $R$ denote the event “is a girl” and $S$ the event “plays a sport.”

For the probability conditional on $R$, the possible outcomes are the $80$ girls and the favorable outcomes are the $40$ girls who play a sport, so $P(S \mid R) = \dfrac{1}{2}$. For the probability conditional on $S$, the possible outcomes are the $120$ students who play a sport and the probability is $\dfrac{40}{120} = \dfrac{1}{3}$. The two conditional probabilities differ because the first denominator is the number of girls and the second is the number of students who play a sport.

The fraction of all students who play a sport is $\dfrac{3}{5}$, whereas the fraction among girls is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since $P(S \mid R) \neq P(S)$, the events $R$ and $S$ **are not independent**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Urn $U_1$ contains $3$ white balls and $2$ black balls, and urn $U_2$ contains $1$ white ball and $4$ black balls. Toss a coin. If it lands heads, draw a ball from $U_1$, otherwise draw from $U_2$. Calculate the probability that the ball is white and, given that it is white, the probability that it came from $U_1$.

$$
P(U_1) = P(U_2) = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
P(B \mid U_1) = \frac{3}{5},
\qquad
P(B \mid U_2) = \frac{1}{5}
$$
$$
P(B) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{5} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{3}{10} + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{4}{10} = \frac{2}{5}
$$
$$
P(U_1 \mid B) = \frac{P(U_1) \cdot P(B \mid U_1)}{P(B)} = \frac{\dfrac{3}{10}}{\dfrac{4}{10}} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball comes from $U_1$ or $U_2$, and the two possibilities are mutually exclusive, so the events $U_1$ and $U_2$ form a partition. They each have probability $\dfrac{1}{2}$ because a coin determines the choice of urn. The conditional probabilities of drawing a white ball can be read from the contents of the urns.

By the **law of total probability**, the probability of drawing a white ball is the sum of the probabilities of the two paths, “choose $U_1$ and draw a white ball” and “choose $U_2$ and draw a white ball.” This gives $P(B) = \dfrac{2}{5}$.

**Bayes’ theorem** gives the probability of the cause $U_1$ given that a white ball was observed. The numerator is the probability of the path through $U_1$, namely $\dfrac{3}{10}$, and the denominator is $P(B)$. This gives $\dfrac{3}{4}$, which is greater than $\dfrac{1}{2}$ because $U_1$ contains more white balls than $U_2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Three machines $A$, $B$, and $C$ produce $50\%$, $30\%$, and $20\%$ of a factory’s parts, respectively. Defective parts account for $1\%$ of those produced by $A$, $2\%$ of those produced by $B$, and $5\%$ of those produced by $C$. Choose a part at random. Calculate the probability that it is defective and, given that it is defective, the probability that it was produced by machine $C$ and the probability that it was produced by $A$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(D)
&= P(A) \cdot P(D \mid A) + P(B) \cdot P(D \mid B) + P(C) \cdot P(D \mid C) \\[6pt]
&= 0.5 \cdot 0.01 + 0.3 \cdot 0.02 + 0.2 \cdot 0.05 \\[6pt]
&= 0.005 + 0.006 + 0.010 \\[6pt]
&= 0.021
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
P(C \mid D) = \frac{0.010}{0.021} = \frac{10}{21} \approx 0.476,
\qquad
P(A \mid D) = \frac{0.005}{0.021} = \frac{5}{21} \approx 0.238
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each part is produced by just one of the machines, so the events $A$, $B$, and $C$ form a partition with probabilities $0.5$, $0.3$, and $0.2$. The defect rates are the conditional probabilities $P(D \mid A) = 0.01$, $P(D \mid B) = 0.02$, and $P(D \mid C) = 0.05$.

By the **law of total probability**, the probability of a defective part is $0.021$, or $2.1\%$ of all parts.

By **Bayes’ theorem**, the probability that a defective part came from $C$ is the contribution from $C$, which is $0.010$, divided by $P(D)$, and equals $\dfrac{10}{21}$. Machine $C$ produces only $20\%$ of all parts but almost half the defective parts because it has the highest defect rate. Similarly, $P(A \mid D) = \dfrac{5}{21}$, and therefore $P(B \mid D) = \dfrac{6}{21}$. The three probabilities sum to $\dfrac{5 + 6 + 10}{21} = 1$ because a defective part comes from exactly one of the three machines.

</div>
