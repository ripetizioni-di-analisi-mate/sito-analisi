## Exercise 1

An urn contains $3$ white balls and $2$ black balls. Draw $2$ balls together and let $X$ be the number of white balls drawn. Find the probability distribution of $X$, its mean, variance, and standard deviation.

$$
\binom{5}{2} = 10 \text{ possible outcomes}
$$
$$
P(X = 0) = \frac{\dbinom{2}{2}}{10} = \frac{1}{10},
\qquad
P(X = 1) = \frac{\dbinom{3}{1} \dbinom{2}{1}}{10} = \frac{6}{10},
\qquad
P(X = 2) = \frac{\dbinom{3}{2}}{10} = \frac{3}{10}
$$

| $x_i$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $p_i$ | $\dfrac{1}{10}$ | $\dfrac{6}{10}$ | $\dfrac{3}{10}$ |

$$
M(X) = 0 \cdot \frac{1}{10} + 1 \cdot \frac{6}{10} + 2 \cdot \frac{3}{10} = \frac{12}{10} = \frac{6}{5}
$$
$$
M\big( X^2 \big) = 0 \cdot \frac{1}{10} + 1 \cdot \frac{6}{10} + 4 \cdot \frac{3}{10} = \frac{18}{10} = \frac{9}{5}
$$
$$
\operatorname{var}(X) = \frac{9}{5} - \left( \frac{6}{5} \right)^2 = \frac{45}{25} - \frac{36}{25} = \frac{9}{25},
\qquad
\sigma = \frac{3}{5}
$$

The most likely value is $1$, and the mean is $\dfrac{6}{5}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The variable $X$ can take the values $0$, $1$, or $2$. The balls are drawn together, so the possible outcomes are the combinations counted by $\dbinom{5}{2} = 10$. For $X = 0$, both black balls are drawn, which can happen in only one way. For $X = 1$, choose one white ball from $3$ and one black ball from $2$, in $6$ ways. For $X = 2$, choose two white balls from $3$, in $3$ ways. The probabilities sum to $\dfrac{1 + 6 + 3}{10} = 1$, which checks the counts.

The **mean** is the sum of the values multiplied by their probabilities and equals $\dfrac{6}{5} = 1.2$. For the variance, use the **formula for calculating variance**, which requires $M\big( X^2 \big)$, the sum of the squared values multiplied by their probabilities. This gives $\operatorname{var}(X) = \dfrac{9}{25}$. The standard deviation is the square root of the variance and equals $\dfrac{3}{5} = 0.6$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

To play a game, pay a fee of $a$ euros and roll a die. The payout is $12$ euros for rolling $6$, $3$ euros for rolling $4$ or $5$, and nothing otherwise. Find $a$ so that the game is fair and, using that value, calculate the variance and standard deviation of the net gain.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{outcome} & 6 & 4 \text{ or } 5 & 1, 2, 3 \\
\hline
G & 12 - a & 3 - a & -a \\
\hline
p & \dfrac{1}{6} & \dfrac{2}{6} & \dfrac{3}{6}
\end{array}
$$
$$
M(G) = (12 - a) \cdot \frac{1}{6} + (3 - a) \cdot \frac{2}{6} - a \cdot \frac{3}{6} = \frac{12 + 6 - 6a}{6} = 3 - a
$$
$$
M(G) = 0
\iff
a = 3
$$
$$
M\big( G^2 \big) = 81 \cdot \frac{1}{6} + 0 \cdot \frac{2}{6} + 9 \cdot \frac{3}{6} = \frac{81 + 27}{6} = 18
$$
$$
\operatorname{var}(G) = 18 - 0^2 = 18,
\qquad
\sigma = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2} \approx 4.24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The net gain $G$ is the payout minus the fee. The probability of rolling $6$ is $\dfrac{1}{6}$, the probability of rolling $4$ or $5$ is $\dfrac{2}{6}$, and the probability of rolling one of the numbers $1$, $2$, and $3$ is $\dfrac{3}{6}$. In calculating the mean, the terms with $a$ give $-a \cdot \dfrac{1 + 2 + 3}{6} = -a$ and the terms without $a$ give $\dfrac{12 + 6}{6} = 3$.

The game is **fair** when the expected net gain is $0$, so the fair fee is $a = 3$ euros. In other words, the fair fee is the expected payout.

With $a = 3$, the net gain is $9$, $0$, or $-3$. The mean is $0$, so the variance equals $M\big( G^2 \big) = 18$ and the standard deviation is $3\sqrt{2}$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A random variable $X$ takes the values $1$, $2$, $3$, and $4$ with probabilities $P(X = k) = c\,k$, where $c$ is a constant. Find $c$, calculate $P(X \geq 3)$, the mean and variance of $X$, and the mean and variance of $Y = 2X + 1$.

$$
c \cdot 1 + c \cdot 2 + c \cdot 3 + c \cdot 4 = 1
\iff
10c = 1
\iff
c = \frac{1}{10}
$$
$$
P(X \geq 3) = \frac{3}{10} + \frac{4}{10} = \frac{7}{10}
$$
$$
M(X) = \frac{1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 3 + 4 \cdot 4}{10} = \frac{30}{10} = 3
$$
$$
M\big( X^2 \big) = \frac{1 \cdot 1 + 4 \cdot 2 + 9 \cdot 3 + 16 \cdot 4}{10} = \frac{100}{10} = 10,
\qquad
\operatorname{var}(X) = 10 - 3^2 = 1
$$
$$
M(Y) = 2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7,
\qquad
\operatorname{var}(Y) = 2^2 \cdot 1 = 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum of the probabilities of all possible values of a random variable is $1$. This condition gives the equation $10c = 1$, so $c = \dfrac{1}{10}$ and the distribution is $\dfrac{1}{10}, \dfrac{2}{10}, \dfrac{3}{10}, \dfrac{4}{10}$. All probabilities are positive, so this is a valid distribution.

The event $X \geq 3$ is the union of the mutually exclusive events $X = 3$ and $X = 4$, so its probability is their sum, $\dfrac{7}{10}$.

In the mean, each value $k$ is multiplied by $\dfrac{k}{10}$, giving $3$. The formula for calculating variance gives $\operatorname{var}(X) = 1$ and hence also $\sigma = 1$.

For $Y = 2X + 1$, apply the formulas for the mean and variance of $aX + b$ with $a = 2$ and $b = 1$. The mean becomes $7$. The variance is multiplied by $a^2 = 4$, while the constant $1$ has no effect on it, so it becomes $4$.

</div>
