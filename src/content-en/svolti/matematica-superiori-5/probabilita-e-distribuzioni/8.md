## Exercise 1

Consider the function $f$ that equals $a\,x^2 (3 - x)$ for $0 \leq x \leq 3$ and $0$ elsewhere, where $a$ is a real parameter.

1. Find $a$ such that $f$ is the probability density of a random variable $X$.
2. Using this value, analyze $f$ on the interval $[0, 3]$ and find the value of $x$ at which the density is greatest.
3. Calculate $P(X \leq 1)$ and the mean of $X$.
4. Run $5$ independent repetitions of the experiment described by $X$. Find the probability that the event $X \leq 1$ occurs exactly $2$ times and the probability that it occurs at least once.

**Part 1**
$$
\int_0^3 a \left( 3x^2 - x^3 \right) dx = a \left[ x^3 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_0^3 = a \left( 27 - \frac{81}{4} \right) = \frac{27}{4}\,a = 1
\iff
a = \frac{4}{27}
$$

**Part 2**
$$
f(x) = \frac{4}{27} \left( 3x^2 - x^3 \right),
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{4}{27} \left( 6x - 3x^2 \right) = \frac{4}{9}\,x\,(2 - x)
$$
$$
f'(x) > 0 \quad \text{for } 0 < x < 2,
\qquad
f'(x) < 0 \quad \text{for } 2 < x < 3
$$
$$
f(0) = 0,
\qquad
f(2) = \frac{4}{27} \cdot 4 = \frac{16}{27},
\qquad
f(3) = 0
$$

**Part 3**
$$
P(X \leq 1) = \frac{4}{27} \left[ x^3 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_0^1 = \frac{4}{27} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \frac{1}{9}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
M(X)
&= \int_0^3 x \cdot \frac{4}{27} \left( 3x^2 - x^3 \right) dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{27} \left[ \frac{3x^4}{4} - \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_0^3 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{27} \left( \frac{243}{4} - \frac{243}{5} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{27} \cdot \frac{243}{20} = \frac{9}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Part 4**
$$
Y = \text{number of times } X \leq 1,
\qquad
n = 5, \quad p = \frac{1}{9}, \quad q = \frac{8}{9}
$$
$$
P(Y = 2) = \binom{5}{2} \left( \frac{1}{9} \right)^2 \left( \frac{8}{9} \right)^3 = 10 \cdot \frac{512}{59\,049} = \frac{5120}{59\,049} \approx 0.087
$$
$$
P(Y \geq 1) = 1 - \left( \frac{8}{9} \right)^5 = 1 - \frac{32\,768}{59\,049} = \frac{26\,281}{59\,049} \approx 0.445
$$

::grafico{f="x<0||x>3?0:4*x^2*(3-x)/27" area="4*x^2*(3-x)/27;0;0;1" punti="2,0.5926,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,0.5926" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-0.1 ymax=1 legenda="y = f(x)" titolo="The density $f(x) = \dfrac{4}{27}\,x^2 (3 - x)$ on $[0, 3]$ reaches its maximum at $x = 2$, where it equals $\dfrac{16}{27}$. The shaded area is $P(X \leq 1) = \dfrac{1}{9}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** A density is never negative and has integral $1$ over the entire real line. On the interval $[0, 3]$, we have $x^2 \geq 0$ and $3 - x \geq 0$, so the function is nonnegative if $a > 0$. Outside $[0, 3]$, the function is zero, so it suffices to integrate over $[0, 3]$. Expanding $x^2 (3 - x) = 3x^2 - x^3$ gives an integral of $\dfrac{27}{4}\,a$, and setting this equal to $1$ gives $a = \dfrac{4}{27}$, which is positive.

**Part 2.** The derivative is $\dfrac{4}{27}\,(6x - 3x^2)$. Factoring out $3x$ and simplifying $\dfrac{4 \cdot 3}{27} = \dfrac{4}{9}$ gives $\dfrac{4}{9}\,x\,(2 - x)$. On the interval $(0, 3)$ the factor $x$ is positive, so the derivative has the sign of $2 - x$. The function is increasing on $[0, 2]$ and decreasing on $[2, 3]$, so it reaches its **maximum** at $x = 2$, where it equals $\dfrac{16}{27}$. In statistics, the point where a density is greatest is called its mode. At the endpoints, the density equals $0$.

**Part 3.** The probability $P(X \leq 1)$ is the area under the density on $[0, 1]$. Using the same antiderivative as in Part 1 gives $\dfrac{1}{9}$. For the mean, integrate $x\,f(x)$. The two terms of the antiderivative at $3$ are $\dfrac{3 \cdot 81}{4}$ and $\dfrac{243}{5}$, and their difference is $\dfrac{243}{20}$. Multiplying by $\dfrac{4}{27}$ gives $M(X) = \dfrac{9}{5} = 1.8$. The mean is less than the mode $2$ because the density is positive on an interval of length $2$ to the left of the mode and on an interval of length $1$ to its right.

**Part 4.** In each repetition the event $X \leq 1$ occurs with probability $\dfrac{1}{9}$, and the repetitions are independent. The number $Y$ of times the event occurs therefore has a **binomial distribution** with $n = 5$ and $p = \dfrac{1}{9}$. We have $9^5 = 59\,049$ and $8^3 = 512$, which gives $P(Y = 2) \approx 0.087$. The complement of “at least once” is “never,” which has probability $\left( \dfrac{8}{9} \right)^5$, so $P(Y \geq 1) \approx 0.445$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A machine $M_1$ makes bolts whose diameters in millimeters are normally distributed with $\mu = 10$ and $\sigma = 0.2$. A bolt is rejected if its diameter differs from $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ by more than $0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$. We are given that $\Phi(2) = 0.9772$.

1. Calculate the probability that a bolt made by $M_1$ is rejected.
2. A box contains $20$ bolts made by $M_1$. Calculate the probability that none are rejected and the mean number rejected.
3. A second machine $M_2$ makes bolts that are rejected with probability $0.1$. The factory produces $60\%$ of its bolts using $M_1$ and $40\%$ using $M_2$. Given that a bolt selected at random from all the bolts was rejected, calculate the probability that it came from $M_2$.

**Part 1**
$$
P(9.6 \leq X \leq 10.4) = \Phi\left( \frac{10.4 - 10}{0.2} \right) - \Phi\left( \frac{9.6 - 10}{0.2} \right) = \Phi(2) - \Phi(-2) = 2\,\Phi(2) - 1 = 0.9544
$$
$$
P(\text{rejection}) = 1 - 0.9544 = 0.0456
$$

**Part 2**
$$
P(\text{none rejected}) = 0.9544^{20} \approx 0.393,
\qquad
M = 20 \cdot 0.0456 = 0.912
$$

**Part 3**
$$
P(S) = 0.6 \cdot 0.0456 + 0.4 \cdot 0.1 = 0.02736 + 0.04 = 0.06736
$$
$$
P(M_2 \mid S) = \frac{0.04}{0.06736} \approx 0.594
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** A bolt is accepted if its diameter is between $10 - 0.4 = 9.6$ and $10 + 0.4 = 10.4$. Under **standardization**, the endpoints become $z = -2$ and $z = 2$ because $0.4$ is twice the standard deviation. By symmetry, $\Phi(-2) = 1 - \Phi(2)$, so the probability of acceptance is $2\,\Phi(2) - 1 = 0.9544$ and the probability of rejection is its complement to $1$, namely $0.0456$.

**Part 2.** Each bolt in the box is rejected with probability $p = 0.0456$, independently of the others, so the number of rejected bolts has a binomial distribution with $n = 20$. The probability that none are rejected is $q^{20} = 0.9544^{20} \approx 0.393$. The mean number rejected is $np \approx 0.91$, just under one bolt per box.

**Part 3.** The bolts produced by $M_1$ and those produced by $M_2$ form a partition with probabilities $0.6$ and $0.4$. By the **law of total probability**, the probability of rejecting a randomly selected bolt is $0.06736$. By **Bayes’ theorem**, the probability that a rejected bolt came from $M_2$ is the contribution from $M_2$, which is $0.04$, divided by $0.06736$, giving approximately $0.594$. Machine $M_2$ produces fewer bolts than $M_1$ but accounts for about $59\%$ of the rejected bolts because its rejection probability is more than twice that of $M_1$.

</div>
