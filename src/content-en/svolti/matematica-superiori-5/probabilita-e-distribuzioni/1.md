## Exercise 1

Roll two dice. Find the probability that the two numbers add up to $8$ and the probability that the numbers differ.

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = D'_{6,2} = 6^2 = 36
$$
$$
\text{sum } 8: \quad (2, 6),\ (3, 5),\ (4, 4),\ (5, 3),\ (6, 2)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
P(\text{sum } 8) = \frac{5}{36}
$$
$$
\text{different numbers}: \quad D_{6,2} = 6 \cdot 5 = 30
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
P(\text{different numbers}) = \frac{30}{36} = \frac{5}{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outcome is an ordered pair because the first and second dice are distinguishable. Each die has $6$ possible outcomes and the numbers can repeat, so the possible outcomes are the ordered selections with replacement counted by $6^2 = 36$. The $36$ outcomes are equally likely, so **classical probability** applies.

List the pairs with sum $8$ by varying the first number. The first number cannot be $1$ because the second would have to be $7$. From $2$ to $6$, however, there is exactly one pair for each value. There are $5$ pairs, so the probability is $\dfrac{5}{36}$. The pair $(4, 4)$ is counted only once, whereas $(3, 5)$ and $(5, 3)$ are two different outcomes.

The pairs with different numbers are ordered selections without replacement because there are $5$ choices for the second number that differ from the first. There are $30$ such pairs, and the probability is $\dfrac{5}{6}$. The same result follows by observing that there are $6$ pairs with equal numbers, so there are $36 - 6 = 30$ pairs with different numbers.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An urn contains $5$ red balls and $3$ blue balls. Draw $2$ balls together. Calculate the probability that both are red and the probability that they are different colors.

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = \binom{8}{2} = \frac{8 \cdot 7}{2} = 28
$$
$$
P(\text{two red}) = \frac{\dbinom{5}{2}}{28} = \frac{10}{28} = \frac{5}{14}
$$
$$
P(\text{one red and one blue}) = \frac{\dbinom{5}{1} \dbinom{3}{1}}{28} = \frac{5 \cdot 3}{28} = \frac{15}{28}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The balls are drawn together, so order does not matter. The possible outcomes are the **combinations** of $8$ balls taken in groups of $2$. With the $2$ balls in each group unordered, there are $28$ outcomes. The balls are treated as distinct even when they have the same color, which is why the $28$ outcomes are equally likely.

The favorable outcomes for the first event are the pairs formed from two of the $5$ red balls, giving $\dbinom{5}{2} = 10$. For the second event, choose one red ball from $5$ and one blue ball from $3$. Each choice of a red ball can be paired with each choice of a blue ball, so the number of outcomes is $5 \cdot 3 = 15$.

There are $\dbinom{3}{2} = 3$ pairs of blue balls. We have $10 + 15 + 3 = 28$, confirming that the three types of pairs account for all possibilities.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Form a random code of $4$ digits chosen from $0, 1, \dots, 9$ with repetitions allowed. Calculate the probability that the digits are all different and the probability that the digits, read from left to right, are in strictly increasing order.

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = D'_{10,4} = 10^4 = 10\,000
$$
$$
P(\text{distinct digits}) = \frac{D_{10,4}}{10\,000} = \frac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7}{10\,000} = \frac{5040}{10\,000} = \frac{63}{125}
$$
$$
P(\text{increasing digits}) = \frac{\dbinom{10}{4}}{10\,000} = \frac{210}{10\,000} = \frac{21}{1000}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A code is an ordered sequence of $4$ digits that can repeat, so the possible codes are ordered selections with replacement from $10$ digits. There are $4$ positions, and any of the digits may appear in each of the $4$ positions, giving $10\,000$ codes. The code is generated at random, so all codes are equally likely.

The codes with distinct digits are the **ordered selections without replacement** counted by $D_{10,4}$ because there are $10$ choices for the first digit, $9$ for the second, $8$ for the third, and $7$ for the fourth. The probability is $\dfrac{5040}{10\,000} = 0.504$, or $\dfrac{63}{125}$.

A code with digits in strictly increasing order is determined by the set of its four digits. For example, the set $\{2, 5, 7, 9\}$ gives just one increasing code, $2579$. The increasing codes therefore correspond to the **combinations** of $10$ digits taken in groups of $4$. Each group of $4$ gives one code, so there are $\dbinom{10}{4} = \dfrac{10 \cdot 9 \cdot 8 \cdot 7}{4!} = 210$ codes and the probability is $\dfrac{21}{1000}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

From a deck of $40$ cards with $4$ suits of $10$ cards each, draw $3$ cards together. Calculate the probability that the three cards are all of the same suit and the probability that exactly two of them are aces.

$$
\text{possible outcomes} = \binom{40}{3} = \frac{40 \cdot 39 \cdot 38}{6} = 9880
$$
$$
P(\text{same suit}) = \frac{4 \cdot \dbinom{10}{3}}{9880} = \frac{4 \cdot 120}{9880} = \frac{480}{9880} = \frac{12}{247}
$$
$$
P(\text{two aces}) = \frac{\dbinom{4}{2} \dbinom{36}{1}}{9880} = \frac{6 \cdot 36}{9880} = \frac{216}{9880} = \frac{27}{1235}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cards are drawn together, so the possible outcomes are the combinations of $40$ cards drawn in groups of $3$, with the $3$ cards in each group unordered. In calculating $40 \cdot 39 \cdot 38 = 59\,280$, divide by $3! = 6$ to obtain $9880$.

For three cards of the same suit, first choose the suit in $4$ ways and then choose three of the $10$ cards of that suit in $\dbinom{10}{3} = 120$ ways. There are $480$ favorable outcomes, and dividing the numerator and denominator by $40$ gives the probability $\dfrac{12}{247} \approx 0.049$.

For exactly two aces, choose $2$ aces from the $4$ aces in the deck and choose the third card from the $36$ cards that are not aces. The third card must be chosen from the non-aces because otherwise there would be three aces. There are $6 \cdot 36 = 216$ favorable outcomes, and dividing by $8$ gives $\dfrac{27}{1235} \approx 0.022$.

</div>
