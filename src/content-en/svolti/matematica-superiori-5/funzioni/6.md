## Exercise 1

Given $f(x) = x^2 + 1$ and $g(x) = 2x - 3$, find $(f \circ g)(x)$ and $(g \circ f)(x)$ and evaluate $(f \circ g)(1)$ and $(g \circ f)(1)$.

$$
(f \circ g)(x) = f(2x - 3) = (2x - 3)^2 + 1 = 4x^2 - 12x + 10
$$
$$
(g \circ f)(x) = g\left( x^2 + 1 \right) = 2\left( x^2 + 1 \right) - 3 = 2x^2 - 1
$$
$$
(f \circ g)(1) = 4 - 12 + 10 = 2,
\qquad
(g \circ f)(1) = 2 - 1 = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **composite function** $f \circ g$, we apply $g$ first and $f$ second. We therefore substitute the expression $2x - 3$ for $x$ in the formula for $f$ and expand the square of the binomial, $(2x - 3)^2 = 4x^2 - 12x + 9$. In the composite $g \circ f$ the order is reversed, and we substitute $x^2 + 1$ for $x$ in the formula for $g$.

The two composite functions are different, as their values at $1$ confirm. Evaluating one step at a time gives the same results because $g(1) = -1$ and $f(-1) = 2$, whereas $f(1) = 2$ and $g(2) = 1$. Both functions are defined on $\mathbb{R}$, so both composites also have domain $\mathbb{R}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $f(x) = \ln x$ and $g(x) = x^2 - 4x + 3$, write down $f \circ g$ and $g \circ f$ with their domains, and find the zeros of $g \circ f$.

$$
(f \circ g)(x) = \ln \left( x^2 - 4x + 3 \right),
\qquad
x^2 - 4x + 3 > 0
\iff
x < 1 \quad \text{or} \quad x > 3
$$
$$
D_{f \circ g} = (-\infty, 1) \cup (3, +\infty)
$$
$$
(g \circ f)(x) = \ln^2 x - 4 \ln x + 3,
\qquad
D_{g \circ f} = (0, +\infty)
$$
$$
\ln^2 x - 4 \ln x + 3 = 0
\iff
(\ln x - 1)(\ln x - 3) = 0
\iff
x = e \quad \text{or} \quad x = e^{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the composite $f \circ g$ the inner function is the polynomial $g$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$, and the outer function is the logarithm. The domain therefore consists of the numbers $x$ for which $g(x)$ lies in the domain of the logarithm, that is, $g(x) > 0$. The trinomial $x^2 - 4x + 3$ has roots $1$ and $3$ and its graph opens upward, so it is positive for $x < 1$ and for $x > 3$.

In the composite $g \circ f$ the inner function is the logarithm, which requires $x > 0$. The polynomial $g$ is defined for every input, so it imposes no further conditions and the domain is $(0, +\infty)$. The notation $\ln^2 x$ denotes the square $(\ln x)^2$.

To find the zeros of $g \circ f$, set $t = \ln x$. The equation becomes $t^2 - 4t + 3 = 0$, whose solutions are $t = 1$ and $t = 3$. Since $\ln x = 1$ is equivalent to $x = e$ and $\ln x = 3$ is equivalent to $x = e^{3}$, the zeros are $e$ and $e^{3}$. Both belong to the domain because they are positive.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the functions $h(x) = e^{\sqrt{x - 1}}$ and $k(x) = \cos^3 (2x)$ as compositions of elementary functions.

$$
h(x) = f_3\Big( f_2\big( f_1(x) \big) \Big),
\qquad
f_1(x) = x - 1,
\quad
f_2(x) = \sqrt{x},
\quad
f_3(x) = e^{x}
$$
$$
k(x) = g_3\Big( g_2\big( g_1(x) \big) \Big),
\qquad
g_1(x) = 2x,
\quad
g_2(x) = \cos x,
\quad
g_3(x) = x^3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To decompose a function, follow the order of operations when a number is substituted for $x$. For $h$, start with $x$, subtract $1$, then take the square root, and finally evaluate the exponential. The innermost function is therefore $f_1(x) = x - 1$ and the outermost function is $f_3(x) = e^{x}$. For example, with $x = 5$ we obtain $f_1(5) = 4$, then $f_2(4) = 2$, and finally $h(5) = e^{2}$.

For $k$, the notation $\cos^3 (2x)$ means $\big( \cos (2x) \big)^3$. First multiply $x$ by $2$, then take the cosine, and finally cube the result, so the outermost function is the power function $g_3(x) = x^3$. This decomposition is used to differentiate functions by the chain rule, as discussed in the chapter on derivatives.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the function $f$ such that $f(x + 1) = x^2 + 3x$ for every real $x$.

$$
t = x + 1
\iff
x = t - 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(t)
&= (t - 1)^2 + 3\,(t - 1) \\[6pt]
&= t^2 - 2t + 1 + 3t - 3 \\[6pt]
&= t^2 + t - 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f(x) = x^2 + x - 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The identity says that $f$, evaluated at $x + 1$, equals $x^2 + 3x$. To find the formula for $f$, call the input $t$, that is, $t = x + 1$, and express $x$ in terms of $t$, giving $x = t - 1$. Substituting on the right gives $f(t)$ as a polynomial in $t$.

The letter used for the variable does not change the function, so $f(x) = x^2 + x - 2$. As a check, evaluating the composition gives $f(x + 1) = (x + 1)^2 + (x + 1) - 2 = x^2 + 2x + 1 + x + 1 - 2 = x^2 + 3x$, the original expression.

</div>
