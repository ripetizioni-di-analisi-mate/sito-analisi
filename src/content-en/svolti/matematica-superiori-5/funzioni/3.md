## Exercise 1

Find the intercepts and determine the sign of the function $f(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 6x$.

$$
f(x) = x\left( x^2 - x - 6 \right) = x\,(x + 2)(x - 3)
$$
$$
f(x) = 0
\iff
x = -2, \quad x = 0, \quad x = 3
$$

| | $x < -2$ | $-2 < x < 0$ | $0 < x < 3$ | $x > 3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x + 2$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x - 3$ | $-$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $f(x)$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
f(x) > 0 \iff -2 < x < 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x > 3
$$

::grafico{f="x^3-x^2-6*x" punti="-2,0,pieno;0,0,pieno;3,0,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-12 ymax=8 titolo="The graph of $f(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 6x$ meets the $x$-axis at $-2$, $0$, and $3$ and lies above the axis for $-2 < x < 0$ and for $x > 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so its domain is $\mathbb{R}$. Factor out $x$ and factor the trinomial $x^2 - x - 6$, whose roots are $-2$ and $3$ because $-2 + 3 = 1$ and $(-2) \cdot 3 = -6$. The **zeros** are therefore $-2$, $0$, and $3$, and the graph meets the $x$-axis at the points $(-2, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, and $(3, 0)$. The origin is also the intersection with the $y$-axis because $f(0) = 0$.

The sign of the product follows from the signs of its three factors, each of which is positive to the right of its zero. In the row for $f(x)$, the sign is the product of the signs in the column. The function is therefore **positive** for $-2 < x < 0$ and for $x > 3$ and negative for $x < -2$ and for $0 < x < 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the intercepts and determine the sign of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 + x}$.

$$
x^2 + x = x\,(x + 1) \neq 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 0\}
$$
$$
f(x) = \frac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{x\,(x + 1)} = 0
\iff
x = -2 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 2
$$

| | $x < -2$ | $-2 < x < -1$ | $-1 < x < 0$ | $0 < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x - 2$ | $-$ | $-$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| $x + 2$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x$ | $-$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $x + 1$ | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $f(x)$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ | $+$ |

$$
f(x) > 0 \iff x < -2 \quad \text{or} \quad -1 < x < 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x > 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First find the domain. The denominator vanishes at $x = 0$ and $x = -1$, so these two numbers are excluded. In particular, $0$ does not belong to the domain, so the graph **does not meet** the $y$-axis.

The zeros of the function are the zeros of the numerator that belong to the domain, namely $-2$ and $2$, and the points of intersection with the $x$-axis are $(-2, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$. To determine the sign, factor the numerator and denominator into four linear factors, which change sign at $-2$, $-1$, $0$, and $2$. On each interval, the sign of the fraction is the product of the signs of the four factors. For example, for $-2 < x < -1$ there are three negative factors, so the fraction is negative. As a check, $f(-3) = \dfrac{5}{6} > 0$ and $f(1) = -\dfrac{3}{2} < 0$, in agreement with the table.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the intercepts and determine the sign of the function $f(x) = (2 - x) \ln x$.

$$
D = (0, +\infty)
$$
$$
f(x) = 0
\iff
2 - x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad \ln x = 0
\iff
x = 2 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 1
$$

| | $0 < x < 1$ | $1 < x < 2$ | $x > 2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $2 - x$ | $+$ | $+$ | $-$ |
| $\ln x$ | $-$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| $f(x)$ | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ |

$$
f(x) > 0 \iff 1 < x < 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument of the logarithm must be positive, so the domain is $(0, +\infty)$. The number $0$ does not belong to the domain, so the graph does not meet the $y$-axis.

A product is zero when one of its factors is zero. The factor $2 - x$ vanishes at $x = 2$ and the factor $\ln x$ vanishes at $x = 1$ because $\ln 1 = 0$. The intersections with the $x$-axis are therefore $(1, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$. The factor $2 - x$ is positive for $x < 2$, whereas $\ln x$ is positive for $x > 1$ because the natural logarithm is increasing. From the table, the function is **positive** only for $1 < x < 2$ and negative for $0 < x < 1$ and for $x > 2$. For example, $f\left( \dfrac{3}{2} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2} \ln \dfrac{3}{2} > 0$, whereas $f(e) = 2 - e < 0$.

</div>
