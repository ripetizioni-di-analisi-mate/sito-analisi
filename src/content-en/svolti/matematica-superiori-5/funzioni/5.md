## Exercise 1

Given $f(x) = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 3}$, find its domain and range, verify that $f$ is injective, and find its inverse.

$$
D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}
$$
$$
f(x_1) = f(x_2)
\iff
(2x_1 + 1)(x_2 - 3) = (2x_2 + 1)(x_1 - 3)
\iff
-7x_1 = -7x_2
\iff
x_1 = x_2
$$
$$
y = \frac{2x + 1}{x - 3}
\iff
y\,(x - 3) = 2x + 1
\iff
x\,(y - 2) = 3y + 1
$$
$$
x = \frac{3y + 1}{y - 2} \quad \text{for } y \neq 2,
\qquad
f(D) = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{2\}
$$
$$
f^{-1}(x) = \frac{3x + 1}{x - 2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator vanishes at $x = 3$, so the domain is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}$. To verify that $f$ is **injective**, assume $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$ and show that $x_1 = x_2$ follows. Cross-multiplying and expanding the two products gives $2x_1 x_2 - 6x_1 + x_2 - 3 = 2x_1 x_2 - 6x_2 + x_1 - 3$. The terms $2x_1 x_2$ and $-3$ occur on both sides and cancel, leaving $-7x_1 = -7x_2$, or $x_1 = x_2$.

To find the range, determine for which $y$ the equation $y = f(x)$ has a solution. Multiplying by $x - 3$ and moving the terms involving $x$ to the left gives $x\,(y - 2) = 3y + 1$. If $y \neq 2$, division by $y - 2$ gives a unique solution. If $y = 2$, the equation becomes $0 = 7$, which is impossible, so $2$ has no preimages. The range is therefore $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{2\}$, and the function from $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}$ to $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{2\}$ is bijective.

The formula obtained for $x$, with the letters interchanged, gives the **inverse function**, defined for $x \neq 2$. As a check, $f(4) = 9$ and $f^{-1}(9) = \dfrac{28}{7} = 4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $f(x) = e^{2x - 1} + 3$, defined on $\mathbb{R}$, find its range and inverse function, including the domain of the inverse.

$$
e^{2x - 1} > 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
f(x) > 3,
\qquad
f(\mathbb{R}) = (3, +\infty)
$$
$$
y = e^{2x - 1} + 3
\iff
e^{2x - 1} = y - 3
\iff
2x - 1 = \ln (y - 3)
\iff
x = \frac{1 + \ln (y - 3)}{2}
$$
$$
f^{-1}(x) = \frac{1 + \ln (x - 3)}{2},
\qquad
D_{f^{-1}} = (3, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $2x - 1$ is increasing, and the exponential function with base $e$ is increasing, so $f$ is increasing on $\mathbb{R}$. By the theorem on **monotone functions**, it is therefore injective.

An exponential function takes all positive values and only those values, so $e^{2x - 1}$ has range $(0, +\infty)$, and adding $3$ gives the range $(3, +\infty)$. To find the inverse, isolate the exponential term and take logarithms. This is possible because $y - 3 > 0$ for every $y$ in the range. The domain of the **inverse function** is the range of $f$, namely $(3, +\infty)$. It agrees with the natural domain of the formula we found because the argument of the logarithm must be positive. As a check, $f\left( \dfrac{1}{2} \right) = e^{0} + 3 = 4$ and $f^{-1}(4) = \dfrac{1 + \ln 1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The function $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 1$ is defined on $\mathbb{R}$. Verify that it is not injective, find its restriction to the interval $[2, +\infty)$ and the corresponding range, and write down the inverse of this restriction.

$$
f(0) = 1 = f(4)
$$
$$
f(x) = (x - 2)^2 - 3,
\qquad
V(2, -3)
$$
$$
x \geq 2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
f(x) \geq -3,
\qquad
f\big( [2, +\infty) \big) = [-3, +\infty)
$$
$$
y = (x - 2)^2 - 3
\iff
(x - 2)^2 = y + 3
\iff
x - 2 = \sqrt{y + 3}
$$
$$
f^{-1}(x) = 2 + \sqrt{x + 3},
\qquad
x \geq -3
$$

The vertex $(2, -3)$ corresponds to the point $(-3, 2)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two different numbers, $0$ and $4$, have the same image $1$, so $f$ **is not injective** on $\mathbb{R}$. Completing the square explains why. Since $x^2 - 4x + 1 = (x^2 - 4x + 4) - 3$, the graph is a parabola with vertex $V(2, -3)$, symmetric about the line $x = 2$.

On the interval $[2, +\infty)$ the parabola is increasing, so the restriction is injective. The square $(x - 2)^2$ takes all values greater than or equal to $0$, so the range of the restriction is $[-3, +\infty)$. In the equation $(x - 2)^2 = y + 3$, take the positive square root because $x \geq 2$ implies $x - 2 \geq 0$. This gives $x = 2 + \sqrt{y + 3}$, and interchanging the letters gives the **inverse** $f^{-1}(x) = 2 + \sqrt{x + 3}$, defined for $x \geq -3$.

As a check, $f(5) = 25 - 20 + 1 = 6$ and $f^{-1}(6) = 2 + \sqrt{9} = 5$. In the figure, the points $(5, 6)$ and $(6, 5)$ are reflections of each other across the line $y = x$.

</div>
