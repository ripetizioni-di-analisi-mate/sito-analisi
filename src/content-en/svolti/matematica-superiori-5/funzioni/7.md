## Exercise 1

Sketch the graph of $y = \sqrt{x - 2} + 1$ starting from the graph of $y = \sqrt{x}$, and find the domain and range.

$$
y = \sqrt{x}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = \sqrt{x - 2}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = \sqrt{x - 2} + 1
$$
$$
(0, 0)
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
(2, 0)
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
(2, 1)
$$
$$
D = [2, +\infty),
\qquad
f(D) = [1, +\infty)
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(x-2)+1;sqrt(x)" punti="0,0,pieno;2,1,pieno;6,3,pieno;4,2,pieno" vettori="0,0,2,1" xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-1 ymax=4 uguale=1 legenda="y = \sqrt{x - 2} + 1;y = \sqrt{x}" titolo="The graph of $y = \sqrt{x - 2} + 1$ is the graph of $y = \sqrt{x}$ shifted $2$ to the right and $1$ upward. The point $(4, 2)$ moves to $(6, 3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is obtained from $\sqrt{x}$ by two **translations**. Substituting $x - 2$ for $x$ shifts the graph $2$ to the right, and adding $1$ to the entire expression shifts it $1$ upward. The starting point $(0, 0)$ on the graph of $\sqrt{x}$ therefore moves to $(2, 1)$.

The domain can be read from the graph and confirmed by the condition on the radicand, $x - 2 \geq 0$, or $x \geq 2$. The square root takes all values greater than or equal to $0$, so $\sqrt{x - 2} + 1$ takes all values greater than or equal to $1$, and the range is $[1, +\infty)$. As a check, for $x = 6$ we have $y = \sqrt{4} + 1 = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Sketch the graph of $y = 2 - e^{-x}$ starting from the graph of $y = e^{x}$, and find the intercepts and range.

$$
y = e^{x}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = e^{-x}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = -e^{-x}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = 2 - e^{-x}
$$
$$
x = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
y = 2 - 1 = 1
$$
$$
2 - e^{-x} = 0
\iff
e^{-x} = 2
\iff
x = -\ln 2
$$
$$
f(\mathbb{R}) = (-\infty, 2)
$$

::grafico{f="2-exp(-x);exp(x)" punti="0,1,pieno;-0.693,0,pieno" asintoti="y=2" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 legenda="y = 2 - e^{-x};y = e^{x}" titolo="The graph of $y = 2 - e^{-x}$ is obtained from the graph of $y = e^{x}$ by two reflections and an upward translation. It passes through $(0, 1)$ and $(-\ln 2, 0)$ and approaches the line $y = 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Construct the graph in three steps. Substituting $-x$ for $x$ reflects the graph of $e^{x}$ across the $y$-axis, giving the decreasing graph of $e^{-x}$. Changing the sign of the entire function gives its **reflection** across the $x$-axis, so $-e^{-x}$ is negative and increasing. Finally, adding $2$ shifts the graph $2$ upward.

The graph of $e^{-x}$ lies above the $x$-axis and approaches the $x$-axis for large $x$. After the reflection and translation, the graph therefore lies below the line $y = 2$ and approaches it for large $x$, so the range is $(-\infty, 2)$. For $x = 0$ we have $e^{0} = 1$, so the graph meets the $y$-axis at $(0, 1)$. To find the intersection with the $x$-axis, solve $e^{-x} = 2$. Taking logarithms gives $-x = \ln 2$, so $x = -\ln 2 \approx -0.69$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Sketch the graph of $y = \left| x^2 - 2|x| - 3 \right|$ starting from the parabola $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$.

$$
f(x) = x^2 - 2x - 3 = (x - 3)(x + 1),
\qquad
V(1, -4)
$$
$$
y = f(|x|) = x^2 - 2|x| - 3:
\qquad
\text{zeros } x = \pm 3,
\qquad
\text{vertices } (\pm 1, -4),
\qquad
f(|0|) = -3
$$
$$
y = \big| f(|x|) \big|:
\qquad
(\pm 1, -4) \longrightarrow (\pm 1, 4),
\qquad
(0, -3) \longrightarrow (0, 3)
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-2*abs(x)-3);x^2-2*abs(x)-3" punti="-3,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;1,4,pieno;-1,4,pieno;0,3,pieno" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 legenda="y = \left| x^2 - 2|x| - 3 \right|;y = x^2 - 2|x| - 3" titolo="The graph of $y = x^2 - 2|x| - 3$ is symmetric about the $y$-axis. The outer absolute value reflects the parts below the $x$-axis across that axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola $y = x^2 - 2x - 3$ has zeros $-1$ and $3$ and a vertex at horizontal coordinate $1$, where its value is $1 - 2 - 3 = -4$. The first step is to graph $f(|x|)$. For $x \geq 0$ we have $|x| = x$, so the graph **coincides** with the parabola. For $x < 0$ it is the reflection across the $y$-axis of the part with $x \geq 0$ because the function $f(|x|)$ is even. The zero $-1$ of the parabola lies in $x < 0$ and does not belong to the new graph. This leaves the zero $3$ and its reflection $-3$, and the two vertices $(1, -4)$ and $(-1, -4)$.

The second step applies the outer absolute value. Between $-3$ and $3$ the graph of $f(|x|)$ lies below the $x$-axis, so this part is replaced by its reflection across the $x$-axis. The vertices become $(\pm 1, 4)$ and the point $(0, -3)$ becomes $(0, 3)$. The final graph lies entirely on or above the $x$-axis and touches it only at $x = -3$ and $x = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Write the function $y = \dfrac{x + 1}{x - 1}$ in the form $y = 1 + \dfrac{a}{x - 1}$ and derive its graph from that of $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

$$
\frac{x + 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(x - 1) + 2}{x - 1} = 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1}
$$
$$
y = \frac{1}{x}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = \frac{2}{x}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = \frac{2}{x - 1}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1}
$$
$$
D = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\},
\qquad
f(D) = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\},
\qquad
\text{center } (1, 1)
$$

::grafico{f="(x+1)/(x-1);1/x" punti="-1,0,pieno;0,-1,pieno" asintoti="x=1;y=1" xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=6 legenda="y = \dfrac{x + 1}{x - 1};y = \dfrac{1}{x}" titolo="The graph of $y = \dfrac{x + 1}{x - 1}$ is the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ stretched vertically by a factor of $2$ and translated so that its center is at $(1, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the numerator, write $x + 1$ as $(x - 1) + 2$ so that the fraction separates into $\dfrac{x - 1}{x - 1} + \dfrac{2}{x - 1} = 1 + \dfrac{2}{x - 1}$, with $a = 2$. This form shows which transformations to apply.

Multiplying $\dfrac{1}{x}$ by $2$ gives a vertical **stretch** by a factor of $2$. Substituting $x - 1$ for $x$ shifts the graph $1$ to the right, and adding $1$ shifts it $1$ upward. The lines $x = 0$ and $y = 0$, which the branches of the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$ approach, become the lines $x = 1$ and $y = 1$, and the center of symmetry moves from the origin to $(1, 1)$.

The value $1$ is excluded from the domain because it makes the denominator zero. The value $y = 1$ is excluded from the range because $\dfrac{2}{x - 1}$ never equals $0$. As a check, the graph passes through $(-1, 0)$ and $(0, -1)$ because $\dfrac{-1 + 1}{-1 - 1} = 0$ and $\dfrac{0 + 1}{0 - 1} = -1$.

</div>
