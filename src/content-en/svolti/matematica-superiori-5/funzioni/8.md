## Exercise 1

Consider the function $f(x) = \dfrac{a x + 3}{x + b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are real parameters.

1. Find $a$ and $b$ such that the domain of $f$ is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}$ and the graph passes through the point $A(4, 7)$.
2. Using these values, find the intercepts of the graph and determine the sign of $f$.
3. Verify that $f$ is invertible and find its inverse function and the domain of the inverse.
4. Write $f$ in the form $f(x) = 1 + \dfrac{k}{x - 3}$ and deduce that its graph is symmetric about the point $C(3, 1)$.

**Part 1**
$$
x + b = 0 \iff x = -b,
\qquad
-b = 3
\iff
b = -3
$$
$$
f(4) = \frac{4a + 3}{4 - 3} = 7
\iff
4a = 4
\iff
a = 1
$$
$$
f(x) = \frac{x + 3}{x - 3}
$$

**Part 2**
$$
f(x) = 0 \iff x = -3,
\qquad
f(0) = \frac{3}{-3} = -1
$$
$$
f(x) > 0
\iff
x < -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x > 3
$$

**Part 3**
$$
y = \frac{x + 3}{x - 3}
\iff
y\,(x - 3) = x + 3
\iff
x\,(y - 1) = 3y + 3
$$
$$
f^{-1}(x) = \frac{3x + 3}{x - 1},
\qquad
D_{f^{-1}} = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}
$$

**Part 4**
$$
\frac{x + 3}{x - 3} = \frac{(x - 3) + 6}{x - 3} = 1 + \frac{6}{x - 3}
$$
$$
y = \frac{6}{x}
\quad \longrightarrow \quad
y = 1 + \frac{6}{x - 3},
\qquad
O(0, 0) \longrightarrow C(3, 1)
$$

::grafico{f="(x+3)/(x-3);(3*x+3)/(x-1)" punti="4,7,pieno,A;-3,0,pieno;0,-1,pieno" segmenti="-6,-6,10,10" xmin=-6 xmax=10 ymin=-6 ymax=10 uguale=1 legenda="y = \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 3};y = \dfrac{3x + 3}{x - 1}" titolo="The graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{x + 3}{x - 3}$ and that of its inverse are reflections of each other across the line $y = x$. The first has center of symmetry $(3, 1)$ and the second has center $(1, 3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The domain of a fraction excludes values that make the denominator zero. The denominator $x + b$ vanishes only at $x = -b$, so the domain is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}$ if and only if $-b = 3$, or $b = -3$. Passing through $A(4, 7)$ means that $f(4) = 7$. With $b = -3$, the denominator equals $1$, and the equation $4a + 3 = 7$ gives $a = 1$.

**Part 2.** The numerator vanishes at $x = -3$, which belongs to the domain, so the graph meets the $x$-axis at $(-3, 0)$. The number $0$ belongs to the domain and $f(0) = -1$, so the graph meets the $y$-axis at $(0, -1)$. The numerator is positive for $x > -3$ and the denominator for $x > 3$. The fraction is positive when they have the same sign, that is, for $x < -3$ and for $x > 3$, and is **negative** for $-3 < x < 3$.

**Part 3.** To decide whether $f$ is invertible, solve $y = f(x)$ for $x$. If $y \neq 1$, divide by $y - 1$ to obtain the unique solution $x = \dfrac{3y + 3}{y - 1}$, which differs from $3$ because the equality $\dfrac{3y + 3}{y - 1} = 3$ is equivalent to $3y + 3 = 3y - 3$, which is impossible. If $y = 1$, the equation becomes $0 = 6$, which has no solutions. Each $y \neq 1$ therefore has exactly one preimage, and the function from $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}$ to $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}$ is **bijective**. Interchanging the letters gives the inverse, whose domain is the range of $f$, namely $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}$. As a check, $f(4) = 7$ and $f^{-1}(7) = \dfrac{24}{6} = 4$.

**Part 4.** Writing the numerator as $(x - 3) + 6$ lets us split the fraction to obtain $k = 6$. The function $y = \dfrac{6}{x}$ is odd, so its graph is symmetric about the origin. The graph of $f$ is obtained from that of $\dfrac{6}{x}$ by a **translation** of $3$ to the right and $1$ upward that takes the origin to the point $C(3, 1)$. A translation takes two points symmetric about the origin to two points symmetric about $C$, so the graph of $f$ is symmetric about $C(3, 1)$. For example, the points $A(4, 7)$ and $(2, -5)$ lie on the graph because $f(2) = \dfrac{5}{-1} = -5$, and their midpoint is $\left( \dfrac{4 + 2}{2}, \dfrac{7 - 5}{2} \right) = (3, 1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle is inscribed in a semicircle with center $O$ and radius $2$. The rectangle $PQRS$ has side $PQ$ on the diameter and vertices $R$ and $S$ on the semicircle. Let $x$ be the distance from $Q$ to $O$.

1. Express the area of the rectangle as a function of $x$ and give the domain of the function $A(x)$ in this problem.
2. Calculate the area for $x = 1$.
3. Find $x$ such that the rectangle is a square, and calculate the area of that square.

**Part 1**
$$
PQ = 2x,
\qquad
QR = \sqrt{4 - x^2}
$$
$$
A(x) = 2x\,\sqrt{4 - x^2},
\qquad
0 < x < 2
$$

**Part 2**
$$
A(1) = 2 \cdot 1 \cdot \sqrt{4 - 1} = 2\sqrt{3}
$$

**Part 3**
$$
\sqrt{4 - x^2} = 2x
\iff
4 - x^2 = 4x^2
\iff
x^2 = \frac{4}{5}
\iff
x = \frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}
$$
$$
A = (2x)^2 = 4x^2 = 4 \cdot \frac{4}{5} = \frac{16}{5}
$$

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,3.1416" linee="-1,0 1,0 1,1.732 -1,1.732 -1,0;-2,0 2,0" segmenti="0,0,1,1.732" punti="0,0,pieno;-1,0,pieno,P;1,0,pieno,Q;1,1.732,pieno,R;-1,1.732,pieno,S" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The inscribed rectangle in the semicircle of radius $2$ for $x = OQ = 1$. The vertex $R$ is at a distance $2$ from $O$, so $QR = \sqrt{4 - 1} = \sqrt{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The rectangle is symmetric about the perpendicular to the diameter through $O$, so $P$ is also at a distance $x$ from $O$ and the base is $PQ = 2x$. The vertex $R$ lies on the semicircle, so $OR = 2$. The triangle $OQR$ has a right angle at $Q$, and by the **Pythagorean theorem** its height is $QR = \sqrt{OR^2 - OQ^2} = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$. The area is the product of base and height.

The formula is defined for $-2 \leq x \leq 2$, but in this problem $x$ is a distance and the rectangle must be nondegenerate. At $x = 0$ the base is zero and at $x = 2$ the height is zero, so the domain of the function in this problem is the open interval $(0, 2)$.

**Part 2.** Substituting $x = 1$ gives a base of $2$ and a height of $\sqrt{3}$, as in the figure, and the area is $2\sqrt{3}$.

**Part 3.** The rectangle is a square when its height equals its base, that is, $\sqrt{4 - x^2} = 2x$. Both sides are positive for $0 < x < 2$, so squaring gives an equivalent equation. From $5x^2 = 4$ we obtain $x = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}$, the positive solution, which belongs to the domain because it is less than $2$. The side of the square is $2x$, so its area is $4x^2 = \dfrac{16}{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cup of tea is left to cool in a room. Its temperature in degrees Celsius after $t$ minutes is
$$
T(t) = 20 + 60\,e^{-\frac{t}{10}},
\qquad
t \geq 0 .
$$

1. Calculate the initial temperature.
2. Verify that $T$ is decreasing and find its range.
3. Find the inverse function $t(T)$, which gives the time at which the tea reaches temperature $T$, and calculate how many minutes it takes to reach $50\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$.

**Part 1**
$$
T(0) = 20 + 60\,e^{0} = 80\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}
$$

**Part 2**
$$
0 \leq t_1 < t_2
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
-\frac{t_1}{10} > -\frac{t_2}{10}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
e^{-\frac{t_1}{10}} > e^{-\frac{t_2}{10}}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
T(t_1) > T(t_2)
$$
$$
0 < e^{-\frac{t}{10}} \leq 1
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
20 < T(t) \leq 80
$$

**Part 3**
$$
T = 20 + 60\,e^{-\frac{t}{10}}
\iff
e^{-\frac{t}{10}} = \frac{T - 20}{60}
\iff
-\frac{t}{10} = \ln \frac{T - 20}{60}
$$
$$
t(T) = 10 \ln \frac{60}{T - 20},
\qquad
20 < T \leq 80
$$
$$
t(50) = 10 \ln \frac{60}{30} = 10 \ln 2 \approx 6.93 \text{ minutes}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** At the initial time $t = 0$, the exponential term equals $1$, so the temperature is $20 + 60 = 80\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$.

**Part 2.** If $t_1 < t_2$, multiplying by $-\dfrac{1}{10}$ reverses the inequality. The exponential function with base $e$ is increasing and therefore preserves the inequality, and multiplying by $60$ and adding $20$ also preserve it. It follows that $T(t_1) > T(t_2)$, which means the function is **decreasing**. For $t \geq 0$ the exponent $-\dfrac{t}{10}$ is less than or equal to $0$, so $e^{-\frac{t}{10}}$ lies between $0$, excluded, and $1$, included. The range is therefore $(20, 80]$, and the value $20\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, the room temperature, is never reached.

**Part 3.** The function is decreasing and hence injective by the theorem on monotone functions, so it has an inverse on its range. Isolate the exponential term and take logarithms. Since $-\ln \dfrac{T - 20}{60} = \ln \dfrac{60}{T - 20}$, we obtain $t(T) = 10 \ln \dfrac{60}{T - 20}$, defined for $20 < T \leq 80$. For $T = 50$ the fraction equals $2$, and the tea reaches $50\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ after $10 \ln 2 \approx 6.93$ minutes, or about $7$ minutes. As a check, $t(80) = 10 \ln 1 = 0$, which corresponds to the initial time.

</div>
