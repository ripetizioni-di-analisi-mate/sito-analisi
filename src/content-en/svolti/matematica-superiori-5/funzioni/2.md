## Exercise 1

Find the domain of the function $f(x) = \ln \left( x^2 - 9 \right)$.

$$
x^2 - 9 > 0
\iff
(x - 3)(x + 3) > 0
\iff
x < -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x > 3
$$
$$
D = (-\infty, -3) \cup (3, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is **logarithmic**, and the argument of a logarithm must be greater than $0$. The inequality is strict because the logarithm of $0$ is undefined.

The binomial $x^2 - 9$ is a difference of squares and vanishes at $x = \pm 3$. Its graph is a parabola that opens upward, so the binomial is positive outside the interval $[-3, 3]$. The domain is therefore the union of the two open rays $(-\infty, -3)$ and $(3, +\infty)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the domain of the function $f(x) = e^{\frac{1}{x - 2}} + \ln (5 - x)$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x - 2 \neq 0 \\[4pt]
5 - x > 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x \neq 2 \\[4pt]
x < 5
\end{cases}
$$
$$
D = (-\infty, 2) \cup (2, 5)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponential function with base $e$ is defined for every real exponent, so the only restriction from the first term concerns the exponent $\dfrac{1}{x - 2}$, which is defined if its denominator differs from $0$. The second term is a logarithm, and its argument $5 - x$ must be positive.

The two conditions must hold simultaneously because the sum is defined only if both terms are defined. The second gives $x < 5$. From the ray $(-\infty, 5)$, we remove the number $2$. The domain is therefore $D = (-\infty, 2) \cup (2, 5)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the domain of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{\ln (x - 1)}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x - 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
\ln (x - 1) \geq 0
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x > 1 \\[4pt]
x - 1 \geq 1
\end{cases}
\iff
x \geq 2
$$
$$
D = [2, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The conditions are twofold. The argument of the logarithm must be positive, and the radicand of the square root, which here is the logarithm itself, must be greater than or equal to $0$.

The natural logarithm is increasing and takes the value $0$ at $1$, so $\ln (x - 1) \geq 0$ is equivalent to $x - 1 \geq 1$, or $x \geq 2$. All numbers $x \geq 2$ also satisfy the first condition $x > 1$. The domain is therefore $D = [2, +\infty)$. For example, $f(2) = \sqrt{\ln 1} = 0$, whereas for $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$ we have $\ln \dfrac{1}{2} < 0$ and the square root is undefined.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the domain of the function $f(x) = \sqrt{4^{x} - 3 \cdot 2^{x} - 4}$.

$$
4^{x} - 3 \cdot 2^{x} - 4 \geq 0,
\qquad
t = 2^{x} > 0
$$
$$
t^2 - 3t - 4 \geq 0
\iff
(t - 4)(t + 1) \geq 0
\iff
t \leq -1 \quad \text{or} \quad t \geq 4
$$
$$
2^{x} \geq 4 = 2^{2}
\iff
x \geq 2
$$
$$
D = [2, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand must be greater than or equal to $0$. Since $4^{x} = \left( 2^{2} \right)^{x} = \left( 2^{x} \right)^{2}$, the substitution $t = 2^{x}$ makes the inequality quadratic in the variable $t$.

The trinomial $t^2 - 3t - 4$ has roots $-1$ and $4$ and its graph opens upward, so it is greater than or equal to $0$ for $t \leq -1$ and for $t \geq 4$. An exponential function is always positive, so the condition $2^{x} \leq -1$ has no solutions. This leaves $2^{x} \geq 4$, or $2^{x} \geq 2^{2}$. The exponential function with base $2$ is increasing, so this inequality is equivalent to $x \geq 2$. The domain is $D = [2, +\infty)$.

</div>
