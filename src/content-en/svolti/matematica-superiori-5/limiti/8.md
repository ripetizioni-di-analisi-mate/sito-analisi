## Exercise 1

Find the values of the real parameters $a$ and $b$ for which
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 + a x + b}{x - 1} = 5 .
$$

$$
1 + a + b = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
b = -1 - a
$$
$$
x^2 + a x - 1 - a = (x^2 - 1) + a\,(x - 1) = (x - 1)(x + 1 + a)
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(x - 1)(x + 1 + a)}{x - 1} = \lim_{x \to 1} (x + 1 + a) = 2 + a
$$
$$
2 + a = 5
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
a = 3, \qquad b = -4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator approaches $0$. If the numerator approached a number $\ell \neq 0$, the limit would be infinite by the rule $\dfrac{\ell}{0} = \infty$. Therefore, for the limit to equal $5$, the numerator must approach $0$, meaning that it must vanish at $x = 1$. This condition gives $1 + a + b = 0$, so $b = -1 - a$.

With this choice, the numerator factors by taking out $x - 1$ because the group of terms $x^2 - 1$ is a difference of squares and the group $a x - a$ equals $a\,(x - 1)$. After cancellation, the limit equals $2 + a$. Setting this equal to $5$ gives $a = 3$, so $b = -4$. As a check, $\dfrac{x^2 + 3x - 4}{x - 1} = \dfrac{(x - 1)(x + 4)}{x - 1} = x + 4$, which approaches $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a circle with center $O$ and radius $r$, consider the central angle $A\widehat{O}B = 2x$, with $0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

1. In terms of $x$, express the length $\ell(x)$ of the chord $AB$ and the length $s(x)$ of the arc $AB$ subtended by the angle $2x$, and calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\ell(x)}{s(x)}$.
2. Express the area $T(x)$ of triangle $OAB$ and the area $S(x)$ of circular sector $OAB$, and calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{T(x)}{S(x)}$.

**Part 1**
$$
\ell(x) = 2r \sin x,
\qquad
s(x) = r \cdot 2x = 2r x
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\ell(x)}{s(x)} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{2r \sin x}{2r x} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1
$$

**Part 2**
$$
T(x) = \frac{1}{2}\, r^2 \sin (2x),
\qquad
S(x) = \frac{1}{2}\, r^2 \cdot 2x = r^2 x
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{T(x)}{S(x)} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\dfrac{1}{2}\, r^2 \sin (2x)}{r^2 x} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\sin (2x)}{2x} = 1
$$

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="0,0,1.6209,1.1716;0,0,1.6209,-1.1716;1.6209,1.1716,1.6209,-1.1716;0,0,1.6209,0" punti="0,0,pieno,O;1.6209,1.1716,pieno,A;1.6209,-1.1716,pieno,B;1.6209,0,pieno,H" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda=0 titolo="The chord $AB$ is the base of isosceles triangle $OAB$. The altitude $OH$ divides the angle $2x$ into two angles of measure $x$, and $AH = r \sin x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** Triangle $OAB$ is isosceles because $OA = OB = r$. The altitude $OH$ to the base $AB$ also bisects the central angle, so it divides the angle $2x$ into two angles of measure $x$ and the chord into two equal parts. In right triangle $OHA$, the leg $AH$ is opposite the angle $x$. Right-triangle trigonometry gives $AH = r \sin x$, so $\ell(x) = 2r \sin x$. The length of an arc equals the radius times the angle in radians, so $s(x) = 2rx$. The factor $2r$ cancels in the quotient, and by the **standard sine limit** the quotient approaches $1$. This means that for small angles the chord and arc have nearly the same length.

**Part 2.** The area of a triangle is half the product of two sides and the sine of their included angle, so $T(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}\, r^2 \sin (2x)$. The area of the circular sector with angle $\alpha$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}\, r^2 \alpha$, and with $\alpha = 2x$ we obtain $S(x) = r^2 x$. After canceling $r^2$, the quotient is $\dfrac{\sin (2x)}{2x}$, which approaches $1$ by the standard sine limit with $t = 2x$. The ratio of the two areas therefore approaches $1$, meaning that for small angles the triangle and sector have nearly the same area. Both limits are independent of the radius $r$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider the function
$$
f(x) = \frac{a x^2 + b x}{x^2 - 4} ,
$$
where $a$ and $b$ are real parameters.

1. Find $a$ so that the line $y = 2$ is a horizontal asymptote of the graph of $f$.
2. Using this value of $a$, find $b$ so that the limit of $f(x)$ as $x \to 2$ exists and is finite, and calculate this limit.
3. Using these values, calculate the limits of $f(x)$ as $x \to -2^+$ and as $x \to -2^-$.
4. Let $a_n = f(n)$ for $n \geq 3$. Calculate the limit of the sequence $a_n$ and find the first value of $n$ from which all terms are within $\dfrac{1}{100}$ of the limit.

**Part 1**
$$
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{a x^2 + b x}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{a}{1} = a,
\qquad
a = 2
$$

**Part 2**
$$
2 \cdot 4 + 2b = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
b = -4
$$
$$
f(x) = \frac{2x^2 - 4x}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{2x\,(x - 2)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} = \frac{2x}{x + 2}
\qquad (x \neq 2)
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{2x}{x + 2} = \frac{4}{4} = 1
$$

**Part 3**
$$
\lim_{x \to -2^+} \frac{2x}{x + 2} = \frac{-4}{0^+} = -\infty,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to -2^-} \frac{2x}{x + 2} = \frac{-4}{0^-} = +\infty
$$

**Part 4**
$$
a_n = \frac{2n}{n + 2},
\qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{2n}{n + 2} = 2
$$
$$
|a_n - 2| = \left| \frac{2n - 2n - 4}{n + 2} \right| = \frac{4}{n + 2} < \frac{1}{100}
\iff
n + 2 > 400
\iff
n > 398
$$

::grafico{f="2*x/(x+2)" asintoti="x=-2;y=2" punti="2,1,vuoto" xmin=-10 xmax=10 ymin=-6 ymax=10 legenda="y = \dfrac{2x^2 - 4x}{x^2 - 4}" titolo="With $a = 2$ and $b = -4$, the graph of $f$ has horizontal asymptote $y = 2$, vertical asymptote $x = -2$ and, at $x = 2$, a missing point with vertical coordinate $1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** As $x \to \pm\infty$ the function is a quotient of polynomials of the same degree, so it approaches the quotient of the leading coefficients, which is $a$. The line $y = 2$ is a horizontal asymptote if the limit equals $2$, that is, if $a = 2$. The value of $b$ plays no role in this limit.

**Part 2.** As $x \to 2$ the denominator approaches $0$. As in Exercise 1, the limit is finite only if the numerator $2x^2 + bx$ also vanishes at $x = 2$. Otherwise the limit would be infinite. The condition $8 + 2b = 0$ gives $b = -4$. With this value the numerator is $2x\,(x - 2)$, and the factor $x - 2$ cancels with the corresponding factor in the denominator. By substitution the limit equals $1$.

**Part 3.** As $x \to -2$ the numerator of the simplified function approaches $-4$ and the denominator approaches $0$. For $x > -2$ the denominator $x + 2$ is positive, and the quotient of a negative number and a small positive number approaches $-\infty$. For $x < -2$ the denominator is negative, and the limit is $+\infty$. The line $x = -2$ is therefore a vertical asymptote, whereas at $x = 2$ the graph has only a missing point.

**Part 4.** For $n \geq 3$ we have $n \neq 2$, so $a_n = \dfrac{2n}{n + 2}$. By the theorem relating limits of sequences to limits of functions, the sequence has the same limit as the function as $x \to +\infty$, namely $2$. The distance to the limit is $\dfrac{4}{n + 2}$ because $n + 2$ is positive. The inequality $\dfrac{4}{n + 2} < \dfrac{1}{100}$ is equivalent to $n + 2 > 400$, that is, to $n > 398$. Therefore the terms are within one hundredth of $2$ **starting at** $n = 399$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A ball is dropped from a height of $2\,\mathrm{m}$. After each bounce it rises to $\dfrac{3}{4}$ times the height from which it fell.

1. Find the height $h_n$ reached after the $n$th bounce and its limit as $n \to +\infty$.
2. Find the total distance $d_n$ traveled by the ball up to the moment it hits the ground for the $(n + 1)$th time and its limit as $n \to +\infty$.

**Part 1**
$$
h_n = 2 \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{n},
\qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} 2 \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{n} = 0
$$

**Part 2**
$$
d_n = 2 + 2 \left( h_1 + h_2 + \ldots + h_n \right)
$$
$$
h_1 + h_2 + \ldots + h_n = \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{1 - \left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{n}}{1 - \dfrac{3}{4}} = 6 \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{n} \right]
$$
$$
d_n = 2 + 12 \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{n} \right] = 14 - 12 \left( \frac{3}{4} \right)^{n}
$$
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} d_n = 14\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** Each height is obtained by multiplying the previous one by $\dfrac{3}{4}$, so the heights form a **geometric sequence** with common ratio $q = \dfrac{3}{4}$. After the first bounce the height is $2 \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$, after the second it is $2 \cdot \left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^2 = \dfrac{9}{8}$, and after the $n$th it is $2 \left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{n}$. Since $0 < q < 1$, the power $q^n$ approaches $0$, so $h_n$ also approaches $0$.

**Part 2.** The ball travels $2\,\mathrm{m}$ during its initial fall. Then, on each bounce, it rises to a height $h_k$ and falls the same distance, traveling $2 h_k$ in all. By the $(n + 1)$th time it hits the ground, it has made $n$ bounces, so $d_n = 2 + 2 \left( h_1 + \ldots + h_n \right)$. The sum $h_1 + \ldots + h_n$ is the sum of the first $n$ terms of a geometric sequence with first term $h_1 = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and common ratio $\dfrac{3}{4}$, and is given by the formula $S_n = a_1 \cdot \dfrac{1 - q^n}{1 - q}$. The denominator $1 - \dfrac{3}{4}$ equals $\dfrac{1}{4}$, so the sum is $6 \left[ 1 - \left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{n} \right]$.

The term $\left( \dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{n}$ approaches $0$, so $d_n$ approaches $14$. The distance traveled increases with each bounce but always remains below $14\,\mathrm{m}$ and approaches that value.

</div>
