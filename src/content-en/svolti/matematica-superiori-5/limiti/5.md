## Exercise 1

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin (4x)}{3x}$.

$$
\frac{\sin (4x)}{3x} = \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{\sin (4x)}{4x}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{\sin (4x)}{4x} = \frac{4}{3} \cdot 1 = \frac{4}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ both numerator and denominator approach $0$, so the limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The **standard limit** for sine applies when the denominator is the argument of the sine function, namely $4x$. For this reason, multiply and divide by $4$ and take the factor $\dfrac{4}{3}$ outside the limit.

Set $t = 4x$. When $x \to 0$, we also have $t \to 0$, so by the theorem on limits of composite functions $\dfrac{\sin (4x)}{4x} = \dfrac{\sin t}{t}$ approaches $1$. The limit therefore equals $\dfrac{4}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x \sin x}$.

$$
\frac{1 - \cos x}{x \sin x} = \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \cdot \frac{x^2}{x \sin x} = \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\sin x}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\sin x} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The numerator contains $1 - \cos x$, and the corresponding standard limit has denominator $x^2$. Therefore multiply and divide by $x^2$ and simplify the quotient $\dfrac{x^2}{x \sin x}$ to $\dfrac{x}{\sin x}$.

The first factor approaches $\dfrac{1}{2}$ by the **standard cosine limit**. The second is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$, which approaches $1$, so the second factor also approaches $1$. By the product law for limits, the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos (3x)}{x \tan (2x)}$.

$$
\frac{1 - \cos (3x)}{x \tan (2x)}
= \frac{1 - \cos (3x)}{(3x)^2} \cdot \frac{9x^2}{x \tan (2x)}
= \frac{1 - \cos (3x)}{(3x)^2} \cdot \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{2x}{\tan (2x)}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos (3x)}{(3x)^2} \cdot \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{2x}{\tan (2x)} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{9}{2} \cdot 1 = \frac{9}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$ and contains two trigonometric functions with different arguments, $3x$ and $2x$. For the cosine, multiply and divide by $(3x)^2 = 9x^2$ to bring out the standard limit $\dfrac{1 - \cos t}{t^2}$ with $t = 3x$. This leaves the quotient $\dfrac{9x^2}{x \tan (2x)} = \dfrac{9x}{\tan (2x)}$, which can be written as $\dfrac{9}{2} \cdot \dfrac{2x}{\tan (2x)}$ by multiplying and dividing by $2$.

The first factor approaches $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The last is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{\tan (2x)}{2x}$, which approaches $1$ by the **standard tangent limit** with $t = 2x$, so the last factor approaches $1$. The limit therefore equals $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{9}{2} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \pi} \frac{\sin x}{x - \pi}$.

$$
t = x - \pi,
\qquad
x = t + \pi,
\qquad
x \to \pi \iff t \to 0
$$
$$
\sin x = \sin (t + \pi) = -\sin t
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to \pi} \frac{\sin x}{x - \pi} = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{-\sin t}{t} = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to \pi$ we have $\sin \pi = 0$, so the limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The standard sine limit applies when the argument approaches $0$, whereas here $x$ approaches $\pi$. For this reason, make the **change of variable** $t = x - \pi$, which approaches $0$ as $x$ approaches $\pi$.

The identity for angles that differ by $\pi$ gives $\sin (t + \pi) = -\sin t$. The limit therefore becomes $\displaystyle\lim_{t \to 0} \left( -\frac{\sin t}{t} \right)$, which equals $-1$. The minus sign agrees with the sign of the function because for $x$ slightly greater than $\pi$ the numerator is negative and the denominator is positive.

</div>
