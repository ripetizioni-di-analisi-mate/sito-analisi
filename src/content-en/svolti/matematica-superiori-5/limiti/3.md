## Exercise 1

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \left( 2x^3 - 5x^2 + 1 \right)$.

$$
2x^3 - 5x^2 + 1 \;\to\; -\infty - \infty + 1
$$
$$
2x^3 - 5x^2 + 1 = x^3 \left( 2 - \frac{5}{x} + \frac{1}{x^3} \right)
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} x^3 \left( 2 - \frac{5}{x} + \frac{1}{x^3} \right) = (-\infty) \cdot 2 = -\infty
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to -\infty$ the term $2x^3$ approaches $-\infty$ because $x^3$ is negative, and the term $-5x^2$ also approaches $-\infty$. The sum of two limits equal to $-\infty$ is $-\infty$, so there is no indeterminate form in this case. Factoring out $x^3$ nevertheless illustrates the general method. The two quotients $\dfrac{5}{x}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^3}$ approach $0$, so the factor $2 - \dfrac{5}{x} + \dfrac{1}{x^3}$ approaches $2$. The limit is the product of $-\infty$ and $2$, namely $-\infty$, the same as the limit of the leading term $2x^3$ alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{4x^2 - 3x}{1 - 2x^2}$ and interpret the result on the graph.

$$
\frac{4x^2 - 3x}{1 - 2x^2}
= \frac{x^2 \left( 4 - \dfrac{3}{x} \right)}{x^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{x^2} - 2 \right)}
= \frac{4 - \dfrac{3}{x}}{\dfrac{1}{x^2} - 2}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{4 - \dfrac{3}{x}}{\dfrac{1}{x^2} - 2} = \frac{4}{-2} = -2
$$

::grafico{f="(4*x^2-3*x)/(1-2*x^2)" asintoti="y=-2;x=-0.7071;x=0.7071" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-8 ymax=4 legenda="y = \dfrac{4x^2 - 3x}{1 - 2x^2}" titolo="As $x \to \pm\infty$ the function $y = \dfrac{4x^2 - 3x}{1 - 2x^2}$ approaches $-2$, and the line $y = -2$ is a horizontal asymptote"}

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to \pm\infty$ both the numerator and the denominator grow without bound in absolute value, so the limit has the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Factor the highest power, $x^2$, out of both the numerator and the denominator and then cancel it. After cancellation, the terms $\dfrac{3}{x}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ approach $0$, leaving the quotient of the leading coefficients, $\dfrac{4}{-2} = -2$.

The result agrees with the rule for rational functions. The numerator and denominator have the same degree, so the limit is the quotient of their leading coefficients. The limit is the same as $x \to +\infty$ and as $x \to -\infty$, so the line $y = -2$ is a **horizontal asymptote** at both ends.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{x^3 + 2}{3x - x^2}$.

$$
\frac{x^3 + 2}{3x - x^2}
= \frac{x^3 \left( 1 + \dfrac{2}{x^3} \right)}{x^2 \left( \dfrac{3}{x} - 1 \right)}
= x \cdot \frac{1 + \dfrac{2}{x^3}}{\dfrac{3}{x} - 1}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} x \cdot \frac{1 + \dfrac{2}{x^3}}{\dfrac{3}{x} - 1} = (-\infty) \cdot \frac{1}{-1} = +\infty
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The limit has the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. The numerator has degree $3$ and the denominator has degree $2$, so factor out $x^3$ in the numerator and $x^2$ in the denominator. The quotient $\dfrac{x^3}{x^2}$ equals $x$, which approaches $-\infty$, while the remaining fraction approaches $\dfrac{1}{-1} = -1$.

By the sign rules, the product of $-\infty$ and the negative number $-1$ is $+\infty$. The limit is therefore $+\infty$. The sign can also be checked using just the leading terms, because $\dfrac{x^3}{-x^2} = -x$, and the limit of $-x$ as $x \to -\infty$ is $+\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x^2}{x + 1} - x \right)$.

$$
\frac{x^2}{x + 1} - x \;\to\; +\infty - \infty
$$
$$
\frac{x^2}{x + 1} - x = \frac{x^2 - x\,(x + 1)}{x + 1} = \frac{x^2 - x^2 - x}{x + 1} = \frac{-x}{x + 1}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{-x}{x + 1} = \frac{-1}{1} = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first term approaches $+\infty$ because the numerator has higher degree than the denominator, and the second term approaches $-\infty$. The limit therefore has the **indeterminate form** $+\infty - \infty$. To eliminate it, subtract the terms using the common denominator $x + 1$. In the numerator the terms $x^2$ cancel, leaving $-x$.

The resulting function is a quotient of two linear polynomials, so as $x \to +\infty$ it approaches the quotient of their leading coefficients, which is $\dfrac{-1}{1} = -1$. The limit is therefore $-1$. The result means that, for large $x$, the graph of $\dfrac{x^2}{x + 1}$ approaches the line $y = x - 1$. This line is an oblique asymptote. Oblique asymptotes are studied in the chapter on continuity and asymptotes.

</div>
