## Exercise 1

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{\sqrt{x + 1} - 2}{x - 3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{x + 1} - 2}{x - 3}
&= \frac{\left( \sqrt{x + 1} - 2 \right)\left( \sqrt{x + 1} + 2 \right)}{(x - 3)\left( \sqrt{x + 1} + 2 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x + 1 - 4}{(x - 3)\left( \sqrt{x + 1} + 2 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + 2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x + 1} + 2} = \frac{1}{2 + 2} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting $x = 3$ gives $\sqrt{4} - 2 = 0$ in the numerator and $0$ in the denominator, so the limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The difference $\sqrt{x + 1} - 2$ is eliminated by **rationalization**: multiply the numerator and denominator by the sum $\sqrt{x + 1} + 2$. The product of the difference and the sum of the same two terms is the difference of their squares, so the numerator becomes $(x + 1) - 4 = x - 3$.

The factor $x - 3$ cancels because $x \neq 3$ when calculating the limit. The remaining function is defined at $3$, and substitution gives the limit $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x}{\sqrt{1 + x} - \sqrt{1 - x}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{\sqrt{1 + x} - \sqrt{1 - x}}
&= \frac{x \left( \sqrt{1 + x} + \sqrt{1 - x} \right)}{(1 + x) - (1 - x)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x \left( \sqrt{1 + x} + \sqrt{1 - x} \right)}{2x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sqrt{1 + x} + \sqrt{1 - x}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{1 + x} + \sqrt{1 - x}}{2} = \frac{1 + 1}{2} = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ the numerator approaches $0$ and the denominator approaches $1 - 1 = 0$, so the expression has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Here the difference of square roots is in the denominator. Multiply the numerator and denominator by the sum $\sqrt{1 + x} + \sqrt{1 - x}$. The denominator becomes the difference of the radicands, $(1 + x) - (1 - x) = 2x$.

The factor $x$ now appears in both numerator and denominator and cancels. The remaining expression approaches $\dfrac{2}{2}$, so the limit equals $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 - 2x + 3} - x \right)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x^2 - 2x + 3} - x
&= \frac{\left( x^2 - 2x + 3 \right) - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 - 2x + 3} + x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{-2x + 3}{\sqrt{x^2 - 2x + 3} + x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x \left( -2 + \dfrac{3}{x} \right)}{x \left( \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{2}{x} + \dfrac{3}{x^2}} + 1 \right)} \qquad (x > 0)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{-2 + \dfrac{3}{x}}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{2}{x} + \dfrac{3}{x^2}} + 1} = \frac{-2}{1 + 1} = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square root approaches $+\infty$ and the term $-x$ approaches $-\infty$, so the limit has the **indeterminate form** $+\infty - \infty$. Multiply and divide by the sum $\sqrt{x^2 - 2x + 3} + x$ to obtain a difference of squares in the numerator, in which the $x^2$ terms cancel. The form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$ remains.

To factor out $x$ under the square root, write $\sqrt{x^2 - 2x + 3} = \sqrt{x^2} \cdot \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{2}{x} + \dfrac{3}{x^2}}$ and use $\sqrt{x^2} = |x| = x$, which holds because $x > 0$. After canceling $x$, the numerator approaches $-2$ and the denominator approaches $2$, so the limit equals $-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + x} + x \right)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x
&= \frac{\left( x^2 + x \right) - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + x} - x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x}{|x| \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} - x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x}{-x \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} - x} \qquad (x < 0) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x}{-x \left( \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} + 1 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} + 1}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \left( -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} + 1} \right) = -\frac{1}{1 + 1} = -\frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to -\infty$ the square root approaches $+\infty$ and $x$ approaches $-\infty$, so once again the expression has the form $+\infty - \infty$. Multiply and divide by $\sqrt{x^2 + x} - x$, which for negative $x$ is the sum of two positive numbers and therefore cannot be zero. This leaves $x$ in the numerator.

The step that requires care is factoring under the square root. Since $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$ and $x < 0$, we have $|x| = -x$, so $\sqrt{x^2 + x} = -x \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}}$. Factor out $-x$ in the denominator and cancel the factor $x$ with the numerator, leaving the minus sign. The expression approaches $-\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the limit equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Writing $\sqrt{x^2} = x$ would give the denominator $x\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} - x$, which is still an indeterminate form, so the calculation would fail to give the result.

</div>
