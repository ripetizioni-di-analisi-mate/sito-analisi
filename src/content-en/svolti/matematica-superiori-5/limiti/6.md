## Exercise 1

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{3x} - 1}{2x}$.

$$
\frac{e^{3x} - 1}{2x} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{e^{3x} - 1}{3x}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{e^{3x} - 1}{3x} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 1 = \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ we have $e^{0} - 1 = 0$, so the limit has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The **standard limit** $\dfrac{e^{t} - 1}{t} \to 1$ requires the exponent $3x$ to appear in the denominator. Therefore multiply and divide by $3$ and take the factor $\dfrac{3}{2}$ outside the limit. With $t = 3x$, which approaches $0$, the second factor approaches $1$, and the limit equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2^{x} - 3^{x}}{x}$.

$$
\frac{2^{x} - 3^{x}}{x} = \frac{\left( 2^{x} - 1 \right) - \left( 3^{x} - 1 \right)}{x} = \frac{2^{x} - 1}{x} - \frac{3^{x} - 1}{x}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{2^{x} - 1}{x} - \frac{3^{x} - 1}{x} \right) = \ln 2 - \ln 3 = \ln \frac{2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ the numerator approaches $1 - 1 = 0$, so the expression has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Add and subtract $1$ in the numerator to obtain two expressions of the form $a^{x} - 1$, and split the fraction into a difference of two fractions.

By the **standard limit** $\dfrac{a^{x} - 1}{x} \to \ln a$, the first fraction approaches $\ln 2$ and the second approaches $\ln 3$. Both limits are finite, so the sum law gives the limit $\ln 2 - \ln 3$, which equals $\ln \dfrac{2}{3}$ by the properties of logarithms. The result is negative because $\dfrac{2}{3} < 1$. Indeed, for small $x > 0$, we have $2^{x} < 3^{x}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln \left( 1 + x^2 \right)}{x \left( e^{x} - 1 \right)}$.

$$
\frac{\ln \left( 1 + x^2 \right)}{x \left( e^{x} - 1 \right)} = \frac{\ln \left( 1 + x^2 \right)}{x^2} \cdot \frac{x}{e^{x} - 1}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln \left( 1 + x^2 \right)}{x^2} \cdot \frac{x}{e^{x} - 1} = 1 \cdot 1 = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ the numerator approaches $\ln 1 = 0$ and the denominator approaches $0 \cdot 0 = 0$, so the expression has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The **standard logarithmic limit** $\dfrac{\ln (1 + t)}{t} \to 1$ applies with $t = x^2$, which approaches $0$. For this reason, multiply and divide by $x^2$. After cancellation, the quotient $\dfrac{x^2}{x \left( e^{x} - 1 \right)} = \dfrac{x}{e^{x} - 1}$ remains.

The first factor approaches $1$. The second is the reciprocal of $\dfrac{e^{x} - 1}{x}$, which approaches $1$, so the second factor also approaches $1$. The limit therefore equals $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x + 1}{x - 2} \right)^{x}$.

$$
\frac{x + 1}{x - 2} = \frac{(x - 2) + 3}{x - 2} = 1 + \frac{3}{x - 2}
$$
$$
t = x - 2,
\qquad
x = t + 2,
\qquad
x \to +\infty \iff t \to +\infty
$$
$$
\left( 1 + \frac{3}{t} \right)^{t + 2} = \left( 1 + \frac{3}{t} \right)^{t} \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{3}{t} \right)^{2}
$$
$$
\lim_{t \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{3}{t} \right)^{t} \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{3}{t} \right)^{2} = e^{3} \cdot 1 = e^{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base $\dfrac{x + 1}{x - 2}$ approaches $1$ because it is a quotient of linear polynomials with equal leading coefficients, and the exponent approaches $+\infty$. The limit therefore has the **indeterminate form** $1^{\infty}$. Write the base in the form $1 + \dfrac{3}{x - 2}$ by adding and subtracting $2$ in the numerator.

With the substitution $t = x - 2$, the exponent becomes $t + 2$, and the laws of exponents split the power into two factors. The first approaches $e^{3}$ by the standard limit $\left( 1 + \dfrac{k}{t} \right)^{t} \to e^{k}$ with $k = 3$. In the second factor the base approaches $1$ and the exponent is fixed, so the factor approaches $1$. The limit therefore equals $e^{3}$.

</div>
