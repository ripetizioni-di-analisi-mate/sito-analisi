## Exercise 1

As $x \to 0$, compare the infinitesimals $f(x) = 1 - \cos (2x)$ and $g(x) = x \sin x$, and find the order of $f$ relative to $x$.

$$
\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{1 - \cos (2x)}{x \sin x} = \frac{1 - \cos (2x)}{(2x)^2} \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{x}{\sin x}
\;\to\; \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 1 = 2
$$
$$
\frac{f(x)}{x^2} = \frac{1 - \cos (2x)}{(2x)^2} \cdot 4 \;\to\; \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both functions approach $0$ as $x \to 0$, so they are infinitesimals. To compare them, calculate the limit of their quotient, which has the form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Multiplying and dividing by $(2x)^2 = 4x^2$ brings out the standard cosine limit with $t = 2x$ and leaves the factor $\dfrac{4x^2}{x \sin x} = 4 \cdot \dfrac{x}{\sin x}$, which approaches $4$.

The quotient approaches $2$, a finite number different from $0$, so $f$ and $g$ are **infinitesimals of the same order** without being equivalent. Similarly, $\dfrac{f(x)}{x^2}$ approaches $2$, so $f$ is an infinitesimal of **order** $2$ relative to $x$. The function $g$ also has order $2$ because $\dfrac{x \sin x}{x^2} = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ approaches $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Use substitution of equivalent infinitesimals to calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin (3x) + x^2}{\ln (1 + x)}$.

$$
\sin (3x) + x^2 \sim \sin (3x) \sim 3x,
\qquad
\ln (1 + x) \sim x
\qquad (x \to 0)
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin (3x) + x^2}{\ln (1 + x)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{3x}{x} = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is the sum of two infinitesimals, $\sin (3x)$ of order $1$ and $x^2$ of order $2$. The higher-order infinitesimal in a sum can be neglected, so the numerator is equivalent to $\sin (3x)$, which in turn is equivalent to $3x$ by the standard sine limit. By the standard logarithmic limit, the denominator is equivalent to $x$.

By the **substitution principle**, the limit equals that of $\dfrac{3x}{x} = 3$. The limit therefore equals $3$. The same result follows by dividing the numerator and denominator by $x$. The numerator becomes $3 \cdot \dfrac{\sin (3x)}{3x} + x$, which approaches $3$, and the denominator becomes $\dfrac{\ln (1 + x)}{x}$, which approaches $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^4 + 3^{x}}{2^{x} + 3^{x + 1}}$.

$$
\frac{x^4 + 3^{x}}{2^{x} + 3^{x + 1}} = \frac{x^4 + 3^{x}}{2^{x} + 3 \cdot 3^{x}} = \frac{\dfrac{x^4}{3^{x}} + 1}{\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{x} + 3}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\dfrac{x^4}{3^{x}} + 1}{\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{x} + 3} = \frac{0 + 1}{0 + 3} = \frac{1}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator and denominator approach $+\infty$, so the form is $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. By the **hierarchy of growth rates**, in the numerator the exponential $3^{x}$ grows faster than the power $x^4$, and in the denominator $3^{x + 1} = 3 \cdot 3^{x}$ grows faster than $2^{x}$ because it has the larger base. Thus the fastest-growing term is $3^{x}$. Divide the numerator and denominator by $3^{x}$.

The quotient $\dfrac{x^4}{3^{x}}$ approaches $0$ by the hierarchy of growth rates, and $\dfrac{2^{x}}{3^{x}} = \left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{x}$ approaches $0$ because its base lies between $0$ and $1$. The limit therefore equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Calculate $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{2x + \cos x}{x + 1}$.

$$
-1 \leq \cos x \leq 1
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\frac{2x - 1}{x + 1} \leq \frac{2x + \cos x}{x + 1} \leq \frac{2x + 1}{x + 1}
\qquad (x > -1)
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{2x - 1}{x + 1} = 2,
\qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{2x + 1}{x + 1} = 2
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{2x + \cos x}{x + 1} = 2
$$

::grafico{f="(2*x+cos(x))/(x+1);(2*x+1)/(x+1)" asintoti="y=2" xmin=0 xmax=20 ymin=0 ymax=3 legenda="y = \dfrac{2x + \cos x}{x + 1};y = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x + 1}" titolo="The graph of $y = \dfrac{2x + \cos x}{x + 1}$ oscillates below the graph of $y = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x + 1}$ and approaches the line $y = 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $\cos x$ has no limit as $x \to +\infty$, so the limit laws cannot be applied directly to the numerator. However, $\cos x$ lies between $-1$ and $1$. Adding $2x$ to all three expressions and dividing by $x + 1$, which is positive for $x > -1$, gives a compound inequality that holds for every $x > -1$.

The two bounding functions are rational with linear numerators and denominators. Both approach the quotient of their leading coefficients, namely $2$. By the **squeeze theorem**, the function between them also approaches $2$. The limit therefore equals $2$, and the line $y = 2$ is a horizontal asymptote of the graph.

</div>
