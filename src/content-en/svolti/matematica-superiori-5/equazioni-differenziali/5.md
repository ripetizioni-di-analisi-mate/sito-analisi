## Exercise 1

Find the general solution of the equation $y'' - 3y' + 2y = 4x^2$.

$$
\lambda^2 - 3\lambda + 2 = 0
\iff
\lambda = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad \lambda = 2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y_0 = c_1\,e^{x} + c_2\,e^{2x}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = Ax^2 + Bx + C,
\qquad
\bar{y}' = 2Ax + B,
\qquad
\bar{y}'' = 2A
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar{y}'' - 3\bar{y}' + 2\bar{y}
&= 2A - 3\,(2Ax + B) + 2\,(Ax^2 + Bx + C) \\[6pt]
&= 2A\,x^2 + (2B - 6A)\,x + (2A - 3B + 2C)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
2A = 4 \\
2B - 6A = 0 \\
2A - 3B + 2C = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
A = 2, \quad B = 6, \quad C = 7
$$
$$
y = c_1\,e^{x} + c_2\,e^{2x} + 2x^2 + 6x + 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on the **general solution of a nonhomogeneous equation**, we seek the general solution $y_0$ of the homogeneous equation and a particular solution $\bar{y}$. The characteristic equation has real roots $1$ and $2$, so $y_0 = c_1\,e^{x} + c_2\,e^{2x}$.

The forcing term is a quadratic polynomial and the coefficient of $y$ is $c = 2 \neq 0$, so by the **method of undetermined coefficients** we try a quadratic polynomial $Ax^2 + Bx + C$. Substituting and collecting like powers of $x$ gives a polynomial that must equal $4x^2$ for every $x$. Two polynomials are identical when their coefficients agree, so matching the coefficients of $x^2$, the coefficients of $x$, and the constant terms gives three simultaneous equations.

The first equation gives $A = 2$, the second gives $2B = 12$, that is, $B = 6$, and the third gives $4 - 18 + 2C = 0$, that is, $C = 7$. Adding $y_0$ and $\bar{y}$ gives the general solution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the general solution of the equation $y'' + y' = 2x + 1$.

$$
\lambda^2 + \lambda = 0
\iff
\lambda\,(\lambda + 1) = 0
\iff
\lambda = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad \lambda = -1
$$
$$
y_0 = c_1 + c_2\,e^{-x}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = x\,(Ax + B) = Ax^2 + Bx,
\qquad
\bar{y}' = 2Ax + B,
\qquad
\bar{y}'' = 2A
$$
$$
2A + 2Ax + B = 2x + 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\begin{cases}
2A = 2 \\
2A + B = 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
A = 1, \quad B = -1
$$
$$
y = c_1 + c_2\,e^{-x} + x^2 - x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The roots of the characteristic equation are $0$ and $-1$. Since $e^{0 \cdot x} = 1$, the general solution of the homogeneous equation is $y_0 = c_1 + c_2\,e^{-x}$ and includes the constant functions.

The equation has no term in $y$, that is, $c = 0$, whereas $b = 1 \neq 0$. A linear polynomial $Ax + B$ does not suffice because its constant term $B$ solves the homogeneous equation and thus disappears from the left-hand side. Therefore, as prescribed by the **method of undetermined coefficients**, we try a linear polynomial multiplied by $x$.

Substitution gives $2Ax + (2A + B)$, which must equal $2x + 1$. Equating the coefficients of $x$ gives $A = 1$, and equating the constant terms gives $2 + B = 1$, that is, $B = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' - 4y = 6\,e^{x} \\
y(0) = 0 \\
y'(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\lambda^2 - 4 = 0
\iff
\lambda = \pm 2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y_0 = c_1\,e^{2x} + c_2\,e^{-2x}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = A\,e^{x}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
A\,e^{x} - 4A\,e^{x} = 6\,e^{x}
\iff
-3A = 6
\iff
A = -2
$$
$$
y = c_1\,e^{2x} + c_2\,e^{-2x} - 2\,e^{x},
\qquad
y' = 2c_1\,e^{2x} - 2c_2\,e^{-2x} - 2\,e^{x}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
c_1 + c_2 - 2 = 0 \\
2c_1 - 2c_2 - 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\begin{cases}
c_1 + c_2 = 2 \\
c_1 - c_2 = 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
c_1 = \frac{3}{2}, \quad c_2 = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
y = \frac{3}{2}\,e^{2x} + \frac{1}{2}\,e^{-2x} - 2\,e^{x}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic equation has roots $2$ and $-2$. The forcing term is $6\,e^{x}$ with $\mu = 1$, and $1$ is not a root of the characteristic equation, so we try a particular solution of the form $A\,e^{x}$. Its derivatives are both equal to $A\,e^{x}$, and dividing by $e^{x}$ gives $-3A = 6$, that is, $A = -2$.

Impose the initial conditions on the general solution of the nonhomogeneous equation, that is, after adding $\bar{y}$. For $x = 0$ all the exponentials equal $1$, so $y(0) = c_1 + c_2 - 2$ and $y'(0) = 2c_1 - 2c_2 - 2$. Dividing the second equation by $2$ gives $c_1 - c_2 = 1$. Adding the two equations gives $2c_1 = 3$, that is, $c_1 = \dfrac{3}{2}$, and therefore $c_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the general solution of the equation $y'' - 2y' + y = 2\,e^{x}$.

$$
\lambda^2 - 2\lambda + 1 = (\lambda - 1)^2 = 0
\iff
\lambda = 1 \quad \text{double root}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y_0 = (c_1 + c_2\,x)\,e^{x}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = A\,x^2 e^{x},
\qquad
\bar{y}' = A\,(x^2 + 2x)\,e^{x},
\qquad
\bar{y}'' = A\,(x^2 + 4x + 2)\,e^{x}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar{y}'' - 2\bar{y}' + \bar{y}
&= A\,e^{x} \left( x^2 + 4x + 2 - 2x^2 - 4x + x^2 \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2A\,e^{x}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
2A\,e^{x} = 2\,e^{x}
\iff
A = 1
$$
$$
y = (c_1 + c_2\,x)\,e^{x} + x^2 e^{x}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic equation has the double root $\lambda = 1$, so the functions $e^{x}$ and $x\,e^{x}$ solve the homogeneous equation. The forcing term contains $e^{x}$ with $\mu = 1$, which is precisely the double root. Neither $A\,e^{x}$ nor $A\,x\,e^{x}$ can produce the forcing term because substituting either into the left-hand side gives $0$. By the **method of undetermined coefficients**, we therefore try $\bar{y} = A\,x^2 e^{x}$.

Use the product rule to calculate the derivatives. The derivative of $x^2 e^{x}$ is $2x\,e^{x} + x^2 e^{x} = (x^2 + 2x)\,e^{x}$, and differentiating $(x^2 + 2x)\,e^{x}$ once more gives $(2x + 2)\,e^{x} + (x^2 + 2x)\,e^{x} = (x^2 + 4x + 2)\,e^{x}$.

On the left-hand side, the terms in $x^2$ give $1 - 2 + 1 = 0$ and those in $x$ give $4 - 4 = 0$, leaving the constant term $2$. Thus the left-hand side equals $2A\,e^{x}$, and equating it to $2\,e^{x}$ gives $A = 1$.

</div>
