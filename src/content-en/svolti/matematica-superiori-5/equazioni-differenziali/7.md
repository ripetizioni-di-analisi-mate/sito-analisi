## Exercise 1

A sample of radioactive material has initial mass $40\,\mathrm{g}$, and after $6$ days its mass is $10\,\mathrm{g}$. Assuming that its mass $m(t)$ satisfies the equation $m'(t) = -k\,m(t)$, find the decay constant $k$, the half-life, the mass after $9$ days, and the time when the mass equals $2.5\,\mathrm{g}$.

$$
m(t) = 40\,e^{-kt}
$$
$$
40\,e^{-6k} = 10
\iff
e^{-6k} = \frac{1}{4}
\iff
k = \frac{\ln 4}{6} = \frac{\ln 2}{3} \approx 0.231\ \text{days}^{-1}
$$
$$
T = \frac{\ln 2}{k} = 3\ \text{days}
$$
$$
m(t) = 40 \cdot 2^{-\frac{t}{3}},
\qquad
m(9) = 40 \cdot 2^{-3} = 5\,\mathrm{g}
$$
$$
40 \cdot 2^{-\frac{t}{3}} = 2.5
\iff
2^{-\frac{t}{3}} = \frac{1}{16} = 2^{-4}
\iff
t = 12\ \text{days}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation $m' = -k\,m$ is the law of **radioactive decay**, and the solution with $m(0) = 40$ is $m(t) = 40\,e^{-kt}$. The condition $m(6) = 10$ gives $e^{-6k} = \dfrac{1}{4}$. Taking logarithms gives $-6k = -\ln 4$, and since $\ln 4 = 2 \ln 2$, we find $k = \dfrac{\ln 2}{3}$.

The half-life is $T = \dfrac{\ln 2}{k} = 3$ days. The data give the same result: in $6$ days the mass has fallen to one quarter of its initial value, so it has halved twice. Since $e^{-kt} = e^{-\frac{\ln 2}{3} t} = 2^{-\frac{t}{3}}$, the mass can be written as $m(t) = 40 \cdot 2^{-\frac{t}{3}}$, and after $9$ days, that is, three half-lives, the mass is $\dfrac{40}{8} = 5\,\mathrm{g}$.

For the last question, divide by $40$. The number $\dfrac{2.5}{40} = \dfrac{1}{16}$ equals $2^{-4}$, so $\dfrac{t}{3} = 4$ and the mass equals $2.5\,\mathrm{g}$ after **12 days**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cup of tea at $90\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is left in a room at a constant temperature of $20\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. After $5$ minutes the tea is at $55\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. Use Newton's law of cooling to find the temperature $T(t)$ as a function of time, the time when the tea reaches $37.5\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, and the temperature after $20$ minutes.

$$
T'(t) = -k\,\big( T(t) - 20 \big),
\qquad
T(0) = 90
$$
$$
T(t) = 20 + 70\,e^{-kt}
$$
$$
20 + 70\,e^{-5k} = 55
\iff
e^{-5k} = \frac{1}{2}
\iff
k = \frac{\ln 2}{5}
$$
$$
T(t) = 20 + 70 \cdot 2^{-\frac{t}{5}}
$$
$$
20 + 70 \cdot 2^{-\frac{t}{5}} = 37.5
\iff
2^{-\frac{t}{5}} = \frac{17.5}{70} = \frac{1}{4}
\iff
t = 10\ \text{min}
$$
$$
T(20) = 20 + 70 \cdot 2^{-4} = 20 + 4.375 \approx 24.4\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}
$$

::grafico{f="20+70*exp(-log(2)*x/5)" punti="0,90,pieno;5,55,pieno;10,37.5,pieno" asintoti="y=20" xmin=0 xmax=20 ymin=0 ymax=100 nomi="t,T" legenda="T = 20 + 70 \cdot 2^{-\frac{t}{5}}" titolo="The tea temperature approaches the room temperature, $20\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, and the temperature difference is halved every $5$ minutes"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **law of cooling**, the function $T(t) - 20$ satisfies the decay equation, so $T(t) - 20 = \big( T(0) - 20 \big)\,e^{-kt} = 70\,e^{-kt}$. The condition $T(5) = 55$ gives $70\,e^{-5k} = 35$, that is, $e^{-5k} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and therefore $k = \dfrac{\ln 2}{5}$ per minute.

With this value, $e^{-kt} = 2^{-\frac{t}{5}}$, so the temperature difference between the tea and the room halves every $5$ minutes. The temperature $37.5\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ corresponds to a difference of $17.5\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, one quarter of the initial difference $70\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. Two halvings take $10$ minutes.

After $20$ minutes the difference has halved four times and equals $\dfrac{70}{16} = 4.375\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. The temperature is therefore approximately $24.4\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object of mass $m = 0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ falls from rest in a fluid that exerts a drag force $-\gamma\,v$, with $\gamma = 0.25\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. Using $g = 9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, write the differential equation for the velocity $v(t)$, solve it with the condition $v(0) = 0$, and find the terminal velocity and the time at which the velocity reaches half the terminal velocity.

$$
m\,v' = mg - \gamma\,v
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
0.5\,v' = 4.9 - 0.25\,v
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
v' + \frac{1}{2}\,v = 9.8
$$
$$
v(t) = \frac{9.8}{\frac{1}{2}} + c\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}} = 19.6 + c\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}}
$$
$$
v(0) = 19.6 + c = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
c = -19.6
$$
$$
v(t) = 19.6 \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{2}} \right),
\qquad
v_L = \lim_{t \to +\infty} v(t) = 19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
19.6 \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{2}} \right) = 9.8
\iff
e^{-\frac{t}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}
\iff
t = 2 \ln 2 \approx 1.39\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces on the object are its weight $mg = 0.5 \cdot 9.8 = 4.9\,\mathrm{N}$, directed downward, and the drag force $-\gamma\,v$, opposite to its velocity. Take the positive axis to point downward. By Newton's second law, $m\,v' = mg - \gamma\,v$. Dividing by $m = 0.5$ gives the constant-coefficient linear equation $v' + \dfrac{1}{2}\,v = 9.8$.

By the observation on **equations with constant coefficients**, with $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $b = 9.8$ the general solution is $v = \dfrac{b}{a} + c\,e^{-at} = 19.6 + c\,e^{-\frac{t}{2}}$. The condition $v(0) = 0$ gives $c = -19.6$.

As $t \to +\infty$, the exponential approaches $0$, so the velocity approaches the **terminal velocity** $v_L = 19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which equals $\dfrac{mg}{\gamma} = \dfrac{4.9}{0.25}$. The velocity is half of $v_L$ when $1 - e^{-\frac{t}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, when $e^{-\frac{t}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Taking logarithms gives $-\dfrac{t}{2} = -\ln 2$ and therefore $t = 2 \ln 2 \approx 1.39\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The number $P(t)$ of individuals in an insect population, with $t$ in days, satisfies the logistic equation
$$
P'(t) = P(t) \left( 1 - \frac{P(t)}{600} \right),
\qquad
P(0) = 100 .
$$
Solve the equation by separating the variables, and find the time when the population reaches $300$ individuals and the value that $P(t)$ approaches.

$$
\frac{600}{P\,(600 - P)}\,P' = 1,
\qquad
\frac{600}{P\,(600 - P)} = \frac{1}{P} + \frac{1}{600 - P}
$$
$$
\int \left( \frac{1}{P} + \frac{1}{600 - P} \right) dP = \int dt
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\ln P - \ln (600 - P) = t + c
$$
$$
\ln \frac{P}{600 - P} = t + c,
\qquad
c = \ln \frac{100}{500} = \ln \frac{1}{5}
$$
$$
\frac{P}{600 - P} = \frac{1}{5}\,e^{t}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
5P = (600 - P)\,e^{t}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
P\,\left( 5 + e^{t} \right) = 600\,e^{t}
$$
$$
P(t) = \frac{600\,e^{t}}{5 + e^{t}} = \frac{600}{1 + 5\,e^{-t}}
$$
$$
\frac{600}{1 + 5\,e^{-t}} = 300
\iff
5\,e^{-t} = 1
\iff
t = \ln 5 \approx 1.61\ \text{days}
$$
$$
\lim_{t \to +\infty} P(t) = 600
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side can be written as $\dfrac{P\,(600 - P)}{600}$, so the equation is **separable**. The constant solutions are $P = 0$ and $P = 600$, neither of which satisfies $P(0) = 100$. Multiply by $\dfrac{600}{P\,(600 - P)}$ and decompose the fraction into partial fractions. The decomposition is correct because writing $\dfrac{1}{P} + \dfrac{1}{600 - P}$ over the common denominator $P\,(600 - P)$ gives the numerator $600 - P + P = 600$.

The solution we seek takes values between $0$ and $600$ because it starts at $100$ and, by the existence and uniqueness theorem, cannot reach either constant solution. Therefore $P > 0$ and $600 - P > 0$, and the absolute values in the logarithms can be omitted. An antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{600 - P}$ is $-\ln(600 - P)$ because the derivative of $600 - P$ is $-1$.

For $t = 0$ we have $\dfrac{100}{500} = \dfrac{1}{5}$, so $c = \ln \dfrac{1}{5}$ and, on exponentiating, $\dfrac{P}{600 - P} = \dfrac{1}{5}\,e^{t}$. Solve for $P$ by collecting all the terms containing $P$ on the left. Dividing the numerator and denominator by $e^{t}$ gives the form $\dfrac{600}{1 + 5\,e^{-t}}$, which is the formula for the **logistic model** with $M = 600$, $P_0 = 100$, and $k = 1$.

The population equals $300$ when the denominator equals $2$, that is, when $e^{-t} = \dfrac{1}{5}$, so this occurs after $\ln 5 \approx 1.61$ days. As $t \to +\infty$, the term $5\,e^{-t}$ approaches $0$ and the population approaches $600$ individuals.

</div>
