## Exercise 1

Find the general solution of the equation $y'' + 3y' + 2y = 10 \sin x$.

$$
\lambda^2 + 3\lambda + 2 = 0
\iff
\lambda = -1 \quad \text{or} \quad \lambda = -2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y_0 = c_1\,e^{-x} + c_2\,e^{-2x}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = A \cos x + B \sin x,
\qquad
\bar{y}' = -A \sin x + B \cos x,
\qquad
\bar{y}'' = -A \cos x - B \sin x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar{y}'' + 3\bar{y}' + 2\bar{y}
&= (-A + 3B + 2A) \cos x + (-B - 3A + 2B) \sin x \\[6pt]
&= (A + 3B) \cos x + (B - 3A) \sin x
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
A + 3B = 0 \\
B - 3A = 10
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
A = -3, \quad B = 1
$$
$$
y = c_1\,e^{-x} + c_2\,e^{-2x} + \sin x - 3 \cos x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic equation has roots $-1$ and $-2$, so the homogeneous equation has general solution $c_1\,e^{-x} + c_2\,e^{-2x}$. The forcing term is $10 \sin x$, with $\omega = 1$. Since $b = 3 \neq 0$, the functions $\cos x$ and $\sin x$ do not solve the homogeneous equation, so by the **method of undetermined coefficients** we try $\bar{y} = A \cos x + B \sin x$. Although the forcing term contains only sine, we need both terms in the trial function because differentiating sine gives cosine.

After substituting, collect the terms in $\cos x$ and the terms in $\sin x$. The left-hand side must equal $0 \cdot \cos x + 10 \sin x$ for every $x$, so the coefficient of $\cos x$ is $0$ and the coefficient of $\sin x$ is $10$. The first equation gives $A = -3B$. Substituting into the second gives $B + 9B = 10$, that is, $B = 1$, and therefore $A = -3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' + y = 2 \cos x \\
y(0) = 0 \\
y'(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
and describe how the solution behaves.

$$
\lambda^2 + 1 = 0,
\qquad
\alpha = 0, \quad \beta = 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y_0 = c_1 \cos x + c_2 \sin x
$$
$$
\bar{y} = x\,(A \cos x + B \sin x)
$$
$$
\bar{y}' = A \cos x + B \sin x + x\,(-A \sin x + B \cos x)
$$
$$
\bar{y}'' = -2A \sin x + 2B \cos x - x\,(A \cos x + B \sin x)
$$
$$
\bar{y}'' + \bar{y} = -2A \sin x + 2B \cos x = 2 \cos x
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
A = 0, \quad B = 1
$$
$$
y = c_1 \cos x + c_2 \sin x + x \sin x
$$
$$
y(0) = c_1 = 0,
\qquad
y' = c_2 \cos x + \sin x + x \cos x,
\qquad
y'(0) = c_2 = 0
$$
$$
y = x \sin x
$$

::grafico{f="x*sin(x);x" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=20 ymin=-20 ymax=20 legenda="y = x\sin x;y = x" titolo="The solution $y = x \sin x$ oscillates between the lines $y = x$ and $y = -x$ with increasing amplitude. This phenomenon is called resonance"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The homogeneous equation $y'' + y = 0$ has general solution $c_1 \cos x + c_2 \sin x$. The forcing term $2 \cos x$ has $\omega = 1$, and we have $b = 0$ and $\omega^2 = 1 = \dfrac{c}{a}$. Thus $\cos x$ and $\sin x$ solve the homogeneous equation, so by the method of undetermined coefficients we try $A \cos x + B \sin x$ multiplied by $x$.

Use the product rule to calculate the derivatives. In the second derivative the term $-x\,(A \cos x + B \sin x)$ is the negative of $\bar{y}$, so it cancels in the sum $\bar{y}'' + \bar{y}$ and only $-2A \sin x + 2B \cos x$ remains. Comparing with $2 \cos x$ gives $A = 0$ and $B = 1$, that is, $\bar{y} = x \sin x$.

For $x = 0$ we have $y(0) = c_1$, so $c_1 = 0$. The derivative of $x \sin x$ is $\sin x + x \cos x$, which equals $0$ at $x = 0$, so $y'(0) = c_2$ and also $c_2 = 0$. The solution is $y = x \sin x$. Since $-1 \leq \sin x \leq 1$, for $x > 0$ the graph lies between the lines $y = -x$ and $y = x$, touching them alternately, so the amplitude of the oscillations **grows without bound**. This is the phenomenon of resonance.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' - y = 2x + 3\,e^{2x} \\
y(0) = 1 \\
y'(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\lambda^2 - 1 = 0
\iff
\lambda = \pm 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y_0 = c_1\,e^{x} + c_2\,e^{-x}
$$
$$
\bar{y}_1 = Ax + B
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
0 - (Ax + B) = 2x
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
A = -2, \quad B = 0
$$
$$
\bar{y}_2 = C\,e^{2x}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
4C\,e^{2x} - C\,e^{2x} = 3\,e^{2x}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
C = 1
$$
$$
y = c_1\,e^{x} + c_2\,e^{-x} - 2x + e^{2x},
\qquad
y' = c_1\,e^{x} - c_2\,e^{-x} - 2 + 2\,e^{2x}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
c_1 + c_2 + 1 = 1 \\
c_1 - c_2 - 2 + 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
c_1 = 0, \quad c_2 = 0
$$
$$
y = e^{2x} - 2x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forcing term is the sum of a linear polynomial and an exponential. By the **method of undetermined coefficients**, we find a particular solution for each term and add the results. The function $\bar{y}_1$ solves $y'' - y = 2x$ and $\bar{y}_2$ solves $y'' - y = 3\,e^{2x}$. Their sum $\bar{y}_1 + \bar{y}_2$ therefore solves the given equation because the left-hand side evaluated at the sum is the sum of the two left-hand sides.

For $\bar{y}_1 = Ax + B$ the second derivative is zero, and the equality $-Ax - B = 2x$ gives $A = -2$ and $B = 0$. For $\bar{y}_2$ the number $\mu = 2$ does not satisfy the characteristic equation, so we try $C\,e^{2x}$. Its second derivative is $4C\,e^{2x}$, giving $3C = 3$, that is, $C = 1$.

For $x = 0$ we have $y(0) = c_1 + c_2 + 1$ and $y'(0) = c_1 - c_2 - 2 + 2 = c_1 - c_2$. The conditions give $c_1 + c_2 = 0$ and $c_1 - c_2 = 0$, so both constants are zero. Thus the solution of the initial value problem coincides with the particular solution $e^{2x} - 2x$.

</div>
