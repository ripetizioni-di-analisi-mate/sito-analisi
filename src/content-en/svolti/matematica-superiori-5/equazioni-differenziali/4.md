## Exercise 1

Solve the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' - 5y' + 6y = 0 \\
y(0) = 1 \\
y'(0) = 4
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6 = 0
\iff
(\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 3) = 0
\iff
\lambda_1 = 2, \quad \lambda_2 = 3
$$
$$
y = c_1\,e^{2x} + c_2\,e^{3x},
\qquad
y' = 2c_1\,e^{2x} + 3c_2\,e^{3x}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
c_1 + c_2 = 1 \\
2c_1 + 3c_2 = 4
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
c_1 = -1, \quad c_2 = 2
$$
$$
y = 2\,e^{3x} - e^{2x}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To write the **characteristic equation**, replace $y''$ by $\lambda^2$, $y'$ by $\lambda$, and $y$ by $1$. Its discriminant is $\Delta = 25 - 24 = 1 > 0$ and its roots are $2$ and $3$. When $\Delta > 0$, the general solution is a linear combination of the two exponentials $e^{2x}$ and $e^{3x}$.

To impose the initial conditions, we also need the derivative of the general solution. For $x = 0$ the exponentials equal $1$, so $y(0) = c_1 + c_2$ and $y'(0) = 2c_1 + 3c_2$. The conditions must hold simultaneously, so they form a system. Subtracting twice the first equation from the second gives $c_2 = 2$, and therefore $c_1 = 1 - 2 = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' + 6y' + 9y = 0 \\
y(0) = 2 \\
y'(0) = -5
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\lambda^2 + 6\lambda + 9 = 0
\iff
(\lambda + 3)^2 = 0
\iff
\lambda = -3 \quad \text{double root}
$$
$$
y = (c_1 + c_2\,x)\,e^{-3x}
$$
$$
y' = c_2\,e^{-3x} - 3\,(c_1 + c_2\,x)\,e^{-3x}
$$
$$
y(0) = c_1 = 2,
\qquad
y'(0) = c_2 - 3c_1 = -5
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
c_2 = 1
$$
$$
y = (2 + x)\,e^{-3x}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side of the characteristic equation is the square of the binomial $\lambda + 3$, so $\Delta = 36 - 36 = 0$ and there is just one double root, $\lambda = -3$. When $\Delta = 0$, the general solution contains both $e^{-3x}$ and the function $x\,e^{-3x}$, and is written as $y = (c_1 + c_2\,x)\,e^{-3x}$.

Differentiate using the **product rule**. The derivative of $c_1 + c_2\,x$ is $c_2$, and the derivative of $e^{-3x}$ is $-3\,e^{-3x}$. For $x = 0$ we have $y(0) = c_1$, so $c_1 = 2$. In addition, $y'(0) = c_2 - 3c_1 = c_2 - 6$, and the condition $y'(0) = -5$ gives $c_2 = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' + 9y = 0 \\
y(0) = 1 \\
y'(0) = 6
\end{cases}
$$
and find the period and maximum value of the solution.

$$
\lambda^2 + 9 = 0,
\qquad
\Delta = -36,
\qquad
\alpha = 0, \quad \beta = \frac{\sqrt{36}}{2} = 3
$$
$$
y = c_1 \cos 3x + c_2 \sin 3x,
\qquad
y' = -3c_1 \sin 3x + 3c_2 \cos 3x
$$
$$
y(0) = c_1 = 1,
\qquad
y'(0) = 3c_2 = 6
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
c_2 = 2
$$
$$
y = \cos 3x + 2 \sin 3x,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi}{3},
\qquad
\sqrt{1^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{5}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(3*x)+2*sin(3*x)" punti="0,1,pieno" segmenti="-1,2.24,5,2.24;-1,-2.24,5,-2.24" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=3 legenda="y = \cos 3x + 2\sin 3x" titolo="The solution $y = \cos 3x + 2\sin 3x$ has period $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ and oscillates between $-\sqrt{5}$ and $\sqrt{5}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic equation $\lambda^2 + 9 = 0$ has no real roots because its discriminant is $-36 < 0$. With $a = 1$, $b = 0$, and $c = 9$ we have $\alpha = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = 0$ and $\beta = \dfrac{\sqrt{-\Delta}}{2a} = 3$. The factor $e^{\alpha x}$ equals $1$, so the general solution contains only $\cos 3x$ and $\sin 3x$.

For $x = 0$ we have $\cos 0 = 1$ and $\sin 0 = 0$, so $y(0) = c_1$ and $y'(0) = 3c_2$. The conditions give $c_1 = 1$ and $c_2 = 2$.

The functions $\cos 3x$ and $\sin 3x$ have period $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, so the solution also has **period** $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. An expression $c_1 \cos \beta x + c_2 \sin \beta x$ can be written in the form $A \cos(\beta x + \varphi)$ with $A = \sqrt{c_1^2 + c_2^2}$, as in simple harmonic motion. The maximum value of the solution is therefore $A = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.24$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve the initial value problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' - 2y' + 2y = 0 \\
y(0) = 0 \\
y'(0) = 1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\lambda^2 - 2\lambda + 2 = 0,
\qquad
\Delta = 4 - 8 = -4,
\qquad
\alpha = \frac{2}{2} = 1, \quad \beta = \frac{\sqrt{4}}{2} = 1
$$
$$
y = e^{x} \left( c_1 \cos x + c_2 \sin x \right)
$$
$$
y(0) = c_1 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y = c_2\,e^{x} \sin x
$$
$$
y' = c_2\,e^{x} \left( \sin x + \cos x \right),
\qquad
y'(0) = c_2 = 1
$$
$$
y = e^{x} \sin x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discriminant of the characteristic equation is negative, so we calculate $\alpha = -\dfrac{b}{2a} = -\dfrac{-2}{2} = 1$ and $\beta = \dfrac{\sqrt{-\Delta}}{2a} = \dfrac{\sqrt{4}}{2} = 1$. The general solution is the product of $e^{x}$ and a linear combination of $\cos x$ and $\sin x$.

Impose the condition $y(0) = 0$ first because for $x = 0$ we have $e^{0} = 1$, $\cos 0 = 1$, and $\sin 0 = 0$, so $y(0) = c_1$. With $c_1 = 0$ the function becomes $c_2\,e^{x} \sin x$, which we differentiate using the product rule. The condition $y'(0) = 1$ gives $c_2 = 1$. The solution $y = e^{x} \sin x$ oscillates with increasing amplitude because $\alpha = 1 > 0$.

</div>
