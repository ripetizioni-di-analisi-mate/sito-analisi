## Exercise 1

Verify that $x^3 + x - 3 = 0$ has a solution in $[1, 2]$, and approximate it by bisection until the length of the final interval is less than $0.1$.

$$
f(x) = x^3 + x - 3,
\qquad
f(1) = -1 < 0,
\qquad
f(2) = 7 > 0
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2^n} < 0.1
\iff
2^n > 10
\iff
n \geq 4
$$

| interval | $m$ | $f(m)$ | new interval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $[1, 2]$ | $1.5$ | $1.875 > 0$ | $[1;\ 1.5]$ |
| $[1;\ 1.5]$ | $1.25$ | $0.203\ldots > 0$ | $[1;\ 1.25]$ |
| $[1;\ 1.25]$ | $1.125$ | $-0.451\ldots < 0$ | $[1.125;\ 1.25]$ |
| $[1.125;\ 1.25]$ | $1.1875$ | $-0.137\ldots < 0$ | $[1.1875;\ 1.25]$ |

The solution lies between $1.1875$ and $1.25$. The midpoint $1.21875$ approximates it with an error less than $0.03125$.

::grafico{f="x^3+x-3" punti="1,-1,pieno;2,7,pieno;1.5,1.875,pieno;1.25,0.2031,pieno;1.125,-0.4512,pieno;1.2134,0,vuoto" segmenti="1.5,0,1.5,1.875;1.125,0,1.125,-0.4512" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=-4 ymax=9 legenda="y = x^3 + x - 3" titolo="The function $x^3 + x - 3$ changes sign between $1$ and $2$. The midpoints $1.5$, $1.25$ and $1.125$ narrow the interval containing the zero, approximately $1.213$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is continuous on $[1, 2]$, and it has opposite signs at the endpoints. By **Bolzano's theorem**, the equation has a solution in $(1, 2)$. The initial interval has length $1$, and after $n$ steps the length is $\dfrac{1}{2^n}$. For the length to be less than $0.1$, we need $2^n > 10$. Since $2^3 = 8$ and $2^4 = 16$, this takes $4$ steps.

At each step, evaluate $f$ at the midpoint and keep the half whose endpoint values of $f$ have opposite signs. In the first step, $f(1.5) > 0$, whereas $f(1) < 0$, so the zero lies in $[1;\ 1.5]$. In the second step, $f(1.25) > 0$, and the zero is again in the left half. In the third and fourth steps, the value at the midpoint is negative, so we keep the right half.

After four steps the zero lies in an interval of length $0.0625$. The distance from its midpoint to the zero is less than half the length, that is, less than $0.03125$. Using more steps gives the value of the zero as $1.2134\ldots$

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that $\ln x + x - 2 = 0$ has exactly one solution and that it lies between $1$ and $2$. Perform three bisection steps and determine how many steps are needed for the interval length to be less than $0.01$.

$$
f(x) = \ln x + x - 2,
\qquad
f(1) = -1 < 0,
\qquad
f(2) = \ln 2 \approx 0.693 > 0
$$

| interval | $m$ | $f(m)$ | new interval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $[1, 2]$ | $1.5$ | $-0.095\ldots < 0$ | $[1.5;\ 2]$ |
| $[1.5;\ 2]$ | $1.75$ | $0.310\ldots > 0$ | $[1.5;\ 1.75]$ |
| $[1.5;\ 1.75]$ | $1.625$ | $0.111\ldots > 0$ | $[1.5;\ 1.625]$ |

$$
\frac{1}{2^n} < 0.01
\iff
2^n > 100
\iff
n \geq 7
$$

After three steps the solution lies between $1.5$ and $1.625$. To obtain a length less than $0.01$ takes $7$ steps.

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined and continuous for $x > 0$, so it is continuous on $[1, 2]$. At the endpoints it takes the negative value $-1$ and the positive value $\ln 2$, so by **Bolzano's theorem** it has a zero in $(1, 2)$. Moreover, $\ln x$ and $x - 2$ are strictly increasing, so their sum is also strictly increasing, and the zero is **unique**.

In the first step, $f(1.5) = \ln 1.5 - 0.5 \approx 0.405 - 0.5 < 0$, so the zero lies in the right half $[1.5;\ 2]$. In the second step, $f(1.75) > 0$, and in the third, $f(1.625) > 0$, so we keep the left half in both cases.

The length after $n$ steps is $\dfrac{1}{2^n}$. Since $2^6 = 64$ and $2^7 = 128$, the inequality $2^n > 100$ holds starting at $n = 7$. The value of the zero is $1.557\ldots$, which lies in the interval obtained after three steps.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider the equation $2^{x} = 3x$.

1. Prove that it has a solution in $(0, 1)$ and a solution in $(3, 4)$.
2. Approximate the smaller solution by bisection until the final interval has length less than $0.1$.

**Part 1**

| $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f(x) = 2^{x} - 3x$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | $4$ |

**Part 2**

| interval | $m$ | $f(m)$ | new interval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $[0, 1]$ | $0.5$ | $-0.086\ldots < 0$ | $[0;\ 0.5]$ |
| $[0;\ 0.5]$ | $0.25$ | $0.439\ldots > 0$ | $[0.25;\ 0.5]$ |
| $[0.25;\ 0.5]$ | $0.375$ | $0.172\ldots > 0$ | $[0.375;\ 0.5]$ |
| $[0.375;\ 0.5]$ | $0.4375$ | $0.042\ldots > 0$ | $[0.4375;\ 0.5]$ |

The smaller solution lies between $0.4375$ and $0.5$.

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The solutions of the equation are the zeros of $f(x) = 2^{x} - 3x$, which is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ because it is the difference of an exponential function and a polynomial. The table shows that $f$ changes sign between $0$ and $1$ and between $3$ and $4$. **Bolzano's theorem** applies on each closed interval, so the equation has a solution in $(0, 1)$ and one in $(3, 4)$. Isolating the solutions in separate intervals is the first step before applying the bisection method, which approximates one solution at a time.

**Part 2.** Start with $[0, 1]$, where $f(0) > 0$ and $f(1) < 0$. In the first step, $f(0.5) = \sqrt{2} - 1.5 \approx -0.086 < 0$, so the zero lies between $0$ and $0.5$. In each of the next three steps the value at the midpoint is positive, so we keep the right half each time. After four steps the length is $\dfrac{1}{16} = 0.0625 < 0.1$. The value of the solution is $0.4578\ldots$, while the other solution is approximately $3.31$.

</div>
