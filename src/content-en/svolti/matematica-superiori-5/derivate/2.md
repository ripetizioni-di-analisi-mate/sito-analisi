## Exercise 1

Write the equations of the tangent and normal lines to the graph of $f(x) = x^3 - 2x + 1$ at the point with horizontal coordinate $x_0 = 1$.

$$
f(1) = 1 - 2 + 1 = 0
$$
$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2,
\qquad
f'(1) = 3 - 2 = 1
$$
$$
\text{tangent:} \quad y - 0 = 1 \cdot (x - 1)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y = x - 1
$$
$$
\text{normal:} \quad y - 0 = -1 \cdot (x - 1)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y = -x + 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tangent line passes through $\big( 1, f(1) \big) = (1, 0)$ and has slope $f'(1)$. Differentiate the polynomial term by term using the **sum rule** and the power rule, giving $\left( x^3 \right)' = 3x^2$ and $(2x)' = 2$, while the derivative of the constant $1$ is $0$. This gives $f'(1) = 1$, so the tangent line is $y = x - 1$.

The normal line is perpendicular to the tangent line, so its slope is $-\dfrac{1}{f'(1)} = -1$. The normal line also passes through $(1, 0)$, so its equation is $y = -x + 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the point on the graph of $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 5$ where the tangent is parallel to the line $y = 2x + 3$, and write the equation of this tangent.

$$
f'(x) = 2x - 4
$$
$$
2x - 4 = 2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x = 3
$$
$$
f(3) = 9 - 12 + 5 = 2
$$
$$
y - 2 = 2\,(x - 3)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y = 2x - 4
$$

The required point is $(3, 2)$ and the tangent line is $y = 2x - 4$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Two lines are parallel when they have the same slope. The line $y = 2x + 3$ has slope $2$, so we look for points where the derivative, which is the slope of the tangent, equals $2$. The equation $2x - 4 = 2$ has the unique solution $x = 3$.

Substituting $x = 3$ into the function gives the vertical coordinate, $2$. The tangent at $(3, 2)$ has slope $2$ and equation $y = 2x - 4$. This line is distinct from $y = 2x + 3$ because their constant terms differ, so the two lines are **parallel** and distinct.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the equations of the lines through $P(1, -3)$ that are tangent to the graph of $f(x) = x^2$.

$$
\text{tangent at } \left( a, a^2 \right): \quad y - a^2 = 2a\,(x - a)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y = 2a\,x - a^2
$$
$$
\text{passing through } P(1, -3): \quad -3 = 2a - a^2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
a^2 - 2a - 3 = 0
$$
$$
(a - 3)(a + 1) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
a = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad a = -1
$$
$$
a = 3: \quad y = 6x - 9,
\qquad
a = -1: \quad y = -2x - 1
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;6*x-9;-2*x-1" punti="1,-3,pieno,P;3,9,pieno;-1,1,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-12 ymax=18 legenda="y = x^2;y = 6x - 9;y = -2x - 1" titolo="Two tangents to the graph can be drawn from $P(1, -3)$. For the graph of $x^2$, their points of tangency are $(3, 9)$ and $(-1, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $P$ is not on the graph because $f(1) = 1 \neq -3$. Thus the point of tangency is unknown, and we denote it by $\left( a, a^2 \right)$. The derivative is $f'(x) = 2x$, so the tangent at the point with horizontal coordinate $a$ has slope $2a$. Expanding $y - a^2 = 2a\,(x - a)$ gives $y = 2a\,x - 2a^2 + a^2 = 2a\,x - a^2$.

Now require the tangent to pass through $P$ by substituting $x = 1$ and $y = -3$. This gives the quadratic equation $a^2 - 2a - 3 = 0$ with two solutions, $a = 3$ and $a = -1$. Thus $P$ lies on **two tangents**: for $a = 3$, the line $y = 6x - 9$ touches the graph at $(3, 9)$, and for $a = -1$, the line $y = -2x - 1$ touches the graph at $(-1, 1)$. As a check, both lines at $x = 1$ give $-3$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the values of the parameter $k$ for which the line $y = 3x + k$ is tangent to the graph of $f(x) = x^3$.

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 = 3
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x^2 = 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -1
$$
$$
x = 1: \quad f(1) = 1, \qquad 1 = 3 \cdot 1 + k \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad k = -2
$$
$$
x = -1: \quad f(-1) = -1, \qquad -1 = 3 \cdot (-1) + k \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad k = 2
$$

The line is tangent for $k = -2$ at the point $(1, 1)$, and for $k = 2$ at the point $(-1, -1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

All the lines $y = 3x + k$ have slope $3$. One of them is tangent to the graph at the point with horizontal coordinate $x_0$ if the derivative at $x_0$ equals $3$ and the line passes through the point $\big( x_0, f(x_0) \big)$, that is, if
$$
\begin{cases}
3x_0^2 = 3 \\
x_0^3 = 3x_0 + k .
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation has solutions $x_0 = 1$ and $x_0 = -1$.

For each of these horizontal coordinates, require the line to pass through the corresponding point on the graph. At $(1, 1)$ we get $k = -2$, and at $(-1, -1)$ we get $k = 2$. Thus there are **two tangent lines** with slope $3$, namely $y = 3x - 2$ and $y = 3x + 2$. Like the graph of $x^3$, the pair of lines is symmetric about the origin.

</div>
