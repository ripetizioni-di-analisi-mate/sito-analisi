## Exercise 1

A particle moves along a straight line with position function $s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$, where $s$ is in meters and $t \geq 0$ is in seconds. Find its velocity and acceleration as functions of time, the times when the particle is at rest and its position at those times, and its velocity when its acceleration is zero.

$$
v(t) = s'(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3\,(t - 1)(t - 3)
$$
$$
a(t) = v'(t) = 6t - 12
$$
$$
v(t) = 0
\iff
t = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad t = 3,
\qquad
s(1) = 4\,\mathrm{m}, \qquad s(3) = 0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
a(t) = 0
\iff
t = 2,
\qquad
v(2) = 12 - 24 + 9 = -3\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Instantaneous velocity is the derivative of position with respect to time, and acceleration is the derivative of velocity, that is, the **second derivative** of position. The velocity is a quadratic trinomial. Factoring out $3$ leaves $t^2 - 4t + 3$, whose roots are $1$ and $3$, so it factors as $(t - 1)(t - 3)$.

The particle is at rest when its velocity is zero, that is, at $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t = 3\,\mathrm{s}$. Substituting into the position function gives the positions $s(1) = 1 - 6 + 9 = 4$ and $s(3) = 27 - 54 + 27 = 0$. Between these two times the velocity is negative because the quadratic is negative between its roots, so the particle moves back from $4\,\mathrm{m}$ to the origin.

The acceleration vanishes at $t = 2\,\mathrm{s}$. At that time the velocity is $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The negative sign means that the particle is moving in the direction opposite to the positive axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

As a capacitor charges, the charge on its plates is $q(t) = 6\left( 1 - e^{-2t} \right)$, where $q$ is in millicoulombs and $t \geq 0$ is in seconds. Find the current $i(t)$, its initial value, and the time when it equals $3\,\mathrm{mA}$.

$$
i(t) = q'(t) = 6 \cdot \left( 0 - e^{-2t} \cdot (-2) \right) = 12\,e^{-2t}
$$
$$
i(0) = 12\,e^{0} = 12\,\mathrm{mA}
$$
$$
12\,e^{-2t} = 3
\iff
e^{-2t} = \frac{1}{4}
\iff
-2t = -\ln 4
\iff
t = \frac{\ln 4}{2} = \ln 2 \approx 0.69\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **electric current** is the derivative of charge with respect to time. The derivative of the constant $1$ is $0$, and the derivative of $e^{-2t}$ follows from the chain rule: multiply $e^{-2t}$ by the derivative of the exponent, which is $-2$. The minus sign in front of the exponential and the factor $-2$ give a positive product, so $i(t) = 12\,e^{-2t}$. Charge is in millicoulombs and time is in seconds, so current is in milliamperes.

At the initial time the exponential equals $1$ and the current is $12\,\mathrm{mA}$. To find when the current equals $3\,\mathrm{mA}$, divide by $12$ and take logarithms. Since $\ln \dfrac{1}{4} = -\ln 4$ and $\ln 4 = 2 \ln 2$, this gives $t = \ln 2$. As $t \to +\infty$, the current approaches $0$ and the charge approaches $6\,\mathrm{mC}$, so the capacitor becomes fully charged.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider the function $f(x) = a x^3 + b x^2$, where $a$ and $b$ are real parameters.

1. Find $a$ and $b$ so that the graph of $f$ passes through $A(1, 2)$ and the tangent line at $A$ is parallel to the line $y = 3x$.
2. Using these values, write the equation of the line $r$ tangent to the graph at $A$ and verify that $r$ and the graph have only the point $A$ in common.
3. For $0 \leq t \leq 2$, the function $s(t) = f(t)$ gives the position in meters of a particle moving along a straight line, with $t$ in seconds. Find its velocity and acceleration, the time when its velocity is greatest, and the value of that velocity.
4. Use the differential to estimate the displacement of the particle between times $t = 1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t = 1.1\,\mathrm{s}$, and compare the estimate with the exact value.

**Part 1**
$$
f'(x) = 3a x^2 + 2b x
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
f(1) = 2 \\
f'(1) = 3
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\begin{cases}
a + b = 2 \\
3a + 2b = 3
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
a = -1, \quad b = 3
$$
$$
f(x) = -x^3 + 3x^2
$$

**Part 2**
$$
r: \quad y - 2 = 3\,(x - 1)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
y = 3x - 1
$$
$$
-x^3 + 3x^2 = 3x - 1
\iff
x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 1 = 0
\iff
(x - 1)^3 = 0
\iff
x = 1
$$

**Part 3**
$$
v(t) = s'(t) = -3t^2 + 6t,
\qquad
a(t) = v'(t) = -6t + 6
$$
$$
\text{vertex of the parabola } v(t): \quad t = -\frac{6}{2 \cdot (-3)} = 1,
\qquad
v(1) = -3 + 6 = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

**Part 4**
$$
ds = v(1) \cdot dt = 3 \cdot 0.1 = 0.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
s(1.1) - s(1) = \left( -1.331 + 3.63 \right) - 2 = 0.299\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="-x^3+3*x^2;3*x-1" punti="1,2,pieno,A" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=5 legenda="y = -x^3 + 3x^2;y = 3x - 1" titolo="The tangent line $r$ with equation $y = 3x - 1$ meets the graph of $f(x) = -x^3 + 3x^2$ only at $A(1, 2)$, where it crosses the graph"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** Passing through $A(1, 2)$ means that $f(1) = 2$, that is, $a + b = 2$. The line $y = 3x$ has slope $3$, and the tangent at $A$ is parallel to it if it has the same slope, that is, if $f'(1) = 3$. The derivative $f'(x) = 3a x^2 + 2b x$ evaluated at $1$ equals $3a + 2b$. The two conditions must hold simultaneously, so they form a system. The first equation gives $b = 2 - a$, and substituting into the second gives $3a + 4 - 2a = 3$, that is, $a = -1$. Thus $b = 3$.

**Part 2.** The **tangent line** at $A$ passes through $(1, 2)$ with slope $3$, and its equation is $y = 3x - 1$. To find the points common to the line and the graph, solve their equations simultaneously by equating the two expressions for $y$. This gives the equation $x^3 - 3x^2 + 3x - 1 = 0$, whose left-hand side is the cube of the binomial $x - 1$. Its only solution is $x = 1$, so $r$ and the graph have **only the point** $A$ in common. As the figure shows, at $A$ the graph crosses the tangent, so $A$ is an inflection point. Inflection points are covered in the chapter on maxima, minima, inflection points, and curve sketching.

**Part 3.** Velocity is the derivative of position, and acceleration is the derivative of velocity. The graph of $v(t) = -3t^2 + 6t$ is a downward-opening parabola because the coefficient of $t^2$ is negative, so its maximum occurs at the vertex, whose horizontal coordinate is $t = 1$. The vertex lies in the interval $[0, 2]$, so the **maximum velocity** is $v(1) = 3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At that time the acceleration equals $a(1) = -6 + 6 = 0$, consistent with the fact that velocity is increasing for $t < 1$ and decreasing for $t > 1$. In addition, $v(t) = 3t\,(2 - t) \geq 0$ for $0 \leq t \leq 2$, so the particle moves in the positive direction throughout the interval.

**Part 4.** The **differential** of position is $ds = s'(1) \cdot dt = v(1) \cdot dt$ with $dt = 0.1\,\mathrm{s}$, giving the estimate $0.3\,\mathrm{m}$. To obtain the exact value, calculate $s(1.1)$. Since $1.1^2 = 1.21$ and $1.1^3 = 1.331$, we have $s(1.1) = -1.331 + 3 \cdot 1.21 = 2.299$, and the displacement is $2.299 - 2 = 0.299\,\mathrm{m}$. The estimate differs from the exact value by one millimeter, that is, by $0.001\,\mathrm{m}$, because the velocity stays close to $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over such a short time interval.

</div>
