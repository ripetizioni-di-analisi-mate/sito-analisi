## Exercise 1

Find and classify the points where $f(x) = \left| x^2 - 2x \right|$ is not differentiable.

$$
x^2 - 2x = x\,(x - 2) \geq 0
\iff
x \leq 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x \geq 2
$$
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^2 - 2x & \text{if } x \leq 0 \text{ or } x \geq 2 \\
2x - x^2 & \text{if } 0 < x < 2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f'(x) =
\begin{cases}
2x - 2 & \text{if } x < 0 \text{ or } x > 2 \\
2 - 2x & \text{if } 0 < x < 2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f'_-(0) = \lim_{x \to 0^-} (2x - 2) = -2,
\qquad
f'_+(0) = \lim_{x \to 0^+} (2 - 2x) = 2
$$
$$
f'_-(2) = \lim_{x \to 2^-} (2 - 2x) = -2,
\qquad
f'_+(2) = \lim_{x \to 2^+} (2x - 2) = 2
$$

The points $x = 0$ and $x = 2$ are **corners**.

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-2*x)" punti="0,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=4 titolo="The function $\left| x^2 - 2x \right|$ has two corners, at $(0, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$, with left-hand derivative $-2$ and right-hand derivative $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula for the absolute value changes where its argument changes sign. The trinomial $x\,(x - 2)$ is positive outside the interval $[0, 2]$ and negative inside it, so on $(0, 2)$ the function equals the negative of the trinomial, $2x - x^2$. Except at $0$ and $2$, the function agrees locally with a polynomial and is therefore differentiable. The only points to examine are $0$ and $2$.

The function is continuous because it is the absolute value of a polynomial. The **differentiability criterion** therefore applies at $0$ and $2$, and the right-hand and left-hand derivatives are the limits of the two expressions for $f'$. At both points the left-hand derivative is $-2$ and the right-hand derivative is $2$. The two derivatives are finite and unequal, so $0$ and $2$ are **corners**. On the graph, the part of the parabola between $0$ and $2$ is reflected above the $x$-axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine whether $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{(x - 1)^2}$ is differentiable at $x_0 = 1$.

$$
f(x) = (x - 1)^{\frac{2}{3}},
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{2}{3}\,(x - 1)^{-\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x - 1}} \qquad \text{for } x \neq 1
$$
$$
f'_+(1) = \lim_{x \to 1^+} \frac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x - 1}} = +\infty,
\qquad
f'_-(1) = \lim_{x \to 1^-} \frac{2}{3\sqrt[3]{x - 1}} = -\infty
$$

The point $(1, 0)$ is a **cusp**.

::grafico{f="cbrt((x-1)^2)" punti="1,0,pieno" segmenti="1,0,1,2" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="The function $\sqrt[3]{(x - 1)^2}$ has a cusp at $(1, 0)$, where the tangent is the vertical line $x = 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The cube root is defined for every real number, so the function is defined and continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. For $x \neq 1$ the function is differentiable, and its derivative follows from the power rule and the chain rule. The derivative of $x - 1$ is $1$. At $x = 1$ the expression for $f'$ is undefined because the denominator vanishes.

By the **differentiability criterion**, the right-hand and left-hand derivatives at $1$ are the one-sided limits of $f'$. As $x \to 1^+$, the root $\sqrt[3]{x - 1}$ approaches $0$ through positive values, so the fraction approaches $+\infty$. As $x \to 1^-$, the root approaches $0$ through negative values, so the fraction approaches $-\infty$. The two derivatives are infinite with opposite signs, so the point $(1, 0)$ is a **cusp**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and classify the points where $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x^2 - 1}$ is not differentiable.

$$
f(x) = \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^{\frac{1}{3}},
\qquad
f'(x) = \frac{1}{3} \left( x^2 - 1 \right)^{-\frac{2}{3}} \cdot 2x = \frac{2x}{3\sqrt[3]{\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^2}} \qquad \text{for } x \neq \pm 1
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to 1^-} f'(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^+} f'(x) = +\infty
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -1^-} f'(x) = \lim_{x \to -1^+} f'(x) = -\infty
$$

The points $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$ are **inflection points with vertical tangents**.

::grafico{f="cbrt(x^2-1)" punti="1,0,pieno;-1,0,pieno" segmenti="1,-1,1,1;-1,-1,-1,1" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=3 titolo="The function $\sqrt[3]{x^2 - 1}$ has two inflection points with vertical tangents, at $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$, where the graph crosses the lines $x = 1$ and $x = -1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined and continuous on $\mathbb{R}$. Its derivative follows from the **chain rule**. The derivative of the power function $t^{\frac{1}{3}}$ is $\dfrac{1}{3}\,t^{-\frac{2}{3}}$, which must be multiplied by the derivative of $x^2 - 1$, namely $2x$. The expression for $f'$ is defined at every point where the denominator differs from $0$, that is, for $x \neq \pm 1$. At $x = 0$ the denominator does not vanish, so the function is differentiable with $f'(0) = 0$.

The denominator $3\sqrt[3]{\left( x^2 - 1 \right)^2}$ is the cube root of a square, so it is positive on both sides of $1$ and $-1$ and approaches $0$. Near $x = 1$ the numerator approaches $2$, which is positive, so $f'$ approaches $+\infty$ from both the right and the left. Near $x = -1$ the numerator approaches $-2$, so $f'$ approaches $-\infty$ from both sides. By the differentiability criterion, at each of the two points the right-hand and left-hand derivatives are infinite with the same sign, so the two points are **inflection points with vertical tangents**.

</div>
