## Exercise 1

A particle moves along a straight line with velocity $v(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9$, where $v$ is in meters per second and $t$ is in seconds. Find its displacement, distance traveled, and average velocity over the time interval $[0, 4]$.

$$
v(t) = 3\,(t - 1)(t - 3) ,
\qquad
s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t \ \text{ is an antiderivative of } v
$$
$$
\text{displacement} = \int_0^4 v(t)\,dt = \Big[ t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t \Big]_0^4 = 64 - 96 + 36 = 4\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\int_0^1 v(t)\,dt = 4 ,
\qquad
\int_1^3 v(t)\,dt = 0 - 4 = -4 ,
\qquad
\int_3^4 v(t)\,dt = 4 - 0 = 4
$$
$$
\text{distance traveled} = \int_0^4 |v(t)|\,dt = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{average velocity} = \frac{4}{4 - 0} = 1\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

The displacement is $4 - 4 + 4 = 4$ and the distance traveled is $12$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is a quadratic trinomial with roots $1$ and $3$. It is positive on $[0, 1)$ and $(3, 4]$, where the particle moves forward, and negative on $(1, 3)$, where it moves backward. An antiderivative of the velocity is $s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$, with $s(0) = 0$, $s(1) = 4$, $s(3) = 0$, and $s(4) = 4$.

By the evaluation formula, the **displacement** is the integral of velocity over $[0, 4]$, namely $s(4) - s(0) = 4\,\mathrm{m}$. The **distance traveled** is the integral of the absolute value of velocity, so split the interval at $1$ and $3$ and change the sign of the integral over $[1, 3]$. The particle moves forward by $4\,\mathrm{m}$, moves back by $4\,\mathrm{m}$ to its initial position, and then moves forward by another $4\,\mathrm{m}$, so it travels $12\,\mathrm{m}$.

Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time, that is, the average value of $v$ on $[0, 4]$, and equals $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal gas expands at constant temperature from volume $V_1 = 2\,\mathrm{L}$ to volume $V_2 = 6\,\mathrm{L}$. During expansion the pressure and volume satisfy $pV = 600\,\mathrm{J}$. Find the work done by the gas, given that $W = \displaystyle\int_{V_1}^{V_2} p\,dV$.

$$
p(V) = \frac{600}{V}
$$
$$
W = \int_{2}^{6} \frac{600}{V}\,dV = 600 \Big[ \ln V \Big]_2^6 = 600\,(\ln 6 - \ln 2) = 600 \ln 3 \approx 659\,\mathrm{J}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas exerts a varying force on the walls because its pressure falls as its volume increases. As with the **work done by a variable force**, the work is therefore an integral. From $pV = 600$ we obtain $p = \dfrac{600}{V}$, and an antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{V}$ is $\ln V$.

The difference $\ln 6 - \ln 2$ is the logarithm of the quotient $\dfrac{6}{2} = 3$. The product $pV$ is measured in joules, and the volume ratio is independent of the unit chosen, so the volumes can be left in liters and the work is obtained in joules. The work is positive because the gas expands.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The current in a circuit is $i(t) = t\,e^{-t}$, where $i$ is in amperes and $t \geq 0$ is in seconds. Find the charge that passes through a cross section of the circuit during the first $2$ seconds and the total charge as $t$ runs from $0$ to $+\infty$.

$$
\int t\,e^{-t}\,dt = -t\,e^{-t} + \int e^{-t}\,dt = -(t + 1)\,e^{-t} + c
$$
$$
Q = \int_0^2 t\,e^{-t}\,dt = \Big[ -(t + 1)\,e^{-t} \Big]_0^2 = -3\,e^{-2} + 1 = 1 - \frac{3}{e^2} \approx 0.59\,\mathrm{C}
$$
$$
Q_{\text{tot}} = \lim_{T \to +\infty} \Big[ -(t + 1)\,e^{-t} \Big]_0^{T} = \lim_{T \to +\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{T + 1}{e^{T}} \right) = 1\,\mathrm{C}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **charge** is the integral of the current. Find an antiderivative of $t\,e^{-t}$ by parts, taking $t$ as the factor to differentiate and $e^{-t}$ as the factor to integrate, whose antiderivative is $-e^{-t}$. This gives $-t\,e^{-t} - e^{-t} = -(t + 1)\,e^{-t}$.

At $t = 2$ the antiderivative equals $-3\,e^{-2}$ and at $t = 0$ it equals $-1$, so the charge in the first two seconds is $1 - \dfrac{3}{e^2}$, approximately $0.59\,\mathrm{C}$. The total charge is an improper integral over an unbounded interval. The quotient $\dfrac{T + 1}{e^{T}}$ tends to $0$ because the exponential function grows faster than any polynomial, so the total charge is $1\,\mathrm{C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Consider the family of functions $f(x) = kx - x^2$, with $k > 0$, and let $R$ be the region between the graph of $f$ and the $x$-axis.

1. Find $k$ so that the area of $R$ is $36$.
2. Using the value of $k$ just found, determine the slope $m$ of the line $y = mx$ that divides $R$ into two parts of equal area.
3. Find the volume of the solid generated by a full rotation of $R$ about the $x$-axis.
4. Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^x f(t)\,dt$. Find the maximum of $F$ for $x \geq 0$ and the value $b > 0$ for which $F(b) = 0$, and interpret the result.

**Part 1**
$$
kx - x^2 = 0
\iff
x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = k
$$
$$
\int_0^k \left( kx - x^2 \right) dx = \left[ \frac{kx^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^k = \frac{k^3}{2} - \frac{k^3}{3} = \frac{k^3}{6}
$$
$$
\frac{k^3}{6} = 36
\iff
k^3 = 216
\iff
k = 6
$$

**Part 2**
$$
6x - x^2 = mx
\iff
x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 6 - m ,
\qquad
0 < m < 6
$$
$$
\int_0^{6 - m} \left( (6 - m)\,x - x^2 \right) dx = \frac{(6 - m)^3}{6}
$$
$$
\frac{(6 - m)^3}{6} = 18
\iff
(6 - m)^3 = 108
\iff
6 - m = \sqrt[3]{108} = 3\sqrt[3]{4}
\iff
m = 6 - 3\sqrt[3]{4} \approx 1.24
$$

**Part 3**
$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&= \pi \int_0^6 \left( 6x - x^2 \right)^2 dx
= \pi \int_0^6 \left( 36x^2 - 12x^3 + x^4 \right) dx \\[6pt]
&= \pi \left[ 12x^3 - 3x^4 + \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_0^6
= \pi \left( 2592 - 3888 + \frac{7776}{5} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1296\pi}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Part 4**
$$
F(x) = \int_0^x \left( 6t - t^2 \right) dt = 3x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3} ,
\qquad
F'(x) = 6x - x^2 = x\,(6 - x)
$$
$$
F'(x) > 0 \ \text{ for } 0 < x < 6 ,
\qquad
F'(x) < 0 \ \text{ for } x > 6 ,
\qquad
F(6) = 108 - 72 = 36
$$
$$
F(b) = 0
\iff
3b^2 - \frac{b^3}{3} = 0
\iff
b^2\,(9 - b) = 0
\iff
b = 9 \quad (b > 0)
$$

The shaded part is the parabolic segment between the line and the parabola"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** Since $k > 0$, the parabola $y = kx - x^2$ meets the $x$-axis at $0$ and at $k$ and lies above the axis between these points because it opens downward. The area of $R$ is therefore the integral of $f$ on $[0, k]$, which equals $\dfrac{k^3}{6}$, in agreement with the parabolic segment formula. The equation $k^3 = 216$ has the unique real solution $k = 6$.

**Part 2.** A line through the origin with $0 < m < 6$ intersects the parabola again at horizontal coordinate $6 - m$, which lies between $0$ and $6$, and cuts $R$ into two parts. The part between the line and the parabola is a parabolic segment. The difference between the parabola and the line is $(6 - m)\,x - x^2$, which has the same form as $f$ with $6 - m$ in place of $k$, so by part 1 its area is $\dfrac{(6 - m)^3}{6}$. The two parts have equal area when this is half of $36$, namely $18$. Since $108 = 27 \cdot 4$, the cube root of $108$ is $3\sqrt[3]{4}$, giving $m = 6 - 3\sqrt[3]{4}$, approximately $1.24$, which lies between $0$ and $6$.

**Part 3.** The function $6x - x^2$ is nonnegative on $[0, 6]$, so the formula for the **volume of a solid of revolution** about the $x$-axis applies. Expand the square of the binomial, $\left( 6x - x^2 \right)^2 = 36x^2 - 12x^3 + x^4$, and integrate term by term. With $6^3 = 216$, $6^4 = 1296$, and $6^5 = 7776$ the result is $\pi \left( -1296 + \dfrac{7776}{5} \right) = \pi \cdot \dfrac{-6480 + 7776}{5} = \dfrac{1296\pi}{5}$, approximately $814$.

**Part 4.** By the **fundamental theorem of calculus**, the derivative of $F$ is $f$. For $x \geq 0$ the derivative is positive between $0$ and $6$ and negative beyond $6$, so $F$ increases up to $x = 6$ and then decreases, and its **maximum** is $F(6) = 36$. This value is the area of $R$ because $F(6)$ is the integral of $f$ over $[0, 6]$, where $f \geq 0$. The equation $F(b) = 0$ has the positive solution $b = 9$. The integral of $f$ over $[0, 9]$ is therefore zero. For $x > 6$ the function $f$ is negative, so the region between the graph and the $x$-axis over $[6, 9]$ lies below the axis and has the same area as $R$, namely $36$.

</div>
