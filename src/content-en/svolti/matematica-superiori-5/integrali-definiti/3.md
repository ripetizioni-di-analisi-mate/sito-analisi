## Exercise 1

Find the area of the region bounded by the parabola $y = x^2 - 4x + 3$ and the line $y = x - 1$.

$$
x^2 - 4x + 3 = x - 1
\iff
x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0
\iff
x = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 4
$$
$$
(x - 1) - \left( x^2 - 4x + 3 \right) = -x^2 + 5x - 4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A
&= \int_1^4 \left( -x^2 + 5x - 4 \right) dx
= \left[ -\frac{x^3}{3} + \frac{5x^2}{2} - 4x \right]_1^4 \\[6pt]
&= \left( -\frac{64}{3} + 40 - 16 \right) - \left( -\frac{1}{3} + \frac{5}{2} - 4 \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{8}{3} + \frac{11}{6} = \frac{9}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-4*x+3;x-1" area="x-1;x^2-4*x+3;1;4" punti="1,0,pieno;4,3,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=6 legenda="y = x^2 - 4x + 3;y = x - 1" titolo="The region between the parabola $y = x^2 - 4x + 3$ and the line $y = x - 1$, with intersections $(1, 0)$ and $(4, 3)$, has area $\dfrac{9}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Only the two graphs bound the region, so the limits of integration are the horizontal coordinates of their intersections. Setting the two expressions for $y$ equal gives a quadratic equation with solutions $1$ and $4$, and the intersection points are $(1, 0)$ and $(4, 3)$.

To see which curve lies above the other, evaluate the two functions at an interior point, for example at $x = 2$. The line has value $1$ and the parabola has value $4 - 8 + 3 = -1$, so on $[1, 4]$ the line lies above the parabola. By the theorem on the **area between two curves**, integrate the difference between the line and the parabola. The antiderivative equals $\dfrac{8}{3}$ at $4$ and $-\dfrac{11}{6}$ at $1$, and the difference is $\dfrac{16}{6} + \dfrac{11}{6} = \dfrac{27}{6} = \dfrac{9}{2}$.

Check the result with the formula for a **parabolic segment**. The trinomial $-x^2 + 5x - 4$ has $|a| = 1$ and roots $1$ and $4$, so the area is $\dfrac{1 \cdot 3^3}{6} = \dfrac{9}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the area of the region bounded by the parabolas $y = x^2$ and $y = 8 - x^2$.

$$
x^2 = 8 - x^2
\iff
x^2 = 4
\iff
x = -2 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 2
$$
$$
A = \int_{-2}^{2} \left( 8 - x^2 - x^2 \right) dx = 2 \int_0^2 \left( 8 - 2x^2 \right) dx = 2 \left[ 8x - \frac{2x^3}{3} \right]_0^2 = 2 \left( 16 - \frac{16}{3} \right) = \frac{64}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two parabolas intersect at horizontal coordinates $-2$ and $2$. At $x = 0$ the first has value $0$ and the second has value $8$, so the parabola $y = 8 - x^2$ lies above the other between these points. The difference is $8 - 2x^2$, an even function, and the interval is symmetric about the origin. By the property of integrals of even functions, the integral over $[-2, 2]$ is therefore twice the one over $[0, 2]$.

The parabolic segment formula, with $|a| = 2$ and roots $-2$ and $2$, gives $\dfrac{2 \cdot 4^3}{6} = \dfrac{128}{6} = \dfrac{64}{3}$, the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the area of the region bounded by the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ and the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$.

$$
\sqrt{x} = \frac{x}{2}
\iff
\begin{cases}
x \geq 0 \\
x = \dfrac{x^2}{4}
\end{cases}
\iff
x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 4
$$
$$
A = \int_0^4 \left( \sqrt{x} - \frac{x}{2} \right) dx = \left[ \frac{2}{3}\,x\sqrt{x} - \frac{x^2}{4} \right]_0^4 = \frac{16}{3} - 4 = \frac{4}{3}
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(x);x/2" area="sqrt(x);x/2;0;4" punti="0,0,pieno;4,2,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=3 uguale=1 legenda="y = \sqrt{x};y = \dfrac{x}{2}" titolo="The region between $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$, with intersections $(0, 0)$ and $(4, 2)$, has area $\dfrac{4}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation $\sqrt{x} = \dfrac{x}{2}$ is a radical equation. Require $x \geq 0$ so that both sides are defined and nonnegative, then square both sides. The equation $4x = x^2$ has solutions $0$ and $4$, both of which satisfy $x \geq 0$.

At $x = 1$ the radical has value $1$ and the line has value $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so on $[0, 4]$ the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$ lies above the line. An antiderivative of $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac{1}{2}}$ is $\dfrac{2}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,x\sqrt{x}$, which at $4$ equals $\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 8 = \dfrac{16}{3}$. Subtracting $\dfrac{16}{4} = 4$ gives the area, $\dfrac{4}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the area of the region in the first quadrant bounded by the line $y = x$, the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$, and the line $x = 3$.

$$
x = \frac{1}{x}
\iff
x^2 = 1
\iff
x = 1 \quad (x > 0)
$$
$$
A = \int_1^3 \left( x - \frac{1}{x} \right) dx = \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - \ln x \right]_1^3 = \left( \frac{9}{2} - \ln 3 \right) - \frac{1}{2} = 4 - \ln 3 \approx 2.90
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line $y = x$ and the hyperbola intersect in the first quadrant at $(1, 1)$. The region therefore lies between the vertical lines $x = 1$ and $x = 3$. At $x = 2$ the line has value $2$ and the hyperbola has value $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the line lies above the hyperbola on $[1, 3]$.

An antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{x}$ for $x > 0$ is $\ln x$. At $x = 1$ the antiderivative equals $\dfrac{1}{2} - \ln 1 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and at $x = 3$ it equals $\dfrac{9}{2} - \ln 3$. The area is $4 - \ln 3$, or approximately $2.90$.

</div>
