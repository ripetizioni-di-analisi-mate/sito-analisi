## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^3 \left( 3x^2 - 4x + 1 \right) dx$.

$$
\int \left( 3x^2 - 4x + 1 \right) dx = x^3 - 2x^2 + x + c
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^3 \left( 3x^2 - 4x + 1 \right) dx
&= \Big[ x^3 - 2x^2 + x \Big]_1^3 \\[6pt]
&= (27 - 18 + 3) - (1 - 2 + 1) \\[6pt]
&= 12 - 0 = 12
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **evaluation formula for definite integrals**, a definite integral is the difference between the values of an antiderivative at the endpoints. The integrand is a polynomial, continuous on $[1, 3]$, so the formula applies. Find an antiderivative term by term: an antiderivative of $3x^2$ is $x^3$, one of $-4x$ is $-2x^2$, and one of $1$ is $x$. The constant $c$ is unnecessary because it cancels in the difference.

Evaluate the antiderivative $G(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + x$ at the upper limit, $G(3) = 12$, and at the lower limit, $G(1) = 0$. Their difference gives the result, $12$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( \sin x + 2\cos x \right) dx$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( \sin x + 2\cos x \right) dx
&= \Big[ -\cos x + 2\sin x \Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \\[6pt]
&= \left( -\cos \frac{\pi}{2} + 2\sin \frac{\pi}{2} \right) - \left( -\cos 0 + 2\sin 0 \right) \\[6pt]
&= (0 + 2) - (-1 + 0) = 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An antiderivative of $\sin x$ is $-\cos x$ because the derivative of $-\cos x$ is $\sin x$, and an antiderivative of $2\cos x$ is $2\sin x$. By linearity, adding the antiderivatives gives an antiderivative of the sum.

At the upper limit, using $\cos \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\sin \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$ gives the antiderivative the value $2$. At the lower limit, using $\cos 0 = 1$ and $\sin 0 = 0$ gives it the value $-1$. Subtracting a negative number adds its opposite, so the result is $2 + 1 = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_1^4 \left( \sqrt{x} + \frac{1}{x^2} \right) dx$.

$$
\sqrt{x} + \frac{1}{x^2} = x^{\frac{1}{2}} + x^{-2}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\int \left( x^{\frac{1}{2}} + x^{-2} \right) dx = \frac{2}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}} - \frac{1}{x} + c
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^4 \left( \sqrt{x} + \frac{1}{x^2} \right) dx
&= \left[ \frac{2}{3}\,x\sqrt{x} - \frac{1}{x} \right]_1^4 \\[6pt]
&= \left( \frac{2}{3} \cdot 8 - \frac{1}{4} \right) - \left( \frac{2}{3} - 1 \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{16}{3} - \frac{1}{4} - \frac{2}{3} + 1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{14}{3} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{65}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the square root and the reciprocal of the square as powers so that the formula $\displaystyle\int x^{a}\,dx = \dfrac{x^{a + 1}}{a + 1} + c$, valid for $a \neq -1$, applies. For $a = \dfrac{1}{2}$ this gives $\dfrac{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,x^{\frac{3}{2}}$, and for $a = -2$ it gives $\dfrac{x^{-1}}{-1} = -\dfrac{1}{x}$. The power $x^{\frac{3}{2}}$ equals $x\sqrt{x}$, which at $x = 4$ has the value $4 \cdot 2 = 8$.

The integrand is continuous on $[1, 4]$ because $x$ is positive, so the evaluation formula applies. In the last step, add $\dfrac{16}{3} - \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{14}{3}$ and $1 - \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{4}$. Using the common denominator $12$ gives $\dfrac{56}{12} + \dfrac{9}{12} = \dfrac{65}{12}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_0^4 \left| x^2 - 3x \right| dx$.

$$
x^2 - 3x = x\,(x - 3) \leq 0 \quad \text{for } 0 \leq x \leq 3 ,
\qquad
x^2 - 3x \geq 0 \quad \text{for } 3 \leq x \leq 4
$$
$$
\int_0^4 \left| x^2 - 3x \right| dx = \int_0^3 \left( 3x - x^2 \right) dx + \int_3^4 \left( x^2 - 3x \right) dx
$$
$$
\int_0^3 \left( 3x - x^2 \right) dx = \left[ \frac{3x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^3 = \frac{27}{2} - 9 = \frac{9}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_3^4 \left( x^2 - 3x \right) dx
&= \left[ \frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{3x^2}{2} \right]_3^4 \\[6pt]
&= \left( \frac{64}{3} - 24 \right) - \left( 9 - \frac{27}{2} \right) \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{8}{3} + \frac{9}{2} = \frac{11}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\int_0^4 \left| x^2 - 3x \right| dx = \frac{9}{2} + \frac{11}{6} = \frac{27}{6} + \frac{11}{6} = \frac{19}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To write the integrand without the absolute value, examine the sign of its argument. The trinomial $x^2 - 3x$ has roots $0$ and $3$ and is negative between them, so on $[0, 3]$ we have $\left| x^2 - 3x \right| = 3x - x^2$, while on $[3, 4]$ the absolute value equals the trinomial itself.

By **additivity over intervals**, the integral over $[0, 4]$ is the sum of the integrals over $[0, 3]$ and $[3, 4]$, and on each interval the integrand is a polynomial. The first integral equals $\dfrac{9}{2}$. In the second, the antiderivative equals $-\dfrac{8}{3}$ at $4$ and $-\dfrac{9}{2}$ at $3$, so the difference is $\dfrac{9}{2} - \dfrac{8}{3} = \dfrac{11}{6}$. Adding gives $\dfrac{19}{3}$. The result is **positive**, as expected, because the integrand is nonnegative.

</div>
