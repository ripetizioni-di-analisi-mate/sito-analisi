## Exercise 1

Find the area of the region bounded by the graph of $f(x) = -x^2 + 4x$ and the $x$-axis.

$$
-x^2 + 4x = 0
\iff
x\,(4 - x) = 0
\iff
x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 4
$$
$$
f(x) \geq 0 \quad \text{for } 0 \leq x \leq 4
$$
$$
A = \int_0^4 \left( -x^2 + 4x \right) dx = \left[ -\frac{x^3}{3} + 2x^2 \right]_0^4 = -\frac{64}{3} + 32 = \frac{32}{3}
$$

::grafico{f="-x^2+4*x" area="-x^2+4*x;0;0;4" punti="0,0,pieno;4,0,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=6 legenda="y = -x^2 + 4x" titolo="The region between the parabola $y = -x^2 + 4x$ and the $x$-axis has area $\dfrac{32}{3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola and the $x$-axis bound the region, so the limits of integration are the horizontal coordinates where the parabola meets the axis, that is, the zeros of $f$. Factoring out $x$ gives $x = 0$ and $x = 4$.

The parabola opens downward because the coefficient of $x^2$ is negative, so it lies above the axis between the two zeros. The region is therefore the **curvilinear trapezoid** under $f$ on $[0, 4]$, and its area is the integral of $f$. An antiderivative of $-x^2 + 4x$ is $-\dfrac{x^3}{3} + 2x^2$, which equals $\dfrac{32}{3}$ at $4$ and $0$ at $0$. The same result follows from the formula for a parabolic segment, $\dfrac{1 \cdot 4^3}{6} = \dfrac{32}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate the integral $\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2} \left( x^3 - 4x \right) dx$ and find the area of the region between the graph of $f(x) = x^3 - 4x$ and the $x$-axis on the interval $[-2, 2]$.

$$
\int_{-2}^{2} \left( x^3 - 4x \right) dx = 0
$$
$$
x^3 - 4x = x\,(x - 2)(x + 2) ,
\qquad
f(x) \leq 0 \quad \text{for } 0 \leq x \leq 2
$$
$$
A = 2 \int_0^2 \left( 4x - x^3 \right) dx = 2 \left[ 2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_0^2 = 2\,(8 - 4) = 8
$$

::grafico{f="x^3-4*x" area="x^3-4*x;0;-2;2" etichette="-1.15,1.2,+;1.15,-1.2,−" punti="-2,0,pieno;0,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-4 ymax=4 legenda="y = x^3 - 4x" titolo="The two regions between $y = x^3 - 4x$ and the $x$-axis on $[-2, 2]$ each have area $4$. The integral is $0$ and the total area is $8$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $x^3 - 4x$ is odd because $f(-x) = -x^3 + 4x = -f(x)$, and the interval $[-2, 2]$ is symmetric about the origin. By the property of **integrals of odd functions**, the integral is therefore $0$ without any calculation.

The area differs from the integral because the function changes sign. The factorization $x\,(x - 2)(x + 2)$ shows that $f$ is positive on $(-2, 0)$ and negative on $(0, 2)$. The two regions are symmetric about the origin, so they have equal areas, and it is enough to find the area over $[0, 2]$ and multiply it by $2$. On $[0, 2]$ the function is negative, so the area is the integral of $-f(x) = 4x - x^3$, which equals $4$. The total area is therefore $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Evaluate the integral and find the area of the region between the graph of $f(x) = \sin x$ and the $x$-axis on the interval $\left[ 0, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right]$.

$$
\int_0^{\frac{3\pi}{2}} \sin x\,dx = \Big[ -\cos x \Big]_0^{\frac{3\pi}{2}} = 0 - (-1) = 1
$$
$$
\int_0^{\pi} \sin x\,dx = \Big[ -\cos x \Big]_0^{\pi} = 1 - (-1) = 2 ,
\qquad
\int_{\pi}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}} (-\sin x)\,dx = \Big[ \cos x \Big]_{\pi}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}} = 0 - (-1) = 1
$$
$$
A = 2 + 1 = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Evaluate the integral using the antiderivative $-\cos x$. Since $\cos \dfrac{3\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\cos 0 = 1$, the result is $0 - (-1) = 1$.

To find the area, account for the sign. Sine is positive on $(0, \pi)$ and negative on $\left( \pi, \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \right)$. On the first interval the area is the integral of $\sin x$, which equals $2$. On the second it is the integral of $-\sin x$, whose antiderivative is $\cos x$, and it equals $1$. The area is therefore $3$. The integral, which equals $2 - 1 = 1$, is the area above the axis minus the area below the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the area of the region bounded by the graph of $f(x) = \ln x$, the $x$-axis, and the line $x = e$.

$$
\ln x = 0 \iff x = 1 ,
\qquad
\ln x \geq 0 \quad \text{for } x \geq 1
$$
$$
\int \ln x\,dx = x \ln x - x + c
$$
$$
A = \int_1^e \ln x\,dx = \Big[ x \ln x - x \Big]_1^e = (e - e) - (0 - 1) = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph of the logarithm meets the $x$-axis at the point with horizontal coordinate $1$ because $\ln 1 = 0$. The region therefore lies between $x = 1$ and $x = e$, where $\ln x \geq 0$, and is the curvilinear trapezoid under $\ln x$ on $[1, e]$.

The antiderivative $x \ln x - x$ follows from integration by parts, as shown in the chapter on indefinite integrals. Differentiating it gives $\ln x + x \cdot \dfrac{1}{x} - 1 = \ln x$. At $x = e$ we have $\ln e = 1$, so the antiderivative equals $e - e = 0$. At $x = 1$ it equals $0 - 1 = -1$. The area is therefore $0 - (-1) = 1$.

</div>
