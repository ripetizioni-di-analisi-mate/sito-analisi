## Exercise 1

Find the volume of the solid generated by a full rotation about the $x$-axis of the curvilinear trapezoid under $f(x) = x^2$ on the interval $[0, 2]$.

$$
V = \pi \int_0^2 \left( x^2 \right)^2 dx = \pi \int_0^2 x^4\,dx = \pi \left[ \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_0^2 = \frac{32\pi}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $x^2$ is continuous and nonnegative on $[0, 2]$, so the formula for the **volume of a solid of revolution** about the $x$-axis applies. The cross section in the plane perpendicular to the axis at $x$ is a disk of radius $x^2$ and area $\pi x^4$. Integrating $\pi x^4$ from $0$ to $2$ gives $\dfrac{32\pi}{5}$, approximately $20.1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the volume of the solid generated by a full rotation about the $x$-axis of the region bounded by the line $y = 2x$ and the parabola $y = x^2$.

$$
2x = x^2
\iff
x\,(x - 2) = 0
\iff
x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 2
$$
$$
0 \leq x^2 \leq 2x \quad \text{for } 0 \leq x \leq 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&= \pi \int_0^2 \left( (2x)^2 - \left( x^2 \right)^2 \right) dx
= \pi \int_0^2 \left( 4x^2 - x^4 \right) dx \\[6pt]
&= \pi \left[ \frac{4x^3}{3} - \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_0^2
= \pi \left( \frac{32}{3} - \frac{32}{5} \right)
= \frac{64\pi}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x;x^2" area="2*x;x^2;0;2" punti="0,0,pieno;2,4,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=5 legenda="y = 2x;y = x^2" titolo="The region between the line $y = 2x$ and the parabola $y = x^2$ generates a solid with a hole when rotated about the $x$-axis. Its volume is $\dfrac{64\pi}{15}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The line and parabola intersect at horizontal coordinates $0$ and $2$. At $x = 1$ the line has value $2$ and the parabola has value $1$, so on $[0, 2]$ the line lies above the parabola, and both are nonnegative.

When the region rotates about the $x$-axis, the cross section at $x$ is a **washer** with outer radius $2x$ and inner radius $x^2$. Its area is the difference between the areas of the two disks, $\pi\,(2x)^2 - \pi \left( x^2 \right)^2$. Therefore integrate the difference of the squares of the two functions. Using the common denominator $15$ gives $\dfrac{32}{3} - \dfrac{32}{5} = \dfrac{160 - 96}{15} = \dfrac{64}{15}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the volume of the solid generated by a full rotation about the $y$-axis of the region in the first quadrant bounded by the parabola $y = 4 - x^2$ and the coordinate axes. Check the result by rotating the same region described as a function of $y$.

$$
V = 2\pi \int_0^2 x \left( 4 - x^2 \right) dx = 2\pi \int_0^2 \left( 4x - x^3 \right) dx = 2\pi \left[ 2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_0^2 = 2\pi\,(8 - 4) = 8\pi
$$
$$
y = 4 - x^2 , \ x \geq 0
\iff
x = \sqrt{4 - y} , \ 0 \leq y \leq 4
$$
$$
V = \pi \int_0^4 \left( \sqrt{4 - y} \right)^2 dy = \pi \int_0^4 (4 - y)\,dy = \pi \left[ 4y - \frac{y^2}{2} \right]_0^4 = 8\pi
$$

::grafico{f="4-x^2+0*sqrt(x*(2-x))" area="4-x^2;0;0;2" punti="2,0,pieno;0,4,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=5 legenda="y = 4 - x^2" titolo="The region in the first quadrant under the parabola $y = 4 - x^2$, when rotated about the $y$-axis, generates a solid of volume $8\pi$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola meets the $x$-axis at $x = 2$ and the $y$-axis at $y = 4$, so the region is the curvilinear trapezoid under $4 - x^2$ on $[0, 2]$. Since $0 \leq x$ throughout the interval, the cylindrical shell formula for **rotation** about the $y$-axis gives the volume as $2\pi$ times the integral of $x\,f(x)$. The calculation gives $8\pi$.

To check, describe the same region using the variable $y$. Solving the equation of the parabola for $x$, with $x \geq 0$, gives $x = \sqrt{4 - y}$. The region lies between this graph, the $y$-axis, and the lines $y = 0$ and $y = 4$. The cross section in the plane perpendicular to the $y$-axis at $y$ is a disk of radius $\sqrt{4 - y}$. The formula for a solid of revolution therefore applies with the variables interchanged, and the integral of $\pi\,(4 - y)$ on $[0, 4]$ again equals $8\pi$. The two methods give the **same volume**.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The base of a solid is the region in the $xy$-plane bounded by the parabola $x = y^2$ and the line $x = 4$. Each cross section of the solid in a plane perpendicular to the $x$-axis is a square with one side on the base. Find the volume of the solid.

$$
x = y^2
\iff
y = \pm \sqrt{x} ,
\qquad
0 \leq x \leq 4
$$
$$
\ell(x) = \sqrt{x} - \left( -\sqrt{x} \right) = 2\sqrt{x} ,
\qquad
S(x) = \ell(x)^2 = 4x
$$
$$
V = \int_0^4 4x\,dx = \Big[ 2x^2 \Big]_0^4 = 32
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola $x = y^2$ is symmetric about the $x$-axis and has its vertex at the origin. For each $x$ between $0$ and $4$, the vertical line with horizontal coordinate $x$ intersects the base in the segment from $y = -\sqrt{x}$ to $y = \sqrt{x}$, of length $2\sqrt{x}$.

This segment is the side of the square forming the cross section, so the cross-sectional area is $S(x) = \left( 2\sqrt{x} \right)^2 = 4x$, which is continuous on $[0, 4]$. By the **method of cross sections**, the volume is the integral of $S(x)$ from $0$ to $4$, which equals $32$.

</div>
