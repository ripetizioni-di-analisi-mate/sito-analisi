## Exercise 1

Calculate the distance from the point $P(2, 1, -2)$ to the plane $\pi : x + 2y - 2z + 4 = 0$ and the distance between $\pi$ and the plane $\pi' : 2x + 4y - 4z - 1 = 0$.

$$
d(P, \pi) = \frac{|2 + 2 + 4 + 4|}{\sqrt{1 + 4 + 4}} = \frac{12}{3} = 4
$$
$$
\pi' : \quad x + 2y - 2z - \frac{1}{2} = 0
$$
$$
d(\pi, \pi') = \frac{\left| 4 - \left( -\dfrac{1}{2} \right) \right|}{3} = \frac{\dfrac{9}{2}}{3} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To use the formula for the **distance from a point to a plane**, substitute the coordinates of $P$ into the left-hand side of the equation of $\pi$, take the absolute value and divide by the magnitude of the normal vector $(1, 2, -2)$, which is $3$. The numerator equals $2 + 2 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot (-2) + 4 = 12$, so the distance is $4$.

The normal vectors $(1, 2, -2)$ and $(2, 4, -4)$ are proportional, so the two planes are parallel. The constant terms $4$ and $-1$ are not in the same proportion, so the planes are distinct. To use the formula for the **distance between two parallel planes**, make the coefficients of the variables match by dividing both sides by $2$ in the equation of $\pi'$. The distance is the absolute value of the difference of the constant terms divided by $3$, namely $\dfrac{3}{2}$. As a check, the point $\left( 0, 0, -\dfrac{1}{4} \right)$ lies on $\pi'$ and is at distance $\dfrac{\left| 0 + 0 + \frac{1}{2} + 4 \right|}{3} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ from $\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the orthogonal projection $H$ of the point $P(1, -1, 4)$ onto the plane $\pi : x - y + z - 3 = 0$ and the point $P'$ obtained by reflecting $P$ across $\pi$.

$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + t \\
y = -1 - t \\
z = 4 + t
\end{cases}
$$
$$
(1 + t) - (-1 - t) + (4 + t) - 3 = 3t + 3 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t = -1
$$
$$
H(0, 0, 3)
$$
$$
P' = 2H - P = (0 - 1,\ 0 + 1,\ 6 - 4) = (-1, 1, 2)
$$
$$
d(P, \pi) = \overline{PH} = \sqrt{1 + 1 + 1} = \sqrt{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The orthogonal projection of $P$ is the point where the plane meets the **line through $P$ perpendicular** to $\pi$. This line has the normal vector $(1, -1, 1)$ of the plane as its direction vector, and its parametric equations are those given for $r$.

Substituting the equations of $r$ into the plane equation gives $3t + 3 = 0$, so $t = -1$ and $H(0, 0, 3)$, which satisfies the equation of $\pi$ because $0 - 0 + 3 - 3 = 0$.

The reflection $P'$ is the point for which $H$ is the midpoint of $PP'$. By the midpoint formula, each coordinate of $P'$ is twice the corresponding coordinate of $H$ minus that of $P$. This gives $P'(-1, 1, 2)$. Substituting $t = -2$ into the equations of $r$ gives the same point. The distance from $P$ to the plane is $\overline{PH} = \sqrt{3}$, and the distance formula gives the same value, $\dfrac{|1 + 1 + 4 - 3|}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate the distance from the point $P(4, -1, 0)$ to the line $r : x = 1 + 2t$, $y = t$, $z = 2 - 2t$.

$$
\vec{v} = (2, 1, -2)
$$
$$
\sigma : \quad 2\,(x - 4) + (y + 1) - 2z = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2x + y - 2z - 7 = 0
$$
$$
2\,(1 + 2t) + t - 2\,(2 - 2t) - 7 = 9t - 9 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t = 1
$$
$$
H(3, 1, 0),
\qquad
d(P, r) = \overline{PH} = \sqrt{(3 - 4)^2 + (1 + 1)^2 + 0^2} = \sqrt{5}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-4,0 2,0" vettori="-3,0,0,0,v" segmenti="0,0,0,2.236;-3,0,0,2.236" punti="-3,0,pieno,A;0,0,pieno,H;0,2.236,pieno,P" etichette="2.1,0.15,r;0.15,1.1,√5;-2,1.5,√14" xmin=-4.5 xmax=2.8 ymin=-0.6 ymax=2.9 titolo="In the plane containing $P$ and $r$, we have $\overline{AH} = |\vec{v}| = 3$, $\overline{PH} = \sqrt{5}$ and $\overline{AP} = \sqrt{14}$. Indeed, $9 + 5 = 14$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on the **distance from a point to a line**, find the point $H$ on $r$ such that $PH$ is perpendicular to $r$. The point $H$ lies in the plane $\sigma$ through $P$ perpendicular to $r$, that is, the plane through $P$ whose normal vector is the direction vector $\vec{v} = (2, 1, -2)$.

Substituting the equations of $r$ into the equation of $\sigma$ gives $2 + 4t + t - 4 + 4t - 7 = 9t - 9$, which vanishes for $t = 1$. Therefore $H(3, 1, 0)$ and the distance is $\sqrt{5}$. The vector $\overrightarrow{PH} = (-1, 2, 0)$ is perpendicular to $\vec{v}$ because $-2 + 2 + 0 = 0$.

The cross product formula provides a check. With $A(1, 0, 2)$ we have $\overrightarrow{AP} = (3, -1, -2)$ and $\overrightarrow{AP} \times \vec{v} = (4, 2, 5)$, which has magnitude $\sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}$. Dividing by $|\vec{v}| = 3$ gives $\sqrt{5}$ again.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the points on the line $r : x = t$, $y = 1 + t$, $z = 2 - t$ that are at distance $2$ from the plane $\pi : 2x + y + 2z - 1 = 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(P_t, \pi)
&= \frac{|2t + (1 + t) + 2\,(2 - t) - 1|}{\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{|t + 4|}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{|t + 4|}{3} = 2
\iff
|t + 4| = 6
\iff
t + 4 = 6 \quad \text{or} \quad t + 4 = -6
$$
$$
t = 2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad P_1(2, 3, 0),
\qquad
t = -10 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad P_2(-10, -9, 12)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A general point on $r$ is $P_t(t, 1 + t, 2 - t)$. By the formula for the **distance from a point to a plane**, its distance from $\pi$ is obtained by substituting the coordinates of $P_t$ into the left-hand side of the equation of $\pi$ and dividing the absolute value by $3$, the magnitude of $(2, 1, 2)$. In the numerator, the terms in $t$ give $2t + t - 2t = t$ and the constant terms give $1 + 4 - 1 = 4$, so the distance equals $\dfrac{|t + 4|}{3}$.

The equation $|t + 4| = 6$ holds when $t + 4$ equals either $6$ or $-6$, so the solutions are $t = 2$ and $t = -10$. The required points are $P_1(2, 3, 0)$ and $P_2(-10, -9, 12)$, which lie on opposite sides of the plane. As a check, for $P_1$ the numerator equals $|4 + 3 + 0 - 1| = 6$ and for $P_2$ it equals $|-20 - 9 + 24 - 1| = 6$.

</div>
