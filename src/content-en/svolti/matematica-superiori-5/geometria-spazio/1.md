## Exercise 1

Given the points $A(1, -2, 3)$ and $B(5, 0, -1)$, calculate the length of the segment $AB$ and the coordinates of its midpoint $M$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{AB}
&= \sqrt{(5 - 1)^2 + (0 + 2)^2 + (-1 - 3)^2} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{16 + 4 + 16} \\[6pt]
&= \sqrt{36} = 6
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
M\left( \frac{1 + 5}{2}, \frac{-2 + 0}{2}, \frac{3 - 1}{2} \right) = M(3, -1, 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **distance between two points** is the square root of the sum of the squared differences between corresponding coordinates. The differences are $5 - 1 = 4$, $0 - (-2) = 2$ and $-1 - 3 = -4$. Their squares are $16$, $4$ and $16$, with sum $36$. Therefore $\overline{AB} = 6$.

The coordinates of the **midpoint** are the averages of the corresponding endpoint coordinates. This gives $M(3, -1, 1)$. As a check, $\overline{AM} = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3$, which is half of $\overline{AB}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that the triangle with vertices $A(1, 0, 1)$, $B(3, 2, 2)$ and $C(3, -1, -1)$ is both right and isosceles, and calculate its perimeter and area.

$$
\overline{AB} = \sqrt{2^2 + 2^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{9} = 3
$$
$$
\overline{AC} = \sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{9} = 3
$$
$$
\overline{BC} = \sqrt{0^2 + (-3)^2 + (-3)^2} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\overline{AB}^2 + \overline{AC}^2 = 9 + 9 = 18 = \overline{BC}^2
$$
$$
2p = 3 + 3 + 3\sqrt{2} = 6 + 3\sqrt{2},
\qquad
\text{Area} = \frac{\overline{AB} \cdot \overline{AC}}{2} = \frac{9}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate the lengths of the three sides using the distance formula. Sides $AB$ and $AC$ both have length $3$, so the triangle is **isosceles** with base $BC$.

To determine whether the triangle is right, use the converse of the Pythagorean theorem. The longest side is $BC$, and its squared length $18$ equals the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides, $9 + 9$. Therefore the triangle has a **right angle** at $A$, the vertex opposite $BC$.

The perimeter is the sum of the three side lengths. In a right triangle, each leg is the altitude corresponding to the other, so the area is half the product of the legs $AB$ and $AC$, namely $\dfrac{9}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the point $P$ on the $z$-axis that is equidistant from the points $A(1, 2, 3)$ and $B(-1, 0, 1)$.

$$
P(0, 0, k)
$$
$$
\overline{PA}^2 = 1 + 4 + (3 - k)^2,
\qquad
\overline{PB}^2 = 1 + 0 + (1 - k)^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
5 + 9 - 6k + k^2 &= 1 + 1 - 2k + k^2 \\[6pt]
14 - 6k &= 2 - 2k \\[6pt]
-4k &= -12 \\[6pt]
k &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
P(0, 0, 3)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A point on the $z$-axis has first and second coordinates zero, so write it as $P(0, 0, k)$ with $k$ unknown. The condition $\overline{PA} = \overline{PB}$ compares two positive numbers, so it is equivalent to $\overline{PA}^2 = \overline{PB}^2$. Working with squares avoids square roots.

Expand the squares $(3 - k)^2 = 9 - 6k + k^2$ and $(1 - k)^2 = 1 - 2k + k^2$. The term $k^2$ appears on both sides and cancels, so the equation is linear and has just one solution, $k = 3$. The required point is $P(0, 0, 3)$. As a check, $\overline{PA}^2 = 1 + 4 + 0 = 5$ and $\overline{PB}^2 = 1 + 0 + 4 = 5$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Given the points $A(1, 0, 2)$, $B(3, 1, 0)$ and $C(4, 3, 1)$, find the point $D$ for which $ABCD$ is a parallelogram and calculate the centroid $G$ of triangle $ABC$.

$$
M_{AC}\left( \frac{1 + 4}{2}, \frac{0 + 3}{2}, \frac{2 + 1}{2} \right) = M_{AC}\left( \frac{5}{2}, \frac{3}{2}, \frac{3}{2} \right)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{3 + x_D}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{1 + y_D}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{0 + z_D}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
D(2, 2, 3)
$$
$$
G\left( \frac{1 + 3 + 4}{3}, \frac{0 + 1 + 3}{3}, \frac{2 + 0 + 1}{3} \right) = G\left( \frac{8}{3}, \frac{4}{3}, 1 \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonals of a parallelogram meet at their common midpoint. In parallelogram $ABCD$ the diagonals are $AC$ and $BD$, so the midpoint of $BD$ must be the midpoint of $AC$, which is $\left( \dfrac{5}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2} \right)$.

The three equations between the coordinates of the two midpoints must hold simultaneously, so they form a system. Multiplying each equation by $2$ gives $x_D = 5 - 3 = 2$, $y_D = 3 - 1 = 2$ and $z_D = 3 - 0 = 3$, or $D(2, 2, 3)$. The same result follows using vectors because in a parallelogram $\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{BC} = (1, 2, 1)$, and therefore $D = (1 + 1,\ 0 + 2,\ 2 + 1)$.

The coordinates of the **centroid** are the averages of the corresponding coordinates of the three vertices. This gives $G\left( \dfrac{8}{3}, \dfrac{4}{3}, 1 \right)$.

</div>
