## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the plane $\pi$ through $P(1, 0, 1)$ with normal vector $\vec{n} = (2, 1, 2)$, and find the points where $\pi$ intersects the coordinate axes.

$$
2\,(x - 1) + 1 \cdot (y - 0) + 2\,(z - 1) = 0
$$
$$
\pi : \quad 2x + y + 2z - 4 = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y = z = 0 &\quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2x = 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad A(2, 0, 0) \\[6pt]
x = z = 0 &\quad \Longrightarrow \quad y = 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad B(0, 4, 0) \\[6pt]
x = y = 0 &\quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2z = 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad C(0, 0, 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-1,-1 4,0 0,2" linee="0,0 -2,-2;-1.68,-1.89 -2,-2 -1.89,-1.68;0,0 5,0;4.70,0.15 5,0 4.70,-0.15;0,0 0,3;-0.15,2.70 0,3 0.15,2.70" segmenti="-1,-1,4,0;4,0,0,2;0,2,-1,-1" punti="-1,-1,pieno;4,0,pieno,B;0,2,pieno,C;-0.5,0.5,pieno,P" etichette="-1.5,-0.95,A;-2.35,-1.75,x;5.1,-0.45,y;0.25,2.9,z" xmin=-2.6 xmax=5.6 ymin=-2.4 ymax=3.4 titolo="The part of the plane $2x + y + 2z - 4 = 0$ between the positive coordinate axes is the triangle with vertices $A(2, 0, 0)$, $B(0, 4, 0)$ and $C(0, 0, 2)$, which contains the point $P(1, 0, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For the **equation of a plane** through a point with a given normal vector, multiply the components of $\vec{n}$ by the differences $x - 1$, $y - 0$ and $z - 1$ and add the products. Expanding gives $2x - 2 + y + 2z - 2 = 0$, or $2x + y + 2z - 4 = 0$.

Points on the $x$-axis satisfy $y = 0$ and $z = 0$. Substituting these values into the plane equation leaves $2x - 4 = 0$, so $x = 2$ and the plane meets the $x$-axis at $A(2, 0, 0)$. Similarly, the other intercepts are $B(0, 4, 0)$ on the $y$-axis and $C(0, 0, 2)$ on the $z$-axis. The point $P$ has second coordinate zero, so it lies on side $AC$ of the triangle. This side lies in the plane $y = 0$. More precisely, $P$ is the midpoint of $AC$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the plane through the points $A(1, 1, 0)$, $B(2, 0, 1)$ and $C(0, 2, 3)$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, -1, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 1, 3)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}
&= \big( (-1) \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 1,\ \ 1 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 3,\ \ 1 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot (-1) \big) \\[6pt]
&= (-4, -4, 0)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\vec{n} = (1, 1, 0)
$$
$$
1 \cdot (x - 1) + 1 \cdot (y - 1) + 0 \cdot z = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x + y - 2 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on the **plane through three points**, the cross product $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}$ is normal to the plane. It equals $(-4, -4, 0)$ and is nonzero, so the three points are not collinear. Every nonzero vector parallel to a normal vector is also normal to the plane, so we can use $\vec{n} = (1, 1, 0)$, obtained by dividing by $-4$.

The plane passes through $A$ and has normal vector $(1, 1, 0)$, so its equation is $x + y - 2 = 0$. The coordinates of $B$ and $C$ satisfy it because $2 + 0 - 2 = 0$ and $0 + 2 - 2 = 0$. The equation contains no $z$ term, so the plane is **parallel to the axis** $z$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given the point $P(1, 0, -2)$:

1. write the equation of the plane through $P$ parallel to the plane $2x - 3y + z + 5 = 0$,
2. write the equation of the plane through $P$ perpendicular to the line through $A(0, 1, 1)$ and $B(2, 3, 0)$.

**Part 1**
$$
2\,(x - 1) - 3\,(y - 0) + (z + 2) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2x - 3y + z = 0
$$

**Part 2**
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 2, -1)
$$
$$
2\,(x - 1) + 2\,(y - 0) - (z + 2) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2x + 2y - z - 4 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** By the condition for **parallel planes**, two planes are parallel if their normal vectors are parallel. We can therefore use the same normal vector $(2, -3, 1)$ as the given plane. Expanding gives a constant term of $-2 + 2 = 0$, so the required plane also passes through the origin.

**Part 2.** The line $AB$ has direction vector $\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 2, -1)$. A plane is perpendicular to a line when the line's direction vector is normal to the plane, so the required plane passes through $P$ and has normal vector $(2, 2, -1)$. Expanding the products gives $2x - 2 + 2y - z - 2 = 0$, or $2x + 2y - z - 4 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Write the equation of the plane through the points $A(1, 2, 1)$ and $B(0, 1, 3)$ perpendicular to the plane $\pi : x - y + z = 0$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, -1, 2),
\qquad
\vec{n}_\pi = (1, -1, 1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{n} = \overrightarrow{AB} \times \vec{n}_\pi
&= \big( (-1) \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot (-1),\ \ 2 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot 1,\ \ (-1) \cdot (-1) - (-1) \cdot 1 \big) \\[6pt]
&= (1, 3, 2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(x - 1) + 3\,(y - 2) + 2\,(z - 1) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x + 3y + 2z - 9 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The normal vector $\vec{n}$ of the required plane must satisfy two conditions. The plane contains $A$ and $B$, so $\vec{n}$ is perpendicular to $\overrightarrow{AB}$. The plane is perpendicular to $\pi$, and by the condition for **perpendicular planes** the two normal vectors are perpendicular, so $\vec{n}$ is also perpendicular to $\vec{n}_\pi$.

A vector perpendicular to two nonparallel vectors is given by their **cross product**, which equals $(1, 3, 2)$. The plane passes through $A$, so its equation is $x + 3y + 2z - 9 = 0$. As a check, $B$ satisfies the equation because $0 + 3 + 6 - 9 = 0$, and the normal vectors are perpendicular because $1 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot (-1) + 2 \cdot 1 = 0$.

</div>
