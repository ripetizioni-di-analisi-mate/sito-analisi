## Exercise 1

Consider the points $A(2, 0, 0)$, $B(0, 4, 0)$, $C(0, 0, 2)$ and $D(3, 3, 2)$ in space.

1. Write the equation of the plane $\pi$ through $A$, $B$ and $C$, and calculate the area of triangle $ABC$.
2. Find the orthogonal projection $H$ of $D$ onto $\pi$ and verify that $H$ is the midpoint of side $AB$.
3. Calculate the volume of tetrahedron $ABCD$.
4. Write the equation of the sphere $\Sigma$ with center $D$ tangent to $\pi$, and find the center and radius of the circle in which $\Sigma$ intersects the plane $z = 0$.

**Part 1**
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (-2, 4, 0),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (-2, 0, 2)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}
&= \big( 4 \cdot 2 - 0 \cdot 0,\ \ 0 \cdot (-2) - (-2) \cdot 2,\ \ (-2) \cdot 0 - 4 \cdot (-2) \big) \\[6pt]
&= (8, 4, 8)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\vec{n} = (2, 1, 2)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2\,(x - 2) + y + 2z = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\pi : \ 2x + y + 2z - 4 = 0
$$
$$
\text{Area}(ABC) = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{64 + 16 + 64} = \frac{12}{2} = 6
$$

**Part 2**
$$
x = 3 + 2t, \quad y = 3 + t, \quad z = 2 + 2t
$$
$$
2\,(3 + 2t) + (3 + t) + 2\,(2 + 2t) - 4 = 9t + 9 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t = -1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
H(1, 2, 0)
$$
$$
M_{AB}\left( \frac{2 + 0}{2}, \frac{0 + 4}{2}, \frac{0 + 0}{2} \right) = (1, 2, 0) = H
$$

**Part 3**
$$
\overline{DH} = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3
$$
$$
V = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \text{Area}(ABC) \cdot \overline{DH} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 6 \cdot 3 = 6
$$

**Part 4**
$$
\Sigma : \ (x - 3)^2 + (y - 3)^2 + (z - 2)^2 = 9
$$
$$
d(D, \{ z = 0 \}) = 2 < 3,
\qquad
H'(3, 3, 0),
\qquad
r = \sqrt{9 - 4} = \sqrt{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** By the theorem on the **plane through three points**, the cross product $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} = (8, 4, 8)$ is normal to the plane. Dividing by $4$ gives another normal vector, $\vec{n} = (2, 1, 2)$. The plane passes through $A$, so its equation is $2x + y + 2z - 4 = 0$. The coordinates of $B$ and $C$ satisfy it because $4 - 4 = 0$ and $4 - 4 = 0$. By the theorem on the **area of a triangle**, the area is half the magnitude of the cross product, that is, half of $\sqrt{144} = 12$.

**Part 2.** The projection $H$ is the intersection of $\pi$ with the **line through $D$ perpendicular** to $\pi$, whose direction vector is $\vec{n} = (2, 1, 2)$. Substituting the line equations into the plane equation gives $6 + 4t + 3 + t + 4 + 4t - 4 = 9t + 9$, which vanishes for $t = -1$. Therefore $H(1, 2, 0)$, and its coordinates agree with those of the midpoint of $AB$.

**Part 3.** The tetrahedron is a pyramid with triangle $ABC$ as its base and with height equal to the distance from $D$ to the plane of the base, namely $\overline{DH} = 3$. The same distance follows from the formula for the **distance from a point to a plane**, $\dfrac{|6 + 3 + 4 - 4|}{3} = 3$. The volume of a pyramid is one third of the base area times the height, so the volume equals $6$.

**Part 4.** The sphere with center $D$ is tangent to $\pi$ when its radius equals the distance from $D$ to $\pi$, namely $3$. The point of tangency is $H$. The plane $z = 0$ is at distance $2$ from $D$ because the height of $D$ is $2$. Since $2 < 3$, by the theorem on the **relative positions of a plane and a sphere**, the plane $z = 0$ is a secant plane. The circle is centered at the projection of $D$ onto the plane $z = 0$, namely $H'(3, 3, 0)$, and has radius $\sqrt{3^2 - 2^2} = \sqrt{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Consider the line $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2 - t$, $z = t$ and, for a variable real parameter $k$, the plane
$$
\pi_k : \quad x + k y + z - k = 0 .
$$

1. Find the value of $k$ for which the line $r$ is parallel to $\pi_k$, and determine whether $r$ lies in the plane for that value.
2. For $k = 1$, find the intersection point of $r$ and $\pi_1$.
3. Find the value of $k$ for which $r$ is perpendicular to $\pi_k$ and the point where $r$ meets the corresponding plane.
4. Calculate the distance between the line $r$ and the plane found in part 1.

**Part 1**
$$
\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n}_k = (1, -1, 1) \cdot (1, k, 1) = 2 - k = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
k = 2
$$
$$
\pi_2 : \ x + 2y + z - 2 = 0,
\qquad
P_0(1, 2, 0) : \ 1 + 4 + 0 - 2 = 3 \neq 0
$$

**Part 2**
$$
\pi_1 : \ x + y + z - 1 = 0
$$
$$
(1 + t) + (2 - t) + t - 1 = t + 2 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t = -2
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
(-1, 4, -2)
$$

**Part 3**
$$
(1, k, 1) = \lambda\,(1, -1, 1)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\lambda = 1, \quad k = -1
$$
$$
\pi_{-1} : \ x - y + z + 1 = 0
$$
$$
(1 + t) - (2 - t) + t + 1 = 3t = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
P_0(1, 2, 0)
$$

**Part 4**
$$
d(r, \pi_2) = d(P_0, \pi_2) = \frac{|1 + 4 + 0 - 2|}{\sqrt{1 + 4 + 1}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{6}} = \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The direction vector of $r$ is $\vec{v} = (1, -1, 1)$ and the normal vector of $\pi_k$ is $\vec{n}_k = (1, k, 1)$. By the theorem on the **relative positions of a line and a plane**, $r$ is parallel to $\pi_k$ if and only if $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n}_k = 0$, that is, for $k = 2$. The point $P_0(1, 2, 0)$ on $r$, obtained for $t = 0$, does not satisfy the equation of $\pi_2$, so $r$ is **parallel to but does not lie in** $\pi_2$.

**Part 2.** For $k = 1$ the dot product is $2 - 1 = 1 \neq 0$, so the line and plane intersect. Substituting the equations of $r$ into the equation of $\pi_1$ gives $t + 2 = 0$, or $t = -2$, and the intersection point is $(-1, 4, -2)$. Indeed, $-1 + 4 - 2 - 1 = 0$.

**Part 3.** By the theorem on a **line perpendicular to a plane**, $r$ is perpendicular to $\pi_k$ if and only if $\vec{n}_k$ is parallel to $\vec{v}$. The first and third components give $\lambda = 1$, and the second gives $k = -\lambda = -1$. The corresponding plane is $x - y + z + 1 = 0$ because the constant term $-k$ equals $1$. Substituting the equations of $r$ gives $3t = 0$, so $r$ meets the plane at the point $P_0(1, 2, 0)$.

**Part 4.** The line $r$ is parallel to $\pi_2$, so all its points are the same distance from the plane. This is called the distance between the line and the plane. It therefore suffices to calculate the **distance from a point to a plane** using the point $P_0$ on $r$. This gives $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{6}}$, which becomes $\dfrac{3\sqrt{6}}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$ after rationalizing the denominator.

</div>
