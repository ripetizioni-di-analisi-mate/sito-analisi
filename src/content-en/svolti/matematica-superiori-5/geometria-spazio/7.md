## Exercise 1

Find the center and radius of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2x + 6y - 4z - 11 = 0$ and determine whether the points $A(4, 1, 2)$ and $O(0, 0, 0)$ lie on, inside or outside it.

$$
C\left( -\frac{-2}{2}, -\frac{6}{2}, -\frac{-4}{2} \right) = C(1, -3, 2)
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{1 + 9 + 4 + 11} = \sqrt{25} = 5
$$
$$
\overline{CA} = \sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (1 + 3)^2 + (2 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5 = R
$$
$$
\overline{CO} = \sqrt{1 + 9 + 4} = \sqrt{14} < 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on **finding the center and radius from the equation**, the coordinates of the center are the negatives of half the coefficients of $x$, $y$ and $z$, giving $C(1, -3, 2)$. The radicand in the radius formula is $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + \gamma^2 - d = 1 + 9 + 4 - (-11) = 25$, which is positive, so the equation does represent a sphere, of radius $5$.

A point lies on the sphere if its distance from the center is $R$, inside if its distance is less than $R$, and outside if it is greater. The point $A$ is at distance $5$ from $C$, so it **lies on** the sphere. Indeed, substituting its coordinates in the equation gives $16 + 1 + 4 - 8 + 6 - 8 - 11 = 0$. The origin is at distance $\sqrt{14} < 5$, so it is **inside**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

1. Write the equation of the sphere whose diameter is the segment with endpoints $A(3, -1, 2)$ and $B(-1, 3, 0)$.
2. Write the equation of the sphere with center $C(2, -1, 3)$ tangent to the plane $\pi : 2x - y + 2z + 4 = 0$.

**Part 1**
$$
C\left( \frac{3 - 1}{2}, \frac{-1 + 3}{2}, \frac{2 + 0}{2} \right) = C(1, 1, 1)
$$
$$
\overline{AB} = \sqrt{16 + 16 + 4} = 6
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
R = 3
$$
$$
(x - 1)^2 + (y - 1)^2 + (z - 1)^2 = 9
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2x - 2y - 2z - 6 = 0
$$

**Part 2**
$$
R = d(C, \pi) = \frac{|4 + 1 + 6 + 4|}{\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4}} = \frac{15}{3} = 5
$$
$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 + (z - 3)^2 = 25
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 4x + 2y - 6z - 11 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The center of a sphere is the midpoint of every diameter, so $C$ is the **midpoint** of $AB$. The radius is half the diameter, namely $3$. Expanding the squares gives a constant term of $1 + 1 + 1 - 9 = -6$.

**Part 2.** By the theorem on the **relative positions of a plane and a sphere**, the plane is tangent when the distance from the center to the plane equals the radius. Thus the required radius is the distance from $C$ to $\pi$, which equals $5$. Expanding the squares gives a constant term of $4 + 1 + 9 - 25 = -11$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Verify that the plane $\pi : x + 2y + 2z - 11 = 0$ intersects the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 4x - 2y + 2z - 10 = 0$ in a circle, and find the center and radius of the circle.

$$
C(2, 1, -1),
\qquad
R = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 1 + 10} = 4
$$
$$
\delta = d(C, \pi) = \frac{|2 + 2 - 2 - 11|}{\sqrt{1 + 4 + 4}} = \frac{9}{3} = 3 < 4
$$
$$
x = 2 + t, \quad y = 1 + 2t, \quad z = -1 + 2t
$$
$$
(2 + t) + 2\,(1 + 2t) + 2\,(-1 + 2t) - 11 = 9t - 9 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
t = 1
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
H(3, 3, 1)
$$
$$
r = \sqrt{R^2 - \delta^2} = \sqrt{16 - 9} = \sqrt{7}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="4*cos(t),4*sin(t),0,6.29" linee="-4.6,3 4.6,3" segmenti="0,0,0,3;0,0,2.646,3" punti="0,0,pieno,C;0,3,pieno,H;2.646,3,pieno,Q" etichette="4.7,3.15,π;-0.45,1.4,3;1.55,1.2,4" xmin=-5 xmax=5.4 ymin=-4.6 ymax=4.6 legenda="0" titolo="In a cross section through $C$ and $H$, we have $\overline{CH} = 3$ and $\overline{CQ} = 4$, so the radius of the circle is $\overline{HQ} = \sqrt{7}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation gives the center $C(2, 1, -1)$ and radius $R = 4$. By the theorem on the **relative positions of a plane and a sphere**, compare the distance $\delta$ from the center to the plane with the radius. This gives $\delta = 3 < 4$, so the plane is a **secant plane** and its intersection with the sphere is a circle.

The center of the circle is the projection $H$ of $C$ onto the plane. The line through $C$ perpendicular to $\pi$ has the normal vector $(1, 2, 2)$ as a direction vector. Substituting its equations into the plane equation gives $t = 1$, or $H(3, 3, 1)$. Indeed, $3 + 6 + 2 - 11 = 0$.

Calculate the radius of the circle using the Pythagorean theorem in right triangle $CHQ$, where $Q$ is any point on the circle. In this triangle the hypotenuse $CQ$ is the radius $R = 4$ and the leg $CH$ is the distance $\delta = 3$, so the radius of the circle is $\sqrt{16 - 9} = \sqrt{7}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Given the sphere $\Sigma : x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2x - 4y - 4 = 0$:

1. verify that the point $T(1, 4, 1)$ lies on $\Sigma$ and write the equation of the tangent plane to $\Sigma$ at $T$,
2. find the other plane tangent to $\Sigma$ and parallel to the plane found in part 1, and find its point of tangency.

**Part 1**
$$
C(-1, 2, 0),
\qquad
R = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 0 + 4} = 3
$$
$$
1 + 16 + 1 + 2 - 16 - 4 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
T \in \Sigma
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{CT} = (2, 2, 1)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2\,(x - 1) + 2\,(y - 4) + (z - 1) = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
2x + 2y + z - 11 = 0
$$

**Part 2**
$$
2x + 2y + z + k = 0,
\qquad
\frac{|-2 + 4 + 0 + k|}{\sqrt{4 + 4 + 1}} = 3
\iff
|k + 2| = 9
$$
$$
k = 7 \quad \text{or} \quad k = -11
$$
$$
2x + 2y + z + 7 = 0,
\qquad
T' = C - \overrightarrow{CT} = (-3, 0, -1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** The coordinates of $T$ satisfy the equation of $\Sigma$, so $T$ lies on the sphere. By the theorem on the **tangent plane to a sphere**, the tangent plane at $T$ is the plane through $T$ perpendicular to the radius $CT$, that is, the plane through $T$ with normal vector $\overrightarrow{CT} = (2, 2, 1)$. Expanding the products gives a constant term of $-2 - 8 - 1 = -11$.

**Part 2.** Planes parallel to the plane just found share its normal vector, so their equations have the form $2x + 2y + z + k = 0$. A plane is tangent to $\Sigma$ if the distance from the center to the plane equals the radius $3$. The normal vector has magnitude $3$, so the condition becomes $|k + 2| = 9$, with solutions $k = 7$ and $k = -11$. The value $k = -11$ gives the plane from part 1, so the other tangent plane is $2x + 2y + z + 7 = 0$.

The point of tangency $T'$ is diametrically opposite $T$ because the two planes are perpendicular to the same line $CT$ and the center is the midpoint of $TT'$. This gives $T' = (-1 - 2,\ 2 - 2,\ 0 - 1) = (-3, 0, -1)$, which satisfies the plane equation because $-6 + 0 - 1 + 7 = 0$.

</div>
