## Exercise 1

Calculate the angle between the vectors $\vec{u} = (2, -1, 2)$ and $\vec{v} = (0, 3, -3)$.

$$
\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 2 \cdot 0 + (-1) \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot (-3) = -9
$$
$$
|\vec{u}| = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3,
\qquad
|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{0 + 9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\cos \theta = \frac{-9}{3 \cdot 3\sqrt{2}} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\theta = \frac{3}{4}\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In coordinates, the **dot product** is the sum of the products of the corresponding components, which here equals $-9$. The magnitude of each vector is the square root of the sum of its squared components.

By the formula for the **angle between two vectors**, the cosine of $\theta$ is the dot product divided by the product of the magnitudes. This gives $\cos \theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and the only angle in the interval $[0, \pi]$ with this cosine is $\dfrac{3}{4}\pi$. The dot product is negative, so the angle is obtuse, as the result shows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

1. Find the value of $k$ for which the vectors $\vec{u} = (k, 2, -1)$ and $\vec{v} = (3, k, 4)$ are perpendicular.
2. Find $h$ and $m$ so that the vector $\vec{w} = (2, h, m)$ is parallel to the vector $(4, -2, 6)$.

**Part 1**
$$
\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 3k + 2k - 4 = 5k - 4 = 0
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
k = \frac{4}{5}
$$

**Part 2**
$$
(2, h, m) = \lambda\,(4, -2, 6)
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\begin{cases}
2 = 4\lambda \\
h = -2\lambda \\
m = 6\lambda
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{1}{2}, \quad h = -1, \quad m = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Part 1.** By the **perpendicularity condition**, two nonzero vectors are perpendicular if and only if their dot product is zero. The dot product is $k \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot k + (-1) \cdot 4 = 5k - 4$, which vanishes for $k = \dfrac{4}{5}$. For this value the vectors are $\left( \dfrac{4}{5}, 2, -1 \right)$ and $\left( 3, \dfrac{4}{5}, 4 \right)$, and indeed $\dfrac{12}{5} + \dfrac{8}{5} - 4 = 0$.

**Part 2.** By the **parallelism condition**, $\vec{w}$ is parallel to $(4, -2, 6)$ if and only if it is a scalar multiple of it, that is, if there is a number $\lambda$ such that $\vec{w} = \lambda\,(4, -2, 6)$. The three coordinate equations must hold simultaneously and therefore form a system. The first equation gives $\lambda = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and substitution into the other two gives $h = -1$ and $m = 3$. The vector $\vec{w} = (2, -1, 3)$ is therefore half of $(4, -2, 6)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate the area of the triangle with vertices $A(1, 2, 0)$, $B(3, 2, 1)$ and $C(1, 4, 2)$ and the altitude to the side $AB$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 0, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (0, 2, 2)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}
&= \big( 0 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 2,\ \ 1 \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot 2,\ \ 2 \cdot 2 - 0 \cdot 0 \big) \\[6pt]
&= (-2, -4, 4)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\left| \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} \right| = \sqrt{4 + 16 + 16} = 6
\qquad
\Longrightarrow
\qquad
\text{Area} = \frac{6}{2} = 3
$$
$$
\overline{AB} = \sqrt{4 + 0 + 1} = \sqrt{5},
\qquad
h = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Area}}{\overline{AB}} = \frac{6}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{6\sqrt{5}}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ are obtained by subtracting the coordinates of $A$ from those of $B$ and $C$. The magnitude of their **cross product** is the area of the parallelogram formed by the two sides, and the triangle has half that area. Therefore the area of the triangle is $3$.

In the cross product the first component is $b c' - c b'$, with $b = 0$, $c = 1$, $b' = 2$ and $c' = 2$, and it equals $-2$. The other two components are calculated in the same way. As a check, the result $(-2, -4, 4)$ is perpendicular to both vectors because $(-2) \cdot 2 + 0 + 4 \cdot 1 = 0$ and $0 - 8 + 8 = 0$.

The area is also half the product of the base $AB$ and the corresponding height $h$. Solving for $h$ gives $h = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{5}}$, which becomes $\dfrac{6\sqrt{5}}{5}$ after rationalizing the denominator.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the unit vectors perpendicular to both $\vec{u} = (1, 0, 1)$ and $\vec{v} = (1, 2, 0)$.

$$
\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \big( 0 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 2,\ \ 1 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0,\ \ 1 \cdot 2 - 0 \cdot 1 \big) = (-2, 1, 2)
$$
$$
|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}| = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3
$$
$$
\text{unit vectors: } \quad \left( -\frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3}, \frac{2}{3} \right)
\quad \text{and} \quad
\left( \frac{2}{3}, -\frac{1}{3}, -\frac{2}{3} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **cross product** of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ is perpendicular to both vectors. Every vector perpendicular to both $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ is a multiple of that cross product because $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are not parallel. The cross product is $(-2, 1, 2)$. Perpendicularity can be checked with the dot products, $-2 + 0 + 2 = 0$ and $-2 + 2 + 0 = 0$.

A **unit vector** is a vector of magnitude $1$. Dividing $(-2, 1, 2)$ by its magnitude $3$ gives a unit vector. Its negative is the other unit vector along the same line. There are therefore two solutions pointing in opposite directions.

</div>
