## Exercise 1

Three samples show: A, an oxidized base recognized by a glycosylase; B, helix-distorting UV photoproducts; C, mismatches remaining after replication. Match each case to the main pathway described in the theory and identify the information guiding restoration.

A → base excision repair.

B → nucleotide excision repair.

C → mismatch repair.

In all three cases, the intact complementary strand provides the information needed to restore the tract.

![Diagram of the data and relationships considered](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genetica-mutazioni/riparazione-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

In sample A, recognition concerns one chemically altered base. Glycosylase removes it from the sugar; other enzymes complete restoration. In sample B, distortion of the double helix matters: a lesion-containing tract is removed and then resynthesized. In sample C, bases remain incorrectly paired after synthesis and the system must identify the new-strand tract.

These mechanisms share recovery of sequence through complementarity, but begin by recognizing different problems. Ligase contributes to final strand sealing and cannot, by itself, determine which base to replace. The matching describes the stated teaching cases; within cells, several systems can cooperate in responding to a treatment that produces different lesions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A double-strand break is repaired in two experiments. In the first, a homologous copy is used as a template and the local sequence is restored. In the second, the ends are processed and joined, losing two bases. Distinguish the mechanisms and assess whether the second outcome would cause a frameshift within a translated region.

First experiment → homologous recombination.

Second experiment → end joining with a two-base deletion.

Within an internal translated region, deletion of two bases causes a frameshift because $2$ is not a multiple of $3$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The decisive information in the first case is use of a homologous template, allowing the missing tract to be reconstructed. The sister chromatid is often available after S phase. The second case shows reconnection of processed ends; the local loss is compatible with that mechanism. Joining can also be precise with suitable ends, so the deletion is the outcome observed here rather than a mandatory property of every event.

Assessing the protein consequence requires a different criterion: the number of deleted nucleotides. Two bases change the grouping of subsequent triplets. Outside the translated region, the term frameshift would not directly describe the effect, although the variant could alter regulation or splicing.

</div>
