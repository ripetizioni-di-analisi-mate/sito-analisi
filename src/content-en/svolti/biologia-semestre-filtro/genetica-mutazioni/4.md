## Exercise 1

In the classical ABO model, a group A and group B couple already has an O child. Infer the parental genotypes and calculate the probability that the next child is AB. Assume ordinary antigen expression and no new mutations.

O child → $ii$. Each parent transmitted $i$.

Parents → $I^Ai\times I^Bi$.

$$
P(\mathrm{AB})=P(I^A)P(I^B)=\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac14.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first parent’s A phenotype would initially allow two genotypes, but the O child shows that this parent can supply i. The parent must therefore be heterozygous. The same reasoning identifies heterozygosity in the B parent. The already-born child resolves uncertainty about the parents.

For the new pregnancy, combine gametes from the inferred genotypes. AB requires $I^A$ from the A parent and $I^B$ from the B parent, each with probability one half. The other combinations produce A, B and O, each also with probability one quarter. The first child does not reduce the available alleles or force the next child to have a different group; fertilizations are separate events.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An $I^AI^B$ parent and an $I^Ai$ parent have a child. In the classical ABO model, list all possible genotypes with probabilities and derive probabilities of groups A, B, AB and O.

Each combination has probability $\dfrac14$:

- $I^AI^A$ → A;
- $I^Ai$ → A;
- $I^AI^B$ → AB;
- $I^Bi$ → B.

$$
P(\mathrm A)=\frac12,\quad P(\mathrm B)=\frac14,\quad
P(\mathrm{AB})=\frac14,\quad P(\mathrm O)=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The AB parent produces A and B gametes, whereas the heterozygous A parent produces A and i. The four gamete pairs have equal weight. Two different genotypes belong to group A because $I^A$ dominates i and the A homozygote expresses the same group. Their two quarters therefore add to one half.

AB expresses both antigens through codominance; $I^Bi$ belongs to B. Group O would require ii, but the AB parent does not carry i and cannot transmit it. The four group probabilities sum to one. This check distinguishes the genotype list, containing four equally likely outcomes, from the phenotype list, which contains classes with different weights.

</div>
