## Exercise 1

In AaBb × Aabb, the loci assort independently. Calculate the probability of aaBb and of phenotype A_B_, assuming complete dominance and penetrance at both loci.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathrm{aaBb})&=P(\mathrm{aa})P(\mathrm{Bb})
=\frac14\cdot\frac12=\frac18,\\[6pt]
P(\mathrm{A\_B\_})&=P(\mathrm{A\_})P(\mathrm{B\_})
=\frac34\cdot\frac12=\frac38.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Separate the two loci using the stated independence. At locus A, the cross is Aa × Aa, with aa probability one quarter and A_ probability three quarters. At locus B, the cross is Bb × bb: offspring are half Bb and half bb, so B_ here coincides with Bb.

For aaBb, two precise genotypes are required and their probabilities are multiplied. For A_B_, AA and Aa are instead grouped together because complete dominance makes their phenotypes indistinguishable. The underscore therefore matters in counting: it indicates that the second allele at the locus can take several compatible values. Automatically applying the nine-to-three-to-three-to-one ratio would be incorrect because the second parent is not heterozygous at both loci.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In AaBb × AaBb, the loci are independent. A_B_ produces purple pigment, A_bb red pigment and aa__ no pigment. Among 160 individuals, calculate expected numbers for the three phenotypes and explain which genotypes are combined in the last class.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(\mathrm{A\_B\_})&=160\cdot\frac9{16}=90,\\[6pt]
E(\mathrm{A\_bb})&=160\cdot\frac3{16}=30,\\[6pt]
E(\mathrm{aa\_\_})&=160\cdot\frac4{16}=40.
\end{aligned}
$$

The unpigmented class comprises aaBB, aaBb and aabb. The expected total is $90+30+40=160$.

![Diagram of the data and relationships considered](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genetica-mutazioni/epistasi-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Purple requires at least one A and at least one B. The probabilities three quarters and three quarters multiply to nine sixteenths. Red requires A_ together with bb, hence three quarters times one quarter, or three sixteenths. With genotype aa, the pathway’s first step is blocked and locus B cannot modify a missing precursor.

The aaBB, aaBb and aabb combinations contribute one, two and one sixteenths respectively to the unpigmented class. Their total is four sixteenths. Multiplying each fraction by 160 gives expected numbers rather than a deterministic prediction for every sample. Random variation can produce different counts; the model concerns the underlying probabilities.

</div>
