## Exercise 1

In the AB/ab × ab/ab testcross, 400 AB/ab, 400 ab/ab, 100 Ab/ab and 100 aB/ab offspring are observed. Identify parental and recombinant classes, calculate recombination frequency and give probabilities of the four classes in the estimated model. Assume equal viability.

Parental: AB and ab. Recombinant: Ab and aB.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=400+400+100+100=1000,\\[6pt]
r&=\frac{100+100}{1000}=0{,}20,\\[6pt]
P(\mathrm{Ab})=P(\mathrm{aB})&=\frac r2=0{,}10,\\[6pt]
P(\mathrm{AB})=P(\mathrm{ab})&=\frac{1-r}{2}=0{,}40.
\end{aligned}
$$

![Diagram of the data and relationships considered](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genetica-mutazioni/linkage-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Phase AB/ab specifies the original combinations on the two homologs. The recessive tester supplies only ab, so each offspring genotype reveals the other parent’s gamete. Add both classes differing from parental phase, obtaining 200 recombinants.

The value r is the combined recombinant fraction, so each reciprocal class has half of r in the symmetric model. The remaining fraction is one minus r and is divided between the two parental classes. The check $0{,}40+0{,}40+0{,}10+0{,}10=1$ confirms normalization. The count estimates recombination frequency; accurate conversion into genetic distance must also consider multiple exchanges, which become important across longer intervals.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A three-locus testcross gives these gamete classes: ABC 420, abc 420, Abc 40, aBC 40, ABc 30, abC 30, AbC 10, aBc 10. The rarest classes are double recombinants. Determine gene order, the two short-interval distances and recombination frequency between the outer markers.

Comparing ABC with AbC, only B changes. The order is A–B–C, equivalent to C–B–A. The total is 1000.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{AB}&=\frac{40+40+10+10}{1000}=0{,}10,\\[6pt]
r_{BC}&=\frac{30+30+10+10}{1000}=0{,}08,\\[6pt]
r_{AC}&=\frac{40+40+30+30}{1000}=0{,}14.
\end{aligned}
$$

The short intervals are approximately 10 cM and 8 cM; their sum along the map is 18 cM.

<div class="spiegazione">

The most frequent classes define parental phase ABC/abc. A double exchange changes the middle gene’s relationship with both outer markers: comparison with the rare classes therefore identifies B. For interval AB, count single exchanges producing Abc and aBC and also add double exchanges, which cross that interval. The same criterion applies to BC.

When only A and C are compared, double exchanges restore the parental outer combination. They are therefore excluded from the $r_{AC}$ numerator despite contributing to both map intervals. The difference between 18 cM and 14% comes from the 20 double recombinants among 1000, counted twice in the interval sum. The centimorgan approximation assumes sufficiently short intervals and unbiased recovery of classes.

</div>
