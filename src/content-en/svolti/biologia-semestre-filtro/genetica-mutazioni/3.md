## Exercise 1

Two Aa parents are considered for a fully penetrant autosomal recessive trait. Calculate the probabilities of AA, Aa and aa in one child. Then calculate carrier probability for a child already known to be unaffected.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\mathrm{AA})&=\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac14,\\[6pt]
P(\mathrm{Aa})&=\frac14+\frac14=\frac12,\\[6pt]
P(\mathrm{aa})&=\frac14.
\end{aligned}
$$

Denoting the unaffected phenotype by U:

$$
P(\mathrm{Aa}\mid U)=\frac{\frac12}{\frac34}=\frac23.
$$

![Diagram of the data and relationships considered](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genetica-mutazioni/punnett-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Each parent supplies A or a with equal probability. The four gamete combinations are equally likely; two produce the heterozygote because A may come from either mother or father. The genotypic probabilities sum to one, as required for all possible outcomes.

Once the child is known to be unaffected, aa is excluded because penetrance is complete. The relevant sample then includes AA and Aa, with total weight three quarters. Dividing the weight of Aa by this total retains the correct relative proportions among remaining outcomes. One half described the child before observation; the new information updates the probability to two thirds without changing the child’s actual genotype.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two Aa parents have three independent children. Calculate the probability that exactly two are aa and the probability that at least one is aa. Assume Mendelian segregation and no differences in viability.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\text{exactly two})
&=3\left(\frac14\right)^2\frac34=\frac9{64},\\[6pt]
P(\text{at least one})
&=1-\left(\frac34\right)^3=\frac{37}{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An aa child has probability one quarter, whereas a child with another genotype has probability three quarters. Exactly two aa children require two one-quarter factors and one three-quarter factor. The non-aa child may be first, second or third: these three disjoint orders explain the factor three.

For at least one aa child, the complement is shorter to calculate. The only excluded case has three non-aa children, with probability twenty-seven sixty-fourths. Subtracting it from one correctly includes cases with one, two or three aa children. Adding three individual one-quarter probabilities would count some families more than once and would not represent the required probability.

</div>
