## Exercise 1

Two unaffected parents have an aa child for a fully penetrant autosomal recessive trait. Their unaffected daughter has a partner known to be Aa. Excluding new mutations, calculate the probability that the couple’s child is aa.

The aa child requires both parents to be Aa. For the unaffected daughter:

$$
P(\mathrm{Aa}\mid U)=\frac23.
$$

With an Aa partner:

$$
P(\text{aa child})=\frac23\cdot\frac14=\frac16.
$$

![Diagram of the data and relationships considered](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genetica-mutazioni/pedigree-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step uses the pedigree to infer genotypes in the preceding generation. Each parent transmitted a to the affected child; because each is unaffected under complete penetrance, each also carries A. The unaffected daughter can therefore be AA or Aa, with conditional probabilities one third and two thirds respectively.

If the daughter is AA, crossing with Aa cannot produce aa. If she is Aa, crossing with the Aa partner produces aa with probability one quarter. Weight this second scenario by the probability that the daughter is a carrier. The product gives one sixth; assigning Aa directly would give one quarter and ignore the remaining uncertainty. The figure shows only the initial nuclear family, from which the deduction starts.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An unaffected individual has an aa sibling and unaffected parents for a fully penetrant autosomal recessive trait. An independent estimate assigns the unaffected partner probability $\dfrac1{20}$ of being Aa and $\dfrac{19}{20}$ of being AA. Calculate an aa child’s probability, excluding new mutations and assuming independence of information about the two parents.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\text{both Aa})&=\frac23\cdot\frac1{20}=\frac1{30},\\[6pt]
P(\text{aa child})&=\frac1{30}\cdot\frac14=\frac1{120}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Family history gives the first individual carrier probability two thirds by the same reasoning conditional on the unaffected phenotype. The partner has a different probability supplied by the question. To produce aa without new mutations, both must be Aa and then both must transmit a.

The first two factors concern uncertainty about parental genotypes; the final factor, one quarter, concerns segregation when both are carriers. Separating these levels prevents treating the partner’s frequency as though it were already the child’s probability. The value is a result of the specified numerical model, rather than an individual estimate obtainable from a pedigree without further evidence. The stated independence allows multiplication of the two parents’ probabilities.

</div>
