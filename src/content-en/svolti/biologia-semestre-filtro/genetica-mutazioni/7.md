## Exercise 1

In a sample of 80 carriers of the same variant, 60 show a trait at a specified age; intensity varies among those 60. Estimate penetrance and distinguish the expressivity finding. Then, for Aa × aa with Aa penetrance equal to that estimate and no trait in aa, calculate the child's phenotype probability.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=\frac{60}{80}=0{,}75=75\%,\\[6pt]
P(\mathrm{Aa})&=\frac12,\\[6pt]
P(\text{trait})&=\frac12\cdot\frac34=\frac38=37{,}5\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

Observed penetrance is 75%; differing intensity among those showing the trait indicates variable expressivity.

<div class="spiegazione">

To estimate penetrance, the denominator includes all carriers, including the 20 without manifestation. The numerator counts only those meeting the phenotype definition at the specified age. Intensity among positive cases answers a different question and describes expressivity.

The child calculation requires two steps: inheriting A and then showing the trait when Aa. The probability of inheriting A remains one half; penetrance enters only at the second step. Multiply by three quarters to obtain three eighths. Directly using 75% would assign the genotype to every child, whereas using only 50% would ignore carriers without manifestation. Applying the estimate to this new context is an explicit assumption of the exercise.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an imprinting model, the maternal gene copy is silenced and the paternal copy is active. An inactivating variant causes the trait only when present on the active copy, with complete penetrance. Compare a heterozygous mother and noncarrier father with a heterozygous father and noncarrier mother. What is the trait probability in their children?

Heterozygous mother → variant transmission with probability $\dfrac12$, on the silenced maternal copy. Trait probability in the model: $0$.

Heterozygous father → variant transmission with probability $\dfrac12$, on the active paternal copy. Trait probability in the model: $\dfrac12$.

![Diagram of the data and relationships considered](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genetica-mutazioni/imprinting-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Allele segregation follows the same Mendelian probability of one half in both cases. What changes is the copy carrying the variant in the child. When transmitted by the mother, the variant lies in the silenced copy, while the intact paternal copy supplies function. When transmitted by the father, half the children receive a variant in the only normally active copy.

The comparison concerns the gene and tissue defined by the exercise. The variant’s grandparental origin does not determine its mark forever: imprints are reorganized in the germ line. One must therefore follow the transmitting parent in the generation studied. This dependence on parental origin differs from mitochondrial inheritance, where the genetic material being transmitted is different.

</div>
