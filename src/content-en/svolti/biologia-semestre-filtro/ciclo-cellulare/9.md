## Exercise 1

Clone A produces differentiated cells for two weeks and then exhausts. Clone B continues through repeated experimental transplants to maintain cells with the same capacity and generate differentiated progeny. Which evidence better supports stemness, and what is missing from A alone?

B provides stronger evidence of **long-term self-renewal together with differentiation**. A demonstrates time-limited proliferation and differentiation, also compatible with a progenitor. Production of differentiated cells alone does not establish durable maintenance of the population generating them.

![Symmetric and asymmetric outcomes of stem-cell divisions](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/ciclo-cellulare/staminali-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The defining property combines two capacities rather than simply faster division. Repeated observations over time and successive passages distinguish a maintained source from one that becomes exhausted. Interpretation of B still depends on experimental conditions and the cell types produced: it does not automatically establish pluripotency, which concerns the range of possible fates.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two daughters receive the same chromosome complement. One remains in contact with the niche and retains stem-cell capacity; the other moves away and differentiates. Does the different fate necessarily require a DNA mutation? Explain an alternative.

A mutation is not required: **different signals and contacts** can alter gene expression and stabilise distinct cellular programmes. The daughter within the niche receives maintenance-supporting conditions; the other may lose these signals or encounter differentiation signals.

<div class="spiegazione">

The same DNA does not imply the same activity of every gene. Division can be asymmetric through determinant distribution or daughter-cell position relative to the environment; this case describes the latter. Population self-renewal can also be sustained by balanced symmetric divisions, so there is no universal requirement for one stem and one differentiated daughter at every division.

</div>
