## Exercise 1

A cell’s securin is non-degradable and continues to inhibit separase. APC/C activates normally. Identify where the sequence normally releasing sister chromatids is interrupted.

The normal sequence is: APC/C tags securin → the proteasome degrades it → separase becomes active → a cohesin subunit is cleaved. Here effective securin degradation does not occur. Separase remains inhibited and cohesion is not removed through this step.

![Securin ubiquitylation, separase activation and cohesin cleavage](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/ciclo-cellulare/apc-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Each component must be assigned its own action: ubiquitylation, degradation and cohesin cleavage are not synonyms. Activating an upstream enzyme does not ensure normal processing of a mutant substrate. The problem is not failure to replicate DNA: the sisters exist, but their connection is not released. This separates a duplication defect from a segregation defect.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A human cell completes mitotic segregation, but an inhibitor prevents contraction of the actin–myosin ring. Two nuclei appear in one cytoplasm. Which process failed? How many chromosomes are expected in each nucleus if segregation was normal?

**Cytokinesis** failed, not necessarily chromosome segregation. Each newly formed nucleus normally contains 46 unduplicated chromosomes, or $2n,2C$. The whole binucleate cell therefore contains the DNA of both nuclei and must not be described as one nucleus with 46 duplicated chromosomes.

<div class="spiegazione">

First establish the counting unit: one nucleus or the whole cell. Normal segregation is given, so each pole received a complete diploid complement. The contractile ring separates the cytoplasm; it does not replicate or redistribute chromosomes already segregated. Binucleation is compatible with this defect and shows why mitosis and physical cell separation are different operations.

</div>
