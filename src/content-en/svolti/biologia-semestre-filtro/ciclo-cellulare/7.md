## Exercise 1

A species has $2n=8$. Count chromosomes, chromatids and DNA content in one cell after S, after meiosis I and after meiosis II. Assume normal divisions and always count one cell at the indicated stage.

| Stage | Chromosomes | Chromatids | DNA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| After S | 8 | 16 | $4C$ |
| After meiosis I | 4 | 8 | $2C$ |
| After meiosis II | 4 | 4 | $1C$ |

Division I separates homologues, reducing two sets to one; division II separates sisters without another S phase.

![Ploidy and DNA content before and after both meiotic divisions](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/ciclo-cellulare/meiosi-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The haploid number is four because $n$ is half of $2n=8$. After division I a cell is already haploid, but each chromosome has two chromatids. DNA content is therefore still twice that of an unreplicated gamete. Division II halves DNA again without halving the number of chromosome types. Separate columns prevent equating haploidy with one chromatid per chromosome at every stage.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For one chromosome pair, compare failure of homologue separation in meiosis I with failure of sister separation in only one of the two cells in meiosis II. All other separations are normal. Classify the four final products.

In the first case the products are **two $n+1$ and two $n-1$**: none is normal for that chromosome. In the second case there are **one $n+1$, one $n-1$ and two $n$**. The difference follows from when incorrect partitioning occurs.

<div class="spiegazione">

If both homologues enter one cell in meiosis I, the other lacks that chromosome; division II cannot recreate it. If only one division II is defective, the other cell divides correctly and produces two normal gametes. The stated proportions depend on the assumptions: another error or subsequent selection of products would change the observed result.

</div>
