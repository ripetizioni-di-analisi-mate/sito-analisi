## Exercise 1

After stress, cytochrome c appears in the cytosol. One sample lacks APAF1 and does not activate caspase 9. Locate the impaired step. Can the block be directly attributed to absent MOMP?

The impaired step is **functional apoptosome assembly** downstream of cytochrome c release. In the canonical model, APAF1 is needed to recruit and activate procaspase 9. Cytosolic cytochrome c is compatible with release having already occurred, so it does not directly support an upstream permeabilisation block.

![Intrinsic and extrinsic pathways converging on executioner caspases](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/ciclo-cellulare/apoptosi-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Locate an interruption by comparing signals before and after it: the mitochondrial component reached the cytosol, but the complex that uses it cannot assemble normally. Cytochrome c is not a caspase and does not itself cleave apoptotic substrates. Further observations are needed to describe the cell’s eventual fate: lack of active caspase 9 does not automatically mean lasting survival.

</div>

## Exercise 2

After a stimulus, many cells at time 1 are annexin-V-positive and negative for a membrane-impermeant dye; at time 2 they are positive for both. What sequence is compatible with the data, and why does time 2 alone not prove primary necrosis?

The sequence is compatible with phosphatidylserine exposure during early apoptosis, followed by loss of membrane integrity in a late phase or secondary necrosis. Double positivity at time 2 indicates both properties but **does not by itself reconstruct their order**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Markers detect properties present at measurement. The time comparison adds information, but assigning the mechanism more securely benefits from morphology, caspase activity and stimulus controls. One indicator does not distinguish every form of death. The correct interpretation is therefore “compatible with”, not certainty that every sampled cell followed the same pathway.

</div>
