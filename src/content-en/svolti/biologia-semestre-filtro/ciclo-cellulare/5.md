## Exercise 1

An intervention disrupts NDC80–microtubule binding without preventing tubulin polymerisation. Spindle poles and fibres are visible, but many chromosomes lack stable attachments. Why does the presence of a spindle not guarantee correct segregation?

Parts of the scaffold remain, but the **functional connection between microtubules and kinetochores** is disrupted. Without suitable attachments, spindle movements are not correctly transmitted to chromosomes. Unattached kinetochores can also maintain checkpoint activity and delay anaphase.

![Kinetochore, interpolar and astral microtubules in the mitotic spindle](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/ciclo-cellulare/fuso-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Visible structure and function must be assessed separately. Tubulin can form fibres even when a component linking them to their cargo is defective. NDC80 is neither the ATP-consuming motor nor centromeric DNA; it belongs to the protein machinery supporting attachment. Inspecting kinetochore attachments and orientation is therefore more informative than simply counting microtubules.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An image shows all chromosomes near the metaphase plate, but one kinetochore is still unattached. With a functional checkpoint, what is predicted for APC/C–Cdc20, and why can the overall image be misleading?

The unattached kinetochore can maintain APC/C–Cdc20 inhibition and delay sister separation. Proximity to the plate measures **position**; checkpoint signalling depends on **attachment conditions**, which an overview image may not resolve.

<div class="spiegazione">

A chromosome can occupy an apparently correct position because of earlier movements or forces exerted through other connections. It need not be far from the centre to signal incomplete attachment. The experiment therefore requires kinetochore observations and, where possible, a checkpoint indicator rather than treating alignment alone as proof of completion.

</div>
