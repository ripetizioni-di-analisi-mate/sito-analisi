## Exercise 1

An idealised 4,000-bp segment contains 20 complete nucleosome repeats. Assume 147 wrapped bp per particle. Calculate average linker length and the total number of proteins in the octamers alone.

![Nucleosome measurements](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genoma-cromatina/nucleosoma-en.svg)

$$\begin{aligned}L_{\mathrm{repeat}}&=\frac{4000}{20}=200\,\mathrm{bp}\\[6pt]L_{\mathrm{linker}}&=200-147=53\,\mathrm{bp}\\[6pt]N_{\mathrm{histones}}&=20\cdot8=160.\end{aligned}$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first division distributes total length among the 20 stated complete repeats. Each repeat includes wrapped DNA and the connecting segment, so linker length is obtained by subtracting 147 from the 200-bp average. Histone counting uses a different fact: each octamer has eight proteins regardless of linker length. H1 is not added because the question concerns octamers alone. A DNA check gives $20\cdot147+20\cdot53=4000$ bp, matching the starting length. The model avoids terminal-linker ambiguity; an experimental fragment with arbitrary ends would require more information.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Partial digestion of the same chromatin produces bands at 200, 400 and 600 bp. Extensive digestion leaves a main band at 147 bp. Interpret both experiments without proposing gene loss.

Partial digestion reveals multiples of a 200-bp repeat. Extensive digestion removes much of the linker and leaves octamer-protected DNA, about 147 bp.

<div class="spiegazione">

DNA between particles is more accessible to cutting. With incomplete digestion, some linkers remain intact, leaving fragments with one, two or three nucleosomes. The constant band interval estimates average spacing. Longer treatment cuts linkers more extensively while wrapped DNA remains relatively protected. The 147 bp therefore describe the particle core and do not contradict the 200-bp repeat. Molecules are cut in the experimental sample; this does not show that cells lose genes during normal compaction.

</div>
