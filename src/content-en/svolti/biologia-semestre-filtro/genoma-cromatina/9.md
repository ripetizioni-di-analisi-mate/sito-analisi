## Exercise 1

Element E produces RNA and a new DNA copy inserts elsewhere; the original copy remains. Starting with 7 copies, two such events occur without losses. Identify the mechanism and final number.

![Interpreting mobile elements](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genoma-cromatina/retrotrasposizione-en.svg)

The pathway is consistent with retrotransposition.
$$N_{\mathrm{final}}=7+2=9.$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The decisive information is DNA production through an RNA intermediate, requiring reverse transcription. Retaining the source and adding a new site increases copy number by one per event. Two events therefore change seven copies to nine without doubling every genomic copy. This count differs from S phase, when chromosomal DNA is replicated for division. A new insertion instead establishes a new locus occupied by the element. The question excludes losses to make the balance determinate; a real system would also require measurement of possible deletions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An Alu copy produces RNA but no new insertions when LINE-1 is inactive. Insertions appear with a functional source of LINE-1 proteins. Explain autonomy and mobility in this experiment.

Alu is non-autonomous because it does not encode the complete apparatus; its mobility can be supported by activities supplied in trans. The comparison is consistent with LINE-1 dependence.

<div class="spiegazione">

Alu transcription supplies an RNA substrate, but the pathway also requires reverse-transcription and insertion activities. When these are missing, RNA may be present without completed mobilisation. A LINE-1 source can supply missing activities and enable new insertions. Non-autonomous therefore describes what the element encodes, whereas mobile describes what can happen in a permissive context. To attribute the effect specifically to LINE-1, Alu RNA abundance, viability and supplied protein activity are controlled. The experiment does not justify treating every genomic LINE-1 copy as functional, since many are inactive.

</div>
