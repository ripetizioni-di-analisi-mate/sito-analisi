## Exercise 1

One molecule contains a CpG site methylated on both strands. Complete replication occurs without any subsequent methylation. Count total strands and strands retaining parental methylation.

![Maintenance methylation](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genoma-cromatina/metilazione-en.svg)

This exercise stops at the hemimethylated state, before the maintenance step shown in the last part of the diagram.

$$\begin{aligned}N_{\mathrm{strands}}&=2\cdot2=4\\[6pt]N_{\mathrm{methylated}}&=2\\[6pt]f&=\frac{2}{4}=\frac12.\end{aligned}$$

Both daughter molecules are hemimethylated at the site considered.

<div class="spiegazione">

Replication is semiconservative: each daughter molecule contains an old strand and a new one. The two parental strands retain their existing modification; the two new strands contain complementary bases but do not automatically receive methyl groups. The result is two hemimethylated molecules, each modified on one side. The calculated fraction concerns strands at the specified site, not the percentage of all genomic CpGs. Active maintenance could modify the new strands and restore symmetry; the question explicitly excludes it to isolate replication alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Follow all descendants of the initial molecule for three replication cycles. There is no maintenance, de novo methylation, methyl removal or molecule loss. What fraction of strands retains the initial methyl group?

$$\begin{aligned}N_{\mathrm{molecules}}&=2^3=8\\[6pt]N_{\mathrm{strands}}&=2\cdot8=16\\[6pt]f&=\frac{2}{16}=\frac18=12{,}5\%.\end{aligned}$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each cycle doubles molecule number, while the two original strands persist without producing newly methylated strands. After three cycles there are eight double helices and sixteen strands, but only two retain the initial modification. The falling fraction represents passive dilution: no methyl group needs to be removed to obtain it. Checking the first cycle gives the same rule, $\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac12$. The model follows all descendant molecules; examining one randomly chosen cell would require considering how they were distributed.

</div>
