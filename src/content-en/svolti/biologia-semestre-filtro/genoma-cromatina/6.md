## Exercise 1

In two cultures of the same type, A has little RNA and a methylated promoter; B has more RNA and a less methylated promoter. The sequence is identical. State what the data demonstrate.

The data show an association between promoter state and RNA abundance. They do not alone determine causal direction, heritable persistence or the contribution of RNA degradation.

<div class="spiegazione">

Identical sequence makes a regulatory difference plausible, but does not automatically identify its causal step. Methylation might contribute to repression or result from other changes shared with it. Measured RNA also depends on production and degradation. Causation can be investigated by targeted alteration of promoter state with specificity controls and measurement of nascent RNA. Memory can be investigated by following the state in daughter cells after stimulus removal. These are separate questions: the initial correlation provides a basis for experiments without already answering all of them.

</div>

## Exercise 2

After a deacetylase inhibitor, acetylation rises. Gene X increases its RNA, Y remains unchanged and Z decreases it. Explain why these outcomes do not contradict the biochemical effect of treatment.

Treatment changes a chromatin reaction. Each gene's RNA level also depends on its regulatory context and transcript stability, so a uniform outcome is not imposed.

<div class="spiegazione">

Inhibition reduces acetyl-group removal and can therefore raise acetylation levels. That is the measured biochemical result. For X, increased accessibility may favour expression when necessary factors are available. Y may be limited by another step and fail to respond. Z may experience indirect regulatory effects or changes in RNA stability. These are possible mechanisms to distinguish experimentally, not explanations already demonstrated for the three genes. The mistake would be treating acetylation as a sufficient and identical instruction for every promoter, ignoring the combination of conditions determining final output.

</div>
