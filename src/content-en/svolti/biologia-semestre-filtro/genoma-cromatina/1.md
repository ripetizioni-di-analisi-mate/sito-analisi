## Exercise 1

A species has $2n=10$. Consider a normal somatic cell in G1 and the same cell in G2. Determine chromosomes, DNA molecules and DNA content expressed in $C$.

![Chromosome and DNA counts](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genoma-cromatina/cromosoma-en.svg)

| Phase | Chromosomes | DNA molecules | Content |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| G1 | 10 | 10 | $2C$ |
| G2 | 10 | $10\cdot2=20$ | $4C$ |

Ploidy is $2n$ in both phases.

<div class="spiegazione">

The datum $2n=10$ specifies two sets containing five chromosomes each. In G1 each chromosome contains one double-stranded molecule. During S each molecule is replicated, so in G2 each chromosome has two sister chromatids. The molecule count therefore doubles, whereas associated sisters still constitute one duplicated chromosome. The symbol $C$ measures DNA and doubles with the molecules. Ploidy instead counts sets of homologues and remains diploid. This comparison shows why a doubled DNA signal does not by itself demonstrate a tetraploid cell. The complementary strands of each double helix are not counted as separate molecules.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The same cell enters anaphase and separates sisters normally. Calculate chromosomes in the entire cell before cytokinesis and those destined for each daughter nucleus.

$$\begin{aligned}N_{\mathrm{total}}&=10\cdot2=20\\[6pt]N_{\mathrm{daughter}}&=\frac{20}{2}=10.\end{aligned}$$

Each daughter nucleus receives 10 chromosomes and 10 DNA molecules, corresponding to $2C$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Separation changes how chromatids are grouped: each separated sister becomes an independent chromosome. The count in the still-undivided cell therefore reaches 20. Normal segregation directs half towards each pole, so each future nucleus receives 10. Total DNA is not synthesised again at anaphase; it is distributed. The balance can be checked by adding material in the two future nuclei: $10+10=20$ molecules, equal to the G2 count. Saying “the cell has 20 chromosomes” without specifying anaphase and the entire cell could incorrectly suggest that each daughter receives 20.

</div>
