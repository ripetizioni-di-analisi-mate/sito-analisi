## Exercise 1

A locus contains a tandem array of CA units. Amplified flanking regions total 80 bp. Two alleles contain 10 and 16 units, respectively. Determine expected fragment lengths.

![Repeat lengths](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genoma-cromatina/ripetizioni-en.svg)

$$\begin{aligned}L_A&=80+10\cdot2=100\,\mathrm{bp}\\[6pt]L_B&=80+16\cdot2=112\,\mathrm{bp}\\[6pt]\Delta L&=112-100=12\,\mathrm{bp}.\end{aligned}$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each CA unit occupies two consecutive bases. The variable portion therefore measures 20 bp in allele A and 32 bp in allele B. Amplification also includes 80 common bp, which must be added to obtain visible fragment length. The difference can be checked directly from repeat counts: $(16-10)\cdot2=12$ bp. Both approaches agree because flanking regions are identical. A result of 20 and 32 bp would describe only the tandem array and omit the required common portion. Different lengths identify variants of the same locus without changing cellular chromosome number.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A 6-bp sequence occurs 15 times consecutively in A and 15 times separated by other regions in B. Compare copy number, length of the units alone and arrangement.

Both contain 15 copies, totalling $15\cdot6=90$ bp for the units alone. A is a 90-bp tandem array; B contains interspersed copies, and its overall span depends on intervening sequences.

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying copy number by unit length counts only bases belonging to repeats. In A there are no interruptions, so the value equals the entire tandem length. In B other sequences intervene, so 90 bp does not describe the span from first to last copy. The comparison shows that copy number and arrangement are independent information. Calculating B's span would require intervening lengths. None of these data establishes whether the copies can transpose; mobility requires information about mechanism and available activities.

</div>
