## Exercise 1

Two samples have the same sequence and the same amount of transcription factor. In A a nucleosome covers the site; in B it is shifted and the factor binds more often. Interpret the comparison.

![Accessibility experiments](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genoma-cromatina/accessibilita-en.svg)

The data support increased site accessibility through repositioning under the stated conditions. They do not by themselves demonstrate increased production of the gene's protein.

<div class="spiegazione">

The comparison holds two alternative causes constant: sequence and factor amount. The observed difference instead concerns nucleosome position and binding frequency. When the site becomes exposed, the factor can reach it more readily; increased binding is therefore consistent with an accessibility effect. This step precedes other events required for transcription, RNA processing and translation. The conclusion cannot automatically be transferred to protein output. A subsequent experiment could measure nascent RNA and protein while controlling viability and cell number.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An active remodeler moves nucleosomes in the presence of ATP. A variant with an inactive ATPase site is present in equal amounts but does not move them. Which hypothesis is supported, and what additional control is useful?

The data support a role for ATPase activity in movement. To attribute loss of movement specifically to catalytic activity, the variant must be checked for proper folding and substrate binding.

<div class="spiegazione">

Equal abundance excludes an explanation based solely on less protein. Lack of movement in the variant is consistent with a role for ATP hydrolysis, which supplies energy for changes in DNA–histone interactions. However, a mutation can also destabilise the protein or prevent binding. If these properties remain intact, the inference about the catalytic role becomes more precise. The additional control distinguishes loss of the energy-consuming reaction from general structural dysfunction. The conclusion remains specific to the experimental conditions without asserting that every remodelling complex always produces the same movement.

</div>
