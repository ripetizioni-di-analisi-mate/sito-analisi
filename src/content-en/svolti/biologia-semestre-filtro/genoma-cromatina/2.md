## Exercise 1

Two artificial linear fragments have functional origins. A retains protected telomeres but no centromere; B retains a functional centromere but has unprotected ends. Predict the direct problem of each.

![Centromere and telomere defects](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/genoma-cromatina/regioni-cromosoma-en.svg)

| Fragment | Impaired function | Expected consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | Spindle attachment | Irregular segregation or loss |
| B | End protection | Damage response and possible fusions |

<div class="spiegazione">

Origins address DNA copying, but maintenance also requires distribution and protection. A may replicate and retain ends recognised as telomeres; however, it lacks the region where the kinetochore assembles, so it has no normal spindle attachment. B has that attachment, but its natural ends may be recognised as damage when protective interactions are missing. The comparison separates two mechanisms that can both cause genomic instability. The prediction concerns the direct defect: it does not determine the outcome of every division with certainty, because cellular responses and control systems also contribute.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cell line has a neocentromere lacking the usual alpha-satellite. CENP-A, a kinetochore and normal segregation are detected. Explain which conclusions are supported and which would overreach the data.

The region performs centromere function under the observed conditions. The usual alpha-satellite block is therefore not required for every functional centromere. The data do not show that sequence is always irrelevant.

<div class="spiegazione">

Reasoning starts from the measured function. Kinetochore formation and normal segregation support regional functionality; CENP-A is consistent with specialised centromeric chromatin. Because this function occurs without the usual repeat block, that sequence cannot be imposed as a universal requirement. However, a counterexample to a universal requirement does not imply that all sequences are equivalent or that usual centromeres lack alpha-satellite. The result shows the contribution of chromatin organisation and distinguishes a structure's frequency from its absolute necessity.

</div>
