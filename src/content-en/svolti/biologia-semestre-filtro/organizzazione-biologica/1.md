## Exercise 1

A cell takes up 6 units of a metabolite per minute and consumes 6. An inhibitor reduces consumption to 2 units per minute while leaving uptake unchanged. Calculate the balance before treatment and the accumulation during the first 5 minutes afterwards, assuming constant flows and no other exchange.

![Balance before and after treatment](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/organizzazione-biologica/worked-balance-en.svg)

The balance is the amount entering minus the amount consumed:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{before: }&6-6=0\\[6pt]
\text{after: }&6-2=4\\[6pt]
\text{accumulation in 5 minutes: }&4\cdot5=20.
\end{aligned}
$$

The internal amount is initially **stable**; after treatment it **increases by 20 units**. A separate check gives 30 units entering and 10 consumed, so again $30-10=20$.

<div class="spiegazione">

A constant amount can conceal two equal active flows. The first result therefore does not show that the substance is immobile or metabolism has stopped. When consumption decreases, uptake exceeds use and the metabolite accumulates. Only the net flow is multiplied by time, because some incoming material continues to be consumed. The calculation applies over the interval in which the stated flows remain constant; later regulation of uptake or consumption would require a new balance. Comparing treatment with control links the change to the function altered by the inhibitor.

</div>

## Exercise 2

After moderate external acidification, internal pH in living cells changes from 7.2 to 7.0 and then returns to 7.2. In cells treated with an inhibitor of ATP production, it remains at 7.0. The treatment does not destroy the membrane. Interpret the findings and identify which conclusion about the specific transporter remains unresolved.

In the control, the disturbance is followed by recovery. In the ATP-depleted sample, recovery is absent despite preservation of the membrane. The findings therefore support **a contribution from energy-dependent processes** to pH maintenance. They do not by themselves identify a single pump or exchanger.

A subsequent control could measure ion flows and transport activity while keeping viability and external conditions comparable.

<div class="spiegazione">

Homeostasis is recognised from a dynamic response rather than the complete absence of variation. The small initial shift is compatible with a system that compensates for a disturbance. Comparison with the treated sample suggests that energy availability contributes to recovery. ATP may directly support a pump or maintain gradients used by other transport processes, so the precise mechanism requires further evidence. Checking membrane integrity prevents the result from being automatically attributed to generalised lysis. This separates the supported conclusion, functional energy dependence, from the molecular identification still to be tested.

</div>
