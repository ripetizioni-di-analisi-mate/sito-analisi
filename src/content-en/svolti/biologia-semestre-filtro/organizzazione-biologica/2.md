## Exercise 1

In the illustrated tree, A and B share a more recent node than the one they share with C. Identify the most closely related groups and explain what happens if the graphical positions of A and B are exchanged by rotating the branches.

![A and B share the most recent node](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/organizzazione-biologica/phylogeny-en.svg)

Following A and B towards the root first reaches their shared node. Connecting A to C requires returning to the older node. Therefore **A and B are the most closely related groups**.

Rotation changes the arrangement of tips while preserving nodes and connections. The relationship remains unchanged.

<div class="spiegazione">

Reading the tree uses topology, meaning the way branches are connected. Two names drawn close together may belong to branches whose shared ancestors are ancient; distance on the page is not a general measure of relatedness. Left-to-right order can also change through equivalent rotations. No tip should automatically be treated as an ancestor of another tip: present-day groups share ancestors within the tree. Because the figure gives no time scale, segment lengths cannot be used to calculate years or evolutionary rates. The result concerns only the represented relationship by descent.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A culture contains 980 bacteria sensitive to antibiotic X and 20 resistant bacteria. After treatment, 49 sensitive and 10 resistant bacteria survive. Calculate the resistant frequency before and after treatment. Variants were present before exposure, and no divisions occur during the interval.

Before treatment the total is $980+20=1000$:

$$
f_{\mathrm{before}}=\frac{20}{1000}=0{,}02=2\%.
$$

After treatment the total is $49+10=59$:

$$
f_{\mathrm{after}}=\frac{10}{59}\approx0{,}1695\approx16{,}95\%.
$$

The resistant frequency **increases**, even though the absolute number falls from 20 to 10.

<div class="spiegazione">

A frequency compares a group count with the total present at the same time. Keeping 1000 as the denominator after treatment would mix two different samples. Sensitive bacteria survive in the proportion $\dfrac{49}{980}=0{,}05$, while resistant bacteria survive in the proportion $\dfrac{10}{20}=0{,}5$: differential survival changes population composition. The result is consistent with selection of pre-existing variants. Because no divisions occur, the percentage increase is not due to production of new resistant bacteria during the interval. In particular, the finding does not show that the antibiotic induced a specific mutation to adapt every cell.

</div>
