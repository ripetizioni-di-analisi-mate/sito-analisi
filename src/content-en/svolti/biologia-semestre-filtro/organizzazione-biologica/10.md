## Exercise 1

Under the stated conditions, a synthesis has a free-energy change of $+18\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$. A mechanistically linked reaction, coupled one to one, has a change of $-29\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$. Calculate the overall balance and explain which information about rate is not contained in the result.

Contributions add with their signs:

$$
\Delta G_{\mathrm{tot}}=18-29=-11\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}.
$$

The coupled process is **thermodynamically favourable** under the stated conditions. The result does not establish how many products form per second because activation barriers and kinetic conditions are not specified.

Checking the one-to-one ratio is necessary: changing coefficients would also change the balance.

<div class="spiegazione">

The negative contribution exceeds the synthesis cost in absolute value. Coupling therefore makes the overall process favourable even though the isolated synthesis would not be favourable under the same conditions. The problem specifies a mechanistic connection: without that assumption, merely placing the two reactions in the same test tube would not guarantee useful energy transfer. Catalysis concerns pathway and rate, whereas the balance compares initial and final states. Separating these pieces of information explains why a favourable conversion may remain slow or require specific enzymes to occur at a biologically relevant rate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A breakdown pathway produces 10 units of an intermediate per minute. Of these, 6 enter biosynthesis, 3 are further degraded and 1 is exported. Calculate the compartment balance and explain how the case links catabolism, anabolism and homeostasis. Assume constant flows and no other input.

The total outflow from the pool is

$$
6+3+1=10\quad\text{units per minute}.
$$

The balance is therefore

$$
10-(6+3+1)=0.
$$

The amount of intermediate **remains constant** despite continuous production and use. Initial breakdown supplies both a biosynthetic pathway and further catabolic processes and export.

<div class="spiegazione">

The pool represents the available amount of metabolite in the compartment considered. A stable concentration does not identify inactive metabolism: a zero balance can result from substantial, compensating flows. The intermediate links a breakdown pathway to a building pathway, showing that catabolism and anabolism share resources within a network. Export must be included because it removes molecules from the compartment even without degrading them. If biosynthesis slowed without compensation, the pool would grow; maintaining stability would require a change in at least one other flow. The reasoning therefore connects pathway function with control of cellular amounts.

</div>
