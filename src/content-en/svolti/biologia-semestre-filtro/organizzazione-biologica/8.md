## Exercise 1

In a schematic energy profile, reactants have free energy 20 and products 5, in kJ/mol. The transition state is at 75 without enzyme and at 40 with enzyme. Calculate the free-energy change and forward barriers for the two pathways.

![Assigned energies of the two pathways](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/organizzazione-biologica/worked-energy-en.svg)

Subtract reactant energy from product energy for the balance, and from transition-state energy for the barrier:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta G&=5-20=-15\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\\[6pt]
\Delta G^{\ddagger}_{\mathrm{without}}&=75-20=55\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\\[6pt]
\Delta G^{\ddagger}_{\mathrm{with}}&=40-20=20\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Catalysis **reduces the barrier by 35 kJ/mol** and preserves the balance of $-15\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The barrier is measured from the starting level to the pathway maximum; using 75 or 40 directly would confuse an energy level with a difference. The balance instead depends only on initial and final states, which remain identical in both pathways. A lower barrier can increase rate, while negative $\Delta G$ describes thermodynamic favourability under the stated conditions. As a check, reverse barriers are $75-5=70$ and $40-5=35\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$: the reverse-direction reduction is also 35. This is consistent with a catalyst that does not shift equilibrium.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Protein A changes from activity 40 to 100 units after phosphorylation; protein B changes from 80 to 20. A phosphatase restores 40 and 80, respectively. Calculate the relative changes after phosphorylation and interpret the phosphatase control.

For A, the change relative to the initial value is

$$
\frac{100-40}{40}\cdot100\%=150\%.
$$

For B, it is

$$
\frac{20-80}{80}\cdot100\%=-75\%.
$$

Phosphorylation **increases A activity by 150%** and **reduces B activity by 75%**. Restoration after dephosphorylation is consistent with reversible regulation.

<div class="spiegazione">

The same chemical modification can produce opposite functional effects in different proteins. Phosphate changes interactions and conformation in the specific context of the site; the reaction’s name alone therefore does not determine activation or inhibition. The phosphatase control links recovery of activity to removal of the modification, provided other factors remain comparable. For percentage calculations, the difference is divided by each protein’s own initial value: one shared denominator would mix two references. The 150% is A’s relative increase; its final value is 250% of the initial value.

</div>
