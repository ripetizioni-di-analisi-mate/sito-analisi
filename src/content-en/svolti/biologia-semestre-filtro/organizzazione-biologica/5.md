## Exercise 1

A mitochondrial protein is encoded in the nucleus. A treatment leaves its cytosolic synthesis unchanged but prevents import into mitochondria. Predict where the precursor initially accumulates and explain how the result relates to endosymbiotic history.

![Mitochondrial dependence on nuclear-encoded proteins](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/organizzazione-biologica/endosymbiosis-en.svg)

The gene is expressed and the precursor is still produced on cytosolic ribosomes. Because entry into the organelle is blocked, the precursor may **accumulate in the cytosol** unless rapidly degraded. The functional amount inside mitochondria may decrease.

The finding is consistent with evolutionary gene transfer to the nucleus and import of their products.

<div class="spiegazione">

A protein’s final location and the site of its synthesis can differ. The bacterial origin of mitochondria does not mean that all present-day proteins are produced from the mitochondrial genome. During evolution, many genes moved to the nucleus or were replaced by nuclear functions, while the organelle became strongly dependent on protein transport. Interpreting accumulation also requires considering degradation and experiment duration. The degradation qualification explains why an import block does not guarantee a large measurable precursor pool under every condition.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two cell types from the same individual retain gene G. Type A has abundant G mRNA and corresponding protein; type B has little of either. No differences in G sequence have been detected. Formulate a compatible explanation and one limit to the conclusion.

The findings are consistent with **different regulation of G expression** in the two cell types. Reduced mRNA production or faster degradation may contribute to its low abundance in B.

Measuring amounts alone does not distinguish transcription rate from RNA stability. They can be separated by measuring newly produced RNA and its disappearance over time.

<div class="spiegazione">

Differentiation allows cells with largely shared genetic information to produce different molecular repertoires. Absence of a gene-sequence difference points towards regulation without proving a single mechanism. A low mRNA level reflects the balance between production and degradation; protein abundance also depends on synthesis and turnover. The strongest conclusion therefore concerns different expression, while identifying its cause requires dynamic data. This distinction prepares the study of transcriptional and post-transcriptional control without confusing possession of a gene with the amount of its product.

</div>
