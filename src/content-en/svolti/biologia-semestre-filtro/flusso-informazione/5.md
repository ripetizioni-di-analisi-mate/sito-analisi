## Exercise 1

A pre-mRNA has exons of 180, 120 and 240 nucleotides and introns of 700 and 500 nucleotides. In mature RNA, the 5′ UTR is 60 nucleotides long and the 3′ UTR is 120. All exons are included; the coding region contains the initial AUG and final stop. Calculate precursor length, exonic mRNA length and amino acids incorporated. Exclude cap and poly-A.

![Diagram of the data and reasoning](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/flusso-informazione/splicing-en.svg)

$$\begin{aligned}L_{\mathrm{pre}}&=180+120+240+700+500=1740,\\[4pt]L_{\mathrm{m}}&=180+120+240=540,\\[4pt]L_{\mathrm{c}}&=540-60-120=360.\end{aligned}$$

Lengths are in nucleotides. There are $\dfrac{360}{3}=120$ codons; one is a stop. The chain therefore contains **119 amino acids**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The precursor includes exons and introns, whereas the exonic length of mature RNA includes only what remains after splicing. UTRs are retained messenger regions and are subtracted only when moving to the translated region. Finally, the stop codon occupies three nucleotides without adding an amino acid. The start codon does incorporate methionine and remains in the count. As a check, 119 sense codons occupy 357 nucleotides; adding the three stop nucleotides recovers the 360-nucleotide coding region.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the preceding exercise’s gene, the central 120-nucleotide exon is entirely coding. An isoform skips it, retains the same UTRs and preserves the frame without new stops. Calculate the isoform’s exonic length and amino acids incorporated; compare with the full isoform.

$$L_{\mathrm{i}}=540-120=420\,\mathrm{nt}.$$

The remaining coding region measures $360-120=240$ nucleotides, including start and stop.

$$n_{\mathrm{aa}}=\frac{240}{3}-1=79.$$

The difference is $119-79=40$ amino acids, equal to $\dfrac{120}{3}$. **The downstream frame remains preserved.**

<div class="spiegazione">

The skipped exon contributes entirely to the coding region, so the same 120 nucleotides are subtracted from both exonic and coding lengths. The UTRs remain unchanged and must not be subtracted again from the difference between proteins. The frame assumption is decisive: it allows the change to be interpreted as loss of forty amino acids without rewriting all subsequent codons. The protein may nevertheless change activity or structure if the removed segment contributed to a functional domain; counting alone does not establish the biological consequence.

</div>
