## Exercise 1

A 360 kb linear DNA segment has one active origin at its centre. Both forks start together at 3 kb/s each. Calculate completion time, ignoring pauses and assembly times.

The distance travelled by each fork is

$$d=\frac{360\,\mathrm{kb}}{2}=180\,\mathrm{kb}.$$

Their common time is

$$t=\frac{d}{v}=\frac{180\,\mathrm{kb}}{3\,\mathrm{kb/s}}=60\,\mathrm{s}.$$

Copying therefore ends after **60 seconds**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The central origin divides the segment into equal halves. Because the forks start together and have the same speed, they reach the ends at the same instant. The required time is the time for either path, while total work includes both halves. As a check, their combined advance is 6 kb/s: in 60 seconds, the forks cover 360 kb together. This check applies under the stated assumptions and excludes possible activation delays or replication stalls.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A 180 kb linear segment has two origins at positions 30 kb and 150 kb, measured from the left end. All forks start together and advance at 1 kb/s. When does replication finish? Compare with one central origin at the same speed.

The outer forks each travel 30 kb, reaching the ends after 30 s. Between the origins, $150-30=120\,\mathrm{kb}$ is covered by two converging forks.

$$t_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{120\,\mathrm{kb}}{2\cdot1\,\mathrm{kb/s}}=60\,\mathrm{s}.$$

Completion requires the longest time, hence **60 s**. With one central origin:

$$t_{\mathrm{1}}=\frac{90\,\mathrm{kb}}{1\,\mathrm{kb/s}}=90\,\mathrm{s}.$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The four forks do not all remain active throughout the process. The outer ones reach the ends first; from then on, only internal regions remain to be copied. Simply dividing 180 kb by the sum of all four initial speeds would give too short a time. The region between origins is the last to finish. The internal forks meet at position 90 kb because each travels 60 kb. This position lies between the origins and confirms the calculation’s symmetry.

</div>
