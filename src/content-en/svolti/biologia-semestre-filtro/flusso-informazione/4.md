## Exercise 1

In a bacterium with an intact lac system, three media are compared: A contains lactose and abundant glucose; B contains lactose and little glucose; C contains little glucose but no inducer. Compare transcription qualitatively in the classical model, explaining both controls.

![Diagram of the data and reasoning](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/flusso-informazione/lac-en.svg)

In A, allolactose reduces repression, but CAP-cAMP activation is weak: transcription remains **low**.

In B, active CAP-cAMP accompanies relief of repression: transcription is **high**.

In C, CAP-cAMP can be active, but LacI remains bound to the operator: transcription is **minimal**, allowing for the system’s basal level.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each medium is analysed by first asking whether LacI can occupy the operator and then whether CAP-cAMP supplies positive activation. Lactose availability affects the first control through allolactose; glucose availability affects the second. This order avoids treating the nutrients as interchangeable signals. The qualitative description does not assign invented percentages to expression levels: it distinguishes the fully induced system from one lacking strong activation and one that remains repressed. The conditions correspond to the figure’s rows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cell contains two copies of the lac system and functional diffusible LacI. Copy A’s operator does not bind LacI; copy B’s is normal. Glucose is scarce. Predict control of both copies before and after inducer addition, assuming intact promoters and CAP sites.

Before inducer addition, A escapes repression because its operator does not bind LacI; with active CAP-cAMP it can be strongly transcribed. B remains repressed by LacI.

After addition, the inducer reduces LacI binding to B’s normal operator. B can also reach high expression. A remains free of that negative control.

The operator defect acts **in cis**, whereas LacI acts **in trans**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Sharing the same cytoplasmic repressor does not make the two operators identical. LacI can diffuse towards both but binds only sequences it recognises. Normal repressor availability does not repair copy A because its defect lies in target DNA. Inducer changes LacI protein behaviour and therefore affects B’s control. This comparison distinguishes an operator mutation from loss of LacI production, which could instead be complemented by a functional lacI gene copy.

</div>
