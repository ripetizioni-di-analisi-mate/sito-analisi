## Exercise 1

Two samples have identical coding DNA. In sample B, a 60-nucleotide coding exon is absent from mRNA; the junctions preserve the frame and the same start and stop codons. Protein A incorporates 210 amino acids. Derive the predicted length for B and identify the regulatory level directly explaining the data.

The removed exon contains

$$\frac{60}{3}=20.$$

Because start and stop remain the same, the predicted chain has

$$n_B=210-20=190\,\mathrm{aa}.$$

The directly altered step is **post-transcriptional processing through alternative splicing**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Comparing DNA and RNA places the change after precursor transcription: the segment exists in both DNA samples but is retained differently in mature products. Absence of a frameshift allows twenty residues to be subtracted without changing downstream codon counts. Later proteolysis could also shorten a protein, but it would not by itself explain the specific exon junction observed in mRNA. Analysing several molecular levels therefore gives a more precise interpretation than comparing protein sizes alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mutant protein is synthesised at the same rate as the normal protein but aggregates and loses activity. Increasing a chaperone reduces aggregates and partially restores activity without changing DNA or mRNA. Explain the improved step and why recovery does not demonstrate correction of the mutation.

![Diagram of the data and reasoning](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/flusso-informazione/proteostasi-en.svg)

The treatment improves **folding management**: it limits inappropriate interactions and increases the fraction of molecules reaching a functional structure.

The coding sequence remains mutated, and the primary sequence can remain that of the variant. Recovery is therefore functional and partial; it is not evidence of DNA repair.

<div class="spiegazione">

Unchanged synthesis rules out explaining the result simply by producing more normal protein. Fewer aggregates together with increased activity are consistent with a changed distribution between non-functional and functional conformations. Chaperones assist physical folding processes and may consume ATP, but they do not use a template to rewrite amino acid sequence. Partial recovery is compatible with a variant that remains less stable than the normal protein. Unrecovered molecules may be directed to degradation systems, as shown in the figure.

</div>
