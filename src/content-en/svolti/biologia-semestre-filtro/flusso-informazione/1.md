## Exercise 1

A fork moves to the right. The upper template strand runs from 3′ on the left to 5′ on the right. Determine the direction of the new chain on this template, whether it is leading or lagging, and which end is extended.

![Diagram of the data and reasoning](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/flusso-informazione/forcella-en.svg)

Polymerase can travel along the upper template from left to right, reading it 3′ → 5′. The new chain is antiparallel, so its 5′ end is on the left and its 3′ end on the right.

The growing 3′ end moves in the same direction as the fork. The chain is therefore **leading** and extends at its **3′-OH**. On the lower template, synthesis instead requires fragments oriented opposite to fork movement.

<div class="spiegazione">

The starting point is the polymerase’s chemical rule: each nucleotide is added to the new chain’s 3′ end. This rule, together with antiparallel orientation, determines synthesis direction. Only then is that direction compared with fork movement. The upper position in the diagram does not by itself identify the leading strand: if the fork moved leftwards, the same template’s relative role would change. As a check, every lower fragment in the diagram also grows from its own 5′ towards its own 3′, even though it points leftwards.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The template strand of a short segment is written 5′-TCACAT-3′. First derive the transcript in the 5′ → 3′ direction and then the coding DNA strand in the same direction. There are no introns or editing.

Paired to the supplied template, the RNA sequence is 3′-AGUGUA-5′. To express it in the required direction, read from the opposite end:

**RNA: 5′-AUGUGA-3′.**

The coding strand matches the RNA in the 5′ → 3′ direction, with T replacing U:

**Coding DNA: 5′-ATGTGA-3′.**

<div class="spiegazione">

The template was supplied 5′ → 3′, whereas polymerase travels along it in the opposite direction. Complementarity alone therefore initially produces RNA written 3′ → 5′, requiring reversal of the written order. The final sequence can be checked by pairing it back to the template: each A matches T, each U matches A and each G matches C. The coding strand is obtained by retaining transcript order and changing only U to T. The procedure separates the orientation problem from the different base used in DNA and RNA.

</div>
