## Exercise 1

A double helix has both strands labelled with a heavy isotope. It is replicated twice in a light medium. Assuming semiconservative replication without degradation or recombination, count hybrid and entirely light double helices.

![Diagram of the data and reasoning](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/flusso-informazione/semiconservativa-en.svg)

After the first round, two hybrid molecules are obtained. In the second round, each produces one hybrid retaining the heavy strand and one entirely light molecule.

$$N=2^2=4,\qquad N_{\mathrm{H}}=2,\qquad N_{\mathrm{L}}=2.$$

The fractions are therefore $\dfrac{2}{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}$ for both classes. **No double helix remains entirely heavy.**

<div class="spiegazione">

Conservation concerns individual parental strands. In the first round, the two heavy strands separate, and each is paired with a new light strand. In the second round, each first-round light strand becomes a template for another light chain. There are still only two original heavy strands, while the total molecule number increases. The count can be checked by following each initial chain separately in the diagram, without assuming that label becomes uniformly distributed across all new molecules.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the same model, eight double helices are observed after three rounds. Separately calculate the fraction of molecules containing at least one heavy strand and the fraction of heavy strands among all strands.

The two initial heavy strands are in two separate molecules.

$$f_{\mathrm{m}}=\frac{2}{8}=\frac{1}{4}.$$

Eight double helices contain sixteen strands altogether:

$$f_{\mathrm{s}}=\frac{2}{2\cdot8}=\frac{1}{8}.$$

The two fractions differ because their denominators differ.

<div class="spiegazione">

A hybrid molecule contains two strands, but the heavy one is enough for the entire molecule to enter the first category. In the second count, each strand is considered separately, so the light strands in hybrids also enter the denominator. This explains why the percentage of labelled molecules and the percentage of labelled DNA are not synonymous. As a check, the fourteen light strands include two paired to heavy strands and twelve in the six entirely light molecules; adding the two heavy strands gives sixteen.

</div>
