## Exercise 1

A muscle cell contains 2400 total GLUT4 transporters. Before insulin, 300 are at the surface; after signalling, 900. The total stays unchanged. Calculate the surface fractions, relative increase in exposed number, and internal transporter numbers at both times.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_0&=\frac{300}{2400}=12{,}5\%,\\[5pt]
f_1&=\frac{900}{2400}=37{,}5\%,\\[5pt]
\text{relative increase}&=\frac{900-300}{300}\cdot100\%=200\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

Internal transporters change from $2400-300=2100$ to $2400-900=1500$. The surface fraction increases by 25 percentage points.

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator depends on the question. The surface fraction uses the total of 2400; the relative increase uses the initial exposed value of 300. Surface number triples, so the final value is 300% of the initial value while the increase is 200%. Conservation of the total and loss from the internal pool are consistent with redistribution by vesicular trafficking. These data do not directly measure glucose-entry rate, which also depends on the gradient and transporter function. Distinguishing location from flux avoids automatically equating the two increases.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two interventions block the insulin response. X prevents PI3K recruitment; Y prevents GLUT4 vesicles from fusing with the membrane. Akt active independently of PIP3 restores GLUT4 exposure after X but not after Y. Reconstruct the functional order and the meaning of failed rescue.

The consistent model is **receptor/IRS → PI3K/PIP3 → Akt → trafficking machinery → GLUT4 exposure**. Active Akt bypasses X, which acts upstream. Y affects the downstream execution step, so the Akt command is insufficient to achieve vesicle fusion.

<div class="spiegazione">

Rescue by Akt distinguishes signal generation from execution of the response. In sample X, trafficking components remain usable and can receive the command introduced downstream of the defect. In sample Y, a function required to physically bring the transporter to the surface is missing. The conclusion requires Akt to be genuinely active in both samples and Y not to directly inhibit its action. It is also limited to the measured branch: other insulin effects, including Ras-MAPK-mediated effects, may respond differently to the same interventions.

</div>
