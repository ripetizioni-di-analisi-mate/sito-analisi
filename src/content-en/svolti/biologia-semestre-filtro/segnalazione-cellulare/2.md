## Exercise 1

Two phenomena are measured in a continuous epithelium: extracellular tracer passage between cells, and transfer of a small dye injected into one cytoplasm to adjacent cells. Treatment X increases only the first; treatment Y reduces only the second. Identify the structures most directly involved.

![Barriers and junctions](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/segnalazione-cellulare/giunzioni-en.svg)

| Altered measurement | Candidate structure |
|---|---|
| X: increased paracellular passage | Tight junctions |
| Y: reduced cytoplasmic transfer | Gap junctions |

The results distinguish an **extracellular barrier** from **direct communication between cytoplasms**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The extracellular tracer travels through the space between cells. Tight junctions regulate this route, and their alteration can increase permeability. The injected dye instead starts in the cytosol: if its size is compatible with the channels, it can pass through paired connexons. Interpreting the measurements requires excluding cell death, membrane rupture and epithelial discontinuities, which would create alternative routes. The selectivity of the treatments supports the proposed assignment but does not alone identify a particular mutated protein. Analysis of claudins or connexins would help specify the molecular target.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tissue contains two adhesion complexes. Complex A joins adjacent cells through cadherins and intermediate filaments. Complex B connects intermediate filaments to laminin through integrins. Classify them and predict which is directly more sensitive to loss of its cadherin.

A connects one cell to another and uses specialised cadherins: it is a **desmosome**. B connects a cell to the basal lamina through integrins: it is a **hemidesmosome**. Loss of A’s cadherin directly impairs the desmosome; B does not use that cadherin as its matrix receptor.

<div class="spiegazione">

The intermediate filament alone does not distinguish the structures because it occurs in both. This information is therefore combined with the identity of the external partner and adhesion receptor. An adjacent cell and cadherins identify A; laminin and integrins identify B. The defect prediction follows the component actually removed. An intermediate-filament alteration could instead affect both systems. In real tissues, initially selective damage may cause secondary effects on overall organisation: the requested answer concerns the direct effect, keeping these later consequences separate.

</div>
