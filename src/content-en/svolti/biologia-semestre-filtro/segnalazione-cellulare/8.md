## Exercise 1

A rod changes from $-38\,\mathrm{mV}$ in darkness to $-63\,\mathrm{mV}$ after a flash. Calculate the potential change and reconstruct the signs of the steps involving rhodopsin, PDE6, cGMP, cation channels and glutamate release.

![Phototransduction and adaptation](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/segnalazione-cellulare/visione-en.svg)

$$
\Delta V=V_{\text{final}}-V_{\text{initial}}
=-63-(-38)=-25\,\mathrm{mV}.
$$

Rhodopsin activates transducin; PDE6 activity increases; cGMP decreases; cation-channel opening decreases. The potential becomes more negative and glutamate release **decreases**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the initial value retains its negative sign, giving a change of minus 25 mV. This is hyperpolarisation. PDE6 activation does not increase the second messenger, because the enzyme degrades it. Channel closure reduces the inward cation current while potassium efflux continues: the current balance shifts the potential towards more negative values. The terminal releases less glutamate. Light is therefore represented by reduced release relative to darkness, without requiring the rod to generate an action potential.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A surface GPCR decreases from 200 to 80 units after prolonged ligand exposure. The cellular total remains 200. After ligand removal, the surface level returns to 160 while the total remains unchanged. Calculate the surface fractions at the three times and propose the simplest receptor fate compatible with the data.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_0&=\frac{200}{200}=100\%,\\[5pt]
f_1&=\frac{80}{200}=40\%,\\[5pt]
f_2&=\frac{160}{200}=80\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

The data are consistent with **internalisation followed by partial recycling**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant total suggests redistribution between the surface and internal compartments. After stimulation, 120 units are no longer exposed; during recovery, 80 return, leaving 40 internal. The finding is consistent with recycling, although localisation measurements and tracking the same molecules would strengthen the conclusion. Receptor number and ability to couple to G protein are also separate properties: return to the surface does not alone establish full recovery of responsiveness. A functional assay should therefore be compared with the receptor count.

</div>
