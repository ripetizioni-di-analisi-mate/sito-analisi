## Exercise 1

Cell A releases a factor into the medium. Collected medium activates a response in cells B even without contact with A. An antibody neutralising the factor eliminates the response. Cells B lacking the receptor do not respond. Reconstruct the steps supported by the data and state whether the experiment alone establishes endocrine signalling.

The supported model is A → diffusible factor → receptor on B → response. Physical contact is unnecessary under the observed conditions. The experiment **does not establish endocrine signalling**, because transport through the circulation in an organism has not been demonstrated.

<div class="spiegazione">

Transferring the medium separates the factor’s presence from the producing cell’s presence. Neutralisation supports a requirement for that factor in the measured response. Receptor removal adds the recognition step in the target cell. These controls describe communication through a diffusible substance; they do not automatically define its distance or transport route in tissue. A paracrine factor can also work in a conditioned-medium experiment. Physiological classification requires information about the relationship between source, target and circulation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For simple equilibrium binding, the occupied receptor fraction is $f=\dfrac{[L]}{K_d+[L]}$. A receptor has $K_d=4\,\mathrm{nM}$. Calculate occupancy at $[L]=4\,\mathrm{nM}$ and $[L]=12\,\mathrm{nM}$; explain why tripling ligand does not triple the occupied fraction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{4}{4+4}=\frac12=50\%,\\[5pt]
f_2&=\frac{12}{4+12}=\frac34=75\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

The occupied fraction increases by 25 percentage points. The finite number of available sites imposes **saturation**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Ligand concentration and dissociation constant use the same unit, which cancels in the ratio. Increasing ligand also increases the denominator: the model is not direct proportionality. At high concentrations, few sites remain free and the fraction approaches one. The calculation concerns occupancy of independent sites in the given model; a cellular response may depend on amplification, receptor reserve and downstream controls. A change from 50% to 75% occupancy therefore does not by itself demonstrate the same percentage increase in biological response.

</div>
