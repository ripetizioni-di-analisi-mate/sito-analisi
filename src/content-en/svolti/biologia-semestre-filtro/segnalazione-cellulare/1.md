## Exercise 1

Compare two cartilage samples of equal volume. Sample A has an intact matrix; sample B selectively loses proteoglycans, while collagen amount and orientation initially remain equal. Sample B retains less water. Predict which mechanical function is directly impaired and identify a necessary control.

![Matrix and anchoring](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/segnalazione-cellulare/matrice-en.svg)

1. Loss of proteoglycans reduces the charged chains that favour water retention.
2. The matrix gel becomes less hydrated.
3. Resistance to compression may therefore decrease.

The expected result is **reduced ability to resist compression**. A control compares responses to the same applied force with sample geometry held equal.

<div class="spiegazione">

The causal connection begins with the modified component. Glycosaminoglycan charges attract cations and water, contributing to the gel properties. Because collagen is initially preserved, the finding does not establish an equivalent loss of tensile strength. Comparing compression requires comparable mechanical conditions: samples with different geometries may deform differently even at equal composition. Cell viability and other matrix changes must also be controlled if the experiment continues over time. The prediction therefore concerns the stated direct effect, without assigning every possible tissue alteration to the treatment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Normal cells and cells lacking a functional talin-actin connection adhere to surfaces with equal fibronectin. Integrin number and ligand binding are equal. Mean force transmitted to the substrate falls from 12 to 3 arbitrary units. Calculate the relative reduction and interpret the defect.

![Matrix and anchoring](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/segnalazione-cellulare/adesione-en.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta F &= 12-3=9,\\[5pt]
\text{reduction} &= \frac{9}{12}\cdot100\%=75\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

$25\%$ of the initial force remains. The finding is consistent with a **defect in the intracellular mechanical connection**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The reduction is expressed relative to the normal value, so the denominator is 12. The value 3 describes the remaining force, not the loss. Controlled fibronectin binding shows that extracellular recognition remains possible. Force transmission, however, requires a connection between integrins and the cytoskeleton: disrupting talin-actin can interrupt it without changing receptor number. The data do not establish complete loss of adhesion, since some force remains measurable. Restoring functional talin and checking viability would also help attribute the result to the studied connection.

</div>
