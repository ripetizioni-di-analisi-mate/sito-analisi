## Exercise 1

An NO donor increases cGMP in a smooth-muscle cell. A soluble-guanylyl-cyclase inhibitor eliminates this increase; introducing an effective cGMP analogue restores the final response. Reconstruct the order of the steps and explain the role of rescue.

The consistent sequence is **NO → soluble guanylyl cyclase → cGMP → effectors → response**. The inhibitor prevents messenger production. The analogue acts downstream of that block and shows that the final machinery can still function under the experimental conditions.

<div class="spiegazione">

The first comparison links NO to the cyclic-nucleotide increase. Selective inhibition identifies a step required for that increase. Rescue by the analogue separates failed production from general damage to effectors: if all effectors were inactive, providing the messenger would not suffice. The conclusion requires the analogue to reach the correct compartment and activate the same studied targets. It does not establish that the compound reproduces every NO function, because NO can also participate in other reactions in different contexts.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Response X appears within seconds after nicotinic-receptor opening. Response Y follows a steroid hormone and requires a new protein. A translation inhibitor does not change the initial receptor number or existing proteins. Predict which response is directly prevented and explain why timing alone does not identify a receptor.

Y requires the new protein and is therefore directly prevented. X can begin through the existing channel and available ion gradients. Timing is consistent with these mechanisms, but identifying them requires knowing the **ligand target and necessary steps**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The decisive point is the stated dependence on new synthesis, not simply the hormone’s category. The inhibitor acts on translation and does not immediately destroy existing proteins. A response using a functional channel can therefore begin in the short term. Over time, synthesis inhibition could have other consequences, excluded from this initial comparison. Moreover, some pathways with intracellular intermediates are rapid, and some receptors produce several types of effect. Timing provides a clue, whereas selective interventions on components distinguish the mechanism.

</div>
