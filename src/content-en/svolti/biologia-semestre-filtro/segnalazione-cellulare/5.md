## Exercise 1

In an amplification model, each of 6 active receptors activates 4 enzymes, and each enzyme produces 25 messenger molecules during the observation period. There is no saturation or degradation. Calculate the total and the ratio of messengers produced to active receptors.

![Amplification and cAMP](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/segnalazione-cellulare/cascata-en.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\text{enzymes}}&=6\cdot4=24,\\[5pt]
N_{\text{messengers}}&=24\cdot25=600,\\[5pt]
A&=\frac{600}{6}=100.
\end{aligned}
$$

**600 messengers** are produced, equivalent to 100 per active receptor.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each receptor activates several enzymes, and each enzyme produces several messengers. The factors multiply because the second yield applies to every enzyme obtained in the first step. The final ratio quantifies amplification during the specified time without being a universal receptor constant. Substrate saturation, enzyme inactivation and messenger degradation would reduce the total relative to this model. The biological response need not equal messenger number: subsequent steps may also have thresholds or saturation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cell initially contains $50\,\mathrm{pmol}$ of cAMP. For 3 minutes, synthesis remains $20\,\mathrm{pmol/min}$ and removal $8\,\mathrm{pmol/min}$. Calculate the final amount. Repeat if an inhibitor reduces removal to $2\,\mathrm{pmol/min}$ without changing synthesis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\text{control}}&=50+(20-8)\cdot3=86\,\mathrm{pmol},\\[5pt]
Q_{\text{inhibitor}}&=50+(20-2)\cdot3=104\,\mathrm{pmol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The inhibitor produces an additional 18 pmol in the model because it **reduces removal**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The final amount includes the initial value plus the net change over time. Removal is subtracted from production before multiplying by minutes. The model drug does not increase adenylyl-cyclase rate: greater accumulation results from reduced cAMP loss. In real systems, rates may vary with concentrations and feedback; here they are specified as constant to make the balance calculable. A cAMP measurement alone would not distinguish increased synthesis from reduced degradation without controls on both contributions.

</div>
