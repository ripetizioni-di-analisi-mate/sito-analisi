## Exercise 1

A receptor activates phospholipase C. In sample A the enzyme is blocked; in sample B it is normal, but the IP3-regulated reticular channel is inactive. Neither shows a cytosolic calcium rise. Predict whether direct introduction of IP3 into the cytosol restores the response, assuming an intact calcium store.

![Phosphoinositides and calcium](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/segnalazione-cellulare/plc-en.svg)

| Sample | Directly introduced IP3 |
|---|---|
| A: phospholipase C blocked | Can restore release through functional IP3 channels. |
| B: inactive IP3 channel | Does not restore release through that channel. |

Rescue distinguishes a defect in **messenger production** from a defect in its **target**.

<div class="spiegazione">

IP3 lies between phospholipase C and the reticular channel. Supplying it independently of the pathway bypasses the production step, provided downstream components function. Sample A meets these requirements; sample B instead lacks the target that must convert IP3 presence into channel opening. The calcium store is specified as intact to exclude another cause of failed release. The result does not show that every pathway capable of raising calcium is blocked in sample B: it concerns the IP3-channel-mediated route.

</div>

## Exercise 2

After an identical pulse, two cells reach $600\,\mathrm{nM}$ cytosolic calcium. After one minute, A is at $120\,\mathrm{nM}$ and B at $360\,\mathrm{nM}$. Baseline is $100\,\mathrm{nM}$ and there is no new release. Calculate the percentage of the initial excess removed by each cell and interpret recovery.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_0&=600-100=500\,\mathrm{nM},\\[5pt]
E_A&=120-100=20\,\mathrm{nM},\\[5pt]
E_B&=360-100=260\,\mathrm{nM}.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r_A&=\frac{500-20}{500}\cdot100\%=96\%,\\[5pt]
r_B&=\frac{500-260}{500}\cdot100\%=48\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

Cell A removes a larger fraction of the excess during the observation period.

<div class="spiegazione">

The signal to remove is the excess above baseline, so 100 nM is subtracted before calculating the percentage. Using 600 nM as the denominator would mix baseline calcium with the transient increase. A recovers faster during the studied minute; B may have reduced uptake, extrusion or buffering capacity. The two measurements do not by themselves identify the altered transporter. Attributing the defect to SERCA, for example, would require a selective intervention or measurement of reticular refilling. The absence of new release makes the recovery difference easier to interpret.

</div>
