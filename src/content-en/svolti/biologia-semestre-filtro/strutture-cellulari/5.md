## Exercise 1

A labelled protein with a mitochondrial presequence is incubated with intact mitochondria. After import, an external protease does not degrade it; adding detergent allows degradation. If the inner potential is dissipated first, the precursor remains protease-accessible. Interpret the comparison without automatically assigning the cargo to the matrix.

Protection depending on membrane integrity supports entry into a protected compartment. Detergent exposes cargo and confirms that the protein can be degraded. Loss of protection after potential dissipation indicates that the gradient is required for efficient import in the assay.

<div class="spiegazione">

The detergent control distinguishes physical protection from intrinsic protease resistance. An intact outer membrane can nevertheless protect proteins in the intermembrane space as well as proteins further inside. Confident assignment to the matrix requires further fractionation or selective permeabilization. The presequence and potential dependence are consistent with the TOM-TIM23 pathway, but the experiment primarily measures accessibility. Cytosolic and matrix chaperones support precursor passage; a signal sequence alone does not remove the energetic requirements of import.

Selective outer-membrane permeabilization, accompanied by compartment markers, would expose intermembrane cargo while matrix cargo remained protected. This second comparison would provide the localization information missing from the first assay.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cytosolic reporter becomes peroxisomal after SKL is added at its carboxy terminus. Adding a tail after SKL makes it predominantly cytosolic again. Amount and folding remain comparable. Explain the result and predict the effect of PEX5 inactivation on the first construct.

The first construct presents an accessible PTS1 at the required terminus. In the second, SKL becomes internal and recognition decreases. Inactivating PEX5 should reduce import of the first reporter even though its signal remains.

<div class="spiegazione">

The comparison shows that sequence position matters as well as its presence. PEX5 recognizes many PTS1 signals and connects cargo to peroxisomal machinery; losing the receptor can therefore resemble losing signal accessibility. The prediction concerns a reporter using this pathway, without excluding other peroxisomal routes. Peroxisomes can import folded proteins, so reporter stability is compatible with transport. The system also requires peroxins and energetically supported receptor recycling: SKL does not cross a membrane on its own.

A PTS2 cargo can provide a comparison for assessing specificity of the observed defect. The result must be interpreted in the chosen experimental system because peroxisomal pathways can share accessory components while recognizing different signals.

</div>
