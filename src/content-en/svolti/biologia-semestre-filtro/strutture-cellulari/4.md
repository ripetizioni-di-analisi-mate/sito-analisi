## Exercise 1

For a teaching balance of complete glucose oxidation, assume ten NADH equivalents with a mean yield of $2.5$ ATP, two FADH₂ with a yield of $1.5$ ATP, and four substrate-level ATP equivalents. Calculate the yield. Recalculate it if two cytosolic equivalents yield $1.5$ instead of $2.5$ ATP each.

In the first model:

$$10\cdot2.5+2\cdot1.5+4=25+3+4=32.$$

In the second model:

$$8\cdot2.5+2\cdot1.5+2\cdot1.5+4=30.$$

The difference is two ATP equivalents per glucose.

![Energy yields and uncoupling](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/strutture-cellulari/mitocondrio-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The balance separates oxidative phosphorylation from substrate-level production. In the second case only the contribution of two cytosolic reducing equivalents changes: each yields one fewer ATP equivalent. The calculation illustrates how different shuttles can produce estimates of thirty or thirty-two while oxidizing the same glucose. Assigned yields are model averages; transport, proton leaks and cellular conditions can change the actual value. Lactate synthesis would not add thirty ATP to glycolysis: it instead regenerates NAD⁺, allowing the cytosolic pathway to continue.

The value four includes two net glycolytic ATP and two equivalents produced in the tricarboxylic acid cycle. These contributions are already included in the total: adding them again after calculation would count the same production twice.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a normalized experiment, control mitochondria consume $10$ oxygen units and produce $50$ ATP units. After a compound they consume $20$ oxygen units and produce $20$ ATP units. The proton gradient decreases and substrates and oxygen are available. Calculate ATP/oxygen ratios and interpret the data.

The experimental ratios are

$$R_{\mathrm{control}}=\frac{50}{10}=5,\qquad R_{\mathrm{treated}}=\frac{20}{20}=1.$$

Relative yield is $\dfrac{1}{5}=20\%$ of control. The pattern is compatible with **uncoupling**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Increased oxygen consumption shows that electron transfer is not completely halted. Simultaneous gradient loss and reduced ATP production per oxygen indicate weaker coupling between respiration and phosphorylation. An alternative proton pathway can dissipate energy without powering ATP synthase. These ratios are normalized experimental indices, so they are not identified with a universal stoichiometry. The data are compatible with the mechanism, but assigning the compound a molecular target would require additional measurements and controls.

Direct ATP synthase blockade can instead initially increase the gradient and slow respiration if the system remains coupled. Gradient measurement therefore helps distinguish different causes of low ATP production.

</div>
