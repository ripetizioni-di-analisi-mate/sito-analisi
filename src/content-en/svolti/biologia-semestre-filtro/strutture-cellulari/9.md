## Exercise 1

Autophagosome numbers increase in two cultures. In culture A, labelled material reaches lysosomes and is degraded faster; in B it persists and lysosomal fusion is reduced. Explain why the same initial count does not imply the same increase in completed autophagy.

In culture A, the data support increased flux through degradation. In B, accumulation is consistent with reduced clearance. The observed number of structures depends on both formation and removal rates.

![Autophagy and flux interpretation](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/strutture-cellulari/endocitosi-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

A snapshot of a compartment measures how much material is present at one time without by itself showing how much traverses the whole pathway. Tracking a label over time adds information about fate. A fusion blockade can increase autophagosomes while reducing cellular turnover capacity. Quantitative measurement requires comparable times, controls for marker degradation and carefully interpreted lysosomal perturbations. The comparison avoids automatically equating increased autophagy with increased vesicle numbers.

Even a population with stable autophagosome numbers can have high flux if formation and removal increase together. Stable counts therefore do not mean no activity, just as accumulation does not prove pathway completion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Part of a mitochondrial network loses function. In the studied model, fission, PINK1-Parkin recognition and delivery to the lysosomal system contribute to its removal. Explain the distinct functions of the three steps and why blocking degradation alone can cause labelled mitochondria to accumulate.

Fission can separate the damaged portion; selective recognition promotes targeting; the autophagic-lysosomal system enables removal. Blocking the last step can keep the amount of recognized cargo high without completing its elimination.

<div class="spiegazione">

The steps perform different tasks, and a recognition signal alone does not demonstrate completed degradation. Mitophagy is defined by mitochondrial cargo selectivity. PINK1 and Parkin participate in the stated model, whereas other contexts can use different regulators. An excessively connected network or inefficient turnover can alter damage distribution, but the phenotype depends on the balance between production, repair and removal. The experimental prediction therefore concerns flux through the specified pathway, keeping labelled-organelle number distinct from the functional quality of the entire population.

Fission can increase fragment number without increasing total mitochondrial mass. Counting microscopic objects and measuring mitochondrial content are therefore complementary controls for distinguishing fragmentation, biogenesis and removal.

</div>
