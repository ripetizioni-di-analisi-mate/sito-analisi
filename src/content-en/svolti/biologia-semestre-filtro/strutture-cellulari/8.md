## Exercise 1

LDL and its receptor are followed with different labels. After endocytosis, the receptor returns to the surface while cargo signal reaches degradative compartments. Reconstruct sorting and explain why a drug preventing acidification can alter both signals.

In the endosome, pH favours separation of receptor and LDL. The receptor enters recycling, while cargo proceeds towards degradation. Preventing acidification can impede separation, sorting and hydrolase function in later compartments.

![Fates of endocytosed cargo](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/strutture-cellulari/endocitosi-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The two labels are needed because receptor and cargo initially share the vesicle but later follow different routes. Receptor recovery allows further uptake cycles without synthesizing a new receptor for every particle. A pH-altering drug does not necessarily represent a defect solely in LDL-receptor binding: it can affect several endolysosomal steps. Transferrin shows another fate, since protein and receptor recycle together after iron release; an activated EGF receptor can instead be directed towards degradation.

Surface binding can first be measured separately from subsequent internalization to distinguish uptake from recycling. Reduced intracellular signal could result from lower initial entry even if recycling of the remaining receptors functions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cell synthesizes several lysosomal hydrolases, but many are detected in the medium and lysosomes accumulate substrates. Mannose-6-phosphate addition is defective. Explain why many substrates can be affected and distinguish this case from mutation of a single hydrolase.

The defect alters a sorting signal shared by many hydrolases. Several enzymes may therefore fail to reach the endolysosomal system adequately and instead be secreted. Mutation of one hydrolase would directly affect that particular enzyme's activity.

<div class="spiegazione">

Enzyme presence in the medium shows that synthesis and part of the secretory pathway function. The problem emerges in destination selection: without signal recognition, endosomal delivery is reduced. Lysosomes can consequently lack several degradative activities and accumulate a variety of substrates. The comparison separates catalytic function from localization. Demonstrating that secreted enzymes retain activity would require testing under appropriate conditions; protein detection alone does not prove correct folding.

The mannose-6-phosphate mechanism concerns many soluble hydrolases; other lysosomal components use different signals. The defect therefore does not justify concluding that every lysosome-destined protein follows exactly the same route.

</div>
