## Exercise 1

A group of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps completes $1200$ cycles in total. Calculate expelled Na⁺, imported K⁺, ATP consumed and net outward transfer of positive charges. Use canonical stoichiometry with no incomplete cycles.

Each cycle transfers three Na⁺ outwards and two K⁺ inwards using one ATP.

$$\begin{aligned}N_{\mathrm{Na,out}}&=3\cdot1200=3600,\\N_{\mathrm{K,in}}&=2\cdot1200=2400,\\N_{\mathrm{ATP}}&=1200,\\N_{\mathrm{charges,out}}&=3600-2400=1200.\end{aligned}$$

![Transport and osmosis balances](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/strutture-cellulari/trasporto-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Counts of the two ions must be distinguished from net electrical transfer. Both carry one positive charge but move in opposite directions, so incoming potassium is subtracted from outgoing sodium. The pump is therefore **electrogenic**. Adding $3600$ and $2400$ would give the total number of ion-transport events, rather than net charge. The difference does not imply that cell volume must change in a simple proportion: other solutes, permeabilities and transport processes contribute to osmotic balance. The pump also maintains gradients usable by other carriers.

The balance can be checked per cycle by dividing every result by cycle number. This recovers three sodium ions, two potassium ions and one ATP, with one net charge per completed cycle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal cell of volume $0.8\,\mathrm{pL}$ contains nonpenetrating solutes at $300\,\mathrm{mOsm/L}$. It is transferred into a very large bath at $400\,\mathrm{mOsm/L}$ of nonpenetrating solute. Neglecting regulation, nonosmotic volume and pressure differences, calculate final volume and percentage change.

The amount of solute stays constant, so

$$\begin{aligned}V_f&=\frac{C_iV_i}{C_f}=\frac{300\cdot0.8}{400}\,\mathrm{pL}=0.6\,\mathrm{pL},\\\frac{V_f-V_i}{V_i}\cdot100\%&=\frac{0.6-0.8}{0.8}\cdot100\%=-25\%.\end{aligned}$$

The cell loses $25\%$ of its initial volume.

<div class="spiegazione">

The external medium is hypertonic relative to the initial state, so initial net water movement is towards the bath. Volume reduction concentrates solutes remaining in the cell until they reach the external concentration. The bath is assumed very large so that its concentration remains unchanged during exchange. Conservation concerns the amount of solute, whereas water amount changes. The negative percentage change confirms the shrinkage predicted before calculation. In a real cell, structural volumes and transport regulation would require a more complete model.

As a check, reconstructed final concentration is $\dfrac{300\cdot0.8}{0.6}=400\,\mathrm{mOsm/L}$, equal to the bath concentration. The resulting volume therefore satisfies both solute conservation and the assumed equilibrium condition.

</div>
