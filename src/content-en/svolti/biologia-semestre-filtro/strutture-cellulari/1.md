## Exercise 1

A membrane region has fluorescence $100$ before bleaching, $20$ immediately afterwards and $80$ at the plateau. Values are background-corrected. Assuming no new synthesis and comparable geometry, estimate the mobile fraction relative to lost fluorescence. Also identify a limit of the conclusion.

Initial loss is $100-20=80$ units; observed recovery is $80-20=60$ units.

$$f_{\mathrm{mobile}}=\frac{80-20}{100-20}=\frac{60}{80}=0.75=75\%.$$

The fraction not recovered within the observation window is $25\%$.

![Membranes and experimental interpretation](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/strutture-cellulari/membrana-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is the fluorescence actually removed by the laser, so the immediate post-bleach value is subtracted. The numerator includes only the fraction returning after that moment. Using $80\%$ directly would confuse residual fluorescence with recovery. The result supports a **75% mobile fraction** in the experimental model: unbleached molecules can reach the region laterally. The remainder may be immobilized or move too slowly for the observation period. A plateau also requires control for photobleaching during measurement. The experiment does not establish protein transfer between the two leaflets.

An unbleached control region allows fluorescence loss caused by measurement alone to be estimated. If that region also loses signal, recovery values require normalization before the difference can be assigned to mobility.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An impermeant extracellular probe recognizes phosphatidylserine. Intact viable cells show little signal; after a treatment initiating apoptosis the signal increases, while an integrity control excludes lysis. Explain the change and propose an alternative interpretation excluded by that control.

The probe stays outside, so signal requires phosphatidylserine accessible in the outer leaflet. Lipid redistribution makes the result consistent with **loss of normal asymmetry**. A ruptured membrane would instead have allowed the probe to reach the cytosolic side as well.

<div class="spiegazione">

Phosphatidylserine is normally enriched in the cytosolic leaflet. In the stated context, activation of redistribution systems can expose it outside, where it contributes to recognition by phagocytes. The integrity control is needed because the same probe could signal after entering lysed cells. On its own, the result measures lipid accessibility; the apoptosis conclusion also relies on the treatment and other process markers. The comparison therefore separates a distribution change from simple barrier failure.

The result alone does not identify which lipid transporter changed. Distinguishing reduced flippase activity from scramblase activation would require separate perturbations and observation of whether the distribution persists or recovers.

</div>
