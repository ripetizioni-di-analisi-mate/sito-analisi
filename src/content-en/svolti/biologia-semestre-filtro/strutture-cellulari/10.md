## Exercise 1

A radial microtubule has its minus end at $x=0$ near the centrosome and its plus end at $x=12\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Cargo starts at $x=8\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ and moves with dynein at constant speed $0.5\,\mathrm{\mu m/s}$ for $6\,\mathrm{s}$. Find its final position and compare it with that of a plus-end-directed motor at the same speed.

The positive axis points towards the periphery. Therefore

$$\begin{aligned}x_{\mathrm{dynein}}&=8-0.5\cdot6=5\,\mathrm{\mu m},\\x_{\mathrm{motor+}}&=8+0.5\cdot6=11\,\mathrm{\mu m}.\end{aligned}$$

Both positions remain within the microtubule interval.

![Motors and cellular mechanics](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/strutture-cellulari/motori-dati-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Speed magnitude does not indicate direction. Cytoplasmic dynein moves towards the minus end, so its velocity is negative relative to the chosen axis. The second motor is explicitly defined as plus-end-directed and increases the coordinate. Checking the interval avoids extending motion beyond the available end. In real cells, pauses, track changes and motor cooperation can occur; uniform motion is an assumption of this problem. Conclusions depend on microtubule orientation, which can differ from the radial model in other cellular arrangements.

The dimensional check gives $\mathrm{\mu m/s}\cdot\mathrm{s}=\mathrm{\mu m}$, so velocity times time can be added to initial position. Using time in minutes without conversion would produce a distance inconsistent with the data.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sarcomere changes from $2.5\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ to $2.0\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ during contraction. Measurements show substantially unchanged thin- and thick-filament lengths. Calculate percentage shortening and explain which geometric variable changes. Connect the result with myosin's role.

Shortening is

$$\Delta L=2.5-2.0=0.5\,\mathrm{\mu m},\qquad \frac{\Delta L}{L_i}\cdot100\%=20\%.$$

The distance between Z lines decreases and filament overlap increases, while their lengths remain consistent with the measurements.

<div class="spiegazione">

The percentage denominator is initial length because the problem compares the contracted unit with its starting state. Conserved filament lengths support the sliding-filament model. Myosin heads use ATP to produce relative movement against actin and bring Z lines closer. Contraction also requires regulation and force transmission. Dystrophin contributes to mechanical connection with membrane and matrix but does not replace myosin's motor cycle. The geometric calculation therefore distinguishes unit shortening from shortening of its components.

Within the normal sliding range, the A band retains the length associated with thick filaments, while regions of lower overlap can narrow. This observable prediction provides a model check beyond measurement of Z lines alone.

</div>
