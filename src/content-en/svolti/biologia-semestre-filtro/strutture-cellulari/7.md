## Exercise 1

Two glycoprotein variants are synthesized in similar amounts. The first reaches the surface; the second remains associated longer with reticulum chaperones and is then degraded. Proteasome inhibition causes the second to accumulate without restoring surface delivery. Interpret the result.

The second variant is consistent with a maturation defect recognized by quality control. Proteasome blockade reduces removal and increases total amount, but does not necessarily correct folding and export. Accumulation therefore does not equal functional rescue.

<div class="spiegazione">

The comparison keeps production similar and focuses on subsequent fate. Prolonged chaperone association and sensitivity to proteasomal degradation are consistent with ERAD, linking reticulum recognition to cytosolic removal. Additional experiments would be needed to demonstrate each pathway step. Surface measurement is necessary because a protein can be abundant in the wrong compartment. Some CFTR defects illustrate this principle: amount synthesized, folding quality and channel availability at the membrane are different measurements.

Impermeant surface labelling can be compared with total amount measured after lysis. An increased total signal without increased surface signal would confirm that inhibition changed turnover without correcting destination.

</div>

## Exercise 2

After misfolded cargo accumulates, one cell temporarily reduces protein synthesis and increases reticulum chaperones. Another maintains high synthesis without increasing folding capacity. Explain which adaptation can reduce the imbalance and why success is not guaranteed.

The first response reduces incoming new chains and increases capacity to handle existing proteins. It is consistent with an adaptive UPR. The second condition instead maintains high demand relative to available capacity and can sustain stress.

<div class="spiegazione">

Reticulum homeostasis depends on the relationship between incoming cargo, folding, export and degradation. Temporarily reducing one source of load and strengthening quality control can allow recovery. Increased chaperones do not automatically eliminate a severe structural defect or persistent stimulus, however. If stress continues, different outcomes, including cell death, may occur. This reasoning interprets an adaptive response without presenting it as a universal solution and distinguishes the UPR response programme from ERAD, a pathway removing selected cargoes.

The conclusion also requires a time-course comparison: transiently reduced synthesis followed by recovery differs from persistent blockade. Measuring only the first phase does not predict the cell’s final outcome.

</div>
