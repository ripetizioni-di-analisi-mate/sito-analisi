## Exercise 1

A large natural protein localizes to the nucleus. Mutating one basic segment leaves it in the cytosol; adding the same sequence to a large cytosolic reporter induces nuclear accumulation. Expression and folding are controlled. Distinguish the conclusions of the two experiments.

Loss of import after mutation supports that segment being **necessary under the studied conditions**. Transfer of the sequence to the reporter supports it being **sufficient for that construct**. The comparisons complement each other and identify a functional candidate NLS.

![Nuclear transport experiments](/figure/biologia-semestre-filtro/strutture-cellulari/ran-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The first experiment removes a function from the natural protein and observes loss of destination. The second adds that function to a protein previously remaining outside the nucleus. Stability and folding controls reduce the possibility that mutation merely destroyed overall structure. Import still requires functional receptors and pores, so the sequence does not act independently of the cell. Other proteins can also use different signals. The result concerns the construct's mechanism and does not prove that every nuclear protein has the same sequence.

The reporter’s large size makes measurement more informative: a sufficiently small molecule could distribute by diffusion through the pore. The nucleus-to-cytosol ratio should also be compared, avoiding confusion between higher total expression and greater import.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cytosolic extract allows cargo release from an exportin-Ran-GTP complex. Release slows after RanGAP inactivation and resumes when functional RanGAP is added. Explain the connection with directionality and why this result does not describe every RNA export pathway.

RanGAP accelerates hydrolysis of Ran-bound GTP on the cytosolic side. This state change favours export-complex dissociation. Recovery after RanGAP readdition supports its causal role in the measured step.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the nucleus, Ran-GTP favours association between exportin and cargo; in the cytosol its hydrolysis favours separation. The asymmetric distribution of RanGEF and RanGAP therefore creates different conditions on the two sides of the pore. The assay directly tests release of the selected complex rather than every possible nuclear pathway. Most mRNAs use NXF1-NXT1 and particle remodelling through a different mechanism. A defect in the Ran-dependent assay therefore does not automatically assign the same step to every mRNA.

In the classical import cycle, Ran-GTP has a different effect on cargo association: it promotes nuclear release from importin. The same Ran state can therefore support different outcomes because it interacts with different receptors.

</div>
