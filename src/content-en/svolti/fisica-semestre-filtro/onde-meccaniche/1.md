## Exercise 1

A point follows $x(t)=(0.050\,\mathrm m)\cos[(4\pi\,\mathrm{s^{-1}})t+\dfrac{\pi}{3}]$. Determine amplitude, period, frequency, maximum speed and acceleration magnitude, initial position and velocity. Predict the initial direction before numerical calculation.

$$
A=0.050\,\mathrm m,\quad \omega=4\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s},\quad T=\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}=0.50\,\mathrm s,\quad f=2.0\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$
$$
v_{\max}=A\omega\simeq0.628\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad a_{\max}=A\omega^2\simeq7.90\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
$$
$$
x(0)=A\cos\dfrac\pi3=0.025\,\mathrm m,\qquad
v_x(0)=-A\omega\sin\dfrac\pi3\simeq-0.544\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

![Position in centimetres versus time, with initial phase π/3.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/onde-meccaniche/oscillator-worked-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient in front of cosine is a length, whereas the coefficient multiplying time is angular frequency: its product with $t$ is an angle. The number $4\pi$ must not be read as frequency in hertz. One cycle requires phase to increase by $2\pi$, giving the period; frequency is its reciprocal.

Initially the phase lies in the first quadrant: cosine is positive and decreasing, so the point is to the right of equilibrium and moves towards smaller $x$. The negative derivative confirms this prediction. Maximum speed and acceleration magnitude follow from the sine and cosine coefficients, but do not occur simultaneously.

The graph uses centimetres: $0.025\,\mathrm m$ becomes $2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, while time remains in seconds. As a check, initial speed is below $v_{\max}$ and initial position lies inside $[-A,A]$. The identity $v_x^2+\omega^2x^2=A^2\omega^2$ also checks the initial magnitude without differentiating the law again.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An oscillator has maximum speed $0.60\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and maximum acceleration magnitude $7.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Reconstruct angular frequency and amplitude. Find speed at $x=0.030\,\mathrm m$ and decide whether the data also determine velocity’s sign.

$$
\omega=\dfrac{a_{\max}}{v_{\max}}=12\,\mathrm{rad/s},\qquad A=\dfrac{v_{\max}}\omega=0.050\,\mathrm m.
$$
$$
T=\dfrac{2\pi}{12}\,\mathrm s\simeq0.524\,\mathrm s,
\qquad |v_x|=\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}=0.48\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two measurements correspond to $A\omega$ and $A\omega^2$. Dividing the second by the first eliminates amplitude; once angular frequency is known, $A$ follows. The unit ratio is $\dfrac{\mathrm{m/s^2}}{\mathrm{m/s}}=\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, consistent with angular frequency.

To find speed at a specified position, eliminate phase using $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$. The radicand is positive because the position is reachable: $|x|<A$. Substitution gives a square root of $0.040\,\mathrm m$, which multiplied by $12\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ gives the result.

Velocity’s sign remains undetermined: the point passes through $x=0.030\,\mathrm m$ both moving right and returning left. Position alone does not identify the full phase. Both possibilities $v_x=\pm0.48\,\mathrm{m/s}$ fit the data. As a limiting check, the same formula gives zero speed at $|x|=A$ and maximum speed at the centre.

</div>
