## Exercise 1

On a frictionless horizontal surface, a mass $m=0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ is attached to a spring with $K=200\,\mathrm{N/m}$. It is released from rest $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from equilibrium. Find period, maximum speed, maximum force and energy. What fraction of energy is kinetic at $x=\dfrac{A}{2}$?

$$
A=0.040\,\mathrm m,\quad\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{200}{0.50}}=20\,\mathrm{rad/s},\quad T\simeq0.314\,\mathrm s.
$$
$$
v_{\max}=0.80\,\mathrm{m/s},\quad F_{\max}=KA=8.0\,\mathrm N,\quad E=\dfrac12KA^2=0.160\,\mathrm J.
$$
$$
\dfrac{U}{E}=\dfrac{x^2}{A^2}=\dfrac14,\qquad
E_{\mathrm{kin}}=\dfrac34E=0.120\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight and the normal reaction balance vertically. Along the spring axis, the elastic force restores the body towards equilibrium. Since release is from rest, the initial displacement is a turning point and equals amplitude. Converting centimetres before squaring avoids a four-order-of-magnitude error in energy.

Maximum force occurs at the endpoints; maximum speed at the centre. The two maxima therefore must not be assigned to the same state. At equilibrium all energy is kinetic: $\dfrac12mv_{\max}^2=0.160\,\mathrm J$, independently checking the elastic result.

At half amplitude, potential energy is one quarter of the total, not half, because it depends on $x^2$. The remainder is kinetic and positive during both passages through that position. Period does not depend on initial energy in the linear model: a different release changes amplitude and maximum speed while retaining the same mass-to-stiffness ratio.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass extends a vertical spring by $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at equilibrium. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The mass is lowered another $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and released from rest. Determine stiffness, period and maximum speed, explaining the origin used for amplitude.

$$
K=\dfrac{mg}{\Delta\ell}=49.05\,\mathrm{N/m}\simeq49.1\,\mathrm{N/m}.
$$
$$
\omega=\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\Delta\ell}}\simeq12.8\,\mathrm{rad/s},\quad
T=2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\Delta\ell}{g}}\simeq0.491\,\mathrm s.
$$
$$
A=0.020\,\mathrm m,\qquad v_{\max}=A\omega\simeq0.256\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equilibrium, weight balances spring force: this static measurement determines $K$ without observing a full oscillation. Choosing downward as positive and the loaded equilibrium as origin, the resultant at displacement $x$ is $mg-K(\Delta\ell+x)=-Kx$. The constant term disappears because of the initial balance.

Oscillation therefore occurs about loaded equilibrium. Amplitude is the additional lowering, not the total extension of $8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from natural length. Using that total would incorrectly quadruple maximum speed.

Substituting $\dfrac{K}{m}=\dfrac{g}{\Delta\ell}$ into the period formula shows that the static extension suffices for these data. This does not mean changing mass on the same spring leaves period unchanged: static extension would also change. Units are consistent because $\dfrac{\Delta\ell}{g}$ is a time squared. Numerical results are rounded only after using the unrounded stiffness.

</div>
