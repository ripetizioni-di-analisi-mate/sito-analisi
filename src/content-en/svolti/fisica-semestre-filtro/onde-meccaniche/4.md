## Exercise 1

A wave follows $y(x,t)=(0.002\,\mathrm m)\cos[(4\pi\,\mathrm{m^{-1}})x-(200\pi\,\mathrm{s^{-1}})t]$. Determine amplitude, wavelength, frequency, direction and propagation speed. Compare that speed with the greatest transverse speed of a string point.

$$
A=2.0\,\mathrm{mm},\quad\lambda=\dfrac{2\pi}{4\pi\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}}=0.50\,\mathrm m,\quad f=100\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$
$$
v=\dfrac\omega k=50\,\mathrm{m/s}\quad(+x),\qquad
u_{\max}=A\omega=0.4\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq1.26\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

![Spatial profiles and a point’s time record: two different readings of one wave.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/onde-meccaniche/profiles-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Wave number multiplies coordinate, whereas angular frequency multiplies time. Each phase increment of $2\pi$ corresponds to one wavelength or one period. These coefficients separately determine the spatial and temporal scales.

Direction follows by holding phase constant: $4\pi x-200\pi t=C$ in the stated units implies a crest’s position increases with time. A crest travels half a metre in one hundredth of a second, confirming $50\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

A material string point instead stays at the same equilibrium coordinate $x$ and oscillates along $y$. Its transverse speed has maximum $A\omega$, much smaller than propagation speed for these data. The two quantities should not be subtracted as velocities along the same axis: they describe motions with different directions and meanings. The ratio $\dfrac{u_{\max}}{v}=Ak\simeq0.0251$ is also consistent with the small slopes required by the string model.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Construct a wave travelling towards $-x$, with amplitude $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, wavelength $0.20\,\mathrm m$ and frequency $40\,\mathrm{Hz}$. At $x=0,t=0$, the medium is at equilibrium and moving towards $+y$. Determine the phase in a cosine representation and check propagation direction.

$$
k=10\pi\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},\qquad\omega=80\pi\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},\qquad \varphi=-\dfrac\pi2.
$$
$$
y(x,t)=(10^{-3}\,\mathrm m)\cos\left[(10\pi\,\mathrm{m^{-1}})x+(80\pi\,\mathrm{s^{-1}})t-\dfrac\pi2\right].
$$
$$
\left.\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial t}\right|_{0,0}=A\omega>0,\qquad v_{\mathrm{phase}}=-\dfrac\omega k=-8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Negative propagation requires a phase containing $kx+\omega t$, with positive coefficients. Wavelength and frequency determine $k$ and $\omega$ before initial conditions are used. A negative amplitude is unnecessary for leftward propagation.

The condition $y(0,0)=0$ requires $\cos\varphi=0$, but leaves two principal possibilities, $\varphi=\pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The time derivative is $-A\omega\sin\varphi$: making it positive selects $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, up to multiples of $2\pi$. Position alone would not distinguish the two phases.

Following a constant phase, coordinate decreases at speed $\dfrac{\omega}{k}=8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The independent check $f\lambda=40(0.20)=8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives the same magnitude. Initial local velocity is instead positive along $y$, without contradicting the wave’s motion towards $-x$.

</div>
