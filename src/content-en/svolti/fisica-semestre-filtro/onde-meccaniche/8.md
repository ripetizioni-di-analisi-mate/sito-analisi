## Exercise 1

An isotropic point source emits $0.10\,\mathrm W$ without absorption. Calculate intensity at $2.0\,\mathrm m$. At what distance does it fall to one quarter of this value? Check total power crossing both spheres.

$$
I_1=\dfrac{0.10}{4\pi(2.0)^2}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}\simeq1.99\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}.
$$
$$
\dfrac{I_2}{I_1}=\left(\dfrac{2.0\,\mathrm m}{r_2}\right)^2=\dfrac14
\Longrightarrow r_2=4.0\,\mathrm m.
$$
$$
I_1(4\pi r_1^2)=I_2(4\pi r_2^2)=0.10\,\mathrm W.
$$

![Intensity decrease from geometry alone.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/onde-meccaniche/spreading-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The relevant area is a sphere’s surface, not a disk of radius $r$. Isotropy makes intensity uniform over that sphere, while absence of absorption ensures crossing power equals emitted power.

Comparing distances does not require recalculating power: taking the ratio cancels $P$ and $4\pi$. Intensity falls to one quarter when distance doubles. Multiplying distance by four would instead reduce intensity to one sixteenth.

The final check recovers the same power through both surfaces. Flow per square metre is smaller farther away, but four times as many square metres are available. The law does not describe energy consumption along the path. The graph shows precisely this ideal trend; a directional beam or a strongly reflecting environment would require more information before the same calculation could be applied.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In steady operation, uniform intensities $I_1=8.0\cdot10^{-3}$ and $I_2=1.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ are measured on concentric spheres of radii $2.0$ and $4.0\,\mathrm m$. Exclude other sources and reflections. What fraction of power crossing the first sphere reaches the second? How much is absorbed in the layer?

$$
\dfrac{P_2}{P_1}=\dfrac{I_2r_2^2}{I_1r_1^2}=\dfrac18\cdot4=\dfrac12.
$$
$$
P_1=0.128\pi\,\mathrm W\simeq0.402\,\mathrm W,\qquad
P_2=0.064\pi\,\mathrm W\simeq0.201\,\mathrm W.
$$
$$
P_{\mathrm{abs}}=P_1-P_2\simeq0.201\,\mathrm W.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The intensity ratio is one eighth, but that does not mean only one eighth of the power survives. Part of the decrease comes from an area four times larger. Isolate absorption by reconstructing power through each sphere, multiplying intensity by area.

Without absorption, the second intensity would be one quarter of the first, $2.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. The measured value is half that prediction: fifty per cent of the initial power reaches the second sphere. This qualitative check agrees with the power ratio.

Steady operation excludes mean accumulation of wave energy in the shell. The difference of approximately $0.201\,\mathrm W$ is energy transferred to the medium per second in the layer between surfaces. By itself it does not establish the resulting temperature: duration, mass, heat capacity and exchange conditions would also be required. Interpretation as absorption depends on the exclusions of reflections, additional sources and directional redistribution stated in the question.

</div>
