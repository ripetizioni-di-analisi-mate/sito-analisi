## Exercise 1

A plane travelling wave has RMS pressure $p_{\mathrm{rms}}=0.020\,\mathrm{Pa}$ in a medium with $\rho v=400\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m}$. Calculate intensity and level relative to $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. Also determine maximum pressure amplitude, assuming a sinusoid.

$$
I=\dfrac{p_{\mathrm{rms}}^2}{\rho v}=\dfrac{(0.020)^2}{400}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}.
$$
$$
L_I=10\log_{10}(10^6)\,\mathrm{dB}=60\,\mathrm{dB},\qquad
p_{\max}=\sqrt2p_{\mathrm{rms}}\simeq0.0283\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

![Level scale for increasing intensity ratios.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/onde-meccaniche/decibels-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The given quantity is RMS pressure, the square root of mean squared pressure. The intensity formula therefore requires no additional factor of $\dfrac{1}{2}$. That factor would appear when using squared peak amplitude of a sinusoid. Confusing the two amplitude types would incorrectly halve the result.

Level uses the intensity-to-reference ratio, one million. Its decimal logarithm is six, and the factor ten gives the numerical decibel value. Neither pressure in pascals nor intensity with units should be inserted alone into the logarithm.

As a check, peak pressure gives $\dfrac{p_{\max}^2}{2\rho v}=I$, matching the first calculation. The pressure–intensity relation applies to the specified travelling wave: oscillating pressure in a standing wave would not guarantee the same net mean flux. A perceptual interpretation is not requested and cannot be inferred from the number $60$ alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two independent incoherent sources, with contributions travelling in the same direction, separately produce $60$ and $50\,\mathrm{dB}$ at one point. Calculate total level using $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. How much does the level fall when the weaker source is switched off?

$$
I_1=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2},\quad I_2=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{W/m^2},\quad I_{\mathrm{tot}}=1.1\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}.
$$
$$
L_{\mathrm{tot}}=60\,\mathrm{dB}+10\log_{10}(1.1)\,\mathrm{dB}\simeq60.41\,\mathrm{dB}.
$$
$$
L_{\mathrm{tot}}-L_1\simeq0.41\,\mathrm{dB}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Levels are not additive quantities: a ten-decibel difference means the weaker source supplies one tenth of the stronger intensity. Therefore convert back to intensities, add incoherent contributions, and only then take the logarithm.

The total is $110\%$ of the greater intensity, so level rises by only about four tenths of a decibel. The approximately three-decibel rule applies to two equal contributions, which is not the present case. Adding $60+50$ or averaging the two levels arithmetically does not reconstruct energy transfer.

Switching off the weaker source returns the level to $60\,\mathrm{dB}$: the decrease is the difference between before and after, not $50\,\mathrm{dB}$. As a limiting check, if the weaker source becomes negligible, the total approaches the stronger source’s level. Specified incoherence excludes stable phase-related cancellation.

</div>
