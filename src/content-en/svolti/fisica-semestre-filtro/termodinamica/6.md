## Exercise 1

A plane wall has two layers in series, each of area $A=1.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The first has $L_1=0.020\,\mathrm m$ and $\kappa_1=0.20\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$; the second has $L_2=0.040\,\mathrm m$ and $\kappa_2=0.10\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}$. Outer faces are maintained at $40$ and $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, with the first layer on the hot side. At steady state, with perfect contact and insulated sides, find transmitted power, interface temperature and heat transferred in $600\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\mathcal R_1=\dfrac{L_1}{\kappa_1A}=0.10\,\mathrm{K\,W^{-1}},\qquad
\mathcal R_2=\dfrac{L_2}{\kappa_2A}=0.40\,\mathrm{K\,W^{-1}}.
$$
$$
P=\dfrac{40-20}{\mathcal R_1+\mathcal R_2}
=\dfrac{20}{0.50}=40\,\mathrm W,
$$
$$
t_{12}=40-P\mathcal R_1=36\,{}^\circ\mathrm C,
\qquad Q=P\Delta t=40\cdot600=24000\,\mathrm J=24\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$

![Temperature profile across the two layers with the interface at 36 degrees](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/termodinamica/conduzione-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

At steady state, no energy accumulates at the interface: the power crossing the first layer must also cross the second. Thermal resistances add because their temperature drops add. The second layer's drop is four times the first, matching the resistance ratio; its greater thickness and lower conductivity both act in the same direction.

Perfect contact imposes temperature continuity at the interface, not equal slopes in the layers. The graph therefore shows two straight segments of different slopes. Finally, $40\,\mathrm W$ is power: obtaining energy requires multiplication by time. This product is valid because outer temperatures and steady conditions are maintained throughout the interval.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A grey surface of area $A=1.00\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and emissivity $\varepsilon=0.90$ is maintained at $310\,\mathrm K$. It sees only large isothermal surroundings at $293.15\,\mathrm K$, with unit view factor and absorptivity equal to emissivity in the adopted model. Neglect other transfers. Using $\sigma=5.67\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-4}}$, find emitted power, net power lost and net heat lost in $600\,\mathrm s$.

$$
P_{\mathrm{em}}=\varepsilon\sigma AT_s^4
=0.90\cdot5.67\cdot10^{-8}\cdot310^4
\simeq471\,\mathrm W,
$$
$$
P_{\mathrm{net}}=\varepsilon\sigma A(T_s^4-T_a^4)
=0.90\cdot5.67\cdot10^{-8}(310^4-293.15^4)
\simeq94.4\,\mathrm W.
$$
$$
Q_{\mathrm{net}}=P_{\mathrm{net}}\Delta t
\simeq56.6\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$

![Net radiative power versus surface temperature, zero at ambient temperature](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/termodinamica/irraggiamento-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The surface emits even when the surroundings have the same temperature, but it simultaneously receives radiation. Net loss is emission minus absorption: here absorbed power is about $377\,\mathrm W$, so using emission alone would greatly overestimate cooling. Fourth powers require absolute temperatures; raising Celsius values to the fourth power would not represent the same physical law.

Positive net outward power agrees with $T_s>T_a$. Maintaining it requires a compensating energy input; if the surface cooled, power would need integration over time. The $56.6\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is the positive magnitude of net energy lost: the corresponding heat in the surface's balance would be negative. The model isolates radiation without treating this surface as a complete human body.

</div>
