## Exercise 1

A cyclic heat engine absorbs $Q_h=3.0\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and rejects $Q_c=2.1\,\mathrm{kJ}$ per cycle, both stated as positive magnitudes. It completes $20$ cycles per second. Find net work per cycle, efficiency and average mechanical power. Neglect changes in macroscopic energies.

$$
\Delta U_{\mathrm{cycle}}=0,\qquad W=Q_h-Q_c=0.90\,\mathrm{kJ},
$$
$$
\eta=\dfrac{W}{Q_h}=\dfrac{0.90}{3.0}=0.30=30\%,
\qquad P=fW=20\cdot900=18000\,\mathrm W=18\,\mathrm{kW}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The working fluid returns to its initial state, so its internal energy does not change over a complete cycle. This does not make heat and work individually zero: their net values must be equal. Since $Q_c$ is a positive rejected magnitude, it enters net absorbed heat with a minus sign.

Efficiency compares useful work with energy absorbed from the hot source, not with rejected heat or the sum of both heats. Power also requires cycle frequency: a slower engine could have the same efficiency but less power. As a check, absorbed thermal power is $60\,\mathrm{kW}$ and rejected thermal power is $42\,\mathrm{kW}$; their difference reproduces the $18\,\mathrm{kW}$ of mechanical output.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A proposed cyclic engine exchanges heat only with reservoirs at $600$ and $350\,\mathrm K$. Per cycle it absorbs $8.00\,\mathrm{kJ}$ from the hot source and produces $3.60\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of work. Check compatibility with the two laws and calculate the maximum possible work for the same heat input.

$$
Q_c=Q_h-W=4.40\,\mathrm{kJ},\qquad
\eta=\dfrac{3.60}{8.00}=0.450.
$$
$$
\eta_C=1-\dfrac{350}{600}=\dfrac5{12}\simeq0.4167,
\qquad W_{\max}=Q_h\eta_C\simeq3.33\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}=-\dfrac{8000}{600}+\dfrac{4400}{350}
=-0.7619\ldots\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}<0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first law closes the balance with positive rejected heat, so it does not by itself reveal the incompatibility. The second law requires that no engine between these temperatures exceed Carnot efficiency. The claimed $45\%$ exceeds the $41.7\%$ limit and demands more work than allowed.

The entropy check reaches the same conclusion by another route. The fluid returns to its initial state and accumulates no entropy; the hot reservoir loses entropy and the cold one gains it. The claimed total would be negative for the isolated combined system, which is impossible. Reducing work to $3.33\,\mathrm{kJ}$ and rejecting the remaining heat gives the reversible limit; irreversibilities can only reduce work further for the same specified data.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cyclic refrigerator extracts $Q_c=900\,\mathrm J$ per cycle from a reservoir at $270\,\mathrm K$ and rejects heat at $300\,\mathrm K$. Calculate minimum work and rejected heat in the reversible limit. Compare with a real machine of $\mathrm{COP}=3.00$ for the same $Q_c$, including the reservoir entropy balance.

$$
\mathrm{COP}_C=\dfrac{T_c}{T_h-T_c}=\dfrac{270}{30}=9,
\quad W_{\min}=\dfrac{Q_c}{\mathrm{COP}_C}=100\,\mathrm J,
\quad Q_{h,C}=1000\,\mathrm J.
$$
$$
W_{\mathrm{real}}=\dfrac{900}{3}=300\,\mathrm J,
\qquad Q_{h,\mathrm{real}}=900+300=1200\,\mathrm J.
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot},C}=-\dfrac{900}{270}+\dfrac{1000}{300}=0,
\qquad \Delta S_{\mathrm{tot},\mathrm{real}}=-\dfrac{900}{270}+\dfrac{1200}{300}
=\dfrac23\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of performance compares the desired effect, heat extracted from the cold source, with work input. It can exceed one because the machine transfers energy already present in the cold source. Work input must then be added to extracted heat to find the energy reaching the hot surroundings.

The reversible limit provides the maximum COP and therefore the minimum work for the same $Q_c$. The real machine needs three times that work and rejects more energy to the hot reservoir. The fluid has zero entropy change per cycle; total reservoir change is zero in the reversible limit and positive in the real case. That positive value measures entropy produced by the combined system, not entropy stored in the fluid at the cycle's end.

</div>
