## Exercise 1

Mix $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $80\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The vessel is adiabatic and has negligible heat capacity. Assume constant $c=4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$, no evaporation and negligible work. Find the final temperature and the heat released by the hot portion.

$$
m_1c(t_f-t_1)+m_2c(t_f-t_2)=0
$$
$$
t_f=\dfrac{m_1t_1+m_2t_2}{m_1+m_2}
=\dfrac{0.30\cdot80+0.50\cdot20}{0.80}
=42.5\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$
$$
Q_1=0.30\cdot4186\cdot(42.5-80)
=-47092.5\,\mathrm J\simeq-47.1\,\mathrm{kJ},\qquad Q_2=-Q_1.
$$

![Heat transfers of the two portions and their sum versus final temperature](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/termodinamica/calorimetro-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The boundary encloses both water portions: the system receives no external energy, but its portions exchange heat. Writing each contribution as $mc(t_f-t_i)$ automatically gives released heat a negative sign. Since both portions have the same specific heat capacity, it cancels when finding temperature; it must still be included when calculating transferred energy.

The final temperature is a weighted mean: it must lie between the initial temperatures and closer to that of the larger mass. The value $42.5\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ passes both checks. In the graph, the sum of the heat transfers crosses zero at precisely this point. An arithmetic mean would give $50\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, assigning equal heat capacities to the portions despite the given masses.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.200\,\mathrm{kg}$ copper block at $150\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is immersed in $0.400\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, inside a $0.100\,\mathrm{kg}$ aluminium vessel initially at $20.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The combined system is isolated and no phase changes occur. Use constant specific heat capacities $c_{\mathrm{Cu}}=385$, $c_{\mathrm{Al}}=900$ and $c_a=4186$, all in $\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$. Calculate the final temperature and the copper's heat transfer.

$$
C_{\mathrm{Cu}}=77.0\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}},\qquad
C_a+C_{\mathrm{Al}}=1674.4+90.0=1764.4\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$
$$
77.0(t_f-150)+1764.4(t_f-20.0)=0
$$
$$
t_f=\dfrac{77.0\cdot150+1764.4\cdot20.0}{1841.4}
=25.436\ldots\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\simeq25.4\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$
$$
Q_{\mathrm{Cu}}=77.0(t_f-150)\simeq-9.59\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vessel absorbs part of the energy released by copper; it cannot be omitted because its heat capacity is specified and not assumed negligible. Water and aluminium start at the same temperature, so they combine into one term with heat capacity $1764.4\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. Copper has a much smaller heat capacity, allowing us to predict a final temperature close to the water's before calculating.

Keep the unrounded value of $t_f$ when finding $Q_{\mathrm{Cu}}$. Its negative sign means transfer out of the copper; the magnitude released is $9.59\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The energy gained by water and vessel is equal and opposite. Celsius temperatures are suitable because only differences enter the balance.

</div>
