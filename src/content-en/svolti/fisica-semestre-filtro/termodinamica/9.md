## Exercise 1

Two moles of monatomic ideal gas start from the same state at $300\,\mathrm K$. In separate experiments they reach $450\,\mathrm K$: first at constant volume, then at constant pressure. Both samples are closed and do only volume work; the isobaric process is mechanically reversible. With $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$, compare absorbed heat and entropy change. Are the final states identical?

$$
Q_V=n\dfrac32R(T_f-T_i)=3741.3\,\mathrm J,
\qquad Q_p=n\dfrac52R(T_f-T_i)=6235.5\,\mathrm J.
$$
$$
\Delta S_V=n\dfrac32R\ln\dfrac{450}{300}
\simeq10.1\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}},
\qquad \Delta S_p=n\dfrac52R\ln\dfrac{450}{300}
\simeq16.9\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$
$$
V_{f,V}=V_i,\quad p_{f,V}=1.50p_i;
\qquad V_{f,p}=1.50V_i,\quad p_{f,p}=p_i.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The internal energy increase is equal in both experiments because the same amount of ideal gas undergoes the same temperature rise. During isobaric heating, however, the gas also does work and must absorb more heat. The appropriate molar capacities give the results: $C_V$ at constant volume and $C_p$ at constant pressure.

Entropy changes differ because the final states differ: matching final temperature alone does not fix the gas state. The isobaric case includes volume expansion, adding $nR\ln\dfrac{V_f}{V_i}$ to the temperature contribution. There is therefore no contradiction with entropy's path independence: that property compares paths having both endpoints identical.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A closed ideal gas of $1.50\,\mathrm{mol}$ is compressed reversibly and isothermally at $300\,\mathrm K$ from $30.0$ to $10.0\,\mathrm L$. It exchanges heat with one reservoir at the same temperature in the reversible limit and does only volume work. Use $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$. Calculate $W$, $Q$ and the entropy changes of gas and reservoir.

$$
\Delta S_g=nR\ln\dfrac{V_f}{V_i}
=1.50\cdot8.314\ln\dfrac13
\simeq-13.7\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$
$$
W=nRT\ln\dfrac13\simeq-4.11\,\mathrm{kJ},
\qquad \Delta U=0,\qquad Q=W.
$$
$$
\Delta S_a=-\dfrac{Q}{T}=-\Delta S_g,
\qquad \Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compression gives negative work by the gas: an external agent supplies energy through work. To keep ideal-gas temperature constant, the same energy must leave as heat. The logarithm of the volume ratio is negative, giving both work and entropy change their expected signs.

Gas entropy decreases without violating the second law because the gas is not isolated. The reservoir receives the rejected heat and gains exactly the entropy lost by the gas. Equality of the contributions follows from reversibility; friction or finite temperature differences would require positive production. Units provide another check: multiplying entropy change by constant temperature gives reversible heat in joules.

</div>
