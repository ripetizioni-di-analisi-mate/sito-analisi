## Exercise 1

In an adiabatic vessel of negligible heat capacity, mix $0.400\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $80.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with $0.600\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. No evaporation or appreciable external work occurs. With constant $c=4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$, find final temperature and total entropy change.

$$
t_f=\dfrac{0.400\cdot80.0+0.600\cdot20.0}{1.000}
=44.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C,\qquad T_f=317.15\,\mathrm K.
$$
$$
\Delta S_1=1674.4\ln\dfrac{317.15}{353.15}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}},
\qquad \Delta S_2=2511.6\ln\dfrac{317.15}{293.15}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}=\Delta S_1+\Delta S_2
=17.61065\ldots\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}\simeq17.6\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}>0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The energy balance first determines final temperature; entropy changes are calculated only afterwards. Net heat exchanged with the surroundings is zero, but this does not make total entropy zero: internal transfer between hot and cold water crosses finite temperature differences and is irreversible.

For each portion, imagine a reversible path between its initial and final temperatures and apply the logarithmic expression using absolute temperatures. The hot portion contributes negatively and the cold one positively. Sum them while retaining sufficient digits: their positive difference is substantially smaller than the individual terms. No entropy of mixing different species is added because both portions contain the same substance; the calculation concerns thermal equilibration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Place $0.100\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at $0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ in $0.500\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $30.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, inside an adiabatic calorimeter of negligible heat capacity. At atmospheric pressure, with constant $c_a=4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}K^{-1}}$ and $\ell_f=333000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$, and no evaporation, determine the final state and total entropy.

$$
Q_{\max}=0.500\cdot4186\cdot30.0=62790\,\mathrm J
>m_g\ell_f=33300\,\mathrm J.
$$
$$
t_f=\dfrac{62790-33300}{0.600\cdot4186}
=11.74152\ldots\,{}^\circ\mathrm C,
\qquad T_f=284.89152\ldots\,\mathrm K.
$$
$$
\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}
=\dfrac{33300}{273.15}
+418.6\ln\dfrac{T_f}{273.15}
+2093\ln\dfrac{T_f}{303.15}
\simeq9.51\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The preliminary comparison shows complete melting: the final state contains $0.600\,\mathrm{kg}$ of liquid water at about $11.7\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The energy balance must first subtract melting energy, then distribute the remainder over all the liquid water.

For entropy, the ice follows two imagined auxiliary reversible stages: melting at constant $273.15\,\mathrm K$ and warming the newly formed water to $T_f$. Initially warm water contributes a negative cooling term. The positive sum confirms that contact is irreversible. Using only latent heat would omit both sensible contributions; Celsius temperatures inside logarithms would even make the term starting at zero degrees undefined. Keep the unrounded $T_f$ in the sum.

</div>
