## Exercise 1

A cylinder contains oxygen modelled as an ideal gas: $V=20.0\,\mathrm L$, absolute pressure $p=1.50\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and temperature $20.0\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. With $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$ and molar mass $M=0.0320\,\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$, find the amount of substance and mass. State the necessary conversions.

$$
V=0.0200\,\mathrm{m^3},\qquad T=20.0+273.15=293.15\,\mathrm K.
$$
$$
n=\dfrac{pV}{RT}
=\dfrac{1.50\cdot10^6\cdot0.0200}{8.314\cdot293.15}
=12.30896\ldots\,\mathrm{mol}\simeq12.3\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$
$$
m=nM=0.393887\ldots\,\mathrm{kg}\simeq0.394\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation of state describes the cylinder's current contents without needing to know how the gas was introduced. The given $R$ uses SI units, so litres and Celsius degrees must be converted before substitution. Pressure is already absolute; adding atmospheric pressure again would introduce an error.

The product $pV$ is an energy, whereas $RT$ is energy per mole: their ratio indeed yields moles. Multiplication by molar mass in kilograms per mole then gives kilograms. The ideal model is an assigned assumption, not a guarantee of exactness at every pressure; a real cylinder may require corrections for non-ideal behaviour.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Ideal air in a rigid, sealed vessel warms from $27\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to $177\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Its initial absolute pressure is $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Determine the final pressure and explain why the ratio of Celsius temperatures cannot be used.

$$
T_i=300.15\,\mathrm K,\qquad T_f=450.15\,\mathrm K,
\qquad \dfrac{p_i}{T_i}=\dfrac{p_f}{T_f}.
$$
$$
p_f=100\cdot\dfrac{450.15}{300.15}\,\mathrm{kPa}
=149.975\ldots\,\mathrm{kPa}\simeq150\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Rigidity fixes volume, and sealing fixes the amount of substance. Both conditions are needed to derive $p$ proportional to $T$ from $pV=nRT$. If a valve allowed air to escape during heating, rigidity alone would not determine the final pressure.

Absolute temperature rises by about half its initial value, so pressure also increases by about half. The ratio of $177$ to $27$ would instead give a much larger factor depending on the arbitrary Celsius zero. Initial and final states must be equilibrated; using their equations of state does not require every intermediate stage of heating to be reversible.

</div>
