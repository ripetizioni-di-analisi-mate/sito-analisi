## Exercise 1

A point charge $q_1 = +4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is fixed at the origin and $q_2 = -2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is fixed at $(0.30\,\mathrm{m};\ 0)$. Calculate potential at midpoint $M$ and at $P = (0;\ 0.40\,\mathrm{m})$, taking zero potential at infinity. The charges are in vacuum; use $k=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

$$
V(P) = \dfrac{k q_1}{r_1} + \dfrac{k q_2}{r_2}
$$
$$
r_{1M} = r_{2M} = 0.15\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
r_{1P} = 0.40\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
r_{2P} = \sqrt{0.30^2 + 0.40^2}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.50\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_M &= \dfrac{k\,(q_1 + q_2)}{0.15\,\mathrm{m}} = \dfrac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.15}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
V_P &= 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \left( \dfrac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.40} - \dfrac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.50} \right)\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
&= 8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 6.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 54\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sources are point charges and the reference is infinity, so each contributes $\dfrac{kq}{r}$. The **potential of the charge system** is the sum of signed contributions. At the midpoint, equal distances allow a common denominator and the sum $q_1+q_2=2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. At $P$, distance from $q_2$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with sides $0.30\,\mathrm m$ and $0.40\,\mathrm m$, hence $0.50\,\mathrm m$. Both potentials are **positive** because the larger positive source’s contribution dominates; it is closer or equally distant at the two specified points.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using the charges from Exercise 1, find the points on the $x$ axis where potential is zero. Then show that the zero-potential locus in the $xy$ plane is a circle. In the coordinate equations, $x$ and $y$ are expressed in metres; points occupied by the sources remain excluded.

$$
V(x) = k \left( \dfrac{q_1}{|x|} + \dfrac{q_2}{|x - 0.30|} \right) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2\,|x - 0.30| = |x| , \qquad x \neq 0 ,\ x \neq 0.30
$$

- for $x < 0$: $\ 2\,(0.30 - x) = -x$, that is $x = 0.60$, which does not satisfy $x < 0$;
- for $0 < x < 0.30$: $\ 2\,(0.30 - x) = x$, that is $x = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$;
- for $x > 0.30$: $\ 2\,(x - 0.30) = x$, that is $x = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(x, y) = 0
&\iff r_1 = 2\, r_2 \\[6pt]
&\iff x^2 + y^2 = 4\left[ (x - 0.30)^2 + y^2 \right] \\[6pt]
&\iff 3x^2 - 2.4\,x + 0.36 + 3y^2 = 0 \\[6pt]
&\iff (x - 0.40)^2 + y^2 = 0.20^2
\end{aligned}
$$

![Zero-potential circle centred at 0.40 metres with radius 0.20 metres and charges on the x axis.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/elettricita-e-magnetismo/potenziale-zero-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Potential vanishes when the two contributions are opposite. Since $q_1=-2q_2$, this requires $r_1=2r_2$: the point must be twice as far from the positive source as from the negative one. On the $x$ axis, distances are absolute values, so consider the three intervals separated by the source locations. To the left of the origin, the algebraic solution lies outside the assumed interval; there every point is closer to the larger positive source. Potential therefore **vanishes** only at $x=0.20\,\mathrm m$ and $x=0.60\,\mathrm m$.

In the plane, square $r_1=2r_2$, since both distances are positive, divide by $3$ and complete the square in $x$. This gives a **circle** centred at $(0.40;\ 0)$ with radius $0.20\,\mathrm m$. Rotational symmetry about the $x$ axis makes the spatial equipotential $V=0$ a sphere of the same centre and radius. The two axial points are the horizontal diameter’s endpoints. The field is not generally zero on this circle: cancelling scalar potentials does not cancel the vector sum of fields.

</div>
