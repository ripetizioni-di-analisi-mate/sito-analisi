## Exercise 1

A proton moves in the page along positive $x$ at $v = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$ into the page. Calculate its magnetic force and acceleration. Its mass is $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$. Use $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and neglect other forces.

![Rightward velocity and an inward field give upward force on the proton.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/elettricita-e-magnetismo/lorentz-en.svg)

$$
\vec{v} = v\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
\vec{B} = -B\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\vec{F} = e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}
$$
$$
\vec{F} = e\,v\,\hat{\imath} \times \left( -B\,\hat{k} \right) = -e v B\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k}) = e v B\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
a = \dfrac{e v B}{m_p}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 9.6 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
a &= \dfrac{9.6 \cdot 10^{-15}}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 5.7 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the page in the $xy$ plane and $z$ towards the reader, an inward field is $-B\hat k$. The proton carries $e$, so **Lorentz force** is $e\vec v\times\vec B$. Since $\hat k\times\hat\imath=\hat\jmath$, reversing factors gives $\hat\imath\times\hat k=-\hat\jmath$; the field’s minus sign therefore makes force point along $+\hat\jmath$, upward. Velocity and field are perpendicular, giving the maximum magnitude $evB$. Acceleration is perpendicular to velocity with magnitude $5.7\cdot10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. This exceeds familiar macroscopic accelerations because the proton has a large charge-to-mass ratio. Speed does not increase: acceleration is centripetal. The corresponding radius is $r=\dfrac{v^2}{a}\simeq1.6\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm m$; magnetic work remains zero because force is instantaneously normal to velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron has velocity $\vec{v} = \left( 2.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = \left( 0.040\,\hat{\imath} - 0.020\,\hat{k} \right)\mathrm{T}$. Calculate magnetic force components and magnitude. Use $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$.

$$
\vec{F} = -e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{v} \times \vec{B}
&= \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\imath} & \hat{\jmath} & \hat{k} \\ 2.0 \cdot 10^6 & 3.0 \cdot 10^6 & 0 \\ 0.040 & 0 & -0.020 \end{vmatrix}\,\mathrm{m\cdot T/s} \\[6pt]
&= \left[ 3.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot (-0.020)\,\hat{\imath} - 2.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot (-0.020)\,\hat{\jmath} - 3.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.040\,\hat{k} \right] \mathrm{m\cdot T/s} \\[6pt]
&= \left( -6.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\jmath} - 1.2 \cdot 10^5\,\hat{k} \right) \mathrm{m\cdot T/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{F} &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot \left( 6.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\jmath} + 1.2 \cdot 10^5\,\hat{k} \right) \mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
&= \left( 9.6\,\hat{\imath} - 6.4\,\hat{\jmath} + 19.2\,\hat{k} \right) \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot \sqrt{3.6 \cdot 10^9 + 1.6 \cdot 10^9 + 14.4 \cdot 10^9}\,\mathrm{N} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.4 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{N} \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity and field are not perpendicular, so calculate the cross product in components using the symbolic determinant. The $\hat\jmath$ term carries a leading minus sign, with minor $v_xB_z-v_zB_x=2.0\cdot10^6\cdot(-0.020)$. The $\hat k$ term is $v_xB_y-v_yB_x=-3.0\cdot10^6\cdot0.040$. Cross-product units are $\mathrm{m/s}$ times tesla, giving newtons after multiplication by charge in coulombs.

The electron carries $-e$, so **Lorentz force** is opposite to $e\vec v\times\vec B$ and every component changes sign. As a check, $\vec F\cdot\vec v$ is proportional to $9.6\cdot2.0-6.4\cdot3.0+19.2\cdot0=0$, so force is perpendicular to velocity as required. Its magnitude is approximately $2.2\cdot10^{-14}\,\mathrm N$.

</div>
