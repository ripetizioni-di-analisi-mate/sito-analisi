## Exercise 1

A coil with $N = 200$ circular turns of radius $r = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ has its axis parallel to a uniform magnetic field whose magnitude rises uniformly from $0$ to $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ in $\Delta t = 0.10\,\mathrm{s}$. It forms a closed circuit of total resistance $R = 4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ with negligible self-inductance. Calculate emf and current magnitudes. Assume the field points towards the observer and also determine current direction as seen by that observer.

$$
\Lambda_B = N\Phi_B = N B\,\pi r^2 , \qquad
\mathcal{E} = -\dfrac{d\Lambda_B}{dt} , \qquad
i = \dfrac{|\mathcal{E}|}{R}
$$
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = N \pi r^2\,\dfrac{\Delta B}{\Delta t} , \qquad
i = \dfrac{N \pi r^2\,\Delta B}{R\,\Delta t}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi r^2 &= \pi \cdot 0.030^2\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 2.83 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}| &= 200 \cdot 2.83 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \dfrac{0.50}{0.10}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 2.8\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i &= \dfrac{2.83}{4.0}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.71\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coil axis is parallel to the field, so the turn normal makes zero angle with $\vec B$ and **linked flux** is $NB$ times one turn’s area. Only field magnitude varies, and it varies uniformly, so flux derivative equals $\dfrac{\Delta\Lambda_B}{\Delta t}$. **Faraday–Neumann–Lenz law** gives constant emf magnitude $\dfrac{N\pi r^2\Delta B}{\Delta t}$. Divide by resistance for current because self-inductance is negligible.

Dimensional check: $\mathrm{T\cdot m^2/s}=\mathrm{Wb/s}$, and a weber per second is a volt. The outward external field increases, so current must produce an inward field and is clockwise for the observer. Current ceases when the field stops increasing in this negligible-self-inductance model. Here $\Phi_B$ denotes flux per turn and $\Lambda_B=N\Phi_B$ total linked flux, avoiding counting turns twice.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A square loop of side $\ell = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ is stationary in a uniform magnetic field, with its normal inclined by $\theta = 60^\circ$ to the field. Field magnitude varies as $B(t) = \alpha t^2$, with $\alpha = 0.40\,\mathrm{T/s^2}$. Neglecting self-inductance, calculate induced current at $t_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and charge passing around the loop between $t = 0$ and $t_1$.

$$
\Phi_B(t) = \alpha t^2\,\ell^2 \cos\theta , \qquad
\mathcal{E}(t) = -\dfrac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -2 \alpha t\,\ell^2 \cos\theta
$$
$$
|i(t_1)| = \dfrac{2 \alpha t_1\,\ell^2 \cos\theta}{R} , \qquad
|q| = \dfrac{|\Phi_B(t_1) - \Phi_B(0)|}{R} = \dfrac{\alpha t_1^2\,\ell^2 \cos\theta}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal{E}(t_1)| &= 2 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 0.20^2 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V} = 0.048\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
|i(t_1)| &= \dfrac{0.048}{0.50}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.096\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
|q| &= \dfrac{0.40 \cdot 3.0^2 \cdot 0.20^2 \cdot 0.50}{0.50}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 0.14\,\mathrm{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The loop is planar and field is uniform, so flux is field times area $\ell^2$ times $\cos\theta$. Area and angle are fixed, so flux changes only through $B(t)$, with derivative $2\alpha t\ell^2\cos\theta$. Emf grows linearly in time and reaches $48\,\mathrm{mV}$ at $t_1$.

With constant resistance and negligible self-inductance, integrate $i=\dfrac{\mathcal E}{R}$ to obtain transferred charge: it depends on the change in flux from the initial zero value to $t_1$. The same result follows by integrating $|i(t)|=\dfrac{2\alpha t\ell^2\cos\theta}{R}$ from $0$ to $t_1$, because $\int_0^{t_1}2t\,dt=t_1^2$. Unrounded charge is $0.144\,\mathrm C$. Multiplying final current by the whole duration would give twice this: the area under the current-time graph is a triangle because current starts at zero and increases linearly.

</div>
