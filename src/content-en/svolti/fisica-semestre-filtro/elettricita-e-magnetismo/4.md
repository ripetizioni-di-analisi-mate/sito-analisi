## Exercise 1

A resistance $R = 50\,\Omega$ is to be made from nichrome wire of diameter $d = 0.40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and resistivity $\rho = 1.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\Omega \cdot \mathrm{m}$. Calculate the required wire length. Assume a uniform cylindrical wire at the temperature for which resistivity is specified.

$$
R = \rho\, \dfrac{\ell}{A}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ell = \dfrac{R A}{\rho} = \dfrac{\pi d^2 R}{4 \rho}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \pi \cdot (0.20 \cdot 10^{-3})^2\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 1.257 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
\ell &= \dfrac{50 \cdot 1.257 \cdot 10^{-7}}{1.1 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 5.7\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire is cylindrical and homogeneous, so solve the **wire resistance formula** for length. Calculate area using radius $0.20\,\mathrm{mm}=0.20\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$. The units are consistent: $\dfrac{\Omega\cdot\mathrm{m^2}}{\Omega\cdot\mathrm m}=\mathrm m$. Area depends on radius squared: using diameter instead would make the area four times too large, giving a length four times too great. At fixed target resistance and cross-section, lower resistivity would require a longer wire. The result concerns the specified temperature: heating during use can change resistance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A copper wire has resistance $R = 2.0\,\Omega$. It is drawn out until its length triples, with no change in volume or resistivity. Calculate the new resistance.

$$
\begin{cases}
\ell' = 3\,\ell \\[4pt]
\ell' A' = \ell A
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A' = \dfrac{A}{3}
$$
$$
R' = \rho\, \dfrac{\ell'}{A'} = \rho\, \dfrac{3\,\ell}{\dfrac{A}{3}} = 9\, \rho\, \dfrac{\ell}{A} = 9 R = 18\,\Omega
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Wire volume is length times cross-sectional area and remains constant. Tripling length therefore reduces area to one third. In the **wire resistance formula**, length is in the numerator and area in the denominator, so both changes increase resistance by a factor $3$, giving $9$ times the initial resistance. In general, stretching by a factor $k$ at constant volume and resistivity increases resistance by $k^2$. Radius is not reduced threefold: because area depends on its square, radius decreases by a factor $\sqrt3$. Actual drawing can also change temperature and material structure; the problem excludes those effects to isolate geometry.

</div>
