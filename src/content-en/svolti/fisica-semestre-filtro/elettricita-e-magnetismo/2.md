## Exercise 1

A proton of mass $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and charge $e$ starts from rest in a uniform electric field of magnitude $E = 4.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Calculate acceleration, speed after travelling $\Delta x = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, and transit time, then compare electric force with weight. Use $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$; neglect other interactions and assume the field is uniform throughout the path.

$$
a = \dfrac{eE}{m_p} , \qquad v = \sqrt{2 a\,\Delta x} , \qquad t = \dfrac{v}{a}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \dfrac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{4}}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 3.8 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \\[6pt]
v &= \sqrt{2 \cdot 3.83 \cdot 10^{12} \cdot 0.020}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.9 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
t &= \dfrac{3.92 \cdot 10^{5}}{3.83 \cdot 10^{12}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\dfrac{eE}{m_p g} &= \dfrac{6.4 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N}}{1.64 \cdot 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{N}} \approx 3.9 \cdot 10^{11}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The uniform field gives the proton constant acceleration $\dfrac{eE}{m_p}$, along the field because its charge is positive. Starting from rest, it undergoes uniformly accelerated straight-line motion along a field line. Obtain speed from $v^2=2a\Delta x$ and time from $v=at$. The electric-force-to-weight ratio is of order $10^{11}$, justifying neglect of gravity. Final speed is about $0.13\%$ of the speed of light, consistent with nonrelativistic kinematics. Here motion follows a field line only because the particle starts at rest and has no transverse velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron enters a region at speed $v_0 = 5.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with a uniform field $E = 1.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{N/C}$ parallel to and in the same direction as its initial velocity. Calculate its stopping distance and the time until it returns to the entry point. Use $m_e=9.11\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$; the field persists until the return and occupies a sufficiently extensive region. Neglect other forces.

![The electron’s initial velocity follows the field, force opposes it, and the position-time parabola shows stopping and return.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/elettricita-e-magnetismo/arresto-elettrone-en.svg)

$$
a = \dfrac{eE}{m_e} , \qquad 0 = v_0^2 - 2 a\,d \;\Longrightarrow\; d = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2a} = \dfrac{m_e v_0^2}{2 e E} , \qquad t_r = \dfrac{2 v_0}{a}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \dfrac{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{3}}{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \\[6pt]
d &= \dfrac{\left( 5.0 \cdot 10^{6} \right)^2}{2 \cdot 1.76 \cdot 10^{14}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 7.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
t_r &= \dfrac{2 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{6}}{1.76 \cdot 10^{14}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 5.7 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The electron is negative, so force $-e\vec E$ opposes the field and therefore its initial velocity: it **slows down** with constant deceleration magnitude $\dfrac{eE}{m_e}$. In $v^2=v_0^2-2ad$, set final velocity to zero to find a stopping distance of about $7.1\,\mathrm{cm}$.

After stopping, force continues in the same direction, so the electron returns. Velocity is $v(t)=v_0-at$ and position $v_0t-\dfrac12at^2$ returns to zero at $t_r=\dfrac{2v_0}{a}$. Then velocity is $-v_0$: the electron returns after $5.7\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm s$ with its original speed but opposite direction.

</div>
