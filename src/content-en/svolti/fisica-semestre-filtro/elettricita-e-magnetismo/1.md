## Exercise 1

Two stationary point charges $q_1 = +3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ and $q_2 = -5.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ are $r = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ apart in vacuum. Calculate the magnitude of the force each exerts on the other and determine its direction. Use $k=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$.

![Mutual forces are equal and opposite; their directions depend on charge signs.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/elettricita-e-magnetismo/coulomb-en.svg)

$$
F = k\,\dfrac{|q_1|\,|q_2|}{r^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \dfrac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.30^2}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
&\approx \dfrac{0.135}{0.090}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.5\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The charges are stationary points in vacuum, so **Coulomb’s law** applies. Use absolute charge values for the force magnitude and determine direction from their signs. Opposite signs mean **attraction**: force on $q_2$ points along the joining line towards $q_1$, and force on $q_1$ points towards $q_2$.

Action and reaction have the same magnitude, $1.5\,\mathrm N$, even though the charge magnitudes differ. The result has two significant figures, like the data. Particle mass does not appear in the law: unequal masses would produce different accelerations without changing equality of the mutual forces. A negative value belongs to a component relative to a chosen axis, not to the magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two identical small spheres carry the same positive charge $q$ and repel with force magnitude $F = 0.90\,\mathrm{N}$ when $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart in vacuum. Treat them as point charges. Find $q$, the number of missing electrons per sphere, and the force magnitude when separation triples. Use $k=8.99\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}$ and $e=1.60\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$.

$$
F = k\,\dfrac{q^2}{r^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q = r\,\sqrt{\dfrac{F}{k}} ,
\qquad
n = \dfrac{q}{e} ,
\qquad
F' = k\,\dfrac{q^2}{(3r)^2} = \dfrac{F}{9}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= 0.050 \cdot \sqrt{\dfrac{0.90}{8.99 \cdot 10^{9}}}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 0.050 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{C} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C} = 0.50\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
n &= \dfrac{5.0 \cdot 10^{-7}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}} \approx 3.1 \cdot 10^{12} \\[6pt]
F' &= \dfrac{0.90}{9}\,\mathrm{N} = 0.10\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spheres have equal charges, so their product in **Coulomb’s law** is $q^2$. Solve by dividing by $k$, multiplying by $r^2$ and taking the positive square root because the charge is stated to be positive. Convert separation to metres, $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}=0.050\,\mathrm m$, because $k$ uses SI units.

A positively charged sphere has lost electrons; by **charge quantization**, the missing count is charge divided by the elementary charge. Finally, force varies inversely with separation squared. Tripling distance reduces force to one ninth without needing to recalculate charge.

</div>
