## Exercise 1

A monochromatic collimated beam has initial power $80.0\,\mathrm{mW}$ and transmits $20.0\,\mathrm{mW}$ after $0.400\,\mathrm{cm}$ of homogeneous material. At fixed geometry, determine $\mu$, the half-value thickness and the power predicted after $0.600\,\mathrm{cm}$.


$$
\mu=\dfrac{\ln(\dfrac{80.0}{20.0})}{0.400\,\mathrm{cm}}
\simeq3.47\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}},\qquad
x_{1/2}=\dfrac{\ln2}{\mu}=0.200\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
$$
P(0.600\,\mathrm{cm})=80.0\cdot2^{-3}\,\mathrm{mW}=10.0\,\mathrm{mW}.
$$

![Powers of 80, 40, 20 and 10 milliwatts at equally spaced thicknesses.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/radiazioni-e-ottica/attenuation-data-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The power ratio equals the intensity ratio because beam area and geometry remain fixed. The given thickness transmits one quarter, so it contains exactly two half-value thicknesses. This observation gives $0.200\,\mathrm{cm}$ before logarithms are even used.

The coefficient requires the natural logarithm, consistent with $e^{-\mu x}$. Using a base-ten logarithm without conversion would underestimate the coefficient. At three half-value thicknesses, one eighth of the initial power remains, rather than a negative power obtained by repeatedly subtracting the first loss. The graph makes the distinction visible: absolute losses become smaller while the fraction removed by each equal layer stays constant. The model concerns the direct beam without collecting scattered radiation in the detector.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solution has absorbance $\mathcal A=0.300$ in a $1.00\,\mathrm{cm}$ cuvette relative to the blank. Its molar coefficient is $\varepsilon=150\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,cm^{-1}}$. Calculate concentration and transmittance; then predict the result for twice the path length, keeping the same solution and valid Beer–Lambert conditions.


$$
c_s=\dfrac{\mathcal A}{\varepsilon\ell}=2.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol/L},\qquad
\mathcal T=10^{-0.300}\simeq0.501.
$$
$$
\mathcal A'=0.600,\qquad \mathcal T'=\mathcal T^2=10^{-0.600}\simeq0.251.
$$

![Absorbance–concentration line and the point at two millimoles per litre.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/radiazioni-e-ottica/beer-calibration-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient units already match centimetres and moles per litre, so path length need not be converted to metres. The concentration is $2.00\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$: forgetting the prefix causes a thousandfold error. The calibration graph has constant slope because only concentration varies.

Absorbance is proportional to path length, but transmittance is not. Each of two equal segments transmits approximately half the light reaching it, leaving roughly one quarter overall. The check $\mathcal T'=\mathcal T^2$ reconstructs the result without recalculating absorbance. The absorbing species and coefficient are assumed unchanged, and the blank is assumed to remove container and solvent contributions in each measurement geometry.

</div>
