## Exercise 1

A thin converging lens in air has $f=10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. A real object $0.200\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall is at $p=30.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the paraxial approximation, determine image position, signed height and nature, as well as optical power. Use $q>0$ for real images on the opposite side from the object.


$$
\dfrac1q=\dfrac1{10.0}-\dfrac1{30.0}=\dfrac1{15.0}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}},
\qquad q=15.0\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
$$
M=-\dfrac qp=-0.500,\qquad h_i=Mh_o=-0.100\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad \mathcal D=\dfrac1{0.100\,\mathrm m}=10.0\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}.
$$

![Three rays converge at the real image fifteen centimetres away.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/radiazioni-e-ottica/lens-real-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The object lies beyond $2f$, so before calculating, predict a real image between $f$ and $2f$, inverted and diminished. The result $q=15.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ confirms all these properties. Distances can remain in centimetres in the conjugate-point equation provided they are consistent; optical power in dioptres instead requires metres.

Negative height indicates inversion relative to the object. It does not mean a negative physical size: the magnitude is $0.100\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the drawing, the principal rays meet exactly at the image tip; a screen in that plane can receive the image. Magnification can also be checked using the similar triangles formed by the ray through the optical centre.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The same $f=10.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ lens now views a real object $0.200\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall at $p=6.00\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine image position and height using the same sign convention. Explain whether a screen placed at the calculated position can receive it directly from the emerging rays.


$$
\dfrac1q=\dfrac1{10.0}-\dfrac1{6.00}=-\dfrac1{15.0}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}},
\qquad q=-15.0\,\mathrm{cm},
$$
$$
M=-\dfrac{-15.0}{6.00}=2.50,\qquad h_i=0.500\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

![Diverging rays and extensions towards the virtual image at minus fifteen centimetres.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/radiazioni-e-ottica/lens-virtual-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The object lies between the lens and its front focus. Predict a virtual, upright, enlarged image: the denominator $p-f$ in $q=\dfrac{fp}{p-f}$ must be negative. The value $q=-15.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ places the image on the object side, while $M>0$ confirms its upright orientation.

Actual rays diverge after the lens. Their backward extensions, dashed in the diagram, intersect at the virtual image tip. A screen there does not receive a real convergence from the emerging rays; an eye or a subsequent optical system can receive them and form another image. Comparing the two exercises shows that a converging lens does not always produce a real image: object position relative to the focus matters.

</div>
