## Exercise 1

Two samples both have activity $1000\,\mathrm{Bq}$. The first radionuclide has half-life $1.00\,\mathrm h$, the second $10.0\,\mathrm h$. Determine the numbers of nuclei present and explain why equal activities do not imply equal emitted power.


$$
N=\dfrac{\mathscr A T_{1/2}}{\ln2},\qquad
N_1=\dfrac{1000\cdot3600}{\ln2}\simeq5.19\cdot10^6,
$$
$$
N_2=10N_1=\dfrac{1000\cdot36000}{\ln2}\simeq5.19\cdot10^7.
$$

![Activity against population for two decay constants.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/radiazioni-e-ottica/activity-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The becquerel uses seconds, so convert both half-lives to seconds before multiplying by activity. The slower radionuclide requires more nuclei to produce the same mean number of decays per second. The ratio can be predicted without arithmetic: at fixed activity, $N$ is proportional to half-life.

If population had instead been held fixed, the shorter-lived sample would have had greater activity. These are different comparisons. Converting activity to power would require mean energy emitted per decay in the channels considered, which is not supplied. The straight-line graph shows the same population–activity relation using shorter illustrative half-lives.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In $60.0\,\mathrm s$, a detector records $900$ counts with the source and $120$ background counts in a separate measurement of equal duration. The observed emission occurs with probability $b=0.650$ per decay and is recorded with efficiency $\eta=0.100$. Estimate activity, assuming stable background, a half-life much longer than the measurement and negligible dead-time losses.


$$
R_{\mathrm{net}}=\dfrac{900-120}{60.0\,\mathrm s}=13.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},
\qquad
\mathscr A=\dfrac{R_{\mathrm{net}}}{\eta b}=200\,\mathrm{Bq}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equal durations allow direct subtraction of counts before division by time. With unequal durations, subtract count rates instead. The value $13.0\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ describes net recordings, not all decays: only a fraction $\eta b=0.0650$ produces a count in the selected channel.

Dividing by that fraction reconstructs source activity. A forward check gives $200\cdot0.0650\cdot60=780$ expected net counts; adding $120$ recovers $900$. This is a central estimate: real measurements fluctuate, and no uncertainty interval is calculated here. Ignoring background would overestimate activity; ignoring efficiency and emission probability would underestimate it.

</div>
