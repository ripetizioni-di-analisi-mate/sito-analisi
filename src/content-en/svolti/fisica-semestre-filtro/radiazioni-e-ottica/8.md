## Exercise 1

An ideal target requires $10.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ for ionization from a specified state. Using $hc\simeq1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}$, determine the maximum wavelength reaching the threshold in single-photon absorption. Compare $100$ and $200\,\mathrm{nm}$. What changes if power at $200\,\mathrm{nm}$ increases one hundredfold over the same area?


$$
\lambda_{0,\max}=\dfrac{1240}{10.0}\,\mathrm{nm}=124\,\mathrm{nm},
\qquad E_{100}=12.4\,\mathrm{eV},\qquad E_{200}=6.20\,\mathrm{eV}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The energy inequality is $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_0}\ge10.0\,\mathrm{eV}$, so the threshold corresponds to a maximum wavelength, not a minimum. The $100\,\mathrm{nm}$ photon has sufficient energy; the $200\,\mathrm{nm}$ photon does not for the specified state.

At fixed frequency, increasing power one hundredfold increases photon rate and intensity over the stated area one hundredfold. Individual energy remains $6.20\,\mathrm{eV}$. The model excludes multiphoton processes and changes in initial state: energies of photons absorbed by different targets cannot automatically be added as though they formed one event. Above threshold, interaction probability is still needed; below threshold, other effects such as excitation or heating remain possible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal source maintains activity $1.00\,\mathrm{MBq}$ for $20.0\,\mathrm s$ and emits one $100\,\mathrm{keV}$ photon per decay. A fraction $25.0\%$ of the emitted energy is deposited in a mass of $0.0200\,\mathrm{kg}$. Calculate absorbed energy and average dose. Use $1\,\mathrm{eV}=1.602\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm J$.


$$
N_\gamma=\mathscr A\Delta t=2.00\cdot10^7,\qquad
E_\gamma=1.602\cdot10^{-14}\,\mathrm J,
$$
$$
E_{\mathrm{ass}}=0.250N_\gamma E_\gamma=8.01\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm J,
\qquad
D=\dfrac{E_{\mathrm{ass}}}{m}\simeq4.01\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Gy}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The calculation has three physically distinct steps: activity and time give decay number; energy per emission gives emitted energy; the deposited fraction selects the energy entering the mass budget. One photon per decay makes photon and decay counts equal.

Divide by mass in kilograms only at the end. Dose is approximately $4.01\,\mathrm{\mu Gy}$, not $4.01\,\mathrm{Gy}$: the micro prefix is essential. The supplied $25.0\%$ fraction summarizes geometry and absorption; it is not inferred from activity. Without it, dose could not be determined. This is an ideal average energy budget, not a prediction of a biological outcome.

</div>
