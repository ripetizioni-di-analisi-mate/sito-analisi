## Exercise 1

Light with vacuum wavelength $600\,\mathrm{nm}$ passes from air, approximated as index one, into water with $n=\dfrac{4}{3}$. Incidence angle from the normal is $45.0^\circ$. Find reflection angle, refraction angle, frequency and wavelength in water. Use $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.


$$
\theta_r=45.0^\circ,\qquad
\sin\theta_t=\dfrac34\sin45^\circ,\qquad\theta_t\simeq32.0^\circ.
$$
$$
\nu=5.00\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz},\qquad
\lambda_2=\dfrac{600\,\mathrm{nm}}{\dfrac{4}{3}}=450\,\mathrm{nm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Draw the normal before applying either law. Reflection preserves the angle in the incident medium, whereas refraction involves both indices. Since water has a higher index than air, the transmitted ray must bend towards the normal. The value $32.0^\circ$ satisfies that prediction and immediately exposes an accidentally inverted index ratio.

Frequency is fixed by the source and remains unchanged at the stationary interface. Wavelength in water decreases because speed is $\dfrac{3c}{4}$. Checking $\lambda_2\nu=2.25\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}=\dfrac{c}{\dfrac43}$ verifies both conversion and refraction. The theoretical air–glass diagram uses a different index but the same geometrical construction and angular references.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ray inside glass with $n_1=1.50$ reaches a surface bordering air with $n_2=1.00$. Compare incidence angles $40.0^\circ$ and $50.0^\circ$. Then replace air with a liquid of index $1.33$: does the $50.0^\circ$ ray still undergo total internal reflection?


$$
\theta_c=\arcsin\dfrac{1.00}{1.50}\simeq41.8^\circ,
\qquad\theta_t(40^\circ)=\arcsin(1.50\sin40^\circ)\simeq74.6^\circ.
$$
$$
\theta_c'=\arcsin\dfrac{1.33}{1.50}\simeq62.5^\circ.
$$

![Incidence below, at and above the critical angle from glass to air.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/radiazioni-e-ottica/total-reflection-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Propagation is from higher to lower index, a necessary condition for a critical angle. At $40.0^\circ$, incidence is below threshold: a refracted ray exists far from the normal. At $50.0^\circ$, Snell would require a sine greater than one, so total internal reflection occurs in the ray model.

With the liquid, the index ratio increases and the critical angle rises to about $62.5^\circ$. Incidence at $50.0^\circ$ is now below the limit, so total internal reflection no longer occurs. This comparison shows why optical guiding also depends on the external material. At the exact critical angle the refracted ray would be grazing; total internal reflection requires an angle strictly above that value.

</div>
