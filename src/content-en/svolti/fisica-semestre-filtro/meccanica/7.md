## Exercise 1

Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A spring with negligible mass remains in the linear elastic regime. A body of mass $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ suspended vertically extends it by $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at equilibrium. Find the spring constant and the mass that produces extension $6.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ on the same spring.

$$
k\,x_1 - m_1 g = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k = \dfrac{m_1 g}{x_1}
$$
$$
k = \dfrac{0.80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}}{0.040\,\mathrm{m}} \approx 196\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
k\,x_2 = m_2 g
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
m_2 = \dfrac{k\,x_2}{g} = m_1\,\dfrac{x_2}{x_1} = 0.80\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot \dfrac{6.5}{4.0} = 1.3\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equilibrium the body experiences downward weight and upward elastic force, whose magnitude is $kx$ by **Hooke's law**. Net force is zero, giving $kx=mg$, and the measured extension gives $k\approx 196\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Extensions must be expressed in metres because spring constant is measured in $\mathrm{N/m}$.

The same equilibrium condition applies with the second mass. Extension is proportional to the suspended weight, so the mass ratio equals the extension ratio and the required mass is $1.3\,\mathrm{kg}$. This second calculation does not require the numerical value of $k$. It is the operating principle of a spring balance, comparing an unknown weight with a known one through their extensions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two ideal springs of negligible mass, remaining in the linear elastic regime, have constants $k_1=300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $k_2=600\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and suspend a body of mass $m=3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$. Find the total equilibrium extension with springs in series and in parallel, the latter arranged so that both extend equally. For the series connection, also find each spring's extension. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
k_{\text{series}} = \dfrac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2} = \dfrac{300 \cdot 600}{900}\,\mathrm{N/m} = 200\,\mathrm{N/m},
\qquad
k_{\text{parallel}} = k_1 + k_2 = 900\,\mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
m g = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 29.43\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
x_{\text{series}} = \dfrac{m g}{k_{\text{series}}} = \dfrac{29.43\,\mathrm{N}}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}} \approx 14.7\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
x_{\text{parallel}} = \dfrac{m g}{k_{\text{parallel}}} = \dfrac{29.43\,\mathrm{N}}{900\,\mathrm{N/m}} = 3.27\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x_1 = \dfrac{m g}{k_1} = 9.81\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
x_2 = \dfrac{m g}{k_2} \approx 4.91\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In series, both springs carry the same force and their extensions add: $x=\dfrac{F}{k_1}+\dfrac{F}{k_2}$. In parallel, extension is shared and forces add: $F=k_1x+k_2x$. Thus each arrangement can be replaced by the equivalent spring given by the **series and parallel spring rules**. In series the reciprocal equivalent constant is the sum of reciprocals, giving $k_{\text{series}}=200\,\mathrm{N/m}$, below both individual constants. In parallel the constants add, giving $k_{\text{parallel}}=900\,\mathrm{N/m}$. At equilibrium the equivalent spring supports the weight, giving extensions $14.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $3.27\,\mathrm{cm}$.

In series the springs have negligible mass, so each carries the body's entire weight, $29.4\,\mathrm{N}$. The softer spring extends $9.81\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the other $4.91\,\mathrm{cm}$; their sum $14.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ agrees with the equivalent-spring result. In parallel both have equal extension and share weight in proportion to their constants: the first supports $300\cdot0.0327\,\mathrm{N}=9.81\,\mathrm{N}$ and the second twice that force.

</div>
