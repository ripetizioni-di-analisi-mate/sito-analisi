## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $m=40\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a horizontal floor, with friction coefficients $\mu_s=0.50$ and $\mu_d=0.35$. Determine whether a horizontal push of $150\,\mathrm{N}$ starts it moving. Find the push threshold above which rest is impossible and its acceleration while sliding if the push is maintained at the exact threshold value. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
N = m g = 40\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} = 392.4\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
f_{s,\max} = \mu_s N = 0.50 \cdot 392.4\,\mathrm{N} = 196.2\,\mathrm{N} > 150\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_* = \mu_s N = 196.2\,\mathrm{N},
\qquad
f_d = \mu_d N = 0.35 \cdot 392.4\,\mathrm{N} = 137.34\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
a = \dfrac{F_* - \mu_d N}{m} = \dfrac{(\mu_s - \mu_d)\,m g}{m} = (\mu_s - \mu_d)\,g = 0.15 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 1.47\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The crate has no vertical acceleration, so normal force equals weight, $392\,\mathrm{N}$. Static friction can take any magnitude up to $\mu_sN=196.2\,\mathrm{N}$. With a $150\,\mathrm{N}$ push, equilibrium requires static friction $150\,\mathrm{N}$, below the maximum; therefore the crate **remains at rest** and friction is exactly $150\,\mathrm{N}$.

In the ideal model rest is still possible at the threshold $F_*=196.2\,\mathrm{N}$; it is impossible if $F>F_*$. Once sliding has started, kinetic friction is $137.34\,\mathrm{N}$. Maintaining $F=F_*$ then gives a positive resultant and $a\approx 1.47\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The expression $(\mu_s-\mu_d)g$ is independent of mass; this change of regime explains why the force needed to maintain uniform motion is smaller than the threshold for starting it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A hockey puck is launched at initial speed $v_0=12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and stops after travelling $d=40\,\mathrm{m}$. Assuming kinetic friction is the only horizontal force, find its constant coefficient and the duration of motion on horizontal ice. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
-\mu_d N = m\,a \\[4pt]
N - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = -\mu_d\,g
$$
$$
0 = v_0^2 - 2\,\mu_d\,g\,d
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu_d = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,g\,d},
\qquad
t = \dfrac{v_0}{\mu_d\,g} = \dfrac{2\,d}{v_0}
$$
$$
\mu_d = \dfrac{(12\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 40\,\mathrm{m}} = \dfrac{144}{784.8} \approx 0.18,
\qquad
t = \dfrac{2 \cdot 40\,\mathrm{m}}{12\,\mathrm{m/s}} \approx 6.7\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The puck slides, so friction is kinetic, with magnitude $\mu_dN$ and direction opposite to velocity. Vertically $N=mg$, hence horizontal acceleration is $a=-\mu_dg$, constant and independent of puck mass, which is therefore not required. Motion is uniformly decelerated until stopping; the same acceleration must not be extended beyond stopping because friction does not launch the puck backwards.

Imposing zero velocity after distance $d$, the velocity–displacement relation gives $\mu_d=0.18$. Duration follows by dividing distance by average velocity, which for uniform deceleration to rest is $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$, giving $t\approx 6.7\,\mathrm{s}$. The coefficient is dimensionless, as required, because $\dfrac{v_0^2}{gd}$ is a ratio of two quantities both measured in $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$.

</div>
