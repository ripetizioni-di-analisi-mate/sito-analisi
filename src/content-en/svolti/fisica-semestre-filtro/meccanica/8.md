## Exercise 1

A stone is thrown from the top of a tower of height $h=15\,\mathrm{m}$ at speed $v_0=8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and negligible air resistance, find its speed on reaching the ground and whether this depends on launch direction. Assume the stone reaches the ground without hitting obstacles.

Choose zero potential energy at ground level and include the interaction with Earth. The only force on the stone is conservative gravity.

$$
\dfrac12mv_0^2+mgh=\dfrac12mv^2,
\qquad v=\sqrt{v_0^2+2gh}.
$$
$$
v=\sqrt{64+294.3}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx18.9\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

This balance compares two states and does not require flight time. Mass cancels because every term is proportional to it. Kinetic energy contains magnitude $v_0$, not angle: impact speed is therefore the same for every launch direction allowed by the problem. Trajectory, flight time, landing point and final velocity direction do change. The result exceeds $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ because the decrease in potential energy adds to initial kinetic energy. In the limit $v_0=0$, it reduces to $v=\sqrt{2gh}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ block begins sliding with negligible initial speed on a fixed straight ramp of length $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and vertical drop $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Kinetic friction coefficient is $\mu_d=0.25$; contact persists and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find friction work and velocity at the bottom.

The ramp is straight, so $\sin\theta=\dfrac35$ and $\cos\theta=\dfrac45$. Set $U_g=0$ at the bottom. The onset of sliding is given: it must not be inferred from the kinetic coefficient.

$$
N=mg\cos\theta=8\,\mathrm{N},\qquad
f_d=\mu_dN=2\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
$$
W_d=-f_ds=-10\,\mathrm{J},\qquad
K_f=mgh+W_d=30\,\mathrm{J}-10\,\mathrm{J}=20\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
$$
v_f=\sqrt{\dfrac{2K_f}{m}}=\sqrt{40}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx6.32\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

![Initial energy 30 J, final kinetic energy 20 J and internal energy 10 J.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/energie-en.svg)

*The final bar distinguishes kinetic energy from energy transferred into internal forms.*

<div class="spiegazione">

Friction acts over the full length $5.0\,\mathrm{m}$, not the vertical drop: confusing them would give incorrect work. Gravity does $30\,\mathrm{J}$, friction $-10\,\mathrm{J}$ and the normal force zero work. Total work is therefore $20\,\mathrm{J}$, equal to the kinetic-energy increase. A dynamics check gives $a=g(0.6-0.25\cdot0.8)=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $v_f^2=2as=40\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Lost mechanical energy increases the internal energy of the block and plane; total energy does not disappear.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart moves on a smooth horizontal track. At $x=0$ its velocity is $+1.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its only horizontal force is $F_x=(4.0\,\mathrm{N/m})x$ for $0\le x\le3.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Find work and velocity at $x=3.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

Weight and normal force do no work. Use the area of the triangular force–position graph without assuming constant acceleration.

$$
F_x(3\,\mathrm{m})=12\,\mathrm{N},\qquad
W=\dfrac12(3\,\mathrm{m})(12\,\mathrm{N})=18\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
$$
K_i=\dfrac12mv_i^2=1\,\mathrm{J},\qquad K_f=K_i+W=19\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
$$
v_f=+\sqrt{\dfrac{2K_f}{m}}=+\sqrt{19}\,\mathrm{m/s}\approx+4.36\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

![Linearly increasing force and triangular area.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/lavoro-en.svg)

*The slope has units N/m; the area has units J.*

<div class="spiegazione">

Force increases with $x$: applying $F_x(3\,\mathrm{m})$ over the whole path would double the work. The integral $\int_0^3 4x\,dx$ in SI units confirms $18\,\mathrm{J}$. The work–kinetic energy theorem requires adding this work to the nonzero initial energy $1\,\mathrm{J}$. Final velocity is positive because the cart starts rightwards and the force never slows it in the specified interval. The root choice is thus justified by the motion, not by the square in the energy balance alone.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A hoist raises a $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ load through $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ in $5.0\,\mathrm{s}$, starting and ending at rest. Neglect other dissipation on the load and use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Overall hoist efficiency is $\eta=0.80$. Find useful work, average mechanical power and average input power.

Useful work increases potential energy. Velocity need not be constant at every instant: initial and final kinetic energies merely need to be equal.

$$
W_u=mgh=1000\,\mathrm{J},\qquad
P_{u,\mathrm m}=\dfrac{W_u}{\Delta t}=200\,\mathrm{W}.
$$
$$
E_{\mathrm{in}}=\dfrac{W_u}{\eta}=1250\,\mathrm{J},\qquad
P_{\mathrm{in,m}}=\dfrac{E_{\mathrm{in}}}{\Delta t}=250\,\mathrm{W}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Gravity does $-1000\,\mathrm{J}$ of work, so the hoist must do $+1000\,\mathrm{J}$ on the load for the net kinetic-energy change to be zero. Efficiency is below one: input energy must exceed useful energy, not be smaller. The difference is $250\,\mathrm{J}$, corresponding to an average $50\,\mathrm{W}$. The data determine average powers but not peak motor power, which would require the time profile of the lift.

</div>
