## Exercise 1

A rigid horizontal forearm of negligible weight supports a $40\,\mathrm{N}$ load at $0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ from the elbow. A tendon exerts an upward vertical force with moment arm $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$. The segment is in static equilibrium. Find tendon force and the elbow's vertical reaction on the segment.

Consider the forearm alone, choosing the elbow as pivot and anticlockwise torques positive. Elbow reaction has no torque about this pivot.

$$
F_m(0.05\,\mathrm{m})-(40\,\mathrm{N})(0.30\,\mathrm{m})=0.
$$
$$
F_m=240\,\mathrm{N},\qquad R_y+F_m-40\,\mathrm{N}=0,
\qquad R_y=-200\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

![Upward muscle force, downward load and joint reaction.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/leva-en.svg)

*Both moment arms are measured from the elbow to the forces' lines of action.*

<div class="spiegazione">

The reaction disappears from the torque equation but not from the force equation. The tendon moment arm is six times shorter than the load arm, so it must exert six times the force. Negative vertical reaction means $200\,\mathrm{N}$ downwards on the segment; this is not an error because a constrained joint is not a simple unilateral support. Checking, both opposing torques have magnitude $12\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ and the vertical force sum is $-200+240-40=0$ in newtons. The model excludes forearm weight and other muscle forces.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform horizontal beam of length $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ weighs $100\,\mathrm{N}$ and is supported at its two ends $A$ and $B$. A $60\,\mathrm{N}$ downward point load acts $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Find the vertical support reactions in static equilibrium.

The beam weight acts at its centre, $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Choosing $A$ as pivot eliminates the torque of $R_A$.

$$
R_B(4.0\,\mathrm{m})-(100\,\mathrm{N})(2.0\,\mathrm{m})
-(60\,\mathrm{N})(3.0\,\mathrm{m})=0.
$$
$$
R_B=95\,\mathrm{N},\qquad
R_A=100\,\mathrm{N}+60\,\mathrm{N}-R_B=65\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
$$
\sum\tau_B=-(65\,\mathrm{N})(4\,\mathrm{m})
+(100\,\mathrm{N})(2\,\mathrm{m})+(60\,\mathrm{N})(1\,\mathrm{m})=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force sum alone gives $R_A+R_B=160\,\mathrm{N}$, one equation for two unknowns. Torque equilibrium supplies information about load distribution. Support $B$ carries more force because the extra load is closer to it; automatically dividing the total by two would be incorrect. Both reactions are positive and therefore compatible with supports that can only push upwards. Checking torques about $B$ confirms the result obtained using $A$.

</div>
