## Exercise 1

A ball is kicked from ground level at speed $v_0=20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, at angle $\theta=30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance and ball size. Calculate flight time, maximum height and range, landing at launch height.

Place the origin at launch and take $y$ upwards.

$$
v_{0x} = v_0\cos\theta \approx 17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}, \qquad v_{0y} = v_0\sin\theta = 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
T = \dfrac{2\,v_{0y}}{g}, \qquad H = \dfrac{v_{0y}^2}{2g}, \qquad R = v_{0x}\,T = \dfrac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta}{g}
$$
$$
T = \dfrac{2 \cdot 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} \approx 2.04\,\mathrm{s}, \qquad H = \dfrac{(10.0\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} \approx 5.10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
R = \dfrac{(20\,\mathrm{m/s})^2 \sin 60^\circ}{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} \approx 35.3\,\mathrm{m}
$$

![Parabolic ball trajectory with apex at half the range.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/proiettile-en.svg)

*Spatial axes use the same scale: the shape represents the specified launch.*

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball can be modelled as a particle and air does not slow it down, so the **projectile position equations** apply. Resolve initial velocity into two components: the horizontal component remains constant during flight, whereas the vertical component determines how high the ball rises and how long it remains airborne. Landing is at launch height, allowing the **flight-time and range expressions**; maximum height uses only the vertical component. Flight lasts $2.04\,\mathrm{s}$, maximum height is $5.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and range is $35.3\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, $v_{0x}T\approx 17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot2.04\,\mathrm{s}\approx 35.3\,\mathrm{m}$, agreeing with the expression involving $\sin2\theta$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A projectile launched from the ground at speed $v_0=25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ must land $R=50\,\mathrm{m}$ from launch on level ground. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and negligible air resistance, determine the possible launch angles and corresponding flight times.

$$
R = \dfrac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta}{g} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin 2\theta = \dfrac{g\,R}{v_0^2}
$$
$$
\sin 2\theta = \dfrac{9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 50\,\mathrm{m}}{(25\,\mathrm{m/s})^2} \approx 0.785
$$
$$
2\theta \approx 51.7^\circ \ \text{ or } \ 2\theta = 180^\circ - 51.7^\circ \approx 128.3^\circ
$$
$$
\theta_1 \approx 25.9^\circ, \qquad \theta_2 \approx 64.1^\circ
$$
$$
T = \dfrac{2\,v_0\sin\theta}{g}, \qquad T_1 = \dfrac{2 \cdot 25 \cdot \sin 25.9^\circ}{9.81}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.22\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
T_2 = \dfrac{2 \cdot 25 \cdot \sin 64.1^\circ}{9.81}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 4.59\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Landing is at launch height, so range is $R=\dfrac{v_0^2\sin2\theta}{g}$, with angle the only unknown. The value $\dfrac{gR}{v_0^2}\approx 0.785$ is below $1$, so the target is reachable. The equation $\sin2\theta\approx 0.785$ has two supplementary solutions with $0^\circ<2\theta<180^\circ$. The launch angles are therefore **complementary**, $25.9^\circ$ and $64.1^\circ$, as expected from the range expression. The $64.1^\circ$ launch has a larger vertical velocity component, giving a longer flight, $4.59\,\mathrm{s}$ versus $2.22\,\mathrm{s}$. If $\dfrac{gR}{v_0^2}>1$, no solution would exist because the maximum range $\dfrac{v_0^2}{g}=63.7\,\mathrm{m}$ would be smaller than $R$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball of mass $m=0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides without friction on a horizontal table. An ideal string constrains it to a circle of radius $r=0.80\,\mathrm{m}$; its period is $T=0.40\pi\,\mathrm{s}$. Find speed, angular velocity, acceleration and string tension.

Study the ball in the table frame, assumed inertial. Weight and normal force balance; the only horizontal force is string tension, directed towards the centre.

$$
\omega=\dfrac{2\pi}{T}=5\,\mathrm{rad/s},\qquad
v=\omega r=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
a_c=\dfrac{v^2}{r}=20\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\qquad
F_T=ma_c=4\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

![Tangential velocity and inward acceleration.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/circolare-en.svg)

*The string provides the radial resultant; vertical forces balance.*

<div class="spiegazione">

The ball travels $2\pi r$ in one period: circumference divided by $T$ again gives $v=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Constant speed does not imply zero acceleration because velocity direction changes. Tension is the actual force providing the centripetal resultant; adding a second centripetal force would count the same interaction twice. Acceleration is always radially inward, while velocity is tangent. The units of $\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ are $\mathrm{m/s^2}$; multiplying by mass gives newtons.

</div>
