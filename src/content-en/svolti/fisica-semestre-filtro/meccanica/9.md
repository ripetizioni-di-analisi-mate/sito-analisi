## Exercise 1

On a straight horizontal track with negligible friction, cart $A$ of mass $m_A=2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ moves at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ rightwards and cart $B$ of mass $m_B=1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ leftwards. They collide and stay coupled. Find final velocity and dissipated kinetic energy.

Choose rightwards positive: $v_A=+3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_B=-3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The system includes both carts; horizontal external forces are negligible.

$$
m_Av_A+m_Bv_B=(m_A+m_B)V,
\qquad V=\dfrac{2\cdot3+1\cdot(-3)}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}=1\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
K_i=\dfrac12m_Av_A^2+\dfrac12m_Bv_B^2=13.5\,\mathrm{J}.
$$
$$
K_f=\dfrac12(m_A+m_B)V^2=1.5\,\mathrm{J},\qquad
K_i-K_f=12\,\mathrm{J}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The collision is completely inelastic because the bodies remain joined. The system conserves horizontal momentum, whereas kinetic energy can decrease through deformation and internal-energy changes. Initial momenta have opposite signs: adding their magnitudes would incorrectly give $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The positive result means rightward motion, the direction of the larger initial momentum magnitude. The loss is approximately $89\,\%$ of initial energy. An independent check is $\dfrac12\dfrac{m_Am_B}{m_A+m_B}(v_A-v_B)^2=12\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A projectile of mass $m=8.0\,\mathrm{g}$ embeds horizontally in a stationary block of mass $M=1.6\,\mathrm{kg}$, suspended by two ideal parallel vertical strings that prevent rotation. The collision is so brief that external horizontal impulse is negligible. The system then rises by $h=12\,\mathrm{cm}$ without dissipation. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find initial projectile speed and the fraction of kinetic energy lost in the collision.

Convert to $m=0.0080\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $h=0.12\,\mathrm{m}$. Distinguish speed $v$ before impact from common speed $V$ immediately afterwards.

$$
mv=(m+M)V,\qquad
\dfrac12(m+M)V^2=(m+M)gh.
$$
$$
V=\sqrt{2gh}\approx1.534\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
v=\dfrac{m+M}{m}\sqrt{2gh}\approx308\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
\dfrac{K_i-K_f}{K_i}=1-\dfrac{m}{m+M}
=\dfrac{1.6}{1.608}\approx0.995.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two equations apply to different phases. During embedding, horizontal momentum is approximately conserved, not kinetic energy; the brief collision allows the strings' departure from vertical to be neglected. During the subsequent rise, the block does not rotate, tensions do no work and mechanical energy is conserved. Maximum height first determines $V$, then the momentum balance determines $v$. The collision loses $99.5\,\%$ of initial kinetic energy. Imposing energy conservation from the freely moving projectile to maximum height would give about $22\,\mathrm{m/s}$: it would ignore the energy converted during embedding.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ ball hits a horizontal floor with vertical velocity $6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ downwards and rebounds at $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ upwards. Contact lasts $0.10\,\mathrm{s}$. Neglect air resistance and use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to find net impulse and the floor's average force on the ball.

Choose upwards positive. Apply the impulse–momentum theorem to the ball, including both contact and weight.

$$
J_y=m(v_f-v_i)=0.50[4-(-6)]\,\mathrm{N\,s}=5\,\mathrm{N\,s}.
$$
$$
\overline F_{\mathrm{tot},y}=\dfrac{J_y}{\Delta t}=50\,\mathrm{N},\qquad
\overline N-mg=50\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
$$
\overline N=55\,\mathrm{N},\qquad
J_N=\overline N\Delta t=5.5\,\mathrm{N\,s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity change is $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ because direction reverses: subtracting speeds alone would give $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and an incorrect balance. The $50\,\mathrm{N}$ is average resultant force, not the floor force. During contact, weight provides impulse $-mg\Delta t=-0.5\,\mathrm{N\,s}$; adding it to floor impulse $+5.5\,\mathrm{N\,s}$ recovers net impulse $+5\,\mathrm{N\,s}$. The data do not determine peak force: many time profiles can have the same area.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A person, modelled as a point of mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$, walks from one end to the other of a uniform cart of length $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and mass $20\,\mathrm{kg}$. The system is initially at rest and horizontal external forces are negligible. Initially the cart centre is at $x=0$ and the person is at the left end. Find the ground-frame displacements of the cart and person.

Include the person in the system. Their displacement relative to the cart is $+4.0\,\mathrm{m}$; relative to the ground it is instead $\Delta x_p=\Delta x_c+4.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
20\Delta x_c+60\Delta x_p=0.
$$
$$
20\Delta x_c+60(\Delta x_c+4.0\,\mathrm{m})=0,
\qquad\Delta x_c=-3.0\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
$$
\Delta x_p=+1.0\,\mathrm{m},\qquad
x_{\mathrm{CM},i}=\dfrac{20\cdot0+60\cdot(-2)}{80}\,\mathrm{m}=-1.5\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
$$
x_{\mathrm{CM},f}=\dfrac{20\cdot(-3)+60\cdot(-1)}{80}\,\mathrm{m}=-1.5\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal centre of mass remains stationary because external resultant force is zero and the system starts with zero velocity. Friction between feet and cart is internal and sets the parts moving without shifting the overall centre of mass. The cart moves back $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$, so the person advances only $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to the ground. Checking final positions confirms unchanged centre of mass. Kinetic-energy conservation is not assumed: muscular activity can transform internal energy.

</div>
