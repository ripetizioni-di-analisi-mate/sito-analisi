## Exercise 1

A cyclist travels the first $12\,\mathrm{km}$ along a straight road at a constant $24\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and, continuing in the same direction, the next $12\,\mathrm{km}$ at a constant $16\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Find the average velocity for the entire journey, in $\mathrm{km/h}$ and $\mathrm{m/s}$.

Orient the axis along the motion. The cyclist never reverses direction, so total displacement is $2d$, with $d=12\,\mathrm{km}$.

$$
t_1 = \dfrac{d}{v_1},
\qquad
t_2 = \dfrac{d}{v_2},
\qquad
v_m = \dfrac{2d}{t_1 + t_2}
$$
$$
v_m = \dfrac{2d}{\dfrac{d}{v_1} + \dfrac{d}{v_2}} = \dfrac{2\,v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}
$$
$$
v_m = \dfrac{2 \cdot 24 \cdot 16}{24 + 16}\,\mathrm{km/h} = 19.2\,\mathrm{km/h} = \dfrac{19.2}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 5.33\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Motion is uniform on each section, so the time taken is length divided by speed: $t_1=0.50\,\mathrm{h}$ and $t_2=0.75\,\mathrm{h}$. **Average velocity** is total displacement divided by total time, hence $\dfrac{24\,\mathrm{km}}{1.25\,\mathrm{h}}$. Since both sections have equal length, $d$ cancels and the result depends only on the two speeds. Average velocity is $19.2\,\mathrm{km/h}$, or $5.33\,\mathrm{m/s}$, less than the arithmetic mean $20\,\mathrm{km/h}$ because more time is spent on the slower section. As a check, the result lies between the two speeds and is closer to the smaller one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On a straight road, car $A$ starts from $P$ towards $Q$ at a constant $90\,\mathrm{km/h}$. At the same instant, car $B$ starts from $Q$, $1200\,\mathrm{m}$ from $P$, travelling towards $A$ at a constant $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Find when they meet and their distance from $P$ at that instant.

Choose the origin at $P$, positive $x$ from $P$ towards $Q$, and $t=0$ when both start. Let $v_A=25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_B=20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ denote their speeds, and $D=1200\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
x_A(t) = v_A\,t,
\qquad
x_B(t) = D - v_B\,t
$$
$$
x_A(t) = x_B(t)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v_A\,t = D - v_B\,t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t = \dfrac{D}{v_A + v_B}
$$
$$
x = v_A\,t = \dfrac{v_A\,D}{v_A + v_B}
$$
$$
t = \dfrac{1200\,\mathrm{m}}{45\,\mathrm{m/s}} \approx 26.67\,\mathrm{s} \approx 26.7\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
x = 25\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 26.67\,\mathrm{s} \approx 667\,\mathrm{m}
$$

![Intersection of two position–time lines at 26.7 s and 667 m.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/incontro-en.svg)

*The slopes have opposite signs; the intersection identifies the meeting position and time.*

<div class="spiegazione">

Describe both cars in the same reference frame. With the axis directed from $P$ to $Q$, car $B$ moves in the negative direction, so its velocity is $-v_B$ and its position function is $D-v_Bt$. The cars meet when they have the same position: impose $x_A(t)=x_B(t)$, a first-degree equation in $t$. The solution $t=\dfrac{D}{v_A+v_B}$ shows that the initial separation closes at speed $v_A+v_B$. The cars **meet** after $26.7\,\mathrm{s}$ at $667\,\mathrm{m}$ from $P$. Checking the other car gives $x_B=1200\,\mathrm{m}-20\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot26.67\,\mathrm{s}\approx 667\,\mathrm{m}$, the same position.

</div>
