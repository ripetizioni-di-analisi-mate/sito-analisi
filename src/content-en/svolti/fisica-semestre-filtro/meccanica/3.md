## Exercise 1

A stone is dropped from rest from a bridge and reaches the water after $2.5\,\mathrm{s}$. Neglect air resistance and use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to calculate the bridge height above the water and the stone's impact velocity.

Choose downward $y$ with origin at the release point, giving $a=+g$. The data are $v_0=0$ and $t=2.5\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
y(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2,
\qquad
v(t) = g\,t
$$
$$
h = \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (2.5\,\mathrm{s})^2 \approx 30.7\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
v = g\,t = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{s} \approx 24.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With negligible air resistance, the **free-fall model** gives constant downward acceleration $g$. Since the axis points downwards, acceleration is positive and the uniformly accelerated equations with $v_0=0$ and $y_0=0$ reduce to $y=\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$ and $v=gt$. The bridge is $30.7\,\mathrm{m}$ high and the stone reaches the water at $24.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ downwards. As a check, $v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2\cdot9.81\cdot30.7}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball is thrown vertically upwards from the ground at $19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglect air resistance and use $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find its maximum height, its return time to the ground, and the times when it is at height $15\,\mathrm{m}$, together with the corresponding velocities.

Choose upward $y$ with origin at ground level and $t=0$ at launch. The data are $y_0=0$, $v_0=19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $h=15\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
y(t) = v_0\,t - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2,
\qquad
v(t) = v_0 - g\,t
$$
$$
t_s = \dfrac{v_0}{g} = \dfrac{19.6\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
h_{\max} = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2g} = \dfrac{(19.6\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 19.6\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
y(t) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t \left( v_0 - \dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t \right) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t_v = \dfrac{2\,v_0}{g} = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\dfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2 - v_0\,t + h = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
t_{1,2} = \dfrac{v_0 \mp \sqrt{v_0^2 - 2gh}}{g}
$$
$$
\sqrt{v_0^2 - 2gh} = \sqrt{384.16 - 294}\ \mathrm{m/s} \approx 9.50\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
t_1 = \dfrac{19.6 - 9.50}{9.8}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 1.03\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
t_2 = \dfrac{19.6 + 9.50}{9.8}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.97\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v(t_{1,2}) = v_0 - g\,t_{1,2} = \pm\sqrt{v_0^2 - 2gh} \approx \pm\, 9.50\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

![Height as a function of time: 15 m crossed at 1.03 s and 2.97 s.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/lancio-verticale-en.svg)

*The two intersections with the specified height are symmetric about the apex time. This is a height–time graph, not the spatial trajectory.*

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the **vertical-throw equations** with upward positive, so acceleration is $-g$. Velocity vanishes at the highest point; the ascent-time and maximum-height expressions give $2.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and $19.6\,\mathrm{m}$. The ball returns to ground when $y(t)=0$. This equation has solution $t=0$, the launch, and $t_v=4.0\,\mathrm{s}$, twice the ascent time as expected from symmetry.

The condition $y(t)=15\,\mathrm{m}$ is a quadratic equation with positive discriminant because $15\,\mathrm{m}$ lies below the maximum height. Thus the ball reaches this height **twice**, ascending at $1.03\,\mathrm{s}$ and descending at $2.97\,\mathrm{s}$. Substitution into the velocity equation gives opposite velocities: $+9.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ upwards and $-9.50\,\mathrm{m/s}$ downwards. Their equal magnitudes also follow from $v^2=v_0^2-2gh$, which does not involve time.

</div>
