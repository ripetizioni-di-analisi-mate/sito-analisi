## Exercise 1

A crate of mass $m=25\,\mathrm{kg}$, initially at rest on a smooth horizontal floor, is pushed by a constant horizontal force $F=60\,\mathrm{N}$. Calculate its acceleration, velocity and distance travelled after $t=4.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
F = m\,a \\[4pt]
N - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \dfrac{F}{m},
\qquad
v = a\,t,
\qquad
s = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2
$$
$$
a = \dfrac{60\,\mathrm{N}}{25\,\mathrm{kg}} = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
v = 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{s} = 9.6\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad
s = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \cdot (4.0\,\mathrm{s})^2 = 19.2\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forces on the crate are weight, the floor's normal force and the push. The floor is smooth, so there is no friction and the push is the only horizontal force. Choose $x$ horizontally along the push and $y$ vertically upwards. The crate does not move along $y$, so **Newton's second law** gives $N=mg$; along $x$ it gives $F=ma$, hence acceleration $2.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

Force is constant, so acceleration is also constant and motion is uniformly accelerated from rest. The motion equations give velocity $9.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and distance $19.2\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, average velocity over $4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ is $\dfrac{0+9.6}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and $4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}\cdot4.0\,\mathrm{s}=19.2\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block of mass $m=4.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a smooth horizontal floor and is pulled by a rope exerting force $F=24\,\mathrm{N}$ at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Calculate its acceleration and the floor's normal force. Also find the force at the same angle corresponding to the contact limit $N=0$, and explain what happens above that threshold. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
F \cos 30^\circ = m\,a \\[4pt]
N + F \sin 30^\circ - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = \dfrac{F \cos 30^\circ}{m},
\qquad
N = m g - F \sin 30^\circ
$$
$$
a = \dfrac{24\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.866}{4.0\,\mathrm{kg}} \approx 5.20\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
$$
$$
N = 4.0\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} - 24\,\mathrm{N} \cdot 0.500 = 39.24\,\mathrm{N} - 12.0\,\mathrm{N} \approx 27.2\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
N = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F = \dfrac{m g}{\sin 30^\circ} = \dfrac{39.24\,\mathrm{N}}{0.500} = 78.48\,\mathrm{N} \approx 78.5\,\mathrm{N}
$$

![Three forces on the block with balanced vertical components.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/meccanica/corpo-libero-en.svg)

*The normal force supports the part of the weight not balanced by the pull.*

<div class="spiegazione">

The **free-body diagram** contains three forces: weight vertically downwards, normal force vertically upwards, and rope force. Resolve the rope force along the axes: horizontal component $F\cos30^\circ$ accelerates the block, while vertical component $F\sin30^\circ$ pulls it upwards. The block remains on the floor, so vertical acceleration is zero and vertical components sum to zero.

The vertical equation gives $N=27.2\,\mathrm{N}$, less than weight $39.2\,\mathrm{N}$ because the rope supports part of it. The floor can only push, so its normal force cannot become negative. When the vertical pull equals weight, $N=0$: this is the **contact limit** $F_*=78.48\,\mathrm{N}\approx78.5\,\mathrm{N}$. At the exact value $F_*$ the vertical resultant is still zero; for $F>F_*$ the floor cannot provide a negative normal force and the block accelerates upwards, separating from it.

</div>
