## Exercise 1

A car starts from rest and reaches $100\,\mathrm{km/h}$ in $8.0\,\mathrm{s}$ with constant acceleration. Calculate its acceleration and distance travelled during that time.

Choose the axis along the motion, with origin at the starting point and $t=0$ at departure. The data are $v_0=0$, $v=100\,\mathrm{km/h}\approx 27.78\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $t=8.0\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
v = v_0 + a\,t,
\qquad
x = v_0\,t + \dfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2
$$
$$
a = \dfrac{v}{t},
\qquad
x = \dfrac{1}{2}\,\dfrac{v}{t}\,t^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,v\,t
$$
$$
a = \dfrac{27.78\,\mathrm{m/s}}{8.0\,\mathrm{s}} \approx 3.47\,\mathrm{m/s^2},
\qquad
x = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 27.78\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 8.0\,\mathrm{s} \approx 111\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Acceleration is constant, so the **uniformly accelerated motion equations** apply with $v_0=0$. The velocity equation gives $a=\dfrac{v}{t}$ after converting speed into $\mathrm{m/s}$ by dividing by $3.6$. Substituting $a$ into the position equation gives distance $\dfrac{1}{2}vt$, the product of average velocity $\dfrac{v}{2}$ and time. The car accelerates at $3.47\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and travels $111\,\mathrm{m}$. The order of magnitude is reasonable: acceleration is approximately one third of $g$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car is travelling at $90\,\mathrm{km/h}$ when its driver sees a stationary obstacle. The driver reacts after $0.80\,\mathrm{s}$, during which the car continues at constant speed, then brakes with constant deceleration of magnitude $5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate stopping distance and stopping time. If the obstacle is $70\,\mathrm{m}$ away, find the velocity at impact.

Orient the axis along the motion and place the origin where the driver sees the obstacle. The data are $v_0=25\,\mathrm{m/s}$, $t_r=0.80\,\mathrm{s}$ and $a=-5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
d_r = v_0\,t_r,
\qquad
0 = v_0^2 + 2\,a\,d_f
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d_f = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2\,|a|},
\qquad
t_f = \dfrac{v_0}{|a|}
$$
$$
d_r = 25\,\mathrm{m/s} \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{s} = 20\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
d_f = \dfrac{(25\,\mathrm{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 5.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}} = 62.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
d = d_r + d_f = 82.5\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
t = t_r + t_f = 0.80\,\mathrm{s} + 5.0\,\mathrm{s} = 5.8\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v_1^2 = v_0^2 + 2\,a\,(L - d_r)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v_1 = \sqrt{v_0^2 - 2\,|a|\,(L - d_r)}
$$
$$
v_1 = \sqrt{625 - 2 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 50}\ \mathrm{m/s} = \sqrt{125}\ \mathrm{m/s} \approx 11.2\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are two phases. During reaction time, velocity is constant, so distance is $v_0t_r$. During braking, acceleration is constant. Since time is not given and is unnecessary for finding distance, use the **velocity–displacement relation** with zero final velocity. Braking time follows from the velocity equation by imposing $v=0$. Stopping distance is $82.5\,\mathrm{m}$ and stopping time is $5.8\,\mathrm{s}$.

The obstacle at $70\,\mathrm{m}$ lies closer than the stopping distance, so the car reaches it while still braking, after travelling $L-d_r=50\,\mathrm{m}$ in the braking phase. The same relation with displacement $50\,\mathrm{m}$ gives impact velocity $11.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, approximately $40\,\mathrm{km/h}$.

</div>
