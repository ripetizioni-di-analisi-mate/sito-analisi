## Exercise 1

Water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ flows through a horizontal pipe of diameter $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at speed $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, then through a throat of diameter $2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Assume steady incompressible flow, negligible viscosity, uniform speed across each section and no pumps between the sections on the same streamline. Calculate flow rate, throat speed and $p_1-p_2$.

$$
Q=\pi(0.020)^2\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
\simeq1.9\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}.
$$
$$
v_2=\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}v_1
=\left(\dfrac{4.0}{2.0}\right)^2\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
=6.0\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
p_1-p_2=\dfrac12\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)
=500(6.0^2-1.5^2)\,\mathrm{Pa}
=16875\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq1.7\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First conserve mass: speed increases because area decreases. Flow rate uses the radius, half the diameter; the area ratio can instead use diameters directly provided they are squared.

Then apply the energy balance. Height is common and gravitational terms cancel. The increase in kinetic energy per unit volume comes from pressure-difference work: $p_1-p_2$ must be positive. Flow rate is about $1.9\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Viscous loss would require an additional term, not an arbitrary sign change.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal Venturi has areas $A_1=20\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and $A_2=5.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Two static taps feed open vertical tubes containing the same water as the conduit; their level difference is $15.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Assume steady ideal flow, uniform section speeds, stationary columns, negligible capillarity and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Determine both speeds and the flow rate.

$$
p_1-p_2=\rho g\Delta h,\qquad v_2=4v_1,
\qquad \rho g\Delta h=\dfrac12\rho(16-1)v_1^2.
$$
$$
v_1=\sqrt{\dfrac{2\cdot9.81\cdot0.150}{15}}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\simeq0.443\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
v_2\simeq1.77\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
Q=A_1v_1=20\cdot10^{-4}\cdot v_1\,\mathrm{m^2}
\simeq8.9\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}=0.89\,\mathrm{L/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The columns measure static pressure difference: the one connected to the wider section is higher. Hydrostatics applies in the side tubes, while continuity and Bernoulli apply in the main conduit. The laws therefore describe different parts of the system.

Substituting $v_2=4v_1$ immediately leaves one unknown. Density cancels because the columns contain the same liquid as the Venturi. It would not cancel in this form with a different manometer liquid. The positive root gives speed; flow direction is not determined by the root alone.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A large open tank discharges water through a small hole $1.25\,\mathrm m$ below the surface. The hole radius is $2.00\,\mathrm{mm}$. Assume ideal quasi-steady outflow, equal atmospheric pressure on the jet and surface, negligible surface speed and no jet contraction. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ calculate instantaneous speed and flow rate.

$$
\rho g\Delta h=\dfrac12\rho v^2,\qquad
v=\sqrt{2\cdot9.81\cdot1.25}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\simeq4.95\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
Q=\pi(2.00\cdot10^{-3})^2v\,\mathrm{m^2}
\simeq6.22\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}=62.2\,\mathrm{mL/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare the free surface with the exiting jet. Pressures cancel; lost potential energy becomes kinetic energy. Density cancels too, but equal endpoint pressures and a very large area ratio are essential assumptions.

Flow rate uses jet area, here assumed equal to hole area. The values are instantaneous: as the level falls, both speed and flow rate decrease. Total draining time therefore cannot be found by dividing initial volume by this flow rate without an additional approximation.

</div>
