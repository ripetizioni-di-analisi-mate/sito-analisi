## Exercise 1

A vertical capillary of internal radius $0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$ dips into a large water reservoir. Use $\gamma=0.072\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and zero contact angle through the liquid. Gas pressure is common; neglect its density and meniscus volume. Calculate the equilibrium height and verify it with a force balance.

![Inverse height-radius curve and the problem point at 0.50 mm.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/fluidi/capillary-en.svg)

$$
h=\dfrac{2\gamma\cos0}{\rho gr}
=\dfrac{0.144}{1000\cdot9.81\cdot5.0\cdot10^{-4}}\,\mathrm m
\simeq0.0294\,\mathrm m=2.94\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
$$
F_\gamma=2\pi r\gamma\simeq2.26\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm N,
\qquad \rho g\pi r^2h\simeq2.26\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The liquid completely wets the wall, so the vertical component of tension is maximal and positive. Force acts around a circumference, while weight grows with cross-sectional area and height. Equating them produces inverse dependence on radius.

The column rises about $2.9\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the data's significant figures. The force check uses unrounded height and gives the same value on both sides. At equilibrium there is no sustained flow: surface tension balances weight rather than continuously accelerating the liquid.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Relative to the first exercise, radius doubles and contact angle through the liquid becomes $60^\circ$; tension, density and gravity are unchanged. Determine the height ratio and new height. Then find the sign and value of the height if, at the new radius, the angle becomes $120^\circ$.

$$
\dfrac{h_2}{h_1}=\dfrac{\cos60^\circ}{\cos0^\circ}\dfrac{r_1}{r_2}
=\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac12=\dfrac14,
\qquad h_2\simeq7.34\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$
$$
\dfrac{h_3}{h_1}=\dfrac{\cos120^\circ}{\cos0^\circ}\dfrac{r_1}{r_2}
=-\dfrac14,\qquad h_3\simeq-7.34\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Radius and angle changes are independent in the model and their factors multiply. Considering radius alone would give half the height, forgetting that the vertical tension component also halves. Use the unrounded initial height for the numerical result.

The obtuse angle makes cosine negative: the level falls below the outside level. The sign does not mean a negative physical length but a lower position relative to the reservoir reference. Magnitudes are equal because the two cosines have equal magnitudes; angles must be measured on the same side, through the liquid.

</div>
