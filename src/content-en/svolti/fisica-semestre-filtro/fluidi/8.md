## Exercise 1

Two linear hydraulic elements with resistances $2.0\cdot10^9$ and $3.0\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}$ are in series, without branches or accumulation and at the same height. A pump maintains $Q=2.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Calculate equivalent resistance, the two pressure drops, total drop and dissipated power.

$$
R_{\mathrm{eq}}=R_1+R_2=5.0\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}.
$$
$$
\Delta p_1=R_1Q=4000\,\mathrm{Pa},\qquad
\Delta p_2=R_2Q=6000\,\mathrm{Pa},\qquad
\Delta p=10000\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$
$$
\mathcal P=\Delta p\,Q=10000\cdot2.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm W
=0.020\,\mathrm W.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same flow rate crosses both elements; it is not divided between them. Pressure difference from inlet to outlet is the sum of successive drops. The more resistive element requires the larger drop.

The energy check multiplies pressure difference by volume transported per unit time. One pascal times one cubic metre is one joule, so the product has units of watts. Adding the powers dissipated in the two elements gives the same result. An ideal pump compensating only these losses must supply this mechanical power.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two linear hydraulic branches with resistances $6.0\cdot10^9$ and $3.0\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}$ are in parallel between the same nodes at the same height, with fixed pressure difference $600\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Determine branch flows, total flow and equivalent resistance; predict what happens when the less resistive branch is closed.

![Series and parallel: distinguish the shared quantity from the quantity that adds.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/fluidi/network-en.svg)

$$
Q_1=\dfrac{600}{6.0\cdot10^9}=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^3/s},\qquad
Q_2=\dfrac{600}{3.0\cdot10^9}=2\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}.
$$
$$
Q=Q_1+Q_2=3\cdot10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^3/s},\qquad
R_{\mathrm{eq}}=\dfrac{600}{3\cdot10^{-7}}=2\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}.
$$
$$
Q'=Q_1=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^3/s},\qquad
R'_{\mathrm{eq}}=6\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The common nodes impose the same pressure difference. The less resistive branch carries twice the flow, while total flow splits according to resistances rather than equally. Equivalent resistance is below either resistance: adding a path facilitates flow.

After closure only the more resistive branch remains. Its flow stays the same because pressure difference is maintained; total flow falls to one third. If a pump instead maintained the original total flow, pressure difference would triple. Stating the constraint is essential.

</div>
