## Exercise 1

A homogeneous wooden cube, with side $20.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, floats in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ with two horizontal faces. Neglect air and surface tension; use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate submerged fraction, submerged height and buoyancy.

![Cube with 60 per cent of its volume submerged and balanced vertical forces.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/fluidi/buoyancy-en.svg)

$$
F_A=\rho_a gV_{\mathrm{sub}}=mg=\rho_c gV,
\qquad \dfrac{V_{\mathrm{sub}}}{V}=\dfrac{600}{1000}=0.600.
$$
$$
h_{\mathrm{sub}}=0.600\cdot20.0\,\mathrm{cm}=12.0\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
$$
F_A=\rho_c L^3g=600\cdot(0.200)^3\cdot9.81\,\mathrm N
\simeq47.1\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cube is stationary and unsupported: weight and buoyancy are the only vertical forces considered. Displaced volume concerns only the submerged part, whereas mass includes the **entire** cube. This distinction produces the density ratio.

Submerged height is the same fraction of the side because the horizontal cross section is constant. This would not hold for an arbitrary shape. Buoyancy can be calculated from the wood's weight or the displaced water's weight, giving the same value. The emerged fraction is one minus the submerged fraction, not the inverse density ratio.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An iceberg floats with $50.0\,\mathrm{m^3}$ above water. Ice and seawater densities are respectively $917\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $1025\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Assume uniform densities, equilibrium and negligible air and surface-tension contributions. Determine total volume and mass.

$$
\dfrac{V_{\mathrm{sub}}}{V}=\dfrac{917}{1025},\qquad
\dfrac{V_{\mathrm{em}}}{V}=1-\dfrac{917}{1025}=\dfrac{108}{1025}.
$$
$$
V=\dfrac{1025}{108}\cdot50.0\,\mathrm{m^3}
\simeq474.537\,\mathrm{m^3}\simeq475\,\mathrm{m^3}.
$$
$$
m=917\cdot\dfrac{1025}{108}\cdot50.0\,\mathrm{kg}
\simeq4.35\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The visible datum is the emerged volume: first find the **emerged fraction** by subtracting the submerged fraction from one. Dividing emerged volume by this fraction reconstructs total volume. Multiplying it directly by the density ratio would instead give a volume smaller than the portion already observed.

Mass uses ice density, not seawater density. Retain the unrounded ratio in the calculation: $474.537\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is already an approximation, not the exact value. Total volume is about nine and a half times the visible volume, consistent with the similar densities.

</div>
