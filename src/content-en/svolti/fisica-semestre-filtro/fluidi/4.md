## Exercise 1

A stationary object suspended from a spring balance weighs $5.00\,\mathrm N$ in air; when fully submerged in water the balance reads $4.40\,\mathrm N$. Neglect air buoyancy and contact with the container. With water density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, determine the object's volume and density.

$$
F_A=P-T=5.00-4.40=0.60\,\mathrm N,
\qquad V=\dfrac{0.60}{1000\cdot9.81}\,\mathrm{m^3}
\simeq6.1\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}=61\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
$$
$$
\rho_c=\dfrac{P}{F_A}\rho_a
=\dfrac{5.00}{0.60}\cdot1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
\simeq8.3\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water reading is tension, not a new value of gravitational force. Vertical equilibrium gives $T+F_A=P$, so the difference between readings measures buoyancy. Since the object is fully submerged, displaced volume equals its volume.

Volume and gravitational acceleration cancel in the weight-to-buoyancy ratio, leaving the density ratio. The difference between readings has two significant figures, so results are presented with two figures. Density above that of water is consistent with positive tension in the string supporting the body from above.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solid aluminium sphere of density $2.70\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and radius $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ hangs from a string and is fully submerged in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. It is stationary and does not touch the container. With $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ calculate weight, buoyancy and tension.

$$
V=\dfrac43\pi(0.030)^3\,\mathrm{m^3}
\simeq1.131\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}.
$$
$$
P=\rho_c Vg\simeq3.00\,\mathrm N,\qquad
F_A=\rho_a Vg\simeq1.11\,\mathrm N.
$$
$$
T+F_A-P=0,\qquad
T=(2700-1000)V\cdot9.81\,\mathrm{N/m^3}
\simeq1.89\,\mathrm N\simeq1.9\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Draw weight downwards and buoyancy and tension upwards. The string supplies only the support not already provided by water. Convert centimetres to metres before cubing the radius; converting afterwards with a linear conversion factor would be incorrect.

Intermediate values retain one extra digit. Consistent with the specified radius, final results are about $3.0\,\mathrm N$, $1.1\,\mathrm N$ and $1.9\,\mathrm N$. The ratio $\dfrac{T}{P}=1-\dfrac{1000}{2700}\simeq0.630$ provides an independent check: the string supports about $63\%$ of the weight.

</div>
