## Exercise 1

A rigid horizontal cylindrical pipe has radius $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and length $0.20\,\mathrm m$. An incompressible Newtonian fluid of viscosity $4.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ undergoes steady, fully developed laminar flow with no slip at the wall. The pressure difference is $160\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Determine flow rate and hydraulic resistance.

$$
R_h=\dfrac{8\eta L}{\pi r^4}
=\dfrac{8\cdot4.0\cdot10^{-3}\cdot0.20}{\pi(10^{-3})^4}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}
\simeq2.04\cdot10^9\,\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}.
$$
$$
Q=\dfrac{160}{R_h}\,\mathrm{Pa}
\simeq7.85\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}
\simeq4.71\,\mathrm{mL/min}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stated conditions allow Poiseuille's law: merely mentioning a tube would not suffice. Convert radius into metres **before** taking the fourth power: $(10^{-3})^4=10^{-12}$. The resulting resistance describes the whole pipe and includes viscosity and geometry.

Dividing pascals by $\mathrm{Pa\,s/m^3}$ gives $\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Convert to millilitres per minute by multiplying by $10^6\cdot60$. Values shown with an extra digit make the conversion clear; to two significant figures flow rate is $4.7\,\mathrm{mL/min}$. A positive sign corresponds to flow from higher to lower pressure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two configurations of the same pipe satisfy Poiseuille's assumptions. In the second, radius is $80\%$ of its initial value while length and viscosity are unchanged. Find the resistance ratio and, first at fixed pressure difference and then at fixed flow rate, the flow-rate and pressure-difference ratios.

$$
\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}=\left(\dfrac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^4
=\dfrac1{0.8^4}=\dfrac1{0.4096}\simeq2.44.
$$
$$
\Delta p_2=\Delta p_1:\quad \dfrac{Q_2}{Q_1}=0.4096,
\qquad 1-\dfrac{Q_2}{Q_1}=0.5904.
$$
$$
Q_2=Q_1:\quad \dfrac{\Delta p_2}{\Delta p_1}=\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}\simeq2.44.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Start with resistance because it depends on geometry and fluid without immediately choosing what the pump holds fixed. A $20\%$ radius decrease has a much larger effect than the same percentage decrease in flow rate.

In the first experiment $40.96\%$ of flow remains and the reduction is $59.04\%$: remaining and lost fractions differ. In the second a pump must increase pressure difference; flow does not fall because it is constrained to remain constant. The two answers describe alternative experimental constraints, not a contradiction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the pipe from the first exercise also assume $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Find mean speed, maximum speed, speed at distance $\dfrac{r}{2}$ from the axis, Reynolds number and wall shear-stress magnitude. Use the problem's unrounded data.

![Parabolic profile and distinction between maximum and cross-sectional mean.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/fluidi/profile-en.svg)

$$
\bar v=\dfrac{Q}{\pi r^2}=0.025\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
v_{\max}=2\bar v=0.050\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$
$$
v(\dfrac{r}{2})=v_{\max}\left(1-\dfrac14\right)=0.0375\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad \mathrm{Re}=\dfrac{1000\cdot0.025\cdot0.002}{0.004}=12.5.
$$
$$
\left|\dfrac{dv}{ds}\right|_{s=r}=\dfrac{2v_{\max}}r=100\,\mathrm{s^{-1}},\qquad
\tau_w=\eta\dfrac{2v_{\max}}r=0.40\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Integrated flow rate determines mean speed; maximum speed must not be used in $Q=A\bar v$. The parabolic profile then gives each local speed. At half-radius speed is three quarters of the maximum, not half the maximum.

The small Reynolds number is consistent with the assumed laminar regime, although it does not replace the other conditions. The profile derivative is greatest in magnitude at the wall, where speed is zero. Stress can also be checked by balancing forces on a fluid cylinder: $\tau_w=\dfrac{\Delta p\,r}{2L}=0.40\,\mathrm{Pa}$.

</div>
