## Exercise 1

A diver is $25.0\,\mathrm m$ below the sea surface. Assume water at rest with constant density $1025\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and atmospheric pressure $101300\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Calculate gauge pressure, absolute pressure and the ratio of absolute pressure to the specified atmospheric pressure.

$$
p_{\mathrm{rel}}=\rho gh=1025\cdot9.81\cdot25.0\,\mathrm{Pa}
=251381.25\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq2.51\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$
$$
p_{\mathrm{ass}}=p_{\mathrm{atm}}+p_{\mathrm{rel}}
=352681.25\,\mathrm{Pa}\simeq3.53\cdot10^5\,\mathrm{Pa},\qquad
\dfrac{p_{\mathrm{ass}}}{p_{\mathrm{atm}}}\simeq3.48.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the free surface as the reference, where pressure equals atmospheric pressure. Moving down increases pressure: the column's weight determines the **increment** $\rho gh$, not absolute pressure. The initial pressure is therefore added only in the second step.

The final ratio is dimensionless and uses the specified local atmospheric pressure. Do not divide gauge pressure alone, because the question asks how many times total pressure exceeds the surface value. The sign check is immediate: both pressures increase with depth, and absolute pressure must exceed gauge pressure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vertical circular porthole of radius $0.20\,\mathrm m$ has its centre $3.0\,\mathrm m$ below the surface of water at rest. Air outside the tank has the same pressure as the free surface. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, calculate the magnitude and direction of the net force on the porthole, neglecting pressure changes in the air.

$$
A=\pi r^2=\pi(0.20)^2\,\mathrm{m^2},\qquad
\overline{\Delta p}=\rho gh_c=1000\cdot9.81\cdot3.0\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$
$$
F=\overline{\Delta p}\,A
=1000\cdot9.81\cdot3.0\cdot\pi(0.20)^2\,\mathrm N
\simeq3.7\cdot10^3\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Atmospheric pressure acts on both sides and cancels in the balance. Water gauge pressure varies across the surface but depends linearly on depth. For a flat area its average equals the value at the depth of the **area centroid**: symmetric strips above and below the centre have opposite pressure changes.

The net force is perpendicular to the porthole and directed from water towards air. Calculating its magnitude does not imply that its point of application is the centre: higher pressure in the lower part places the centre of pressure below it. Multiplying area by absolute pressure would include an atmospheric force that is already balanced.

</div>
