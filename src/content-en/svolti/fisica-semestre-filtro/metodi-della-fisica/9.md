## Exercise 1

A position–time graph is a straight line through $(1\,\mathrm{s};2\,\mathrm{m})$ and $(5\,\mathrm{s};14\,\mathrm{m})$. Find velocity, the time law and the instant of passing the origin. Why is $\dfrac{14}{5}$ not the slope?

$$
v=\dfrac{14\,\mathrm{m}-2\,\mathrm{m}}{5\,\mathrm{s}-1\,\mathrm{s}}=3\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad b=2\,\mathrm{m}-(3\,\mathrm{m/s})(1\,\mathrm{s})=-1\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
$$
x(t)=(3\,\mathrm{m/s})t-1\,\mathrm{m},\qquad x(t_*)=0\Longrightarrow t_*=\dfrac13\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

![Line through both measured points, extended to initial position.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/metodi-della-fisica/position-worked-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Slope uses differences between points on the same line. A coordinate divided by its time would equal slope only for a line through the coordinate origin. Here the intercept is nonzero, so both coordinates must be subtracted.

After obtaining velocity, substitute either point into the linear law to find initial position. The other data pair independently checks the line. Including coefficient units makes it explicit that every term in the time law has length dimensions.

The origin is crossed at one third of a second if the linear law remains valid before the first measurement. Without that model extension, data between one and five seconds would not directly document the event. The intercept is a position, not a negative velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Velocity increases linearly from $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ between $t=0$ and $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$. Find acceleration, reversal time, displacement and distance travelled.

$$
a=\dfrac{4-(-2)}3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\qquad v(t)=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}+(2\,\mathrm{m/s^2})t.
$$
$$
v=0\Longrightarrow t=1\,\mathrm{s},\qquad A_-=-\dfrac12(1\,\mathrm{s})(2\,\mathrm{m/s})=-1\,\mathrm{m},
$$
$$
A_+=\dfrac12(2\,\mathrm{s})(4\,\mathrm{m/s})=4\,\mathrm{m},\qquad \Delta x=3\,\mathrm{m},\qquad d=5\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

![Velocity–time line with a negative triangle up to one second and a positive triangle over the next two seconds.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/metodi-della-fisica/velocity-worked-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The line has constant positive slope, so acceleration is positive even during the initial negative-velocity segment. Initially the point moves in the negative direction while slowing; after the zero crossing it moves in the positive direction. Confusing velocity sign with acceleration sign would erase this distinction.

Displacement is signed area. The small triangle below the axis contributes minus one metre and the triangle above contributes four metres. Distance travelled instead adds the magnitudes of both areas because it counts the whole route regardless of direction.

An independent displacement check uses average velocity for a linear change: the mean of initial and final values is one metre per second; multiplied by three seconds this gives three metres. That check does not give distance travelled when velocity changes sign.

</div>
