## Exercise 1

Determine which relations are dimensionally consistent, where $x$ and $\Delta x$ are lengths, $t$ time, $v$ and $v_0$ velocities, and $a$ acceleration:

$$
\text{(i)}\quad v^2 = v_0^2 + 2 a\,\Delta x,
\qquad\qquad
\text{(ii)}\quad x = v_0 t + a t .
$$

$$
[v] = [L\,T^{-1}],
\qquad
[a] = [L\,T^{-2}],
\qquad
[\Delta x] = [L],
\qquad
[t] = [T]
$$
$$
\text{(i)}\quad [v^2] = [v_0^2] = [L^2\,T^{-2}],
\qquad
[a\,\Delta x] = [L\,T^{-2}]\,[L] = [L^2\,T^{-2}]
$$
$$
\text{(ii)}\quad [x] = [L],
\qquad
[v_0 t] = [L\,T^{-1}]\,[T] = [L],
\qquad
[a t] = [L\,T^{-2}]\,[T] = [L\,T^{-1}]
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dimensional homogeneity requires every added term and both sides of an equality to have the same dimension. The factor two is dimensionless and cannot change a term’s dimensions. In relation (i), every term has dimension $L^2T^{-2}$, so the relation passes this check.

In relation (ii), $at$ has velocity dimensions, whereas $x$ and $v_0t$ are lengths. No choice of metres rather than centimetres can repair that mismatch. Adding another time factor would restore homogeneity but would not determine the numerical coefficient.

Passing the check is necessary, not sufficient. Relation (i) is valid for constant acceleration under the relevant kinematic assumptions; that requires physical derivation. Dimensional analysis alone cannot establish those assumptions or distinguish a correct coefficient from an incorrect dimensionless multiplier.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the dimensions and coherent SI units of constants $k$, $b$ and $G$ in the laws below; $F$ is force, $x$ and $r$ lengths, $v$ velocity, and $m_1$ and $m_2$ masses.

$$
F = k x,
\qquad
F = b v,
\qquad
F = G\,\dfrac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} ,
$$


$$
[F] = [M\,L\,T^{-2}]
$$
$$
[k] = \dfrac{[F]}{[x]} = \dfrac{[M\,L\,T^{-2}]}{[L]} = [M\,T^{-2}]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{kg/s^2} = \mathrm{N/m}
$$
$$
[b] = \dfrac{[F]}{[v]} = \dfrac{[M\,L\,T^{-2}]}{[L\,T^{-1}]} = [M\,T^{-1}]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{kg/s}
$$
$$
[G] = \dfrac{[F]\,[r^2]}{[m_1 m_2]} = \dfrac{[M\,L\,T^{-2}]\,[L^2]}{[M^2]} = [M^{-1}\,L^{3}\,T^{-2}]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{m^3/(kg\cdot s^2)} = \mathrm{N\cdot m^2/kg^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolate each constant before replacing quantities by their dimensions. Force has dimension mass times acceleration, $MLT^{-2}$. In $F=kx$, division by length leaves $MT^{-2}$, so stiffness has unit $\mathrm{N/m}=\mathrm{kg/s^2}$.

For $F=bv$, division by velocity cancels length and one inverse-time factor, leaving $MT^{-1}$. Thus the drag coefficient has unit $\mathrm{kg/s}$. The two constants cannot share a unit merely because both appear in a linear force law: they multiply different quantities.

Finally, $G=\dfrac{Fr^2}{m_1m_2}$ introduces two powers of length and removes two powers of mass. This gives $M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$. Recombining that unit with two masses and inverse squared distance must recover newtons, providing a reverse check.

</div>
