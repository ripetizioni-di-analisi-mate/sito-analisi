## Exercise 1

A circular arc has radius $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and central angle $60^\circ$. Convert the angle to radians, find sine and cosine, and obtain arc length. Why cannot sixty be inserted directly into $s=r\theta$?

$$
\theta=60\dfrac\pi{180}\,\mathrm{rad}=\dfrac\pi3\,\mathrm{rad},\qquad \sin\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt3}2,\qquad\cos\theta=\dfrac12.
$$
$$
s=(12\,\mathrm{cm})\dfrac\pi3=4\pi\,\mathrm{cm}\simeq12.6\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Arc length divided by radius defines the angular value in radians. A complete turn is two pi radians, so sixty degrees is one sixth of a turn. This fraction independently gives arc length as one sixth of the circumference.

The radian has dimension one, but angular convention remains essential: sixty is the degree value, not the arc-to-radius ratio. Substituting it directly would multiply the result by an incorrect factor. The formula uses the radian value.

Sine and cosine belong to the same geometric angle and do not change with the unit used to express it. The calculator must nevertheless interpret the input correctly: degree mode for sixty or radian mode for pi over three. Arc length retains the radius unit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $6\,\mathrm{m}$ displacement is directed $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad}$ anticlockwise from positive $x$. Express the angle in degrees, find components and give an equivalent negative angle. Does adding $\pi$ describe the same vector?

$$
\theta=\dfrac{5\pi}6\dfrac{180^\circ}\pi=150^\circ,\qquad\theta-360^\circ=-210^\circ.
$$
$$
s_x=(6\,\mathrm{m})\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt3}2\right)=-3\sqrt3\,\mathrm{m},\qquad s_y=(6\,\mathrm{m})\dfrac12=3\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle lies in the second quadrant, so the horizontal component must be negative and the vertical component positive. Angular units change without changing physical direction; standard sine and cosine values give exact components.

Subtracting a complete turn preserves direction: the negative angle describes a longer clockwise rotation reaching the same ray. Adding pi radians instead makes a half-turn and gives the opposite vector, reversing both components.

The sum of squared components recovers the square of the original six metres. Equal tangent therefore does not guarantee the same vector: opposite directions share tangent, whereas sine and cosine distinguish their senses. This distinction is essential when reconstructing an angle from components.

</div>
