## Exercise 1

A $12\,\mathrm{N}$ force lies in the first quadrant and makes $30^\circ$ with positive $y$. Find its components and explain what would change if the same angle were measured from $x$.

$$
F_y=(12\,\mathrm{N})\cos30^\circ=6\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N}\simeq10.4\,\mathrm{N},
$$
$$
F_x=(12\,\mathrm{N})\sin30^\circ=6\,\mathrm{N}.
$$
$$
F_x^2+F_y^2=(36+108)\,\mathrm{N^2}=144\,\mathrm{N^2}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle is measured from the vertical axis, so the adjacent side is the vertical component. Cosine therefore belongs to the vertical component, not automatically to the horizontal one. The force lies closer to that axis, consistent with its larger vertical component.

Both components are positive because the vector lies in the first quadrant. The Pythagorean check reconstructs squared magnitude; each component is also smaller than the force magnitude.

If the thirty-degree angle were measured from the horizontal axis, the components would exchange. That would be a different vector despite equal magnitude and equal numerical angle relative to a different axis. The reference direction is therefore part of the physical data.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A displacement has components $s_x=-3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $s_y=3\,\mathrm{m}$. Find magnitude, anticlockwise angle from $+x$ and unit direction vector. Why is the arctangent of the ratio alone insufficient?

$$
s=3\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m},\qquad\theta=135^\circ=\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad}.
$$
$$
\hat s=\dfrac{\vec{s}}{s}=\left(-\dfrac1{\sqrt2};\dfrac1{\sqrt2}\right),\qquad |\hat s|=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Magnitude comes from the sum of squared components, which removes signs but not the length unit. Signs are essential for direction: negative horizontal and positive vertical component place the vector in the second quadrant.

The component ratio is minus one. Principal arctangent would give minus forty-five degrees, a fourth-quadrant direction. Adding one hundred and eighty degrees selects the required vector. Both angles have equal tangent but opposite sine and cosine signs.

Divide the vector by its own positive magnitude to obtain a unit direction vector. It is dimensionless, preserves direction and has magnitude one. Dividing each component by itself would instead erase sign information and produce a different object.

</div>
