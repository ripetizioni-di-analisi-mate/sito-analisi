## Exercise 1

A ball has speed $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ directed $150^\circ$ anticlockwise from positive $x$. Find its velocity components. Then obtain magnitude and angle of displacement $\vec{s}=(-6.0\,\hat{\imath}-8.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
v_x = v\cos\theta = 20 \cdot \cos 150^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = -20 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx -17.3\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_y = v\sin\theta = 20 \cdot \sin 150^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s} = 20 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 10.0\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$


$$
s = \sqrt{s_x^2 + s_y^2} = \sqrt{(-6.0)^2 + (-8.0)^2}\,\mathrm{m} = \sqrt{100}\,\mathrm{m} = 10\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\arctan\dfrac{s_y}{s_x} = \arctan\dfrac{-8.0}{-6.0} \approx 53.1^\circ,
\qquad
\theta = 53.1^\circ + 180^\circ = 233.1^\circ
$$

![Vector with components minus six and minus eight metres in the third quadrant.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/metodi-della-fisica/quadrant-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

For an angle measured from positive $x$, multiply magnitude by cosine for the $x$ component and sine for $y$. At $150^\circ$, the second quadrant requires negative $v_x$ and positive $v_y$. Predicting these signs before calculating catches an incorrect angle or reversed axis.

For the inverse problem, Pythagoras gives the displacement magnitude $10\,\mathrm{m}$. Both displacement components are negative, placing the vector in the third quadrant. The principal arctangent of their ratio gives $53.1^\circ$, which by itself describes the opposite first-quadrant direction.

Add $180^\circ$ to obtain $233.1^\circ$ anticlockwise, equivalently $126.9^\circ$ clockwise. A two-argument arctangent can perform this quadrant selection directly. Substituting the chosen angle into sine and cosine must recover both negative components.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A walker travels $3.0\,\mathrm{km}$ east, then $4.0\,\mathrm{km}$ northeast. Find the magnitude and direction of total displacement, with $x$ east and $y$ north.

$$
\vec{R} = \vec{d}_1 + \vec{d}_2
$$
$$
\vec{d}_1 = (3.0;\ 0)\,\mathrm{km},
\qquad
\vec{d}_2 = (4.0\cos 45^\circ;\ 4.0\sin 45^\circ)\,\mathrm{km} = (2.83;\ 2.83)\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
\vec{R} = (3.0 + 2.83;\ 0 + 2.83)\,\mathrm{km} = (5.83;\ 2.83)\,\mathrm{km}
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{5.83^2 + 2.83^2}\,\mathrm{km} \approx 6.5\,\mathrm{km},
\qquad
\theta = \arctan\dfrac{2.83}{5.83} \approx 25.9^\circ
$$

![East and northeast route with resultant from start to finish.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/metodi-della-fisica/hike-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The second displacement begins at the first displacement’s endpoint, so head-to-tail addition gives the start-to-finish vector. Northeast is $45^\circ$ north of east: its two components are equal, each approximately $2.83\,\mathrm{km}$.

Add corresponding components, retaining unrounded trigonometric values until the final calculation. The resultant lies in the first quadrant, so the arctangent of $\dfrac{R_y}{R_x}$ directly gives its angle north of east. Its magnitude is about $6.5\,\mathrm{km}$ and its direction $25.9^\circ$ north of east.

The travelled route is $7.0\,\mathrm{km}$, which exceeds displacement magnitude because the route changes direction. Applying Pythagoras directly to the two route lengths would incorrectly assume that east and northeast are perpendicular. Components provide the required right-angle construction instead.

</div>
