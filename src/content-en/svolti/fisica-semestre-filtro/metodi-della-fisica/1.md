## Exercise 1

A car travels at $108\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and a cyclist at $7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Express the car’s speed in $\mathrm{m/s}$ and the cyclist’s speed in $\mathrm{km/h}$.

$$
1\,\mathrm{km} = 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{h} = 3600\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
v_{\text{car}} = 108\,\dfrac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{h}} = 108 \cdot \dfrac{10^{3}\,\mathrm{m}}{3600\,\mathrm{s}} = \dfrac{108}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s} = 30\,\mathrm{m/s}
$$
$$
v_{\text{bike}} = 7.5\,\dfrac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}} = 7.5 \cdot \dfrac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{km}}{\dfrac{1}{3600}\,\mathrm{h}} = 7.5 \cdot 3.6\,\mathrm{km/h} = 27\,\mathrm{km/h}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Replace each unit by its value in the target unit. One kilometre is $10^3\,\mathrm{m}$ and one hour is $3600\,\mathrm{s}$, so one kilometre per hour equals $\dfrac{1}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Dividing by $3.6$ therefore converts to metres per second; multiplying converts back.

Both results describe unchanged speeds: only their numerical values change. The car travels thirty metres in one second; the cyclist’s speed corresponds to twenty-seven kilometres in one hour if maintained. Cancelling the units in each conversion factor determines its orientation and avoids applying the conversion in reverse.

As a check, multiplying $30$ by $3.6$ recovers $108$, while dividing $27$ by $3.6$ recovers $7.5$. For positive speeds, the numerical value in metres per second is smaller because that unit is larger.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Aluminium has density $2.70\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Express it in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and find the mass of a solid aluminium cube with edge $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
1\,\mathrm{g} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{cm^3} = (10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^3 = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
\rho = 2.70\,\dfrac{\mathrm{g}}{\mathrm{cm^3}} = 2.70 \cdot \dfrac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}}{10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}} = 2.70 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$
$$
V = \ell^3 = (5.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^3 = 1.25 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}
$$
$$
m = \rho V = 2.70 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3} \cdot 1.25 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3} = 0.3375\,\mathrm{kg} \approx 0.34\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A cubic centimetre is the cube of a centimetre, so the conversion factor $10^{-2}$ must also be cubed. The numerator contributes $10^{-3}$ and the denominator $10^{-6}$; their ratio is $10^3$. Thus density in coherent SI units is $2.70\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

Find volume by cubing the edge in metres, then multiply by density using $m=\rho V$. The same calculation in centimetres gives $125\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $337.5\,\mathrm{g}$, confirming the result after mass conversion.

The unrounded mass is $0.3375\,\mathrm{kg}$. With the usual significant-figure rule, the two-significant-figure edge gives a final mass of about $0.34\,\mathrm{kg}$. Exact unit conversions do not impose an additional rounding limit; keep intermediate digits until the last step.

</div>
