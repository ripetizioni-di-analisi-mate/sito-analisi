## Exercise 1

A force of magnitude $F=80\,\mathrm{N}$ is applied at the end of a $r=0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ wrench in its plane. Find torque magnitude about the bolt axis when force is perpendicular to the wrench and when its angle to the wrench is $60^\circ$. What force at $60^\circ$ gives the same torque as the perpendicular force?

$$
\tau = r F \sin\theta = F\, b,
\qquad
b = r\sin\theta
$$
$$
\theta = 90^\circ:
\qquad
\tau_1 = 0.25\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 80\,\mathrm{N} = 20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\theta = 60^\circ:
\qquad
\tau_2 = 0.25\,\mathrm{m} \cdot 80\,\mathrm{N} \cdot \sin 60^\circ = 20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 17\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
F' = \dfrac{\tau_1}{r\sin 60^\circ} = \dfrac{20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.25\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} \approx 92\,\mathrm{N}
$$

![Lever arm perpendicular to the line of action of a force at sixty degrees.](/figure/fisica-semestre-filtro/metodi-della-fisica/torque-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Torque magnitude is $rF\sin\theta$, where the angle is between the position vector along the wrench and force. The perpendicular lever arm is $b=r\sin\theta$, the shortest distance from the bolt to the line of action, not generally the full wrench length.

At ninety degrees, the lever arm equals $r$ and torque is twenty newton metres. At sixty degrees, the line of action passes closer to the bolt and the lever arm is approximately $0.22\,\mathrm{m}$, giving about seventeen newton metres.

To restore the initial torque while keeping the smaller lever arm, solve for force rather than multiplying by another sine. The required force is about ninety-two newtons, greater than eighty. This confirms that the perpendicular force gives the greatest torque for fixed length and force magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A force $\vec{F}=(3.0\,\hat{\jmath}+4.0\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{N}$ acts at position $\vec{r}=(1.0\,\hat{\imath}+2.0\,\hat{\jmath})\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to point $O$. Find torque $\vec{\tau}$ about $O$, its magnitude, and verify perpendicularity to both $\vec{r}$ and $\vec{F}$.

$$
\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat{\imath} & \hat{\jmath} & \hat{k} \\
1.0 & 2.0 & 0 \\
0 & 3.0 & 4.0
\end{vmatrix}
\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{\tau} &= \big[ (2.0 \cdot 4.0 - 0 \cdot 3.0)\,\hat{\imath} + (0 \cdot 0 - 1.0 \cdot 4.0)\,\hat{\jmath} + (1.0 \cdot 3.0 - 2.0 \cdot 0)\,\hat{k} \big]\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
&= (8.0\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0\,\hat{\jmath} + 3.0\,\hat{k})\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\tau = \sqrt{64 + 16 + 9.0}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = \sqrt{89}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \approx 9.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
$$
$$
\vec{\tau} \cdot \vec{r} = [8.0 \cdot 1.0 + (-4.0) \cdot 2.0 + 3.0 \cdot 0]\,\mathrm{N\,m^2} = 0,
\qquad
\vec{\tau} \cdot \vec{F} = [8.0 \cdot 0 + (-4.0) \cdot 3.0 + 3.0 \cdot 4.0]\,\mathrm{N^2\,m} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Torque is the ordered cross product $\vec{r}\times\vec{F}$. Expanding its symbolic determinant gives the component formula; the minus sign associated with the middle cofactor is already incorporated in $r_zF_x-r_xF_z$. Its unit is the product of position and force units.

The resulting components are eight, minus four and three newton metres. Their squared sum gives magnitude $\sqrt{89}\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, approximately $9.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. This is consistent with a torque no larger than the product of position and force magnitudes.

Both dot products with the original vectors must vanish. The explicit checks confirm perpendicularity and are sensitive to a wrong component sign. Reversing the cross-product order would reverse the torque vector, while leaving magnitude and perpendicularity unchanged; therefore the original order must still be checked.

</div>
