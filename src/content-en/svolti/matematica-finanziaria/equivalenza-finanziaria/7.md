## Exercise 1

Under a multiplicative accumulation law, one euro invested today grows to $a(1)=1.05$ after one year and $a(3)=1.26$ after three years. Find the value at time $1$ of $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ receivable at time $3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(1,3)&=\frac{a(3)}{a(1)}=\frac{1.26}{1.05}=1.2 \\[6pt]
V(1)&=\frac{600}{r(1,3)}=\frac{600}{1.2}=500\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\frac{500}{1.05}&=\frac{600}{1.26}\approx476.19\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The accumulation factor from today to year three includes the first year. Divide $a(3)$ by $a(1)$ to isolate the two years between the valuation date and the due date. Multiplicativity makes this ratio the required factor, as shown by the **unit-account representation** $r(s,t)=\dfrac{a(t)}{a(s)}$. The factor is $1.2$ and carries one euro from time $1$ to time $3$. Over those two years the equivalent constant annual compound rate is $\sqrt{1.2}-1\approx9.54\%$. To work backward from the receipt, divide its future amount by that factor. The claim is worth **500 euros at time 1**. Both amounts also have the same present value because $\dfrac{500}{1.05}=\dfrac{600}{1.26}$. As the final line shows, both quotients are approximately $476.19$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $r(0,1)=1.10$, $r(1,2)=1.20$, and $r(0,2)=1.30$. Determine whether the factors are multiplicative. Find the value at time $2$ of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ carried forward directly and in two stages.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(0,1)r(1,2)&=1.10\cdot1.20=1.32 \\[6pt]
r(0,1)r(1,2)&=1.32\ne1.30=r(0,2) \\[6pt]
100r(0,2)&=100\cdot1.30=130\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
100r(0,1)&=110\,\mathrm{EUR},\qquad 110\,r(1,2)=110\cdot1.20=132\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Multiplicativity** requires the direct accumulation factor to equal the product of the factors on consecutive intervals. Here the product is $1.32$ and the direct factor is $1.30$, so the condition fails. The direct investment gives **130 euros**. In two stages, $100$ euros grows to $110$ euros at time $1$. Applying the second factor, $1.20$, makes it **132 euros** at time $2$. The discrepancy is a property of the given law, apparent from the two accumulated values of the same initial principal. An investor with $100$ euros at time $0$ earns $2$ euros more by investing for one year and reinvesting the proceeds than by investing directly for two years. With the one-period factors held fixed, multiplicativity would require the factor from $0$ to $2$ to equal $1.32$.

</div>
