## Exercise 1

Debts of $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in three years are replaced by their nominal sum. Find the equivalent average due date using annual commercial discount of $10\%$ and today as the valuation date.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=300+900=1200 \\[6pt]
V(0)&=300(1-0.1\cdot1)+900(1-0.1\cdot3) \\[6pt]
&=300\cdot0.9+900\cdot0.7=270+630=900 \\[6pt]
1200(1-0.1T)&=900 \\[6pt]
1-0.1T&=\frac{900}{1200}=0.75 \\[6pt]
T&=\frac{0.25}{0.1}=2.5\,\mathrm{years}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both original dates give positive commercial discount factors, so both amounts can be valued today. The factor for time $t$ is $1-0.1t$, giving $0.9$ for the first debt and $0.7$ for the second. Their total present value is $900$ euros. Apply the same discount law to the nominal sum of $1200$ euros and set its present value equal to $900$. Dividing by $1200$ gives $1-0.1T=0.75$, hence $0.1T=0.25$. The resulting date is **2.5 years**. It equals the weighted arithmetic mean $\dfrac{300\cdot1+900\cdot3}{1200}$ because commercial discount is linear in time. The debt of $900$ euros has the greater weight, so the mean is closer to time $3$ than to time $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two receipts, $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years, are replaced by a payment of $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find its equivalent due date at an annual compound interest rate of $20\%$ and compare it with the arithmetic mean of the dates.

$$
\begin{aligned}
200q^{-T}&=100+100q^{-2} \\[6pt]
q^{-2}&=\frac{1}{1.44}=\frac{25}{36} \\[6pt]
q^{-T}&=\frac{1+\frac{25}{36}}{2}=\frac{61}{72} \\[6pt]
q^{T}&=\frac{72}{61} \\[6pt]
T&=\frac{\log\!\left(\frac{72}{61}\right)}{\log(1.2)}\approx\frac{0.16579}{0.18232}\approx0.9093\,\mathrm{years}
\end{aligned}
$$

The midpoint of the chord has vertical coordinate $\dfrac{61}{72}$ and horizontal coordinate 1. The curve reaches that same height at $T\approx0.91$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The nominal total is $200$ euros. Divide the equation by $200$ to express the required discount factor as the mean of the two original factors. The two-year factor is $q^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{1.44}=\dfrac{25}{36}$, so their mean is $\dfrac{61}{72}$. Taking reciprocals gives $q^T=\dfrac{72}{61}$, and taking logarithms yields the date. The receipts are equal, so their arithmetic mean date is one year. At that date the single payment would have discount factor $\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{60}{72}$, below the required factor. Since the factor decreases with time, equality of present values requires a date before the first anniversary. The **equivalent compound-interest due date is approximately 0.91 years**. The function $q^{-t}$ is convex, so the chord between the two dates in the figure lies above the curve. By Jensen's inequality, the curve reaches the height of the chord's midpoint at an earlier date.

</div>
