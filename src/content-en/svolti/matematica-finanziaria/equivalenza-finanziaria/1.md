## Exercise 1

Find the value at time $2$ of two payments, $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $4$. The annual compound interest rate is $10\%$ and time is measured in years.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=1+0.1=1.1,\qquad q^2=1.21 \\[6pt]
V(2)&=300q^{2-1}+605q^{2-4} \\[6pt]
&=300q+605q^{-2} \\[6pt]
&=300\cdot1.1+\frac{605}{1.21} \\[6pt]
&=330+500=830\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate is annual and time is measured in years, giving the one-period factor $q=1.1$. Each exponent is the focal date minus the payment date, $2-1=1$ for the first receipt and $2-4=-2$ for the second. The **focal date** lies between the receipts, so one is accumulated and the other discounted. The $300$ euros at time $1$ earn a year's interest and are worth $330$ euros at time $2$. The $605$ euros are due two years after the focal date, so divide by the two-year compound accumulation factor. This gives $500$ euros, the principal that grows at $10\%$ to $605$ euros in two years. Both terms now refer to time $2$ and add up to **830 euros**. To check, carry both receipts to time $4$. Their sum is $300q^3+605=1004.3$ euros, equal to $830q^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A business pays $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ now, receives $660\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year, and pays $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in three years. Find the net value at the end of year two at an annual compound interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(2)&=-400q^{2-0}+660q^{2-1}-121q^{2-3} \\[6pt]
&=-400q^2+660q-121q^{-1} \\[6pt]
&=-400\cdot1.21+660\cdot1.1-\frac{121}{1.1} \\[6pt]
&=-484+726-110 \\[6pt]
&=132\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.4 xmax=3.8 ymin=-1.05 ymax=1.5 linee="-0.2,0 3.4,0;0,-0.07 0,0.07;1,-0.07 1,0.07;2,-0.07 2,0.07;3,-0.07 3,0.07" vettori="3.3,0,3.65,0;0,0,0,-0.8,400;1,0,1,1.32,660;3,0,3,-0.242" unicolore=1 etichette="3.5,0.15,t;-0.16,0.15,0;0.84,-0.25,1;1.95,-0.25,2;2.84,0.15,3;3.08,-0.42,121" punti="2,0,pieno" titolo="The firm’s cash flows in euros. The 660 euros received at time 1 point upward. The payments of 400 euros at time 0 and 121 euros at time 3 point downward. The point marks the valuation date $t=2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract each cash flow date from the focal date. The exponents are $2$ for the initial payment, $1$ for the receipt, and $-1$ for the payment in year three. From the business's perspective the first and last payments are **outflows** and retain their negative signs after the change of date. Accumulate the first payment for two years and the receipt for one year because both precede the focal date. At time $2$ the third-year payment is still one year away, so discount it for one year. The algebraic sum is positive. The receipt covers the outflows and leaves a net value of **132 euros at time 2**. The value at time zero would be $-400+600-\dfrac{100}{1.1}=\dfrac{1200}{11}$ euros. Multiplying it by $q^2$ gives the same $132$ euros.

</div>
