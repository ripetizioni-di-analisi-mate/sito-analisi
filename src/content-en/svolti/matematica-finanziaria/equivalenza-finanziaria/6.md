## Exercise 1

An account opens with $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. A withdrawal of $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is made after the first year. Find the final balance if the effective rates are $5\%$ in the first year and $20\%$ in the second.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S(1^-)&=1000\cdot1.05=1050 \\[6pt]
S(1^+)&=1050-250=800 \\[6pt]
S(2)&=800\cdot1.2=960\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
S(2)&=1000\cdot1.05\cdot1.2-250\cdot1.2=1260-300=960\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

The vertical segment at time 1 shows a withdrawal of 250 euros, reducing the balance from 1,050 to 800 euros. The balance at the end of the second year is 960 euros."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Write $S(1^-)$ for the balance immediately before the withdrawal and $S(1^+)$ for the balance immediately after it. The initial principal earns $5\%$ during the first year, giving a balance of $1050$ euros before the withdrawal. Withdrawing at year-end leaves $800$ euros invested in year two. Applying the rate of $20\%$ to that balance gives **960 euros**, including $160$ euros of second-year interest. Alternatively, accumulate the initial amount for both years and subtract the accumulated value of the withdrawal. The withdrawal occurs at time $1$, so only the second year's factor applies. The final line gives the same balance of $960$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An account earns $21\%$ in the first year and $0\%$ in the second. Find the equivalent constant annual compound interest rate when there are no intervening cash flows. If $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is deposited at the end of year one, compare the final balances under the original rates and the constant rate, starting from the same principal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1+i)^2&=1.21\cdot1=1.21 \\[6pt]
i&=\sqrt{1.21}-1=1.1-1=0.10 \\[6pt]
M_{\mathrm{orig}}&=1.21\,C+200\cdot1=1.21\,C+200 \\[6pt]
M_{\mathrm{cost}}&=1.1^2\,C+200\cdot1.1=1.21\,C+220 \\[6pt]
\Delta M&=M_{\mathrm{orig}}-M_{\mathrm{cost}}=200-220=-20\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **equivalent rate** of $10\%$ reproduces the two-year compound accumulation factor, so the initial principal reaches the same amount under both schedules. If $C$ is the initial principal, it grows to $1.21\,C$ after two years in both cases and cancels in the difference. The deposit of $200$ euros is made at time $1$ and earns interest only during the second year. Under the original schedule the second rate is zero, so its contribution remains $200$ euros. Under the constant rate it contributes $220$ euros. The original schedule's balance is therefore **20 euros lower**. The schedules have the same factor over both years together, but their individual one-year factors differ.

</div>
