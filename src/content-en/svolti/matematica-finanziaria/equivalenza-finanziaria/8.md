## Exercise 1

A purchase can be paid for with $580\,\mathrm{EUR}$ immediately or with $330\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $363\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. At an annual compound interest rate of $10\%$, find the cheaper schedule and the difference in present cost.

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_A(0)&=580 \\[6pt]
K_B(0)&=330q^{-1}+363q^{-2} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{330}{1.1}+\frac{363}{1.21}=300+300=600 \\[6pt]
K_B(0)-K_A(0)&=600-580=20\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
20q^2&=20\cdot1.21=24.20\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Treat the outlays as **positive costs**. The first schedule is due immediately, so its present value is its face amount. In the second schedule discount each payment for its own number of years, dividing the first by $q=1.1$ and the second by $q^2=1.21$. Each is worth $300$ euros today, so the second schedule has a present cost of $600$ euros. The immediate payment costs **20 euros less** at time zero. At time $2$ the same difference is worth $20q^2=24.20$ euros and the ordering is preserved. By the theorem on **equivalence under compound interest**, changing the valuation date multiplies both costs by the same positive factor.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A supplier offers payment of $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today or $20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today plus $528\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. Find the annual compound interest rate at which the offers are equivalent and determine which costs less at a rate of $20\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
500&=20+\frac{528}{1+i} \\[6pt]
\frac{528}{1+i}&=500-20=480 \\[6pt]
1+i&=\frac{528}{480}=1.1 \\[6pt]
i&=10\% \\[6pt]
K_B(0)\big|_{i=20\%}&=20+\frac{528}{1.2}=20+440=460\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
K_A(0)-K_B(0)&=500-460=40\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
K_B(0)\big|_{i=10\%}&=20+\frac{528}{1.1}=20+480=500\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fee is due today and enters the deferred schedule's present cost without discounting. To bring total cost to $500$ euros, the future $528$ euros must be worth $480$ euros today. The equation $\dfrac{528}{1+i}=480$ gives $1+i$ as $528$ divided by $480$. This ratio is $1.1$, so the **break-even interest rate** is $10\%$. At that rate the final line gives a present cost of $500$ euros, equal to the immediate price. At $20\%$ the future payment is discounted more heavily and the fee remains $20$ euros. The future $528$ euros are worth $\dfrac{528}{1.2}=440$ euros today. The deferred schedule costs $20+440=460$ euros, **40 euros less** than immediate payment. The latter costs $500$ euros at every rate because it is due on the valuation date.

</div>
