## Exercise 1

A debt of $726\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is due in two years. The borrower pays $220\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and settles the balance after three years. Find the final payment $X$ at an annual compound interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
726&=220q^{2-1}+Xq^{2-3} \\[6pt]
726&=220q+Xq^{-1} \\[6pt]
Xq^{-1}&=726-220\cdot1.1=726-242=484 \\[6pt]
X&=q(726-220q) \\[6pt]
&=1.1\cdot484=532.4\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\frac{726}{1.21}&=600,\qquad \frac{220}{1.1}+\frac{532.4}{1.331}=200+400=600
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the equation of value on the original due date $t=2$ so the debt enters at its face amount. The down payment was made a year earlier and is worth $220q=242$ euros on that date. The difference of $484$ euros is the balance remaining at time $2$. Settlement is postponed to time $3$, so the balance earns one more year's interest. Isolate $Xq^{-1}$ and multiply both sides by $q$. The final payment is **532.40 euros**. To check, the $726$ euros due at time $2$ are worth $600$ euros today. The replacement schedule is also worth $600$ euros today because the down payment is worth $200$ and the final payment $400$.

## Exercise 2

A debt of $630\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is due in two years. The parties agree on a first payment of $X$ in one year and a second payment of twice that amount in three years. Find both payments at an annual compound interest rate of $20\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
630&=Xq^{2-1}+2Xq^{2-3}=X\left(q+\frac{2}{q}\right) \\[6pt]
q+\frac{2}{q}&=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{2}{\frac{6}{5}}=\frac{6}{5}+\frac{5}{3}=\frac{43}{15} \\[6pt]
X&=630\cdot\frac{15}{43}=\frac{9450}{43}\approx219.77\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
2X&=\frac{18900}{43}\approx439.53\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
630q&=756,\qquad Xq^2+2X=\frac{43}{25}\cdot2X=756
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use time $2$ as the focal date. Accumulate the first payment for one year and discount the second for one year. Since the second payment is twice the first, its value on the focal date has coefficient $2q^{-1}$. Factor out $X$ and divide by $q+2q^{-1}$, which is positive. Writing $q=\dfrac{6}{5}$ gives the coefficient as the sum of fractions $\dfrac{6}{5}+\dfrac{5}{3}$, equal to $\dfrac{43}{15}$. Thus $X$ equals $630$ times $\dfrac{15}{43}$. The payments are approximately **219.77 euros** and **439.53 euros**. The second is twice the exact first amount, so the rounded figures may differ from this ratio by one cent. To check at time $3$, use $630q=Xq^2+2X$. The debt is then worth $756$ euros. Since $q^2=\dfrac{36}{25}$, the sum $Xq^2+2X$ equals $\dfrac{86}{25}X$, or $\dfrac{43}{25}\cdot2X$, also worth $756$ euros.

</div>
