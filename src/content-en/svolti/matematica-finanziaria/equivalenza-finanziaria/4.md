## Exercise 1

A payment schedule is worth $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. It will be replaced by a single payment of $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$ on a date to be determined. Find this date at an annual compound interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
605q^{-T}&=500 \\[6pt]
q^T&=\frac{605}{500}=1.21 \\[6pt]
q^2&=1.1^2=1.21 \\[6pt]
q^T&=q^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T=2\,\mathrm{years} \\[6pt]
T&=\frac{\log(1.21)}{\log(1.1)}=\frac{2\log(1.1)}{\log(1.1)}=2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The amount of $605$ euros is fixed and its payment date is unknown. The present value of $605$ euros at time $T$ is $605q^{-T}$ and must equal the original schedule's $500$ euros. Multiply both sides by $q^T$ and divide by $500$ to obtain the **required accumulation factor**, $1.21$. It is the square of $1.1$, corresponding to exactly two years of compound interest. At a positive rate $q^T$ is strictly increasing, so the **end of year two** is the unique solution. The general formula gives the same date because the logarithm of $1.21$ is twice the logarithm of $1.1$. To check, accumulate the original $500$ euros for two years to get exactly $605$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A schedule worth $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today is replaced by a payment of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at an annual compound interest rate of $10\%$. Find its exact equivalent due date and the payment required if settlement must occur on the third anniversary.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1000q^{-T}&=800 \\[6pt]
q^T&=\frac{1000}{800}=1.25 \\[6pt]
T\log(1.1)&=\log(1.25) \\[6pt]
T&=\frac{\log(1.25)}{\log(1.1)}\approx\frac{0.22314}{0.09531}\approx2.3412\,\mathrm{years} \\[6pt]
X(3)&=800q^3=800\cdot1.331=1064.8\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

It reaches 1,000 euros at $T\approx2.34$ and equals 1,064.80 euros on the third anniversary."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The agreed payment divided by the present value is $1.25$. Since the rate is positive and $1000\ge800$, condition $T\ge0$ holds and settlement falls after the valuation date. Since $q^2=1.21$ and $q^3=1.331$, the required factor is reached between the second and third anniversaries. Taking the natural logarithm of both sides of $q^T=1.25$ brings down the exponent, so $T$ is $\log(1.25)$ divided by $\log(1.1)$. The equivalent date is approximately **2.34 years**, or two years and four months. Keeping the payment at $1000$ euros but moving it to the third anniversary would reduce its present value to $\dfrac{1000}{1.331}\approx751.31$ euros. To preserve the present value of $800$ euros, accumulate it for three years. The required payment is **1,064.80 euros**, worth exactly $800$ euros today.

</div>
