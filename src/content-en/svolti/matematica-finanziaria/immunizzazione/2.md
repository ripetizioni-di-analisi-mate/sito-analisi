## Exercise 1

At a flat reference force of interest, receipts due at times $1$ and $5$ have present values of $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The liability is worth $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in total and has a duration of $3$ years. Find $D_A$ and the derivative of the surplus with respect to the force of interest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0&=300+900=1200\,\mathrm{EUR}=L_0,\\[6pt]
D_A &=\frac{300\cdot1+900\cdot5}{1200}
      =\frac{300+4500}{1200}
      =4\,\mathrm{years},\\[6pt]
S'_0 &=-A_0D_A+L_0D_L\\[6pt]
     &=-1200\cdot4+1200\cdot3\\[6pt]
     &=-4800+3600
      =-1200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The values of the receipts due at times $1$ and $5$ are already in euros at the initial valuation date, so they can be used directly as weights in **Macaulay duration**. Their total, $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$, equals the value of the liability.

The longer security accounts for three quarters of the value, so asset duration is $4$ years, greater than the simple average $3$ of the two dates.

The theorem on matching surplus sensitivity gives $S'=-AD_A+LD_L$. Substituting $A_0=L_0=1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$, $D_A=4$ and $D_L=3$ gives $S'_0=-1200\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$. Since the two present values coincide, the same result can be written as $L_0(D_L-D_A)$.

The values match at the reference force of interest, but the assets have longer duration. When the force of interest rises, their present value therefore falls faster to first order than the value of the debt. The derivative of the surplus is **negative**. For a shift of $h=0.001\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$, the linear term is $-1200\cdot0.001=-1.20\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At an effective annual rate of $25\%$, assets and liabilities are worth $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$, respectively. The liabilities have a Macaulay duration of $5$ years. Find the Macaulay duration of the assets that makes the derivative of the surplus with respect to the effective annual rate zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A'(i)&=-\frac{A(i)D_A(i)}{1+i},\qquad
L'(i)=-\frac{L(i)D_L(i)}{1+i},\\[6pt]
S'_0 &=\frac{-1500D_A+1200\cdot5}{1.25},\\[6pt]
S'_0=0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1500D_A=6000,\\[6pt]
D_A &=\frac{6000}{1500}=4\,\mathrm{years}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivative with respect to the **effective rate** uses modified duration $\dfrac{D}{1+i}$. For each side of the balance sheet, the derivative of value is the negative of the present value times its modified duration.

Since the same rate applies to both sides, the divisor $1.25$ is common to both terms in $S'_0$. A fraction with positive denominator vanishes exactly when its numerator vanishes, so equate the products of the present values and their respective Macaulay durations. For the liabilities this product is $1200\cdot5=6000\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}$.

Dividing $6000$ by the value of the assets gives $D_A=4\,\mathrm{years}$. The assets have a larger present value, so they need a shorter duration to have the same sensitivity in euros.

The initial surplus remains $1500-1200=300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ because the calculation imposes only a zero derivative. Since the present values differ, zero sensitivity requires different durations, $4$ years for the assets and $5$ for the liabilities.

</div>
