## Exercise 1

A liability is worth $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and has a duration of $4$ years. A portfolio invests in two zero-coupon bonds maturing in $2$ and $7$ years. Calculate the market values of the two holdings and the face value of the holding in the shorter bond at an effective annual rate of $20\%$.

$$
\begin{cases}
X_1+X_2=1500,\\
2X_1+7X_2=4\cdot1500=6000,
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2X_1+7X_2)-2(X_1+X_2)&=6000-2\cdot1500,\\[6pt]
5X_2&=3000,\\[6pt]
X_2&=600\,\mathrm{EUR},\qquad X_1=1500-600=900\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
F_1&=900(1.2)^2=900\cdot1.44=1296\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A zero-coupon bond has just one cash flow, so its duration equals its maturity. The two bonds therefore have durations of $2$ and $7$ years.

The first equation fixes the total investment at the value of the liability. The second requires the investment-weighted average of the maturities to equal $4$, or equivalently the product of portfolio value and duration to equal $4\cdot1500=6000$. These are the conditions of the theorem giving the amounts to invest in two bonds, which applies because $2\leq4\leq7$.

Subtracting twice the first equation from the second eliminates $X_1$ and gives the value of the holding in the longer bond, $X_2=600\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The remaining capital, $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$, goes into the shorter bond. The fractions are $\dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\dfrac{2}{5}$, as given by the formulas $w_1=\dfrac{7-4}{7-2}$ and $w_2=\dfrac{4-2}{7-2}$.

The shorter bond pays its face value at its two-year maturity. The price of $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is its present value. Accumulating it using the two-year factor $1.2^2=1.44$ gives a redemption payment of $1296\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At the initial force of interest, liability payments have present values of $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $2$ and $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $8$. Two zero-coupon bonds with maturities $1$ and $5$ are available to cover them. Calculate the amount to invest in each.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_0&=600+300=900\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
D_L&=\frac{600\cdot2+300\cdot8}{900}=\frac{1200+2400}{900}=4\,\mathrm{years},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
X_1+X_2=900,\\
X_1+5X_2=4\cdot900=3600,
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4X_2&=3600-900=2700,\\[6pt]
X_2&=\frac{2700}{4}=675\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
X_1&=900-675=225\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the liabilities fall due on two dates, first calculate their duration using the given present values as weights. The total value is $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and duration is $4$ years.

The duration $4$ lies between the bond maturities $1$ and $5$, so the value and duration equations have a solution with nonnegative investments in both bonds. Subtracting the first equation from the second eliminates $X_1$ and gives $4X_2=2700$.

The five-year bond must carry more weight than the one-year bond because the target duration is one year from the longer maturity and three years from the shorter one. Indeed, the solution invests three quarters of the capital in the five-year bond. The average $\dfrac{1\cdot1+5\cdot3}{4}=4$ confirms that the portfolio has the required duration.

As the diagram shows, the cash flows still differ on individual dates. The portfolio receives payments at times $1$ and $5$, while the liabilities fall due at times $2$ and $8$. The hedge therefore matches value and first-order sensitivity. Reinvesting the bond proceeds covers the liability due in year $8$.

</div>
