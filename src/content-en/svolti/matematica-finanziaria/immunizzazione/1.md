## Exercise 1

At an effective annual rate of $10\%$, a security pays $660\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. A liability requires $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Calculate the initial surplus and the additional liability in year two that would reduce it to zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_A &= 660(1.1)^{-1}=\frac{660}{1.1}=600\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
V_L &= 605(1.1)^{-2}=\frac{605}{1.21}=500\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
S_0 &= V_A-V_L=600-500=100\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
E_2(1.1)^{-2} &= S_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E_2 = 100(1.1)^2=121\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.4 xmax=3.6 ymin=-0.95 ymax=0.95 linee="-0.2,0 3.3,0" vettori="1,0,1,0.66;2,0,2,-0.605" unicolore=1 punti="0,0,pieno" etichette="1,0.8,660;2,-0.76,605;0,-0.14,0;1,-0.14,1;2,0.14,2;3.3,-0.14,t" legenda=0 titolo="An inflow of $660$ euros at time $1$ points upward and a payment of $605$ euros at time $2$ points downward. The effective annual rate is $10\%$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Surplus compares the two amounts **on the same date**, here the initial valuation date. The discount factor for $t$ years at the effective rate $i$ is $(1+i)^{-t}$. Divide the receipt at time $1$ by $1.1$ and the payment at time $2$ by $1.1^2=1.21$. This gives $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The assets therefore exceed the liabilities by $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. The positive surplus means that the security covers the obligation with present value to spare.

An additional payment $E_2$ at time $2$ eliminates the surplus if its present value $E_2(1.1)^{-2}$ equals $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Accumulating that value for two years gives $E_2=121\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The same result follows by carrying the receipt directly to time $2$. Its accumulated value is $660\cdot1.1=726\,\mathrm{EUR}$, which exceeds the original payment by $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$, given that the original payment is $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The flat force of interest is $\delta_0=\log(1.1)$. A portfolio pays $550\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at each of times $1$ and $2$. A debt with a present value of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is due immediately. Calculate the initial surplus and the cash contribution needed today to bring it to zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
e^{-\delta_0 t}&=(1.1)^{-t},\\[6pt]
V_A &= 550(1.1)^{-1}+550(1.1)^{-2}\\[6pt]
    &=500+\frac{550}{1.21}\\[6pt]
    &=500+\frac{5000}{11}
      =\frac{10500}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
S_0 &=\frac{10500}{11}-1000=\frac{10500-11000}{11}=-\frac{500}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
X &=-S_0=\frac{500}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx45.45\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $e^{\delta_0}=1.1$, the given force of interest corresponds to an effective annual rate of $10\%$, and the discount factor for $t$ years is $(1.1)^{-t}$.

The two payments have the same nominal amount. The later payment is discounted for two years, so it is worth $\dfrac{5000}{11}\approx454.55\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today, less than the first payment's present value of $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The assets' present values sum to $\dfrac{10500}{11}\approx954.55\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The debt is already due, so its full amount enters the calculation. The surplus is $-\dfrac{500}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Its negative sign indicates a present-value shortfall.

Cash of $X$ available immediately adds to assets without discounting, so the new surplus is $S_0+X$. It is zero for $X=-S_0$, a deposit of $\dfrac{500}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR}$, approximately $45.45\,\mathrm{EUR}$. After the deposit the assets are worth $\dfrac{10500}{11}+\dfrac{500}{11}=\dfrac{11000}{11}=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$, exactly the amount of the debt.

</div>
