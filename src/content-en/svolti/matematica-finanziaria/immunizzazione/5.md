## Exercise 1

At the reference force of interest, the asset payments have present values of $750\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $2$ and $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $6$. The liability has a present value of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $3$. Verify Redington's conditions and estimate the surplus for a shift of $h=-0.02\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S(\delta_0+h)&=750e^{-2h}+250e^{-6h}-1000e^{-3h},\\[6pt]
S_0&=750+250-1000=0,\\[6pt]
S'_0&=-2\cdot750-6\cdot250+3\cdot1000\\[6pt]
    &=-1500-1500+3000=0,\\[6pt]
S''_0&=4\cdot750+36\cdot250-9\cdot1000\\[6pt]
     &=3000+9000-9000=3000,\\[6pt]
\frac12S''_0h^2&=\frac12\cdot3000\cdot(-0.02)^2
 =1500\cdot0.0004=0.60\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After a shift $h$ in the force of interest, multiply the present value of a payment due at time $t$ by $e^{-ht}$. This expresses the surplus as a function of $h$. To obtain its derivatives at $h=0$, multiply each present value by $-t$ for the first derivative or by $t^2$ for the second.

The equality of the present values verifies the first condition. The maturity-weighted sums of the values agree because $1500+1500=3000$, so the first derivative is zero. Both durations are indeed $3$ years.

The sum of the asset values weighted by squared maturities is $12000$, greater than the corresponding liability sum of $9000$. The second derivative is therefore positive. All three conditions hold, so **Redington's theorem** gives a strict local minimum of the surplus at the force of interest $\delta_0$.

For the given shock, the second-order Taylor term is $0.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The sign of $h$ is immaterial because $h$ is squared. Revaluing each payment at the new force of interest gives the exact surplus, approximately $0.65\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The difference comes from terms beyond second order.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The assets have present values of $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $2$ and $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $4$. The liabilities have present values of $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $3$ and $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $5$. Determine the sign of the exact surplus for $h>0$ and for $h<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S(\delta_0+h)
 &=200e^{-2h}-600e^{-3h}+600e^{-4h}-200e^{-5h}\\[6pt]
 &=200e^{-2h}\big(1-3e^{-h}+3e^{-2h}-e^{-3h}\big)\\[6pt]
 &=200e^{-2h}(1-e^{-h})^3,\\[6pt]
S(\delta_0+h)&>0\quad\text{if }h>0,\\[6pt]
S(\delta_0+h)&<0\quad\text{if }h<0.
\end{aligned}
$$

It is negative to the left of $0$ and positive to the right, with a horizontal inflection point at $h=0$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The four signed coefficients sum to zero, so $S_0=0$. The sums weighted by maturities and squared maturities also vanish because $400-1800+2400-1000=0$ and $800-5400+9600-5000=0$. Thus $S'_0=0$ and $S''_0=0$, and Redington's strict second-order condition fails.

The second-order terms do not determine the sign, so examine the exact expression. Factor out the positive term $200e^{-2h}$. The expression $1-3e^{-h}+3e^{-2h}-e^{-3h}$ has the coefficients $1$, $-3$, $3$ and $-1$ of a binomial cube, making it the cube of $1-e^{-h}$.

For positive $h$ the exponential $e^{-h}$ is less than one, so $1-e^{-h}>0$ and the surplus is **positive**. For negative $h$ the exponential exceeds one, the cube is negative and the surplus is **negative**. Near zero $h$ satisfies $1-e^{-h}\approx h$, so the surplus is approximately $200h^3$ and changes sign at $h=0$.

</div>
