## Exercise 1

At time $2$ a portfolio holds two zero-coupon bonds with original maturity dates $3$ and $8$. At revaluation they are worth $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The remaining liabilities are worth $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and have a remaining duration of $3$ years. Calculate the market value of the trades needed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_1&=3-2=1,\qquad T_2=8-2=6,\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{att}}&=\frac{400\cdot1+600\cdot6}{1000}=4\,\mathrm{years},\\[6pt]
X_2&=1000\cdot\frac{3-1}{6-1}=400\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
X_1&=1000-400=600\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
\Delta X_1&=600-400=200\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
\Delta X_2&=400-600=-200\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Liability duration is already measured from time $2$. For a consistent comparison, subtract $2$ from the original bond maturity dates as well. Their remaining maturities are $1$ and $6$ years.

Using these remaining maturities, the existing portfolio has a duration of $D_{\mathrm{att}}=4$ years, longer than the liability duration of $3$ years. The durations no longer match, so the portfolio needs rebalancing.

The target duration $3$ lies between $1$ and $6$, so the two-bond investment formulas give nonnegative weights. The fraction in the longer bond is $\dfrac{3-1}{6-1}=\dfrac{2}{5}$. The target portfolio therefore holds $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in the longer bond and $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in the shorter one.

The existing portfolio is already worth $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so the trades have zero net cost in this model. Buy $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of the shorter bond using the proceeds from selling $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of the longer bond. The resulting portfolio duration is $\dfrac{600\cdot1+400\cdot6}{1000}=3$ years, equal to the liability duration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At time $1$ a holding in a shorter bond maturing on the original date $3$ is worth $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. A holding in a longer bond maturing on the original date $7$ is worth $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The portfolio also has $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in cash. The remaining liabilities are worth $960\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and have a duration of $3$ years. All cash is to be invested in the bonds. Calculate the purchases or sales and the external contribution needed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_1&=3-1=2,\qquad T_2=7-1=6,\\[6pt]
X_2&=960\cdot\frac{3-2}{6-2}=240\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
X_1&=960-240=720\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
\Delta X_1&=720-200=520\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
\Delta X_2&=240-500=-260\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
E&=\Delta X_1+\Delta X_2-100\\[6pt]
 &=520-260-100=160\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Measure maturities from the new valuation date. Subtract $1$ from the original dates to get $2$ and $6$ years. Since all cash must be invested in the bonds, the target portfolio consists entirely of the two zero-coupon bonds and must equal the liability value of $960\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The target duration $3$ lies one year from the shorter maturity and three years from the longer one. The target therefore allocates one quarter of the investment to the longer bond, $240\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and three quarters to the shorter bond, $720\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

Buy $520\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of the shorter bond and sell $260\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of the longer bond. The trades require a net outlay of $520-260=260\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Since the portfolio has $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in cash available, the external contribution needed is $160\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The same shortfall results from comparing the liability value with the total of $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ initially available, giving $200+500+100$. Trades redistribute value between the bonds without changing the total, so the difference of $960-800=160\,\mathrm{EUR}$ must come from outside the portfolio.

</div>
