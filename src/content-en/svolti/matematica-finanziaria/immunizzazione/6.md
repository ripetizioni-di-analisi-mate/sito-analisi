## Exercise 1

One security pays $420\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and another pays $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $3$. After the shock the same effective annual rate of $10\%$ applies to every transfer. Calculate the value at time $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_2&=420(1.1)^{2-1}+605(1.1)^{2-3}\\[6pt]
   &=420\cdot1.1+\frac{605}{1.1}\\[6pt]
   &=462+550=1012\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The dashed line marks the horizon $H=2$. The first inflow is accumulated forward to $H$, and the second is discounted back to $H$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

To obtain the horizon value, carry each cash flow to time $H=2$ at the same rate. Multiply an amount due at time $t$ by $(1.1)^{2-t}$. The exponent is positive for dates before $H$ and negative for dates after it.

At the target date the first redemption payment has already been available for one year. It can therefore be reinvested until time $2$, when its accumulated value is $420\cdot1.1=462\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The second bond matures one year after the horizon. If sold at the horizon, its price is the present value of the remaining redemption payment discounted for one year, $\dfrac{605}{1.1}=550\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

Both values now refer to time $2$ and can be added. The portfolio's horizon value is therefore $1012\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The result depends on the rate because a higher rate raises the accumulated value of the first payment and lowers the price of the second bond.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At zero force of interest a portfolio pays $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$, $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $2$ and $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $4$. Find the horizon that makes the derivative with respect to the force of interest zero, then calculate the derivative at a horizon of $3$ years.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_H(0)&=300+200+500=1000\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
D(0)&=\frac{300\cdot1+200\cdot2+500\cdot4}{1000}\\[6pt]
    &=\frac{300+400+2000}{1000}
      =2.7\,\mathrm{years},\\[6pt]
W'_H(0)&=(H-D)W_H(0)=(H-2.7)\cdot1000,\\[6pt]
W'_H(0)=0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_*=2.7\,\mathrm{years},\\[6pt]
W'_3(0)&=(3-2.7)\cdot1000=300\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At zero force of interest all factors $e^{\delta(H-t)}$ equal $1$. The nominal amounts therefore equal their present values and sum to the horizon value. Duration is the average of the payment dates with these weights, $2.7$ years.

The sensitivity theorem for value at a target date gives $W_H'=(H-D)W_H$. Since $W_H(0)=1000>0$, the horizon value has zero derivative when the target date equals duration, at $H_*=2.7$ years.

At the three-year horizon, each cash flow contributes its amount times $3-t$ to the derivative. The two earlier payments contribute $300\cdot2=600$ and $200\cdot1=200$, respectively, because they are reinvested. The cash flow at time $4$ contributes $500\cdot(-1)=-500$ because it is discounted at time $3$.

The sum $600+200-500=300$ equals $(3-2.7)\cdot1000$ and is positive, so a rise in the force of interest increases the value at time $3$ to first order. The horizon exceeds duration, so the effect of reinvesting the earlier receipts dominates.

</div>
