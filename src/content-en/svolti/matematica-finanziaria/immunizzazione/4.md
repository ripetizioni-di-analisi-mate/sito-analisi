## Exercise 1

Assets and liabilities are each worth $1600\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The assets have two equal present values at times $1$ and $7$. The liability falls due entirely at time $4$. Calculate the difference in convexity and $S''$ with respect to the force of interest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_A&=\frac{800\cdot1+800\cdot7}{1600}=\frac{1+7}{2}=4\,\mathrm{years}=D_L,\\[6pt]
C_A&=\frac{800\cdot1^2+800\cdot7^2}{1600}=\frac{1+49}{2}=25\,\mathrm{years}^2,\\[6pt]
C_L&=4^2=16\,\mathrm{years}^2,\\[6pt]
C_A-C_L&=25-16=9\,\mathrm{years}^2,\\[6pt]
S''_0&=L_0(C_A-C_L)=1600\cdot9
      =14400\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The assets are split between times $1$ and $7$ (upward), while the entire liability falls at time $4$ (downward)."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each asset date accounts for half the total value, $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so the weights are $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Asset duration is therefore $4$ years, equal to liability duration.

Convexity with respect to the force of interest is the present-value-weighted average of the squared maturities. For the assets it equals $25\,\mathrm{years}^2$. For the liability, which falls due on a single date, it is the square of $4$.

Each asset maturity lies three years from duration, giving a maturity variance of $3^2=9\,\mathrm{years}^2$. The liability has only one maturity, so its variance is zero. Convexity is variance plus the square of duration. Since duration is the same for assets and liabilities, the difference in variances equals the difference in convexities.

Equal present values give $S''_0=L_0(C_A-C_L)$. The second derivative of the surplus therefore equals the common value times the difference in convexities and is $14400\,\mathrm{EUR}\cdot\mathrm{year}^2$. The positive sign, together with the matching values and durations, verifies Redington's conditions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At an effective annual rate of $20\%$, assets and liabilities are both worth $1800\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Half the asset value falls at time $2$ and half at time $6$. The entire liability value falls at time $4$. Calculate the second derivative of the surplus with respect to the effective rate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_A&=\frac{2+6}{2}=4\,\mathrm{years}=D_L,\\[6pt]
C_A&=\frac{2^2+6^2}{2}=20\,\mathrm{years}^2,\\[6pt]
C_L&=4^2=16\,\mathrm{years}^2,\\[6pt]
S''_i&=\frac{A_0(C_A+D_A)-L_0(C_L+D_L)}{(1+i)^2}\\[6pt]
     &=\frac{1800\big[(20+4)-(16+4)\big]}{1.2^2}\\[6pt]
     &=\frac{1800\cdot4}{1.44}
       =\frac{7200}{1.44}=5000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the parameter is the **effective annual rate**. The second derivative of a factor $(1+i)^{-t}$ is $t(t+1)(1+i)^{-t-2}$, so the numerator of convexity uses $t(t+1)=t^2+t$ in place of $t^2$, with the additional denominator $(1+i)^2$. On each side, the weighted average of $t^2+t$ is $C+D$, where $C$ and $D$ are computed with respect to the force of interest using the same weights.

The asset weights are $\dfrac{1}{2}$ at times $2$ and $6$, so $D_A=4$ and $C_A=20$. The liability is due on a single date, giving $D_L=4$ and $C_L=16$.

The durations are equal and the present value is common to both sides, so the term linear in $t$ cancels in the difference. This leaves the difference in second moments, $4\,\mathrm{years}^2$.

Multiplying by the common value gives $7200$. Dividing by the squared factor $1.44$ gives the sensitivity to the specified parameter, $S''_i=5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Since values and durations match, the first derivative of the surplus vanishes. The second derivative is positive, so the surplus has a strict local minimum at the rate of $20\%$.

</div>
