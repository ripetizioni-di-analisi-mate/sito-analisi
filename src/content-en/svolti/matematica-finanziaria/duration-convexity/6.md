## Exercise 1

A portfolio holds three units of bond A at $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ each and one of bond B at $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Their modified durations are $2$ and $5$, and their convexities are $8$ and $30$. Calculate both portfolio measures for a parallel shift.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=3\cdot200+900=1500,\\[6pt]
\omega_A&=\frac{600}{1500}=0.4,\qquad \omega_B=\frac{900}{1500}=0.6,\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{mod},V}&=0.4\cdot2+0.6\cdot5=3.8,\\[6pt]
\mathcal C_V&=0.4\cdot8+0.6\cdot30=21.2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{600\cdot2+900\cdot5}{1500}&=\frac{5700}{1500}=3.8,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{DV01}_V&=1500\cdot3.8\cdot10^{-4}=0.57\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Modified duration is 3.8 and convexity is 21.2.**

<div class="spiegazione">

Holdings are fixed and all yields shift by the same amount, so the theorem on **market-value weights** applies. Multiply A's unit price by three because the position derivative includes all three units. B accounts for $60\%$ of the value even though the portfolio holds just one unit. With a common shock, both the first and second derivatives of total value are sums of the corresponding position derivatives. Dividing by portfolio value gives the market-value-weighted means. Checking the sums in euros gives the same $3.8$ because $600\cdot2+900\cdot5=5700$. Portfolio DV01 is $0.57\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and equals the sum of the position DV01s, $0.12+0.45$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Position A is worth $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and has modified duration $2$. Position B is worth $900\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and has modified duration $5$. A's yield rises by $30$ basis points while B's falls by $15$. Estimate the first-order portfolio value change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y_A&=0.003,\qquad \Delta y_B=-0.0015,\\[6pt]
\Delta V&\approx-\sum_j x_jP_jD_{\mathrm{mod},j}\Delta y_j,\\[6pt]
\Delta P_A&\approx-600\cdot2\cdot0.003=-3.60,\\[6pt]
\Delta P_B&\approx-900\cdot5\cdot(-0.0015)=6.75,\\[6pt]
\Delta V&\approx-3.60+6.75=3.15\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**The portfolio value rises by approximately 3.15 euros.**

<div class="spiegazione">

The yields shift by different amounts, so multiply each sensitivity by its own shift. The market-weight formula for portfolio duration assumes a common shift. Here, add the first-order estimates for the individual positions. Thirty basis points is $0.003$ and fifteen is $0.0015$ in decimal form. A loses value as its yield rises. B's yield decline produces a larger gain in absolute terms. Adding the euro changes gives a positive balance. A parallel shift of $+30$ basis points for both positions would instead give $\Delta V\approx-(1200+4500)\cdot0.003=-17.10\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
