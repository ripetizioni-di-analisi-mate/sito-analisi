## Exercise 1

A bond pays $132\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and $106.48\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $3$. Times are in years and the effective annual yield is $10\%$. Calculate modified duration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.1^3&=1.331,\\[6pt]
A_1&=\frac{132}{1.1}=120,\qquad A_2=\frac{106.48}{1.331}=80,\\[6pt]
P&=120+80=200,\\[6pt]
D&=\frac{120+3\cdot80}{200}=0.6+3\cdot0.4=1.8,\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{mod}}&=\frac{1.8}{1.1}
=\frac{18}{11}\approx1.6364.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta P}{P}&\approx-\frac{18}{11}\cdot0.01\approx-0.016364,\\[6pt]
\Delta P&\approx-200\cdot0.016364\approx-3.27\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Modified duration is approximately 1.6364.**

The first cash flow accounts for $60\%$ of the price, so the duration $D=1.8\,\mathrm{years}$ is closer to time $1$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The second payment is due at time $3$, so apply the factor $1.1^3=1.331$ to discount it. Compute Macaulay duration from the present values because the weights must be shares of a common price. The shares are $0.6$ and $0.4$, so the weighted mean of the dates $1$ and $3$ equals $1.8\,\mathrm{years}$. The cash flows are fixed and positive and the yield is effective annual, so the theorem on **modified duration**, $D_{\mathrm{mod}}=\dfrac{D}{1+y}$, applies. Dividing the average by the annual factor $1.1$ gives relative yield sensitivity. The value $1.6364$ means that a one-percentage-point increase in yield gives an estimated first-order loss of approximately $1.6364\%$ of the price. Multiplying by the price $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives an estimated loss of approximately $3.27\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A zero-coupon bond pays $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in three half-years. The nominal annual yield compounded semiannually is $j=4\%$. Calculate modified duration with respect to $j$ and with respect to the effective semiannual rate $i=\dfrac j2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(j)&=1200\left(1+\frac{j}{2}\right)^{-3},\\[6pt]
P(0.04)&=\frac{1200}{1.02^3}\approx1130.79\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
P'(j)&=1200\cdot(-3)\left(1+\frac{j}{2}\right)^{-4}\cdot\frac12,\\[6pt]
-\frac{P'(j)}{P(j)}
&=\frac{3}{2\left(1+\frac j2\right)}
=\frac{3}{2\cdot1.02}=\frac{25}{17},\\[6pt]
-\frac{1}{P}\frac{\mathrm d P}{\mathrm d i}
&=\frac{3}{1+i}=\frac{3}{1.02}=\frac{50}{17}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Sensitivity with respect to $j$ is approximately $1.4706$ and sensitivity with respect to $i$ is approximately $2.9412$.

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three periods because each period is half a year. The half-year factor is $1+\dfrac j2$, which equals $1.02$ for $j=4\%$. Under the chain rule, differentiation with respect to the nominal rate introduces the inner derivative $\dfrac12$. In the ratio $-\dfrac{P'}{P}$, the amount $1200$ and the power $\left(1+\dfrac j2\right)^{-3}$ cancel, leaving $\dfrac{3}{2\cdot1.02}$. The value $\dfrac{25}{17}$ equals the Macaulay duration of $1.5\,\mathrm{years}$ divided by the half-year factor $1.02$. With the semiannual rate itself as variable, that inner derivative is one. The second sensitivity is twice as large because a given absolute change in $i$ corresponds to twice that change in $j$. For a one-basis-point increase in $j$, the estimated loss is $1130.79\cdot\dfrac{25}{17}\cdot10^{-4}\approx0.17\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
