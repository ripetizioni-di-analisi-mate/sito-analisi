## Exercise 1

A bond is worth $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and has modified duration $4.5$. Calculate DV01 and estimate the first-order price change for a yield decrease of $25$ basis points.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta y&=-25\cdot10^{-4}=-0.0025,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{DV01}&=1200\cdot4.5\cdot10^{-4}
=0.54\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
\frac{\Delta P}{P}&\approx-4.5\cdot(-0.0025)=0.01125,\\[6pt]
\Delta P&\approx-1200\cdot4.5\cdot(-0.0025)
=13.50\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{new}}&\approx1200+13.50=1213.50\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
0.54\cdot25=13.50\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$

**DV01 is 0.54 euros and the price rises by approximately 13.50 euros.**

<div class="spiegazione">

One basis point is $10^{-4}$ in decimal form, so $25$ basis points is $\Delta y=-0.0025$. DV01 measures the estimated loss for a one-basis-point increase and equals price times modified duration times $10^{-4}$. The linear approximation $\dfrac{\Delta P}{P}\approx-D_{\mathrm{mod}}\Delta y$ follows from **modified duration** and is accurate for small shifts. Here the yield falls, so the price change has the opposite sign to the loss measured by DV01. The relative change $+1.125\%$ applied to $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives $13.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Since the relationship is linear, multiplying $0.54\,\mathrm{EUR}$ by $25$ gives the same estimate. With positive cash flows the price is convex in yield, so the exact price after the decline is slightly above the linear estimate of $1213.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bond position is worth $4000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and has DV01 of $1.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find modified duration and the first-order price estimate after a yield increase of $35$ basis points.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{DV01}&=P\,D_{\mathrm{mod}}\,10^{-4},\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{mod}}
&=\frac{1.60}{4000\cdot10^{-4}}=\frac{1.60}{0.4}=4,\\[6pt]
\frac{\Delta P}{P}&\approx-4\cdot0.0035=-0.014,\\[6pt]
\Delta P&\approx-1.60\cdot35=-56\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{new}}&\approx 4000-56=3944\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Modified duration is 4 and the estimated price is 3,944 euros.**

<div class="spiegazione">

DV01 is an absolute amount. Divide by the position value and by the decimal equivalent of one basis point to recover relative sensitivity. From the definition $\mathrm{DV01}=P\,D_{\mathrm{mod}}\,10^{-4}$, this gives $D_{\mathrm{mod}}$ by dividing $1.60$ by $4000\cdot10^{-4}=0.4$. Multiply DV01 by the $35$ basis points to estimate the change in euros because the first-order relationship is linear in the shift. The relative change $-4\cdot0.0035=-1.4\%$ applied to $4000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ also gives $-56\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Since yield increases, the estimated price change is negative and the new price is below the initial price. With positive cash flows the price is convex in yield, so the exact price is slightly higher than the estimate of $3944\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
