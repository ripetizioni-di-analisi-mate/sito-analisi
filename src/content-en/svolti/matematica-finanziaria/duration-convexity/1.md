## Exercise 1

Calculate the Macaulay duration of a bond paying $88\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $145.20\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years, at an effective annual yield of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.1^2&=1.21,\\[6pt]
A_1&=\frac{88}{1.1}=80,\qquad A_2=\frac{145.20}{1.21}=120,\\[6pt]
P&=80+120=200,\\[6pt]
w_1&=\frac{80}{200}=0.4,\qquad w_2=\frac{120}{200}=0.6,\\[6pt]
D&=\frac{1\cdot 80+2\cdot 120}{200}\\[6pt]
&=1\cdot0.4+2\cdot0.6\\[6pt]
&=1.6\,\mathrm{years}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Duration is 1.6 years.**

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=2.6 ymin=0 ymax=150 nomi="t,euro" vettori="1,0,1,88,88;2,0,2,145.2" unicolore=1 etichette="2.06,70,145.20" punti="1,80,pieno,80;2,120,pieno,120;1.6,0,pieno,D" legenda=0 titolo="Payments at times $1$ and $2$ (arrows) and their present values at $10\%$ (filled points). The duration $D=1.6\,\mathrm{years}$ is closer to the second date, which accounts for $60\%$ of the price."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two receipts fall on different dates, so discount each before adding them. The second payment is due in two years, so divide by the two-year accumulation factor $1.1^2=1.21$. This later cash flow is worth $120\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and accounts for $60\%$ of the price. The first accounts for the remaining $40\%$. **Macaulay duration** is the average of $1$ and $2$ using these positive weights, which sum to one. The result lies between the first and last maturities and is closer to year two than to year one. Using $88$ and $145.20$ directly would base the weights on amounts payable at different dates. This would give $\dfrac{88+2\cdot145.20}{233.20}\approx1.6226$, which is not the duration of the bond.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At a zero effective annual rate, a payment schedule provides $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$ and $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $4$, with time in years. Move $75\,\mathrm{EUR}$ from the first date to the second. Calculate the change in duration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=200+200=400,\\[6pt]
D_0&=\frac{200+4\cdot200}{400}=2.5,\\[6pt]
C_1&=200-75=125,\qquad C_4=200+75=275,\\[6pt]
D_1&=\frac{125+4\cdot275}{400}=3.0625,\\[6pt]
D_1-D_0&=0.5625\,\mathrm{years}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{75\,(4-1)}{400}=\frac{225}{400}=0.5625.
$$

**Duration rises by 0.5625 years.**

<div class="spiegazione">

At a zero rate present values equal nominal amounts. Before the transfer the amounts are equal, so both weights are $\dfrac12$ and duration is the midpoint $2.5$ of dates $1$ and $4$. The total payment is unchanged and the price remains $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The new weights are $\dfrac{125}{400}=0.3125$ and $\dfrac{275}{400}=0.6875$, so the average moves toward date $4$. In the numerator, the amount transferred now has time weight $4$ instead of $1$. The numerator increases by $75(4-1)=225$. Dividing by $400$ gives the same change as comparing the two weighted means because the denominator is unchanged.

</div>
