## Exercise 1

Observed prices are $100.402$, $100$, and $99.602\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at yields of $3.9\%$, $4\%$, and $4.1\%$, respectively. Estimate modified duration and convexity at the central yield using centered finite differences.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=0.041-0.04=0.001,\\[6pt]
P'(0.04)&\approx\frac{99.602-100.402}{2\cdot0.001}=\frac{-0.8}{0.002}=-400,\\[6pt]
P''(0.04)&\approx\frac{99.602-200+100.402}{0.001^2}=\frac{0.004}{10^{-6}}=4000,\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{mod}}&\approx-\frac{-400}{100}=4,\qquad \mathcal C\approx\frac{4000}{100}=40.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Estimated modified duration is 4 and estimated convexity is 40.**

<div class="spiegazione">

The three yields are equally spaced and the central price corresponds to $4\%$, so use **centered finite differences**. The step from the central yield to either endpoint is $0.001$. The first difference divides the price change between the endpoints by the full interval $2h$. The numerator is $-0.8\,\mathrm{EUR}$, giving an estimated derivative of $-400$ euros per unit of yield. The second difference also uses the central price to measure its deviation from the chord joining the outer prices. Its numerator is only $0.004\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and its divisor $h^2$ equals $10^{-6}$. Divide both results by $100$, reversing the sign only for the first derivative. These estimates give a first-order loss of $100\cdot4\cdot10^{-4}=0.04\,\mathrm{EUR}$ per basis point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

As a check, round the prices $100.402$, $100$, and $99.602\,\mathrm{EUR}$, quoted at $3.9\%$, $4\%$, and $4.1\%$, to the nearest cent. Calculate how the centered convexity estimate changes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{before}}
&=\frac{100.402+99.602-200}{100\cdot0.001^2}=\frac{0.004}{10^{-4}}=40,\\[6pt]
\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{after}}
&=\frac{100.40+99.60-200}{100\cdot0.001^2}=\frac{0}{10^{-4}}=0,\\[6pt]
\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{after}}-
\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{before}}&=-40.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\widehat{D}_{\mathrm{mod,\,after}}=-\frac{99.60-100.40}{100\cdot0.002}=4.
$$

**The estimate drops from 40 to 0.**

<div class="spiegazione">

Curvature is obtained from the difference between the sum of the two outer prices and twice the central price. Before rounding, that difference is only $0.004\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Division by $100\cdot10^{-6}=10^{-4}$ magnifies the difference ten thousandfold, so an error of one thousandth of a euro changes the estimate by $10$. Rounding to cents reduces each outer price by $0.002\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and eliminates the difference. The modified duration estimate remains $4$ because the outer prices still differ by $-0.80\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The loss of information comes from the precision of the prices used. With a step of $h=0.001$, a convexity of about $40$ requires quotes to at least three decimal places.

</div>
