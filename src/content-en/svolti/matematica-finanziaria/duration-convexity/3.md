## Exercise 1

A bond pays $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Find its convexity at a zero effective annual yield.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(y)&=50(1+y)^{-1}+50(1+y)^{-2},\\[6pt]
P'(y)&=-50(1+y)^{-2}-2\cdot50(1+y)^{-3},\\[6pt]
P''(y)&=2\cdot50(1+y)^{-3}+6\cdot50(1+y)^{-4},\\[6pt]
P(0)&=50+50=100,\\[6pt]
P''(0)&=1(1+1)\cdot50+2(2+1)\cdot50=400,\\[6pt]
\mathcal C(0)&=\frac{400}{100}=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Convexity is 4.**

::grafico{f="50/(1+x/100)+50/(1+x/100)^2;100-1.5*x" xmin=-20 xmax=20 ymin=0 ymax=150 nomi="y,P" punti="0,100,pieno" legenda="P(y);\text{Tangent line}" titolo="Price in euros as a function of the yield $y$ in percentage points, with the tangent at $y=0$, where $P'(0)=-150$. The curve lies above its tangent because $P''(0)=400>0$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Write price as a function of yield and differentiate each term twice. The derivative of $(1+y)^{-t}$ is $-t(1+y)^{-t-1}$, giving a second derivative of $t(t+1)(1+y)^{-t-2}$, which at $y=0$ is $t(t+1)$. At a zero yield both receipts contribute their nominal amounts, so their weights are equal. The second derivative multiplies the first cash flow by $2$ and the second by $6$. The second payment therefore contributes three quarters of the second derivative. Dividing by price gives relative curvature. The weighted formula for **convexity**, $2\cdot0.5+6\cdot0.5=4$, gives the same result because at a zero rate its divisor $(1+y)^2$ equals $1$. Convexity is positive, so price is convex as a function of yield and its graph lies above the tangent $P=100-150y$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A zero-coupon bond pays $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in three years. Its effective annual yield is $20\%$. Calculate convexity with respect to this yield and to the equivalent force of interest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(y)&=500(1+y)^{-3},\qquad P''(y)=12\cdot500(1+y)^{-5},\\[6pt]
\mathcal C_y
&=\frac{3(3+1)}{1.2^2}=\frac{12}{1.44}
=\frac{25}{3},\\[6pt]
\delta&=\log1.2\approx0.1823,\\[6pt]
P(\delta)&=500e^{-3\delta},\qquad P''(\delta)=9\cdot500e^{-3\delta},\\[6pt]
\mathcal C_\delta&=\frac{9\cdot500e^{-3\delta}}{500e^{-3\delta}}=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Convexity is approximately 8.3333 with respect to $y$ and 9 with respect to $\delta$.**

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the zero-coupon formula $\mathcal C=\dfrac{T(T+1)}{(1+y)^2}$ with $T=3$ because the bond makes a single positive payment. The initial price is the same in the two representations because $\delta=\log(1+y)$ and hence $e^{-3\delta}=1.2^{-3}$. It equals $500\cdot1.2^{-3}\approx289.35\,\mathrm{EUR}$ with either variable. The derivatives depend on the parameter. Differentiating a power of $1+y$ twice introduces the successive exponents $3$ and $4$. For the exponential, the same coefficient enters both derivatives, giving $3^2=9$. Normalization cancels the discount factor. With respect to $\delta$, zero-coupon convexity is therefore $T^2$, independent of the rate.

</div>
