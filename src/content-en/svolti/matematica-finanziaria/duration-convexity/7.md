## Exercise 1

A zero-coupon bond pays $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in four years. At an effective annual yield of $10\%$, calculate Macaulay duration, modified duration, and convexity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\frac{1000}{1.1^4}=\frac{1000}{1.4641}\approx683.01\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
D&=4\,\mathrm{years},\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{mod}}&=\frac{4}{1.1}=\frac{40}{11},\\[6pt]
\mathcal C&=\frac{4\cdot 5}{1.1^2}=\frac{20}{1.21}=\frac{2000}{121}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\mathrm{DV01}=683.01\cdot\frac{40}{11}\cdot10^{-4}\approx0.2484\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$

**Macaulay duration is 4 years, modified duration is approximately 3.6364, and convexity is approximately 16.5289.**

<div class="spiegazione">

All the value comes from one payment, so the mean payment time equals the maturity. There is a single positive payment with $T=4>0$ and the yield satisfies $y>-1$. The formulas for the **sensitivity of a single payment** therefore apply. Divide the mean by the annual factor $1.1$ to obtain modified duration. For convexity, use $T(T+1)=20$ and divide by the square of the same factor, $1.21$. The redemption amount determines the price, while the relative measures can be calculated without the amount. Price is needed to obtain measures in euros such as DV01, approximately $0.25\,\mathrm{EUR}$ per basis point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A perpetuity pays $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the end of each year. Its effective annual yield is $5\%$. Find its price, modified duration, and convexity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\frac{60}{0.05}=1200\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
P'(y)&=-\frac{60}{y^2},\qquad
P''(y)=\frac{120}{y^3},\\[6pt]
P'(0.05)&=-\frac{60}{0.0025}=-24000,\qquad P''(0.05)=\frac{120}{0.000125}=960000,\\[6pt]
D_{\mathrm{mod}}&=\frac{24000}{1200}=\frac{1}{0.05}=20,\\[6pt]
\mathcal C&=\frac{960000}{1200}=\frac{2}{0.05^2}=800.
\end{aligned}
$$

**The price is 1,200 euros, modified duration is 20, and convexity is 800.**

<div class="spiegazione">

The payments have present value $\dfrac{60}{y}$ because the first is in one year and the yield is positive. Differentiate this closed-form expression to find sensitivity. The derivative of $y^{-1}$ is $-y^{-2}$ and the second derivative is $2y^{-3}$, yielding coefficients $60$ and $120$. Dividing the derivatives by price gives measures independent of the payment amount. The corresponding Macaulay duration is $20\cdot1.05=21\,\mathrm{years}$. Convexity is large because distant payments have large factors $t(t+1)$. The estimated loss for a one-basis-point increase is $1200\cdot20\cdot10^{-4}=2.40\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
