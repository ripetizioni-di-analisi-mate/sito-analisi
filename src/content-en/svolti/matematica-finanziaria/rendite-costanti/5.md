## Exercise 1

A perpetuity pays $60$ euros annually starting today. Find its value immediately before the first payment at an effective annual interest rate of $5\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(0)&=R+\frac Ri \\[6pt]
&=60+\frac{60}{0.05} \\[6pt]
&=60+1200=1260\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(0)&=q\cdot\frac Ri=1.05\cdot1200=1260\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
1200\cdot0.05&=60\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Its value immediately before the first payment is $1260$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payment of $R=60$ euros and the interest rate $i=0.05$ are both positive. The assumptions of the theorem on the **value of a level perpetuity** hold, so the infinite sum of present values is finite. The first payment is due today, making this a perpetuity due.

The initial payment falls on the valuation date and is worth $60$ euros. The remaining payments form a perpetuity whose first payment is one year away. Dividing the payment by the annual interest rate gives their value, $\dfrac{60}{0.05}=1200$ euros.

Each payment of the perpetuity due occurs one year before its counterpart in a perpetuity payable in arrears. Multiplying the value of the latter by $q=1.05$ gives the same value, $1200\cdot1.05=1260$ euros.

A capital sum of $1200$ euros earning $5\%$ produces $60$ euros of interest each year. After the first payment is made from the additional $60$ euros, the remaining $1200$ euros can generate all subsequent payments.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A contract pays $121$ euros annually in perpetuity, beginning at the end of year three. Find its price at an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(2)&=\frac{121}{0.1}=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
V(0)&=1210v^2 \\[6pt]
&=1210\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^2 \\[6pt]
&=1210\cdot\frac{100}{121}=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1000\cdot1.1^2&=1000\cdot1.21=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
1210\cdot0.1&=121\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The price is $1000$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

The third year ends at time $3$, so the payments fall at times $3,4,5,\ldots$. This is a perpetuity payable in arrears with a deferral of $m=2$ periods. Since the interest rate $i=0.1$ is positive, its value is finite.

The formula for a perpetuity payable in arrears gives the value one period before the first payment. Since that payment is at time $3$, dividing the payment by the rate gives the value at time $2$, namely $1210$ euros. Discount this value for two years using $v^2=\dfrac{100}{121}$ to obtain today's price, as in the formula $v^m\dfrac{R}{i}$ for a deferred perpetuity.

To check, start today. Investing $1000$ euros for two years produces $1210$ euros. From then on, the annual interest is exactly $121$ euros, which covers every payment and leaves the principal at $1210$ euros immediately after each payment.

</div>
