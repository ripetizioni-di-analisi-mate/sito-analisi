## Exercise 1

Find the level deposit required at each of the next three year-ends to have $331$ euros at time $3$. The effective annual interest rate is $10\%$ and the initial balance is zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
331&=R(1.1^2+1.1+1), \\[6pt]
331&=R(1.21+1.1+1), \\[6pt]
331&=3.31R, \\[6pt]
R&=\frac{331}{3.31}=100\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(1)&=100\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
M(2)&=100\cdot1.1+100=210\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
M(3)&=210\cdot1.1+100=331\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The annual deposit is $100$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

The target of $331$ euros is specified at the final date, so use the accumulated value of the deposits, $M=R\,s_{\overline 3|0.1}$. The deposits occur at year-end, at times $1$, $2$, and $3$, and the balance is measured immediately after the last one.

The first deposit earns interest for two years, the second for one year, and the third is added directly to the final balance. The deposits are equal, so factor out $R$ and solve a linear equation. The coefficient $1.21+1.1+1=3.31$ is the factor $s_{\overline 3|0.1}=\dfrac{1.1^3-1}{0.1}$. It is positive, so the **unknown payment** is uniquely determined by $R=\dfrac{M}{s_{\overline 3|0.1}}$.

Division gives exactly $100$ euros. With $R=100$, the balance goes from $100$ at time $1$ to $210$ at time $2$, and finally to $331$ at time $3$. At each date, accumulate the previous balance for one year and add the new deposit. The final balance equals the target, confirming the deposit amount.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A debt of $410$ euros is repaid with two equal year-end installments and an additional payment of $242$ euros at the second due date. Find the regular installment at an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
410&=R(v+v^2)+242v^2, \\[6pt]
242v^2&=242\cdot\frac{100}{121}=200, \\[6pt]
v+v^2&=\frac{110}{121}+\frac{100}{121}=\frac{210}{121}, \\[6pt]
R&=\frac{410-200}{\frac{210}{121}}=210\cdot\frac{121}{210} \\[6pt]
&=121\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=410\cdot1.1-121=451-121=330\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
D_1\cdot1.1&=363=121+242\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The regular installment is $121$ euros. A total of $363$ euros is paid at the second due date.

<div class="spiegazione">

The original principal is known at time zero, so value every payment at that date. The equation of value sets the debt equal to the sum of the present values of the two regular installments and the additional payment.

The additional payment is due at time $2$. Multiplying by $v^2=\dfrac{100}{121}$ gives its present value of $200$ euros. The two regular installments must cover the remaining $410-200=210$ euros. Divide that amount by the two-period ordinary annuity factor, $a_{\overline 2|0.1}=v+v^2=\dfrac{210}{121}$. The result is $121$ euros. On the second due date, add the agreed extra payment to the regular installment.

To check, the initial debt of $410$ euros grows to $451$ in the first year. Paying $121$ euros reduces it to $330$. After one more year it grows to $363$ euros, exactly the final payment. The debt is therefore paid off at the second due date.

</div>
