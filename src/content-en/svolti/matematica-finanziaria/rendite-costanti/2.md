## Exercise 1

Find the price of an annuity that pays $121$ euros at the beginning of each of three years. The effective annual interest rate is $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(0)&=121+121v+121v^2 \\[6pt]
&=121+121\cdot\frac{10}{11}+121\cdot\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^2 \\[6pt]
&=121+110+100=331\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{\overline 3|0.1}&=\frac{1-\frac{1000}{1331}}{0.1}=\frac{3310}{1331}, \\[6pt]
\ddot a_{\overline 3|0.1}&=q\,a_{\overline 3|0.1}=\frac{11}{10}\cdot\frac{3310}{1331}=\frac{331}{121}, \\[6pt]
121\,\ddot a_{\overline 3|0.1}&=331\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The price of the three payments is $331$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three payments are equal and annual, and the first falls on the valuation date. The contract is an annuity due with $R=121$ euros and $n=3$. Apply the **present value formula for payments made one period earlier**, $R\,\ddot a_{\overline n|i}$.

The first payment of an annuity due falls today and enters the price at its full amount. The second and third are due in one and two years, so their discount factors are $v$ and $v^2$. There are three terms even though the final date is time $2$.

First calculate the ordinary annuity factor $a_{\overline 3|0.1}=\dfrac{1-v^3}{i}$, where $v^3=\dfrac{1000}{1331}$. Each payment of the annuity due falls one year before its counterpart in the ordinary annuity. Multiply the factor by $q=1.1$ to obtain $\dfrac{331}{121}$, then by $121$ to obtain $331$ euros. To check, move all three payments one year later. The price would be divided by $1.1$ and fall to approximately $300.91$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A contract pays $169$ euros at the beginning of each of three years. The effective annual interest rate is $30\%$. Find the contract value immediately before the first payment and the value of the remaining payments immediately afterward.

$$
v=\frac{1}{1.3}=\frac{10}{13}, \qquad v^2=\frac{1}{1.69}=\frac{100}{169}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(0^-)&=169+\frac{169}{1.3}+\frac{169}{1.3^2} \\[6pt]
&=169+169\cdot\frac{10}{13}+169\cdot\frac{100}{169} \\[6pt]
&=169+130+100=399\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
V(0^+)&=399-169=230\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V(0^+)=169\,a_{\overline 2|0.3}=169\left(\frac{10}{13}+\frac{100}{169}\right)=130+100=230\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$

Receiving the first payment reduces the value from $399$ to $230$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payments fall at the beginning of each year, at times $0$, $1$, and $2$. They form an annuity due with $n=3$. The symbol $0^-$ denotes time zero immediately before the first payment, and $0^+$ denotes time zero immediately after it.

Before the initial payment is received, it forms part of the claim and is due on the valuation date. It is therefore worth $169$ euros. Discount the other two payments using $v=\dfrac{10}{13}$ and $v^2=\dfrac{100}{169}$. Since $169=13^2$, the products are whole numbers, $130$ and $100$ euros.

Receiving the first payment leaves the valuation date unchanged. Only the set of future payments changes. The remaining payments are due one and two years from now. They form an ordinary annuity with $n=2$ and are still worth $130$ and $100$ euros. The change from $0^-$ to $0^+$ therefore reduces the total by exactly the payment received, $169$ euros.

</div>
