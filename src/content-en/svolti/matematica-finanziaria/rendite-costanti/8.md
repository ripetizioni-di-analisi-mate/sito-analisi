## Exercise 1

Find the effective annual interest rate implied by a price of $210$ euros for two year-end payments of $121$ euros.

$$
\begin{aligned}
210&=121v+121v^2, \\[6pt]
0&=121v^2+121v-210, \\[6pt]
\Delta&=121^2+4\cdot121\cdot210=14641+101640=116281=341^2, \\[6pt]
v&=\frac{-121\pm341}{242}, \\[6pt]
v_1&=\frac{220}{242}=\frac{10}{11}, \qquad v_2=\frac{-462}{242}=-\frac{21}{11}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=121v^2+121v-210=(11v-10)(11v+21), \\[6pt]
v&=\frac{10}{11}, \qquad i=\frac{11}{10}-1=10\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

The effective annual interest rate is $10\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payments fall at times $1$ and $2$, so the equation uses the first two powers of the discount factor $v$. The price of $C=210$ euros and the payment are positive. The theorem on **existence and uniqueness of the implied rate** therefore guarantees exactly one solution $i>-1$. The price is less than the nominal total of $242$ euros, so we expect a positive rate.

Moving all terms to the left gives a quadratic equation in $v$. The discriminant is the perfect square $341^2$, so the roots are rational and the polynomial factors as $(11v-10)(11v+21)$. One root is positive and one negative. Since $v=(1+i)^{-1}$ is positive for every admissible rate $i>-1$, take the positive root $v=\dfrac{10}{11}$, which gives $1+i=\dfrac{11}{10}$.

Substituting $v=\dfrac{10}{11}$ gives present values of $110$ and $100$ euros, which add to the price. The implied effective annual interest rate is therefore $10\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two year-end payments of $100$ euros are worth $175$ euros today. Starting with rates of $0\%$ and $10\%$, use one bisection step to bracket the implied interest rate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(0)&=100+100=200\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
V(0.1)&=\frac{100}{1.1}+\frac{100}{1.1^2} \\[6pt]
&\approx90.91+82.64=173.55\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
i_m&=\frac{0+0.1}{2}=0.05, \\[6pt]
V(i_m)&=\frac{100}{1.05}+\frac{100}{1.05^2} \\[6pt]
&\approx95.24+90.70=185.94\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
V(0.05)&>175>V(0.1) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5\%<i<10\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

After one iteration the implied rate lies between $5\%$ and $10\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The price of $175$ euros is less than the nominal sum of $2\cdot100=200$ euros. By the theorem on **existence and uniqueness of the implied rate**, a unique positive solution exists.

The present value $V(i)$ is continuous and decreasing because each payment is positive and due after time zero. At a zero rate the present value exceeds the price, while at $10\%$ it falls below the price. The unique solution is therefore between those two rates.

Evaluate the midpoint $i_m=5\%$, where $1.05^2=1.1025$. At $5\%$ the present value still exceeds the price, so a higher discount rate is needed. The solution therefore lies between $5\%$ and $10\%$.

The interval is $5$ percentage points wide, half its original width. If the midpoint $7.5\%$ is used as the estimate, its error is at most $2.5$ percentage points. Each further iteration halves that bound.

</div>
