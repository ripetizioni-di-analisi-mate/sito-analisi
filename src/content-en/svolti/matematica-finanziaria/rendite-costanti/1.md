## Exercise 1

Find the present value of two payments of $242$ euros to be received one and two years from now, at an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
q=1+0.1=1.1, \qquad v=\frac{1}{q}=\frac{10}{11}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(0)&=242v+242v^2 \\[6pt]
&=242\cdot\frac{10}{11}+242\cdot\frac{100}{121} \\[6pt]
&=220+200=420\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{\overline 2|0.1}&=\frac{1-v^2}{0.1}=\frac{1-\frac{100}{121}}{0.1}=\frac{210}{121}, \\[6pt]
242\,a_{\overline 2|0.1}&=242\cdot\frac{210}{121}=420\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

At a rate of $10\%$ their present values are $220$ and $200$ euros."}

The present value is $420$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two payments are equal and fall due at the ends of years one and two. They form an ordinary annuity with $R=242$ euros and $n=2$. The effective rate and the payments are both annual, so the period is one year and the discount factor is $v=\dfrac{1}{1.1}=\dfrac{10}{11}$.

The first payment is one year away, so discount it by multiplying by $v=\dfrac{10}{11}$. The second payment is a year further away, so multiply it by $v^2=\dfrac{100}{121}$. The two values can now be added because they refer to the same date. At a positive interest rate both are worth less than $242$ euros today, and the second contributes less than the first.

The **present value of level payments in arrears** gives the same result, $R\,a_{\overline n|i}$. The closed-form expression $a_{\overline n|i}=\dfrac{1-v^n}{i}$ applies because it requires $i\neq0$ and here $i=0.1$. The factor is $\dfrac{210}{121}$. Multiplying it by $242$ gives $420$ euros, in agreement with the two payment dates.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the present value of three annual repayments of $64$ euros at times $1,2,3$ if the effective annual interest rate is $-20\%$. Compare the result with the sum of their nominal amounts.

$$
q = 1-0.2=0.8, \qquad v=\frac{1}{0.8}=\frac{5}{4}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(0)&=64\left(\frac54+\frac{25}{16}+\frac{125}{64}\right) \\[6pt]
&=64\cdot\frac54+64\cdot\frac{25}{16}+64\cdot\frac{125}{64} \\[6pt]
&=80+100+125=305\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{\overline 3|-0.2}&=\frac{1-v^3}{-0.2}=\frac{1-\frac{125}{64}}{-\frac{1}{5}}=\frac{-\frac{61}{64}}{-\frac{1}{5}}=\frac{305}{64}, \\[6pt]
3R&=3\cdot64=192\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
V(0)-3R&=305-192=113\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The present value is $305$ euros, which exceeds the sum of the nominal amounts by $113$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate $-20\%$ is greater than $-1$, so the accumulation factor $q=0.8$ is positive and the **present value of level payments in arrears** is defined. The discount factor is $v=\dfrac{5}{4}$, which is greater than one. Discounting therefore increases the value of each payment.

The factor $q$ lies strictly between zero and one. A principal of $80$ euros invested today therefore falls to $64$ euros after one year. To fund the most distant repayment, reverse this calculation three times. Its contribution to the present value is $64\cdot\dfrac{125}{64}=125$ euros. Each installment is worth more today than its face amount, and the difference is larger for later installments.

In the closed-form expression, the numerator $1-v^3=-\dfrac{61}{64}$ and denominator $i=-\dfrac15$ are both negative, so the factor $a_{\overline 3|-0.2}=\dfrac{305}{64}$ is positive. Multiplying it by $64$ again gives $305$ euros. The three values at time zero therefore add up to more than $3R=192$ euros, as expected for an interest rate between $-1$ and $0$.

</div>
