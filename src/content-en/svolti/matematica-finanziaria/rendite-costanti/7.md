## Exercise 1

Find the number of year-end payments of $121$ euros equivalent to an initial debt of $210$ euros at an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
\frac Ri=\frac{121}{0.1}=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}>210\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
210&=121\frac{1-v^n}{0.1}, \\[6pt]
1-v^n&=\frac{210\cdot0.1}{121}=\frac{21}{121}, \\[6pt]
v^n&=1-\frac{21}{121}=\frac{100}{121} \\[6pt]
&=\left(\frac{10}{11}\right)^2=v^2, \\[6pt]
n&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
n=-\frac{\log\frac{100}{121}}{\log1.1}=\frac{2\log1.1}{\log1.1}=2
$$

Two payments repay the debt.

<div class="spiegazione">

A perpetuity with the same payment is worth $\dfrac{R}{i}=1210$ euros. The debt is smaller, so condition $C<\dfrac{R}{i}$ in the **theoretical term** theorem holds and a finite number of payments can repay the debt.

Isolate $v^n$ in the equation of value. Multiply both sides by $0.1$ and divide by $121$ to obtain $1-v^n=\dfrac{21}{121}$, and hence $v^n=\dfrac{100}{121}$. Since $v=\dfrac{10}{11}$ and the power obtained is the square of $v$, two payments are needed. The logarithmic formula gives the same result because $\log\dfrac{100}{121}=-2\log1.1$.

The term is a whole number, so all payments are equal and no separate final settlement is needed. To check, value the two payments individually. Their present values are $110$ and $100$ euros, which add up to the original debt.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A debt of $160$ euros is repaid with regular year-end installments of $100$ euros at an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$. Determine when the debt will be paid off and how much must be paid in place of the final full installment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
100v&\approx90.91\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
100(v+v^2)&\approx90.91+82.64=173.55\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
100v &<160<100(v+v^2), \\[6pt]
n&=-\frac{\log(1-0.16)}{\log1.1}\approx1.83.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=160q-100=176-100=76\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
S_2&=D_1q=76\cdot1.1=83.60\,\mathrm{EUR}, \\[6pt]
100v+83.60v^2&=\frac{1000}{11}+\frac{8360}{121}=\frac{11000+8360}{121}=160\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The loan is paid off at time $2$ with a final payment of $83.60$ euros.

<div class="spiegazione">

A perpetuity paying $100$ euros is worth $1000$ euros, more than the debt, so the theoretical term is finite. With $\dfrac{Ci}{R}=\dfrac{160\cdot0.1}{100}=0.16$, the formula gives $n\approx1.83$, which is not a whole number. Make $\lfloor n\rfloor=1$ regular installment, followed by a smaller final payment at the next due date.

The present value of one installment falls short of the debt, leaving an outstanding balance after the first due date. Two full installments would exceed the debt in present value, so less than $100$ euros is needed at the second due date.

First find $D_1$, the balance immediately after the first installment, by subtracting that payment from the accumulated debt. The accumulated debt is $160\cdot1.1=176$ euros, giving $D_1=176-100=76$ euros. Then accumulate $D_1$ to the following due date, one year later. To check, discount the first installment and the final payment. Their present values sum to $160$ euros.

</div>
