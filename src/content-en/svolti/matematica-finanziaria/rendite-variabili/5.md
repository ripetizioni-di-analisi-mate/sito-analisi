## Exercise 1

A fund receives continuous deposits at a constant rate of $600\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$ from today to time $2$. The force of interest is constant at $\delta=0.05\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. Find the accumulated value at time $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_2&=\int_0^2 600e^{0.05(2-t)}\,dt\\[6pt]
&=\left[-\frac{600}{0.05}e^{0.05(2-t)}\right]_0^2\\[6pt]
&=-12000e^{0}+12000e^{0.10}\\[6pt]
&=12000(e^{0.10}-1)\\[6pt]
&\approx12000\cdot0.10517\approx1262.05.
\end{aligned}
$$

The accumulated value is approximately $1262.05\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the accumulated-value formula for a **continuous cash flow**, $M_T=\int_0^T c(t)e^{\delta(T-t)}\,dt$, with $c(t)=600$ euros per year and $T=2$. Both the payment rate and the force of interest use years as the time unit, so the units agree and the product $c(t)\,dt$ is an amount in euros.

A deposit at time $t$ stays invested until time $2$, so its accumulation factor has exponent $0.05(2-t)$. The negative sign in the antiderivative comes from differentiating $2-t$, whose derivative is $-1$. The coefficient $\dfrac{600}{0.05}$ equals $12000$ euros.

At the upper endpoint the exponent is zero and the antiderivative equals $-12000$. At the lower endpoint the exponent is $0.10$ and the antiderivative equals $-12000e^{0.10}$. Subtracting the lower-endpoint value from the upper-endpoint value gives $12000(e^{0.10}-1)$.

This agrees with the formula $M_T=\dfrac{c(e^{\delta T}-1)}{\delta}$ for a constant payment rate. The accumulated value, **approximately $1262.05$ euros**, exceeds the $1200$ euros deposited because at a positive force of interest each deposit earns interest until time $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A continuous cash flow is paid at a rate of $900\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$ from time $1$ to time $3$. Given a constant force of interest $\delta=0.04\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$, find its value at time $0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_0&=\int_1^3 900e^{-0.04t}\,dt\\[6pt]
&=\left[-\frac{900}{0.04}e^{-0.04t}\right]_1^3\\[6pt]
&=-22500e^{-0.12}+22500e^{-0.04}\\[6pt]
&=22500(e^{-0.04}-e^{-0.12})\\[6pt]
&\approx22500(0.96079-0.88692)\approx1662.05.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V_1=22500\left(1-e^{-0.08}\right)\approx1729.88,\qquad
V_0=e^{-0.04}V_1\approx1662.05
$$

The value at time zero is approximately $1662.05\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payment rate is constant on $[1,3]$ and zero outside this interval, so the integration limits are $1$ and $3$. Since the valuation date is $0$, the exponent of the discount factor uses the time $t$ measured from today.

An antiderivative of $900e^{-0.04t}$ is $-22500e^{-0.04t}$ because the derivative of $e^{-0.04t}$ is $-0.04e^{-0.04t}$ and $\dfrac{900}{0.04}=22500$. Subtracting its value at the lower endpoint from its value at the upper endpoint gives $22500(e^{-0.04}-e^{-0.12})$.

Alternatively, apply $\dfrac{c(1-e^{-\delta T})}{\delta}$ over two years to value the stream at its start. This gives approximately $1729.88$ euros at time $1$. Discount for one more year using $e^{-0.04}$. The calculations agree because factoring $e^{-0.04}$ out of $e^{-0.04}-e^{-0.12}$ gives $e^{-0.04}(1-e^{-0.08})$. The value at time zero is **approximately $1662.05$ euros**, less than the $1800$ euros paid over the two years.

</div>
