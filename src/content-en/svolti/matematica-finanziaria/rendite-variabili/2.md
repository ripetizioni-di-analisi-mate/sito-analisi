## Exercise 1

An annuity makes three year-end payments. The first is $110$ euros and each subsequent payment is $11$ euros larger. Find its present value at an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$.

::grafico{assi=0 unicolore=1 legenda=0 xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.4 ymin=-0.45 ymax=1.75 linee="-0.3,0 3.5,0;3.38,0.06 3.5,0 3.38,-0.06;0,-0.04 0,0.04;1,-0.04 1,0.04;2,-0.04 2,0.04;3,-0.04 3,0.04" vettori="1,0,1,1.167;2,0,2,1.283;3,0,3,1.4" etichette="0.88,1.287,110;1.88,1.403,121;2.88,1.52,132;-0.04,-0.2,0;0.96,-0.2,1;1.96,-0.2,2;2.96,-0.2,3;3.55,-0.03,t (years)" titolo="Payments of $110$, $121$, and $132$ euros at times $1$, $2$, and $3$. Each payment exceeds the previous one by $11$ euros. The arrow heights are proportional to the amounts."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_2&=110+11=121,\qquad R_3=121+11=132,\\[6pt]
V_0&=\frac{110}{1.1}+\frac{121}{1.1^2}+\frac{132}{1.1^3}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{110}{1.1}+\frac{121}{1.21}+\frac{132}{1.331}\\[6pt]
&=100+100+\frac{132}{1.331}\approx299.17.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{\overline 3|0.1}&=v+v^2+v^3\approx2.4869,\\[6pt]
G_3(v)&=v^2+2v^3=\frac{3100}{1331}\approx2.3291,\\[6pt]
V_0&=110\,a_{\overline 3|0.1}+11\,G_3(v)\\[6pt]
&\approx273.55+25.62=299.17.
\end{aligned}
$$

The present value is approximately $299.17\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The $11$ euros represent the difference between consecutive payments. Add this amount once to obtain the second payment and twice to obtain the third, in accordance with the formula $R_k=R_1+(k-1)d$ for **payments with a fixed increment**.

Discount each payment by raising $v=\dfrac{1}{1.1}$ to its payment date. The present values of the first two payments are equal because the ratio of their nominal amounts, $\dfrac{121}{110}=1.1$, equals the one-year accumulation factor. The ratio of the third payment to the second, $\dfrac{132}{121}\approx1.09$, is smaller than $1.1$, so the third payment has a lower present value of approximately $99.17$ euros.

For a second calculation, use the decomposition in the theorem. The term $110\,a_{\overline 3|0.1}$ is the value of three level payments equal to the first. The term $11\,G_3(v)$ is the value of the increments, added once at time $2$ and twice at time $3$. Since $n=3$ gives $G_3(v)=0\cdot v+1\cdot v^2+2\cdot v^3$, the closed-form expression is unnecessary. Both approaches give **approximately $299.17$ euros**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three year-end payments decrease by $30$ euros per payment. At a zero annual interest rate their total present value is $360$ euros. Find the first payment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_2&=R_1-30,\qquad R_3=R_1-60,\\[6pt]
360&=R_1+(R_1-30)+(R_1-60)=3R_1-90,\\[6pt]
3R_1&=360+90=450,\\[6pt]
R_1&=\frac{450}{3}=150.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
R_1+(n-1)d=150+2\cdot(-30)=90>0
$$

The first payment is $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payments form an arithmetic sequence with common difference $d=-30$ euros, so the second payment is $30$ euros less than the first and the third is $2\cdot30=60$ euros less than the first.

At a zero rate every discount factor is one and the present value equals the sum of the nominal payments. In the notation of the theorem, $a_{\overline 3|0}=3$ and $G_3(1)=\dfrac{3\cdot2}{2}=3$, giving $V_0=3R_1+3d=3R_1-90$.

All three payments contain the base amount $R_1$, and their adjustments sum to $-90$ euros. This gives a linear equation with coefficient $3$ and solution $R_1=150$ euros.

Since the payments decrease, check that the smallest, the last payment, is nonnegative. It equals $90$ euros. The three payments, **$150$, $120$, and $90$ euros**, are all positive and sum to $360$ euros.

</div>
