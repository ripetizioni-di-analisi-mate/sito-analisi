## Exercise 1

An account receives $1000$ euros today and earns an effective annual interest rate of $10\%$. Two equal year-end deposits will follow. Find the deposit required to reach $2260$ euros at the end of year two.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2260&=1000\cdot1.1^2+R\cdot1.1+R\\[6pt]
&=1210+2.1R,\\[6pt]
R&=\frac{2260-1210}{2.1}=\frac{1050}{2.1}=500.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_0&=1000,\\[6pt]
B_1&=1.1\cdot1000+500=1600,\\[6pt]
B_2&=1.1\cdot1600+500=2260.
\end{aligned}
$$

Open points show balances before the deposits and filled points show balances immediately afterward."}

Each periodic deposit is $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the **equation for the savings plan**, valuing every deposit at the target date $n=2$. The initial deposit earns interest for two years and contributes $1000\cdot1.21=1210$ euros at the final date.

The two deposits must provide the remaining $2260-1210=1050$ euros. The first, made at the end of year one, earns one year's interest and is worth $1.1R$ at time $2$. The second is made on the target date and is worth $R$. Together the new deposits contribute $1.1R+R=2.1R$, where $2.1=s_{\overline 2|0.1}$ is the accumulated value of two unit year-end payments.

Dividing $1050$ by $2.1$ gives $R=500$ euros. The result also satisfies the balance recurrence $B_k=qB_{k-1}+R_k$. Immediately after the first deposit the balance is $1600$ euros. It grows to $1760$ euros by the end of year two and rises to $2260$ euros with the second deposit. Each periodic deposit is therefore **$500$ euros**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An account earns $10\%$ per year. Three year-end deposits are scheduled, the first of $500$ euros and the others increasing by $10\%$ per year. An additional deposit at time $2$ is required to reach $2000$ euros at time $3$. Find the additional deposit.

$$
R_1=500,\qquad R_2=500\cdot1.1=550,\qquad R_3=550\cdot1.1=605
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_3&=500q^2+550q+605\\[6pt]
&=500\cdot1.21+550\cdot1.1+605\\[6pt]
&=605+605+605=1815,\\[6pt]
qX&=2000-1815=185,\\[6pt]
X&=\frac{185}{1.1}=\frac{1850}{11}\approx168.18.
\end{aligned}
$$

The additional deposit is approximately $168.18\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three scheduled deposits form a geometric sequence with common ratio $1.1$, so their amounts are $500$, $550$, and $605$ euros. Accumulate each deposit to time $3$ using $q^{3-t_k}$, where $t_k$ is its deposit date.

The deposits grow at the same rate as the account earns interest, so at time $3$ each scheduled deposit has the same accumulated value of $605$ euros. Their total accumulated value is $3\cdot605=1815$ euros.

The shortfall of $185$ euros is measured at the target date. The additional deposit is made a year earlier, so it earns one year's interest and has final value $Xq$. The equation for the savings plan is therefore $1815+1.1X=2000$.

Dividing $185$ by $q=1.1$ gives the deposit at time $2$, namely $\dfrac{1850}{11}$ euros. The additional deposit is **approximately $168.18$ euros**.

</div>
