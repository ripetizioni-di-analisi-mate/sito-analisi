## Exercise 1

A continuous annuity runs for four years and pays at a rate of $c(t)=250e^{0.03t}$ euros per year. The force of interest is $\delta=0.03\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. Find the present value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c(t)e^{-\delta t}&=250e^{0.03t}e^{-0.03t}\\[6pt]
&=250e^{(0.03-0.03)t}=250,\\[6pt]
V_0&=\int_0^4 250\,dt=250\cdot4=1000.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
c(4)=250e^{0.12}\approx281.87
$$

The present value is $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payment rate has the form $c_0e^{\gamma t}$ with $c_0=250$ euros per year and $\gamma=0.03\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. Apply the theorem on **valuing an exponential payment rate**. The amount received during a short interval of length $dt$ is $c(t)\,dt$, with present value $c(t)e^{-\delta t}\,dt$.

The payment rate increases, while the discount factor for later payments decreases at exactly the same rate because $\gamma=\delta$. The product of the two exponentials is $e^{0}=1$, giving a constant discounted payment rate of $250$ euros per year.

The integral of a constant over four years is four times that constant. This agrees with the formula $V_0=c_0T$ for the case $\delta=\gamma$.

After time $0$, the nominal payment rate exceeds $250$ euros per year. For example, at the end of year four it is approximately $281.87$ euros per year. Discounting brings each amount back to its initial level, so each year contributes $250$ euros in present value. The total present value is **$1000$ euros**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A continuous perpetuity pays at a rate of $c(t)=300e^{0.01t}$ euros per year from today onward. The force of interest is $0.05\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. Find the value at time $0$ of only the payments from time $2$ onward.

$$
c(t)e^{-0.05t}=300e^{(0.01-0.05)t}=300e^{-0.04t}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_0&=\int_2^{+\infty}300e^{-0.04t}\,dt\\[6pt]
&=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\left[-\frac{300}{0.04}e^{-0.04t}\right]_2^T\\[6pt]
&=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{300}{0.04}
  (e^{-0.08}-e^{-0.04T})\\[6pt]
&=7500e^{-0.08}\approx7500\cdot0.92312\approx6923.37.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\int_0^{2}300e^{-0.04t}\,dt=7500\left(1-e^{-0.08}\right)\approx576.63,
\qquad 7500-576.63=6923.37
$$

The value of payments from time $2$ onward is approximately $6923.37\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payment rate is exponential, with $c_0=300$ euros per year and $\gamma=0.01\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. Multiplying it by the discount factor $e^{-0.05t}$ gives $300e^{-0.04t}$ because the exponents add.

The integral begins at time $2$ because only payments from that date onward are included. Each must still be discounted to time $0$, so its exponent remains $-0.04t$ with $t$ measured from today. The improper integral is the limit of the integrals between $2$ and $T$ as $T\to+\infty$.

The difference $\delta-\gamma=0.04$ is positive, so the term $e^{-0.04T}$ tends to zero and the improper integral **converges**. Only the contribution from the lower endpoint remains, $7500e^{-0.08}$.

The entire annuity starting at time $0$ is worth $\dfrac{c_0}{\delta-\gamma}=\dfrac{300}{0.04}=7500$ euros. Subtracting the present value of the first two years, approximately $576.63$ euros, again gives the value of the remaining payments, **approximately $6923.37$ euros**.

</div>
