## Exercise 1

Find the value at time $1$ of $5000$ euros receivable at time $3$ if the annual force of interest is $\delta(t)=0.02+0.01t$ and $t$ is measured in years.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^3\delta(t)\,dt
&=\Bigl[0.02t+0.005t^2\Bigr]_1^3\\[6pt]
&=(0.06+0.045)-(0.02+0.005)\\[6pt]
&=0.105-0.025=0.08,\\[6pt]
V_1&=5000e^{-0.08}\approx5000\cdot0.92312\approx4615.58.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,0.02+0.01*t,0,4.4" area="0.02+0.01*x;0;1;3" punti="1,0.03,pieno;3,0.05,pieno" segmenti="1,0,1,0.03;3,0,3,0.05" etichette="0.35,0.038,δ(1) = 0.03;2.35,0.058,δ(3) = 0.05" xmin=-0.2 xmax=4.5 ymin=0 ymax=0.07 nomi="t,δ(t)" legenda="\delta(t)=0.02+0.01t" titolo="Increasing force of interest, in $\mathrm{year}^{-1}$. The shaded area between $1$ and $3$ is a trapezoid with parallel sides $0.03$ and $0.05$ and height $2$. Its area is $0.08$."}

The value at time $1$ is approximately $4615.58\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of the **discount factor between two dates**, a payment of $5000$ euros due at time $3$ is worth that amount multiplied by $v(1,3)=\exp\left(-\int_1^3\delta(t)\,dt\right)$ at time $1$. We therefore need the integral of the force of interest between the two dates.

The integral begins at time $1$, the date at which the payment is valued. An antiderivative of $0.02+0.01t$ is $0.02t+0.005t^2$, and the difference between its values at $3$ and $1$ is $0.08$. Integrating from $0$ to $2$ would cover the same length of time but would use lower values of the force and give $0.06$.

The force of interest is an affine function, so the area beneath its graph between $1$ and $3$ is a trapezoid. The force increases from $0.03$ to $0.05$, giving an average of $0.04$. Over two years the integral is $0.04\cdot2=0.08$, in agreement with the antiderivative calculation.

The exponent is dimensionless because a force of interest measured in $\mathrm{year}^{-1}$ is integrated over time measured in years. The value at time $1$ is **approximately $4615.58$ euros**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A continuous cash flow is paid at a rate of $100\,\mathrm{EUR/year}$ from today to time $2$. The force of interest is zero in the first year and $0.04\,\mathrm{year}^{-1}$ in the second year. Find the present value.

$$
v(0,t)=
\begin{cases}
1, & 0\leq t\leq1,\\[4pt]
e^{-0.04(t-1)}, & 1<t\leq2.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_0&=\int_0^1 100\,dt+\int_1^2 100e^{-0.04(t-1)}\,dt\\[6pt]
&=100+\left[-\frac{100}{0.04}e^{-0.04(t-1)}\right]_1^2\\[6pt]
&=100+2500(1-e^{-0.04})\\[6pt]
&\approx100+2500\cdot0.03921\approx198.03.
\end{aligned}
$$

The present value is approximately $198.03\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

A continuous cash flow with a variable force of interest is valued using $\int_0^T c(t)v(0,t)\,dt$, where $v(0,t)$ is the exponential of the negative integral of the force between $0$ and $t$. Since the force differs between years, the discount factor has two expressions. Split the integral into two intervals.

During the first year the discount factor is one because the force is zero throughout the interval preceding each payment. The first interval therefore contributes its nominal amount, $100$ euros.

For a payment at time $t$ during the second year, the integral of the force equals $0$ between $0$ and $1$ and equals $0.04(t-1)$ between $1$ and $t$. Interest in the second year accrues from time $1$ to the payment date $t$, so the exponent contains $t-1$. An antiderivative of $100e^{-0.04(t-1)}$ is $-2500e^{-0.04(t-1)}$, which equals $-2500e^{-0.04}$ at $2$ and $-2500$ at $1$.

The payment rate is the same on both intervals. The discount factor changes between them. The total is consequently just below $200$ euros, **approximately $198.03$ euros**.

</div>
