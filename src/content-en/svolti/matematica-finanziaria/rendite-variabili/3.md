## Exercise 1

The first of three year-end payments is $220$ euros. Subsequent payments grow by $10\%$ per year. Find their present value when the annual interest rate is also $10\%$.

$$
R_1=220,\qquad R_2=220\cdot1.1=242,\qquad R_3=242\cdot1.1=266.20
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=(1+g)v=\frac{1.1}{1.1}=1,\\[6pt]
V_0&=\frac{220}{1.1}(1+\rho+\rho^2)\\[6pt]
&=200(1+1+1)=600.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{242}{1.21}=200,\qquad \frac{266.20}{1.331}=200
$$

The present value is $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The payments satisfy $R_k=R_1(1+g)^{k-1}$ with $g=10\%$, so apply the theorem for **payments with a constant percentage growth rate**. The ratio of consecutive present values is $\rho=(1+g)v$, the payment growth factor divided by the one-year accumulation factor.

Between successive payment dates, the payment growth factor equals the accumulation factor, so $\rho=1$. The two effects cancel in the present value of the next payment. Each contribution therefore equals the present value of the first payment, $\dfrac{220}{1.1}=200$ euros. Discounting the second and third payments directly confirms the result.

The case $i=g$ uses the second formula in the theorem, $V_0=\dfrac{nR_1}{1+i}=\dfrac{3\cdot220}{1.1}$, which gives the same result. Because there are finitely many payments, their present values can be added even when all contributions are equal. The present value is **$600$ euros**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An annuity makes three annual payments at times $2$, $3$, and $4$. The first payment is $648$ euros, annual payment growth is $5\%$, and the effective annual interest rate is $20\%$. Find the present value at time $0$.

::grafico{assi=0 unicolore=1 legenda=0 xmin=-0.5 xmax=5.4 ymin=-0.45 ymax=1.75 linee="-0.3,0 4.5,0;4.38,0.06 4.5,0 4.38,-0.06;0,-0.04 0,0.04;1,-0.04 1,0.04;2,-0.04 2,0.04;3,-0.04 3,0.04;4,-0.04 4,0.04" vettori="2,0,2,1.27;3,0,3,1.333;4,0,4,1.4" etichette="1.88,1.39,648;2.76,1.453,680.40;3.76,1.52,714.42;-0.04,-0.2,0;0.96,-0.2,1;1.96,-0.2,2;2.96,-0.2,3;3.96,-0.2,4;4.55,-0.03,t (years)" titolo="Payments from a deferred geometric annuity, in euros, at times $2$, $3$, and $4$. Payments grow at $5\%$. No payments are made at times $0$ and $1$."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{1.05}{1.20}=\frac{7}{8},\\[6pt]
V_1&=\frac{648}{1.20}(1+\rho+\rho^2)\\[6pt]
&=540\left(1+\frac{7}{8}+\frac{49}{64}\right)=540\cdot\frac{169}{64},\\[6pt]
V_0&=\frac{V_1}{1.20}\\[6pt]
&=450\cdot\frac{169}{64}
\approx1188.28.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{648}{1.2^2}+\frac{680.40}{1.2^3}+\frac{714.42}{1.2^4}
=450+393.75+344.53\approx1188.28
$$

The present value is approximately $1188.28\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The first payment is at time $2$, so the annuity is **deferred** by one period relative to the payment dates $1$, $2$, and $3$ of an ordinary annuity. Time $1$ is one period before the first payment, so the ordinary annuity formula applies there.

The ratio between consecutive present values is $\rho=\dfrac{1.05}{1.20}=\dfrac{7}{8}$, less than $1$ because interest exceeds payment growth. Each present value is therefore $\dfrac{7}{8}$ times the preceding one. The sum $1+\dfrac{7}{8}+\dfrac{49}{64}$ equals $\dfrac{64+56+49}{64}=\dfrac{169}{64}$. Multiplying by $\dfrac{648}{1.2}=540$ euros gives the value at time $1$, approximately $1425.94$ euros.

To obtain today's value, discount for one more year by dividing by $1.20$. The coefficient $540$ then becomes $450$. To check directly, use the payments of $648$, $648\cdot1.05=680.40$, and $680.40\cdot1.05=714.42$ euros, discounting each with an exponent equal to its due date. The present value is **approximately $1188.28$ euros**.

</div>
