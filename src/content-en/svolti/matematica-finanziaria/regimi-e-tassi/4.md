## Exercise 1

Find the present value and rational discount on a receivable of $5250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ due in six months at a simple annual interest rate of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{6}{12}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{year}\\[6pt]
1+it&=1+0.10\cdot\frac{1}{2}=1.05\\[6pt]
V&=\frac{M}{1+it}=\frac{5250}{1.05}=5000\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
D&=M-V=5250-5000=250\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
D=Vit=5000\cdot0.10\cdot\frac{1}{2}=250\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.8 xmax=7.4 ymin=-3 ymax=3.2 linee="-0.4,0 6.6,0" vettori="6,0,6,2.625,+5250;0,0,0,-2.5,−5000;6.5,0,7,0" etichette="-0.28,-0.4,0;5.85,-0.4,6;6.85,-0.4,months" segmenti="1,-0.08,1,0.08;2,-0.08,2,0.08;3,-0.08,3,0.08;4,-0.08,4,0.08;5,-0.08,5,0.08" titolo="Cash flows from the perspective of the party advancing the funds, with time in months. At time $0$ this party pays $5000$ euros (outflow) and six months later receives $5250$ euros (inflow)."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the rate is annual, six months corresponds to $t=\dfrac{1}{2}$. Under **rational discount**, the present value $V$ is the principal that would grow to the maturity value under simple interest. It must therefore satisfy $M=V(1+it)$.

The six-month accumulation factor is $1+it=1.05$. At $10\%$ simple annual interest the principal increases by $5\%$ over six months. The factor is positive, so divide the future amount by $1.05$ to obtain $V=5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The discount is the future amount less the present value, giving $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The formula $D=Vit$ gives the same amount by calculating interest on the present value. Here the discount is $5\%$ of the present value of $5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Commercial discount at the same rate would give $5250\cdot0.05=262.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The present value under rational discount is **$5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$** and the discount is **$250\,\mathrm{EUR}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the annual commercial discount rate that gives the same present value over two years as rational discount at a simple annual interest rate of $12.5\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1+it&=1+0.125\cdot2=1.25\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{1+it}&=\frac{1}{1.25}=0.80\\[6pt]
1-2d&=0.80\\[6pt]
2d&=0.20\\[6pt]
d&=0.10=10\%
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
d=\frac{i}{1+it}=\frac{0.125}{1.25}=0.10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two discount arrangements are **equivalent** if they give the same present value for the same maturity amount $M$. Rational discount gives $\dfrac{M}{1+it}$ and commercial discount gives $M(1-dt)$. Divide both by $M>0$ to compare their discount factors directly.

With $i=0.125$ and $t=2$, the simple-interest accumulation factor is $1.25$, so the rational discount factor is $0.80$. The commercial discount must therefore deduct $20\%$ of the maturity value. Under commercial discount the fraction deducted is $dt=2d$, so solve $1-2d=0.80$.

Commercial discount is proportional to time. The annual rate is therefore half the two-year discount of $20\%$. The formula $d=\dfrac{i}{1+it}$ gives the same result. The condition $dt<1$ holds because $dt=0.20$.

As a check, a two-year receivable of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is worth $\dfrac{100}{1.25}=80\,\mathrm{EUR}$ under rational discount and $100\,(1-0.20)=80\,\mathrm{EUR}$ under commercial discount. The equivalent annual commercial discount rate is **$10\%$**.

</div>
