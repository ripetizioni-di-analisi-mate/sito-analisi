## Exercise 1

Find the accumulated value of $2500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after three years at an effective annual compound interest rate of $10\%$. Also find the interest earned during year three.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_1&=2500\cdot1.10=2750\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
M_2&=2750\cdot1.10=3025\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
I_3&=M_2\cdot0.10=3025\cdot0.10=302.50\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
M_3&=3025+302.50=3327.50\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
M_3=Cq^3=2500\cdot1.10^3=2500\cdot1.331=3327.50\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,2500*1.1^t,0,3.2" xmin=-0.45 xmax=3.5 ymin=2400 ymax=3400 nomi="t,M" legenda="M(t)=2500\cdot1.1^t" punti="0,2500,pieno;1,2750,pieno;2,3025,pieno;3,3327.5,pieno" segmenti="3,3025,3,3327.5;2,3025,3,3025" etichette="0.12,2440,2500;1.1,2700,2750;2.1,2975,3025;2.2,3330,3327.50;3.08,3170,I₃" titolo="Accumulated value of $2500$ euros at an annual compound rate of $10\%$, with $t$ in years. The vertical segment at $t=3$ is the third year’s interest of $302.50$ euros."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate is an effective annual rate, so multiply the current balance by $q=1.10$ at each year-end. The balance after year one is $2750\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Applying the same factor to $2750\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives a balance of $3025\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after year two.

By the start of year three the balance already includes the interest earned in years one and two. Third-year interest therefore accrues on $M_2=3025\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and equals $302.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Under simple interest the deposit would earn only $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ that year, or $10\%$ of the original principal. The difference of $52.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the interest earned on the interest from the first two years.

Add third-year interest to the balance after two years to obtain the three-year accumulated value. The formula $M=Cq^n$ with $n=3$ gives the same answer because $1.10^3=1.331$.

After three years the accumulated value is **$3327.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$**, including **$302.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$** in interest earned during the third year.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At what effective annual compound interest rate does $6400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ grow to $7744\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years?

$$
\begin{aligned}
6400\,(1+i)^2&=7744\\[6pt]
(1+i)^2&=\frac{7744}{6400}=\frac{121}{100}=1.21\\[6pt]
1+i&=\sqrt{1.21}=1.10\\[6pt]
i&=0.10=10\%
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
6400\cdot1.10=7040\,\mathrm{EUR},\qquad 7040\cdot1.10=7744\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Under **compound interest**, the two-year accumulated value is $C(1+i)^2$. Setting this equal to $7744\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives an equation for $i$.

Since the principal $6400$ is nonzero, divide both sides by $6400$. The ratio $\dfrac{7744}{6400}$ is the growth factor for the entire two years. Its numerator and denominator are multiples of $64$ because $7744=64\cdot121$ and $6400=64\cdot100$. The ratio therefore equals $1.21$.

The same annual factor is applied in each year, so $1.21$ is its square. The rate satisfies $i>-1$ and the factor $1+i$ is positive. Take the positive square root. The negative root $1+i=-1.1$ is inadmissible because it would give $i=-2.1<-1$. Subtract $1$ from $1.10$ to find the annual return.

Applying $1.10$ twice to the original principal gives $7744\,\mathrm{EUR}$, confirming that the effective annual compound interest rate is **$10\%$**.

</div>
