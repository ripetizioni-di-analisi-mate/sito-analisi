## Exercise 1

Find the effective annual rate equivalent to a nominal annual rate of $12\%$ compounded semiannually.

$$
\begin{aligned}
i_2&=\frac{j^{(2)}}{2}=\frac{0.12}{2}=0.06\\[6pt]
1+i&=(1+i_2)^2=1.06^2=1.1236\\[6pt]
i&=1.1236-1=0.1236=12.36\%
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
1000\cdot1.06=1060\,\mathrm{EUR},\qquad 1060\cdot1.06=1123.60\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By definition, a **nominal annual rate** compounded $m$ times per year satisfies $j^{(m)}=m\,i_m$, where $i_m$ is the effective rate per compounding period. Here $m=2$, so dividing the nominal rate by two gives an effective six-month rate of $6\%$.

The first six months' closing balance becomes the opening principal for the next six, so multiplying the two six-month factors gives the annual factor $1.06^2=1.1236$. An initial $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ grows to $1060\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in six months and $1123.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year because the second six months' rate of $6\%$ also applies to the $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ earned in the first six months.

Subtract $1$ from the annual factor to obtain the effective rate. It exceeds the nominal rate by $0.36$ percentage points. The difference, $0.06^2=0.0036$, is the second half-year's interest on the first half-year's earnings as a fraction of the initial principal.

The equivalent effective annual interest rate is **$12.36\%$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What nominal annual rate compounded quarterly is equivalent to an effective six-month compound interest rate of $8.16\%$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1+i_4)^2&=1+i_2=1.0816\\[6pt]
1+i_4&=\sqrt{1.0816}=1.04\\[6pt]
i_4&=0.04\\[6pt]
j^{(4)}&=4\,i_4=4\cdot0.04=0.16=16\%
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
1.04^2=1.0816,\qquad 1+i=1.0816^2\approx1.1699
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given effective rate covers six months and the required nominal rate compounds quarterly. Use six months as the common term. Since six months contain two quarters, **rate equivalence** under compound interest gives $(1+i_4)^2=1+i_2$.

The quarterly accumulation factor is positive, so take the positive square root of $1.0816$. This is $1.04$ because $1.04^2=1.0816$. The effective quarterly rate is therefore $4\%$.

There are four quarters per year. By definition, the nominal annual rate compounded quarterly is four times the effective quarterly rate, giving $16\%$.

The effective annual rate differs from the nominal rate. It equals $1.0816^2-1$, approximately $16.99\%$, because it comes from multiplying four quarterly accumulation factors. The equivalent nominal annual rate compounded quarterly is **$16\%$**.

</div>
