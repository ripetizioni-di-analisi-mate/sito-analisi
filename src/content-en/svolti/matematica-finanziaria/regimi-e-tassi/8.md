## Exercise 1

A principal of $4000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is invested at $10\%$ simple annual interest for three years. Compare the direct accumulated value with the balance obtained by closing the deposit after one year and reinvesting the entire amount for two more years at the same simple rate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{\mathrm{direct}}&=4000\,(1+0.10\cdot3)=4000\cdot1.3=5200\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
M_1&=4000\,(1+0.10)=4400\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
M_{\mathrm{rollover}}&=4400\,(1+0.10\cdot2)=4400\cdot1.2=5280\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
M_{\mathrm{rollover}}-M_{\mathrm{direct}}&=5280-5200=80\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C\,i^2st=4000\cdot0.10^2\cdot1\cdot2=80\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,4000+400*t,0,3.1;t,4400+440*(t-1),1,3.1" xmin=-0.45 xmax=3.4 ymin=3600 ymax=5600 nomi="t,M" legenda="\text{Three-year deposit};\text{Reinvestment after one year}" punti="0,4000,pieno;1,4400,pieno;3,5200,pieno;3,5280,pieno" etichette="3.08,5130,5200;3.08,5330,5280" titolo="Accumulated value in euros of $4000$ euros at an annual simple interest rate of $10\%$, with $t$ in years. After reinvestment at time $1$, the line starts again at $4400$ euros with a slope of $440$ euros per year."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For the three-year deposit, use $M=C(1+it)$ with $t=3$. Interest always accrues on the initial $4000\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so the deposit earns $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ each year and reaches an accumulated value of $5200\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

If the deposit is renewed, it first pays $4400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after one year. This amount includes $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in interest and becomes the opening balance of the second deposit, which earns interest for two years with a factor of $1+0.10\cdot2=1.2$. Its accumulated value is $5280\,\mathrm{EUR}$. On the graph the slope rises from $400$ to $440\,\mathrm{EUR}$ per year because from time $1$ onward interest accrues on $4400\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The difference of $80\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is two years' interest on the $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$ earned in year one, namely $20\%$ of $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$. It also equals the term $i^2st$ in the expansion $(1+is)(1+it)=1+i(s+t)+i^2st$ with $s=1$ and $t=2$.

The term $i^2st$ is positive, so $r(1+2)\neq r(1)\,r(2)$ for simple interest. Renewal yields **$80\,\mathrm{EUR}$ more** than the direct deposit, showing that simple interest at this rate **is not multiplicative**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The force of interest varies according to $\delta(u)=0.02+0.02u$, with $u$ in years. Calculate the accumulation factor between times $1$ and $2$ directly and as a ratio of factors starting at time $0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_1^2\delta(u)\,du&=\Big[\,0.02u+0.01u^2\,\Big]_1^2\\[6pt]
&=(0.04+0.04)-(0.02+0.01)=0.05\\[6pt]
r(1,2)&=e^{0.05}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\delta(u)\,du&=0.02+0.01=0.03,\qquad r(0,1)=e^{0.03}\\[6pt]
\int_0^2\delta(u)\,du&=0.04+0.04=0.08,\qquad r(0,2)=e^{0.08}\\[6pt]
\frac{r(0,2)}{r(0,1)}&=\frac{e^{0.08}}{e^{0.03}}=e^{0.08-0.03}=e^{0.05}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force depends on the date, so the accumulation factor from $1$ to $2$ is $r(1,2)=\exp\left(\displaystyle\int_1^2\delta(u)\,du\right)$. An antiderivative of $0.02+0.02u$ is $0.02u+0.01u^2$. The difference between its values at $2$ and $1$ is $0.08-0.03=0.05$, giving the factor $e^{0.05}$ directly.

For the second method, calculate both factors from time $0$. The integral to $1$ is $0.03$ and the integral to $2$ is $0.08$. Additivity gives $\displaystyle\int_0^2=\int_0^1+\int_1^2$, and therefore $r(0,2)=r(0,1)\,r(1,2)$.

Dividing the two-year factor by the first-year factor isolates the second-year factor. Dividing exponentials subtracts their exponents, so the growth common to both factors from time $0$ to time $1$ cancels.

The first-year factor is $e^{0.03}$ and the second-year factor is $e^{0.05}$. The intervals have equal length but different factors because the force increases over time. Both methods give the same factor **$r(1,2)=e^{0.05}\approx1.0513$**.

</div>
