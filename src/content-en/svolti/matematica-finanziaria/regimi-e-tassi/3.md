## Exercise 1

Find the discount and the present value of a receivable of $7200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ discounted three months before maturity at an annual commercial discount rate of $6\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{3}{12}=\frac{1}{4}\,\mathrm{year}\\[6pt]
dt&=0.06\cdot\frac{1}{4}=0.015\\[6pt]
D&=Mdt=7200\cdot0.015=108\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
V&=M-D=7200-108=7092\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V=M(1-dt)=7200\cdot0.985=7092\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discount rate $d=0.06$ is annual, so measure the time to maturity in years. Three months is one-quarter of a year, giving $t=\dfrac{1}{4}$.

Under **commercial discount**, the amount deducted is proportional to the maturity value and the term, as shown by $D=Mdt$. The product $dt=0.015$ gives the fraction deducted for the quarter, $1.5\%$. Applying this percentage to the maturity value of $7200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives a discount of $108\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

Today the lender receives the maturity value less the discount. The condition $dt<1$ holds because $0.015<1$, so the present value is positive. Applying the commercial discount factor $1-dt=0.985$ directly confirms the result. Rational discount at the same rate would give a present value of $\dfrac{7200}{1.015}\approx7093.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$, which is higher because the percentage deduction is based on the smaller present value.

The commercial discount is **$108\,\mathrm{EUR}$** and the present value of the receivable is **$7092\,\mathrm{EUR}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A receivable of $4800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ can be discounted at an annual commercial discount rate of $12\%$. Find the terms $t\geq0$ for which the proceeds are at least $4200\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(t)&=4800\,(1-0.12\,t)=4800-576\,t\\[6pt]
4800-576\,t&\geq 4200\\[6pt]
576\,t&\leq 600\\[6pt]
t&\leq\frac{600}{576}=\frac{25}{24}\,\mathrm{years}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
0\leq t\leq\frac{25}{24}\,\mathrm{years},\qquad \frac{25}{24}\cdot12=12.5\,\mathrm{months}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,4800-576*t,0,1.8;t,4200,0,1.8" xmin=-0.4 xmax=1.9 ymin=3900 ymax=4900 nomi="t,V" legenda="V(t)=4800\,(1-0.12\,t);V=4200" punti="0,4800,pieno;1.0416667,4200,pieno" segmenti="1.0416667,3900,1.0416667,4200" titolo="Present value under commercial discount in euros as a function of the term $t$ in years. The line stays above $4200$ euros up to $t=\dfrac{25}{24}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The discount rate is annual, so $t$ is measured in years. The present value under commercial discount is $V(t)=M(1-dt)$ with $M=4800$ and $d=0.12$. Expanding gives $V(t)=4800-576\,t$, where $4800\cdot0.12=576$ is the discount for one full year.

The function $V$ decreases in $t$, so discounting further ahead of maturity reduces the proceeds. We require $V(t)\geq4200$. Subtracting $4200$ and adding $576\,t$ on both sides gives $576\,t\leq600$. Since $576$ is positive, dividing by $576$ preserves the inequality.

The maximum deduction is $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$, one eighth of the maturity value. The maximum term, $\dfrac{25}{24}$ of a year, is $12.5$ months. Throughout this interval $dt\leq0.12\cdot\dfrac{25}{24}=\dfrac{1}{8}<1$, so the commercial discount formula applies and the factor $1-dt$ stays at or above $\dfrac{7}{8}$.

The proceeds are therefore at least $4200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ **for terms of $0\leq t\leq\dfrac{25}{24}$ years**, that is, for a term of up to twelve and a half months.

</div>
