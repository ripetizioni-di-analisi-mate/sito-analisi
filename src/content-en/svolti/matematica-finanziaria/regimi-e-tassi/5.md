## Exercise 1

Convert an effective annual compound interest rate of $21\%$ to the equivalent effective six-month rate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1+i_2)^2&=1+i=1.21\\[6pt]
1+i_2&=\sqrt{1.21}=1.10\\[6pt]
i_2&=0.10=10\%
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
1.10\cdot1.10=1.21,\qquad 1.105^2=1.221025\neq1.21
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A year contains two six-month periods, so the **rate equivalence** equation $1+i=(1+i_m)^m$ uses $m=2$. Applying the six-month rate $i_2$ twice must reproduce the annual factor $1.21$.

The six-month rate satisfies $i_2>-1$, so the factor $1+i_2$ is positive. Take the positive square root of $1.21$, which is $1.10$. A factor of $1.10$ means that after six months the balance is $110\%$ of the initial principal, an increase of $10\%$.

In the second half of the year the same factor applies to the balance at the end of the first half. The product $1.10\cdot1.10$ reproduces the annual factor $1.21$ and confirms the equivalence.

Halving the effective annual rate would give $10.5\%$ and an annual factor of $1.105^2=1.221025$. This exceeds $1.21$ because halving ignores the compounding of the first six months' interest during the next six months. The equivalent effective six-month rate is **$10\%$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Deposit A earns an effective quarterly rate of $5\%$ and deposit B an effective semiannual rate of $10\%$. Which gives the larger balance on the same principal after six months? Find the difference between their effective semiannual rates.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{A,2}&=(1+0.05)^2=1.05^2=1.1025\\[6pt]
q_{B,2}&=1+0.10=1.10\\[6pt]
i_{A,2}&=1.1025-1=10.25\%\\[6pt]
i_{A,2}-i_{B,2}&=10.25\%-10\%=0.25\ \text{percentage points}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
1000\cdot1.1025=1102.50\,\mathrm{EUR},\qquad 1000\cdot1.10=1100\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use six months as the common term. Compare the accumulation factors for that interval, which covers two quarters and one half-year.

Deposit A compounds twice in six months. By **rate equivalence** under compound interest, its six-month factor is the product of the two quarterly factors, $1.05^2=1.1025$. Deposit B compounds once and has a six-month factor of $1.10$.

Since $1.1025>1.10$, deposit A gives the larger balance for the same positive principal. On $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ the two accumulated values are $1102.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$, a difference of $2.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The equivalent six-month rate for deposit A is $10.25\%$. Its advantage of $0.25$ percentage points comes from the interest earned in the second quarter on the first quarter's interest, since $0.05\cdot0.05=0.0025$. Deposit **A** therefore gives the larger balance, and its six-month rate is higher by **$0.25$ percentage points**.

</div>
