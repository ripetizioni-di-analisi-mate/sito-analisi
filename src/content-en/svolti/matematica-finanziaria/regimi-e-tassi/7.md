## Exercise 1

Find the accumulated value of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after eighteen months at a constant annual force of interest $\delta=0.04$. Round to the nearest cent.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{18}{12}=\frac{3}{2}\,\mathrm{years}\\[6pt]
\int_0^{\frac{3}{2}}0.04\,du&=0.04\cdot\frac{3}{2}=0.06\\[6pt]
r\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)&=e^{0.06}\approx1.0618365\\[6pt]
M&=1000\,e^{0.06}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx1061.84\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
i=e^{0.04}-1\approx4.0811\%,\qquad (1+i)^{\frac{3}{2}}=e^{0.04\cdot\frac{3}{2}}=e^{0.06}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force of interest is annual, so measure the term in years. Eighteen months is one and a half years, giving $t=\dfrac{3}{2}$.

Under **continuous compounding** the accumulation factor is $r(t)=\exp\left(\displaystyle\int_0^t\delta(u)\,du\right)$. Since the force is constant, its integral is the force times the term, $0.06$. This measures the increase in the logarithm of the accumulated value over the investment period. Exponentiating gives the accumulation factor $e^{0.06}\approx1.0618365$.

Multiply the initial principal by the factor to obtain the accumulated value. Rounding to the nearest cent only after multiplication keeps the rounding error below half a cent in this example.

The constant force $\delta=0.04$ corresponds to an effective annual rate of $i=e^{0.04}-1\approx4.08\%$. Exponential accumulation at this rate gives the same factor because $(1+i)^{\frac{3}{2}}=e^{0.06}$. The balance after eighteen months is **$1061.84\,\mathrm{EUR}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A principal of $2000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is invested between times $t=0$ and $t=3$, measured in years, with force of interest $\delta(t)=0.01+0.02t$. Find the equivalent constant force of interest over those three years and the exact accumulated value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^3\delta(u)\,du&=\int_0^3(0.01+0.02u)\,du\\[6pt]
&=\Big[\,0.01u+0.01u^2\,\Big]_0^3\\[6pt]
&=0.03+0.09=0.12\\[6pt]
\bar\delta&=\frac{0.12}{3}=0.04\\[6pt]
M&=2000\,e^{0.12}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx2254.99\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,1+2*t,0,3.2;t,4,0,3.2" area="1+2*x;0;0;3" xmin=-0.3 xmax=3.5 ymin=0 ymax=8 nomi="u,δ (%)" legenda="100\,\delta(u)=1+2u;100\,\bar\delta=4" punti="0,1,pieno;3,7,pieno" segmenti="3,0,3,7" titolo="Force of interest $\delta(u)=0.01+0.02u$ in percentage points, with $u$ in years. The shaded area between $0$ and $3$ equals $12$ percentage points, the same as the rectangle of width $3$ and height $4$ for the equivalent constant force of interest."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The force varies with time, so under **continuous compounding** the accumulation factor uses the integral of $\delta$ over all three years. An antiderivative of $0.01+0.02u$ is $0.01u+0.01u^2$, since the derivative of $0.01u^2$ is $0.02u$. Evaluating it between $0$ and $3$ gives $0.12$.

The equivalent constant force $\bar\delta$ must give the same three-year exponent, $3\bar\delta=0.12$. Otherwise the factor $e^{3\bar\delta}$ would differ from $e^{0.12}$. This gives $\bar\delta=0.04$. In the graph, the area beneath the line $100\,\delta(u)$ therefore equals the area of the rectangle of height $100\,\bar\delta$.

Since $\delta$ is affine, its average on the interval is its value at the midpoint $u=\dfrac{3}{2}$, where $\delta=0.01+0.03=0.04$. Using its final value $\delta(3)=0.07$ would overestimate the accumulated amount.

Multiply the initial principal by $e^{0.12}$ to find the exact final amount. The equivalent constant force is **$\bar\delta=0.04$** and the accumulated value is **$2000\,e^{0.12}\,\mathrm{EUR}$**, approximately $2254.99\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
