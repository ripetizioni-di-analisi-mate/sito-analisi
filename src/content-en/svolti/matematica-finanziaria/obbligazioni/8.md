## Exercise 1

A portfolio holds two zero-coupon bonds costing $95\,\mathrm{EUR}$ each, each redeemable for $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. It also holds three bonds costing $102\,\mathrm{EUR}$ each, each paying an annual coupon of $5\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and redeeming for $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Find the initial value and the two aggregate cash flows.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1&=2,\qquad x_2=3,\\[6pt]
P&=x_1P_1+x_2P_2=2\cdot95+3\cdot102=190+306=496,\\[6pt]
F_1&=2\cdot100+3\cdot5=200+15=215,\\[6pt]
F_2&=2\cdot0+3\cdot(100+5)=315.
\end{aligned}
$$

The initial value is $496\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The cash flows in years one and two are $215$ and $315\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the definitions of portfolio value and **aggregate cash flows** with $x_1=2$ units of the zero-coupon bond and $x_2=3$ units of the coupon bond. Calculate the initial value by multiplying prices from the same date by their respective quantities and adding. On each future date, the aggregate cash flow is $\sum_j x_jF_j(t)$. Multiply the per-unit payments by the holdings and sum.

At the first date, both zero-coupon bonds mature and all three coupon bonds pay their first coupon. By the second date, the zero-coupon bonds have already redeemed, so their per-unit payment is zero. The three final coupons and three principal repayments remain. The initial portfolio value adds amounts at today's date. The amounts $F_1$ and $F_2$ fall on different dates and remain separate entries in the payment schedule. Nominal receipts total $530$ euros, which can be checked by adding $200$ euros from the zero-coupon bonds and $330$ euros from the coupon bonds.

## Exercise 2

Two zero-coupon bonds cost $190$ and $510\,\mathrm{EUR}$, respectively. The first pays $220\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and the second pays $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Find the effective annual yield to maturity on a portfolio containing one of each.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=190+510=700,\\[6pt]
700&=\frac{220}{q}+\frac{605}{q^2},\\[6pt]
0&=700q^2-220q-605,\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{220\pm\sqrt{220^2+4\cdot700\cdot605}}{1400}=\frac{220\pm1320}{1400},\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{1540}{1400}=1.1,\\[6pt]
\frac{220}{1.1}+\frac{605}{1.1^2}
 &=200+500=700.
\end{aligned}
$$

The portfolio yield to maturity is $10\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Use a total price of $700$ euros and the aggregate payments of $220$ euros in year one and $605$ euros in year two to calculate the portfolio yield. Its value is unique because both future receipts are positive and their present value decreases strictly as the yield rises. Multiplying by $q^2>0$ gives a quadratic. Its discriminant is $1320^2$ and its roots are $1.1$ and $-\dfrac{11}{14}$. Reject the second root because $q=1+y$ is positive.

The factor $1.1$ gives the first payment a present value of $200$ euros and the second a present value of $500$ euros. These model values sum exactly to the portfolio price, so the factor solves the equation. The observed individual bond prices are $190$ and $510$ euros. Their deviations from $200$ and $500$ euros offset only in the portfolio as a whole. The individual yields to maturity are $\dfrac{220}{190}-1\approx15.79\%$ and $\sqrt{\dfrac{605}{510}}-1\approx8.92\%$, and the portfolio yield lies between them.

</div>
