## Exercise 1

A zero-coupon bond repays $1452\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Its effective annual yield is $10\%$. Calculate its price today.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=1+0.10=1.1,\\[6pt]
q^2&=1.1^2=1.21,\\[6pt]
Pq^2&=1452,\\[6pt]
P&=\frac{1452}{q^2}=\frac{1452}{1.21}=1200.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
1200\cdot1.1=1320,\qquad 1320\cdot1.1=1452.
$$

The price is $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

This is a **zero-coupon bond**, so apply $P=\dfrac{M}{(1+y)^T}$ with a redemption payment of $M=1452$ euros, yield $y=0.10$, and term $T=2$ years. The yield is effective annual and the term is measured in years, so yield and time use the same unit.

The bond makes a single payment at maturity, so its price is the amount today that must grow to $1452$ euros in two years. The factor $q$ applies to one year. The two-year term therefore appears in the exponent, giving a total factor of $q^2=1.21$. Dividing by $q^2$ discounts the receipt to today. To check, reconstruct the accumulated value. After the first year the equivalent principal is $1320$ euros, and after the second it is $1452$ euros, as the contract specifies.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A zero-coupon bond with face value $5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is quoted at $98$ per $100$ of face value. It repays $5145\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in six months. Calculate the effective annual yield.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=98\cdot\frac{5000}{100}=98\cdot50=4900,\\[6pt]
T&=\frac{1}{2},\\[6pt]
(1+y)^{\frac{1}{2}}&=\frac{5145}{4900}=1.05,\\[6pt]
1+y&=1.05^2=1.1025,\\[6pt]
y&=1.05^2-1=0.1025.
\end{aligned}
$$

The effective annual yield is $10.25\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The quote gives the price per $100$ euros of face value. The bond comprises $50$ such units, giving a purchase price of $4900$ euros. Because the required yield is annual, the six-month term is expressed as $T=\dfrac{1}{2}$ of a year.

The zero-coupon bond formula gives $1+y=\left(\dfrac{M}{P}\right)^{\frac{1}{T}}$, which is well defined because the price is positive. The redemption-to-price ratio of $1.05$ covers six months. Since $\dfrac{1}{T}=2$, the equivalent annual factor is the square of the semiannual factor. The half-year yield is $5\%$ and the annual yield is $10.25\%$. The latter exceeds twice the half-year yield by $0.25$ percentage points because first-half interest itself earns interest in the second half. Applying the half-year factor to $4900$ euros checks the result and gives the contractual payment of $5145$ euros.

</div>
