## Exercise 1

A bond with face value $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ pays two equal coupons per year at a nominal annual coupon rate of $4\%$ and redeems at par in one year. Its effective annual yield is $10.25\%$. Calculate the price immediately after a coupon payment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=\frac{1200\cdot0.04}{2}=24,\\[6pt]
(1+i)^2&=1.1025,\\[6pt]
1+i&=\sqrt{1.1025}=1.05,\\[6pt]
F_1&=24,\qquad F_2=24+1200=1224,\\[6pt]
P&=\frac{24}{1.05}+\frac{1224}{1.05^2}\\[6pt]
 &\approx22.86+1110.20=1133.06.
\end{aligned}
$$

The price is approximately $1133.06\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The contractual coupon rate gives $48$ euros a year, split into two equal coupons. The effective annual yield reflects two successive half-years of compounding. The semiannual yield $i$ therefore satisfies $(1+i)^2=1+y$. Taking the square root of the annual factor gives $i=5\%$.

The payments are one and two half-years from settlement, giving exponents one and two. The first payment is just the coupon of $24$ euros. The final payment includes coupon and principal, totaling $1224$ euros. Since the coupon rate per period is $2\%$ and the semiannual yield is $5\%$, a price below face value is consistent with redemption at par. The identity $P-N=(C-iN)a_{\overline 2|0.05}$ confirms the amount because $(24-60)\cdot1.8594\approx-66.94$ euros and $1200-66.94=1133.06$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bond with face value $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ pays quarterly coupons at a nominal annual coupon rate of $6\%$ and redeems at par in nine months. The nominal annual yield compounded quarterly is $10\%$. Calculate the price immediately after a coupon payment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=\frac{1000\cdot0.06}{4}=15,\\[6pt]
i&=\frac{0.10}{4}=0.025,\qquad q=1.025,\\[6pt]
n&=\frac{9}{3}=3,\qquad F_3=15+1000=1015,\\[6pt]
P&=\frac{15}{q}+\frac{15}{q^2}+\frac{1015}{q^3}\\[6pt]
 &\approx14.63+14.28+942.53=971.44.
\end{aligned}
$$

The price is approximately $971.44\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Quarterly payments determine both the amount of each coupon and the number of payment dates. The yield is quoted as a nominal annual rate compounded quarterly, so the quarterly rate is the nominal rate divided by four, or $2.5\%$. The coupon and yield therefore refer to the same period, as the pricing formula requires.

Nine months contain three quarters and settlement immediately follows a coupon payment. The remaining payments are therefore one, two, and three periods away. Only the final cash flow, which includes principal, takes the exponent three. The quarterly coupon rate of $1.5\%$ is below the yield of $2.5\%$, so the price is below par. The discount to face value is $(15-25)\,a_{\overline 3|0.025}\approx-10\cdot2.8560=-28.56$ euros, consistent with $1000-28.56=971.44$ euros.

</div>
