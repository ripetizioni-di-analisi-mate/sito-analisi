## Exercise 1

A bond pays an annual coupon of $84\,\mathrm{EUR}$ for two years and repays $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ with the final coupon. Calculate the price immediately after a coupon payment if the effective annual yield is $20\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1&=84,\qquad F_2=84+1500=1584,\\[6pt]
P&=\frac{84}{1.2}+\frac{1584}{1.2^2}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{84}{1.2}+\frac{1584}{1.44}\\[6pt]
 &=70+1100=1170.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P-M&=(C-iM)\,a_{\overline 2|0.2}\\[6pt]
 &=(84-300)\left(\frac{1}{1.2}+\frac{1}{1.44}\right)\\[6pt]
 &=-216\cdot\frac{55}{36}=-330.
\end{aligned}
$$

The price is $1170\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Settlement is immediately after a coupon payment, so the next coupon is one year away and the last is two years away. Use the formula for the **price as the present value of the payments**, with discount factors $1.2^{-1}$ and $1.2^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{1.44}$.

Only the coupon is due in year one. In year two principal and the second coupon are due together, giving a single payment of $1584$ euros. The price adds the two cash flows discounted over their respective terms. The ratio $\dfrac{C}{M}=\dfrac{84}{1500}=5.6\%$ is less than the yield of $20\%$, so the price is below the redemption payment. Indeed, $P-M=(C-iM)a_{\overline 2|i}$ gives a difference of $-330$ euros, and $1500-330=1170$. To check, move all amounts to year two. The price of $1170$ euros accumulated for two years is $1684.80$ euros. Accumulating the first coupon to year two and adding the final payment gives the same amount.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bond pays coupons of $66$, $121$, and $55\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the ends of the next three years. On the third date it also repays $1276\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Calculate the price at an effective annual yield of $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_3&=55+1276=1331,\\[6pt]
1.1^2&=1.21,\qquad 1.1^3=1.331,\\[6pt]
P&=\frac{66}{1.1}+\frac{121}{1.1^2}+\frac{1331}{1.1^3}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{66}{1.1}+\frac{121}{1.21}+\frac{1331}{1.331}\\[6pt]
 &=60+100+1000=1160.
\end{aligned}
$$

The price is $1160\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The coupons differ in amount, so the level annuity formula does not apply. Value the cash flow on each date separately using the discount factor $1.1^{-k}$ for its own payment date $k$. At the third date, add the $55$ euros of coupon and $1276$ euros of principal before discounting because they are payable together.

The given amounts are multiples of powers of the annual factor since $66=60\cdot1.1$ and $121=100\cdot1.21$. Thus the first two present values are $60$ and $100$ euros. The final payment contributes exactly $1000$ euros to today's price because its accumulation factor is $1.331$. At maturity, compare $1160q^3$ with $66q^2+121q+1331$ to check the result. Both are $1543.96$ euros.

</div>
