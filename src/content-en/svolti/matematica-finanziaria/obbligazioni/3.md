## Exercise 1

A bond costs $1050\,\mathrm{EUR}$. In one year it pays a coupon of $75\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and repays $1080\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Calculate its yield to maturity and current yield.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1&=75+1080=1155,\\[6pt]
1050&=\frac{1155}{1+y},\\[6pt]
1+y&=\frac{1155}{1050}=1.1,\\[6pt]
y&=0.10,\\[6pt]
\frac{C}{P}&=\frac{75}{1050}=\frac{1}{14}\approx0.0714.
\end{aligned}
$$

The yield to maturity is $10\%$ and the current yield is approximately $7.14\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Yield to maturity equates the price to the discounted value of the total receipt. Because there is only one payment date, the yield equation has a single term, $1050=\dfrac{1155}{1+y}$. The price and receipt are positive, so the solution exists and is unique. The only payment is one year away, so divide the total receipt by the initial outlay and subtract one.

The numerator for current yield includes only the coupon of $75$ euros. The difference of $30$ euros between principal repaid and purchase price also contributes to yield to maturity. Since the redemption amount exceeds the price, the current yield of approximately $7.14\%$ is below the yield to maturity. Adding the two sources of gain gives a check, $105$ euros on an initial investment of $1050$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bond costs $960\,\mathrm{EUR}$. It pays $66\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the end of year one and $1089\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the end of year two, including the final coupon. Find its yield to maturity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
960&=\frac{66}{q}+\frac{1089}{q^2},\qquad q=1+y,\\[6pt]
0&=960q^2-66q-1089,\\[6pt]
\Delta&=66^2+4\cdot960\cdot1089=4186116=2046^2,\\[6pt]
q&=\frac{66\pm2046}{1920},\\[6pt]
q_1&=\frac{2112}{1920}=\frac{11}{10},\qquad q_2=-\frac{1980}{1920}=-\frac{33}{32},\\[6pt]
0&=\left(q-\frac{11}{10}\right)(960q+990).
\end{aligned}
$$

The only admissible factor is $q=1.1$. The yield to maturity is $10\%$.

The price curve intersects the line P = 960 only at y = 10%."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The price is the sum of two receipts discounted over different terms. Because the accumulation factor $q$ is positive, multiplying by $q^2$ preserves the admissible solutions and gives a quadratic equation in $q$. The discriminant is the square of $2046$, so the quadratic formula yields the rational roots $\dfrac{11}{10}$ and $-\dfrac{33}{32}$. Reject the second root, which is negative, because $q=1+y$ is positive when $y>-1$.

Factoring gives the same result. The factor $960q+990$ is positive for every $q>0$, so the only admissible value is $q=\dfrac{11}{10}$. The theoretical price decreases as the yield rises and intersects the purchase-price line just once, as the existence and uniqueness theorem predicts. Check by substituting the yield into the original sum. The first payment is worth $60$ euros today and the second $900$ euros, so their present values add up to the price.

</div>
