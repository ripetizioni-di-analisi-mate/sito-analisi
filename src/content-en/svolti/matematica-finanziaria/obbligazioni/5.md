## Exercise 1

A bond with face value $2500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has a clean price quote of $97.2$ per $100$ of face value and a coupon of $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$. At settlement, $45$ days of a $180$-day period have elapsed. Calculate the price paid using linear accrual and actual days.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{s}}&=97.2\cdot\frac{2500}{100}=97.2\cdot25=2430,\\[6pt]
\alpha&=\frac{45}{180}=\frac{1}{4},\\[6pt]
A&=\alpha C=40\cdot\frac{1}{4}=10,\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{tq}}&=P_{\mathrm{s}}+A=2430+10=2440.
\end{aligned}
$$

The buyer pays $2440\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the definition of the **dirty price**, $P_{\mathrm{tq}}=P_{\mathrm{s}}+A$, with linear accrued interest $A=\alpha C$. Under an actual day count, $\alpha$ is the ratio of the $45$ days since the previous coupon to the $180$ days in the coupon period.

The clean quote is per $100$ of face value and must be multiplied by the number of quotation units, $25$. The accrued interest already refers to the coupon on the full holding. Since a quarter of the period has elapsed, accrued interest is one quarter of $40$ euros. The buyer pays this amount to the seller because the buyer receives the whole next coupon, of which $10$ euros accrued while the seller held the bond. Both amounts are now in euros and can be added. As a check on scale, accrued interest per hundred of face value is $0.4$ and the dirty price per hundred is $97.6$. Multiplying the quote by $25$ reproduces the settlement amount.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bond has two annual coupons of $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$ remaining and repays $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ with the second. Settlement is halfway through the period and the buyer is entitled to the next coupon. Calculate the clean price in euros at an effective annual yield of $21\%$ using linear accrued interest and the same fraction of a period for discounting.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\frac{1}{2},\qquad t_1=1-\alpha=\frac{1}{2},\qquad t_2=2-\alpha=\frac{3}{2},\\[6pt]
F_2&=121+1210=1331,\\[6pt]
1.21^{\frac{1}{2}}&=1.1,\qquad 1.21^{\frac{3}{2}}=1.1^3=1.331,\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{tq}}
 &=\frac{121}{1.21^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1331}{1.21^{\frac{3}{2}}}\\[6pt]
 &=\frac{121}{1.1}+\frac{1331}{1.331}=110+1000=1110,\\[6pt]
A&=\frac{121}{2}=60.50,\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{s}}&=1110-60.50=1049.50.
\end{aligned}
$$

The clean price is $1049.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Half a period remains until the first coupon. The second comes one year later, or one and a half periods from settlement. In general, if a fraction $\alpha$ has elapsed, remaining payment number $k$ is $t_k=k-\alpha$ periods away. Since $1.21=1.1^2$, raising the annual factor to the powers $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$ gives $1.1$ and $1.331$.

Discounting the two payments gives the dirty price because it includes everything the buyer will receive. Subtract accrued interest for the elapsed half-period to find the clean price. Accrued interest uses a linear proportion of the coupon, while the theoretical price uses powers of the accumulation factor. The identity $1049.50+60.50=1110$ recovers the settlement price.

</div>
