## Exercise 1

A bond with face and redemption values of $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ has three annual coupons of $48\,\mathrm{EUR}$. It is valued immediately after a coupon payment at an effective annual yield of $4\%$. By how much does the price exceed par?

$$
\begin{aligned}
iN&=0.04\cdot800=32,\\[6pt]
C-iN&=48-32=16,\\[6pt]
a_{\overline 3|0.04}&=\frac{1-1.04^{-3}}{0.04}\approx2.7751,\\[6pt]
P-N&=16\,a_{\overline 3|0.04}\approx16\cdot2.7751\approx44.40.
\end{aligned}
$$

The price exceeds par by approximately $44.4\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

::grafico{f="48/(1+x/100)+48/(1+x/100)^2+848/(1+x/100)^3;800" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=700 ymax=950 nomi="i,P" legenda="P(i);N=800" punti="4,844.40,pieno;6,800,pieno" segmenti="4,700,4,844.40" titolo="Price in euros of the bond in Exercise 1 as a function of the annual percentage yield i. At 6%, the coupon rate, the bond trades at par. At 4% its price is 844.40 euros."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Face and redemption values coincide, the coupons are level, and valuation immediately follows a coupon payment. The assumptions of $P-N=(C-iN)a_{\overline n|i}$ therefore hold, with $n=3$ and $i=0.04$. The coupon rate $\dfrac{48}{800}=6\%$ exceeds the yield of $4\%$, so the bond trades **above par**.

A bond with the same maturity and redemption amount and a coupon of $32$ euros would trade exactly at face value because its coupon rate would equal the yield. The bond in the question pays $16$ euros more on each date. The price difference is the present value of these three excess payments, calculated as a three-payment annuity in arrears with factor $a_{\overline 3|0.04}\approx2.7751$. An alternative is to value the payments of $48$, $48$, and $848$ euros directly. Their present value is approximately $844.40$ euros. Subtracting $800$ euros gives the same premium.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bond with face value $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is quoted at $102$ per $100$ of face value. In one year it repays $1050\,\mathrm{EUR}$ together with the only remaining coupon. Calculate its annual coupon rate given a yield of $5\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=102\cdot\frac{1000}{100}=1020,\\[6pt]
P&=\frac{C+1050}{1.05},\\[6pt]
C+1050&=1020\cdot1.05=1071,\\[6pt]
C&=1071-1050=21,\\[6pt]
r_c&=\frac{C}{N}=\frac{21}{1000}=0.021.
\end{aligned}
$$

The coupon rate is $2.1\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The quote applies to face value and gives a price of $1020$ euros. The bond has only one payment date in one year, so the pricing equation contains a single cash flow, $C+1050$, discounted for one year. Accumulating the price at the given yield to that payment date gives a total receipt of $1071$ euros. The contract allocates $1050$ euros to principal and the balance to the coupon. Divide the coupon by the face value of $1000$ euros to express it as a percentage because the coupon rate is defined by $C=Nr_c$.

The price exceeds face value even though the coupon rate is below the yield, because the redemption payment exceeds face value. The comparison with the redemption payment follows from $P-M=(C-iM)a_{\overline 1|i}$. Since $C-iM=21-52.50=-31.50$, the price is $\dfrac{31.50}{1.05}=30$ euros below the redemption payment, as confirmed by $1050-30=1020$.

</div>
