## Exercise 1

Calculate the annual IRR of an investment costing $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today that generates a single receipt of $968\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years.

$$
\operatorname{NPV}(i)=-800+\frac{968}{(1+i)^2}=0
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
800&=\frac{968}{(1+i^*)^2} \\[6pt]
(1+i^*)^2&=\frac{968}{800}=1.21 \\[6pt]
1+i^*&=\sqrt{1.21}=1.1 \\[6pt]
i^*&=1.1-1=0.10.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{968}{1.1^2}=\frac{968}{1.21}=800\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$

$$
800\cdot1.1=880\,\mathrm{EUR},\qquad 880\cdot1.1=968\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$

The annual IRR is $10\%$.

The downward arrow shows an outflow of $800$ at time $0$. The upward arrow shows an inflow of $968$ at time $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The IRR makes NPV zero, so its defining equation requires the present value of the single receipt to equal the initial outlay. The receipt arrives in two years, giving a discount factor of $(1+i)^{-2}$. Multiplying by $(1+i^*)^2$ and dividing by $800$ isolates the two-year growth factor, $\dfrac{968}{800}=1.21$.

The equation $(1+i^*)^2=1.21$ has solutions $1+i^*=1.1$ and $1+i^*=-1.1$. An admissible rate must satisfy $i^*>-1$, so $1+i^*>0$ and only the positive square root remains. Subtracting one gives the annual rate of $10\%$.

Substitution confirms that at $10\%$ the receipt is worth exactly $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. The overall gain is $21\%$ of the outlay because the second year's rate also applies to interest earned in year one. The final compound-interest calculation illustrates this. At $10\%$, the original $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ grows to $880\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after one year and $968\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after two years. Year two therefore earns $88\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in interest compared with $80$ in year one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the six-month IRR of an investment costing $1250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and returning $1012.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. Find the equivalent effective annual rate.

$$
-1250+\frac{1012.50}{(1+i_s)^2}=0
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1+i_s)^2&=\frac{1012.50}{1250}=0.81 \\[6pt]
1+i_s&=\sqrt{0.81}=0.9 \\[6pt]
i_s&=0.9-1=-0.10
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
i_a&=(1+i_s)^2-1 \\[6pt]
&=0.81-1=-0.19.
\end{aligned}
$$

The six-month IRR is $-10\%$ and its annual equivalent is $-19\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The required rate is for six months, so measure time in six-month periods. The receipt in one year then occurs at time $2$. Its discount factor is $(1+i_s)^{-2}$. The ratio of the receipt to the outlay, $\dfrac{1012.50}{1250}=0.81$, is the overall factor for the two six-month periods.

Since $1+i_s>0$, take the positive square root of $0.81$, which is $0.9$. The overall factor is less than one, so the six-month factor is also less than one and the rate is negative, $i_s=-10\%$. The investment returns less than it costs, consistent with a negative rate.

To obtain the annual rate, apply the six-month factor $0.9$ twice. The second decrease of $10\%$ applies to an amount that has already fallen, so the annual loss is $19\%$, smaller than the sum $20\%$ of the two six-month losses. The ratio of the receipt to the outlay gives the same result because the receipt arrives exactly one year later. Indeed, $0.81-1=-0.19$.

</div>
