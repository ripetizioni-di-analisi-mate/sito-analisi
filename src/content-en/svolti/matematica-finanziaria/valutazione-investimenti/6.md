## Exercise 1

Two projects have the same initial cost. The first pays $550\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and the second pays $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Calculate the crossover rate and determine which project has the higher NPV at $12\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}_A&=-C_0+\frac{550}{q},\qquad \operatorname{NPV}_B=-C_0+\frac{605}{q^2} \\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}_B-\operatorname{NPV}_A&=\frac{605}{q^2}-\frac{550}{q} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{605-550q}{q^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
605-550q=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q=\frac{605}{550}=1.1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i=10\%.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
605-550\cdot1.12&=605-616=-11 \\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}_B-\operatorname{NPV}_A&=\frac{-11}{1.2544}\approx-8.77\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

The crossover rate is $10\%$. At $12\%$ the **first project** is preferable.

<div class="spiegazione">

The crossover rate makes the two NPVs equal, so find the zero of $\operatorname{NPV}_B-\operatorname{NPV}_A$. The equal initial costs cause the term $-C_0$ to cancel, leaving only the two receipts. Writing the fractions over the common denominator $q^2$ gives $\dfrac{605-550q}{q^2}$.

The denominator $q^2$ is positive, so the difference has the sign of the numerator $605-550q$. The numerator vanishes at $q=1.1$, corresponding to $10\%$, and decreases with $q$. The second project therefore has the higher NPV for $i<10\%$ and the first for $i>10\%$.

At $12\%$ the numerator is $605-550\cdot1.12=-11$, so the second project's NPV is approximately $8.77\,\mathrm{EUR}$ below the first's. The receipts' present values at $12\%$ give the same result, approximately $491.07\,\mathrm{EUR}$ for the first and $482.30\,\mathrm{EUR}$ for the second.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Project $A$ has cash flows $-300$, $100$ and $350\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at times $0,1,2$. Project $B$ has cash flows $-400$, $330$ and $218\,\mathrm{EUR}$ on the same dates. Find the crossover rates and the intervals on which $B$ has the higher NPV.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t=0:&\quad -400-(-300)=-100 \\[6pt]
t=1:&\quad 330-100=230 \\[6pt]
t=2:&\quad 218-350=-132
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}_B-\operatorname{NPV}_A&=-100+230v-132v^2 \\[6pt]
&=-(132v^2-230v+100) \\[6pt]
&=-(11v-10)(12v-10).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
v\in\left\{\frac{5}{6},\frac{10}{11}\right\}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i\in\{20\%,10\%\}.
$$

$$
\operatorname{NPV}_B-\operatorname{NPV}_A\,\big|_{i=15\%}=-100+\frac{230}{1.15}-\frac{132}{1.3225}\approx0.19\,\mathrm{EUR}>0.
$$

The rates are $10\%$ and $20\%$. Project $B$ has the higher NPV for $10\%<i<20\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

By linearity of discounting, the difference between the NPVs equals the NPV of the incremental cash flow, obtained by subtracting the amounts date by date. Both projects have positive future receipts, but at time $2$ the receipt for $B$ is smaller than that for $A$, giving a negative difference on that date. The incremental cash flow changes sign twice, so the uniqueness theorem does not apply and more than one crossover rate is possible.

With $v=(1+i)^{-1}$ the incremental NPV is a quadratic trinomial with roots $v=\dfrac{10}{11}$ and $v=\dfrac{5}{6}$, both positive. Using $i=v^{-1}-1$ gives rates of $10\%$ and $20\%$. The quadratic coefficient is negative, so the polynomial is positive between its roots. Converting from $v$ to $i$ reverses the endpoints and gives the interval $10\%<i<20\%$, on which $B$ has the higher NPV.

The positive value at $15\%$ confirms the sign within the interval. Outside the interval, excluding the crossover rates themselves, $A$ has the higher NPV.

</div>
