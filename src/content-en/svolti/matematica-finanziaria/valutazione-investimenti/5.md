## Exercise 1

Two alternative projects require $200$ and $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today, respectively, and return $260$ and $1254\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. Calculate their individual IRRs and choose between them using NPV at $10\%$.

$$
i^*_A=\frac{260}{200}-1=30\%,\qquad i^*_B=\frac{1254}{1000}-1=25.4\%
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}_A&=-200+\frac{260}{1.1}=\frac{400}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx36.36\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}_B&=-1000+\frac{1254}{1.1}=-1000+1140=140\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}_{B-A}&=-800+\frac{994}{1.1}=\frac{1140}{11}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx103.64\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
i^*_{B-A}&=\frac{994}{800}-1=24.25\%
\end{aligned}
$$

At $10\%$ the NPV criterion selects $B$.

::grafico{f="-200+260/(1+x/100);-1000+1254/(1+x/100)" xmin=0 xmax=50 ymin=-200 ymax=300 punti="10,36.36,pieno;10,140,pieno;24.25,9.26,vuoto;30,0,pieno;25.4,0,pieno" segmenti="10,0,10,140" nomi="i,VAN" legenda="\operatorname{NPV}_A(i);\operatorname{NPV}_B(i)" titolo="NPVs of the two projects in euros, with $i$ in percentage points. The curves meet at $24.25\%$ (open point), the IRR of the incremental cash flow. Below that rate, including at $10\%$, project $B$ has the higher NPV."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each project has a single receipt after one year, so its IRR is the ratio of that receipt to its outlay, less one. IRR measures the percentage return on each outlay, while NPV measures in euros how much the discounted receipt exceeds the outlay. The criteria rank the projects differently because $A$ has the higher IRR and $B$ has the higher NPV.

Use **incremental NPV** to compare them. Switching from $A$ to $B$ costs an additional $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and generates an additional $1254-260=994\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. This additional receipt has a present value of approximately $903.64\,\mathrm{EUR}$, above $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$. At the given rate the extra capital invested in $B$ therefore raises NPV by approximately $103.64\,\mathrm{EUR}$, equal to $\operatorname{NPV}_B-\operatorname{NPV}_A$.

The additional investment yields $24.25\%$, above $10\%$. At every valuation rate below $24.25\%$, the project with the higher NPV is therefore $B$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A two-year machine has an NPV of $120\,\mathrm{EUR}$ per cycle and can be replaced after two years on identical terms. A four-year machine has an NPV of $215\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Compare the alternatives at an annual rate of $10\%$ when four years of service are required.

$$
\frac{120}{1.1^2}=\frac{120}{1.21}=\frac{12000}{121}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx99.17\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}_{\mathrm{two-year}}&=120+\frac{120}{1.1^2} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{14520+12000}{121}=\frac{26520}{121}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx219.17\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}_{\mathrm{four-year}}&=215\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{26520}{121}-215=\frac{505}{121}\,\mathrm{EUR}\approx4.17\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$

Two cycles of the **two-year machine** give the higher NPV.

<div class="spiegazione">

The comparison covers four years, so the two-year machine is purchased twice, at times $0$ and $2$. Replacement on identical terms means the second cycle has the same cash flows as the first, shifted two years later. Its NPV of $120\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is therefore measured at the start of the second cycle, time $2$.

To add this NPV to the first, express it in time-zero euros by dividing by $1.1^2=1.21$, giving approximately $99.17\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The combined NPV of the two cycles is $\dfrac{26520}{121}\,\mathrm{EUR}$, approximately $219.17\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The four-year machine covers the entire horizon in one cycle, so compare its NPV directly with the two-cycle total. The two-year alternative exceeds the four-year machine's value by approximately $4.17\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Simply comparing $120$ with $215\,\mathrm{EUR}$ would use different horizons, two and four years, and reverse the choice.

</div>
