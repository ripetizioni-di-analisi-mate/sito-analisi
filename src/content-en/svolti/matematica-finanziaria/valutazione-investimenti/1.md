## Exercise 1

Calculate the NPV at an annual rate of $10\%$ of a plant costing $700\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. It generates receipts of $330\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and $484\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years.

$$
v=\frac{1}{1.1},\qquad v^2=\frac{1}{1.1^2}=\frac{1}{1.21}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{330}{1.1}&=300\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\frac{484}{1.21}&=400\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}(0.10)&=-700+\frac{330}{1.1}+\frac{484}{1.1^2} \\[6pt]
&=-700+300+400=0\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The NPV is **zero**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate is annual and the dates are in years, so the one-year discount factor is $v=\dfrac{1}{1.1}$ and the two-year factor is $v^2=\dfrac{1}{1.21}$. The cost occurs on the valuation date, so it enters with a negative sign and a factor of one. Divide the first receipt by $1.1$ because it arrives one year later. Divide the second by $1.1^2$ to discount it for two years. Since $330=300\cdot1.1$ and $484=400\cdot1.21$, the exact present values are $300$ and $400\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The present values sum to $700\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so at the given rate the receipts exactly offset the cost. By the **NPV criterion**, undertaking the project at $10\%$ has the same value as declining it. NPV vanishes at $10\%$, so $10\%$ is also an internal rate of return for the cash flow.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the NPV at an annual rate of $10\%$ of a machine that costs $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$, generates $550\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year, needs maintenance costing $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years and is resold for $399.30\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in three years. Also find the highest initial price consistent with a nonnegative NPV.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{550}{1.1}&=500\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\frac{121}{1.1^2}&=\frac{121}{1.21}=100\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\frac{399.30}{1.1^3}&=\frac{399.30}{1.331}=300\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}(0.10)&=-500+\frac{550}{1.1}-\frac{121}{1.1^2}+\frac{399.30}{1.1^3} \\[6pt]
&=-500+500-100+300=200\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}(C_0)&=-C_0+500-100+300=700-C_0 \\[6pt]
700-C_0\geq 0\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad C_0\leq 700\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The NPV is $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the maximum price is $700\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The cash flows include receipts and payments on different dates, so discount each amount using its own date and sign. Maintenance is an outflow, so subtract its present value. The sale proceeds are a receipt in year three and must be discounted for three periods using $1.1^3=1.331$. The three future cash flows have a combined present value of $500-100+300=700\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so at a price of $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ the NPV is $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

To find the break-even price, keep the three future cash flows fixed and replace the initial cost with $C_0$. The NPV becomes $700-C_0$, decreasing by one euro for each additional euro spent at time zero. The condition $\operatorname{NPV}\geq0$ is therefore equivalent to $C_0\leq700\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and the price that gives zero NPV is the **maximum admissible price**.

</div>
