## Exercise 1

An initial outlay of $350\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is followed by receipts of $220\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $181.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after one and two years. Find the IRR and show that it is unique.

$$
\operatorname{NPV}(i)=-350+220v+181.50v^2,\qquad v=\frac{1}{1+i}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
220&=200\cdot1.1 \\[6pt]
181.50&=150\cdot1.21=150\cdot1.1^2
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}(0.10)&=-350+\frac{220}{1.1}+\frac{181.50}{1.1^2} \\[6pt]
&=-350+200+150=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\operatorname{NPV}(0)=-350+220+181.50=51.50>0.
$$

The unique annual IRR is $10\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The dates are integers, so the substitution $v=(1+i)^{-1}$ makes NPV a quadratic polynomial in $v$. Try the factor $q=1.1$ because $220$ is the one-year accumulated value of $200$ and $181.50$ is the two-year accumulated value of $150$. At $10\%$ the present values sum to $350\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and cover the initial outlay, so $10\%$ is a root.

Apply the **existence and uniqueness theorem for the IRR of a conventional investment**. Its assumptions hold because the initial outlay and both subsequent receipts are positive and the receipts occur after the initial date. NPV is therefore strictly decreasing on $i>-1$ and vanishes exactly once, making $10\%$ the **unique IRR**.

The value $\operatorname{NPV}(0)=51.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the sum of the nominal receipts less the outlay. It is positive, so the theorem gives a positive IRR, in agreement with the computed rate of $10\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An investment costing $275\,\mathrm{EUR}$ pays $160\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at each year-end for two years. Determine whether its unique IRR lies between $8\%$ and $10\%$, between $10\%$ and $12\%$, or between $12\%$ and $14\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{160}{1.1}&\approx145.45,\qquad \frac{160}{1.21}\approx132.23 \\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}(0.10)&\approx-275+145.45+132.23\approx2.69\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{160}{1.12}&\approx142.86,\qquad \frac{160}{1.2544}\approx127.55 \\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}(0.12)&\approx-275+142.86+127.55\approx-4.59\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\operatorname{NPV}(0.11)=-275+\frac{160}{1.11}+\frac{160}{1.11^2}\approx-1.00\,\mathrm{EUR}.
$$

The IRR lies between $10\%$ and $12\%$, and more precisely between $10\%$ and $11\%$.

The NPV is positive at $10\%$ and negative at both $11\%$ and $12\%$, so the root lies between $10\%$ and $11\%$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both future receipts and the initial outlay are positive. By the **existence and uniqueness theorem for the IRR of a conventional investment**, NPV is continuous and strictly decreasing. The IRR is the only rate at which its sign changes, so the signs of NPV at the proposed endpoints suffice.

At $10\%$ the two receipts have a combined present value of approximately $277.69\,\mathrm{EUR}$, above the cost, so NPV is positive. At $12\%$ their combined present value is approximately $270.41\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so NPV is negative. The middle interval has opposite NPV signs at its endpoints, so the intermediate value theorem places the root in that interval.

One step of **bisection** narrows the interval. The midpoint is $11\%$, where NPV is approximately $-1.00\,\mathrm{EUR}$. NPV is decreasing and already negative at $11\%$, so the root lies in the left half, between $10\%$ and $11\%$.

</div>
