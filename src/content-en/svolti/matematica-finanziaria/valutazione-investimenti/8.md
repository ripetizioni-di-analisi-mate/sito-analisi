## Exercise 1

A project costs $250\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. It pays $121\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the end of each of the first two years and $66.55\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year three. Find the discounted payback period at an annual rate of $10\%$.

$$
\frac{121}{1.1}=110,\qquad \frac{121}{1.1^2}=\frac{121}{1.21}=100,\qquad \frac{66.55}{1.1^3}=\frac{66.55}{1.331}=50
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1&=110\,\mathrm{EUR}<250\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
S_2&=110+100=210\,\mathrm{EUR}<250\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
S_3&=210+50=260\,\mathrm{EUR}\geq250\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The cost is recovered at the **end of year three**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Discounted payback occurs on the first date when the receipts' present values together reach the cost. Calculate the cumulative total on each receipt date. Discount each payment to time $0$ using the factor for its date, $1.1$, $1.21$ and $1.331$ for years one, two and three.

The first two payments total $242\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in nominal terms but have a present value of $210\,\mathrm{EUR}$, below the cost. At the end of year two, $40\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of the cost therefore remains unrecovered in initial-date euros. The third receipt adds $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in initial-date euros, bringing the cumulative amount to $260\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The cumulative amount first reaches the cost on the third payment date. Simple payback based on nominal amounts would also occur in year three because $242<250$. The final excess of $S_3-250=10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the project's NPV at $10\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A project requires $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and generates $176$, $121$ and $39.93\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the ends of the next three years. Compare simple and discounted payback at $10\%$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
176+121&=297<300 \\[6pt]
176+121+39.93&=336.93>300
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{176}{1.1}=160,\qquad \frac{121}{1.21}=100,\qquad \frac{39.93}{1.331}=30
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1&=160\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
S_2&=160+100=260\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
S_3&=260+30=290\,\mathrm{EUR}<300\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Simple payback occurs in year three. **Discounted payback does not occur** by then.

::grafico{f="300" xmin=0 xmax=3.5 ymin=0 ymax=400 punti="1,176,vuoto;2,297,vuoto;3,336.93,vuoto;1,160,pieno;2,260,pieno;3,290,pieno" nomi="t,euro" legenda="C_0=300" titolo="Cumulative inflows in euros, with $t$ in years. Open points mark the undiscounted totals $176$, $297$, and $336.93$. Filled points give the totals discounted at $10\%$, which are $160$, $260$, and $290$. Only the open points rise above the cost line, at time $3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Simple payback compares the cost with total nominal receipts. The first two receipts total $297\,\mathrm{EUR}$, below the cost, so simple payback occurs only with the third payment, when the total reaches $336.93\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

For discounted payback, bring the receipts back to the initial date using the factors $1.1$, $1.21$ and $1.331$. Their present values are $160$, $100$ and $30\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Since all receipts are positive, their cumulative present value increases and reaches its maximum on the third date.

The maximum is $290\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so no payment date reaches the threshold of $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The unrecovered cost is $10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in initial-date euros. The difference $S_3-300=-10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is the project's NPV at $10\%$. It is negative even though simple payback occurs within the horizon.

</div>
