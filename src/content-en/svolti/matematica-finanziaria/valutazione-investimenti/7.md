## Exercise 1

A loan with face amount $1560\,\mathrm{EUR}$ charges upfront fees of $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and requires a repayment of $1620\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. Calculate the effective rate on the borrower's cash flow.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C-S&=1560-60=1500\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
1500&=\frac{1620}{1+i} \\[6pt]
1+i&=\frac{1620}{1500}=1.08 \\[6pt]
i&=1.08-1=0.08.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{1620}{1560}-1\approx0.0385.
$$

The effective annual rate is $8\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The fees and the disbursement occur on the same date, so the amount available to the borrower is their difference, $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. This is an inflow at time $0$ for the borrower, and the repayment is an outflow at time $1$. The effective rate sets NPV to zero, so the net proceeds must equal the present value of the repayment, discounted for one year.

Since the loan has only one repayment, the ratio of that repayment to the net proceeds directly gives the annual factor $1.08$ and a rate of $8\%$. The cash flow changes sign once, so the uniqueness theorem gives a single effective rate.

The last calculation uses face amount in place of net proceeds and gives approximately $3.85\%$. This value is lower because it compares the repayment with more initial funds than the borrower actually receives. The upfront fees cost the borrower $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today, and the effective rate accounts for them.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A loan with face amount $2050\,\mathrm{EUR}$ withholds $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ upfront. The borrower pays $1010\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one month and $1020.10\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two months. Verify the monthly rate and calculate its annual equivalent.

$$
C-S=2050-50=2000\,\mathrm{EUR}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1010}{1.01}&=1000\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\frac{1020.10}{1.01^2}&=\frac{1020.10}{1.0201}=1000\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\frac{1010}{1.01}+\frac{1020.10}{1.01^2}&=1000+1000=2000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
i_a&=1.01^{12}-1 \\[6pt]
&\approx1.126825-1=0.126825.
\end{aligned}
$$

The monthly borrowing cost is $1\%$ and the equivalent annual rate is approximately $12.6825\%$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The borrower receives $2000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ net because the fees withheld at disbursement reduce the face amount. The dates are in months, so the root of the rate equation is a monthly rate. The payments fall due in one and two months, giving discount factor exponents of $1$ and $2$.

At the monthly rate of $1\%$, each payment is worth $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today because $1010=1000\cdot1.01$ and $1020.10=1000\cdot1.0201$. The present values together offset the net proceeds, so $1\%$ makes the borrower's NPV zero. Reversing all signs gives an initial outlay followed by receipts. By the uniqueness theorem $1\%$ is therefore the **only monthly rate** that makes NPV zero.

A year contains twelve months, so calculating the equivalent annual rate requires applying the monthly factor $1.01$ twelve times. The effective annual rate of approximately $12.68\%$ exceeds the $12\%$ obtained by multiplying the monthly rate by twelve, because each month's interest also accrues on interest earned in earlier months.

</div>
