## Exercise 1

A project costs $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today, generates a receipt of $260\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year and requires a closure payment of $168\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years. Find the IRRs and the interval on which NPV is positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}(i)&=-100+260v-168v^2 \\[6pt]
&=-(168v^2-260v+100) \\[6pt]
&=-(12v-10)(14v-10).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
12v-10=0\quad&\Longrightarrow\quad v=\frac{5}{6}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i=\frac{6}{5}-1=20\% \\[6pt]
14v-10=0\quad&\Longrightarrow\quad v=\frac{5}{7}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i=\frac{7}{5}-1=40\%
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\operatorname{NPV}(0.30)=-100+\frac{260}{1.3}-\frac{168}{1.69}\approx0.59\,\mathrm{EUR}>0.
$$

The IRRs are $20\%$ and $40\%$. NPV is positive for $20\%<i<40\%$.

::grafico{f="-100+260/(1+x/100)-168/(1+x/100)^2" xmin=0 xmax=50 ymin=-10 ymax=2 punti="0,-8,pieno;20,0,pieno;40,0,pieno" nomi="i,VAN" legenda="\operatorname{NPV}(i)" titolo="NPV in euros for the project with a closing cost, with $i$ in percentage points. Its NPV is $-8$ euros when $i=0$, equals zero at $20\%$ and $40\%$, and is positive only between the two IRRs."}

<div class="spiegazione">

With $v=(1+i)^{-1}$, NPV is a quadratic polynomial in $v$. The final cost is negative in the cash flow, giving the polynomial a negative quadratic coefficient. After factoring out the minus sign, the trinomial $168v^2-260v+100$ factors as $(12v-10)(14v-10)$, as expanding the product confirms. Both roots, $\dfrac{5}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5}{7}$, are positive values of $v$ and therefore give admissible rates. Using $i=v^{-1}-1$ gives $20\%$ and $40\%$.

The negative of the product is positive when the two factors have opposite signs, that is, for $\dfrac{5}{7}<v<\dfrac{5}{6}$. Since $i=v^{-1}-1$ decreases with $v$, converting the interval to $i$ reverses the order of its endpoints and gives $20\%<i<40\%$. The value at $30\%$, inside this interval, is positive and confirms the sign.

The cash flow changes sign twice, so it does not satisfy the uniqueness theorem and NPV can vanish more than once. At a zero rate NPV is $-100+260-168=-8\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A project has cash flows $-100$, $240$ and $-144\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at times $0$, $1$ and $2$ in years. Determine its IRRs and whether NPV changes sign.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}(i)&=-100+240v-144v^2 \\[6pt]
&=-(144v^2-240v+100) \\[6pt]
&=-(12v-10)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
v=\frac{10}{12}=\frac{5}{6}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i=\frac{6}{5}-1=20\%.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{NPV}(0.10)&=-\left(\frac{120}{11}-10\right)^2=-\frac{100}{121}\approx-0.83\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
\operatorname{NPV}(0.30)&=-\left(\frac{120}{13}-10\right)^2=-\frac{100}{169}\approx-0.59\,\mathrm{EUR}
\end{aligned}
$$

The IRR is $20\%$, and NPV is negative at every other admissible rate.

<div class="spiegazione">

With $v=(1+i)^{-1}$, NPV is a quadratic trinomial in $v$. The trinomial $144v^2-240v+100$ has discriminant $240^2-4\cdot144\cdot100=0$, so it is the square of a binomial, $(12v-10)^2$. The factorization gives a single zero, $v=\dfrac{5}{6}$, corresponding to the rate $20\%$.

The square is positive for every other $v$, so the leading minus sign makes NPV negative on both sides. The values calculated at $10\%$ and $30\%$, one on either side of the IRR, are indeed negative.

Here the IRR identifies a break-even point because NPV vanishes there, and NPV **does not change sign**. No valuation rate gives the project a higher value than declining it, and comparing the IRR with the valuation rate does not determine the sign of NPV.

</div>
