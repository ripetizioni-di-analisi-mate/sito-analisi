## Exercise 1

A loan of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at an annual interest rate of $10\%$ is repaid with successive year-end payments of $300$, $400$, and $528\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find the balance immediately after the second installment using both methods.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_2 &= \frac{528}{1.1}=480 \\[6pt]
D_2 &= 1000\cdot1.1^2-300\cdot1.1-400 \\[6pt]
    &= 1210-330-400=480\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rate of $10\%$ is constant and the third payment pays off the loan. The theorem on the **prospective and retrospective methods** therefore applies. Immediately after the second installment only the payment at the end of year three remains, so the prospective method discounts it for one year. The retrospective method accumulates the original principal for two years. Accumulate the first payment for one year because it was made one year before the valuation date. Subtract the second at face value because it has just been made. The three terms are the accumulated principal, $1000\cdot1.21=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$, the accumulated first installment, $300\cdot1.1=330\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and the second installment. The balance recurrence gives a first balance of $1100-300=800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and a second balance of $800\cdot1.1-400=480\,\mathrm{EUR}$, confirming the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A loan of $3000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at an annual interest rate of $10\%$ has two regular installments of $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and an additional payment of $1320\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the final due date. Find the balance immediately after the first installment and explain how each method accounts for the final payment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1 &= \frac{1100+1320}{1.1}=\frac{2420}{1.1}=2200 \\[6pt]
D_1 &= 3000\cdot1.1-1100 \\[6pt]
    &= 3300-1100=2200\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= 0.10\cdot3000=300,\qquad C_1=1100-300=800 \\[6pt]
I_2 &= 0.10\cdot2200=220,\qquad C_2=2420-220=2200.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The valuation date is the end of year one, immediately after the payment of $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The prospective method discounts both amounts due at the end of year two, a total of $2420\,\mathrm{EUR}$, for one year. The retrospective method uses past cash flows. The original $3000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ grows to $3300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in one year. Subtract the installment just paid. Since the additional payment is still to come, it appears only in the prospective calculation. The first installment includes interest of $0.10\cdot3000=300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and repays $800\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of principal, reducing the balance from $3000$ to $2200\,\mathrm{EUR}$, as both methods show. At the final date interest is $220\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the total payment of $2420\,\mathrm{EUR}$ repays $2200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of principal. To check that the loan is paid off, the balance grows to $2200\cdot1.1=2420\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the end of year two, equal to the total final payment.

</div>
