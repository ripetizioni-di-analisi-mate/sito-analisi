## Exercise 1

Immediately after the current installment the outstanding balance is $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The effective annual interest rate is $21\%$. The borrower pays off the loan six months later without making another installment. Interest accrues exponentially over partial periods, and a fixed fee of $25\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is payable on the settlement date. Find the total outlay.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.21)^{\frac{1}{2}} &= \sqrt{1.21}=1.1 \\[6pt]
D_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=1500(1.21)^{\frac{1}{2}}
             =1500\cdot1.1=1650\\[6pt]
E &= 1650+25=1675\,\mathrm{EUR} \\[6pt]
E-(1500+25) &= 1675-1525=150\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The balance is known immediately after the installment, half a year before settlement. Since there are no intervening payments, accumulate it for half a period. Under exponential interpolation the amount owed a fraction $\theta$ of a period after the installment is $D_kq^\theta$. Here $\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the accumulation factor is the square root of the annual factor, $\sqrt{1.21}=1.1$. The six months' interest is $1650-1500=150\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The fee of $25\,\mathrm{EUR}$ is already expressed at the settlement date, so add it to the accumulated balance. An immediate payoff after the installment would have cost $1500+25=1525\,\mathrm{EUR}$, so the six-month delay costs exactly the accrued interest, $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$. As a check, discounting the outlay net of the fee to the previous installment date gives $\dfrac{1650}{1.1}=1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Payments of $1210$ and $1331\,\mathrm{EUR}$ remain due in one and two years, respectively. The contractual interest rate is $10\%$ and the market rate is $21\%$. Find the contractual balance and the market value of the lender's claim.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= \frac{1210}{1.1}+\frac{1331}{1.21}
   =1100+1100=2200\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
V &= \frac{1210}{1.21}+\frac{1331}{1.21^2}\\[6pt]
  &= \frac{1210}{1.21}+\frac{1331}{1.4641}\\[6pt]
  &= 1000+\frac{10000}{11}\approx1909.09\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
D-V &= 2200-1909.09\approx290.91\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both values refer to today and to the same remaining payments. Discount at the contractual rate to find the balance and at the investor's required yield to find the market value. Discount the first payment for one year and the second for two. At the contractual rate the discount factors are $1.1$ and $1.1^2=1.21$, and each payment is worth $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today. At the market rate the factors are $1.21$ and $1.21^2=1.4641$, giving present values of $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and $\dfrac{1331}{1.4641}=\dfrac{10000}{11}\approx909.09\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Since the market rate exceeds the contractual rate and the remaining payments are positive, the market price is lower than the contractual balance. The difference is approximately $290.91\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
