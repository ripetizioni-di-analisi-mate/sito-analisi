## Exercise 1

The third and final principal portion of a level-payment loan bearing interest at $10\%$ per period is $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find the first two principal portions and the original principal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_2 &= \frac{1210}{1.1}=1100 \\[6pt]
C_1 &= \frac{1100}{1.1}=1000 \\[6pt]
D_0 &= 1000+1100+1210=3310\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_2 &= C_3=1210 \\[6pt]
R &= C_3+0.10\cdot D_2=1210+121=1331\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interest rate of $10\%$ is constant throughout the loan, so the **level-payment theorem** applies and the installment is fixed. Each payment reduces the balance by its principal portion, so the following period's interest falls by $i$ times that portion. The principal portion of the following installment increases by the same amount. Each principal portion is therefore $1.1$ times the preceding one. Starting with the third portion, divide twice by $1.1$. All principal is repaid, so the three portions sum to the original loan. Since the last installment pays off the loan, the balance immediately after the second installment equals the third principal portion, $D_2=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Add the final period's interest, $0.10\cdot1210=121\,\mathrm{EUR}$, to obtain a last installment of $1331\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The first installment is $1000+0.10\cdot3310=1331\,\mathrm{EUR}$, equal to the last as required.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A three-period level-payment loan has an installment of $1728\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Its first two principal portions are $1000$ and $1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find the effective interest rate per period and the original principal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= \frac{C_2}{C_1}=\frac{1200}{1000}=1.2\\[6pt]
i &= 1.2-1=20\%\\[6pt]
C_3 &= 1.2\cdot1200=1440\\[6pt]
D_0 &= 1000+1200+1440=3640\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1 &= 3640-1000=2640 \\[6pt]
I_2 &= 0.20\cdot2640=528,\qquad C_2=1728-528=1200 \\[6pt]
I_3 &= 0.20\cdot1440=288,\qquad C_3+I_3=1440+288=1728.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two principal portions belong to successive installments, so their ratio is the one-period accumulation factor. Subtract one to find the effective rate, then use the same factor to find the third principal portion. Because the loan is paid off at maturity, the three portions sum to $D_0$. To check against the given installment, the interest on the first payment is $1728-1000=728\,\mathrm{EUR}$, which equals $0.20\cdot3640$. In the second period the balance is $3640-1000=2640\,\mathrm{EUR}$, giving interest of $528\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and a principal portion of $1728-528=1200\,\mathrm{EUR}$, as stated. In the last period the balance equals the third principal portion, so interest is $0.20\cdot1440=288\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and the installment $1440+288$ is again $1728\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The check uses the breakdown of each payment, while the original principal follows from the sum of the principal portions.

</div>
