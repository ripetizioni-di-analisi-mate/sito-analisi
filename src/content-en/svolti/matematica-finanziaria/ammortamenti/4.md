## Exercise 1

A loan of $3150\,\mathrm{EUR}$ pays annual interest at $6\%$ and repays the full principal in two years. A sinking fund earning $10\%$ annually receives two equal year-end deposits. Find the borrower's annual cash outlay.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= 0.06\cdot3150=189 \\[6pt]
s_{\overline 2|0.1} &= 1.1+1=2.1 \\[6pt]
Q(1.1+1)&=3150 \\[6pt]
Q &= \frac{3150}{2.1}=1500 \\[6pt]
I+Q&=1689\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1 &= 1500 \\[6pt]
F_2 &= 1500\cdot1.1+1500=1650+1500=3150.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,3150+0*t,0,2;t,1500*1.1^(t-1),1,2" segmenti="1,0,1,1500;2,1650,2,3150" punti="0,0;1,1500;2,3150;2,1650,vuoto" xmin=-0.2 xmax=2.5 ymin=0 ymax=3500 nomi="t,euro" legenda="\text{Principal owed to the lender};\text{Fund balance}" titolo="Principal owed to the lender and balance of the fund earning $10\%$, in euros. The fund receives $1500$ euros at times $1$ and $2$. The open point shows the balance of $1650$ euros immediately before the second deposit."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The lender receives interest on the full principal each year because all principal remains outstanding until maturity. The fund earns interest only on deposits it has received. The first deposit grows for one year at $10\%$, while the second is available at maturity without earning interest. Together they must accumulate to the principal due, so calculate $Q$ at the final date. The coefficient of $Q$ is the accumulated value of two unit year-end deposits, $s_{\overline 2|0.1}=1.1+1=2.1$, which gives $Q=\dfrac{D_0}{s_{\overline 2|0.1}}=1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The fund holds $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ immediately after the first deposit and $1500\cdot1.1=1650\,\mathrm{EUR}$ a year later before the second. In the final year the borrower deposits another $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in the fund and pays $189\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in interest. This deposit brings the fund to $1650+1500=3150\,\mathrm{EUR}$, which the borrower transfers to the lender. The borrower's cash outlay is therefore $189+1500=1689\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in each year.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A loan of $5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ pays annual interest at $4\%$ and repays all principal at the end of year three. The borrower has deposited $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at each of the three year-ends in a fund earning $5\%$. Find the extra amount required at maturity and the borrower's total cash outlay in the final year, including the last fund deposit.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_3 &= 1500(1.05^2+1.05+1) \\[6pt]
    &= 1500(1.1025+1.05+1) \\[6pt]
    &= 1500\cdot3.1525=4728.75 \\[6pt]
X &= 5000-4728.75=271.25 \\[6pt]
I &= 0.04\cdot5000=200\\[6pt]
I+1500+X &=1971.25\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three deposits earn interest for two, one, and zero years, respectively, because the last is deposited on the repayment date. The first grows to $1500\cdot1.1025=1653.75\,\mathrm{EUR}$, the second to $1500\cdot1.05=1575\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and the third remains $1500\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The fund covers $4728.75\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of the $5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ due, so the borrower supplies the difference. The final year's outlay includes the last fund deposit and the interest due to the lender. Add the shortfall, with the fund paying the rest of the principal. Annual interest paid to the lender is always $0.04\cdot5000=200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ because the full principal remains outstanding until maturity under a sinking-fund loan. The final year's outlay is therefore $200+1500+271.25=1971.25\,\mathrm{EUR}$. To check the principal repayment, $4728.75+271.25=5000\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
