## Exercise 1

Immediately after an installment, a balance of $2100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ remains to be repaid over two years. From this date the effective annual interest rate is $10\%$. Find the two new equal year-end installments and the first year's interest portion.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{1.1}+\frac{1}{1.1^2}
  &= \frac{1.1+1}{1.21}=\frac{210}{121}\\[6pt]
2100 &= \frac{R}{1.1}+\frac{R}{1.1^2}=\frac{210}{121}R\\[6pt]
R &= 2100\cdot\frac{121}{210}=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
I &=0.10\cdot2100=210\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_1 &= 1210-210=1000,\qquad D_1=2100-1000=1100 \\[6pt]
I_2 &= 0.10\cdot1100=110,\qquad C_2=1210-110=1100.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The new schedule begins immediately after the last installment under the old schedule. The outstanding principal on that date is $2100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The new schedule retains two equal installments, so the outstanding balance becomes the initial principal of a level-payment loan, with installment $R=\dfrac{D_k}{a_{\overline 2|0.1}}$. The new installments are due one and two years after the revision. Discount them at the new rate for one and two periods, respectively. With common denominator $1.21$ the annuity factor is $\dfrac{1.1+1}{1.21}=\dfrac{210}{121}$, giving $R=2100\cdot\dfrac{121}{210}=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The first interest charge accrues on the full balance of $2100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. To check, the first installment repays $1210-210=1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of principal. The remaining $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ accrues $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ of interest in the last year and the second installment pays off the loan.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Immediately after an installment the outstanding balance is $4700\,\mathrm{EUR}$. On that date the borrower pays an extra $600\,\mathrm{EUR}$ toward principal. The interest rate for subsequent periods is $5\%$ and the remaining loan will be repaid in two equal annual installments. Find the new installment and the principal portion of the first payment under the revised schedule.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{new}} &= 4700-600=4100\\[6pt]
4100 &= \frac{R}{1.05}+\frac{R}{1.05^2}\\[6pt]
R &= 4100\cdot\frac{441}{820}
   =2205\,\mathrm{EUR}\\[6pt]
C_1 &= 2205-0.05\cdot4100 \\[6pt]
    &= 2205-205=2000\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
600+\frac{2205}{1.05}+\frac{2205}{1.05^2}
=600+2100+2000=4700.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The extra payment is made when the new schedule begins and reduces the principal immediately, so subtract it directly from the balance of $4700\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Apply the rate of $5\%$ to the $4100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ actually refinanced. That rate determines the present value of the installments and the first interest charge. The two-year annuity factor at $5\%$ is $\dfrac{1}{1.05}+\dfrac{1}{1.05^2}=\dfrac{2.05}{1.1025}=\dfrac{820}{441}$, giving $R=4100\cdot\dfrac{441}{820}=2205\,\mathrm{EUR}$. First-year interest is $0.05\cdot4100=205\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Subtract it from the installment of $2205\,\mathrm{EUR}$ to obtain the first principal portion. The balance falls to $2100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. After one year at $5\%$ it grows to $2205\,\mathrm{EUR}$, exactly the final installment. Discounting the extra payment and both installments at $5\%$ gives a combined value of $600+2100+2000=4700\,\mathrm{EUR}$, the outstanding balance at the revision date.

</div>
