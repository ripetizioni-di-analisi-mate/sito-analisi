## Exercise 1

Security $A$ pays $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one and costs $96\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Security $B$ pays $8$ and $108\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in the two years and costs $104.88\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find the holdings and cost of a portfolio that pays $58$ and $108\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
100x_A+8x_B=58\\[4pt]
108x_B=108
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_B&=\frac{108}{108}=1,\\[6pt]
x_A&=\frac{58-8\cdot1}{100}=\frac{50}{100}=0.5,\\[6pt]
P&=0.5\cdot96+1\cdot104.88\\[6pt]
&=48+104.88=152.88\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v(0,1)&=\frac{96}{100}=0.96,\\[6pt]
v(0,2)&=\frac{104.88-8\cdot0.96}{108}=0.9,\\[6pt]
58\cdot0.96+108\cdot0.9&=55.68+97.20=152.88\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The portfolio must reproduce the target cash flow on both dates, so write one equation per date. Security $A$ matures after one year. The year-two payment therefore requires one unit of $B$, since $B$ is the only security paying on that date.

In year one this unit already pays $8\,\mathrm{EUR}$. To reach $58\,\mathrm{EUR}$, the portfolio needs another $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$, provided by half a unit of $A$. After checking both dates, add the purchase prices of the required holdings.

By the **pricing theorem for replicable cash flows**, in the absence of arbitrage the cash flow $(58,108)$ costs the same as the portfolio. Bootstrapping provides a check. Security $A$ gives $v(0,1)=0.96$ and security $B$ gives $v(0,2)=0.9$. The value of the cash flow on the resulting curve equals the portfolio cost.

The replicating portfolio holds half a unit of $A$ and one unit of $B$ and costs $152.88\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two securities have cash flows of $(100,100)$ and $(50,150)$ euros, respectively, in years $1$ and $2$. Their prices are $185$ and $177.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Fractional and negative holdings are allowed. Replicate the cash flow $(0,100)$ and calculate its price.

$$
\begin{cases}
100x_A+50x_B=0\\[4pt]
100x_A+150x_B=100
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
100x_B&=100,\\[6pt]
x_B&=1,\qquad x_A=-\frac{50\cdot1}{100}=-0.5,\\[6pt]
P&=-0.5\cdot185+1\cdot177.50\\[6pt]
&=-92.50+177.50=85\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write one equation per date, since the portfolio must pay $0$ in year one and $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two. Subtracting the equations eliminates the holding of $A$ because security $A$ pays the same amount in both years. This gives $x_B=1$ and, on substitution in the first equation, $x_A=-0.5$.

One unit of $B$ provides $50$ and $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Selling half a unit of $A$ introduces an outflow of $50\,\mathrm{EUR}$ on each date. The first outflow cancels the intermediate payment, and the second leaves the desired redemption payment of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The net cost includes the sale proceeds with a negative sign, giving $-0.5\cdot185=-92.50\,\mathrm{EUR}$. By the **pricing theorem for replicable cash flows**, the cash flow $(0,100)$ has the same price as the portfolio. The two securities imply the discount factors $v(0,1)=1$ and $v(0,2)=0.85$, and the resulting curve values the cash flow at $100\cdot0.85=85\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

Buying one unit of $B$ and selling half a unit of $A$ short replicates the cash flow. Its price is $85\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>
