## Exercise 1

Let $z(1)=3\%$ and $z(4)=4.5\%$ be the continuously compounded spot rates. Calculate the average continuously compounded forward rate from year one to year four and the accumulation factor over that interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log v(0,1)&=-z(1)\cdot1=-0.03,\\[6pt]
\log v(0,4)&=-z(4)\cdot4=-4\cdot0.045=-0.18,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi_{1,4}&=\frac{\log v(0,1)-\log v(0,4)}{4-1}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{-0.03+0.18}{3}=\frac{0.15}{3}=5\%,\\[6pt]
K&=\frac{v(0,1)}{v(0,4)}=e^{0.05\cdot3}=e^{0.15}\approx1.1618.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Under continuous compounding the discount factor is $v(0,T)=e^{-z(T)T}$, so its natural logarithm is $-z(T)T$. The product of the spot rate and the maturity determines the exponent. First use each spot rate to calculate the logarithm of the corresponding discount factor. This gives $-0.03$ at maturity $1$ and $-0.18$ at maturity $4$.

The **average continuously compounded forward rate** is $\phi_{s,t}=\dfrac{\log v(0,s)-\log v(0,t)}{t-s}$. The difference between the logarithms isolates the interval from year one to year four and equals $0.15$. Dividing by three years gives $\phi_{1,4}=0.05$, or $5\%$ per year with continuous compounding.

The accumulation factor over the interval is the ratio of the two discount factors, $e^{-0.03}$ divided by $e^{-0.18}$, which equals $e^{0.15}\approx1.1618$. Multiplying the accumulation factor for the first year by $e^{0.15}$ gives $e^{0.18}$, the accumulation factor for all four years.

The average continuously compounded forward rate is $5\%$, equivalent to an effective annual rate of $e^{0.05}-1\approx5.13\%$. One euro invested at the end of year one grows to approximately $1.16\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at the end of year four.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the curve $v(0,T)=e^{-0.03T-0.01T^2}$, calculate the instantaneous forward rate at time $2$ and compare it with the average continuously compounded forward rate between $1$ and $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\log v(0,T)&=0.03T+0.01T^2,\\[6pt]
g(T)&=-\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dT}\log v(0,T)=0.03+0.02T,\\[6pt]
g(2)&=0.03+0.04=0.07=7\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\log v(0,1)&=0.03+0.01=0.04,\\[6pt]
-\log v(0,2)&=0.06+0.04=0.10,\\[6pt]
\phi_{1,2}&=\frac{0.10-0.04}{2-1}=0.06=6\%,\\[6pt]
\int_1^2 g(u)\,\mathrm du&=\Big[0.03u+0.01u^2\Big]_1^2=0.10-0.04=0.06.
\end{aligned}
$$

The shaded area between $T=1$ and $T=2$ equals $6$, so the average of $g$ over the interval, denoted by $\phi_{1,2}$, equals $6\%$. The dashed segment marks this average."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The logarithm of the discount factor is $-0.03T-0.01T^2$, a differentiable function of $T$. The **instantaneous forward rate** is $g(T)=-\dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dT}\log v(0,T)$ and measures the local rate of change of $-\log v(0,T)$ with maturity. Differentiating $0.03T+0.01T^2$ gives $0.03+0.02T$, which at time $2$ equals $7\%$.

The average forward rate measures the change in the same function over the entire interval. The negative logarithm is $0.04$ at one year and $0.10$ at two years. The difference is $0.06$ over an interval of one year, so $\phi_{1,2}=6\%$.

Since $g$ is continuous, the fundamental theorem of calculus expresses this difference as the integral of $g$ from $1$ to $2$. The average forward rate is therefore the integral average of the instantaneous forward rate over the interval. The function $g$ is affine and rises from $5\%$ to $7\%$, so its average equals its value at the midpoint, $g(1.5)=6\%$.

The instantaneous forward rate at time $2$ is $7\%$. It is one percentage point above the average continuously compounded forward rate between $1$ and $2$, which is $6\%$.

</div>
