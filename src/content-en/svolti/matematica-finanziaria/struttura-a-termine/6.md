## Exercise 1

A cash flow requires a payment of $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and provides receipts of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year one and $60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two. Calculate its net value using $v(0,1)=0.97$ and $v(0,2)=0.9$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=-150\,v(0,0)+100\,v(0,1)+60\,v(0,2)\\[6pt]
&=-150\cdot1+100\cdot0.97+60\cdot0.9\\[6pt]
&=-150+97+54=1\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The initial outflow is $150$, and the inflows are $100$ and $60$ at times $1$ and $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **value of a cash flow on the discount curve** is the sum of the signed amounts, each multiplied by its own discount factor. The initial payment is an outflow and carries a negative sign. Since it is due today, its factor is $v(0,0)=1$ and its present value equals the amount paid.

The other two amounts are receipts on different dates, so multiply each by its own factor. The first receipt is worth $100\cdot0.97=97\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today and the second is worth $60\cdot0.9=54\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Their combined present value is $151\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

Subtracting the initial $150\,\mathrm{EUR}$ leaves a positive net value of one euro. The undiscounted sum of the cash flows is $-150+100+60=10\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Discounting the future receipts explains the difference from $1\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The net value of the cash flow on the discount curve is $1\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The two receipts are therefore worth one euro more than the initial outlay.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $v(0,1)=0.9$ and $v(0,3)=0.729$ be given. Using linear interpolation of the logarithms of the discount factors, calculate the price of a receipt of $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log v(0,2)&=\frac{\log(0.9)+\log(0.729)}{2},\\[6pt]
0.729&=0.9^3,\\[6pt]
\log v(0,2)&=\frac{\log(0.9)+3\log(0.9)}{2}=2\log(0.9),\\[6pt]
v(0,2)&=0.9^2=\sqrt{0.9\cdot0.729}=0.81,\\[6pt]
P&=200\cdot0.81=162\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{0.9+0.729}{2}&=0.8145,\\[6pt]
200\cdot0.8145&=162.90\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Year two is not a node of the curve, so use the specified interpolation convention to obtain its discount factor. Year two lies halfway between the nodes, giving each node weight $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since the convention applies to the logarithms, average those logarithms.

Since $0.729=0.9^3$, the average of the logarithms is $2\log(0.9)$. Exponentiating gives the square root of the product, the geometric mean of the two discount factors, which equals $0.81$. Multiplying $200$ by $0.81$ gives the price of the receipt.

As a check, the ratios $\dfrac{0.9}{0.81}$ and $\dfrac{0.81}{0.729}$ both equal $\dfrac{10}{9}$. The log-discount curve therefore has the same slope over the two subintervals, and the average continuously compounded forward rate is $\log\dfrac{10}{9}\approx10.54\%$ on both.

Linear interpolation of the factors themselves would give the arithmetic mean $0.8145$ and a price of $162.90\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The price thus depends on the convention. Under the stated convention, a receipt of $200\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two is worth $162\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today.

</div>
