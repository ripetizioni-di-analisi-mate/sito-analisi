## Exercise 1

A zero-coupon bond that pays $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in two years costs $1000\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Calculate the effective annual spot rate and the total return over the two years.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v(0,2)&=\frac{1000}{1210}=\frac{100}{121},\\[6pt]
1+s_2&=v(0,2)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{121}{100}}=1.1,\\[6pt]
s_2&=10\%,\\[6pt]
(1+s_2)^2-1&=1.21-1=21\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1000\cdot1.1&=1100\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
1100\cdot1.1&=1210\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the price by the redemption payment gives the discount factor $v(0,2)=\dfrac{100}{121}$. Its reciprocal is the two-year accumulation factor, $\dfrac{121}{100}=1.21$.

The **spot rate** satisfies $v(0,T)=(1+s_T)^{-T}$, so $s_T=v(0,T)^{-\frac{1}{T}}-1$. The rate is quoted annually and the term is $T=2$, so applying the annual factor twice must reproduce the redemption payment. Take the positive square root of $1.21$, which gives $1.1$. The two-year spot rate is therefore $10\%$ per year.

The total return over two years is $1.21-1=0.21$, or $21\%$ of the amount invested. This exceeds twice the annual rate, which would be $20\%$, because the second year's interest accrues on the balance at the end of the first year. The balance reaches $1100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ after one year, so the second year earns $110\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in interest instead of $100$. Substituting the result shows that $1000\cdot1.1^2$ gives exactly $1210\,\mathrm{EUR}$ again.

The effective annual spot rate is $10\%$, and the total two-year return is $21\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A six-month zero-coupon bond pays $102\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and costs $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Calculate the effective annual spot rate using compound interest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v\!\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)&=\frac{100}{102}=\left(1+s_{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\\[6pt]
(1+s_{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}&=\frac{102}{100}=1.02,\\[6pt]
1+s_{\frac{1}{2}}&=1.02^2=1.0404,\\[6pt]
s_{\frac{1}{2}}&=4.04\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
1.02^2=1+2\cdot0.02+0.02^2=1+0.04+0.0004.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The term is half a year, so the maturity in the definition of the spot rate is $T=\dfrac{1}{2}$. The discount factor is the ratio of price to redemption payment, $\dfrac{100}{102}$. Setting it equal to $\left(1+s_{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ and taking reciprocals gives the six-month accumulation factor $1.02$.

To annualize under compound interest, apply the same equivalent six-month rate twice by squaring $1.02$. The product is $1.0404$ because interest for the second six months accrues on the balance after the first six months. Expanding the square separates the two contributions. The term $2\cdot0.02=0.04$ represents two six-month periods at $2\%$ on the initial investment, and $0.02^2=0.0004$ is the interest earned in the second period on the first period's interest.

Discounting the redemption payment using the square root of this annual factor recovers the price of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The effective annual spot rate is therefore $4.04\%$, slightly higher than the $4\%$ obtained by doubling the six-month rate.

</div>
