## Exercise 1

A one-year zero-coupon bond with face value $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ costs $96\,\mathrm{EUR}$. A two-year bond with an annual coupon of $4\,\mathrm{EUR}$ and a redemption payment of $100\,\mathrm{EUR}$ costs $97.44\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Find $v(0,2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v(0,1)&=\frac{96}{100}=0.96,\\[6pt]
97.44&=4\,v(0,1)+104\,v(0,2),\\[6pt]
104v(0,2)&=97.44-4\cdot0.96\\[6pt]
&=97.44-3.84=93.60,\\[6pt]
v(0,2)&=\frac{93.60}{104}=0.9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the price of the zero-coupon bond by its redemption payment gives the one-year discount factor, $0.96$. The price of the two-year bond includes the first coupon and the full payment in the final year. The price equation therefore has one unknown, $v(0,2)$.

The price of the zero-coupon bond already determines the present value of the first coupon, $4\cdot0.96=3.84\,\mathrm{EUR}$. Subtracting it leaves $93.60\,\mathrm{EUR}$ attributable to year two. The final payment includes both the coupon and the principal, so the divisor is $104$.

The **bootstrap** formula applies because the final payment $C_2=104$ is positive and the bond price exceeds the present value of the payment at the known date. The resulting discount factor is therefore positive. Substituting it back gives $3.84+93.60=97.44\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The two-year discount factor is $0.9$. The corresponding spot rate is $0.9^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\approx5.41\%$, above the one-year spot rate of $\dfrac{1}{0.96}-1\approx4.17\%$. The two discount factors also determine the forward rate from year one to year two, $\dfrac{0.96}{0.9}-1\approx6.67\%$. It exceeds both spot rates because the yield curve slopes upward.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The discount factors $v(0,1)=0.98$ and $v(0,2)=0.93$ are known. A bond priced at $95.32\,\mathrm{EUR}$ pays $4$, $6$ and $104\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in years $1$, $2$ and $3$, respectively. Find the third discount factor and the annual forward rate between years $2$ and $3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
104v(0,3)&=95.32-4\cdot0.98-6\cdot0.93\\[6pt]
&=95.32-3.92-5.58=85.82,\\[6pt]
v(0,3)&=\frac{85.82}{104}\approx0.8252,
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1+f_{2,3}&=\frac{v(0,2)}{v(0,3)}=\frac{0.93\cdot104}{85.82}=\frac{96.72}{85.82},\\[6pt]
f_{2,3}&\approx1.12701-1\approx12.701\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bond makes three payments. Since the first two discount factors are known, the price equation has only one unknown, $v(0,3)$. The first two payments differ in amount and discount factor. Calculate their present values separately, $4\cdot0.98=3.92$ and $6\cdot0.93=5.58\,\mathrm{EUR}$, and subtract them from the price.

The remainder, $85.82\,\mathrm{EUR}$, belongs to the third payment, so divide it by $104$. The **bootstrap** formula gives a positive discount factor because the price exceeds the combined present values of the payments at the known dates.

For the forward rate between years $2$ and $3$, use the implied forward rate formula with $t-s=1$. The exponent is $1$, so only the ratio of the discount factors is needed. Keep the exact factor $\dfrac{85.82}{104}$, since rounding before dividing would change the last digit of the rate. Dividing $0.93$ by $\dfrac{85.82}{104}$ is equivalent to multiplying $0.93$ by $104$ and dividing by $85.82$.

The three-year discount factor is approximately $0.8252$. It is less than the two-year factor, so the forward rate is positive, approximately $12.70\%$ per year.

</div>
