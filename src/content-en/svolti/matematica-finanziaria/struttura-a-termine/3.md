## Exercise 1

Given $v(0,1)=0.968$ and $v(0,3)=0.8$, calculate the effective annual rate on a loan from year one to year three and the repayment of a principal of $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1+f_{1,3})^{3-1}&=\frac{v(0,1)}{v(0,3)},\\[6pt]
(1+f_{1,3})^2&=\frac{0.968}{0.8}=1.21,\\[6pt]
f_{1,3}&=\sqrt{1.21}-1=10\%,\\[6pt]
M&=500\cdot1.21=605\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
500\cdot0.968&=484\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
605\cdot0.8&=484\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The lender pays $500$ at time $1$ and receives $605$ at time $3$. Both cash flows have a present value of $484$ euros."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The loan is agreed today, starts at the end of year one and ends at the end of year three, so its term is $t-s=2$ years. For such a loan to have zero value today, the initial loan payment in year one and the repayment in year three must have equal present values. The ratio of the discount factors therefore gives the accumulated value of one euro over the two years of the loan, $1.21$. This is the **implied forward rate** formula, which applies because both discount factors are positive.

The square root expresses the same growth as an annual factor. Since $\sqrt{1.21}=1.1$, the effective annual forward rate is $10\%$.

Multiplying the principal of $500\,\mathrm{EUR}$ by the two-year accumulation factor gives a repayment of $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$. The identity $500\cdot0.968=605\cdot0.8=484\,\mathrm{EUR}$ checks the calculation. To replicate the loan, sell $500$ unit zero-coupon bonds maturing in year one and buy $605$ unit zero-coupon bonds maturing in year three. Both positions have the same value today, $484\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The rate on the loan from year one to year three is $10\%$ per year, and the repayment in year three is $605\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The one-year discount factor is $v(0,1)=0.945$. The effective annual forward rate from year one to year two is $5\%$. Find $v(0,2)$ and the price today of a payment of $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1+f_{1,2}&=\frac{v(0,1)}{v(0,2)},\\[6pt]
v(0,2)\cdot1.05&=0.945,\\[6pt]
v(0,2)&=\frac{0.945}{1.05}=0.9,\\[6pt]
P&=300\cdot0.9=270\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{300}{1.05}&\approx285.71\,\mathrm{EUR},\\[6pt]
285.71\cdot0.945&\approx270\,\mathrm{EUR}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One year separates the two maturities, so the exponent in the **implied forward rate** formula is $1$ and the accumulation factor is $1.05$. A payment of $1.05\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two must have the same present value as one euro in year one. Dividing $0.945$ by $1.05$ therefore gives the discount factor for the second date. The result, $v(0,2)=0.9$, is less than $v(0,1)$, as expected when the forward rate is positive.

Multiplying the factor by the payment of $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ gives a price of $270\,\mathrm{EUR}$. As a check, first discount $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ from year two to year one and then from year one to today. The first step uses the forward rate and gives approximately $285.71\,\mathrm{EUR}$ at time $1$. The second uses $v(0,1)$ and gives $270\,\mathrm{EUR}$.

The discount factors also determine the spot rates. We have $s_1=\dfrac{1}{0.945}-1\approx5.82\%$ and $s_2=0.9^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1\approx5.41\%$. The two-year spot rate lies between $s_1$ and the forward rate of $5\%$ because $(1+s_2)^2=(1+s_1)(1+f_{1,2})$.

The two-year discount factor is $0.9$, and a payment of $300\,\mathrm{EUR}$ in year two costs $270\,\mathrm{EUR}$ today.

</div>
