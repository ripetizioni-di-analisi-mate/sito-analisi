## Exercise 1

A theater sells $150$ tickets, charging $10$ euros for a full-price ticket and $7$ euros for a discounted ticket, and takes in $1290$ euros in total. How many discounted tickets did it sell?

- A) $80$
- B) $70$
- C) $75$
- D) $10$

$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 150 \\
10x + 7y = 1290
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
10x + 7(150 - x) &= 1290 \\[6pt]
10x + 1050 - 7x &= 1290 \\[6pt]
3x &= 240 \\[6pt]
x &= 80, \qquad y = 150 - 80 = 70
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the number of full-price tickets and $y$ the number of discounted tickets. The total ticket count gives $x + y = 150$, and the total receipts give $10x + 7y = 1290$. Using **substitution**, rearrange the first equation to get $y = 150 - x$ and substitute into the second to obtain $3x = 240$, so $x = 80$ and $y = 70$. To check, note that $800 + 490 = 1290$. Choice A gives the number of full-price tickets, answering a different question. Choice C divides the tickets equally, and choice D gives the difference between the two ticket counts. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + ky = 3 \\
4x + 6y = 5
\end{cases}
$$
has no solution for which parameter value?

- A) $k = 3$
- B) $k = -3$
- C) $k = 12$
- D) no value of $k$

$$
D = 2 \cdot 6 - 4 \cdot k = 12 - 4k
$$
$$
D = 0 \iff k = 3
$$
$$
k = 3:\qquad
\begin{cases}
2x + 3y = 3 \\
4x + 6y = 5
\end{cases}
\qquad
2 \cdot (2x + 3y) = 6 \neq 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By **Cramer’s rule**, the system has a unique solution when the determinant $D = 12 - 4k$ is nonzero, so it can have no solution only for $k = 3$. At $k = 3$, doubling the first equation gives $4x + 6y = 6$, whereas the second equation requires $4x + 6y = 5$. No pair satisfies both equations, so the system is **inconsistent**. Geometrically, the two lines are parallel and distinct. Choice B comes from $D = 12 + 4k$, with the wrong sign. Choice C calculates the determinant as $12 - k$. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The solutions of the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 1 \\
x^2 + y^2 = 13
\end{cases}
$$
are given by which choice?

- A) only $(3;-2)$
- B) $(\sqrt{6};1 - \sqrt{6})$ and $(-\sqrt{6};1 + \sqrt{6})$
- C) $(-3;2)$ and $(2;-3)$
- D) $(3;-2)$ and $(-2;3)$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + (1 - x)^2 &= 13 \\[6pt]
x^2 + 1 - 2x + x^2 &= 13 \\[6pt]
2x^2 - 2x - 12 &= 0 \\[6pt]
x^2 - x - 6 &= 0 \\[6pt]
x = 3 \quad &\vee \quad x = -2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = 3 \Rightarrow y = -2,
\qquad
x = -2 \Rightarrow y = 3
$$

::grafico{curve="sqrt(13)*cos(t),sqrt(13)*sin(t),0,6.29" f="1-x" punti="3,-2,pieno;-2,3,pieno" uguale=1 xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 legenda="x + y = 1;x^2 + y^2 = 13" titolo="The line $x + y = 1$ meets the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 13$ at $(3;-2)$ and $(-2;3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The system includes a **quadratic equation** and a linear equation, so solve the linear equation to get $y = 1 - x$ and substitute into the second equation. Expanding the square $(1 - x)^2 = 1 - 2x + x^2$ gives $x^2 - x - 6 = 0$, with roots $3$ and $-2$. For each root, calculate $y = 1 - x$. The solutions are $(3;-2)$ and $(-2;3)$. Indeed, $9 + 4 = 13$. Choice B comes from $(1 - x)^2 = 1 + x^2$, omitting the middle term in the square of the binomial. Choice C comes from $y = -1 - x$, with the wrong sign, which gives $x^2 + x - 6 = 0$. Indeed, the coordinates in each of its pairs add up to $-1$. Choice A omits one of the two solutions. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
