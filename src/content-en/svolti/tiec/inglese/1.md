## Exercise 1

Complete the sentence: "Look! It …… outside, so take an umbrella."

- A) rains
- B) is raining
- C) rained
- D) has rained

The clue *Look!* points to an action in progress at the moment of speaking. The tense is the present continuous, *is raining*.

<div class="spiegazione">

The exclamation *Look!* draws attention to something happening at the moment of speaking. An action in progress now takes the **present continuous**, so the correct form is *is raining*. The present simple *rains* describes a habitual occurrence, such as frequent rain during a particular season. *Rained* and *has rained* place the rain in the past, while the suggestion to take an umbrella tells us it is raining now. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Complete the sentence: "Maria …… for this company since 2018."

- A) works
- B) is working
- C) worked
- D) has worked

The clue *since 2018* gives the starting point in the past of a situation that is still in progress. The tense is the present perfect, *has worked*.

<div class="spiegazione">

*Since* gives the time when a situation began, and the sentence tells us that Maria still works for the company. A state that began in the past and continues now takes the **present perfect**, so the form is *has worked* (in Italian, *lavora per questa azienda dal 2018*). Options A and B translate the Italian present tense *lavora* (“she works”) too literally. An English present tense is incorrect with *since* in this sentence. Option C would imply that her employment has ended, and the past simple does not fit *since* here. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Complete the sentence: "When I arrived at the station, the train …… , so I took a taxi."

- A) had already left
- B) has already left
- C) already leaves
- D) is already leaving

There are two past events. The train left before the speaker arrived at the station. The earlier event takes the past perfect, *had already left*.

<div class="spiegazione">

The sentence describes two past events, the speaker’s arrival at the station (*arrived*) and the train’s departure. The train left *before* the speaker arrived, which explains why the speaker took a taxi. An action that occurred before another point in the past takes the **past perfect**, so the correct form is *had already left* (*it was already gone*). The present perfect *has already left* connects the event to the present and does not fit a narrative in the past simple. Both *already leaves* and *is already leaving* are present-tense forms. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
