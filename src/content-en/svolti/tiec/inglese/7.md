## Exercise 1

Complete the sentence: "The meeting starts …… 9 o'clock …… Monday."

- A) at / on
- B) in / on
- C) at / in
- D) on / at

A clock time takes *at*. A day of the week takes *on*.

<div class="spiegazione">

Among the **prepositions of time**, *at* goes with a precise time and *on* goes with a day of the week, so the correct sentence is *The meeting starts at 9 o'clock on Monday*. Option B uses *in* with a clock time. Option C uses *in* with a day, whereas *in* goes with months, years, and seasons. Option D reverses the two prepositions. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Complete the sentence: "Because of the strike, the company …… the conference. It will not take place."

- A) put on
- B) took over
- C) called off
- D) turned up

*It will not take place* means the event will not happen. *Call off* means cancel. Its past simple is *called off*.

<div class="spiegazione">

The second sentence says that the conference will not take place, so the company has canceled it. The **phrasal verb** meaning *cancel* is *call off*, whose past simple is *called off*. *Put on* means *organize or stage* a show or event and would imply the opposite. *Took over* means *assumed control*, and *turned up* means *showed up*. Neither makes sense here with *the conference* as its object. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Complete the sentence: "I thought the exam was on Monday, but …… it is on Tuesday."

- A) currently
- B) actually
- C) eventually
- D) at the moment

*Actually* means “in fact.” The Italian false friend *attualmente* translates as *currently* or *at the moment*.

<div class="spiegazione">

With *but*, the speaker corrects a mistaken idea, having thought that the exam was on Monday when it is actually on Tuesday. The Italian *In realtà* (“in fact”) translates as *actually*, a **false friend** because it resembles the Italian *attualmente* but has a different meaning. Options A and D mean *currently, at this moment* and appeal to readers who translate by sound. Option C, *eventually*, means *in the end*. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
