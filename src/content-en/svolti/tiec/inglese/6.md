## Exercise 1

Complete the sentence: "The woman …… car was stolen called the police."

- A) who
- B) which
- C) whose
- D) whom

The car belongs to the woman, so the possessive relative pronoun is *whose* (*whose car*).

<div class="spiegazione">

There is a possessive relationship between *the woman* and *car* because the stolen car belongs to the woman. The **relative pronoun** expressing possession is *whose*, so the correct sentence is *The woman whose car was stolen called the police* (*the owner of the stolen car called the police*). *Who* refers to a person but would leave *car* without an article or a link to the rest of the sentence. *Which* refers to things, and *whom* refers to a person as an object without expressing possession. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Complete the sentence: "This is …… film I have ever seen."

- A) the more boring
- B) the boringest
- C) most boring than
- D) the most boring

The comparison is with all the films the speaker has seen, so a superlative is needed. *Boring* has two syllables and does not end in *-y*, giving *the most boring*.

<div class="spiegazione">

The sentence compares the film with all those the speaker has seen (*I have ever seen*), so it requires a **superlative**. *Boring* has two syllables and does not end in *-y*, so its superlative is formed with *the most*, giving *the most boring*. Option A is a comparative. Option B adds *-est* to an adjective whose superlative uses *most*. Option C combines a superlative with *than*, which introduces the second term of a comparative. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Complete the sentence: "There isn't …… milk left, and there are only a …… eggs."

- A) much / few
- B) many / few
- C) much / little
- D) many / little

The noncount noun *milk* takes *much*, and the plural count noun *eggs* takes *a few*.

<div class="spiegazione">

*Milk* is a **noncount noun**, so the negative sentence takes *much*. *Eggs* is a plural count noun, so *a* must be followed by *few*. The expression *a few eggs* means *a small number of eggs*. Options B and D use *many* with *milk*, which has no plural in this sense. Options C and D use *little* with *eggs*, which can be counted. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
