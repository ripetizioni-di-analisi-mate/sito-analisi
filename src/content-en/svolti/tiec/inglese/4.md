## Exercise 1

Complete the sentence: "You …… smoke in this area. It is part of the hospital."

- A) mustn't
- B) don't have to
- C) needn't
- D) shouldn't have

A prohibition takes *mustn't* (*you are not allowed to*). Both *Don't have to* and *needn't* express the absence of obligation (*it is unnecessary*).

<div class="spiegazione">

The context indicates a prohibition because the area is part of a hospital. A **prohibition** is expressed with *mustn't*, so the correct sentence is *You mustn't smoke in this area*. *Don't have to* and *needn't* mean that something is unnecessary, which would make the sentence mean *you are under no obligation to smoke*. These options appeal to readers who translate the Italian *non devi* without distinguishing between prohibition and lack of obligation. *Shouldn't have* takes a past participle and refers to the past, so it does not fit the base form *smoke*. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Complete the sentence: "Paul isn't answering his phone, and his calendar shows a meeting until five. He …… be in the meeting."

- A) can't
- B) must
- C) mustn't
- D) should to

Two present clues point to a firm conclusion. A deduction about the present that the speaker considers certain takes *must* + base form, giving *must be*.

<div class="spiegazione">

The sentence gives two clues. Paul is not answering the phone, and his calendar shows a meeting until five. The speaker therefore concludes with confidence that he is in the meeting. A **firm deduction** about the present takes *must* followed by the base form. *Can't* would express the opposite conclusion, which conflicts with the clues. *Mustn't* expresses a prohibition rather than a deduction. *Should to* is ungrammatical because modal verbs do not take *to*. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Complete the sentence: "I can't find my keys anywhere. I …… them at the office yesterday."

- A) must leave
- B) can have left
- C) must have left
- D) should leave

A deduction about the past (*yesterday*) takes *must have* + past participle, giving *must have left* (*I conclude that I left them*).

<div class="spiegazione">

The speaker cannot find the keys and deduces where they were left yesterday. A **firm deduction about the past** takes *must have* followed by the past participle, so the correct form is *must have left*. *Must leave* and *should leave* refer to the present or future and do not fit *yesterday*. *Can have left* is incorrect because this use of *can* to make a deduction about the past requires the negative form *can't have left*. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
