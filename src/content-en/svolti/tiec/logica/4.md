## Exercise 1

“All club members are adults. No adult can participate in the youth tournament.” Which conclusion necessarily follows?

- A) Some club members can participate in the youth tournament.
- B) No club member can participate in the youth tournament.
- C) All nonmembers of the club can participate in the youth tournament.
- D) All adults are club members.

| First premise | Second premise | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All members are adults | No adult participates | No member participates |

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,b,c" nomi="club,adults,entrants" titolo="Club members are all adults, and no adult is among the youth tournament entrants. The only shaded region in the club members' circle lies outside the entrants' circle."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The premises follow the pattern “All $A$ are $B$, and no $B$ is $C$,” one of the **valid patterns**, which gives “No $A$ is $C$.” By the first premise, a member is an adult, and by the second, an adult cannot participate in the tournament. Consequently no member can participate. Choice A contradicts this conclusion. Choice D reverses the first premise, which cannot be read in both directions. Choice C concerns people about whom the premises say nothing, such as adults who are not members of the club. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

“Some employees are economics graduates. All economics graduates have passed the statistics exam.” Which conclusion necessarily follows?

- A) All employees have passed the statistics exam.
- B) Some employees have not passed the statistics exam.
- C) Everyone who has passed the statistics exam is an economics graduate.
- D) Some employees have passed the statistics exam.

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="a,ac,abc,bc,c" nomi="staff,grads,passed" elementi="abc:×" titolo="The part of the economics graduates' circle outside the set of people who passed the statistics exam is empty. The employee marked $\times$ is an economics graduate, so this person belongs to all three sets."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first premise guarantees at least one employee who is an economics graduate. By the second premise, this employee has passed the statistics exam. There is therefore an employee who has passed the exam. The pattern “Some $A$ are $B$, and all $B$ are $C$” is one of the **valid patterns** and gives “Some $A$ are $C$.” Choice A does not follow because employees who are not graduates might not have passed the exam. Choice B does not follow because all employees could be economics graduates. Choice C reverses the second premise. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

“All painters are artists. No sculptor is a painter. Some sculptors are artists.” Which statement is necessarily true?

- A) Some artists are not painters.
- B) Some sculptors are not artists.
- C) All artists are painters or sculptors.
- D) Some painters are sculptors.

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,b,bc,c" nomi="painter,artist,sculptor" elementi="bc:×" titolo="Painters belong to the set of artists and lie outside the set of sculptors. The sculptor who is an artist, marked $\times$, lies in the region for artists who are not painters."}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **three-circle diagram**, the first premise leaves the part of the painters' circle outside the artists' circle empty. The second leaves the regions common to painters and sculptors empty. The third places a $\times$ in the region common to sculptors and artists, outside the painters' circle. This element is an artist who is not a painter, so “Some artists are not painters” is necessarily true. Choice D contradicts the second premise. Choices B and C may be true, but neither is guaranteed. All sculptors might be artists, and artists such as musicians might be neither painters nor sculptors. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
