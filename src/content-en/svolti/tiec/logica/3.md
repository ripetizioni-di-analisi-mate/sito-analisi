## Exercise 1

Which statement is equivalent to “If a customer has a loyalty card, then they receive the discount”?

- A) If a customer receives the discount, then they have a loyalty card.
- B) If a customer does not have a loyalty card, then they do not receive the discount.
- C) If a customer does not receive the discount, then they do not have a loyalty card.
- D) A customer has a loyalty card and does not receive the discount.

$$
T \Rightarrow S \;\equiv\; \lnot S \Rightarrow \lnot T
$$

| $T$ | $S$ | $T \Rightarrow S$ | $\lnot S \Rightarrow \lnot T$ | $S \Rightarrow T$ | $\lnot T \Rightarrow \lnot S$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T | T | T | T | T | T |
| T | F | F | F | T | T |
| F | T | T | T | F | F |
| F | F | T | T | T | T |

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $T$ = “the customer has a loyalty card” and $S$ = “the customer receives the discount.” The statement is the implication $T \Rightarrow S$. By **equivalence with the contrapositive**, it is equivalent to $\lnot S \Rightarrow \lnot T$, or choice C. Indeed, if every customer with a card receives the discount, a customer who does not receive it cannot have a card. Choice A is the converse and B the inverse. In the second and third rows of the truth table, their columns differ from the column for $T \Rightarrow S$. Choice D is the negation of the given statement. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

“Having a high school diploma is a necessary condition for college enrollment.” Given this statement, which of the following is true?

- A) Anyone with a high school diploma is enrolled in college.
- B) Anyone who is not enrolled in college does not have a high school diploma.
- C) Having a high school diploma is sufficient for college enrollment.
- D) Anyone without a high school diploma is not enrolled in college.

$$
U \Rightarrow D \;\equiv\; \lnot D \Rightarrow \lnot U
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $U$ = “is enrolled in college” and $D$ = “has a high school diploma.” To say that $D$ is a **necessary condition** for $U$ means that without $D$, $U$ cannot hold, or $U \Rightarrow D$. By the contrapositive the statement is equivalent to $\lnot D \Rightarrow \lnot U$, so anyone without a diploma is not enrolled in college. Choices A and C treat a diploma as a *sufficient* condition and therefore read the statement as $D \Rightarrow U$, which it does not assert. Choice B is the inverse $\lnot U \Rightarrow \lnot D$. A high school graduate who works instead of going to college is a counterexample. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

“If it rains, the game is postponed” and “If the game is postponed, the tickets are refunded” are both true. The tickets were not refunded. Which conclusion is correct?

- A) It rained, but the game was not postponed.
- B) It did not rain.
- C) The game was postponed, but it did not rain.
- D) It cannot be determined whether it rained.

$$
P \Rightarrow R, \qquad R \Rightarrow B, \qquad \lnot B
$$
$$
\lnot B \;\Rightarrow\; \lnot R \;\Rightarrow\; \lnot P
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $P$ = “it rains,” $R$ = “the game is postponed,” and $B$ = “the tickets are refunded.” Since $R \Rightarrow B$ holds and $B$ is false, the rules for **valid inferences** imply that $R$ is false, so the game was not postponed. Since $P \Rightarrow R$ holds and $R$ is false, the same reasoning shows that $P$ is false, so it did not rain. Choice A contradicts the first statement, which guarantees postponement if it rains. Choice C contradicts the second, which guarantees refunds if the game is postponed. Choice D would be correct if we knew that tickets *were* refunded, because inferring rain from a refund affirms the consequent. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
