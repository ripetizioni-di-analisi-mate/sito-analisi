## Exercise 1

If $\triangle + \triangle + \square = 17$ and $\triangle + \square = 11$, what is $\square$?

- A) $5$
- B) $6$
- C) $11$
- D) $3$

$$
(\triangle + \triangle + \square) - (\triangle + \square) = 17 - 11
\iff
\triangle = 6
$$
$$
\square = 11 - 6 = 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two equations differ by just one $\triangle$, so subtracting the second from the first gives $\triangle = 17 - 11 = 6$. Substituting into the second gives $\square = 11 - 6 = 5$. As a check, $6 + 6 + 5 = 17$. Choice B is the value of $\triangle$, which answers a different question. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The equations $\triangle + \square = 12$, $\square + \bigcirc = 15$, and $\triangle + \bigcirc = 13$ hold. What is $\triangle \cdot \bigcirc$?

- A) $35$
- B) $56$
- C) $20$
- D) $40$

$$
2\,(\triangle + \square + \bigcirc) = 12 + 15 + 13 = 40
\iff
\triangle + \square + \bigcirc = 20
$$
$$
\bigcirc = 20 - 12 = 8,
\qquad
\triangle = 20 - 15 = 5,
\qquad
\square = 20 - 13 = 7
$$
$$
\triangle \cdot \bigcirc = 5 \cdot 8 = 40
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each symbol appears in two of the three equations, so **adding all three** gives twice the sum of the three symbols, or $\triangle + \square + \bigcirc = 20$. Subtract each equation from $20$ to obtain the symbol missing from it. This gives $\bigcirc = 8$, $\triangle = 5$, and $\square = 7$, so the requested product is $5 \cdot 8 = 40$. Choices A and B are the products $\triangle \cdot \square$ and $\square \cdot \bigcirc$, whereas choice C is the sum of the three symbols. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The letters $A$ and $B$ represent two digits, neither of which is $0$, with $A > B$. The two-digit number $AB$ added to $BA$ gives $121$, and the difference between its digits is $A - B = 3$. What is the number $AB$?

- A) $74$
- B) $47$
- C) $83$
- D) $65$

$$
AB + BA = (10A + B) + (10B + A) = 11\,(A + B) = 121
\iff
A + B = 11
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
A + B = 11 \\
A - B = 3
\end{cases}
\iff
A = 7, \quad B = 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In **place-value notation**, the two-digit number $AB$ is $10A + B$, so the sum $AB + BA$ equals $11\,(A + B)$. From $11\,(A + B) = 121$ it follows that $A + B = 11$. Both $A + B = 11$ and $A - B = 3$ must hold. Adding gives $2A = 14$, or $A = 7$, and hence $B = 4$. The number is therefore $74$, and indeed $74 + 47 = 121$. Choice B reverses the digits and fails to satisfy $A > B$. Choice D satisfies $A + B = 11$ but its digits differ by $1$. The digits in choice C differ by $5$. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
