## Exercise 1

The number $27^{\frac{2}{3}}$ is equal to:

- A) $18$
- B) $3$
- C) $9$
- D) $\dfrac{1}{9}$

$$
27^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left( \sqrt[3]{27} \right)^{2} = 3^{2} = 9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of a **power with a fractional exponent**, the denominator $3$ calls for the cube root, and the numerator $2$ calls for squaring. Since $3^3 = 27$, the cube root of $27$ is $3$, and its square is $9$. Choice A multiplies the base by the exponent, giving $27 \cdot \dfrac{2}{3} = 18$. Choice B stops at the cube root. Choice D treats the exponent as negative. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $a > 0$, the expression $\dfrac{\sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt[3]{a^{2}}}{a}$ is equal to:

- A) $\sqrt[5]{a^{3}}$
- B) $\sqrt[6]{a}$
- C) $\sqrt[3]{a}$
- D) $a^{\frac{7}{6}}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt[3]{a^{2}}}{a}
&= \frac{a^{\frac{1}{2}} \cdot a^{\frac{2}{3}}}{a^{1}} \\[6pt]
&= a^{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{3} - 1} \\[6pt]
&= a^{\frac{3 + 4 - 6}{6}} \\[6pt]
&= a^{\frac{1}{6}} = \sqrt[6]{a}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the roots as **powers with fractional exponents** so that all factors have base $a$. By the properties of powers, add the exponents in the numerator and subtract the exponent in the denominator. Using the common denominator $6$ gives an exponent of $\dfrac{3 + 4 - 6}{6} = \dfrac{1}{6}$. Choice A adds the numerators and denominators of the exponents separately, giving $\dfrac{1 + 2}{2 + 3}$. Choice C multiplies the exponents instead of adding them. Choice D forgets to divide by $a$. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The price of an item rose by a total of $21\%$ over two years, with the same percentage increase each year. The annual percentage increase was:

- A) $10.5\%$
- B) $11\%$
- C) $4.6\%$
- D) $10\%$

$$
(1 + r)^{2} = 1.21
\iff
1 + r = \sqrt{1.21} = 1.1
\iff
r = 0.1 = 10\%
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An annual growth rate of $r$ multiplies the price by $1 + r$ each year. Over two years the factor is $(1 + r)^2$, which must equal $1.21$. The annual factor is therefore the **square root** of $1.21$, or $1.1$, since $1.1^2 = 1.21$. The annual increase is thus $10\%$. Choice A divides $21\%$ by two, but successive percentage increases do not add. In fact, two increases of $10.5\%$ give $1.105^2 \approx 1.221$. Choice C gives $\sqrt{21} \approx 4.6$ by taking the square root of the percentage rather than the growth factor. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
