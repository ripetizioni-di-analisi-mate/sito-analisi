## Exercise 1

The value of $\log_{6} 3 + \log_{6} 12$ is:

- A) $\log_{6} 15$
- B) $2$
- C) $36$
- D) $\log_{6} 9$

$$
\log_{6} 3 + \log_{6} 12 = \log_{6} (3 \cdot 12) = \log_{6} 36 = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two logarithms have the same base. By the rule for the **logarithm of a product**, their sum is the logarithm of the product of their arguments. Since $3 \cdot 12 = 36 = 6^{2}$, the result is $2$. Choice A adds the arguments, but adding logarithms corresponds to multiplying their arguments. Choice C gives the product $36$, the argument, without taking its logarithm. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The value of the expression $\log_{2} 48 - \log_{2} 3 + \log_{2} \sqrt{2}$ is:

- A) $5$
- B) $\log_{2} 45 + \dfrac{1}{2}$
- C) $\dfrac{9}{2}$
- D) $\dfrac{33}{2}$

$$
\log_{2} 48 - \log_{2} 3 = \log_{2} \frac{48}{3} = \log_{2} 16 = 4
$$
$$
\log_{2} \sqrt{2} = \log_{2} 2^{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
4 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{9}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the rule for the **logarithm of a quotient**, the difference of the first two logarithms is $\log_2 \dfrac{48}{3} = \log_2 16 = 4$. To evaluate the third logarithm, write $\sqrt{2} = 2^{\frac{1}{2}}$, which gives $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The sum is $\dfrac{9}{2}$. Choice A assigns $\log_2 \sqrt{2}$ the value $1$. Choice B subtracts the arguments instead of dividing them. Choice D adds $16$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ without evaluating $\log_2 16$. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An investment earning $10\%$ annual compound interest doubles after $n$ years, where $n$ satisfies $1.1^{n} = 2$. The number $n$ is equal to:

- A) $\dfrac{\log_{10} 2}{\log_{10} 1.1}$
- B) $\log_{2} 1.1$
- C) $\log_{10} 2 - \log_{10} 1.1$
- D) $\dfrac{\log_{10} 2}{1.1}$

$$
1.1^{n} = 2
\iff
n = \log_{1.1} 2 = \frac{\log_{10} 2}{\log_{10} 1.1}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of a logarithm, the exponent $n$ that produces $1.1^{n} = 2$ is $\log_{1.1} 2$. The **change-of-base formula** expresses this logarithm in base $10$ as the logarithm of the argument $2$ divided by the logarithm of the base $1.1$. Its value is approximately $\dfrac{0.301}{0.041} \approx 7.3$ years. Choice B switches the base and argument. Choice C incorrectly applies the logarithm-of-a-quotient rule to a quotient of logarithms. Choice D divides by the base instead of its logarithm. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
