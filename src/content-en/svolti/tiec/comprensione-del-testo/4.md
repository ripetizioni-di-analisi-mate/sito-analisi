## Passage

> Music, movies, computer programs, even coffee and pet food: in recent years, a growing range of products has been sold by subscription. For businesses, the advantage is clear. A customer who pays every month provides regular income that is easier to predict than individual sales and allows the business to plan production with less waste.
>
> For consumers, the picture is more nuanced. A subscription saves them from making the same purchase repeatedly and often costs less than buying the product outright, at least for the first few months. The other side of the coin is that small monthly charges add up without anyone noticing. When asked how much they spend on subscriptions in total, many households give a figure well below the actual amount because they forget services they rarely use and that renew automatically.
>
> Some businesses see this oversight as an asset and deliberately make cancellation laborious, with forms to print, phone lines that are always busy, and options hidden at the bottom of pages. This strategy pays off in the short term but erodes customers’ trust. In fact, people who manage to cancel rarely come back.

## Exercise 1

In the passage, “the other side of the coin” means:

- A) the downside of a situation that also has advantages
- B) the savings in the first few months of a subscription
- C) the hidden part of a contract
- D) a sudden change in shopping habits

| option | sentence after substitution | does it keep its meaning? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | “The downside is that small charges add up” | yes |
| B | “The savings in the first few months are that small charges add up” | no |
| C | “The hidden part of the contract is that the charges add up” | no |
| D | “The sudden change is that the charges add up” | no |

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression is **figurative**. The preceding sentence lists advantages of subscriptions, while the sentence containing the expression gives a disadvantage. In the **substitution test**, option A produces a sentence with the same meaning: charges adding up are the downside of a convenient choice. Option B identifies the advantage described earlier, so it reverses the meaning. Option C appeals to readers who remember the “options hidden” in the third paragraph, but those concern cancellation, not the sentence in question. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the sentence “For consumers, the picture is more nuanced,” what does “more nuanced” mean?

- A) gone, lost
- B) more favorable than it is for businesses
- C) less clear-cut, with both advantages and disadvantages
- D) entirely negative

| option | comparison with the paragraph | assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | another sense of the Italian *sfumare*, as in “the deal fell through” | does not fit the context |
| B | the paragraph does not compare the advantages for consumers with those for businesses | does not fit |
| C | the paragraph lists advantages and then disadvantages | fits |
| D | the paragraph acknowledges advantages as well | too strong |

<div class="spiegazione">

The first paragraph says the advantage for businesses is clear, or unambiguous. The second presents both advantages and disadvantages for consumers, so “more nuanced” describes a less clear-cut picture with positive and negative aspects. This is the **meaning in context** given by option C. Option A gives a dictionary meaning of the original Italian *sfumare*, but it makes no sense in this sentence. Option D overlooks the advantages listed immediately afterward: savings and convenience. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the third paragraph, “this oversight” refers to:

- A) the effort of making the same purchase repeatedly
- B) the fact that many consumers fail to notice how much they spend on subscriptions
- C) the difficulty of canceling a subscription
- D) the habit of using some services only rarely

| option | what it identifies in the passage | is it an oversight by consumers? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | what the subscription avoids | no |
| B | charges adding up “without anyone noticing” | yes |
| C | what businesses do *afterward* to exploit the oversight | no |
| D | the reason some services are forgotten | no |

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression “this oversight” **refers back** to a fact described immediately before it. The end of the second paragraph says that charges add up unnoticed and households underestimate the total. The oversight, then, is failing to notice how much is spent, as option B says. Option C describes what businesses do to take advantage of the oversight, a consequence. Option D comes close, but rarely using a service is the reason it is forgotten, while the oversight is failing to notice the expense. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
