## Passage

> A midsize city surveyed residents about how they get to work, asking the same questions five years apart. In the first survey, $55\%$ of respondents commuted by car, $25\%$ by public transportation, $12\%$ by bike, and $8\%$ on foot. In the second survey the share commuting by car fell to $46\%$, the share using public transportation rose to $28\%$, and the share cycling rose to $18\%$. The share walking to work stayed the same.
>
> The city administration attributes the decline in car use to two measures: thirty kilometers of new bike lanes and a discounted transit pass for workers. The survey authors, however, urge caution. Fuel prices rose over the same period, and some residents began working from home several days a week. Moreover, the questions concerned the mode of transportation used most often, not the number of trips.
>
> The survey also shows differences by age. Among residents under thirty, the share cycling to work rose from $15\%$ to $30\%$, while among those over fifty it stayed around $6\%$.

## Exercise 1

According to the figures in the passage, by how much did the share of people commuting by car fall between the two surveys?

- A) By $9\%$
- B) By $46$ percentage points
- C) By $3$ percentage points
- D) By $9$ percentage points

$$
55\% - 46\% = 9 \text{ percentage points}
$$

$$
\frac{55 - 46}{55} \cdot 100 \approx 16.4\%
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The shares are $55\%$ and $46\%$, so the difference is $9$ **percentage points**, as option D says. Option A confuses percentage points with the percent change, which is about $16\%$ of the initial value. Thus “by $9\%$” is wrong. Option B gives the final value instead of the difference. Option C gives the increase in public transportation use, from $25\%$ to $28\%$, and may attract readers who think all the former drivers switched to public transportation. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

According to the passage, between the first and second surveys the share of people commuting by bike:

- A) doubled
- B) increased by $50\%$
- C) increased by $18$ percentage points
- D) increased more than the share of residents under thirty who cycle to work

$$
18\% - 12\% = 6 \text{ percentage points}
$$

$$
\frac{18 - 12}{12} \cdot 100 = \frac{6}{12} \cdot 100 = 50\%
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The share rose from $12\%$ to $18\%$, an increase of $6$ percentage points. The **percent change** is the increase divided by the initial value, expressed as a percentage, so it is $\dfrac{6}{12} \cdot 100 = 50\%$, as option B says. Option A applies to residents under thirty, whose share rose from $15\%$ to $30\%$ and therefore doubled. Option C confuses the final value with the increase. Option D is wrong because the increase among younger residents was $15$ points, more than the $6$ points for all residents. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which of the following statements is supported by the passage?

- A) Bike lanes are the main cause of the decline in car use
- B) Cycling has doubled among residents over fifty
- C) According to the survey authors, factors other than the city’s measures may also explain the decline in car use
- D) The number of car trips fell by $9\%$

| option | line from the passage | assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | “The city administration attributes…” but the authors “urge caution” | unsupported |
| B | “it stayed around $6\%$” | contradicted |
| C | fuel prices and working from home “over the same period” | supported |
| D | the questions concerned “not the number of trips” | unsupported |

<div class="spiegazione">

The survey authors urge caution and mention fuel prices and working from home, two developments during the same period separate from the city’s measures. Option C therefore reports their view. Option A presents as certain the administration’s explanation that the authors question. Two facts occurring at the same time are not necessarily **cause and effect**. Option D is wrong for two reasons: the survey measures the mode used most often rather than the number of trips, and $9$ is the change in the share in percentage points. Option B contradicts the figures for residents over fifty. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
