## Passage

> In just a few years, paying by card or phone has become the norm in neighborhood stores, even for small purchases such as a coffee or a newspaper. The benefits for retailers are tangible. They keep less cash in the till, so they face less risk of theft and spend less time counting coins at the end of the day. In addition, payments are recorded automatically, which simplifies bookkeeping.
>
> Every electronic payment, however, has a cost. The retailer pays the service provider a fee equal to a small percentage of the transaction. The fee can weigh more heavily on a coffee than on a hundred-euro purchase because some contracts also include a fixed charge per transaction. For this reason some retailers have sought contracts with lower fees for small purchases, and several providers now offer them.
>
> Customers have not all made the same shift to electronic payments. Younger people almost always pay by phone. Many older people still prefer cash, both out of habit and because they want to keep close track of their spending. Some retailers worry that if cash ever disappeared, some older customers would feel excluded, so they continue to accept both forms of payment.

## Exercise 1

Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the passage?

- A) Fees are higher at neighborhood stores than at large stores
- B) Electronic payment simplifies bookkeeping for retailers
- C) Some contracts include a fixed charge per transaction
- D) Many older people prefer to pay in cash

| option | line from the passage |
| --- | --- |
| A | none: the passage does not mention large stores |
| B | “payments are recorded automatically, which simplifies bookkeeping” |
| C | “some contracts also include a fixed charge per transaction” |
| D | “Many older people still prefer cash” |

<div class="spiegazione">

The question is negative, so look for the **supporting line** for each option and choose the only one without one. Options B, C, and D restate sentences from the passage. Option A compares fees at neighborhood stores with those at large stores, yet the passage never mentions large stores. It is therefore unsupported. It is a *true but unstated* distractor that may seem plausible to readers who expect a small store to get worse terms. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

According to the passage, why can the fee weigh more heavily on a coffee than on a hundred-euro purchase?

- A) Because the percentage charged on coffee is higher
- B) Because young people pay for coffee by phone
- C) Because some contracts include a fixed charge per transaction that weighs more heavily on a small purchase
- D) Because providers charge lower fees on small purchases

| option | comparison with the passage | assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | the passage does not say the percentage changes with the product | not stated |
| B | the third paragraph mentions young people in a different context | irrelevant |
| C | “because some contracts also include a fixed charge” | explicit |
| D | lower fees are the *response* to the problem | reversed |

<div class="spiegazione">

The passage introduces the cause with *because* and says some contracts include a fixed charge per transaction. The fixed charge is the same for a coffee and a hundred-euro purchase, so it accounts for a larger proportion of the smaller amount, as option C says. Option D is a *reversed* distractor because lower fees are the solution some retailers sought, not the cause of the problem. Option A invents a different percentage for coffee, which the passage does not mention. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which statement best summarizes the last paragraph?

- A) Cash will soon disappear from neighborhood stores
- B) Payment habits vary by age, and some retailers still accept cash to avoid excluding older customers
- C) Older people do not know how to pay by phone
- D) Retailers prefer cash to avoid fees

| option | comparison with the last paragraph | type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | “if cash ever disappeared” is hypothetical | exaggerated |
| B | differences between younger and older customers, and retailers accepting both forms of payment | correct |
| C | the reasons given are habit and keeping track of spending | true but unstated |
| D | the reason given is to avoid excluding older customers | not stated |

<div class="spiegazione">

The last paragraph makes two points: younger and older customers pay differently, and some retailers continue to take cash so that older customers are not excluded. Option B covers both and therefore summarizes the paragraph. Option A turns a possibility introduced by “if cash ever disappeared” into a certain prediction, so it is *exaggerated*. Option C attributes an inability to older people that the passage does not mention. The reasons given are habit and the desire to monitor spending. Option D substitutes a different reason for the retailers’ decision, one the text does not give. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
