## Passage

> Companies trying a four-day workweek without reducing pay are often portrayed as generous businesses willing to give up some output for their employees’ well-being. That description is wrong. Reducing hours can benefit the business itself, provided the work is reorganized.
>
> Having one less day forces a company to eliminate unnecessary activities such as overlong meetings, duplicated procedures, and messages that constantly interrupt work. At a software company that adopted the shorter workweek, the number of projects delivered on time held steady, while the number of voluntary resignations nearly halved. Replacing an employee who leaves costs a business months of recruitment and training, so fewer resignations represent real savings.
>
> One objection is that the model does not work where service must be available every day, as in stores or hospitals. The objection is only partly justified. In such cases, each employee’s four days can be scheduled in shifts so the service remains open six days out of seven. Anyone who sees the shorter week as a gift to workers counts only the hours lost and overlooks those wasted.

## Exercise 1

What thesis does the author defend?

- A) Companies that adopt a shorter workweek are generous to employees
- B) A shorter workweek cannot work in stores or hospitals
- C) With work reorganized, a shorter workweek can benefit the business
- D) Employees prefer to work four days a week

| statement or option | role in the passage |
| --- | --- |
| businesses are generous (A) | description the author calls wrong |
| the model does not work in stores or hospitals (B) | objection |
| reducing hours can benefit the business (C) | thesis |
| employee preferences (D) | not mentioned |

<div class="spiegazione">

The **thesis** is the statement the others help support. The figures from the software company and the reasoning about the cost of resignations show that reducing hours can benefit a business. The thesis is therefore option C, which appears at the end of the first paragraph. Option A repeats a description that the author immediately calls wrong, so it is a *reversed* distractor. Option B is the objection in the third paragraph, which the author accepts only in part. Option D is plausible, but the passage does not discuss employee preferences. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the passage, the figures on resignations at the software company serve to:

- A) support the claim that a shorter workweek can save a business money
- B) prove that the shorter workweek works for every company
- C) report an objection to the shorter workweek
- D) show that projects were delivered late

| element | content | function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| finding | voluntary resignations nearly halved | concrete example |
| next sentence | replacing an employee is costly, so fewer resignations mean savings | connects the finding to the thesis |

<div class="spiegazione">

The figures on resignations are followed by a sentence linking them to the thesis: replacing an employee costs months of recruitment and training, so fewer resignations mean savings. The figures are an **example** supporting the claim that a shorter workweek can save a business money, as option A says. Option B is *exaggerated*: one company cannot establish a result for every company, and the author specifies that work must be reorganized. Option D contradicts the passage, which states that the number of projects delivered on time stayed the same. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

How does the author respond to the objection about stores and hospitals?

- A) The author rejects it entirely because the shorter workweek works without changes in those sectors
- B) The author accepts it and concludes that the shorter workweek should be limited to offices
- C) The author does not consider it
- D) The author accepts it in part and proposes scheduling the four days in shifts

| option | wording in the passage | assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | “only partly justified,” and shifts are needed | contradicted |
| B | shifts allow the service to stay open | contradicted |
| C | the third paragraph discusses the objection | contradicted |
| D | “only partly justified … can be scheduled in shifts” | explicit |

<div class="spiegazione">

The author calls the objection “only partly justified” and thus accepts it in part. The next sentence gives a solution: shifts that keep the service open six days out of seven, as option D says. Option A is wrong because the author acknowledges a problem and proposes a change, the use of shifts. Option B takes the objection further than the author, who does not limit the shorter workweek to offices. An entire paragraph is devoted to the objection, which rules out C. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
