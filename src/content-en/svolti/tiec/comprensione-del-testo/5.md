## Passage

> During the hottest summers, temperatures in a city center can be several degrees higher than in the nearby countryside. In fact, asphalt and concrete absorb heat during the day and release it at night, while cars and air conditioners add more heat to the air. Known as the urban heat island, this phenomenon makes summer nights especially difficult for older people and for those who live in small, poorly ventilated apartments.
>
> Trees are among the most widely studied remedies. Their shade keeps the sun from heating surfaces. Moreover, their leaves release water vapor, which removes heat from the air. For this reason, many local governments have launched plans to plant thousands of trees along avenues and in schoolyards.
>
> Planting a tree, however, is not enough. A young tree needs water and care for its first few years, and many urban trees die before they grow large enough to provide shade. Some towns have therefore decided to plant fewer trees and entrust their care to specialist firms for at least three years. Although this choice reduces the number of trees announced, its supporters believe it will produce more shade in the long run.

## Exercise 1

In the first paragraph, “in fact” introduces:

- A) a consequence of the rise in temperature
- B) an explanation for the temperature difference between the city center and the countryside
- C) an objection to the preceding sentence
- D) an example of the people most affected by the heat

| sentence | content | relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| first | the city center is hotter than the countryside | fact |
| sentence with *in fact* | asphalt, concrete, cars, and air conditioners produce or retain heat | reason for the fact |

<div class="spiegazione">

The connective *in fact* introduces an **explanation**, giving the reason for what was just stated. The first sentence says it is hotter in the city center than in the countryside. The sentence with *in fact* explains why by naming asphalt, concrete, cars, and air conditioners. The sentence introduced by *in fact* therefore explains the temperature difference. Option A reverses the relationship because the heat retained by asphalt is a cause of the temperature increase rather than a consequence. Option D concerns the next sentence, which mentions older people. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which connective can replace “however” in “Planting a tree, however, is not enough” without changing the meaning?

- A) therefore
- B) in fact
- C) moreover
- D) nevertheless

| connective | relationship | does it fit? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| therefore | consequence | no |
| in fact | explanation | no |
| moreover | addition | no |
| nevertheless | contrast | yes |

<div class="spiegazione">

The second paragraph presents trees as a remedy, while the sentence with *however* goes in the other direction by saying that planting them is insufficient. The relationship is one of **contrast**, and *nevertheless* is the only connective of contrast among the options. With *therefore*, the sentence would present the limitation as a consequence of the benefits of trees. *In fact* and *moreover* would link it to the preceding paragraph as an explanation or a further point of the same kind. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the final sentence of the passage, the clause introduced by “Although” expresses:

- A) a fact that would lead one to expect a conclusion opposite to the supporters’ conclusion
- B) the reason the choice produces more shade in the long run
- C) an additional piece of information unrelated to the main clause
- D) a necessary condition for trees to grow

| clause | content | what it leads one to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| clause with *although* | fewer trees are planted | less shade |
| main clause | more shade in the long run | the opposite of the expectation |

<div class="spiegazione">

The connective *although*, followed in the Italian original by the subjunctive “riduca” (“reduces”), introduces a **concession**. Planting fewer trees would suggest less shade, yet supporters expect more. The clause with *although* therefore states a fact that would lead one to expect the opposite of the main clause’s conclusion, as option A says. Option B reads the concession as a cause, but the prospect of more shade is explained by specialist care, not by the smaller number of trees. Option D attributes a conditional relationship to the sentence that none of its connectives expresses. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>
