## Passage

> Until a few years ago, the neighborhood library was mainly a place to borrow books: you went in, chose a book, and left. Today, in many cities, people visit their neighborhood library for different reasons. University students spend entire days there because they have no quiet place at home. Older residents read the newspapers there. Families bring children to Saturday morning story sessions, and some adults take free classes on how to use public services online.
>
> This change affects how libraries are organized. Reading rooms designed for a few people sitting in silence must now accommodate those who study, those who talk quietly, and those attending a meeting. Many libraries have therefore divided up the space, with a quiet study room, a children’s area, and a classroom. Hours have changed too, because students want to stay into the evening and many people who work can come only after 6 p.m.
>
> Meanwhile, book borrowing has not declined. It has even increased in several libraries because people who come to study or attend a class often end up taking a novel home. The neighborhood library has thus become a meeting place where books remain central alongside many other activities.

## Exercise 1

What is the main idea of the passage?

- A) Libraries should stay open into the evening
- B) University students have nowhere to study at home
- C) The neighborhood library now serves many purposes and continues to lend books
- D) Book borrowing has increased in every library

| paragraph | one-sentence summary |
| --- | --- |
| first | people visit the library for many reasons besides borrowing books |
| second | the spaces and hours have been reorganized for the new activities |
| third | borrowing has not declined, and the library is a meeting place where books remain central |

<div class="spiegazione">

The **main idea** is the statement that encompasses the summaries of all the paragraphs. The three paragraphs discuss the library’s new functions, the reorganization they required, and the continued strength of book borrowing. Option C covers all three. Option B is a detail from the first paragraph, so it is *too narrow*. Option D is *exaggerated*: the passage says borrowing rose “in several libraries,” not in all of them. Option A presents as the author’s proposal a request the passage attributes to students. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which title best fits the passage?

- A) From lending books to bringing people together: the changing neighborhood library
- B) Reading in Italy
- C) Saturday morning story sessions for children
- D) Why people borrow fewer and fewer books from libraries

| title | assessment |
| --- | --- |
| A | covers the whole passage |
| B | too broad |
| C | too narrow |
| D | reversed: the passage says borrowing has not declined |

<div class="spiegazione">

The **best title** must cover the whole passage and only the passage. Title A describes the change from a place for borrowing books to a meeting place, the subject of the entire text. Title B names a much broader topic because the passage does not discuss reading in general. Title C picks out an activity mentioned in a single line of the first paragraph. Title D contradicts the third paragraph, which says borrowing has not declined. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What function does the last paragraph serve in the passage?

- A) It reports an objection to the changes in libraries
- B) It proposes a solution to problems with opening hours
- C) It describes the new arrangement of the rooms
- D) It shows that the new activities have not reduced borrowing and draws a conclusion

| paragraph | content | function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| first | new users and new activities | introduces the change |
| second | separate spaces and longer hours | describes its consequences |
| third | stable or rising borrowing, a sentence containing *thus* | completes the picture and draws a conclusion |

<div class="spiegazione">

The last paragraph reports that borrowing has not declined, then sums up the passage in the sentence containing *thus*. The paragraph therefore shows that lending remains an important activity and draws a conclusion, as option D says. Option C describes the second paragraph, which deals with space and hours. Option A is wrong because no one in the passage objects to the changes. Option B attributes a proposal to the passage that it does not make. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
