## Exercise 1

What is the least common multiple of $36$ and $60$?

- A) $12$
- B) $2160$
- C) $180$
- D) $360$

$$
36 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2,
\qquad
60 = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\mathrm{mcm}(36, 60) = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 180
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First **factor both numbers into primes**. The **LCM** is the product of all the prime factors that appear, each raised to the larger exponent, so $2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 180$. As a check, $\mathrm{MCD}(36, 60) = 2^2 \cdot 3 = 12$ and $12 \cdot 180 = 2160 = 36 \cdot 60$. Choice A gives the GCD. Choice B is the product of the numbers, a common multiple but not the least one. Choice D uses the exponent $3$ for the factor $2$, although that exponent for this factor occurs in neither factorization. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A store receives a coffee delivery every $15$ days and a sugar delivery every $20$ days. Both deliveries arrived today. How long will it be before they next arrive on the same day?

- A) $5$ days
- B) $300$ days
- C) $35$ days
- D) $60$ days

$$
15 = 3 \cdot 5,
\qquad
20 = 2^2 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\mathrm{mcm}(15, 20) = 2^2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 60
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Counting from today, coffee deliveries occur at multiples of $15$ days and sugar deliveries at multiples of $20$ days. Both arrive on days corresponding to common multiples. The first such day is the **least common multiple**, $60$. As a check, $60 = 4 \cdot 15 = 3 \cdot 20$. Choice A is the GCD. Choice B is the product $15 \cdot 20$, a day when both deliveries arrive but not the first such day. Choice C is the sum of the time intervals. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A store owner has $168$ pens and $120$ pencils and wants to make as many identical packages as possible. Each package must contain the same number of pens and the same number of pencils as every other package, with nothing left over. How many pens will each package contain?

- A) $24$
- B) $7$
- C) $5$
- D) $14$

$$
168 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 7,
\qquad
120 = 2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5
$$
$$
\mathrm{MCD}(168, 120) = 2^3 \cdot 3 = 24,
\qquad
\frac{168}{24} = 7,
\qquad
\frac{120}{24} = 5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number of packages must divide both $168$ and $120$ because nothing can be left over. It must also be as large as possible, so it is the **greatest common divisor**, $24$. Each package therefore contains $\dfrac{168}{24} = 7$ pens and $\dfrac{120}{24} = 5$ pencils. Choice A is the number of packages instead of the number of pens per package. Choice C is the number of pencils per package. Choice D is the number of pens per package if there are $12$ packages, a possible arrangement that does not give the greatest number of packages. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
