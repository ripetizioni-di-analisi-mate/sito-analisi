## Exercise 1

Which of the following points lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$?

- A) $(3;-4)$
- B) $(5;5)$
- C) $(1;4)$
- D) $(0;25)$

$$
3^2 + (-4)^2 = 9 + 16 = 25
$$
$$
5^2 + 5^2 = 50,
\qquad
1^2 + 4^2 = 17,
\qquad
0^2 + 25^2 = 625
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A point lies on a circle if its coordinates satisfy its equation. Only $(3;-4)$ gives $25$, so only that point lies on the circle, which is centered at the origin and has radius $5$. The coordinates of $(1;4)$ add up to $5$, but the equation uses the sum of the squares. The point $(0;25)$ confuses the radius with its square. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$, which statement describes the line $3x + 4y - 5 = 0$?

- A) It is a secant.
- B) It lies outside the circle.
- C) It is tangent.
- D) It passes through the center.

$$
d = \frac{|3 \cdot 0 + 4 \cdot 0 - 5|}{\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}} = \frac{5}{5} = 1 = r
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circle is centered at the origin and has radius $1$. The distance from its center to the line is $1$, equal to the radius. The criterion for the **position of a line relative to a circle** therefore says that the line is tangent. Choice D can be ruled out immediately because the coordinates $(0;0)$ give $-5 \neq 0$. Comparing the constant term $5$ with the radius $1$ would incorrectly place the line outside the circle, as in choice B. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On a Cartesian coordinate plane with distances measured in kilometers, the free delivery area of a warehouse at the origin is the disk of radius $5$ km. A straight road has equation $x + y - 6 = 0$. Which of the following statements is true?

- A) The road lies outside the delivery area because $6 > 5$.
- B) The road has exactly one point in common with the boundary of the delivery area.
- C) The road passes through the warehouse.
- D) The road crosses the delivery area because its distance from the warehouse is less than $5$ km.

$$
d = \frac{|0 + 0 - 6|}{\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2}} = \frac{6}{\sqrt{2}} = 3\sqrt{2} \approx 4.24
$$
$$
4.24 < 5
$$

::grafico{curve="5*cos(t),5*sin(t),0,6.29" f="6-x" punti="0,0,pieno,O;3,3,pieno,H" segmenti="0,0,3,3" legenda="x + y - 6 = 0" xmin=-6 xmax=7 ymin=-6 ymax=7 uguale=1 nomi="km,km" titolo="The road $x + y - 6 = 0$ is $3\sqrt{2} \approx 4.24$ km from the warehouse $O$, less than the radius $5$, so it crosses the delivery area"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The road crosses the delivery area if the distance from the warehouse to the line is less than the radius. The formula for the distance from a point to a line gives $3\sqrt{2}$, or about $4.24$ km, so the line is a **secant**. The point on the road closest to the warehouse is $H(3;3)$, and indeed $\sqrt{3^2 + 3^2} = 3\sqrt{2}$. Choice A compares the constant term $6$ with the radius, although the constant term does not give the distance. Choice C is false because $(0;0)$ gives $-6 \neq 0$. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>
