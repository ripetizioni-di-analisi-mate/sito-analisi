## Exercise 1

The hypotenuse of a right triangle is $10$ cm and an acute angle is $30^\circ$. How long is the leg adjacent to this angle?

- A) $5$ cm
- B) $5\sqrt{3}$ cm
- C) $10\sqrt{3}$ cm
- D) $\dfrac{10\sqrt{3}}{3}$ cm

$$
10 \cdot \cos 30^\circ = 10 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When **solving a right triangle**, the leg adjacent to an angle equals the hypotenuse times the cosine of the angle. Since $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, the leg is $5\sqrt{3}$ cm long, about $8.7$ cm, which is shorter than the hypotenuse as required. Choice A gives the opposite leg, found using the sine. Choice C exceeds the hypotenuse and can be ruled out immediately. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A loading ramp must rise $1.2$ m at an angle of $30^\circ$ to the floor. The ramp costs $150$ euros per meter of length. What is its cost?

- A) $90$ euros
- B) $180\sqrt{3}$ euros
- C) $60\sqrt{3}$ euros
- D) $360$ euros

$$
1.2 = \ell \cdot \sin 30^\circ
\iff
\ell = \frac{1.2}{\frac{1}{2}} = 2.4
$$
$$
2.4 \cdot 150 = 360
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 2.078,0 2.078,1.2" etichette="1,-0.2,floor;2.4,0.6,1.2 m;0.85,0.85,ℓ;0.4,0.08,30°" xmin=-0.3 xmax=2.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=1.6 titolo="The ramp is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with a vertical leg of $1.2$ m opposite the $30^\circ$ angle"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The ramp is the hypotenuse of a right triangle, and the rise is the leg opposite the angle of $30^\circ$. When **solving a right triangle**, the opposite leg equals the hypotenuse times the sine, so the length of the ramp is $\dfrac{1.2}{\sin 30^\circ} = 2.4$ m. Its cost is therefore $2.4 \cdot 150 = 360$ euros. Choice A multiplies the rise by the sine instead of dividing by it, choice B uses the horizontal projection of $1.2\sqrt{3}$ m, and choice C multiplies the rise by the tangent. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The legs of a right triangle have lengths $6$ and $8$. If $\alpha$ is the acute angle opposite the leg of length $6$, what is $\cos\alpha$?

- A) $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- B) $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- C) $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- D) $\dfrac{4}{3}$

$$
i = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10
$$
$$
\cos\alpha = \frac{8}{10} = \frac{4}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the Pythagorean theorem, the hypotenuse has length $10$. The leg adjacent to $\alpha$ has length $8$. The formulas for **solving a right triangle** give this length as $10\cos\alpha$, so $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{4}{5}$. Choice A gives $\sin\alpha$, choice B gives $\tan\alpha$, and choice D gives the reciprocal of the tangent. This value is greater than $1$, so it cannot be a cosine. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>
