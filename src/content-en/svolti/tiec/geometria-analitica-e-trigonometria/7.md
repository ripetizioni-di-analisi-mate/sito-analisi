## Exercise 1

On a pie chart showing the market shares in an industry, what is the central angle of the sector for a company with $15\%$ of the market?

- A) $\dfrac{3\pi}{20}$
- B) $\dfrac{\pi}{12}$
- C) $\dfrac{3\pi}{5}$
- D) $\dfrac{3\pi}{10}$

$$
\frac{15}{100} \cdot 360^\circ = 54^\circ
$$
$$
54^\circ = \frac{54\,\pi}{180} = \frac{3\pi}{10}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The full pie is one full turn, or $360^\circ$, which equals $2\pi$ radians, so $15\%$ corresponds to $54^\circ$. To convert to **radians**, multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$, giving $\dfrac{3\pi}{10}$. The same result follows from $\dfrac{15}{100} \cdot 2\pi$. Choice A takes $15\%$ of $\pi$ instead of $2\pi$. Choice B gives the radian measure of $15^\circ$ by confusing $15\%$ with $15^\circ$. The correct answer is **D**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $\sin x = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $90^\circ < x < 180^\circ$, what is $\cos x$?

- A) $-\dfrac{4}{5}$
- B) $\dfrac{4}{5}$
- C) $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- D) $-\dfrac{16}{25}$

$$
\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x = 1 - \frac{9}{25} = \frac{16}{25}
$$
$$
\cos x = -\sqrt{\frac{16}{25}} = -\frac{4}{5}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" punti="-0.8,0.6,pieno,P" vettori="0,0,-0.8,0.6" segmenti="-0.8,0,-0.8,0.6" etichette="-0.4,-0.15,cos x;-1.12,0.3,sin x" legenda="0" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 uguale=1 titolo="In the second quadrant, the point $P$ has vertical coordinate $\sin x = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and negative horizontal coordinate $\cos x = -\dfrac{4}{5}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **Pythagorean identity** $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$, the square of the cosine is $\dfrac{16}{25}$, so $\cos x = \pm\dfrac{4}{5}$. The angle is in the second quadrant, where the cosine is negative, so $\cos x = -\dfrac{4}{5}$. Choice B has the wrong sign, choice C calculates $1 - \sin x$, and choice D omits the square root. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the value of $2\sin^2\dfrac{\pi}{3} - \tan\dfrac{\pi}{4} + \cos\pi$?

- A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- B) $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- C) $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- D) $-\dfrac{3}{2}$

$$
\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},
\qquad
\tan\frac{\pi}{4} = 1,
\qquad
\cos\pi = -1
$$
$$
2 \cdot \frac{3}{4} - 1 - 1 = \frac{3}{2} - 2 = -\frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substitute the **special angle values**. The angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is $60^\circ$, with sine $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, and $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is $45^\circ$, with tangent $1$. The angle $\pi$ corresponds to the point $(-1;0)$ on the unit circle, so $\cos\pi = -1$. The expression therefore equals $\dfrac{3}{2} - 1 - 1 = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Choice C results from using $\cos\pi = 1$, choice A from $\cos\pi = 0$, and choice D from interchanging $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
