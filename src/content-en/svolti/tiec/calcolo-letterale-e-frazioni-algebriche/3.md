## Exercise 1

What is the quotient when $x^3 - 5x^2 + 2x + 8$ is divided by $x - 2$?

- A) $x^2 - 3x - 4$
- B) $x^2 - 7x + 16$
- C) $x^3 - 3x^2 - 4x$
- D) $x^2 - 3x + 4$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc|c}
 & 1 & -5 & 2 & 8 \\
2 & & 2 & -6 & -8 \\ \hline
 & 1 & -3 & -4 & 0
\end{array}
$$
$$
Q(x) = x^2 - 3x - 4,
\qquad
R = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisor is $x - 2$, so write $2$ on the left for **synthetic division**. Bring down $1$. The next steps give $1 \cdot 2 = 2$ and $-5 + 2 = -3$, then $-3 \cdot 2 = -6$ and $2 - 6 = -4$, and finally $-4 \cdot 2 = -8$ and $8 - 8 = 0$. The degree of the quotient is one less than the degree of the dividend, so the quotient is $x^2 - 3x - 4$ and the remainder is zero. Choice B writes $-2$ on the left. Choice C overlooks the drop in degree. The correct answer is **A**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The total cost of a shipment is $(2x^2 + 5x - 3)$ euros and is split equally among $(x + 3)$ lots, where $x$ is a positive integer. What is the cost of each lot in euros?

- A) $2x + 11$
- B) $x - 1$
- C) $2x - 1$
- D) $2x + 1$

$$
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
 & 2 & 5 & -3 \\
-3 & & -6 & 3 \\ \hline
 & 2 & -1 & 0
\end{array}
$$
$$
\frac{2x^2 + 5x - 3}{x + 3} = 2x - 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cost per lot is the quotient of the total cost divided by $x + 3$. Since $x + 3 = x - (-3)$, write $-3$ on the left for **synthetic division**. The remainder is $0$, so the division is exact and each lot costs $2x - 1$ euros. As a check, with $x = 2$ the total cost is $8 + 10 - 3 = 15$ euros for $5$ lots, and each costs $3 = 2 \cdot 2 - 1$ euros. Choice A writes $+3$ on the left, and choice B omits the leading coefficient $2$. The correct answer is **C**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the remainder when $2x^3 + x^2 - 4x + 3$ is divided by $x^2 + 1$?

- A) $2x + 1$
- B) $-6x + 2$
- C) $-2x + 2$
- D) $-6x + 4$

$$
\frac{2x^3}{x^2} = 2x,
\qquad
2x \cdot (x^2 + 1) = 2x^3 + 2x
$$
$$
(2x^3 + x^2 - 4x + 3) - (2x^3 + 2x) = x^2 - 6x + 3
$$
$$
\frac{x^2}{x^2} = 1,
\qquad
(x^2 - 6x + 3) - (x^2 + 1) = -6x + 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisor has degree $2$, so the usual synthetic division rule does not apply. Use **polynomial long division**. The first term of the quotient is $2x$. Subtracting $2x(x^2 + 1)$ leaves $x^2 - 6x + 3$. The second term is $1$. Subtracting $x^2 + 1$ leaves $-6x + 2$, which has degree less than $2$ and is therefore the remainder. Thus $2x^3 + x^2 - 4x + 3 = (x^2 + 1)(2x + 1) - 6x + 2$. Choice A gives the quotient. Choice C uses the wrong sign when subtracting $2x$. The correct answer is **B**.

</div>
