## Exercise 1

Given $\vec A=(2,-3,6)$, calculate the unit vector in the direction of $\vec A$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec A|&=\sqrt{2^2+(-3)^2+6^2}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{4+9+36}=\sqrt{49}=7\\[6pt]
\widehat A&=\frac{\vec A}{|\vec A|}
=\left(\frac27,-\frac37,\frac67\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
|\widehat A|^2=\frac{4}{49}+\frac{9}{49}+\frac{36}{49}=\frac{49}{49}=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unit vector in the direction of $\vec A$ is $\dfrac{\vec A}{|\vec A|}$. The division is possible because $\vec A$ is nonzero, since its components are not all zero.

Calculate the magnitude using the Pythagorean theorem in three dimensions by adding the squares of the three components. The square of $-3$ is $9$, so the sum is $4+9+36=49$ and the magnitude is $7$.

Next divide each component by $7$. The divisor is positive, so every component keeps its sign. The unit vector therefore points in the same direction as $\vec A$. In particular, its $y$ component remains negative, like that of $\vec A$.

Checking the magnitude confirms the result. The sum of the squares of the unit vector's components is $\dfrac{49}{49}=1$, so its magnitude is $1$. The components are dimensionless and are the cosines of the angles that $\vec A$ makes with the three axes.

The required unit vector is $\widehat A=\left(\dfrac27,-\dfrac37,\dfrac67\right)$.

</div>


## Exercise 2

Calculate the scalar projection of $\vec A=(3,1)$ in the direction of $\vec B=(-4,3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec B|&=\sqrt{(-4)^2+3^2}=\sqrt{25}=5\\[6pt]
\widehat B&=\left(-\frac45,\frac35\right)\\[6pt]
\vec A\cdot\widehat B
&=3\cdot\left(-\frac45\right)+1\cdot\frac35\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{12}{5}+\frac35=-\frac95.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **scalar projection** of $\vec A$ in the direction of $\vec B$ is the dot product of $\vec A$ with the unit vector $\widehat B$. This unit vector exists because $\vec B$ is nonzero.

The magnitude of $\vec B$ is $5$, so divide both components of $\vec B$ by $5$ to find the unit vector. Its dot product with $\vec A$ has terms $3\cdot\left(-\dfrac45\right)=-\dfrac{12}{5}$ and $1\cdot\dfrac35=\dfrac35$. Their sum is $-\dfrac95$.

The same number follows by first calculating $\vec A\cdot\vec B=-12+3=-9$ and then dividing by $|\vec B|=5$. The two methods agree because $\widehat B$ is $\vec B$ divided by its magnitude.

The negative sign indicates that the component of $\vec A$ along the line of $\vec B$ points opposite to $\vec B$. Consequently, the angle between the vectors is obtuse. The corresponding vector projection is $-\dfrac95\,\widehat B=\left(\dfrac{36}{25},-\dfrac{27}{25}\right)$.

The required scalar projection is $-\dfrac95$.

</div>


## Exercise 3

Resolve $\vec A=(5,0)$ into components parallel and perpendicular to $\vec B=(3,4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec B|&=\sqrt{9+16}=5,
\qquad
\widehat B=\left(\frac35,\frac45\right)\\[6pt]
\vec A\cdot\widehat B&=5\cdot\frac35+0\cdot\frac45=3\\[6pt]
\vec A_{\parallel}&=3\,\widehat B=\left(\frac95,\frac{12}5\right)\\[6pt]
\vec A_{\perp}&=\vec A-\vec A_{\parallel}
=\left(5-\frac95,\;0-\frac{12}{5}\right)
=\left(\frac{16}5,-\frac{12}5\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\vec A_{\perp}\cdot\vec B=\frac{16}{5}\cdot3+\left(-\frac{12}{5}\right)\cdot4=\frac{48}{5}-\frac{48}{5}=0.
$$

The perpendicular component $A_{\perp}$ runs from its head to the head of $A$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the rule for **resolving a vector along a chosen direction**. Its condition $\vec B\ne\vec0$ is satisfied here. The parallel component is the scalar projection of $\vec A$ in the direction of $\vec B$ multiplied by the unit vector $\widehat B$.

The unit vector in the direction of $\vec B$ is $\left(\dfrac35,\dfrac45\right)$ and the scalar projection is $3$. The parallel component is therefore $3\,\widehat B=\left(\dfrac95,\dfrac{12}5\right)$ and has magnitude $3$.

The perpendicular component is what remains after subtracting the parallel component from $\vec A$. For the first component calculate $5-\dfrac95=\dfrac{25}{5}-\dfrac95=\dfrac{16}{5}$, and for the second calculate $0-\dfrac{12}{5}=-\dfrac{12}{5}$.

The dot product of $\vec A_{\perp}$ and $\vec B$ is zero, confirming that the second part is perpendicular to $\vec B$. The perpendicular part has magnitude $\sqrt{\dfrac{256+144}{25}}=4$, and the magnitudes $3$ and $4$ satisfy $3^2+4^2=5^2=|\vec A|^2$, as the Pythagorean theorem requires for two perpendicular components.

This gives $\vec A_{\parallel}=\left(\dfrac95,\dfrac{12}5\right)$ and $\vec A_{\perp}=\left(\dfrac{16}5,-\dfrac{12}5\right)$.

</div>
