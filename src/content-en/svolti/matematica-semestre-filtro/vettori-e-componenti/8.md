## Exercise 1

A force $\vec F=(0,20,0)\,\mathrm{N}$ acts at a point $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pivot $O$ along the positive $x$ axis. Calculate the $z$ component of its torque about $O$ in a right-handed Cartesian coordinate system.

$$
\vec r=(0.25;\,0;\,0)\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\vec F=(0,20,0)\,\mathrm{N}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{O,z}&=r_x F_y-r_y F_x\\[6pt]
&=0.25\,\mathrm{m}\cdot20\,\mathrm{N}-0\,\mathrm{m}\cdot0\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=5\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque about $O$ is the cross product $\vec M_O=\vec r\times\vec F$, where $\vec r$ is the position vector from the pivot to the point of application. This point is on the positive $x$ axis at a distance $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the pivot, so $\vec r=(0.25;\,0;\,0)\,\mathrm{m}$.

The position vector and force lie in the $xy$ plane, so the torque has only a $z$ component, given by $r_xF_y-r_yF_x$. The second term is zero because $r_y=0$ and $F_x=0$. This leaves the product $0.25\cdot20=5$, in newton-meters.

The same value follows by multiplying the force magnitude by the moment arm. The force is vertical and the position vector horizontal, so the line of action is perpendicular to the lever and the moment arm equals the distance $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$. The product $20\,\mathrm{N}\cdot0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ is $5\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.

The positive sign indicates counterclockwise rotation when viewed from the positive side of the $z$ axis. This agrees with the physical situation because an upward force applied to the right of the pivot turns the lever counterclockwise.

The $z$ component of torque is $M_{O,z}=5\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.

</div>


## Exercise 2

Two downward forces act on a horizontal lever. The first is $15\,\mathrm{N}$ and acts $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left of the pivot. The second is $10\,\mathrm{N}$ and acts $0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right. Calculate the $z$ component of the total torque in a right-handed Cartesian coordinate system with $x$ pointing right and $y$ pointing up.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec r_1&=(-0.20;\,0;\,0)\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad \vec F_1=(0,-15,0)\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
\vec r_2&=(0.60;\,0;\,0)\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad \vec F_2=(0,-10,0)\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{1,z}&=r_{1x}F_{1y}-r_{1y}F_{1x}=(-0.20)\cdot(-15)\,\mathrm{N\,m}-0
=3\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
M_{2,z}&=r_{2x}F_{2y}-r_{2y}F_{2x}=0.60\cdot(-10)\,\mathrm{N\,m}-0
=-6\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
M_{\mathrm{tot},z}&=M_{1,z}+M_{2,z}=(3-6)\,\mathrm{N\,m}
=-3\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-3.6 xmax=7.4 ymin=-4.2 ymax=1.6 linee="-3,0 7,0" riferimento="-3,-3.6,0,1,x,y" vettori="-2,0,-2,-3,F₁;6,0,6,-2,F₂" punti="0,0,pieno,O" etichette="-1,0.45,0.20 m;3,0.45,0.60 m;-2.9,-1.6,15 N;6.8,-1.1,10 N" titolo="The force $F_1$ acts to the left of the pivot $O$ and the force $F_2$ to its right. The arrow lengths are proportional to the force magnitudes."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The total torque about the pivot is the sum of the torques of the two forces about that point. For each force, write the position vector relative to the pivot and the components of the force. The first force acts to the left, so $r_{1x}$ is negative. Both forces point downward, so their $y$ components are negative.

The torque of the first force contains the product of two negative numbers, so $M_{1,z}=3\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ is positive and corresponds to counterclockwise rotation. Indeed, a downward force to the left of the pivot lowers the left end of the lever. For the second force the product is negative, so $M_{2,z}=-6\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ corresponds to clockwise rotation.

The second torque has the larger magnitude because $10\cdot0.60=6$ exceeds $15\cdot0.20=3$. The smaller force produces the larger torque because its moment arm is three times as long. The sum of the torques is $-3\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.

The $z$ component of the total torque is $M_{\mathrm{tot},z}=-3\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, so the lever tends to turn clockwise.

</div>


## Exercise 3

A force $\vec F=(0,3,4)\,\mathrm{N}$ acts at a point with position vector $\vec r=(1,-2,0)\,\mathrm{m}$ relative to $O$. Calculate $\vec M_O$ in a right-handed Cartesian coordinate system.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{O,x}&=r_yF_z-r_zF_y=((-2)\cdot4-0\cdot3)\,\mathrm{N\,m}
=-8\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
M_{O,y}&=r_zF_x-r_xF_z=(0\cdot0-1\cdot4)\,\mathrm{N\,m}
=-4\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
M_{O,z}&=r_xF_y-r_yF_x=(1\cdot3-(-2)\cdot0)\,\mathrm{N\,m}
=3\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
\vec M_O&=(-8,-4,3)\,\mathrm{N\,m}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec M_O\cdot\vec F&=(-8)\cdot0+(-4)\cdot3+3\cdot4=0\\[6pt]
\vec M_O\cdot\vec r&=(-8)\cdot1+(-4)\cdot(-2)+3\cdot0=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque about $O$ is the cross product $\vec M_O=\vec r\times\vec F$, with the position vector first and the force second. Use the cross product formula for a right-handed coordinate system, substituting $r$ for the first vector and $F$ for the second.

For the $x$ component, calculate $(-2)\cdot4=-8$ and subtract $0\cdot3=0$. For the $y$ component, the first product is zero because $r_z=0$, and subtracting $1\cdot4=4$ gives $-4$. For the $z$ component, $1\cdot3=3$ remains because $F_x=0$.

The torque is perpendicular to both the position vector and the force, so its dot products with each must vanish. Using the three calculated components gives zero in both checks, as expected. Reversing the order of the factors would give $\vec F\times\vec r=(8,4,-3)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, the opposite vector.

The torque of the force about $O$ is $\vec M_O=(-8,-4,3)\,\mathrm{N\,m}$.

</div>
