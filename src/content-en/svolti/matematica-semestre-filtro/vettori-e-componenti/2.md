## Exercise 1

Given $\vec A=(5,2)$ and $\vec B=(1,4)$, calculate $\vec A-\vec B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\vec B&=(-1,-4)\\[6pt]
\vec A-\vec B&=\vec A+(-\vec B)\\[6pt]
&=(5-1,\;2-4)\\[6pt]
&=(4,-2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference $\vec A-\vec B$ is the sum of $\vec A$ and the opposite vector $-\vec B$. Reverse the signs of all the components to find the opposite vector, giving $-\vec B=(-1,-4)$.

Add component by component. The first component is $5+(-1)=4$ and the second is $2+(-4)=-2$. The same result follows by subtracting the corresponding component of $\vec B$ from each component of $\vec A$.

The second component is negative because the $y$ component of $\vec B$, which is $4$, exceeds that of $\vec A$, which is $2$. In a diagram with the tails of $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ at the origin, the difference runs from the head $(1,4)$ of $\vec B$ to the head $(5,2)$ of $\vec A$. Indeed, to go from $(1,4)$ to $(5,2)$, move $4$ forward along $x$ and $2$ down along $y$.

To check, add $\vec B$ to the difference. This gives $(1+4,\;4-2)=(5,2)$, which equals $\vec A$. Therefore $\vec A-\vec B=(4,-2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $\vec A=(2,-3)$, calculate $-2\vec A$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2\vec A&=\bigl((-2)\cdot2,\;(-2)\cdot(-3)\bigr)\\[6pt]
&=(-4,6)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec A|&=\sqrt{2^2+(-3)^2}=\sqrt{13}\\[6pt]
|-2\vec A|&=\sqrt{(-4)^2+6^2}=\sqrt{52}=2\sqrt{13}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To multiply a vector by a real number, multiply each component by that number. The first component is $(-2)\cdot2=-4$. The second is $(-2)\cdot(-3)=6$ because the product of two negative numbers is positive.

The factor $-2$ is negative, so $-2\vec A$ points in the opposite direction to $\vec A$ along the same line. The component signs confirm this. The vector $\vec A$ points toward positive $x$ and negative $y$, while $-2\vec A$ points toward negative $x$ and positive $y$.

The magnitude of the multiple equals the magnitude of $\vec A$ times the **absolute value** of the factor, in this case $2$. The vector $\vec A$ has magnitude $\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13}$, so $-2\vec A$ has magnitude $2\sqrt{13}$. A direct calculation confirms this, since $\sqrt{52}=\sqrt{4\cdot13}=2\sqrt{13}$.

The required vector is $-2\vec A=(-4,6)$, twice as long as $\vec A$ and pointing in the opposite direction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given $\vec A=(2,-1)$ and $\vec B=(-1,4)$, calculate $2\vec A-\vec B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\vec A&=(2\cdot2,\;2\cdot(-1))=(4,-2)\\[6pt]
-\vec B&=(1,-4)\\[6pt]
2\vec A-\vec B&=2\vec A+(-\vec B)\\[6pt]
&=(4+1,\;-2-4)\\[6pt]
&=(5,-6).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=6.4 ymin=-7 ymax=1.2 vettori="0,0,4,-2,2A;4,-2,5,-6,−B;0,0,5,-6" punti="4,-2;5,-6,pieno" etichette="1.25,-4,2A − B" titolo="The tail of $-B$ lies at the head of $2A$. The combination $2A-B$ runs from the origin to $(5,-6)$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression contains a scalar multiple and a difference. As in arithmetic, multiply by $2$ first. Doubling both components of $\vec A$ gives $2\vec A=(4,-2)$.

Write the difference as a sum using the negative of $\vec B$. The opposite vector has components $(1,-4)$ because the signs of $-1$ and $4$ are reversed. Adding component by component gives $4+1=5$ and $-2+(-4)=-6$.

The first component of the result exceeds that of $2\vec A$ because we subtract the negative component $-1$ of $\vec B$. Subtracting a negative number is equivalent to adding its opposite. The figure shows the head-to-tail construction. The tail of $-\vec B$ lies at the head of $2\vec A$, so the head of $-\vec B$ marks the endpoint of the result.

This gives $2\vec A-\vec B=(5,-6)$.

</div>
