## Exercise 1

A force of magnitude $14\,\mathrm{N}$ makes an angle of $210^\circ$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis. Calculate its components.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos210^\circ&=\cos(180^\circ+30^\circ)=-\cos30^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\[6pt]
\sin210^\circ&=\sin(180^\circ+30^\circ)=-\sin30^\circ=-\frac12
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_x&=14\,\mathrm{N}\cdot\left(-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)
=-7\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N}\approx-12.1\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
F_y&=14\,\mathrm{N}\cdot\left(-\frac12\right)
=-7\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a vector of magnitude $F$ at an angle $\theta$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis, the components are $F_x=F\cos\theta$ and $F_y=F\sin\theta$. These formulas hold in every quadrant because the signs of the cosine and sine give the signs of the components.

The angle $210^\circ$ exceeds $180^\circ$ by $30^\circ$, so the vector lies in the **third quadrant**. The reference angle is $30^\circ$. In the third quadrant, cosine and sine are both negative, so $\cos210^\circ=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and $\sin210^\circ=-\dfrac12$.

Multiplying the magnitude by these two values gives the components. Check by recalculating the magnitude, which is $\sqrt{147+49}\,\mathrm{N}=\sqrt{196}\,\mathrm{N}=14\,\mathrm{N}$ as specified in the problem.

The force components are $F_x=-7\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N}$ and $F_y=-7\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>


## Exercise 2

Given $\vec A=(-6,-6)$, calculate its direction angle, measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis in the range from $0^\circ$ inclusive to $360^\circ$ exclusive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan\alpha&=\frac{|A_y|}{|A_x|}=\frac{6}{6}=1
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \alpha=45^\circ\\[6pt]
\theta&=180^\circ+45^\circ=225^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec A|&=\sqrt{36+36}=6\sqrt2\\[6pt]
|\vec A|\cos225^\circ&=6\sqrt2\cdot\left(-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)=-6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First find the **reference angle** $\alpha$, the acute angle that the vector makes with the $x$ axis. Its tangent is the ratio of the absolute values of the components. The two components have the same absolute value, so $\tan\alpha=1$ and $\alpha=45^\circ$.

The tangent alone does not determine the direction, since the first-quadrant angle $45^\circ$ also has tangent $1$. Use the signs of the components to identify the quadrant. Both are negative, so the vector lies in the third quadrant. Angles in this quadrant lie between $180^\circ$ and $270^\circ$, so add $45^\circ$ to $180^\circ$.

Check using the component formulas. The magnitude is $6\sqrt2$. Multiplying it by $\cos225^\circ$ gives $-6$, the given $x$ component. The same holds for the $y$ component because $\sin225^\circ=\cos225^\circ$.

The direction angle of $\vec A$ is $\theta=225^\circ$.

</div>


## Exercise 3

Two forces have magnitudes $16\,\mathrm{N}$ and $12\,\mathrm{N}$. Their angles measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis are $30^\circ$ and $150^\circ$, respectively. Calculate the magnitude of their resultant.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F_1&=(16\cos30^\circ,\;16\sin30^\circ)\,\mathrm{N}=(8\sqrt3,\,8)\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
\vec F_2&=(12\cos150^\circ,\;12\sin150^\circ)\,\mathrm{N}=(-6\sqrt3,\,6)\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
\vec F_{\mathrm{ris}}&=(8\sqrt3-6\sqrt3,\;8+6)\,\mathrm{N}=(2\sqrt3,\,14)\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec F_{\mathrm{ris}}|&=\sqrt{(2\sqrt3)^2+14^2}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{12+196}\,\mathrm{N}=\sqrt{208}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=4\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{N}\approx14.4\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnitude of the resultant follows from its components, so first resolve the two forces. For the first force, $\cos30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and $\sin30^\circ=\dfrac12$. The angle $150^\circ$ lies in the second quadrant with reference angle $30^\circ$, so $\cos150^\circ=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and $\sin150^\circ=\dfrac12$.

The horizontal components have opposite signs and partially cancel, while the vertical components are both positive and add. Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the magnitude of the resultant. Since $208=16\cdot13$, the root simplifies to $4\sqrt{13}$.

The value is less than the sum of the magnitudes, $28\,\mathrm{N}$, because the forces point in different directions. The angle between them is $150^\circ-30^\circ=120^\circ$, and the formula for the **magnitude of the sum of two vectors** gives the same squared magnitude because $256+144+2\cdot16\cdot12\cdot\left(-\dfrac12\right)=400-192=208$.

The resultant has magnitude $4\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{N}$, approximately $14.4\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>
