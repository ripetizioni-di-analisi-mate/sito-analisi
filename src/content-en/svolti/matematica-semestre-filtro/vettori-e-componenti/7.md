## Exercise 1

Determine whether $\vec A=(1,-1,0)$ and $\vec B=(1,1,2)$ are perpendicular.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\cdot\vec B&=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z\\[6pt]
&=1\cdot1+(-1)\cdot1+0\cdot2\\[6pt]
&=1-1+0=0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
|\vec A|=\sqrt{1+1+0}=\sqrt2,
\qquad
|\vec B|=\sqrt{1+1+4}=\sqrt6.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For two nonzero vectors we have $\vec A\cdot\vec B=|\vec A|\,|\vec B|\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the vectors. Their dot product is therefore zero if and only if $\cos\theta=0$, that is, if the angle is a right angle. This angle criterion requires both vectors to be nonzero.

The magnitudes are $\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt6$, so both vectors are nonzero. To calculate the dot product, multiply corresponding components and add. The first product is $1$, the second $-1$, and the third $0$ because the $z$ component of $\vec A$ is zero.

The three products sum to zero. Since the magnitudes are positive, the formula gives $\cos\theta=\dfrac{0}{\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt6}=0$. The angle between two vectors lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, and in this interval the cosine is zero only at $90^\circ$.

The two vectors **are perpendicular**.

</div>


## Exercise 2

Find the value of $t$ for which $\vec A=(3,-2)$ and $\vec B=(t,6)$ are perpendicular.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\cdot\vec B&=3\cdot t+(-2)\cdot6=3t-12\\[6pt]
3t-12&=0\\[6pt]
3t&=12\\[6pt]
t&=4
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\vec B=(4,6),
\qquad
\vec A\cdot\vec B=3\cdot4+(-2)\cdot6=12-12=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two nonzero vectors are perpendicular if and only if their dot product is zero. The vector $\vec A$ is nonzero. The vector $\vec B$ is also nonzero for every $t$ because its second component is $6$. Perpendicularity is therefore equivalent to $\vec A\cdot\vec B=0$.

The dot product contains the term $3t$, which depends on the parameter, and the constant term $(-2)\cdot6=-12$. Setting their sum equal to zero gives a linear equation in $t$. Move $-12$ to the right-hand side and divide by $3$ to get $t=4$.

Check by substituting $t=4$. The vector becomes $\vec B=(4,6)$ and the dot product is $12-12=0$. The equation has only one solution, so for every other value of $t$ the dot product is nonzero and the vectors are not perpendicular.

The vectors are perpendicular when $t=4$.

</div>


## Exercise 3

Calculate the angle between $\vec A=(2,2)$ and $\vec B=(0,-5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\cdot\vec B&=2\cdot0+2\cdot(-5)=-10\\[6pt]
|\vec A|&=\sqrt{4+4}=2\sqrt2,
\qquad |\vec B|=\sqrt{0+25}=5\\[6pt]
\cos\theta&=\frac{\vec A\cdot\vec B}{|\vec A|\,|\vec B|}
=\frac{-10}{2\sqrt2\cdot5}
=-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
=-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\\[6pt]
\theta&=180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-2.4 xmax=3.6 ymin=-5.8 ymax=3 vettori="0,0,2,2,A;0,0,0,-5,B" curve="0.9*cos(t),0.9*sin(t),-1.5708,0.7854" etichette="1.35,-0.75,135°" titolo="The vector $A$ makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the positive $x$ axis, and $B$ makes an angle of $90^\circ$ on the other side, so the angle between them is $135^\circ$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle $\theta$ between two nonzero vectors follows from $\cos\theta=\dfrac{\vec A\cdot\vec B}{|\vec A|\,|\vec B|}$. Both vectors have positive magnitude, so the formula applies.

The dot product is $-10$ because the first component of $\vec B$ is zero, leaving only the product $2\cdot(-5)$. The magnitude of $\vec A$ is $\sqrt8=2\sqrt2$ and that of $\vec B$ is $5$. Dividing gives $-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$, which becomes $-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$ after rationalizing the denominator.

The angle between two vectors lies between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. The cosine is negative, so the angle is obtuse. The absolute value of the cosine, $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, is the cosine of $45^\circ$, so the angle is $180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ$.

The figure confirms the result. The vector $\vec A$ makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the positive $x$ axis, and $\vec B$ points along the negative $y$ axis. The angle between them is therefore $45^\circ+90^\circ=135^\circ$.

The angle between the two vectors is $\theta=135^\circ$.

</div>
