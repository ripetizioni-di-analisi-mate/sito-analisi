## Exercise 1

A vector has components $(3,4)\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate its magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec A|&=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{9+16}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{25}\,\mathrm{m}=5\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=4.8 ymin=-1 ymax=5.2 vettori="0,0,3,4,A" segmenti="0,0,3,0;3,0,3,4" punti="3,4,pieno" etichette="1.5,-0.45,3;3.25,2,4" titolo="The components $3$ and $4$ are the legs of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the vector."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Place the tail at the origin. The head is then at the point with coordinates $(3,4)$. The horizontal segment from the origin to $(3,0)$ and the vertical segment from $(3,0)$ to $(3,4)$ are perpendicular because the Cartesian axes are perpendicular. The vector is therefore the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs of lengths $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$.

By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the square of the magnitude is the sum of the squares of the components. This gives $9+16=25$. Take the positive square root because the magnitude is a length. The components are in meters, so the magnitude is also in meters.

The vector has magnitude $5\,\mathrm{m}$. This is greater than either component and less than their sum $7\,\mathrm{m}$, as is true of the hypotenuse and legs of any right triangle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $\vec A=(3,1)$ and $\vec B=(2,-4)$, calculate $\vec A+\vec B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A+\vec B&=(A_x+B_x,\;A_y+B_y)\\[6pt]
&=(3+2,\;1+(-4))\\[6pt]
&=(5,-3).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=6.2 ymin=-4 ymax=2.2 vettori="0,0,3,1,A;3,1,5,-3,B;0,0,5,-3" punti="3,1;5,-3,pieno" etichette="2.1,-1.85,A + B" titolo="In the head-to-tail construction, the tail of $B$ is at the head of $A$, and the sum runs from the origin to the point $(5,-3)$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Add two vectors **component by component**. The first component of the sum is the sum of the first components, and the second is the sum of the second components. This works because making one displacement after another adds the coordinate changes along each axis.

The first component is $3+2=5$. The second component of $\vec B$ is negative, so in the second sum add $-4$ to $1$ to get $-3$. The minus sign shows that this component of the sum points in the negative $y$ direction.

The figure shows the same addition using the head-to-tail rule. Place the tail of $\vec B$ at the head of $\vec A$, the point $(3,1)$. The vector $\vec B$ moves $2$ forward along $x$ and $4$ down along $y$, so its head reaches $(5,-3)$. The sum runs from the tail of $\vec A$ to the head of $\vec B$, so $\vec A+\vec B=(5,-3)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object moves from $P=(-2,3,1)\,\mathrm{m}$ to $Q=(2,0,13)\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the components and magnitude of its displacement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{PQ}&=(x_Q-x_P,\;y_Q-y_P,\;z_Q-z_P)\\[6pt]
&=(2-(-2),\;0-3,\;13-1)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=(4,-3,12)\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\overrightarrow{PQ}|&=\sqrt{4^2+(-3)^2+12^2}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{16+9+144}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{169}\,\mathrm{m}=13\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The displacement is the vector from the initial position $P$ to the final position $Q$. Its components are therefore the coordinates of $Q$ minus those of $P$, in the order head minus tail.

In the first component, a negative number is subtracted, so $2-(-2)=2+2=4$. The second component is $0-3=-3$, so the object moves in the negative $y$ direction. The third component is $13-1=12$.

Find the magnitude using the **Pythagorean theorem in three dimensions**. The square of $-3$ is $9$, so the negative component contributes to the magnitude just as the others do. The calculation can also be done in two steps. The projection of the displacement onto the $xy$ plane has magnitude $\sqrt{16+9}\,\mathrm{m}=5\,\mathrm{m}$, and the right triangle with legs $5\,\mathrm{m}$ and $12\,\mathrm{m}$ has hypotenuse $\sqrt{25+144}\,\mathrm{m}=13\,\mathrm{m}$.

The displacement has components $(4,-3,12)\,\mathrm{m}$ and magnitude $13\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
