## Exercise 1

Given $\vec A=(2,3)$ and $\vec B=(4,-1)$, calculate $\vec A\cdot\vec B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\cdot\vec B&=A_xB_x+A_yB_y\\[6pt]
&=2\cdot4+3\cdot(-1)\\[6pt]
&=8-3=5
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
|\vec A|=\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13},
\qquad
|\vec B|=\sqrt{16+1}=\sqrt{17}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate the **dot product** by multiplying corresponding components and adding the products. The product of the first components is $2\cdot4=8$. The product of the second components is $3\cdot(-1)=-3$ because the $y$ component of $\vec B$ is negative.

The sum of the products is $8-3=5$. The result is a scalar and therefore has no components.

The sign of the dot product gives information about the angle $\theta$ between the vectors. Both vectors are nonzero, with magnitudes $\sqrt{13}$ and $\sqrt{17}$, so the formula $\vec A\cdot\vec B=|\vec A|\,|\vec B|\cos\theta$ applies. This gives
$$
\cos\theta=\frac{5}{\sqrt{13}\cdot\sqrt{17}}=\frac{5}{\sqrt{221}}.
$$
The cosine is positive, so the angle between the vectors is acute. Dividing the dot product by $|\vec B|$ gives the scalar projection of $\vec A$ in the direction of $\vec B$. It equals $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{17}}$ and is positive for the same reason.

The dot product is $\vec A\cdot\vec B=5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A constant force of magnitude $20\,\mathrm{N}$ makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with a displacement of $5\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the work done by the force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\parallel}&=F\cos30^\circ
=20\,\mathrm{N}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}
=10\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
W&=F_{\parallel}\,s
=10\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N}\cdot5\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=50\sqrt3\,\mathrm{J}\approx 86.6\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The work done by a constant force is the dot product of the force and the displacement, $W=\vec F\cdot\vec s$. Both magnitudes and the angle between the vectors are known, so use the geometric form $W=F\,s\cos\theta$.

The factor $F\cos\theta$ is the component of the force along the displacement. For $\theta=30^\circ$ the cosine is $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so the component is $20\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\,\mathrm{N}=10\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N}$. The component perpendicular to the displacement does not enter the calculation because its dot product with $\vec s$ is zero.

Multiplying the component by the displacement magnitude gives $50\sqrt3\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. A newton times a meter equals a joule, so the result is in joules. The angle is less than $90^\circ$, so the cosine and the work are both positive. The approximate value follows from $\sqrt3\approx1.732$, since $50\cdot1.732=86.6$.

The force does work $W=50\sqrt3\,\mathrm{J}$, approximately $86.6\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two constant forces $\vec F_1=(6,1)\,\mathrm{N}$ and $\vec F_2=(-4,3)\,\mathrm{N}$ act during a displacement $\vec s=(2,-1)\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the total work.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_1&=\vec F_1\cdot\vec s=(6\cdot2+1\cdot(-1))\,\mathrm{J}=11\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]
W_2&=\vec F_2\cdot\vec s=((-4)\cdot2+3\cdot(-1))\,\mathrm{J}=-11\,\mathrm{J}\\[6pt]
W&=W_1+W_2=0\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F_{\mathrm{ris}}&=(6-4,\;1+3)\,\mathrm{N}=(2,4)\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
\vec F_{\mathrm{ris}}\cdot\vec s&=(2\cdot2+4\cdot(-1))\,\mathrm{J}=0\,\mathrm{J}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-1.2 xmax=3.6 ymin=-1.8 ymax=4.6 vettori="0,0,2,4,F;0,0,2,-1,s" titolo="The resultant $F=(2,4)$ is perpendicular to the displacement $s=(2,-1)$, so the total work is zero."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The total work is the sum of the work done by the individual forces. The work done by $\vec F_1$ is $12-1=11\,\mathrm{J}$ and is positive. The work done by $\vec F_2$ is $-8-3=-11\,\mathrm{J}$ and is negative because the $x$ component of $\vec F_2$ points opposite to the corresponding component of the displacement. The two contributions have equal absolute values, so their sum is zero.

The same result follows by first finding the resultant and then taking a single dot product. This is valid because the dot product is **distributive** over addition, so $\vec F_1\cdot\vec s+\vec F_2\cdot\vec s=(\vec F_1+\vec F_2)\cdot\vec s$.

The resultant $(2,4)\,\mathrm{N}$ and the displacement $(2,-1)\,\mathrm{m}$ are both nonzero and their dot product is zero. They are therefore perpendicular, as the figure shows. Thus the resultant has zero component along the displacement.

The total work done by the two forces is $0\,\mathrm{J}$.

</div>
