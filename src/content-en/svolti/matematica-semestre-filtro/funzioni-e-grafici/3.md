## Exercise 1

A volume of $15\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a homogeneous liquid has mass $18\,\mathrm{g}$. Calculate the mass of $35\,\mathrm{mL}$ of the same liquid.

$$
\begin{aligned}\rho&=\frac{m_1}{V_1}=\frac{18\,\mathrm{g}}{15\,\mathrm{mL}}=\frac{6}{5}\,\mathrm{g/mL}=1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL},\\[6pt]m_2&=\rho V_2=(1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL})(35\,\mathrm{mL})=42\,\mathrm{g}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The liquid is homogeneous, so its density $\rho$ is the same throughout. Its mass and volume are therefore **directly proportional** according to $m=\rho V$, and the graph of mass versus volume is a straight line through the origin.

The known pair of values gives the constant of proportionality. Dividing the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{18}{15}$ by $3$ simplifies the ratio to $\dfrac{6}{5}$, or $1.2$. Thus the density is $1.2\,\mathrm{g/mL}$, meaning that each milliliter of liquid has mass $1.2\,\mathrm{g}$.

Multiplying the density by the new volume cancels the milliliters and leaves a mass, $42\,\mathrm{g}$. On the graph of mass versus volume, the points $(15,18)$ and $(35,42)$ lie on the line $m=1.2V$, which passes through the origin because zero volume corresponds to zero mass.

The same result follows from direct proportionality. Changing the volume from $15$ to $35\,\mathrm{mL}$ multiplies it by $\dfrac{35}{15}=\dfrac{7}{3}$. The mass is multiplied by the same factor, so it is $18\cdot\dfrac{7}{3}\,\mathrm{g}=42\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A fixed amount of an ideal gas at constant temperature occupies $4\,\mathrm{L}$ at $150\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate its volume at a pressure of $80\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}p_1V_1&=(150\,\mathrm{kPa})(4\,\mathrm{L})=600\,\mathrm{kPa}\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]p_2V_2&=p_1V_1,\\[6pt]V_2&=\frac{p_1V_1}{p_2}=\frac{600\,\mathrm{kPa}\,\mathrm{L}}{80\,\mathrm{kPa}}=7.5\,\mathrm{L}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="600/x" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=200 nomi="V,p" legenda="p=\dfrac{600}{V}" punti="4,150;7.5,80" segmenti="4,0,4,150;0,150,4,150;7.5,0,7.5,80;0,80,7.5,80" titolo="Pressure $p$ in kilopascals and volume $V$ in liters. The two states of the gas lie on the same branch of the hyperbola $pV = 600$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For an ideal gas, $pV=nRT$. The amount of gas $n$ and the temperature $T$ are constant, so the product $pV$ is constant. Pressure and volume are therefore **inversely proportional**, with constant $k=600\,\mathrm{kPa}\,\mathrm{L}$ determined from the initial state.

The product has the same value in the final state. Dividing both sides of $p_2V_2=600\,\mathrm{kPa}\,\mathrm{L}$ by $p_2=80\,\mathrm{kPa}$ cancels the kilopascals and leaves a volume, $V_2=7.5\,\mathrm{L}$.

The result can also be understood in terms of a scale factor. Changing the pressure from $150$ to $80\,\mathrm{kPa}$ divides it by $\dfrac{150}{80}=\dfrac{15}{8}$. In an inverse proportion, the volume is multiplied by the same factor, so $V_2=4\cdot\dfrac{15}{8}\,\mathrm{L}=7.5\,\mathrm{L}$. Since the pressure decreases, the final volume is greater than the initial volume.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object of constant mass has initial kinetic energy $32\,\mathrm{J}$. Its speed is multiplied by $\dfrac{3}{2}$. Calculate its final kinetic energy.

$$
\begin{aligned}E_{c,1}&=\frac{1}{2}mv_1^2,\qquad v_2=\frac{3}{2}v_1,\\[6pt]E_{c,2}&=\frac{1}{2}m\left(\frac{3}{2}v_1\right)^2=\frac{9}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2}mv_1^2=\frac{9}{4}E_{c,1},\\[6pt] E_{c,2}&=\frac{9}{4}(32\,\mathrm{J})=72\,\mathrm{J}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The kinetic energy is $E_c=\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. The mass $m$ is constant, so the energy is proportional to the square of the speed and the relationship is **quadratic**.

Substitute $v_2=\dfrac{3}{2}v_1$ into the formula for the final energy. The square of a product is the product of the squares, so $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}v_1\right)^2=\dfrac{9}{4}v_1^2$. Factoring out $\dfrac{9}{4}$ leaves $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_1^2$, the initial energy. Thus the final energy is $\dfrac{9}{4}$ times the initial energy.

Multiplying gives $\dfrac{9}{4}\cdot 32=9\cdot 8=72$, so the final energy is $72\,\mathrm{J}$, an increase of $40\,\mathrm{J}$. The mass does not appear in the calculation because the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}m$ cancels in the ratio of the two energies.

Multiplying the energy by the same factor $\dfrac{3}{2}$ as the speed would give $48\,\mathrm{J}$. That result would be correct for direct proportionality, but kinetic energy depends on the square of the speed.

</div>
