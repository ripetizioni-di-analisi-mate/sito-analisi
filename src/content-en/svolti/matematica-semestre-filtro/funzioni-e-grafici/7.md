## Exercise 1

An instrument’s calibration is $A(c)=(4\,\mathrm{L/mol})c+0.05$ for $0\le c\le 0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. Find the concentration that gives $A=0.45$ and determine whether $A=0.69$ is consistent with the calibration range.

$$
\begin{aligned}(4\,\mathrm{L/mol})c+0.05&=0.45,\\[6pt](4\,\mathrm{L/mol})c&=0.40,\\[6pt]c_{0.45}&=\frac{0.40}{4\,\mathrm{L/mol}}=0.10\,\mathrm{mol/L},\\[6pt]A(0)&=0.05,\qquad A(0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L})=(4\,\mathrm{L/mol})(0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L})+0.05=0.65,\\[6pt]0.69&>0.65.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the concentration that produces response $0.45$, read the relationship in reverse by solving $A(c)=0.45$. Subtracting $0.05$ from both sides leaves $(4\,\mathrm{L/mol})c=0.40$. Dividing by $4\,\mathrm{L/mol}$ gives $c=0.10\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ because the reciprocal of $\mathrm{L/mol}$ is $\mathrm{mol/L}$. This value lies within the calibration range, so it is an admissible solution.

For the second value, determine the range of possible responses. The slope $4\,\mathrm{L/mol}$ is positive, so the response increases and its extreme values occur at the endpoints of the domain. At $c=0$ the response is $0.05$, and at $c=0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ it is $0.60+0.05=0.65$.

The value $0.69$ exceeds $0.65$, so no concentration in the calibration range produces it. Solving the equation anyway would give $c=0.16\,\mathrm{mol/L}$, which is outside the model’s domain. The response $0.69$ **is incompatible** with the specified range.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two objects have positions $s_A(t)=2\,\mathrm{m}+(2\,\mathrm{m/s})t$ and $s_B(t)=14\,\mathrm{m}-(1\,\mathrm{m/s})t$ for $0\le t\le 8\,\mathrm{s}$. Find the time and position at which they meet.

$$
\begin{aligned}2\,\mathrm{m}+(2\,\mathrm{m/s})t&=14\,\mathrm{m}-(1\,\mathrm{m/s})t,\\[6pt] (2\,\mathrm{m/s})t+(1\,\mathrm{m/s})t&=14\,\mathrm{m}-2\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt] (3\,\mathrm{m/s})t&=12\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]t&=4\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]s_A(4\,\mathrm{s})&=2\,\mathrm{m}+8\,\mathrm{m}=10\,\mathrm{m},\qquad s_B(4\,\mathrm{s})=14\,\mathrm{m}-4\,\mathrm{m}=10\,\mathrm{m}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2+2*x;14-x" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=0 ymax=20 nomi="t,s" legenda="s_A=2+2t;s_B=14-t" punti="4,10" segmenti="4,0,4,10;0,10,4,10" titolo="Positions $s$ in meters and time $t$ in seconds. At the intersection, the horizontal coordinate is 4 and the vertical coordinate is 10."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two objects meet when they occupy the same position at the same time. On the position–time graph this is the point of **intersection** of the two lines, so we solve $s_A(t)=s_B(t)$.

Move the terms containing $t$ to the left and the constant terms to the right. The term $-(1\,\mathrm{m/s})t$ moves to the left and changes sign, and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ moves to the right and changes sign. This gives $(3\,\mathrm{m/s})t=12\,\mathrm{m}$, and dividing by $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ yields $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$.

The lines have different slopes, so the solution is unique. The time $4\,\mathrm{s}$ belongs to the common domain $0\le t\le 8\,\mathrm{s}$, so the objects do meet. Substituting $t=4\,\mathrm{s}$ into both formulas gives $10\,\mathrm{m}$, so the objects meet **after four seconds, at a position of ten meters**.

The value $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is the rate at which the distance between the two objects decreases from its initial value of $12\,\mathrm{m}$. Indeed, the objects are moving toward each other in opposite directions.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two liquid levels are given by $h_A(t)=4\,\mathrm{cm}+(2\,\mathrm{cm/min})t$ and $h_B(t)=17\,\mathrm{cm}-(1\,\mathrm{cm/min})t$, with both formulas valid only for $0\le t\le 4\,\mathrm{min}$. Determine whether the levels reach the same height during this interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}4\,\mathrm{cm}+(2\,\mathrm{cm/min})t&=17\,\mathrm{cm}-(1\,\mathrm{cm/min})t,\\[6pt](3\,\mathrm{cm/min})t&=13\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]t&=\frac{13}{3}\,\mathrm{min}>4\,\mathrm{min},\\[6pt]h_A(4\,\mathrm{min})&=12\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad h_B(4\,\mathrm{min})=13\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4+2*x;17-x" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=20 nomi="t,h" legenda="h_A=4+2t;h_B=17-t" punti="4,12;4,13" segmenti="4,0,4,13" titolo="Levels $h$ in centimeters and time $t$ in minutes. At the endpoint $t = 4$ the lines are still 1 centimeter apart."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two levels are equal at a time when the formulas give the same value. Setting them equal and collecting terms in $t$ gives $(3\,\mathrm{cm/min})t=13\,\mathrm{cm}$. The slopes differ, so the equation gives only one candidate time, $t=\dfrac{13}{3}\,\mathrm{min}$.

The value $\dfrac{13}{3}$ exceeds $4$ because $4=\dfrac{12}{3}$. The candidate time therefore falls outside the interval of validity, and the formulas do not describe the levels at that time.

At the endpoint $t=4\,\mathrm{min}$ the levels are $12$ and $13\,\mathrm{cm}$. The difference $h_B(t)-h_A(t)=13\,\mathrm{cm}-(3\,\mathrm{cm/min})t$ is positive for every $t<\dfrac{13}{3}\,\mathrm{min}$, so the level $h_B$ stays above $h_A$ throughout the interval. The two levels **do not reach the same height** during the specified interval.

</div>
