## Exercise 1

An object is at position $-3\,\mathrm{m}$ at time $1\,\mathrm{s}$ and at $-9\,\mathrm{m}$ at time $4\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate its average velocity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta s&=-9\,\mathrm{m}-(-3\,\mathrm{m})=-9\,\mathrm{m}+3\,\mathrm{m}=-6\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta t&=4\,\mathrm{s}-1\,\mathrm{s}=3\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
v_m&=\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}=\frac{-6\,\mathrm{m}}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **average velocity** between two times is the displacement divided by the elapsed time. In chronological order, the displacement is the final position minus the initial position. The initial position is negative, so subtracting it is equivalent to adding $3\,\mathrm{m}$, giving a displacement of $-6\,\mathrm{m}$.

The elapsed time is the final time minus the initial time, $3\,\mathrm{s}$, and is positive because $t_2>t_1$. A negative displacement divided by a positive elapsed time is negative, so $v_m=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. On the position–time graph, this is the slope of the secant line through $(1,-3)$ and $(4,-9)$.

The minus sign means that, on average, the object moves opposite to the positive direction at a rate of two meters each second. Its distance from the origin increases from $3$ to $9\,\mathrm{m}$ despite the negative velocity, because both positions are on the negative side of the origin. The sign of the average velocity is the sign of the displacement and is independent of the signs of the positions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The velocity changes from $-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ between $3$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the average acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta v&=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}-(-6\,\mathrm{m/s})=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}+6\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
\Delta t&=5\,\mathrm{s}-3\,\mathrm{s}=2\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
a_m&=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}=\frac{4\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **average acceleration** is the change in velocity divided by the elapsed time. To find the change, subtract the initial velocity from the final velocity, keeping the sign of each. Subtracting $-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is equivalent to adding $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so $\Delta v=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The elapsed time is $2\,\mathrm{s}$. Dividing meters per second by seconds gives units of $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, so the average acceleration is $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. It is positive because the signed velocity increases from $-6$ to $-2$.

The speed, however, decreases from $6$ to $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The object is moving in the negative direction and slowing down, so velocity and acceleration have opposite signs. Positive acceleration means that the signed velocity increases. It corresponds to an increase in speed only when the velocity is also positive.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A position–time graph passes through $(0,2)$, $(2,8)$, and $(6,4)$, with time in seconds and position in meters. Straight line segments connect adjacent points. Calculate the average velocity over each segment and over the entire interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}v_{m,1}&=\frac{8-2}{2-0}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\frac{6}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]v_{m,2}&=\frac{4-8}{6-2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\frac{-4}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}=-1\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]v_m&=\frac{4-2}{6-0}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\frac{2}{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]v_m&=\frac{(3\,\mathrm{m/s})(2\,\mathrm{s})+(-1\,\mathrm{m/s})(4\,\mathrm{s})}{6\,\mathrm{s}}=\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,2 2,8 6,4" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=10 nomi="t,s" punti="0,2;2,8;6,4" segmenti="0,2,6,4" titolo="Position $s$ in meters and time $t$ in seconds. The dashed secant line through the first and last points has slope $\dfrac{1}{3}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

On each interval the graph is a line segment, so the average velocity on that interval is its slope. On the first interval, position increases by $6\,\mathrm{m}$ in $2\,\mathrm{s}$, so $v_{m,1}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. On the second it decreases by $4\,\mathrm{m}$ in $4\,\mathrm{s}$, so $v_{m,2}=-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The slope changes sign at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$, when the object reverses direction.

Over the entire interval, only the difference between the final and initial positions, $4-2=2\,\mathrm{m}$, enters the displacement. Dividing by the total elapsed time of $6\,\mathrm{s}$ gives $v_m=\dfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the slope of the dashed secant line.

The arithmetic mean of the two segment velocities, $\dfrac{3+(-1)}{2}=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, differs from the result because the segments last $2$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$, respectively. The last line of the calculation shows that the overall average velocity is the mean of the segment velocities **weighted by their durations**. It equals the sum of the two displacements, $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and $-4\,\mathrm{m}$, divided by the total elapsed time.

</div>
