## Exercise 1

The velocity varies linearly from $3$ to $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ between $0$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the displacement and the distance traveled.

$$
\begin{aligned}a&=\frac{-1\,\mathrm{m/s}-3\,\mathrm{m/s}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}=-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]v(t)&=3\,\mathrm{m/s}-(1\,\mathrm{m/s^2})t,\\[6pt]v(t)=0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=3\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]S_+&=\frac{(3\,\mathrm{s})(3\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=\frac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]S_-&=\frac{(1\,\mathrm{s})(1\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]\Delta s&=S_+-S_-=\frac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{m}-\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=4\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]d&=S_++S_-=\frac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{m}+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=5\,\mathrm{m}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,3-t,0,4" legenda="v=3-t" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=4 nomi="t,v" punti="0,3;3,0;4,-1" area="3-x;0;0;3|0;3-x;3;4" segmenti="4,0,4,-1" titolo="Velocity $v$ in meters per second and time $t$ in seconds. The zero of the velocity at $t = 3$ separates the two triangles."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity changes from positive to negative, so it is zero at an intermediate time and the object reverses direction. To find that time, write the velocity as a function of time. The slope of the velocity–time graph is the acceleration, $-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, and its intercept is $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Setting $v(t)=0$ gives $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$.

The time $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ divides the region into two triangles. The first lies above the axis, with base $3\,\mathrm{s}$, height $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and area $\dfrac{9}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$. The second lies below the axis, with base $1\,\mathrm{s}$, geometric height $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and area $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}$.

Displacement is signed area, so the area of the second triangle is subtracted and $\Delta s=4\,\mathrm{m}$. The **formula for displacement when velocity varies linearly** gives the same result because $\dfrac{3+(-1)}{2}\cdot 4=4$. To find the distance traveled, add the unsigned areas because the object travels half a meter backward during the second interval. This gives $d=5\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A velocity–time graph passes through $(0,0)$, $(2,4)$, $(4,4)$, and $(6,-4)$, with straight segments joining successive points, time in seconds, and velocity in meters per second. The initial position is $-2\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the final position and the distance traveled.

$$
\begin{aligned}v(t)&=4\,\mathrm{m/s}-(4\,\mathrm{m/s^2})(t-4\,\mathrm{s})\quad\text{for } 4\,\mathrm{s}\le t\le 6\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]v(t)=0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t-4\,\mathrm{s}=1\,\mathrm{s}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=5\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]S_+&=\left(\frac{2\cdot4}{2}+2\cdot4+\frac{1\cdot4}{2}\right)\mathrm{m}=(4+8+2)\,\mathrm{m}=14\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]S_-&=\frac{1\cdot4}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=2\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]s(6\,\mathrm{s})&=-2\,\mathrm{m}+14\,\mathrm{m}-2\,\mathrm{m}=10\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]d&=14\,\mathrm{m}+2\,\mathrm{m}=16\,\mathrm{m}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 2,4 4,4 6,-4" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7 ymin=-5 ymax=6 nomi="t,v" punti="0,0;2,4;4,4;5,0;6,-4" area="2*x;0;0;2|4;0;2;4|20-4*x;0;4;5|0;20-4*x;5;6" titolo="Velocity $v$ in meters per second and time $t$ in seconds. The last region, beginning at $t = 5$, lies below the axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first three regions lie above the time axis, while the final segment crosses the axis. On that segment the velocity changes from $4$ to $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $2\,\mathrm{s}$, so the slope is $-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and the velocity reaches zero after $1\,\mathrm{s}$, at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$. The endpoint velocities are opposites, so the zero lies halfway through the interval.

Before the zero crossing, three areas are added. The triangle between $0$ and $2\,\mathrm{s}$ has area $4\,\mathrm{m}$, the rectangle between $2$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$ has area $8\,\mathrm{m}$, and the triangle between $4$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$ has area $2\,\mathrm{m}$. Together they give $14\,\mathrm{m}$ in the positive direction. The triangle between $5$ and $6\,\mathrm{s}$ lies below the axis and has unsigned area $2\,\mathrm{m}$.

The overall displacement is $14\,\mathrm{m}-2\,\mathrm{m}=12\,\mathrm{m}$. Adding it to the initial position gives the **final position of $10\,\mathrm{m}$**. The distance traveled is the sum of the unsigned areas because the object travels backward during the last interval, giving $16\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object maintains $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from $0$ to $3\,\mathrm{s}$. From $3$ to $9\,\mathrm{s}$ its velocity decreases linearly from $4$ to $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate its average velocity and average speed over the entire interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}a&=\frac{-2\,\mathrm{m/s}-4\,\mathrm{m/s}}{9\,\mathrm{s}-3\,\mathrm{s}}=-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]v(t)=4\,\mathrm{m/s}-(1\,\mathrm{m/s^2})(t-3\,\mathrm{s})=0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=7\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]S_+&=\left(3\cdot4+\frac{4\cdot4}{2}\right)\mathrm{m}=(12+8)\,\mathrm{m}=20\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]S_-&=\frac{2\cdot2}{2}\,\mathrm{m}=2\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]v_m&=\frac{20\,\mathrm{m}-2\,\mathrm{m}}{9\,\mathrm{s}}=\frac{18\,\mathrm{m}}{9\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]\text{average speed}&=\frac{20\,\mathrm{m}+2\,\mathrm{m}}{9\,\mathrm{s}}=\frac{22}{9}\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,4 3,4 9,-2" xmin=-0.5 xmax=10 ymin=-3 ymax=5 nomi="t,v" punti="0,4;3,4;7,0;9,-2" area="4;0;0;3|7-x;0;3;7|0;7-x;7;9" titolo="Velocity $v$ in meters per second and time $t$ in seconds. The rectangle and triangle above the axis represent a total of 20 meters, and the triangle below the axis represents 2 meters."}

<div class="spiegazione">

On the sloping segment the velocity decreases by $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $6\,\mathrm{s}$, so the acceleration is $-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Starting from $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the velocity reaches zero $4\,\mathrm{s}$ after the beginning of the segment, at $t=7\,\mathrm{s}$.

Above the axis are the rectangle between $0$ and $3\,\mathrm{s}$, of area $12\,\mathrm{m}$, and the triangle between $3$ and $7\,\mathrm{s}$, of base $4\,\mathrm{s}$, height $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and area $8\,\mathrm{m}$. Below the axis is the triangle between $7$ and $9\,\mathrm{s}$, of base $2\,\mathrm{s}$, geometric height $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and area $2\,\mathrm{m}$.

The displacement is the difference between the areas, $18\,\mathrm{m}$, while the distance traveled is their sum, $22\,\mathrm{m}$. Both averages have the same denominator, the total time of $9\,\mathrm{s}$. The **average velocity** is therefore $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the **average speed** is $\dfrac{22}{9}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, approximately $2.44\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The average speed exceeds the magnitude of the average velocity because the object travels backward between $7$ and $9\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>
