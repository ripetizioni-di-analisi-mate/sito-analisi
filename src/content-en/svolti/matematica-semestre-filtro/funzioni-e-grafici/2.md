## Exercise 1

The volume in a container is $V(t)=5\,\mathrm{L}+(0.4\,\mathrm{L/min})t$ for $0\le t\le 10\,\mathrm{min}$. Find the initial volume and the slope.

$$
\begin{aligned}V(0)&=5\,\mathrm{L}+(0.4\,\mathrm{L/min})(0\,\mathrm{min})=5\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]V(5\,\mathrm{min})&=5\,\mathrm{L}+(0.4\,\mathrm{L/min})(5\,\mathrm{min})=5\,\mathrm{L}+2\,\mathrm{L}=7\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]m&=\frac{V(5\,\mathrm{min})-V(0)}{5\,\mathrm{min}-0\,\mathrm{min}}=\frac{7\,\mathrm{L}-5\,\mathrm{L}}{5\,\mathrm{min}}=0.4\,\mathrm{L/min}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relationship has the affine form $V=q+mt$, with time as the independent variable. The initial volume is the value at $t=0$, which belongs to the domain. The term involving $t$ is zero, so $V(0)=5\,\mathrm{L}$. This is the graph’s **intercept** on the volume axis.

The **slope** is the change in volume divided by the corresponding elapsed time. Choose a second time in the domain, for example $t=5\,\mathrm{min}$. The product $(0.4\,\mathrm{L/min})(5\,\mathrm{min})$ equals $2\,\mathrm{L}$ because the minutes cancel, so $V(5\,\mathrm{min})=7\,\mathrm{L}$. Dividing the change of $2\,\mathrm{L}$ by $5\,\mathrm{min}$ gives $0.4\,\mathrm{L/min}$, the coefficient of $t$ in the formula.

The slope is measured in liters per minute because it is a volume divided by a time. Thus $0.4\,\mathrm{L}$ enters the container each minute. At the endpoint $t=10\,\mathrm{min}$ the volume is $5\,\mathrm{L}+4\,\mathrm{L}=9\,\mathrm{L}$, so the model describes volumes between $5$ and $9\,\mathrm{L}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The graph of an affine function passes through $(1,4)$ and $(4,-2)$. Find the equation of the line and its intersection with the horizontal axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}m&=\frac{-2-4}{4-1}=\frac{-6}{3}=-2,\\[6pt]q&=y_1-mx_1=4-(-2)\cdot1=4+2=6,\\[6pt]y&=-2x+6,\\[6pt]-2x+6&=0,\\[6pt]2x&=6\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=3.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-2*x+6" xmin=-0.8 xmax=5.2 ymin=-4 ymax=8 punti="1,4;3,0;4,-2" segmenti="1,4,4,4;4,4,4,-2" titolo="The slope is the change in the vertical coordinate, $-6$, divided by the change in the horizontal coordinate, $3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two points have different horizontal coordinates, so the slope formula applies. The numerator is the difference between the vertical coordinates, $-2-4=-6$. The denominator is the difference between the horizontal coordinates taken in the same order, $4-1=3$. The ratio is $-2$. The vertical change is negative and the horizontal change is positive, so the line **slopes downward**.

To find the intercept, use $q=y_1-mx_1$ with the point $(1,4)$. The product $(-2)\cdot 1$ equals $-2$, and subtracting $-2$ is equivalent to adding $2$, so $q=6$. The point $(4,-2)$ gives the same value because $-2-(-2)\cdot 4=-2+8=6$. The equation of the line is $y=-2x+6$.

On the horizontal axis the vertical coordinate is zero. Therefore set $-2x+6=0$, move the term involving $x$ to the right-hand side, and divide by $2$. The line meets the horizontal axis at $(3,0)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pressure increases linearly between $0$ and $8\,\mathrm{s}$. At $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$ it is $13\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and at $t=5\,\mathrm{s}$ it is $25\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Calculate when it reaches $33\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}m&=\frac{25\,\mathrm{kPa}-13\,\mathrm{kPa}}{5\,\mathrm{s}-2\,\mathrm{s}}=\frac{12\,\mathrm{kPa}}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=4\,\mathrm{kPa/s},\\[6pt]q&=13\,\mathrm{kPa}-(4\,\mathrm{kPa/s})(2\,\mathrm{s})=13\,\mathrm{kPa}-8\,\mathrm{kPa}=5\,\mathrm{kPa},\\[6pt]p(t)&=5\,\mathrm{kPa}+(4\,\mathrm{kPa/s})t,\\[6pt]5\,\mathrm{kPa}+(4\,\mathrm{kPa/s})t&=33\,\mathrm{kPa},\\[6pt]t&=\frac{33\,\mathrm{kPa}-5\,\mathrm{kPa}}{4\,\mathrm{kPa/s}}=\frac{28\,\mathrm{kPa}}{4\,\mathrm{kPa/s}}=7\,\mathrm{s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relationship is affine, so its graph is a line and two measurements determine it. The slope is the change in pressure, $12\,\mathrm{kPa}$, divided by the elapsed time, $3\,\mathrm{s}$, giving $4\,\mathrm{kPa/s}$.

The intercept is the pressure at time zero. Starting at $t=2\,\mathrm{s}$, go back by $2\,\mathrm{s}$ and account for the pressure change of $(4\,\mathrm{kPa/s})(2\,\mathrm{s})=8\,\mathrm{kPa}$ over that time. Therefore $q=13\,\mathrm{kPa}-8\,\mathrm{kPa}=5\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the formula is $p(t)=5\,\mathrm{kPa}+(4\,\mathrm{kPa/s})t$.

To find the required time, set $p(t)=33\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Subtracting the intercept from both sides leaves $(4\,\mathrm{kPa/s})t=28\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Dividing by the slope cancels the kilopascals and leaves a time, $t=7\,\mathrm{s}$. This time is within the interval of validity $0\le t\le 8\,\mathrm{s}$, so the pressure reaches $33\,\mathrm{kPa}$ after **seven seconds**.

At the endpoint $t=8\,\mathrm{s}$, the formula gives $5\,\mathrm{kPa}+32\,\mathrm{kPa}=37\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The pressure increases from $5$ to $37\,\mathrm{kPa}$ over the interval of validity, so it does reach $33\,\mathrm{kPa}$, one second before the end.

</div>
