## Exercise 1

An object moves at a constant velocity of $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $5\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate its displacement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=(5\,\mathrm{s})(3\,\mathrm{m/s})=15\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta s&=(-3\,\mathrm{m/s})(5\,\mathrm{s})=-15\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
d&=\lvert\Delta s\rvert=15\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,-3,0,5" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6.5 ymin=-4.5 ymax=2 nomi="t,v" legenda="v=-3" area="0;-3;0;5" punti="0,-3;5,-3" segmenti="5,0,5,-3" titolo="Velocity $v$ in meters per second and time $t$ in seconds. The rectangle lies below the time axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

On a velocity–time graph a constant velocity gives a horizontal segment. Here the segment is at $v=-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $t$ between $0$ and $5\,\mathrm{s}$, so the region between the graph and the time axis is a rectangle below the axis.

The geometric area $S$ of the rectangle is its base times its height. The base is the elapsed time, $5\,\mathrm{s}$, and the height is the magnitude of the velocity, $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Seconds times meters per second gives meters, so $S=15\,\mathrm{m}$.

The displacement is the **signed area**. The rectangle lies below the axis, so it contributes negatively and $\Delta s=-15\,\mathrm{m}$. Multiplying the signed velocity directly by the elapsed time gives the same value.

The negative sign means that the object moves fifteen meters in the direction opposite to the chosen positive direction. The velocity does not change sign, so the object never reverses direction and the distance traveled is the absolute value of the displacement, $15\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The velocity increases linearly from $2$ to $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $4\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the displacement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta s&=\frac{v_1+v_2}{2}\,\Delta t=\frac{2\,\mathrm{m/s}+8\,\mathrm{m/s}}{2}(4\,\mathrm{s})=(5\,\mathrm{m/s})(4\,\mathrm{s})=20\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta s&=(2\,\mathrm{m/s})(4\,\mathrm{s})+\frac{(4\,\mathrm{s})(6\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=8\,\mathrm{m}+12\,\mathrm{m}=20\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="t,2+1.5*t,0,4" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=10 nomi="t,v" legenda="v=2+\dfrac{3}{2}t" punti="0,2;4,8" area="2+1.5*x;0;0;4" segmenti="0,2,4,2;4,0,4,8" titolo="Velocity $v$ in meters per second and time $t$ in seconds. The trapezoid splits into a rectangle of height 2 and a triangle of height 6."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity varies linearly, so the velocity–time graph is a line segment from $(0,2)$ to $(4,8)$. Both velocities are positive, so the region under the graph lies above the time axis and is a trapezoid with vertical bases.

The bases of the trapezoid are the two velocities, $2$ and $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and its height is the elapsed time, $4\,\mathrm{s}$. By the **formula for displacement when velocity varies linearly**, the displacement is the arithmetic mean of the two velocities, $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, multiplied by the elapsed time. This gives $\Delta s=20\,\mathrm{m}$.

The second line of the calculation divides the trapezoid along the dashed horizontal segment. Below that segment is a rectangle of base $4\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with area $8\,\mathrm{m}$. Above it is a triangle with the same base and height $8-2=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with area $12\,\mathrm{m}$. The two areas again add up to $20\,\mathrm{m}$.

The average velocity over the interval is $\dfrac{20\,\mathrm{m}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}=5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, exactly the mean of the velocities at its endpoints.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Between $t=1\,\mathrm{s}$ and $t=7\,\mathrm{s}$, the velocity varies linearly from $-2$ to $-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate the displacement and the distance traveled.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta t&=7\,\mathrm{s}-1\,\mathrm{s}=6\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]\frac{v_1+v_2}{2}&=\frac{-2\,\mathrm{m/s}+(-8\,\mathrm{m/s})}{2}=-5\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]\Delta s&=(-5\,\mathrm{m/s})(6\,\mathrm{s})=-30\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]d&=\lvert\Delta s\rvert=30\,\mathrm{m}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interval starts at $t=1\,\mathrm{s}$, so the elapsed time is the difference between the two times, $6\,\mathrm{s}$. Using $7\,\mathrm{s}$ as the duration would include the first second, for which the velocity is unspecified.

The velocity varies linearly, so the **formula for displacement when velocity varies linearly** applies. The arithmetic mean of the initial and final velocities is $-5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and multiplying this by the elapsed time gives $\Delta s=-30\,\mathrm{m}$. On the velocity–time graph the region is a trapezoid below the time axis, with bases $2$ and $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and height $6\,\mathrm{s}$, so its contribution to displacement is negative.

Both endpoint velocities are negative and the change is linear, so the velocity stays negative throughout the interval. The object keeps moving in the same direction and never turns back. Thus the distance traveled equals the absolute value of the displacement, $30\,\mathrm{m}$. The object moves in the negative direction while its speed increases from $2$ to $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>
