## Exercise 1

A table lists the points $(1,12)$, $(2,6)$, and $(4,3)$. Determine whether the data are consistent with $y=kx$, $y=\dfrac{k}{x}$, or $y=kx^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y=kx:&\qquad \frac{12}{1}=12,\qquad \frac{6}{2}=3,\qquad 12\ne 3,\\[6pt]
y=kx^2:&\qquad \frac{12}{1^2}=12,\qquad \frac{6}{2^2}=\frac{3}{2},\qquad 12\ne\frac{3}{2},\\[6pt]
y=\frac{k}{x}:&\qquad 1\cdot12=12,\qquad 2\cdot6=12,\qquad 4\cdot3=12,\\[6pt]
y&=\frac{12}{x}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="12/x" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=14 nomi="x,y" legenda="y=\dfrac{12}{x}" punti="1,12;2,6;4,3" titolo="The three points in the table lie on the branch of the hyperbola $y = \dfrac{12}{x}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each model predicts that a particular quantity has the same value at every point. For $y=kx$ it is the ratio $\dfrac{y}{x}$, for $y=kx^2$ it is the ratio $\dfrac{y}{x^2}$, and for $y=\dfrac{k}{x}$ it is the product $xy$. The horizontal coordinates are nonzero, so all these quantities are defined.

The ratio $\dfrac{y}{x}$ is $12$ at the first point and $3$ at the second. The two values differ, so no coefficient $k$ makes the direct proportion fit both points. Likewise, the ratio $\dfrac{y}{x^2}$ takes the values $12$ and $\dfrac{6}{4}=\dfrac{3}{2}$, so the quadratic model is also ruled out. To rule out a model, it is enough to find two points at which the quantity it predicts to be constant takes different values.

All three products $xy$ equal $12$. The points therefore fit the inverse proportion with coefficient $k=12$, giving the relationship $y=\dfrac{12}{x}$. The table also illustrates the scaling property of **inverse proportionality**. Doubling the horizontal coordinate from $1$ to $2$ and then from $2$ to $4$ halves the vertical coordinate each time.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A table lists $(1,2)$, $(2,8)$, and $(3,18)$. Check the relationship $y=kx^2$ and calculate the predicted value at $x=4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{2}{1^2}&=\frac{2}{1}=2,\qquad\frac{8}{2^2}=\frac{8}{4}=2,\qquad\frac{18}{3^2}=\frac{18}{9}=2,\\[6pt]k&=2,\qquad y=2x^2,\\[6pt]y(4)&=2\cdot4^2=2\cdot16=32,\\[6pt]\frac{8-2}{2-1}&=6,\qquad\frac{18-8}{3-2}=10.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the relationship $y=kx^2$, dividing both sides by $x^2$ for $x\ne 0$ gives $\dfrac{y}{x^2}=k$. To test it, divide each vertical coordinate by the square of its horizontal coordinate.

The squares of the horizontal coordinates are $1$, $4$, and $9$. The ratios $\dfrac{2}{1}$, $\dfrac{8}{4}$, and $\dfrac{18}{9}$ all give $2$, so the three points fit the **quadratic relationship** with coefficient $k=2$, namely $y=2x^2$.

To find the predicted value at $x=4$, substitute into the formula. The square of $4$ is $16$, so $y(4)=32$. This prediction is outside the measured range of horizontal coordinates. It is valid if the same relationship continues to hold up to $x=4$.

The last line of the calculation shows that the slopes between successive points are $6$ and $10$. The slopes differ, so the points do not lie on a straight line and an affine relationship does not fit the data. For this quadratic relationship, the increases in $y$ grow as $x$ increases, even for equal steps in the horizontal coordinate.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A liquid level is $9$, $15$, and $23\,\mathrm{cm}$ at times $1$, $4$, and $8\,\mathrm{s}$, respectively. Determine whether the data fit an affine relationship. If the relationship holds up to $10\,\mathrm{s}$, find the predicted level at that time.

$$
\begin{aligned}m_1&=\frac{15\,\mathrm{cm}-9\,\mathrm{cm}}{4\,\mathrm{s}-1\,\mathrm{s}}=\frac{6\,\mathrm{cm}}{3\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{cm/s},\\[6pt]m_2&=\frac{23\,\mathrm{cm}-15\,\mathrm{cm}}{8\,\mathrm{s}-4\,\mathrm{s}}=\frac{8\,\mathrm{cm}}{4\,\mathrm{s}}=2\,\mathrm{cm/s},\\[6pt]q&=9\,\mathrm{cm}-(2\,\mathrm{cm/s})(1\,\mathrm{s})=7\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]h(t)&=7\,\mathrm{cm}+(2\,\mathrm{cm/s})t,\\[6pt]h(10\,\mathrm{s})&=7\,\mathrm{cm}+(2\,\mathrm{cm/s})(10\,\mathrm{s})=7\,\mathrm{cm}+20\,\mathrm{cm}=27\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The intervals between measurements last $3$ and $4\,\mathrm{s}$, so the rises in level, $6$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, cannot be compared directly. Compare the slopes by dividing each rise by the corresponding elapsed time.

Both slopes equal $2\,\mathrm{cm/s}$. By the **condition for collinearity**, three points with distinct horizontal coordinates lie on the same line when the slopes between successive points are equal. Thus the data fit an affine relationship with slope $2\,\mathrm{cm/s}$.

The intercept is the level at $t=0$. From the first point, go back by $1\,\mathrm{s}$. The level changes by $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ over that time, so $q=7\,\mathrm{cm}$. The ratios of level to time are $9$, $\dfrac{15}{4}$, and $\dfrac{23}{8}$ in $\mathrm{cm/s}$. Since they differ, the relationship is affine and the level is not directly proportional to time.

The problem states that the relationship holds up to $10\,\mathrm{s}$, so we can use it to predict the level beyond the last measurement. Substituting $t=10\,\mathrm{s}$ gives a level of $27\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
