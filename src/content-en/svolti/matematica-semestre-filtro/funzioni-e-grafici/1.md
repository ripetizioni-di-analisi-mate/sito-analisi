## Exercise 1

Given $f(x)=4-3x$, calculate $f(-2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-2)&=4-3\cdot(-2)\\[6pt]
&=4-(-6)\\[6pt]
&=4+6\\[6pt]
&=10.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rule $f(x)=4-3x$ is a first-degree polynomial, so it is defined for every real number and $-2$ belongs to its domain. To calculate $f(-2)$, substitute $-2$ for every occurrence of $x$ in the rule.

The term $3x$ becomes the product $3\cdot(-2)$. One factor is positive and the other is negative, so the product equals $-6$. Subtracting $-6$ is equivalent to adding $6$, so $4-(-6)=4+6=10$.

The graph of $f$ therefore contains the point $(-2,10)$. Ignoring the sign of $-2$ in the substitution would give $4-3\cdot 2=-2$. This is the value of $f(2)$ and corresponds to a different point on the graph, $(2,-2)$. The slope $-3$ means that each unit increase in $x$ decreases $f$ by $3$. From $x=-2$ to $x=2$ the horizontal coordinate increases by $4$, so the vertical coordinate decreases by $12$, from $10$ to $-2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solution has concentrations $8$, $6$, and $3\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ at times $2$, $4$, and $7\,\mathrm{min}$, respectively. Use the data to find the concentration at $t=4\,\mathrm{min}$ and the measurement time at which it equals $3\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$.

| $t$ in minutes | $c$ in $\mathrm{mmol/L}$ |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 8 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 7 | 3 |

$$
\begin{aligned}c(4\,\mathrm{min})&=6\,\mathrm{mmol/L},\\[6pt] c(7\,\mathrm{min})&=3\,\mathrm{mmol/L}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=7\,\mathrm{min}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.5 xmax=8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=10 nomi="t,c" punti="2,8;4,6;7,3" segmenti="4,0,4,6;0,6,4,6;7,0,7,3;0,3,7,3" titolo="The three measurements of concentration $c$ in mmol/L at time $t$ in minutes. The dashed lines show how to read the concentration at $t = 4$ and work backward from $c = 3$ to find the time."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each measurement is an ordered pair $(t,c)$, in which time is the independent variable and concentration is the dependent variable. The three pairs correspond to points $(2,8)$, $(4,6)$, and $(7,3)$ on the graph of concentration versus time.

The first part gives the time $t=4\,\mathrm{min}$. It is the first coordinate of the second pair, so the required concentration is the second coordinate of that pair, $6\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$. This is a **direct reading**, since we start with the horizontal coordinate and read the vertical coordinate.

The second part gives the concentration $3\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$. This value appears only in the third pair, so the measurement time is $t=7\,\mathrm{min}$. This is a **reverse reading**, since we start with the vertical coordinate and find the horizontal coordinate.

The question asks for a time at which the concentration was *measured*. A value such as $5\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ does not appear in the table, so determining a time for it would require an assumption about how the concentration changes between $4$ and $7\,\mathrm{min}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $f(u)=\sqrt{9-u^2}$, determine the real domain and calculate $f(-2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}9-u^2&\ge 0,\\[6pt] u^2&\le 9,\\[6pt] |u|&\le 3,\\[6pt] D&=[-3,3],\\[6pt] f(-2)&=\sqrt{9-(-2)^2}=\sqrt{9-4}=\sqrt{5}.\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="3*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,3.141592653589793" legenda="y=\sqrt{9-u^2}" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=4 nomi="u,y" uguale=1 punti="-3,0;3,0;-2,2.236,pieno" segmenti="-2,0,-2,2.236" titolo="The domain is the interval $[-3,3]$. At its endpoints the radicand is zero, so the function is zero. The point above $u = -2$ has vertical coordinate $\sqrt{5}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

A real square root is defined only when the radicand is nonnegative. The domain is therefore the solution set of the inequality $9-u^2\ge 0$.

Moving $u^2$ to the right-hand side gives $u^2\le 9$. Since $\sqrt{u^2}=|u|$ and $\sqrt{9}=3$, the inequality is equivalent to $|u|\le 3$, or $-3\le u\le 3$. The endpoints are included because at $u=\pm 3$ the radicand is zero and $\sqrt{0}=0$ is a real number. The domain is therefore the closed interval $[-3,3]$.

The value $-2$ is in the domain, so it can be substituted into the formula. The square of a negative number is positive, so $(-2)^2=4$ and the radicand is $9-4=5$. This gives $f(-2)=\sqrt{5}$, approximately $2.24$.

The variable $u$ appears only as a square, so $f(2)=f(-2)$ and the graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis. It is the semicircle centered at the origin with radius $3$ that lies in the half-plane $y\ge 0$, because $y=\sqrt{9-u^2}$ is equivalent to $u^2+y^2=9$ with $y\ge 0$.

</div>
