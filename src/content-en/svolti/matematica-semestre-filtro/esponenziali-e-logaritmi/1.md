## Exercise 1

Solve $5^{2x-1}=125$.

$$
125=5\cdot5\cdot5=5^3
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{2x-1}&=5^3,\\[6pt]
2x-1&=3,\\[6pt]
2x&=4,\\[6pt]
x&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown appears only in the exponent on the left-hand side. The right-hand side $125$ is positive. This is necessary because every power with a positive base is positive. Express one hundred twenty-five as a power of five, since $125=5\cdot5\cdot5=5^3$. The equation then equates two powers with the same base $5$.

Since the base-five exponential function is strictly increasing, equal powers must have the same exponent. Indeed, a strictly increasing function takes each value only once, so $5^{2x-1}=5^3$ implies $2x-1=3$.

Solve the resulting linear equation by adding $1$ to both sides, which gives $2x=4$, and then dividing by $2$. The equation **has the unique solution** $x=2$. For this value, the exponent $2x-1$ equals $3$ and the left-hand side is indeed $5^3=125$.

We can compare the exponents because both sides can be written as powers of the same base. With a right-hand side such as $120$, which is not an integer power of $5$, we would use a logarithm and obtain $2x-1=\log_5 120$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\log_{\frac12}8$.

$$
\log_{\frac12}8=u\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left(\frac12\right)^u=8
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac12\right)^u&=8,\\[6pt]
\left(2^{-1}\right)^u&=2^3,\\[6pt]
2^{-u}&=2^3,\\[6pt]
-u&=3,\\[6pt]
u&=-3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By definition, we seek the exponent to which one-half must be raised to obtain eight. Denoting this exponent by $u$ gives the equation $\left(\dfrac12\right)^u=8$. The base $\dfrac12$ is positive and different from $1$, and the argument $8$ is positive, so the logarithm is defined.

Express one-half as the reciprocal of two, namely $\dfrac12=2^{-1}$, and write $8=2^3$. When raising a power to a power, multiply the exponents, so $\left(2^{-1}\right)^u=2^{-u}$. Then compare the exponents of the powers of two. Since $y=2^x$ is strictly increasing, the equality $2^{-u}=2^3$ implies $-u=3$, and therefore **the logarithm equals** $-3$.

The result is negative because the base lies between zero and one and the argument is greater than one. Indeed, a power of $\dfrac12$ with a positive exponent is less than $1$, whereas obtaining a number greater than $1$ requires a negative exponent. The exponent $u=-3$ gives precisely $\left(\dfrac12\right)^{-3}=2^3=8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $4^{x+1}=8^{2x-1}$.

$$
4=2^2,\qquad 8=2^3
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(2^2\right)^{x+1}&=\left(2^3\right)^{2x-1},\\[6pt]
2^{2(x+1)}&=2^{3(2x-1)},\\[6pt]
2x+2&=6x-3,\\[6pt]
2+3&=6x-2x,\\[6pt]
5&=4x,\\[6pt]
x&=\frac54.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4^(x+1);8^(2*x-1)" xmin=-1 xmax=2 ymin=-2 ymax=30 punti="1.25,22.627" segmenti="1.25,0,1.25,22.627" legenda="y=4^{x+1};y=8^{2x-1}" titolo="The two curves intersect at just one point, with horizontal coordinate $\dfrac54$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Four and eight are powers of two, since $4=2^2$ and $8=2^3$. Substitute these expressions on the two sides. Raising a power to a power multiplies the exponents, so the left-hand side becomes $2^{2(x+1)}$ and the right-hand side becomes $2^{3(2x-1)}$.

Both sides therefore have the same base, so equate the two new exponents. This is valid because $y=2^x$ is strictly increasing and therefore takes each value only once. The common base $2$ is the smallest integer base of which both $4$ and $8$ are integer powers.

Expanding the products gives the linear equation $2x+2=6x-3$. Collecting the unknowns on the same side gives the solution. Move $-3$ to the left and $2x$ to the right, changing their signs, to obtain $5=4x$. Dividing by $4$ gives $x=\dfrac54$, which is **the unique solution**.

On the graph the two exponential curves meet at a single point, with horizontal coordinate $\dfrac54$ and vertical coordinate $4^{\frac94}=2^{\frac92}\approx22.6$.

</div>
