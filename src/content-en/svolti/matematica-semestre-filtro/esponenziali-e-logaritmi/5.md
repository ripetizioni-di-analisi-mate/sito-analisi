## Exercise 1

Solve $5\cdot3^{x-2}=35$ and give two consecutive integers between which the solution lies.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5\cdot3^{x-2}&=35,\\[6pt]
3^{x-2}&=\frac{35}{5}=7,\\[6pt]
x-2&=\log_3 7,\\[6pt]
x&=2+\log_3 7.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
3^1<7<3^2\ \Longrightarrow\ 1<\log_3 7<2\ \Longrightarrow\ 3<x<4.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolate the power by dividing both sides by five to obtain $3^{x-2}=7$. The number $7$ is not an integer power of $3$, so we cannot directly equate exponents. Instead, apply the definition of a logarithm, according to which $3^u=7$ is equivalent to $u=\log_3 7$, with $u=x-2$. The argument seven is positive, so the base-three logarithm is defined. Adding $2$ gives the exact solution $x=2+\log_3 7$.

To locate the solution, compare $7$ with powers of $3$. Seven lies between three and nine, or between $3^1$ and $3^2$. Since the base-three logarithm is increasing, the logarithm of seven lies between one and two. Adding $2$ to all three parts of the inequality gives $3<x<4$.

The solution **lies between the consecutive integers** $3$ and $4$. A calculator gives $\log_3 7=\dfrac{\ln7}{\ln3}\approx1.77$, so $x\approx3.77$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\log_2(x-1)-\log_2(x-4)=2$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x-1>0,\\
x-4>0
\end{cases}
\Longleftrightarrow\ x>4,\qquad D=(4,+\infty)
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2\left(\frac{x-1}{x-4}\right)&=2,\\[6pt]
\frac{x-1}{x-4}&=2^2=4,\\[6pt]
x-1&=4(x-4),\\[6pt]
x-1&=4x-16,\\[6pt]
15&=3x,\\[6pt]
x&=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both arguments must be positive, so we work with values greater than four. Indeed, the system requires both $x>1$ and $x>4$, and numbers greater than $4$ also satisfy the first condition.

On the domain, the difference of the logarithms becomes the logarithm of the quotient by the rule $\log_a x-\log_a y=\log_a\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)$ with positive $x$ and $y$. By the definition of a logarithm, $\log_2 w=2$ is equivalent to $w=2^2=4$. On the domain the denominator is positive, so we can multiply both sides by it to obtain $x-1=4x-16$. Moving the terms containing $x$ to the right and the constants to the left gives $15=3x$, so $x=5$.

The value five lies in the domain and makes the two arguments four and one. Therefore $\log_2 4-\log_2 1=2-0=2$, and the equation **has the unique solution** $x=5$. Checking the domain is necessary because combining the logarithms into a logarithm of a quotient requires positive arguments. Solving without considering the domain could yield inadmissible values.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A quantity in arbitrary units is given by $Q(t)=18+54\left(\dfrac13\right)^{\frac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}}$ for $t\ge0$. Find the time at which $Q(t)=20$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
18+54\left(\frac13\right)^{\frac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}}&=20,\\[6pt]
54\left(\frac13\right)^{\frac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}}&=2,\\[6pt]
\left(\frac13\right)^{\frac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}}&=\frac{2}{54}=\frac1{27}=\left(\frac13\right)^3,\\[6pt]
\frac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}&=3,\\[6pt]
t&=6\,\mathrm h.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="18+54*(1/3)^(x/2)" xmin=-0.5 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=80 nomi="t,Q" asintoti="y=18" punti="0,72;6,20" segmenti="6,0,6,20" legenda="Q(t)=18+54\left(\dfrac13\right)^{\frac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}}" titolo="Time $t$ is in hours. The quantity starts at $72$, equals $20$ for $t=6$, and approaches the level $18$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the equilibrium value of eighteen to isolate the excess, the part $Q(t)-18$ that decays exponentially. The equation becomes $54\left(\dfrac13\right)^{\frac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}}=2$, and the excess must decrease from $54$ to $2$.

Dividing by fifty-four gives the remaining fraction of the initial excess, which is $\dfrac2{54}=\dfrac1{27}$. This fraction is the cube of one-third, since $27=3^3$. The function $y=\left(\dfrac13\right)^u$ is strictly decreasing, so it takes each value only once and the exponents must be equal, giving $\dfrac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}=3$.

The ratio $\dfrac{t}{2\,\mathrm h}$ counts two-hour intervals. Thus three two-hour intervals have elapsed and the quantity equals $20$ **after** $6\,\mathrm h$. Initially the quantity equals $18+54=72$. The line $Q=18$ is a horizontal asymptote because the excess is positive at every finite time and approaches zero. Therefore any requested value less than or equal to $18$ would never be reached.

</div>
