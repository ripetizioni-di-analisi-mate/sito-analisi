## Exercise 1

A sound intensity is one-hundredth of the reference intensity. Calculate its level in decibels.

$$
\frac{I}{I_0}=\frac{1}{100}=10^{-2}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
L&=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I}{I_0}\right)\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&=10\log_{10}\left(10^{-2}\right)\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&=10\cdot(-2)\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&=-20\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sound intensity level is defined by $L=10\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{I}{I_0}\right)\,\mathrm{dB}$, with $I$ and $I_0$ expressed in the same units. The problem gives the ratio $\dfrac{I}{I_0}=\dfrac1{100}$ directly. It is dimensionless, so the calculation is independent of the unit of measurement.

The ratio is a power of ten with exponent negative two, since $100=10^2$ and the reciprocal of $10^2$ is $10^{-2}$. By the definition of the logarithm, $\log_{10}\left(10^{-2}\right)=-2$, and multiplying by $10$ gives $-20\,\mathrm{dB}$.

The level is negative because the intensity is below the reference and the base-ten logarithm of a number between $0$ and $1$ is negative. The intensity itself is positive. The level **equals** $-20\,\mathrm{dB}$, or twenty decibels below the reference level, which corresponds to $0\,\mathrm{dB}$. With the conventional reference $I_0=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, the intensity would be $\dfrac{10^{-12}}{100}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}=10^{-14}\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, a positive value with a negative level.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The sound intensity level changes from $52\,\mathrm{dB}$ to $72\,\mathrm{dB}$ relative to the same reference. Calculate the ratio of final to initial intensity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_2-L_1&=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I_2}{I_0}\right)\mathrm{dB}-10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I_1}{I_0}\right)\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I_2}{I_1}\right)\,\mathrm{dB},\\[6pt]
L_2-L_1&=72\,\mathrm{dB}-52\,\mathrm{dB}=20\,\mathrm{dB},\\[6pt]
10\log_{10}\left(\frac{I_2}{I_1}\right)&=20,\\[6pt]
\log_{10}\left(\frac{I_2}{I_1}\right)&=2,\\[6pt]
\frac{I_2}{I_1}&=10^2=100.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="10*x" xmin=-0.5 xmax=8.5 ymin=0 ymax=85 nomi="z,L (dB)" punti="5.2,52;7.2,72" segmenti="5.2,0,5.2,52;7.2,0,7.2,72" legenda="L=10z\,\mathrm{dB}" titolo="With $z=\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{I}{I_0}\right)$, an increase of twenty decibels corresponds to an increase of two units in $z$, or a factor of one hundred in intensity."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the two levels using the definition and the same reference $I_0$. The common reference cancels when we subtract the levels, since the difference of two logarithms is the logarithm of the quotient and $\dfrac{I_2}{I_0}\cdot\dfrac{I_0}{I_1}=\dfrac{I_2}{I_1}$.

The level difference is $72-52=20\,\mathrm{dB}$. Dividing the difference by ten decibels gives the logarithm of the intensity ratio, which equals $2$. The base-ten exponential therefore gives the ratio, since $\log_{10}y=2$ is equivalent to $y=10^2$.

The final intensity **is one hundred times** the initial intensity. On the graph the two levels correspond to $z=5.2$ and $z=7.2$. The result depends only on the difference in levels, so an increase from $30$ to $50\,\mathrm{dB}$ would also correspond to a hundredfold increase in intensity. Each increase of $10\,\mathrm{dB}$ multiplies the intensity by $10$, so two successive increases of $10\,\mathrm{dB}$ multiply it by $10\cdot10=100$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At the measurement point, two incoherent sources separately produce levels of $50\,\mathrm{dB}$ and $60\,\mathrm{dB}$ relative to the same $I_0$. Their intensities add. Calculate the resulting level using $\log_{10}(1.1)\approx0.0414$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{I_1}{I_0}&=10^{\frac{50}{10}}=10^5,\\[6pt]
\frac{I_2}{I_0}&=10^{\frac{60}{10}}=10^6,\\[6pt]
\frac{I_{\mathrm{tot}}}{I_0}&=10^5+10^6=0.1\cdot10^6+10^6=1.1\cdot10^6,\\[6pt]
L_{\mathrm{tot}}&=10\log_{10}\left(1.1\cdot10^6\right)\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&=10\left[\log_{10}(1.1)+6\right]\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&\approx10\cdot6.0414\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&\approx60.41\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two levels give the ratios of the individual intensities to the reference. The definition $L=10\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{I}{I_0}\right)\,\mathrm{dB}$ gives $\dfrac{I}{I_0}=10^{\frac{L}{10\,\mathrm{dB}}}$, so $50\,\mathrm{dB}$ corresponds to $10^5$ and $60\,\mathrm{dB}$ to $10^6$.

Since the sources are incoherent, add the intensities and take the logarithm of the total. To factor out the power of ten, write $10^5=0.1\cdot10^6$, so the sum becomes $1.1\cdot10^6$. The logarithm of the product separates the coefficient $1.1$ from the power $10^6$, whose logarithm is $6$. Using the given value of $\log_{10}(1.1)$ gives $10\cdot6.0414\approx60.41\,\mathrm{dB}$.

The resulting level **is approximately** $60.41\,\mathrm{dB}$. The weaker source raises the level by about four-tenths of a decibel because its intensity is one-tenth that of the other source. The sum of the two levels, $110\,\mathrm{dB}$, would correspond to the ratio $10^5\cdot10^6=10^{11}$, the product of the relative intensities, whereas the intensities of incoherent sources add.

</div>
