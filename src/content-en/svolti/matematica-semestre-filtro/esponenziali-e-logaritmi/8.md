## Exercise 1

A dilute ideal solution of a strong monoprotic acid with $\mathrm{pH}=2.8$ is diluted from $5\,\mathrm{mL}$ to $500\,\mathrm{mL}$. Assume complete dissociation and a negligible contribution from water before and after dilution. Estimate the final pH.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{\mathrm i}V_{\mathrm i}&=c_{\mathrm f}V_{\mathrm f},\\[6pt]
\frac{c_{\mathrm f}}{c_{\mathrm i}}&=\frac{V_{\mathrm i}}{V_{\mathrm f}}=\frac{5\,\mathrm{mL}}{500\,\mathrm{mL}}=10^{-2},\\[6pt]
\mathrm{pH}_{\mathrm f}-\mathrm{pH}_{\mathrm i}&\approx-\log_{10}\left(\frac{c_{\mathrm f}}{c_{\mathrm i}}\right)=-\log_{10}\left(10^{-2}\right)=2,\\[6pt]
\mathrm{pH}_{\mathrm f}&\approx2.8+2\\[6pt]
&=4.8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dilution preserves the amount of acid, or number of moles, equal to the concentration times the volume. Under the assumptions of the problem, complete dissociation and a negligible contribution from water, the hydronium concentration equals the acid concentration. The ratio of the hydronium concentrations is therefore the reciprocal of the ratio of the volumes and equals $\dfrac{5}{500}=\dfrac1{100}=10^{-2}$.

Subtract the two pH values and use the logarithm of a quotient as in the relation $\mathrm{pH}_2-\mathrm{pH}_1=-\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{a_2}{a_1}\right)$. Since $\log_{10}\left(10^{-2}\right)=-2$, the difference in pH is $+2$. The pH rises by two units because the concentration falls by two orders of magnitude.

The final pH **is approximately** $4.8$. The dilution factor suffices for this calculation because the pH depends on the logarithm of the concentration. The final value is still far from $7$, so the assumption about the contribution from water is plausible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point source emits sound uniformly, with no absorption or reflection. The measured level is $L_1=64\,\mathrm{dB}$ at $r_1=2\,\mathrm m$. Estimate the level at $r_2=8\,\mathrm m$ using $\log_{10}2\approx0.3010$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{I_2}{I_1}&=\left(\frac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2=\left(\frac{2\,\mathrm m}{8\,\mathrm m}\right)^2=\frac1{16},\\[6pt]
L_2-L_1&=10\log_{10}\left(\frac1{16}\right)\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&=-10\log_{10}\left(2^4\right)\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&=-40\log_{10}2\,\mathrm{dB}\\[6pt]
&\approx-40\cdot0.3010\,\mathrm{dB}=-12.04\,\mathrm{dB},\\[6pt]
L_2&\approx64\,\mathrm{dB}-12.04\,\mathrm{dB}=51.96\,\mathrm{dB}.
\end{aligned}
$$

From $2$ to $8$ meters, the distance doubles twice and the level falls by approximately $12$ dB."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Under the stated conditions the intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance, so $I=\dfrac{C}{r^2}$ with constant $C$. Taking the ratio of the two intensities cancels the constant, leaving $\left(\dfrac{r_1}{r_2}\right)^2$. The distance quadruples, so the intensity falls to one-sixteenth of its initial value.

The logarithm of the intensity ratio determines how much the level decreases, since the difference between the levels relative to the same reference is $10\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{I_2}{I_1}\right)\,\mathrm{dB}$. Write $\dfrac1{16}=2^{-4}$ and use the power rule to obtain $-40\log_{10}2\,\mathrm{dB}$. With the given value of $\log_{10}2$, the change is approximately $-12.04\,\mathrm{dB}$.

At eight meters the level **is approximately** $51.96\,\mathrm{dB}$. From $2$ to $8\,\mathrm m$, the distance doubles twice, and each doubling reduces the level by $20\log_{10}2\approx6.02\,\mathrm{dB}$. Indeed, $2\cdot6.02=12.04$, which agrees with the calculated change. The coefficient $20$ comes from the square of the distance in the intensity law.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two quantities measured in the same units follow $A(t)=144\left(\dfrac12\right)^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}$ and $B(t)=18\cdot2^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}$ for $t\ge0$. Find the time at which they are equal and their common value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
144\left(\frac12\right)^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}&=18\cdot2^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}},\\[6pt]
144\cdot2^{-\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}&=18\cdot2^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}},\\[6pt]
\frac{144}{18}&=2^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}\cdot2^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}},\\[6pt]
2^{\frac{2t}{3\,\mathrm h}}&=8=2^3,\\[6pt]
\frac{2t}{3\,\mathrm h}&=3,\\[6pt]
t&=\frac92\,\mathrm h,\\[6pt]
B\left(\frac92\,\mathrm h\right)&=18\cdot2^{\frac32}=18\cdot2\sqrt2=36\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="144*(1/2)^(x/3);18*2^(x/3)" xmin=-0.5 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=160 nomi="t,Q" punti="4.5,50.9117" segmenti="4.5,0,4.5,50.9117" legenda="A(t)=144\left(\dfrac12\right)^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}};B(t)=18\cdot2^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}" titolo="Time $t$ is in hours. The two curves intersect at $t=\dfrac92$, with common value $36\sqrt2\approx50.9$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Equate the two expressions and write $\left(\dfrac12\right)^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}$ as $2^{-\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}$ so that the powers have the same base. Divide both sides by $18$ and by the power $2^{-\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}$, which is positive. Dividing by $2^{-\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $2^{\frac{t}{3\,\mathrm h}}$. The left-hand side reduces to $\dfrac{144}{18}=8$. On the right, multiplying two powers with the same base adds their exponents, giving $\dfrac{2t}{3\,\mathrm h}$.

Since the two remaining powers have base two and $8=2^3$, we can equate their exponents because $y=2^x$ is strictly increasing. The equation $\dfrac{2t}{3\,\mathrm h}=3$ gives $t=\dfrac92\,\mathrm h$.

Substituting the time into either model gives the common value. In the model $B$ the exponent is $\dfrac92\cdot\dfrac13=\dfrac32$ and $2^{\frac32}=2\sqrt2$. The two quantities **are equal after** $\dfrac92\,\mathrm h$, and their common value is $36\sqrt2\approx50.9$ in the shared unit.

</div>
