## Exercise 1

A sufficiently dilute, ideal aqueous solution has $c=[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]=2\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. Estimate the pH using $\log_{10}2\approx0.30$.

$$
\frac{c}{c^\circ}=\frac{2\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol/L}}{1\,\mathrm{mol/L}}=2\cdot10^{-4}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{pH}&\approx -\log_{10}\left(2\cdot10^{-4}\right)\\[6pt]
&=-\left[\log_{10}2+\log_{10}\left(10^{-4}\right)\right]\\[6pt]
&=-\left(\log_{10}2-4\right)\\[6pt]
&=4-\log_{10}2\\[6pt]
&\approx4-0.30\\[6pt]
&=3.70.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Divide the concentration by $c^\circ=1\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ to obtain a dimensionless ratio, because the logarithm takes a pure number as its argument. The ratio is $2\cdot10^{-4}$, or $0.0002$. In a dilute ideal solution the activity approximately equals this ratio, so the pH can be calculated using the approximate formula in the definition.

The logarithm of a product separates the coefficient from the power of ten. The second term equals $\log_{10}\left(10^{-4}\right)=-4$ by the definition of a logarithm. The minus sign changes the signs of both contributions, giving $4-\log_{10}2$.

With $\log_{10}2\approx0.30$, the pH **is approximately** $3.70$. The value lies between $3$ and $4$, since the concentration lies between $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. The coefficient $2$ in front of $10^{-4}$ lowers the pH by $\log_{10}2$ from the value $4$, because doubling the concentration gives a more acidic solution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A dilute ideal aqueous solution has $\mathrm{pH}=4.30$. Estimate $[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]$ using $\log_{10}2\approx0.30$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{10}\left(\frac{c}{c^\circ}\right)&\approx-4.30,\\[6pt]
\frac{c}{c^\circ}&\approx10^{-4.30}\\[6pt]
&=10^{-4}\cdot10^{-0.30}\\[6pt]
&\approx10^{-4}\cdot\frac12\\[6pt]
&=5\cdot10^{-5},\\[6pt]
c&\approx5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol/L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the definition $\mathrm{pH}\approx-\log_{10}\left(\dfrac{c}{c^\circ}\right)$, the base-ten logarithm of the ratio is approximately $-4.30$. To invert the relation, raise ten to the negative of the pH, since $\log_{10}y=z$ is equivalent to $y=10^z$.

The exponent $-4.30$ is the sum of $-4$ and $-0.30$. When two powers of the same base are multiplied, their exponents add, so $10^{-4.30}=10^{-4}\cdot10^{-0.30}$. The factor with exponent negative 0.30 is approximately the reciprocal of two. Indeed, $\log_{10}2\approx0.30$ means $10^{0.30}\approx2$, so $10^{-0.30}\approx\dfrac12$.

The ratio is therefore $\dfrac{10^{-4}}{2}=0.5\cdot10^{-4}=5\cdot10^{-5}$. Multiplying by $c^\circ$ gives **a concentration of approximately** $5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. This value lies between ten to the power of negative five and ten to the power of negative four moles per liter, just as the pH lies between $4$ and $5$. The result is consistent with the preceding exercise because a pH higher by $0.6$ units corresponds to a concentration divided by $10^{0.6}\approx4$, and indeed $\dfrac{2\cdot10^{-4}}{5\cdot10^{-5}}=4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two samples have $\mathrm{pH}_A=3.6$ and $\mathrm{pH}_B=4.6$. Calculate the ratio of the hydrogen ion activity in sample A to that in sample B.

$$
a_A=10^{-\mathrm{pH}_A}=10^{-3.6},\qquad a_B=10^{-\mathrm{pH}_B}=10^{-4.6}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a_A}{a_B}&=\frac{10^{-3.6}}{10^{-4.6}}\\[6pt]
&=10^{-3.6-(-4.6)}\\[6pt]
&=10^{\mathrm{pH}_B-\mathrm{pH}_A}\\[6pt]
&=10^{1}=10.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-x" xmin=-5.6 xmax=0.8 ymin=0 ymax=6 nomi="z,pH" punti="-3.6,3.6,pieno,A;-4.6,4.6,pieno,B" segmenti="-3.6,0,-3.6,3.6;-4.6,0,-4.6,4.6" legenda="\mathrm{pH}=-z" titolo="With $z=\log_{10}a$, the two samples are one horizontal unit apart, corresponding to a factor of ten in activity."}

<div class="spiegazione">

We can obtain the activity from the definition $\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10}a$, since $\log_{10}a=-\mathrm{pH}$ is equivalent to $a=10^{-\mathrm{pH}}$. The activity corresponding to each pH is therefore given by ten raised to the negative of that pH, giving $a_A=10^{-3.6}$ and $a_B=10^{-4.6}$.

When dividing powers with the same base, subtract the exponents. The resulting exponent is $-3.6+4.6=1$, which is the difference $\mathrm{pH}_B-\mathrm{pH}_A$. Since the pH of A is one unit lower, its activity is ten times that of B, and the ratio **equals** $10$.

Only the difference between the pH values is needed to find the ratio, so there is no need to calculate the individual activities. On the graph the horizontal coordinate is $z=\log_{10}a$, and points A and B are one horizontal unit apart, corresponding to a factor of ten between the activities. The sample with the lower pH is more acidic.

</div>
