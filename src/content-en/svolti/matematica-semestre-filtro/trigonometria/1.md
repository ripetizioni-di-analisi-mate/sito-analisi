## Exercise 1

A right triangle has legs of lengths $9\,\mathrm{m}$ and $12\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the length of its hypotenuse.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c^2&=a^2+b^2\\[6pt]
&=(9^2+12^2)\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
&=(81+144)\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
&=225\,\mathrm{m^2},\\[6pt]
c&=\sqrt{225}\,\mathrm{m}=15\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
9&=3\cdot3,\qquad 12=3\cdot4,\qquad 15=3\cdot5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two given sides are the legs, which form the right angle. The third side is therefore the hypotenuse. Since the triangle is right, we can use the **Pythagorean theorem** to find it.

The squares of the leg lengths are $81\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $144\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the square of the hypotenuse is $225\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The unit is the square meter because the two lengths in meters have been squared.

The equation $c^2=225\,\mathrm{m^2}$ has two solutions, $15\,\mathrm{m}$ and $-15\,\mathrm{m}$. A length is positive, so only the positive root is retained.

The second line shows that the three side lengths are $3$, $4$, and $5$ multiplied by $3$. The triangle is therefore similar to the triangle with sides $3$, $4$, and $5$, which checks the result without repeating the calculation. The hypotenuse has length **$15\,\mathrm{m}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has a base of $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a diagonal of $50\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate its height and area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^2&=b^2+h^2,\\[6pt]
h^2&=d^2-b^2\\[6pt]
&=(50^2-40^2)\,\mathrm{cm^2}\\[6pt]
&=(2500-1600)\,\mathrm{cm^2}\\[6pt]
&=900\,\mathrm{cm^2},\\[6pt]
h&=\sqrt{900}\,\mathrm{cm}=30\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
A&=b\cdot h=40\,\mathrm{cm}\cdot30\,\mathrm{cm}
=1200\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.6 xmax=4.9 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.5 linee="0,0 4,0 4,3 0,3 0,0;0,0 4,3;3.75,0 3.75,0.25 4,0.25" etichette="2,-0.3,40 cm;4.25,1.5,h;1.55,1.65,50 cm" titolo="The diagonal is the hypotenuse of the triangle whose legs are the base and the height $h$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The angles of a rectangle are right angles. Its diagonal therefore divides it into two congruent right triangles, each with the base and height as legs and the diagonal as hypotenuse.

By the **Pythagorean theorem**, the square of the diagonal is the sum of the squares of the base and height. The unknown is a leg, so isolate $h^2$ by subtracting $b^2$ from both sides. The difference $2500-1600$ equals $900$, so $h^2=900\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, and taking the positive square root gives $h=30\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The area of the rectangle is its base times its height. Both lengths are in centimeters, so the area is in square centimeters.

The height is **$30\,\mathrm{cm}$** and the area is **$1200\,\mathrm{cm^2}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A triangle has side lengths $7\,\mathrm{cm}$, $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine whether it is a right triangle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
7^2+8^2&=49+64=113,\\[6pt]
10^2&=100,\\[6pt]
7^2+8^2&\ne 10^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
10^2&<7^2+8^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is opposite the largest angle, the right angle, and is therefore the longest side. If this triangle were right, its hypotenuse would be the side of length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Compare the square of the longest side with the sum of the squares of the other two. The sum is $113$ and the square of the longest side is $100$, so the Pythagorean equality does not hold.

The Pythagorean theorem and its converse state that a triangle is right if and only if the square of its longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. Here the equality fails, so the triangle **is not a right triangle**.

The inequality $100<113$ tells us more. The side opposite the largest angle is shorter than it would be if the sides of lengths $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ met at a right angle. Thus this angle is less than $90^\circ$ and the triangle is acute.

</div>
