## Exercise 1

Calculate $\cos225^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
180^\circ&<225^\circ<270^\circ,\\[6pt]
225^\circ-180^\circ&=45^\circ,\\[6pt]
\cos225^\circ&=-\cos45^\circ\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\sin225^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},
\qquad
\cos^2225^\circ+\sin^2225^\circ=\frac{2}{4}+\frac{2}{4}=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the unit circle, the cosine of an angle is the horizontal coordinate of the point $P$ where its terminal side meets the circle. To find it, first identify the quadrant and then determine the reference angle.

The angle $225^\circ$ lies between $180^\circ$ and $270^\circ$, so the point $P$ is in the third quadrant. The angle between the radius $OP$ and the negative $x$-axis is $225^\circ-180^\circ=45^\circ$. Thus the point $P$ is the reflection across the origin of the point at $45^\circ$. Its coordinates have the same magnitudes as those of the point at $45^\circ$ but opposite signs.

The magnitude of the cosine is therefore $\cos45^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$. The horizontal coordinate is negative in the third quadrant, so take the negative of that value.

The second line checks the result with the Pythagorean identity, since the sum of the squares of the coordinates is $1$.

The result is $\cos225^\circ=-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, or approximately $-0.707$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\tan300^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
270^\circ&<300^\circ<360^\circ,\\[6pt]
360^\circ-300^\circ&=60^\circ,\\[6pt]
\sin300^\circ&=-\sin60^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\\[6pt]
\cos300^\circ&=\cos60^\circ=\frac{1}{2},\\[6pt]
\tan300^\circ&=\frac{\sin300^\circ}{\cos300^\circ}
=\frac{-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\cdot2
=-\sqrt{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Tangent is the ratio of sine to cosine, defined whenever the cosine is nonzero. First find the two coordinates of the point at $300^\circ$ on the unit circle.

The angle $300^\circ$ lies between $270^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, so it lies in the fourth quadrant. The radius must rotate another $360^\circ-300^\circ=60^\circ$ to return to the positive $x$-axis, so the reference angle is $60^\circ$. The point at $300^\circ$ is the reflection across the $x$-axis of the point at $60^\circ$. Thus it has the same horizontal coordinate and the opposite vertical coordinate.

In the fourth quadrant the horizontal coordinate is positive and the vertical coordinate is negative. The sine is therefore $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and the cosine is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, which is nonzero.

Dividing by one-half is equivalent to multiplying by $2$, so the ratio is $-\sqrt{3}$. Its negative sign agrees with the quadrant because the coordinates have opposite signs.

The result is $\tan300^\circ=-\sqrt{3}$, or approximately $-1.73$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An angle $\theta$ lies in the fourth quadrant and satisfies $\sin\theta=-\dfrac{8}{17}$. Calculate $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos^2\theta
&=1-\sin^2\theta\\[6pt]
&=1-\frac{64}{289}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{289-64}{289}
=\frac{225}{289},\\[6pt]
\cos\theta&=+\sqrt{\frac{225}{289}}=\frac{15}{17},\\[6pt]
\tan\theta
&=\frac{-\frac{8}{17}}{\frac{15}{17}}
=-\frac{8}{17}\cdot\frac{17}{15}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{8}{15}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sine and the quadrant are given. By the **Pythagorean identity**, the square of the cosine is $1-\sin^2\theta$. The square of the sine is $\dfrac{64}{289}$, and writing $1$ as $\dfrac{289}{289}$ gives $\cos^2\theta=\dfrac{225}{289}$.

The equation $\cos^2\theta=\dfrac{225}{289}$ has two solutions, $\dfrac{15}{17}$ and $-\dfrac{15}{17}$, because $225=15^2$ and $289=17^2$. The choice depends on the quadrant. In the fourth quadrant the horizontal coordinate is positive, so $\cos\theta=\dfrac{15}{17}$.

The tangent is the sine divided by the cosine. Dividing by $\dfrac{15}{17}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $\dfrac{17}{15}$, and the factor $17$ cancels. The result is negative because sine and cosine have opposite signs in the fourth quadrant.

The results are $\cos\theta=\dfrac{15}{17}$ and $\tan\theta=-\dfrac{8}{15}$.

</div>
