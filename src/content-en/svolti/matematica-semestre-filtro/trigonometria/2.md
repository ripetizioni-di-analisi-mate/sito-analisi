## Exercise 1

Express $225^\circ$ in radians.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta&=225\cdot\frac{\pi}{180}\,\mathrm{rad}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{225\pi}{180}\,\mathrm{rad}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{45\cdot5\,\pi}{45\cdot4}\,\mathrm{rad}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
225^\circ=180^\circ+45^\circ,
\qquad
\pi+\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{5\pi}{4}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A straight angle measures $180^\circ$ in degrees and $\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$ in radians. The two measures of the same angle are proportional, and one degree corresponds to $\dfrac{\pi}{180}\,\mathrm{rad}$. Therefore multiply the degree measure by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$.

The product gives the fraction $\dfrac{225\pi}{180}$. The greatest common divisor of $225$ and $180$ is $45$ since $225=45\cdot5$ and $180=45\cdot4$. Dividing numerator and denominator by $45$ gives $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

The second line checks the result another way. The angle $225^\circ$ is a straight angle plus $45^\circ$, and $45^\circ$ is one-quarter of a straight angle, or $\dfrac{\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad}$. The sum $\pi+\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ again gives $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

The angle therefore measures **$\dfrac{5\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad}$**, or approximately $3.93\,\mathrm{rad}$. Its terminal side is in the third quadrant because $225^\circ$ lies between $180^\circ$ and $270^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Express $-\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad}$ in degrees and give the angle with the same terminal side in the interval $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta&=-\frac{7\pi}{6}\cdot\frac{180}{\pi}\,^\circ\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{7\cdot180}{6}\,^\circ\\[6pt]
&=-7\cdot30^\circ
=-210^\circ,\\[6pt]
\theta_{\mathrm{equiv}}
&=-210^\circ+360^\circ\\[6pt]
&=150^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To convert radians to degrees, multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$, the number of degrees in one radian. The factor $\pi$ appears in the numerator and denominator, so it cancels. This leaves $-\dfrac{7\cdot180}{6}$, and since $\dfrac{180}{6}=30$, the measure is $-210^\circ$.

The minus sign indicates that the angle is measured clockwise from the positive $x$-axis. Conversion preserves the sign because the conversion factor is positive.

Two angles that differ by an integer multiple of $360^\circ$ have the same terminal side because a full turn returns the radius to the same position. The measure $-210^\circ$ is less than $0^\circ$, so add one full turn. The result is $150^\circ$, which belongs to the interval $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$.

The angle measures **$-210^\circ$** and has the same terminal side as **$150^\circ$**, in the second quadrant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel of radius $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ rotates counterclockwise. A point on the rim travels along an arc of length $33\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate the angle swept out in degrees.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta&=\frac{s}{r}
=\frac{33\pi\,\mathrm{cm}}{18\,\mathrm{cm}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3\cdot11\,\pi}{3\cdot6}\,\mathrm{rad}
=\frac{11\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad},\\[6pt]
\theta_{\mathrm{gradi}}
&=\frac{11\pi}{6}\cdot\frac{180}{\pi}\,^\circ\\[6pt]
&=11\cdot30^\circ
=330^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
2\pi r=36\pi\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
\frac{33\pi}{36\pi}=\frac{11}{12}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radian measure of a central angle is the ratio of arc length to radius, $\theta=\dfrac{s}{r}$. Both lengths are in centimeters, so the units cancel and the ratio is dimensionless. The greatest common divisor of $33$ and $18$ is $3$, so the fraction reduces to $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$.

To express the angle in degrees, multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$. The factor $\pi$ cancels, leaving $11\cdot30^\circ=330^\circ$.

The second line checks the result using proportions. The wheel has circumference $2\pi\cdot18\,\mathrm{cm}=36\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$. The arc traveled is therefore $\dfrac{11}{12}$ of the circumference, and $\dfrac{11}{12}$ of $360^\circ$ equals $330^\circ$.

The point rotates through **$330^\circ$**, or $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad}$. The angle is less than a full turn, so the point does not complete a revolution.

</div>
