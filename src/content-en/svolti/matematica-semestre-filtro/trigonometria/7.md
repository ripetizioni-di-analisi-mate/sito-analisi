## Exercise 1

An object of mass $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ rests on a plane inclined at $30^\circ$. Take $g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Calculate the components of its weight, taking the uphill direction and the outward normal to the plane as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=mg=5\cdot9.8\,\mathrm{N}
=49\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
P_{x'}&=-P\sin30^\circ
=-49\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{N}
=-24.5\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
P_{y'}&=-P\cos30^\circ
=-49\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=-24.5\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}\approx-42.4\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The magnitude of the weight is the mass times $g$. Since $1\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot\mathrm{m/s^2}=1\,\mathrm{N}$, the result is $P=49\,\mathrm{N}$.

The $x'$-axis is parallel to the plane and the $y'$-axis is perpendicular to it. The negative $y'$-axis makes the same angle with the downward vertical as the plane makes with the horizontal, because a rotation of $90^\circ$ takes one pair of directions into the other. Thus the weight makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the inward normal to the plane.

In the right triangle formed by the projections, the weight is the hypotenuse. The projection perpendicular to the plane is the leg adjacent to the angle of $30^\circ$, so its magnitude is $P\cos30^\circ$. The parallel projection is the opposite leg, so its magnitude is $P\sin30^\circ$.

The weight points downhill and into the plane, opposite to the directions chosen as positive. Both components are therefore negative.

The components are **$P_{x'}=-24.5\,\mathrm{N}$** and **$P_{y'}=-24.5\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$**, the latter approximately $-42.4\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object of mass $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ is on a plane inclined at $60^\circ$. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, with the downhill direction and the outward normal as positive. Calculate the components of the weight.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=mg=4\cdot10\,\mathrm{N}=40\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{discesa}}
&=+P\sin60^\circ
=40\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{N}
=20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}\approx34.6\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{normale}}
&=-P\cos60^\circ
=-40\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=-20\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The weight has magnitude $40\,\mathrm{N}$. As in the previous case, the angle between the weight and the inward normal to the plane equals the inclination, here $60^\circ$. The parallel projection therefore has magnitude $P\sin60^\circ$ and the perpendicular projection has magnitude $P\cos60^\circ$.

The parallel projection points downhill. In this exercise, downhill is the positive direction along the plane, so the parallel component is positive and equals $20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$.

The perpendicular projection points into the plane, while the positive direction is along the outward normal. The normal component is therefore negative and equals $-20\,\mathrm{N}$.

Comparison with the preceding exercise shows that the magnitudes depend only on the weight and inclination, whereas the signs depend on the directions chosen as positive. For a plane inclined at $60^\circ$, we have $\sin60^\circ>\cos60^\circ$, so the projection along the plane is greater than the perpendicular projection.

The components are **$P_{\mathrm{discesa}}=20\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$** and **$P_{\mathrm{normale}}=-20\,\mathrm{N}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A weight of magnitude $60\,\mathrm{N}$ has an uphill component of $-30\,\mathrm{N}$ on an inclined plane. The inclination $\alpha$ is acute. Calculate $\alpha$ and the component of weight along the outward normal to the plane.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{x'}&=-P\sin\alpha,\\[6pt]
-30\,\mathrm{N}
&=-60\sin\alpha\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
\sin\alpha&=\frac{-30}{-60}=\frac{1}{2},\\[6pt]
\alpha&=\arcsin\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=30^\circ,\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{normale}}
&=-P\cos30^\circ
=-60\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=-30\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}\approx-52.0\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(-30)^2+(-30\sqrt{3})^2=900+2700=3600=60^2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Taking uphill as positive, the parallel component of weight is $-P\sin\alpha$ because the weight points downhill. Substituting the data gives an equation for the unknown $\sin\alpha$. Dividing both sides by $-60\,\mathrm{N}$ gives $\sin\alpha=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The inclination is acute. Between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ the sine is strictly increasing, so a value of one-half determines one angle, namely $\alpha=\arcsin\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=30^\circ$.

The component along the outward normal has magnitude $P\cos\alpha$. The weight points into the plane, opposite the outward normal, so the component has a negative sign. Using $\cos30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ gives $-30\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$.

The last line checks the result using the Pythagorean theorem. Since the components are perpendicular, the sum of their squares must be the square of the weight magnitude.

The inclination is **$\alpha=30^\circ$** and the normal component is **$-30\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$**, or approximately $-52.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>
