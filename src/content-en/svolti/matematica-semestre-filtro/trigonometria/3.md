## Exercise 1

In a right triangle, the leg opposite the acute angle $\theta$ has length $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the adjacent leg has length $24\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate the sine, cosine, and tangent of $\theta$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=\sqrt{7^2+24^2}\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{49+576}\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{625}\,\mathrm{cm}
=25\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\sin\theta&=\frac{7}{25},\\[6pt]
\cos\theta&=\frac{24}{25},\\[6pt]
\tan\theta&=\frac{7}{24}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=\frac{49}{625}+\frac{576}{625}=\frac{625}{625}=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Sine and cosine are ratios that use the hypotenuse, which is not given. Since the triangle is right, find the hypotenuse using the **Pythagorean theorem**. The sum of the squares of the legs is $625$, so the hypotenuse has length $25\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The sine of $\theta$ is the opposite leg divided by the hypotenuse, or $\dfrac{7}{25}$. The cosine is the adjacent leg divided by the hypotenuse, or $\dfrac{24}{25}$. The tangent is the opposite leg divided by the adjacent leg, or $\dfrac{7}{24}$. The units cancel in each ratio, so all three values are dimensionless.

The last line checks the results using the **Pythagorean identity**, since the sum of the squares is exactly $1$. Since $\theta$ is acute, all three values are positive.

The results are $\sin\theta=\dfrac{7}{25}$, $\cos\theta=\dfrac{24}{25}$, and $\tan\theta=\dfrac{7}{24}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A straight ramp makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with a horizontal floor and rises $6\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate the length of the ramp and the horizontal distance between its ends.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin30^\circ&=\frac{6\,\mathrm{m}}{L},\\[6pt]
L&=\frac{6\,\mathrm{m}}{\sin30^\circ}
=\frac{6\,\mathrm{m}}{\frac{1}{2}}
=12\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
d&=L\cos30^\circ
=12\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}\approx10.39\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
d^2+6^2=108+36=144=12^2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ramp, the rise, and the horizontal distance form a right triangle, with the right angle between the vertical rise and the floor. The ramp is the hypotenuse $L$, and the rise of $6\,\mathrm{m}$ is the leg opposite the angle of $30^\circ$.

The sine of $30^\circ$ is therefore the ratio of $6\,\mathrm{m}$ to $L$. Multiplying both sides by $L$ and dividing by $\sin30^\circ$ isolates the ramp length. Since $\sin30^\circ=\dfrac{1}{2}$, dividing by one-half is equivalent to multiplying by $2$, and $L=12\,\mathrm{m}$.

The horizontal distance is the leg adjacent to the angle of $30^\circ$, so multiply the hypotenuse by $\cos30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. This gives $6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$.

The last line checks both results with the Pythagorean theorem, using $(6\sqrt{3})^2=108$. The ramp is **$12\,\mathrm{m}$** long, and the horizontal distance is **$6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$**, or approximately $10.39\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An observer stands on level ground $14\,\mathrm{m}$ from the base of a vertical tower. The observer’s eyes are $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground, and the angle of elevation to the top is $45^\circ$. Calculate the height of the tower.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan45^\circ&=\frac{h_{\mathrm{occhi,cima}}}{14\,\mathrm{m}},\\[6pt]
h_{\mathrm{occhi,cima}}
&=14\,\mathrm{m}\cdot\tan45^\circ\\[6pt]
&=14\,\mathrm{m}\cdot1=14\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
h_{\mathrm{torre}}
&=h_{\mathrm{occhi,cima}}+1.6\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=(14+1.6)\,\mathrm{m}
=15.6\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-3.2 xmax=17 ymin=-2 ymax=17 linee="-2,0 16.5,0;0,0 0,1.6;14,0 14,15.6;0,1.6 14,15.6" segmenti="0,1.6,14,1.6" curve="2.4*cos(t),1.6+2.4*sin(t),0,0.785398" legenda=0 etichette="3.1,2.6,45°;7,-1.1,14 m;-2.4,0.8,1.6 m;12.4,9.5,14 m" titolo="One vertex of the right triangle is at the observer’s eyes, $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle of elevation is measured between the line of sight and the horizontal through the observer’s eyes. Thus one vertex of the right triangle is at the eyes, the horizontal leg extends $14\,\mathrm{m}$ to the tower, and the vertical leg runs from eye level to the top.

The vertical leg is opposite the angle of $45^\circ$ and the horizontal leg is adjacent. The tangent therefore relates them. Multiplying the horizontal distance by $\tan45^\circ$ gives the vertical distance from eye level to the top. Since $\tan45^\circ=1$, the vertical distance equals the horizontal distance, $14\,\mathrm{m}$.

This rise starts at eye level. To find the height of the tower above the ground, add the observer’s eye height, $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$.

The tower is **$15.6\,\mathrm{m}$** tall.

</div>
