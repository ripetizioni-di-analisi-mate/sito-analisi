## Exercise 1

A force of magnitude $18\,\mathrm{N}$ makes an angle of $60^\circ$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis. Calculate its two Cartesian components.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_x&=F\cos60^\circ
=18\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{N}
=9\,\mathrm{N},\\[6pt]
F_y&=F\sin60^\circ
=18\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{N}\\[6pt]
&=9\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}\approx15.6\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_x^2+F_y^2=(81+243)\,\mathrm{N^2}=324\,\mathrm{N^2}=(18\,\mathrm{N})^2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A vector of magnitude $F$ making an angle $\theta$ with the positive $x$-axis has components $F_x=F\cos\theta$ and $F_y=F\sin\theta$. These formulas hold because a vector of magnitude $1$ in the same direction has as its components the coordinates of the point at angle $\theta$ on the unit circle. Multiplying the magnitude by $F$ also multiplies the components by $F$.

The angle is measured from the horizontal axis, so cosine gives the horizontal component and sine the vertical component. With $\cos60^\circ=\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin60^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, the results are $9\,\mathrm{N}$ and $9\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$.

The angle of $60^\circ$ lies in the first quadrant, so both components are positive. The second line checks the result with the Pythagorean theorem because $(9\sqrt{3})^2=243$ and the sum of the squares of the components equals the square of the magnitude.

The components are **$F_x=9\,\mathrm{N}$** and **$F_y=9\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}$**, the latter approximately $15.6\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cable of length $12\,\mathrm{m}$ rises from left to right, making an angle of $60^\circ$ with the vertical. Calculate the lengths of its horizontal and vertical projections.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_x&=L\sin60^\circ
=12\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{m}
=6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}\approx10.39\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
L_y&=L\cos60^\circ
=12\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=6\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
90^\circ-60^\circ=30^\circ,
\qquad
L_x=12\cos30^\circ\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad
L_y=12\sin30^\circ\,\mathrm{m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cable and its horizontal and vertical projections form a right triangle with hypotenuse $12\,\mathrm{m}$. One side of the $60^\circ$ angle lies along the vertical, so the vertical leg is *adjacent* to that angle and the horizontal leg is *opposite*.

The adjacent leg is the hypotenuse times the cosine, so the vertical projection is $12\cos60^\circ\,\mathrm{m}=6\,\mathrm{m}$. The opposite leg is the hypotenuse times the sine, so the horizontal projection is $12\sin60^\circ\,\mathrm{m}=6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$.

The second line gives the same result using the angle measured from the horizontal. Since the axes are perpendicular, the cable makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal. We can then use the familiar formulas, with cosine for the horizontal projection and sine for the vertical projection. The two approaches agree because $\sin60^\circ=\cos30^\circ$ and $\cos60^\circ=\sin30^\circ$.

The horizontal projection has length **$6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$**, or about $10.39\,\mathrm{m}$, and the vertical projection has length **$6\,\mathrm{m}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A displacement has magnitude $10\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m}$ and direction $315^\circ$ measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis. Calculate its signed components.

$$
\begin{aligned}
360^\circ-315^\circ&=45^\circ,\\[6pt]
\cos315^\circ&=\cos45^\circ=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\\[6pt]
\sin315^\circ&=-\sin45^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\\[6pt]
\Delta x&=10\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{m}
=\frac{10\cdot2}{2}\,\mathrm{m}
=10\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta y&=10\sqrt{2}\cdot\left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=-10\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-2 xmax=12.5 ymin=-12 ymax=2.5 vettori="0,0,10,-10,s" segmenti="10,0,10,-10;0,-10,10,-10" curve="2.2*cos(t),2.2*sin(t),-0.785398,0" legenda=0 etichette="2.9,-1.1,45°;5,0.7,10 m;11.1,-5,−10 m" titolo="The displacement at $315^\circ$ makes an angle of $45^\circ$ below the positive $x$-axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle $315^\circ$ lies between $270^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, so its direction is in the fourth quadrant. The radius needs another $45^\circ$ to complete a full turn, so the reference angle is $45^\circ$ and the sine and cosine both have magnitude $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

In the fourth quadrant the horizontal coordinate is positive and the vertical coordinate is negative. The cosine is therefore $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and the sine is $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

To find the components, multiply the magnitude by the cosine and sine of the direction angle. The product $\sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{2}$ equals $2$, which cancels with the denominator, so the components have magnitude $10\,\mathrm{m}$. The displacement magnitude is positive, so the vertical component has the same sign as the sine and is negative.

The components are **$\Delta x=10\,\mathrm{m}$** and **$\Delta y=-10\,\mathrm{m}$**, corresponding to ten meters to the right and ten meters downward.

</div>
