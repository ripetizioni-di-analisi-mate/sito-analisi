## Exercise 1

A point starts at $(20,0)$ on a circle of radius $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ centered at the origin, with coordinates given in centimeters. It rotates counterclockwise through $135^\circ$. Calculate the length of the arc traveled and the final horizontal coordinate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta&=135\cdot\frac{\pi}{180}\,\mathrm{rad}
=\frac{3\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad},\\[6pt]
s&=r\theta
=20\cdot\frac{3\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{cm}
=15\pi\,\mathrm{cm}\approx47.1\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\cos135^\circ&=-\cos45^\circ=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\\[6pt]
x_f&=r\cos135^\circ
=20\cdot\left(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
&=-10\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}\approx-14.1\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-27 xmax=27 ymin=-24 ymax=26 curve="20*cos(t),20*sin(t),0,6.283185307179586;6*cos(t),6*sin(t),0,2.35619449" legenda=0 linee="0,0 -14.142135624,14.142135624" punti="20,0,pieno,A;-14.142135624,14.142135624,pieno,B" segmenti="-14.142135624,0,-14.142135624,14.142135624" etichette="3.2,7.4,135°" titolo="The point moves from $A$ to $B$. The horizontal coordinate of $B$ is $20\cos135^\circ=-10\sqrt{2}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The arc length formula $s=r\theta$ requires the angle in radians because radian measure is defined as the ratio of arc length to radius. Convert $135^\circ$ by multiplying by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$, then simplify by canceling $45$ to obtain $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad}$.

The point starts on the positive $x$-axis and rotates through $135^\circ$, so the angle subtended by its arc is $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\,\mathrm{rad}$. Multiplying by the radius gives $15\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The same angle gives the final position. A point on a circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin has horizontal coordinate $r\cos\theta$ because its coordinates are the corresponding unit-circle coordinates multiplied by $r$. The angle of $135^\circ$ lies in the second quadrant with reference angle $180^\circ-135^\circ=45^\circ$. Its cosine is therefore $-\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

The arc length is **$15\pi\,\mathrm{cm}$**, or approximately $47.1\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the final horizontal coordinate is **$-10\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{cm}$**, or approximately $-14.1\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rod of length $12\,\mathrm{m}$ is hinged at one end and rotates in a vertical plane, increasing its angle to the horizontal from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$. Calculate the rise of the free end and the length of the arc it travels.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_i&=12\sin30^\circ\,\mathrm{m}=6\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
y_f&=12\sin60^\circ\,\mathrm{m}=6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta y&=y_f-y_i
=12(\sin60^\circ-\sin30^\circ)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=6(\sqrt{3}-1)\,\mathrm{m}\approx4.39\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta\theta&=60^\circ-30^\circ=30^\circ=\frac{\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad},\\[6pt]
s&=12\cdot\frac{\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=2\pi\,\mathrm{m}\approx6.28\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set the origin at the hinge with the $x$-axis horizontal. The free end always remains a distance $12\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge, so it moves on a circle of radius $12\,\mathrm{m}$. Its height relative to the hinge is the rod’s vertical projection, its length times the sine of its angle to the horizontal.

Initially the angle is $30^\circ$ and the height is $6\,\mathrm{m}$. Finally the angle is $60^\circ$ and the height is $6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$. The rise is the final height minus the initial height. Factoring out $6$ gives $6(\sqrt{3}-1)\,\mathrm{m}$.

The rise differs from the arc length because the end does not move vertically. The arc is centered at the hinge, has radius $12\,\mathrm{m}$, and subtends the change in angle $30^\circ$. The formula $s=r\theta$ uses the angle in radians, so $30^\circ=\dfrac{\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad}$.

The rise is **$6(\sqrt{3}-1)\,\mathrm{m}$**, or approximately $4.39\,\mathrm{m}$, and the arc length is **$2\pi\,\mathrm{m}$**, or approximately $6.28\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point moves $12\,\mathrm{m}$ in a direction $60^\circ$ counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis. It then moves $12\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the net displacement, giving the angle in $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=12\cos60^\circ\,\mathrm{m}-12\,\mathrm{m}
=(6-12)\,\mathrm{m}=-6\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\Delta y&=12\sin60^\circ\,\mathrm{m}+0\,\mathrm{m}
=6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
D&=\sqrt{(-6)^2+(6\sqrt{3})^2}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{36+108}\,\mathrm{m}
=12\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\tan\beta&=\frac{6\sqrt{3}}{6}=\sqrt{3},
\qquad
\beta=60^\circ,\\[6pt]
\theta&=180^\circ-60^\circ\\[6pt]
&=120^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-9 xmax=9 ymin=-1.5 ymax=12.5 vettori="0,0,6,10.392304845,a;6,10.392304845,-6,10.392304845,b;0,0,-6,10.392304845,D" curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,2.094395102" legenda=0 etichette="1.9,2.2,120°" titolo="The net displacement $D$ extends from the origin to the end of the second segment and makes an angle of $120^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The net displacement is the sum of the two displacements, so its components are the sums of their corresponding components. The first displacement has components $12\cos60^\circ=6\,\mathrm{m}$ and $12\sin60^\circ=6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$. The second points to the left, so its horizontal component is $-12\,\mathrm{m}$ and its vertical component is zero.

Adding gives $\Delta x=-6\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\Delta y=6\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$. Applying the **Pythagorean theorem** to the two perpendicular components gives a magnitude of $12\,\mathrm{m}$.

To find the direction, first determine the reference angle $\beta$ from the ratio of the component magnitudes. This ratio gives its tangent, $\sqrt{3}$, so $\beta=60^\circ$. The horizontal component is negative and the vertical component positive, so the displacement lies in the second quadrant and its direction is $180^\circ-\beta$.

The triangle formed by the two segments has two sides of length $12\,\mathrm{m}$ and an included angle of $60^\circ$, so it is equilateral, consistent with $D=12\,\mathrm{m}$.

The net displacement has magnitude **$12\,\mathrm{m}$** and direction **$120^\circ$**.

</div>
