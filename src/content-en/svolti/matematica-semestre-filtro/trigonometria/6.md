## Exercise 1

A right triangle has a hypotenuse of length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. The leg opposite the acute angle $\theta$ has length $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate $\theta$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta&=\frac{5\,\mathrm{cm}}{10\,\mathrm{cm}}
=\frac{1}{2},\\[6pt]
\theta&=\arcsin\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
=30^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b&=\sqrt{10^2-5^2}\,\mathrm{cm}=\sqrt{75}\,\mathrm{cm}=5\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\cos\theta&=\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{10}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}=\cos30^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two known sides are the leg opposite $\theta$ and the hypotenuse. By definition their ratio is the sine of $\theta$, so $\sin\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}$. The units cancel because both sides are measured in centimeters.

Use the inverse sine to recover the angle. The angle is acute because it is one of the two nonright angles in a right triangle. Between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ the sine is strictly increasing, so every value between $0$ and $1$ corresponds to a unique angle. The sine of $30^\circ$ equals one-half, as can be seen from half an equilateral triangle, so $\theta=\arcsin\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)=30^\circ$.

The second calculation checks the result. By the Pythagorean theorem, the adjacent leg has length $5\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its ratio to the hypotenuse is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, which is precisely the cosine of $30^\circ$.

The angle measures **$\theta=30^\circ$**, or $\dfrac{\pi}{6}\,\mathrm{rad}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A displacement has horizontal component $-6\,\mathrm{m}$ and vertical component $6\,\mathrm{m}$. Find its direction in $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$, measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan\beta&=\frac{|6|}{|-6|}=1,\\[6pt]
\beta&=\arctan1
=45^\circ,\\[6pt]
\begin{cases}
\Delta x<0\\
\Delta y>0
\end{cases}
&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{second quadrant},\\[6pt]
\theta&=180^\circ-\beta\\[6pt]
&=135^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The arctangent has values between $-90^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, so taking the arctangent of the ratio of the components does not directly give a second- or third-quadrant angle. First use the component magnitudes to calculate the acute angle $\beta$ between the segment and the horizontal axis.

The tangent of $\beta$ is the magnitude of the vertical component divided by that of the horizontal component. Both magnitudes are $6\,\mathrm{m}$, so the ratio is $1$ and $\beta=45^\circ$.

The horizontal component is negative and the vertical component is positive, so the segment points to the left and upward, into the second quadrant.

In the second quadrant the segment makes an angle $\beta$ with the *negative* $x$-axis. Measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis, the direction is therefore a straight angle minus $\beta$.

The direction of the displacement is **$\theta=135^\circ$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A velocity has horizontal component $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and vertical component $-4\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Calculate its magnitude and its direction in $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$, measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{4^2+(4\sqrt{3})^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{16+48}\,\mathrm{m/s}
=8\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
\tan\beta&=\frac{4\sqrt{3}}{4}=\sqrt{3},\\[6pt]
\beta&=\arctan\sqrt{3}=60^\circ,\\[6pt]
\theta&=360^\circ-60^\circ\\[6pt]
&=300^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The components are perpendicular and form the legs of a right triangle with the velocity vector as its hypotenuse. Thus the **Pythagorean theorem** gives the magnitude. The square of $4\sqrt{3}$ is $16\cdot3=48$. Adding $16$ gives $64$, whose square root is $8$.

To determine the direction, first calculate the acute angle $\beta$ the vector makes with the horizontal axis. Its tangent is the ratio of the magnitudes of the vertical and horizontal components, $\sqrt{3}$. The value $\sqrt{3}$ is the tangent of $60^\circ$, so $\beta=60^\circ$.

The horizontal component is positive and the vertical component is negative, so the vector points to the right and downward, into the fourth quadrant. Here the vector makes an angle of $\beta$ below the positive $x$-axis, so subtract $\beta$ from a full turn to obtain the counterclockwise angle.

The velocity has magnitude **$8\,\mathrm{m/s}$** and direction **$300^\circ$**.

</div>
