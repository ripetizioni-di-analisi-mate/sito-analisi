## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{3}{x-2}=\dfrac{x+1}{x-2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-2&\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\ne2 \\[6pt]
(x-2)\cdot\frac{3}{x-2}&=(x-2)\cdot\frac{x+1}{x-2} \\[6pt]
3&=x+1 \\[6pt]
x&=2 \\[6pt]
2&\notin\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2\} \\[6pt]
S&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both sides have the same denominator $x-2$, which is zero at $x=2$. The domain of the equation is therefore $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2\}$, so every solution must be a real number other than $2$.

The factor $x-2$ is nonzero on the domain, so we can multiply both sides by $x-2$. The resulting equation is equivalent to the original *on that domain*. The denominator cancels on both sides, leaving the numerators equal, $3=x+1$.

Subtracting $1$ gives $x=2$, the only candidate. However, this value makes both original denominators zero, so it is outside the domain and must be excluded. At $x=2$, both fractions are undefined, so the equality is undefined there.

The simplified equation $3=x+1$ has a solution, but it is equivalent to the original equation only for $x\ne2$. The original equation **has no solutions**, and its solution set is empty.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\sqrt{2x+3}=x$.

$$
\begin{cases}
2x+3\ge0\\
x\ge0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\ge0
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x+3&=x^2 \\[6pt]
x^2-2x-3&=0 \\[6pt]
(x-3)(x+1)&=0 \\[6pt]
x=3\quad&\text{or}\quad x=-1 \\[6pt]
S&=\{3\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(2*x+3);x" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=5 punti="3,3,pieno;-1,1,vuoto;-1,-1,vuoto" segmenti="-1,-1,-1,1" legenda="y=\sqrt{2x+3};y=x" titolo="The curves $y=\sqrt{2x+3}$ and $y=x$ meet only at $(3,3)$. At $x=-1$, the two sides equal $1$ and $-1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand must be nonnegative, so $2x+3\ge0$. The square root itself is nonnegative, so the right side must also be nonnegative, giving the requirement $x\ge0$. The two conditions must hold simultaneously. Since $x\ge0$ implies $2x+3\ge3>0$, the combined condition reduces to $x\ge0$.

For $x\ge0$, both sides are nonnegative. By the theorem on **squaring two nonnegative sides**, the equation is therefore equivalent to $2x+3=x^2$. Moving all terms to the right gives $x^2-2x-3=0$. The numbers $-3$ and $1$ have sum $-2$ and product $-3$, so the trinomial factors as $(x-3)(x+1)$. By the zero-product property, the candidates are $3$ and $-1$.

The value $-1$ fails the condition $x\ge0$ and is excluded. Indeed, at $x=-1$, the left side equals $\sqrt{1}=1$ and the right side equals $-1$. At $x=3$, however, $\sqrt{9}=3$. The equation **has the unique solution** $x=3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2-5x+6}=\dfrac{x+2}{x-3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-5x+6&=(x-2)(x-3) \\[6pt]
x^2-5x+6\ne0\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\ne2\ \text{and}\ x\ne3 \\[6pt]
x^2-4&=(x-2)(x+2) \\[6pt]
\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-5x+6}&=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-2)(x-3)} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{x+2}{x-3}\qquad\text{for } x\ne2,\ x\ne3 \\[6pt]
S&=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2,3\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the domain, factor the denominator on the left. The numbers $-2$ and $-3$ have sum $-5$ and product $6$, so $x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)$, which is zero at $x=2$ and at $x=3$. The denominator on the right is zero at $x=3$, a value already excluded. The domain is therefore $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2,3\}$.

The numerator on the left is a difference of squares, $x^2-2^2$, so it factors as $(x-2)(x+2)$. On the domain, the factor $x-2$ is nonzero, so it can be canceled from the numerator and denominator. After cancellation, the left side equals the right side.

The two expressions therefore have the same value at every point of the domain, and the equation is an identity there. For example, at $x=0$ both sides equal $-\dfrac{2}{3}$. The value $x=2$ remains excluded even though the simplified expression $\dfrac{x+2}{x-3}$ is defined there, because the original left side has a zero denominator at $x=2$. The solution set is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2,3\}$.

</div>
