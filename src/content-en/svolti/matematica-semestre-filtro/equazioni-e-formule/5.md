## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2+x-20=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5+(-4)&=1,\qquad 5\cdot(-4)=-20 \\[6pt]
(x+5)(x-4)&=x^2-4x+5x-20=x^2+x-20 \\[6pt]
(x+5)(x-4)&=0 \\[6pt]
x+5=0\quad&\text{or}\quad x-4=0 \\[6pt]
x=-5\quad&\text{or}\quad x=4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $x^2$ in the trinomial is $1$, so we can factor it by finding two numbers whose sum is the coefficient of $x$, namely $1$, and whose product is the constant term, $-20$. The product is negative, so the numbers have opposite signs. Among the factor pairs of $20$, the numbers $5$ and $-4$ have sum $1$.

Using these two numbers, the trinomial can be written as $(x+5)(x-4)$. Expanding the product gives $x^2-4x+5x-20$, and since $-4x+5x=x$, this recovers the original trinomial, confirming the factorization.

The equation now sets a product equal to zero. By the **zero-product property**, a product of real numbers is zero if and only if at least one factor is zero. The equation is therefore equivalent to $x+5=0$ or $x-4=0$. These two linear equations give $x=-5$ and $x=4$. The original equation **has two solutions**, $-5$ and $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $3x^2-12x+12=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x^2-12x+12&=3\left(x^2-4x+4\right) \\[6pt]
x^2-4x+4&=x^2-2\cdot2\cdot x+2^2=(x-2)^2 \\[6pt]
3(x-2)^2&=0 \\[6pt]
(x-2)^2&=0 \\[6pt]
x-2&=0 \\[6pt]
x&=2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{check:}\qquad \Delta=(-12)^2-4\cdot3\cdot12=144-144=0 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three coefficients $3$, $-12$, and $12$ are multiples of $3$, so factor out $3$. The remaining trinomial $x^2-4x+4$ contains the square of $x$, the square of $2$, and twice their product, $2\cdot2\cdot x=4x$, with a minus sign. It is therefore the square of the binomial $x-2$.

The factor $3$ is nonzero, so dividing both sides by $3$ gives $(x-2)^2=0$. A real number has square zero only if the number itself is zero, so $x-2=0$ and $x=2$.

The quadratic formula gives $x=\dfrac{12\pm\sqrt{0}}{6}=2$, so the two values corresponding to the $\pm$ signs coincide, and $2$ is a **double root**. Checking the discriminant gives $\Delta=0$, in agreement with the quadratic formula theorem, which gives a single solution when the discriminant is zero. The solution set is therefore $\{2\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2x^2-3x-2=0$ using the discriminant.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=2,\qquad b=-3,\qquad c=-2 \\[6pt]
\Delta&=b^2-4ac=(-3)^2-4\cdot2\cdot(-2) \\[6pt]
&=9+16=25 \\[6pt]
x&=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}=\frac{3\pm5}{4} \\[6pt]
x_1&=\frac{3-5}{4}=\frac{-2}{4}=-\frac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
x_2&=\frac{3+5}{4}=\frac{8}{4}=2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x^2-3*x-2" xmin=-2 xmax=3.5 ymin=-4 ymax=6 punti="-0.5,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" legenda="y=2x^2-3x-2" titolo="The parabola $y=2x^2-3x-2$ meets the $x$-axis at the points with horizontal coordinates $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation has the form $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with $a=2$, $b=-3$, and $c=-2$. Since $a\ne0$, we can apply the theorem on the **discriminant and the quadratic formula**.

In the discriminant, the square of $-3$ is $9$. The product $4\cdot2\cdot(-2)$ equals $-16$ and is subtracted, so we add $16$ to obtain $\Delta=25$. The discriminant is positive, so the equation has two distinct real roots. The square root of the discriminant is $\sqrt{25}=5$.

In the formula, the numerator is $-b\pm\sqrt{\Delta}$. Since $b=-3$, the term $-b$ equals $3$. The denominator $2a$ equals $4$. The minus sign gives $\dfrac{-2}{4}=-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the plus sign gives $\dfrac{8}{4}=2$.

At $x=2$, the left side equals $8-6-2=0$. At $x=-\dfrac{1}{2}$, it equals $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{3}{2}-2=0$. The equation **has two solutions**, $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $2$, the horizontal coordinates where the parabola crosses the horizontal axis in the figure.

</div>
