## Exercise 1

An object of mass $0.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ has kinetic energy $16\,\mathrm{J}$. Use $E_c=\dfrac12mv^2$ to calculate the magnitude $v$ of its velocity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_c&=\frac{1}{2}mv^2 \\[6pt]
2E_c&=mv^2 \\[6pt]
v^2&=\frac{2E_c}{m} \\[6pt]
v&=\sqrt{\frac{2E_c}{m}}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^2&=\frac{2\cdot16\,\mathrm{J}}{0.5\,\mathrm{kg}}=\frac{32\,\mathrm{J}}{0.5\,\mathrm{kg}}=64\,\frac{\mathrm{m^2}}{\mathrm{s^2}} \\[6pt]
v&=\sqrt{64\,\frac{\mathrm{m^2}}{\mathrm{s^2}}}=8\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To eliminate the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$, multiply both sides by $2$ to obtain $2E_c=mv^2$. Since the mass is positive, dividing both sides by $m$ isolates $v^2$.

Substitute the given values. The numerator is $2\cdot16=32$ joules, and the quotient $\dfrac{32}{0.5}$ is $64$, since dividing by $0.5$ is equivalent to multiplying by $2$. For the units, one joule is one $\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s^2}$, so division by kilograms leaves $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, the unit of speed squared.

The equation $v^2=64$ has two real solutions, $8$ and $-8$, because both numbers have square $64$. The problem asks for the *magnitude* of the velocity, which is nonnegative, so take the principal square root $\sqrt{64}=8$ and discard $-8$.

Substitution gives $\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot0.5\cdot64=16$ joules, recovering the given energy. The speed is **8 meters per second**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a model of vertical motion, the height in meters is $h(t)=10+3t-t^2$, where $t$ is the numerical value of the elapsed time in seconds. Calculate the time of first contact with the ground for $t\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
10+3t-t^2&=0 \\[6pt]
t^2-3t-10&=0 \\[6pt]
(t-5)(t+2)&=0 \\[6pt]
t=5\quad&\text{or}\quad t=-2 \\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{contatto}}&=5\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="10+3*x-x^2" xmin=-3 xmax=6 ymin=-6 ymax=14 nomi="t,h" punti="-2,0,vuoto;5,0,pieno;0,10,pieno" legenda="h=10+3t-t^2" titolo="With $t$ in $\mathrm{s}$ and $h$ in $\mathrm{m}$, the height starts at $10\,\mathrm{m}$ and reaches zero at $t=5$. The root $t=-2$ is excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The object hits the ground when its height is zero, so solve $h(t)=0$. Multiply both sides by $-1$ to make the coefficient of $t^2$ positive and obtain $t^2-3t-10=0$.

The numbers $-5$ and $2$ have sum $-3$ and product $-10$, so the trinomial factors as $(t-5)(t+2)$. By the **zero-product property**, the roots are $t=5$ and $t=-2$. The discriminant is $9+40=49$, and the quadratic formula $\dfrac{3\pm7}{2}$ gives the same result.

The model describes the motion for $t\ge0$. Therefore $-2$, which corresponds to a time before the motion begins, fails the constraint and is excluded. This leaves $t=5$, the only admissible zero and hence the first. As a check, $h(5)=10+15-25=0$. First contact with the ground occurs **5 seconds** after the initial time.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a model of vertical motion, the height in meters is $h(t)=-t^2+8t+9$, where $t$ is the numerical value of the elapsed time in seconds. The maximum height occurs at time $t=4$. At what time after the maximum is the height $21\,\mathrm{m}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
-t^2+8t+9&=21 \\[6pt]
-t^2+8t-12&=0 \\[6pt]
t^2-8t+12&=0 \\[6pt]
(t-2)(t-6)&=0 \\[6pt]
t=2\quad&\text{or}\quad t=6 \\[6pt]
t>4\quad&\Longrightarrow\quad t_{\mathrm{discesa}}=6\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-x^2+8*x+9;21" xmin=-1 xmax=9.5 ymin=-4 ymax=30 nomi="t,h" punti="2,21,pieno;6,21,pieno;4,25,pieno" segmenti="6,0,6,21;4,0,4,25" legenda="h=-t^2+8t+9;h=21" titolo="The height is $21\,\mathrm{m}$ at $t=2$ on the way up and at $t=6$ on the way down, after the maximum $h(4)=25$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

We want the times when the height is $21$ meters, so set $h(t)$ equal to $21$. Subtracting $21$ from both sides gives $-t^2+8t-12=0$, and multiplying by $-1$ gives $t^2-8t+12=0$.

The numbers $-2$ and $-6$ have sum $-8$ and product $12$, so the trinomial factors as $(t-2)(t-6)$. By the **zero-product property**, the times are $t=2$ and $t=6$. Both are positive, so both occur during the motion.

The vertex of the parabola is at $t=4$, where the height is $h(4)=-16+32+9=25$ meters. The object passes through a height of $21$ meters once on the way up, before the maximum, and once on the way down, after the maximum. The problem asks for the time after the maximum, so require $t>4$. Only $t=6$ remains. As a check, $h(6)=-36+48+9=21$. The object reaches $21$ meters on the way down **6 seconds** after the initial time.

</div>
