## Exercise 1

Solve $7x-9=26$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
7x-9+9&=26+9 \\[6pt]
7x&=35 \\[6pt]
\frac{7x}{7}&=\frac{35}{7} \\[6pt]
x&=5 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{check:}\qquad 7\cdot5-9=35-9=26 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is defined for every real $x$ because both sides are polynomials. To isolate $7x$, add $9$ to both sides. This gives an equivalent equation by the rule for **reversible operations on both sides**. On the left, $-9+9$ is zero, leaving $7x$, while the right side becomes $26+9=35$.

The equation $7x=35$ has the form $ax=b$ with $a=7$ and $b=35$. Since the coefficient $a=7$ is nonzero, both sides can be divided by $7$ without losing or introducing solutions. The quotient $\dfrac{35}{7}$ equals $5$.

The difference between the two sides is $L-R=7x-9-26=7x-35$. At $x=4$ it is $-7$, and at $x=6$ it is $7$. Since the difference increases by $7$ for every unit increase in $x$, it is zero at exactly one value between $4$ and $6$. To check, substitute $5$ into the original equation. The left side is $35-9=26$, which equals the right side, so the equation **has the unique solution** $x=5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $3(2x+1)=5x+12$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\cdot2x+3\cdot1&=5x+12 \\[6pt]
6x+3&=5x+12 \\[6pt]
6x-5x+3&=12 \\[6pt]
x+3&=12 \\[6pt]
x&=12-3=9 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{check:}\qquad 3(2\cdot9+1)=3\cdot19=57,\qquad 5\cdot9+12=45+12=57 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the distributive property, the factor $3$ multiplies both terms of $2x+1$, namely $2x$ and $1$, so the left side becomes $6x+3$. If $3$ were applied only to $2x$, the result would be $6x+1=5x+12$, giving $x=11$. At $x=11$, the original left side is $3\cdot23=69$ and the right side is $67$, so $11$ is not a solution. In the equation $6x+3=5x+12$, the unknown appears on both sides.

Subtract $5x$ from both sides to collect the terms containing the unknown on the left. Since $6x-5x=x$, the coefficient of $x$ reduces to $1$ and the equation becomes $x+3=12$. Finally, subtracting $3$ from both sides isolates the unknown and gives $x=9$. Both subtractions are reversible, so every equation in the sequence is equivalent to the original one.

To check, substitute $9$ into each side separately. The left side equals $3\cdot19=57$ and the right side equals $45+12=57$. The values agree, so the equation **has the unique solution** $x=9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $4(2-x)+3(x-1)=2x+14$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
8-4x+3x-3&=2x+14 \\[6pt]
5-x&=2x+14 \\[6pt]
5-x-2x-5&=2x+14-2x-5 \\[6pt]
-3x&=9 \\[6pt]
x&=\frac{9}{-3}=-3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="5-x;2*x+14" xmin=-7 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=16 punti="-3,8,pieno" segmenti="-3,0,-3,8" legenda="y=5-x;y=2x+14" titolo="The left side, $5-x$, and the right side, $2x+14$, both equal $8$ at $x=-3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Expand the two products on the left separately. The factor $4$ multiplies $2$ and $-x$, giving $8-4x$. The factor $3$ multiplies $x$ and $-1$, giving $3x-3$. Combining like terms gives $8-3=5$ and $-4x+3x=-x$, so the left side equals $5-x$.

Then subtract $2x$ and $5$ from both sides. This leaves $-x-2x=-3x$ on the left and $14-5=9$ on the right, giving $-3x=9$. The coefficient $-3$ is nonzero, so divide by $-3$. The quotient of two numbers of opposite signs is negative, so $x=-3$.

At $x=-3$, the left side equals $4\cdot5+3\cdot(-4)=20-12=8$ and the right side equals $-6+14=8$. The graph shows the two sides as straight lines that intersect at the point with horizontal coordinate $-3$ and vertical coordinate $8$. The equation **has the unique solution** $x=-3$.

</div>
