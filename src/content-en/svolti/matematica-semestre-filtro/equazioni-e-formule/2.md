## Exercise 1

A sample occupies $18\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and has density $2.5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Use $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$ to calculate its mass $m$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m}{V} \\[6pt]
\rho\cdot V&=\frac{m}{V}\cdot V \\[6pt]
m&=\rho V
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=2.5\,\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\mathrm{cm^3}}\cdot18\,\mathrm{cm^3} \\[6pt]
&=(2.5\cdot18)\,\mathrm{g} \\[6pt]
&=45\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2.5*x;45" xmin=0 xmax=24 ymin=0 ymax=60 nomi="V,m" legenda="m=2.5V;m=45" punti="18,45,pieno" segmenti="18,0,18,45" titolo="With $V$ in $\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $m$ in $\mathrm{g}$, the line $m=2.5\,V$ gives $45\,\mathrm{g}$ at $V=18\,\mathrm{cm^3}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The quantity to be found is mass, so in the relation $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$, mass is the unknown and density and volume are given. Since the sample volume is positive, multiplying both sides by $V$ gives an equivalent equality. On the right, $V$ cancels, leaving the **rearranged formula** $m=\rho V$.

Substitute the given values with their units. The product of the numbers is $2.5\cdot18=45$. In the product of the units, cubic centimeters appear in the denominator of the density and in the numerator of the volume, so they cancel. This leaves grams, as required for a mass.

The graph shows mass as a function of volume. Since the density is constant, the graph is a straight line through the origin with slope $2.5$. The point with horizontal coordinate $18$ has vertical coordinate $45$. The sample therefore has a mass of **45 grams**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solution contains $0.06\,\mathrm{mol}$ of solute and has molar concentration $0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$. Use the relation $c=\dfrac{n}{V}$, where $n$ is the amount of substance, to find the volume $V$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=\frac{n}{V} \\[6pt]
cV&=n \\[6pt]
V&=\frac{n}{c}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac{0.06\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{0.06}{0.15}\,\mathrm{L}
=\frac{6}{15}\,\mathrm{L}
=\frac{2}{5}\,\mathrm{L} \\[6pt]
&=0.4\,\mathrm{L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the relation $c=\dfrac{n}{V}$, the unknown appears in the denominator. Since the volume is positive, multiplying both sides by $V$ gives the equivalent equality $cV=n$, in which $V$ appears as a factor.

The concentration is $0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}$, so it is nonzero and we can divide both sides by $c$. This gives the **rearranged formula** $V=\dfrac{n}{c}$.

To evaluate the quotient, multiply the numerator and denominator by $100$. This gives $\dfrac{0.06}{0.15}=\dfrac{6}{15}$, which can be divided through by $3$ to obtain $\dfrac{2}{5}=0.4$. For the units, dividing moles by moles per liter amounts to multiplying moles by liters per mole, so moles cancel and liters remain.

As a check, $0.15\,\mathrm{mol/L}\cdot0.4\,\mathrm{L}=0.06\,\mathrm{mol}$, which recovers the given amount of solute. The solution volume is therefore **0.4 liters**, or $400\,\mathrm{mL}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A flat surface of area $35\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ is subject to a uniform pressure of $18\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Use $p=\dfrac{F}{A}$ to find the magnitude $F$ of the normal force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=pA \\[6pt]
p&=18\,\mathrm{kPa}=18\cdot10^{3}\,\mathrm{Pa}=18\,000\,\mathrm{Pa} \\[6pt]
1\,\mathrm{cm^2}&=\left(10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}\right)^2=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
A&=35\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}=0.0035\,\mathrm{m^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=18\,000\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot0.0035\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
&=18\cdot35\cdot10^{3}\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
&=630\cdot10^{-1}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
&=63\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area is positive, so multiplying both sides of $p=\dfrac{F}{A}$ by $A$ gives the **rearranged formula** $F=pA$.

To obtain the force in newtons, express the data in SI units. The prefix kilo means $10^3$, so the pressure is $18\,000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. One centimeter is $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$, and a square centimeter is the square of this length. The area conversion factor is therefore $\left(10^{-2}\right)^2=10^{-4}$, giving an area of $0.0035\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

Multiply the numerical factors $18\cdot35=630$ and the powers of ten $10^{3}\cdot10^{-4}=10^{-1}$ separately, giving the numerical value $63$. Since one pascal equals one newton per square meter, the product of pascals and square meters is measured in newtons. The normal force therefore has a magnitude of **63 newtons**.

</div>
