## Exercise 1

A sample at $21\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ absorbs $700\,\mathrm{J}$. Its heat capacity is constant at $140\,\mathrm{J/K}$, and the sample remains in the same phase. Use $Q=mc(T_f-T_i)$ to calculate $T_f$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q&=mc\,(T_f-T_i) \\[6pt]
T_f-T_i&=\frac{Q}{mc} \\[6pt]
T_f&=T_i+\frac{Q}{mc}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Q}{mc}&=\frac{700\,\mathrm{J}}{140\,\mathrm{J/K}}=5\,\mathrm{K} \\[6pt]
T_f&=21\,^\circ\mathrm{C}+5\,^\circ\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
&=26\,^\circ\mathrm{C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sample’s heat capacity is the product $mc$ of its mass and specific heat capacity, so the given value $140\,\mathrm{J/K}$ replaces the entire product $mc$ in the formula. Since mass and specific heat capacity are positive, $mc$ is also positive. We can therefore divide both sides by $mc$. This gives the temperature change $T_f-T_i=\dfrac{Q}{mc}$, and adding $T_i$ to both sides isolates the final temperature.

The sample absorbs heat, so $Q=+700\,\mathrm{J}$. In the quotient $\dfrac{700}{140}=5$, joules cancel and kelvins remain, so the change is $5\,\mathrm{K}$. A kelvin and a degree Celsius are the same size, so a change of $5\,\mathrm{K}$ is equivalent to a change of $5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and can be added to the initial temperature in degrees Celsius.

The change is positive, as is the heat absorbed, in agreement with the sign predicted by the model. The sample’s final temperature is therefore **26 degrees Celsius**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample releases $540\,\mathrm{J}$ and reaches $17\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Its heat capacity is constant at $180\,\mathrm{J/K}$, and the sample remains in the same phase. Use $Q=mc(T_f-T_i)$ to calculate the initial temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_f-T_i&=\frac{Q}{mc} \\[6pt]
-T_i&=\frac{Q}{mc}-T_f \\[6pt]
T_i&=T_f-\frac{Q}{mc}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Q}{mc}&=\frac{-540\,\mathrm{J}}{180\,\mathrm{J/K}}=-3\,\mathrm{K} \\[6pt]
T_i&=17\,^\circ\mathrm{C}-(-3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}) \\[6pt]
&=17\,^\circ\mathrm{C}+3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}=20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given value $180\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is the product $mc$, which is positive, so we can divide by $mc$ to obtain the change $T_f-T_i$. To isolate $T_i$, subtract $T_f$ from both sides and then multiply both sides by $-1$. This gives the rearranged formula $T_i=T_f-\dfrac{Q}{mc}$.

Since the sample releases heat, $Q$ is negative in the model, so write $Q=-540\,\mathrm{J}$. The quotient $\dfrac{-540}{180}$ equals $-3$, so the temperature change is $-3\,\mathrm{K}$, or $-3\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Subtracting a negative number amounts to adding its opposite, giving $T_i=17+3=20$ degrees Celsius.

The result agrees with the sign of the heat exchanged. A sample that releases heat cools, so the initial temperature is higher than the final temperature. The initial temperature of the sample is **20 degrees Celsius**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sample releases $1260\,\mathrm{J}$ and cools from $25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ to $20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. Its specific heat capacity is constant at $420\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$, and the sample remains in the same phase. Use $Q=mc(T_f-T_i)$ to calculate the mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_f-T_i&=20\,^\circ\mathrm{C}-25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}=-5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}=-5\,\mathrm{K} \\[6pt]
m&=\frac{Q}{c\,(T_f-T_i)}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
c\,(T_f-T_i)&=420\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{kg\,K}}\cdot(-5\,\mathrm{K})=-2100\,\frac{\mathrm{J}}{\mathrm{kg}} \\[6pt]
m&=\frac{-1260\,\mathrm{J}}{-2100\,\mathrm{J/kg}} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{1260}{2100}\,\mathrm{kg}=\frac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.6\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First calculate the temperature change by subtracting the initial value from the final value. This gives $-5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, which equals $-5\,\mathrm{K}$ because the two scales have increments of the same size. The sample releases heat, so $Q$ is also negative and equals $-1260\,\mathrm{J}$.

The unknown $m$ appears in the product $mc(T_f-T_i)$. The specific heat capacity is positive and the temperature change is nonzero, so the product $c(T_f-T_i)$ is nonzero and both sides can be divided by it. This gives the formula rearranged for mass.

The denominator equals $420\cdot(-5)=-2100$ joules per kilogram. The numerator and denominator are both negative, so their quotient is positive, as a mass must be. The quotient $\dfrac{1260}{2100}$ reduces by a common factor of $420$ to give $\dfrac{3}{5}$. In the units, joules cancel, and dividing by joules per kilogram leaves kilograms. The sample therefore has a mass of **0.6 kilograms**, or $600\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>
