## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{2x+5}{3}=7$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\cdot\frac{2x+5}{3}&=3\cdot7 \\[6pt]
2x+5&=21 \\[6pt]
2x&=21-5=16 \\[6pt]
x&=\frac{16}{2}=8 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{check:}\qquad \frac{2\cdot8+5}{3}=\frac{21}{3}=7 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is the number $3$, which is independent of $x$, so the equation is defined for every real $x$. To clear the fraction, multiply both sides by $3$. Since $3$ is nonzero, this gives an equivalent equation by the rule for **reversible operations on both sides**. On the left, the factor $3$ cancels with the denominator, leaving the entire numerator $2x+5$. The right side becomes $21$. In other words, the quotient of $2x+5$ and $3$ equals $7$ exactly when the numerator equals $3\cdot7=21$.

Then subtract $5$ from both sides, leaving just $2x$ on the left and $21-5=16$ on the right. The coefficient of $x$ is $2$, which is nonzero, so divide by $2$ to find $x=8$.

Checking the result gives $16+5=21$ for the numerator. The quotient $\dfrac{21}{3}$ equals $7$, which is also the right side. The equation **has the unique solution** $x=8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{x-3}{5}+\dfrac{x+2}{2}=4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
10\cdot\frac{x-3}{5}+10\cdot\frac{x+2}{2}&=10\cdot4 \\[6pt]
2(x-3)+5(x+2)&=40 \\[6pt]
2x-6+5x+10&=40 \\[6pt]
7x+4&=40 \\[6pt]
7x&=36 \\[6pt]
x&=\frac{36}{7}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\text{check:}\qquad
\frac{\frac{36}{7}-3}{5}+\frac{\frac{36}{7}+2}{2}
=\frac{15}{35}+\frac{50}{14}
=\frac{3}{7}+\frac{25}{7}=\frac{28}{7}=4 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators $5$ and $2$ are numbers, so the equation is defined for every real $x$. Their least common multiple is $10$ because $5$ and $2$ have no common factor greater than one. Multiply both sides by $10$, including the term $4$ written without a fraction. Since $10$ is nonzero, the resulting equation is equivalent to the original.

In the first term, $\dfrac{10}{5}=2$, so the numerator $x-3$ is multiplied by $2$. In the second, $\dfrac{10}{2}=5$, so $x+2$ is multiplied by $5$. Expanding the products gives $2x-6+5x+10$, and collecting like terms gives $7x+4$. Subtract $4$ from both sides and divide by the nonzero coefficient $7$.

The solution $\dfrac{36}{7}$ is not an integer, so use fractions to check it. We have $\dfrac{36}{7}-3=\dfrac{15}{7}$ and $\dfrac{36}{7}+2=\dfrac{50}{7}$, and the two terms equal $\dfrac{3}{7}$ and $\dfrac{25}{7}$. Their sum is $4$, so the equation **has the unique solution** $x=\dfrac{36}{7}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{1}{x-2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{6}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-2&\ne0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\ne2 \\[6pt]
\frac{1}{x-2}&=\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{3} \\[6pt]
\frac{1}{x-2}&=\frac{5}{6}-\frac{2}{6}=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
2(x-2)\cdot\frac{1}{x-2}&=2(x-2)\cdot\frac{1}{2} \\[6pt]
2&=x-2 \\[6pt]
x&=4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="1/(x-2)+1/3;5/6" xmin=-3 xmax=8 ymin=-3 ymax=3 asintoti="x=2;y=0.3333" punti="4,0.8333,pieno" segmenti="4,0,4,0.8333" legenda="y=\frac{1}{x-2}+\frac{1}{3};y=\frac{5}{6}" titolo="The curve $y=\dfrac{1}{x-2}+\dfrac{1}{3}$ meets the line $y=\dfrac{5}{6}$ only at the point with horizontal coordinate $4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $x-2$ is zero at $x=2$, so the domain is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2\}$ and the value $2$ cannot be a solution.

Subtract $\dfrac{1}{3}$ from both sides, leaving the fraction containing the unknown alone on the left. To compute the difference, write $\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{2}{6}$, which gives $\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{2}{6}=\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

On the domain, the factor $x-2$ is nonzero, so $2(x-2)$ is also nonzero. We can therefore multiply both sides by $2(x-2)$ to obtain an equivalent equation. This leaves $2$ on the left and $x-2$ on the right. Adding $2$ gives $x=4$.

The value $4$ differs from $2$ and therefore belongs to the domain. At $x=4$, the left side equals $\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{5}{6}$. The equation **has the unique solution** $x=4$, the horizontal coordinate of the intersection of the curve and the line in the figure.

</div>
