## Exercise 1

A $375\,\mathrm{g}$ sample contains $16\%$ of a component by mass. Calculate the mass of the component and the combined mass of the other components.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathrm{comp}}
&=\frac{16}{100}\cdot375\,\mathrm{g}
=\frac{4}{25}\cdot375\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=4\cdot15\,\mathrm{g}=60\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m_{\mathrm{altri}}
&=375\,\mathrm{g}-60\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=315\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{315\,\mathrm{g}}{375\,\mathrm{g}}=\frac{21}{25}=\frac{84}{100}=84\%.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass percentage refers to the entire sample, so the base is $375\,\mathrm{g}$. By the definition of a **percentage**, the mass of the component is the base times $\dfrac{16}{100}$.

Dividing the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{16}{100}$ by $4$ gives $\dfrac{4}{25}$. Since $375=25\cdot15$, the denominator $25$ cancels with a factor in the base, leaving $4\cdot15=60$ grams.

Each gram of the sample belongs either to the specified component or to the other components. The mass of the other components is therefore the total mass minus that of the specified component, or $315\,\mathrm{g}$.

We can check the result using **complementary percentages**. The other components make up $100\%-16\%=84\%$ of the sample, and the ratio of $315\,\mathrm{g}$ to $375\,\mathrm{g}$ is indeed $\dfrac{21}{25}$, or eighty-four hundredths.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A solution contains $18\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute and $102\,\mathrm{g}$ of solvent. Calculate the mass percentage of the solute.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(18+102)\,\mathrm{g}
=120\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
\frac{m_{\mathrm{soluto}}}{m_{\mathrm{tot}}}
&=\frac{18\,\mathrm{g}}{120\,\mathrm{g}}
=\frac{6\cdot3}{6\cdot20}=\frac{3}{20}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3\cdot5}{20\cdot5}=\frac{15}{100}=15\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass percentage of the solute compares the mass of the solute with the **mass of the entire solution**. The given masses refer to two different components, so adding them gives a total of $120\,\mathrm{g}$.

Next divide the solute mass by the total mass. Both masses are in grams, so the units cancel and the ratio is dimensionless. The greatest common divisor of $18$ and $120$ is $6$, and dividing by $6$ gives $\dfrac{3}{20}$.

To write the ratio as a percentage, use the denominator $100$. Since $100=20\cdot5$, multiplying numerator and denominator by $5$ gives fifteen hundredths. The mass percentage of the solute is therefore $15\%$.

The ratio of solute to solvent is different. It is $\dfrac{18}{102}=\dfrac{3}{17}$, or about $17.6\%$, because its denominator includes only the mass of the solvent.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A binary solution contains $276\,\mathrm{g}$ of solvent. The solute makes up $8\%$ of the total mass. Calculate the total mass and the solute mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathrm{solvente}}
&=\left(1-\frac{8}{100}\right)m_{\mathrm{tot}}
=\frac{92}{100}\,m_{\mathrm{tot}},\\[6pt]
\frac{92}{100}\,m_{\mathrm{tot}}&=276\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m_{\mathrm{tot}}
&=276\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\frac{100}{92}
=3\cdot100\,\mathrm{g}=300\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m_{\mathrm{soluto}}
&=300\,\mathrm{g}-276\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=24\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solution is **binary**: it consists only of solute and solvent. Their mass percentages use the same base and sum to $100\%$, so the solvent makes up $92\%$ of the total mass.

The $276\,\mathrm{g}$ of solvent is therefore ninety-two hundredths of the unknown total mass. The factor $\dfrac{92}{100}$ is nonzero, so divide both sides by it, or equivalently multiply by $\dfrac{100}{92}$. Since $276=3\cdot92$, the factor $92$ cancels, giving a total mass of $300\,\mathrm{g}$.

Subtract the solvent mass from the total to find the solute mass, $24\,\mathrm{g}$. The result agrees with the given percentage because $8\%$ of $300\,\mathrm{g}$ is $\dfrac{8}{100}\cdot300\,\mathrm{g}=24\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>
