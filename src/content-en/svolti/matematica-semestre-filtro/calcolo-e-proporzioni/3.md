## Exercise 1

A homogeneous solution contains $14\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute in $350\,\mathrm{mL}$. Calculate the mass of solute in a $125\,\mathrm{mL}$ sample taken from the same solution.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{m}{125\,\mathrm{mL}}&=\frac{14\,\mathrm{g}}{350\,\mathrm{mL}},\\[6pt]
m\cdot350&=14\,\mathrm{g}\cdot125,\\[6pt]
m&=14\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\frac{125}{350}
=14\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\frac{5}{14}\\[6pt]
&=5\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x/25" xmin=0 xmax=400 ymin=0 ymax=17 nomi="V,m" punti="125,5;350,14" segmenti="125,0,125,5;0,5,125,5;350,0,350,14;0,14,350,14" etichette="160,4.2,(125, 5);300,15,(350, 14)" legenda="m=\dfrac{V}{25}" titolo="In the homogeneous solution, solute mass is proportional to volume, so both points lie on the same line through the origin ($V$ in milliliters, $m$ in grams)."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The solution is homogeneous, so the ratio of solute mass to volume is the same in each sample. Set up a **proportion** between the sample in question and the reference sample, with the unknown mass $m$ in the numerator of the first ratio.

By the cross-product property of proportions, the product of the extremes equals the product of the means. The volume $350\,\mathrm{mL}$ is nonzero, so we can divide by it to find $m$. Dividing both terms of $\dfrac{125}{350}$ by $25$ gives $\dfrac{5}{14}$. The factor $14$ then cancels with $14\,\mathrm{g}$, leaving $m=5\,\mathrm{g}$.

The **mass per unit volume** gives the same result. The solution contains $\dfrac{14\,\mathrm{g}}{350\,\mathrm{mL}}=\dfrac{1}{25}\,\mathrm{g/mL}$, or $40\,\mathrm{g/L}$. Multiplying by $125\,\mathrm{mL}$ again gives $5\,\mathrm{g}$. The volume sampled is smaller than the reference volume, and the mass found is correspondingly smaller than $14\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart travels $36\,\mathrm{m}$ in $15\,\mathrm{s}$ at constant speed. Find the speed and the time needed to travel a second stretch of length $60\,\mathrm{m}$ at the same speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=\frac{36\,\mathrm{m}}{15\,\mathrm{s}}
=\frac{3\cdot12}{3\cdot5}\,\mathrm{m/s}
=\frac{12}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}=2.4\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
t&=\frac{60\,\mathrm{m}}{\frac{12}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}}
=60\cdot\frac{5}{12}\,\mathrm{s}\\[6pt]
&=5\cdot5\,\mathrm{s}=25\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The speed is constant, so on any stretch it equals the **ratio of distance traveled to elapsed time**. On the first stretch this ratio is $\dfrac{36}{15}$ meters per second. The numerator and denominator have a common divisor $3$, so the speed is $\dfrac{12}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, meaning the cart travels $2.4$ meters each second.

The cart travels the second stretch at the same speed, so the ratio of its length to its duration must give the same value. The relation $v=\dfrac{s}{t}$ gives $t=\dfrac{s}{v}$ because the speed is nonzero.

Dividing by $\dfrac{12}{5}$ is equivalent to multiplying by the reciprocal $\dfrac{5}{12}$. Since $60=5\cdot12$, the factor $12$ cancels, leaving $5\cdot5=25$. Dividing meters by meters per second gives seconds, so the second stretch takes $25\,\mathrm{s}$.

To check, use the direct proportion between distance and time. The distance increases from $36\,\mathrm{m}$ to $60\,\mathrm{m}$, a factor of $\dfrac{60}{36}=\dfrac{5}{3}$. The time also increases by a factor of $\dfrac{5}{3}$, and indeed $15\cdot\dfrac{5}{3}=25$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pump transfers a given volume in $18\,\mathrm{min}$ at a constant flow rate of $5\,\mathrm{L/min}$. A second pump has a flow rate of $7.5\,\mathrm{L/min}$. Calculate how long the second pump takes to transfer the same volume.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=5\,\mathrm{L/min}\cdot18\,\mathrm{min}
=90\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]
t&=\frac{90\,\mathrm{L}}{7.5\,\mathrm{L/min}}
=\frac{900}{75}\,\mathrm{min}\\[6pt]
&=12\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{t}{18\,\mathrm{min}}=\frac{5}{7.5}=\frac{2}{3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t=\frac{2}{3}\cdot18\,\mathrm{min}=12\,\mathrm{min}.
$$

::grafico{f="90/x" xmin=0 xmax=12 ymin=0 ymax=34 nomi="Q,t" punti="5,18;7.5,12" segmenti="5,0,5,18;0,18,5,18;7.5,0,7.5,12;0,12,7.5,12" etichette="6.6,20,first pump;9.4,14,second pump" legenda="t=\dfrac{90}{Q}" titolo="For the fixed volume of $90\,\mathrm{L}$, time is inversely proportional to flow rate ($Q$ in liters per minute, $t$ in minutes)."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For a pump operating at a constant rate, the volume transferred is the **product of flow rate and time**. For the first transfer, multiply $5\,\mathrm{L/min}$ by $18\,\mathrm{min}$. The minutes cancel, giving a volume of $90\,\mathrm{L}$.

The second pump must transfer the same volume, so the time required is $90\,\mathrm{L}$ divided by its flow rate. To clear the decimal, multiply the dividend and divisor by $10$, giving $\dfrac{900}{75}=12$. Dividing liters by liters per minute gives minutes.

The volume is fixed, so flow rate times time is constant. Thus flow rate and time are **inversely proportional**. The ratio of the times is therefore the reciprocal of the ratio of the flow rates. The second flow rate is one and a half times the first, so the transfer takes two-thirds of $18\,\mathrm{min}$, or $12\,\mathrm{min}$, as in the direct calculation.

</div>
