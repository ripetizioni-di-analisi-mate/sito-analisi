## Exercise 1

The measured mass of a sample increases from $96\,\mathrm{g}$ to $108\,\mathrm{g}$. Calculate the percent increase.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta m&=(108-96)\,\mathrm{g}=12\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
r&=\frac{\Delta m}{m_0}=\frac{12\,\mathrm{g}}{96\,\mathrm{g}}
=\frac{12}{8\cdot12}=\frac{1}{8},\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{8}&=\frac{100}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{100}=\frac{12.5}{100}\\[6pt]
&=12.5\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
m_1=96\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{8}\right)=96\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\frac{9}{8}=108\,\mathrm{g}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **absolute increase** is the final value minus the initial value, or $12\,\mathrm{g}$. It is expressed in grams and does not account for the starting mass.

To find the **relative change**, divide the difference by the initial value $m_0=96\,\mathrm{g}$, the base for the comparison. The units cancel, so $r$ is dimensionless. Since $96=8\cdot12$, the ratio simplifies to $\dfrac{1}{8}$.

To express $\dfrac{1}{8}$ in hundredths, calculate $100:8=12.5$. The result is $\dfrac{12.5}{100}$, an increase of $12.5\%$. The sign of $r$ is positive, as expected because the mass increased.

The last line gives the final value using the **multiplicative factor** $1+r=\dfrac{9}{8}$. Multiplying the initial mass by this factor gives the stated $108\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tank contains $250\,\mathrm{L}$. The volume decreases by $30\%$ and then increases by $20\%$ of the remaining volume. Calculate the final volume and the overall percent change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1-\frac{30}{100}&=\frac{7}{10},
\qquad
1+\frac{20}{100}=\frac{6}{5},\\[6pt]
V_1&=250\,\mathrm{L}\cdot\frac{7}{10}
=175\,\mathrm{L},\\[6pt]
V_2&=175\,\mathrm{L}\cdot\frac{6}{5}
=35\cdot6\,\mathrm{L}=210\,\mathrm{L},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{V_2-V_0}{V_0}=\frac{210-250}{250}
=-\frac{40}{250}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{16}{100}=-16\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A decrease of $30\%$ leaves $70\%$ of the volume, so the first **multiplicative factor** is $\dfrac{7}{10}$. Applying it to $250\,\mathrm{L}$ gives $175\,\mathrm{L}$.

The problem states that the increase of $20\%$ is based on the remaining volume. Thus the base for the second step is $175\,\mathrm{L}$, and the factor $\dfrac{6}{5}$ brings it to $210\,\mathrm{L}$. The increase is therefore $35\,\mathrm{L}$, while the preceding decrease was $75\,\mathrm{L}$, because the two percentages use different bases.

For the overall comparison, the denominator is the original volume $V_0=250\,\mathrm{L}$. The difference $210-250$ is $-40$. To write $\dfrac{40}{250}$ in hundredths, divide its numerator and denominator by $2.5$. The overall change is $-16\%$, negative because the final volume is smaller than the initial volume.

Multiplying the factors gives the same result because $\dfrac{7}{10}\cdot\dfrac{6}{5}=\dfrac{42}{50}=\dfrac{84}{100}$, and $84\%$ of the initial volume corresponds to a decrease of $16\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A mass increases by $10\%$ and then decreases by $5\%$ of the mass reached. The final mass is $125.4\,\mathrm{g}$. Calculate the starting mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=\left(1+\frac{10}{100}\right)\left(1-\frac{5}{100}\right)\\[6pt]
&=\frac{11}{10}\cdot\frac{19}{20}
=\frac{209}{200},\\[6pt]
m_0\cdot\frac{209}{200}&=125.4\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
m_0&=125.4\,\mathrm{g}\cdot\frac{200}{209}
=\frac{25080}{209}\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=120\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An increase of $10\%$ corresponds to a factor of $\dfrac{11}{10}$, and a decrease of $5\%$ to a factor of $\dfrac{19}{20}$. The second change applies to the mass reached after the first, so the final mass is the initial mass multiplied by both factors. The **overall factor** is therefore their product, $F=\dfrac{209}{200}$.

The initial mass $m_0$ is the unknown in the equation $m_0\cdot F=125.4\,\mathrm{g}$. The factor $F$ is positive, so we can divide both sides by $F$, or equivalently multiply them by $\dfrac{200}{209}$. The product $125.4\cdot200$ equals $25080$, and $25080=209\cdot120$. The starting mass is therefore $120\,\mathrm{g}$.

Check the calculation by applying the two changes to $120\,\mathrm{g}$. The first increase brings the mass to $132\,\mathrm{g}$. Five percent of $132\,\mathrm{g}$ is $6.6\,\mathrm{g}$, and $132-6.6=125.4$. The decrease therefore gives the stated final value.

</div>
