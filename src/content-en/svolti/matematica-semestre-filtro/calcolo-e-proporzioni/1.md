## Exercise 1

Reduce $\dfrac{18}{30}$ to lowest terms and compare the result with $\dfrac{5}{8}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
18&=6\cdot3,\qquad 30=6\cdot5,\\[6pt]
\frac{18}{30}&=\frac{6\cdot3}{6\cdot5}=\frac{3}{5},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{5}&=\frac{3\cdot8}{5\cdot8}=\frac{24}{40},\\[6pt]
\frac{5}{8}&=\frac{5\cdot5}{8\cdot5}=\frac{25}{40},\\[6pt]
\frac{24}{40}&<\frac{25}{40}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{3}{5}<\frac{5}{8}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The greatest common divisor of the numerator and denominator of the first fraction is $6$. Dividing them by $6$ gives $3$ and $5$, which are relatively prime. Thus $\dfrac{3}{5}$ is in lowest terms and is equivalent to $\dfrac{18}{30}$.

To compare the fractions, express them with the same denominator. Since $5$ and $8$ have no common divisor greater than $1$, their least common multiple is their product $40$. Multiply the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{3}{5}$ by $8$, and those of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ by $5$. By the rule for **equivalent fractions**, the values of the two fractions stay the same.

The fractions now count parts of equal size, namely fortieths. The second contains $25$ such parts and the first $24$, so $\dfrac{5}{8}$ exceeds $\dfrac{3}{5}$ by one-fortieth. **Comparing the cross products** gives the same result because $3\cdot8=24$ is less than $5\cdot5=25$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Arrange $-\dfrac{7}{12}$, $-\dfrac{2}{3}$, and $\dfrac{1}{4}$ in increasing order.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{2}{3}&=-\frac{2\cdot4}{3\cdot4}=-\frac{8}{12},\\[6pt]
\frac{1}{4}&=\frac{1\cdot3}{4\cdot3}=\frac{3}{12},\\[6pt]
-\frac{8}{12}&<-\frac{7}{12}<\frac{3}{12},\\[6pt]
-\frac{2}{3}&<-\frac{7}{12}<\frac{1}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The least common multiple of the denominators $12$, $3$, and $4$ is $12$ because $12$ is already a multiple of $3$ and $4$. Therefore only two of the three fractions need to be rewritten. Multiply the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{2}{3}$ by $4$, and those of $\dfrac{1}{4}$ by $3$.

The common denominator is positive, so the order of the fractions is the order of their numerators $-8$, $-7$, and $3$. The only positive fraction, $\dfrac{1}{4}$, is the greatest. Of the two negative fractions, the one with numerator $-8$ comes first because $-8<-7$.

On the number line, $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ lies to the left of $-\dfrac{7}{12}$. Of two negative numbers, the one with the larger absolute value is smaller, and $\dfrac{8}{12}$ is greater than $\dfrac{7}{12}$. The requested increasing order is therefore $-\dfrac{2}{3}$, $-\dfrac{7}{12}$, $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A slider moves $\dfrac{5}{8}\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right, $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left, and $\dfrac{1}{12}\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right. Find its net displacement, taking rightward as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=\left(\frac{5}{8}-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{12}\right)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\left(\frac{5\cdot3}{24}-\frac{1\cdot8}{24}+\frac{1\cdot2}{24}\right)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{15-8+2}{24}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{9}{24}\,\mathrm{m}
=\frac{3}{8}\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.08 xmax=0.8 ymin=-0.14 ymax=0.62 vettori="0,0.45,0.625,0.45;0.625,0.3,0.291666666667,0.3;0.291666666667,0.15,0.375,0.15;0,0,0.375,0" segmenti="0,-0.05,0,0.5" etichette="0.31,0.51,1st segment;0.46,0.36,2nd segment;0.34,0.21,3rd segment;0.19,0.06,Δx;0,-0.1,start" titolo="The three displacements are $\dfrac{5}{8}\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right, $\dfrac{1}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left, and $\dfrac{1}{12}\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right. The net displacement is $\Delta x=\dfrac{3}{8}\,\mathrm{m}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Assign a positive sign to the first and third displacements because they are to the right. Assign a negative sign to the second because it is to the left. The net displacement is therefore the **signed sum** of the three displacements.

The denominators are $8$, $3$, and $12$. Their least common multiple is $24$ since $24=8\cdot3=12\cdot2$. Multiply the numerators by $3$, $8$, and $2$, respectively. The three displacements are then $15$, $8$, and $2$ twenty-fourths of a meter.

The signed sum of the numerators is $15-8+2=9$. Dividing the numerator and denominator of $\dfrac{9}{24}$ by $3$ gives $\dfrac{3}{8}$. The result is positive, so the slider stops $\dfrac{3}{8}\,\mathrm{m}$, or $37.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, **to the right** of its starting position.

</div>
