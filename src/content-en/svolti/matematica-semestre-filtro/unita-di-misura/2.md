## Exercise 1

Express $250\,\mathrm{mL}$ in cubic meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{mL}&=1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=(10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^3=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
250\,\mathrm{mL}
&=250\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
&=2.5\cdot10^{2}\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
&=2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A milliliter equals one cubic centimeter because a liter is one cubic decimeter, and a cubic decimeter contains one thousand cubic centimeters. A centimeter is ten to the power of minus two meters, so a cubic centimeter is ten to the power of minus six cubic meters. The factor is the cube of the linear factor because volume is the product of three lengths.

Therefore multiply $250$ by $10^{-6}$. To write the result in scientific notation, set $250=2.5\cdot10^2$. When powers with the same base are multiplied, their exponents add, and $2+(-6)=-4$. Thus the result is $2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, or $0.00025\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

Converting first to liters gives the same value. We have $250\,\mathrm{mL}=0.250\,\mathrm{L}$ and $1\,\mathrm{L}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so $0.250\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}=2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The cubic meter is a much larger unit than the milliliter, and the numerical value decreases accordingly.

The volume is **$2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 2

Express $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$ in meters per second.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=72\,\frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{h}}\cdot
\frac{1000\,\mathrm{m}}{1\,\mathrm{km}}\cdot
\frac{1\,\mathrm{h}}{3600\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{72\cdot1000}{3600}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{72\,000}{3600}\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
&=20\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Speed is expressed as a quotient of units, so convert the length in the numerator and the time in the denominator separately. For each, write the conversion factor so that the original unit cancels.

Kilometers are in the numerator, so the length conversion factor has them in its denominator. Hours are in the denominator of the speed and in the numerator of the time conversion factor. After cancellation, meters per second remain.

It remains to carry out the numerical operations. The product $72\cdot1000$ equals $72\,000$, and dividing by $3600$ gives $20$. Since $\dfrac{1000}{3600}=\dfrac{1}{3.6}$, converting from kilometers per hour to meters per second is equivalent to dividing the numerical value by $3.6$.

Check the result using the definition of speed. At $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in one hour, or $3600\,\mathrm{s}$, the distance traveled is $20\cdot3600=72\,000\,\mathrm{m}$, or $72\,\mathrm{km}$, as specified in the original measurement.

The speed is **$20\,\mathrm{m/s}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 3

A vehicle travels $1.8\,\mathrm{km}$ in $75\,\mathrm{s}$ along a straight line without changing direction. Calculate the magnitude of its average velocity in kilometers per hour.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{\mathrm{m}}
&=\frac{1.8\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{m}}{75\,\mathrm{s}}
=\frac{1800}{75}\,\mathrm{m/s}
=24\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
&=24\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}\cdot
\frac{1\,\mathrm{km}}{1000\,\mathrm{m}}\cdot
\frac{3600\,\mathrm{s}}{1\,\mathrm{h}}\\[6pt]
&=24\cdot3.6\,\mathrm{km/h}\\[6pt]
&=86.4\,\mathrm{km/h}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The slope of the line is $\dfrac{1800\,\mathrm{m}}{75\,\mathrm{s}}=24\,\mathrm{m/s}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the vehicle travels in one direction along a straight line, the distance traveled equals the magnitude of its displacement. Therefore calculate the ratio in meters per second. The distance is $1.8\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{m}=1800\,\mathrm{m}$, and $1800$ divided by $75$ gives $24$, so the average velocity is $24\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

One hour contains $3600$ seconds and one kilometer contains $1000$ meters, so converting to kilometers per hour multiplies the numerical value by $\dfrac{3600}{1000}=3.6$. The result is $24\cdot3.6=86.4$.

The calculation can also be done without first converting to meters per second. The time interval in hours is $\dfrac{75}{3600}\,\mathrm{h}=\dfrac{1}{48}\,\mathrm{h}$, so the average velocity is $1.8\cdot48=86.4\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Both methods give the same value.

In the figure the average velocity is the slope of the line joining the origin to the point with coordinates $75\,\mathrm{s}$ and $1.8\,\mathrm{km}$.

The magnitude of the average velocity is **$86.4\,\mathrm{km/h}$**.

</div>
