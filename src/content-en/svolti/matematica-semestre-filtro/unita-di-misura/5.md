## Exercise 1

A fiber has diameter $4.2\,\mu\mathrm{m}$. Express it in nanometers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mu\mathrm{m}&=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}=10^{3}\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}=10^3\,\mathrm{nm}\\[6pt]
d&=4.2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=4.2\cdot10^3\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}
=4.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{nm}\\[6pt]
&=4200\,\mathrm{nm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The prefix micro corresponds to $10^{-6}$ and the prefix nano to $10^{-9}$. A nanometer is ten to the power of minus nine meters. To compare the two units, write $10^{-6}$ as the product of $10^{-9}$ and another power of ten. Since $-6=3+(-9)$, we have $10^{-6}=10^3\cdot10^{-9}$, so a micrometer contains $10^3$ nanometers.

Writing the conversion factor in terms of that power makes it possible to read off the numerical value in nanometers. The product $10^3\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}$ is one nanometer multiplied by one thousand, so $4.2\,\mu\mathrm{m}=4.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{nm}$.

The nanometer is smaller than the micrometer, so it takes more nanometers to express the same length, as the calculation shows. Converting the result back to meters recovers the given value because $4200\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}=4.2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}$. Writing $4.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{nm}$ preserves the two significant figures of the original measurement, while the trailing zeros in $4200\,\mathrm{nm}$ leave the precision ambiguous.

The diameter of the fiber is **$4.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{nm}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 2

A rectangular opening has sides $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $35\,\mathrm{mm}$. Calculate its area in square meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b&=18\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}=0.18\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
h&=35\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}=0.035\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
A&=b\,h=0.18\,\mathrm{m}\cdot0.035\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=0.0063\,\mathrm{m^2}
=6.3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
A=18\,\mathrm{cm}\cdot3.5\,\mathrm{cm}=63\,\mathrm{cm^2}
=63\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}=6.3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}.
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-6.5 xmax=19 ymin=-1.8 ymax=4.5 poligoni="0,0 18,0 18,3.5 0,3.5" etichette="9,-0.8,b = 18 cm;-3.3,1.75,h = 35 mm;9,1.75,A" titolo="The rectangular opening drawn to scale. The height of $35\,\mathrm{mm}$ equals $3.5\,\mathrm{cm}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

To obtain square meters, convert the two sides to meters separately. The first is in centimeters and the second in millimeters, so the conversion factors differ. The prefix centi corresponds to $10^{-2}$, so $b=0.18\,\mathrm{m}$. The prefix milli corresponds to $10^{-3}$, so $h=0.035\,\mathrm{m}$.

The area of the rectangle is its base times its height. The product of the numbers is $0.18\cdot0.035=0.0063$, and the product of the units is $\mathrm{m}\cdot\mathrm{m}=\mathrm{m^2}$. In scientific notation the result is $6.3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The value is small because both sides are fractions of a meter, $0.18$ and $0.035$, and their numerical product is smaller than either factor.

The same value is obtained by first calculating the area in square centimeters. We have $35\,\mathrm{mm}=3.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the area is $63\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. One square centimeter equals $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ because the linear factor $10^{-2}$ must be squared, so $63\,\mathrm{cm^2}=6.3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

The area of the opening is **$6.3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 3

The internal dimensions of a rectangular container are $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, $50\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $0.025\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate its capacity in milliliters and its volume in cubic meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
50\,\mathrm{mm}&=50\cdot10^{-1}\,\mathrm{cm}=5\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
0.025\,\mathrm{m}&=0.025\cdot100\,\mathrm{cm}=2.5\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]
V&=8\cdot5\cdot2.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}
=40\cdot2.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}
=100\,\mathrm{cm^3}\\[6pt]
&=100\,\mathrm{mL}\\[6pt]
&=100\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three dimensions are given in three different units, so express them all in the same unit before multiplying. The centimeter is convenient because the resulting volume in cubic centimeters has the same numerical value in milliliters.

One centimeter contains ten millimeters, so $50\,\mathrm{mm}=5\,\mathrm{cm}$. One meter contains one hundred centimeters, so $0.025\,\mathrm{m}=2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The volume of the rectangular box is the product of its three dimensions, $8\cdot5\cdot2.5=100$, and its unit is the cubic centimeter.

One milliliter is one cubic centimeter, so the capacity is $100\,\mathrm{mL}$. To convert to cubic meters, use the cube of the linear conversion factor from centimeters to meters, $(10^{-2})^3=10^{-6}$. This gives $100\cdot10^{-6}=10^{2-6}=10^{-4}$.

Check the result using liters. We have $100\,\mathrm{mL}=0.1\,\mathrm{L}$ and $1\,\mathrm{L}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so the volume is $0.1\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

The capacity is **$100\,\mathrm{mL}$** and the volume is **$10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$**.

</div>
