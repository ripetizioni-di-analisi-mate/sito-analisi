## Exercise 1

In the formula $F=F_0+kx^2$, $F$ and $F_0$ are forces and $x$ is a length. Find the dimension of the constant $k$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
[F]&=[F_0]=MLT^{-2}\\[6pt]
[kx^2]&=[F]=MLT^{-2}\\[6pt]
[k]\cdot L^2&=MLT^{-2}\\[6pt]
[k]&=\frac{MLT^{-2}}{L^2}=ML^{1-2}T^{-2}=ML^{-1}T^{-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Force is mass times acceleration, so its dimension is $M\cdot LT^{-2}=MLT^{-2}$. The right-hand side of the formula is a sum, and only quantities with the same dimension can be added. To add the quadratic term to a force, both terms must have the dimension of force. Thus $[kx^2]=MLT^{-2}$.

The dimension of a product is the product of the dimensions, so $[kx^2]=[k]\cdot[x]^2=[k]\cdot L^2$. Dividing both sides by $L^2$ isolates $[k]$. Since length squared already contributes two factors of length, one remains in the denominator of the dimension of $k$. The calculation with exponents is $L^{1}\cdot L^{-2}=L^{-1}$.

The result can be expressed as an SI unit. Since $1\,\mathrm{N}=1\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s^2}$, the constant $k$ is measured in newtons per square meter, or $\mathrm{kg/(m\,s^2)}$. For example, with $k=200\,\mathrm{N/m^2}$ and $x=0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, the term $kx^2$ equals $200\cdot0.01=2\,\mathrm{N}$, which is indeed a force.

The dimension of the constant is **$[k]=ML^{-1}T^{-2}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 2

A normal force of magnitude $16\,\mathrm{N}$ is distributed uniformly over an area of $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Calculate the pressure in pascals and give its dimension.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=4\,\mathrm{cm^2}=4\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
p&=\frac{F}{A}=\frac{16\,\mathrm{N}}{4\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}}\\[6pt]
&=4\cdot10^{4}\,\mathrm{N/m^2}
=4\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}\\[6pt]
[p]&=\frac{[F]}{[A]}=\frac{MLT^{-2}}{L^2}=ML^{-1}T^{-2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The average pressure is the ratio $p=\dfrac{F}{A}$ of the magnitude of the normal force to the area over which the force is distributed. A pascal is a newton per square meter, so convert the area before taking the ratio. One square centimeter equals $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ because the linear factor $10^{-2}$ must be squared, so $A=4\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

In the quotient, $\dfrac{16}{4}=4$ and $\dfrac{1}{10^{-4}}=10^{4}$, so the pressure is $4\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$, or $40\,000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The calculation can be checked in another unit. The force per square centimeter is $\dfrac{16\,\mathrm{N}}{4\,\mathrm{cm^2}}=4\,\mathrm{N/cm^2}$, and one square meter contains $10^4$ square centimeters, so the same pressure over one square meter corresponds to $4\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{N}$.

To find the dimension, divide the dimension of force by the square of the dimension of length. The factor $L$ from the force and the factor $L^2$ from the area give $L^{1-2}=L^{-1}$.

The pressure is **$4\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{Pa}$** and its dimension is **$ML^{-1}T^{-2}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 3

A pressure difference has the form $\Delta p=C\rho^a g^b h^c$, where $C$ is dimensionless, $\rho$ is a density, $g$ is an acceleration, and $h$ is a length. Find the exponents that make the formula dimensionally consistent.

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\Delta p]&=ML^{-1}T^{-2}\\[6pt]
[\rho^a]&=(ML^{-3})^a=M^aL^{-3a}\\[6pt]
[g^b]&=(LT^{-2})^b=L^bT^{-2b},
\qquad [h^c]=L^c\\[6pt]
[\rho^a g^b h^c]&=M^a L^{-3a+b+c}T^{-2b}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
a=1\\
-2b=-2\\
-3a+b+c=-1
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{cases}
a=1\\
b=1\\
c=-1+3a-b=1
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density is mass divided by volume, so $[\rho]=ML^{-3}$. Acceleration has dimension $LT^{-2}$, and $h$ has dimension $L$. Raising a dimension to a power multiplies each exponent in that dimension by the power, so $[\rho^a]=M^aL^{-3a}$ and $[g^b]=L^bT^{-2b}$. The coefficient $C$ is dimensionless, so it does not affect the dimension of the right-hand side.

The exponents of mass, length, and time must agree on the two sides. All three equalities must hold at once, so they form a system. The mass exponents give $a$, and the time exponents give $b$. Substituting both into the equality for length gives $c$, since $-3+1+c=-1$ gives $c=1$.

The formula therefore becomes $\Delta p=C\rho gh$. The dimensional check determines the exponents but leaves the number $C$ undetermined. Finding the value of the coefficient $C$ requires a physical model. For a fluid at rest the hydrostatic pressure law gives $C=1$.

The exponents are **$a=b=c=1$**.

</div>
