## Exercise 1

A container collects $450\,\mathrm{mL}$ of water in $30\,\mathrm{s}$. Calculate the average volumetric flow rate in cubic meters per second.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=450\,\mathrm{mL}=450\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}=4.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
Q&=\frac{V}{\Delta t}=\frac{4.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}}{30\,\mathrm{s}}\\[6pt]
&=0.15\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}\\[6pt]
&=1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **average volumetric flow rate** is the volume passing through a cross section divided by the time interval, $Q=\dfrac{V}{\Delta t}$. The average flow rate refers to the volume that passes during the entire interval. Since the time is already in seconds, only the volume needs to be converted to cubic meters before division.

One milliliter is one cubic centimeter, and one cubic centimeter equals $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ because the linear factor $10^{-2}$ must be cubed. Thus $450\,\mathrm{mL}=450\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, or $4.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ in scientific notation.

In the division, divide the numerical coefficients separately and retain the power of ten. The quotient $\dfrac{4.5}{30}$ equals $0.15$, and since $0.15=1.5\cdot10^{-1}$, the product with $10^{-4}$ becomes $1.5\cdot10^{-5}$.

Check the result in liters per minute. At that average rate, the container would collect twice as much in one minute as it did in thirty seconds, or $900\,\mathrm{mL}=0.9\,\mathrm{L}$. Also, multiplying $1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ by $60\,\mathrm{s}$ gives $9\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, again equal to $0.9\,\mathrm{L}$.

The average flow rate is **$1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 2

A sample of mass $0.072\,\mathrm{kg}$ occupies $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Calculate its density in kilograms per cubic meter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=20\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}
=2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
\rho&=\frac{m}{V}=\frac{0.072\,\mathrm{kg}}{2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}}\\[6pt]
&=0.036\cdot10^{5}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\\[6pt]
&=3.6\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density is the ratio $\rho=\dfrac{m}{V}$ of the sample’s mass to its volume. The mass is already expressed in the required unit. For the volume, use one millionth of a cubic meter per cubic centimeter, so $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}=20\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}=2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

In the quotient, divide the coefficients, $\dfrac{0.072}{2}=0.036$. The power in the denominator moves to the numerator with the sign of its exponent reversed because $\dfrac{1}{10^{-5}}=10^{5}$. This gives $0.036\cdot10^5$, and writing $0.036=3.6\cdot10^{-2}$ yields $3.6\cdot10^{3}$.

The volume in cubic meters has a very small numerical value and is in the denominator, so the numerical value of the density in SI units is large.

Use laboratory units to check the value. The mass is $72\,\mathrm{g}$, so the density is $\dfrac{72\,\mathrm{g}}{20\,\mathrm{cm^3}}=3.6\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Converting from $\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ to $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ multiplies the numerical value by $\dfrac{10^{-3}}{10^{-6}}=10^3$, giving $3600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ again.

The density is **$3.6\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 3

Fluid flows through a pipe of cross-sectional area $2.5\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ at a rate of $3\,\mathrm{L}$ per minute. Calculate its average speed across the section, given that it equals the volumetric flow rate divided by the cross-sectional area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=2.5\,\mathrm{cm^2}=2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
Q&=\frac{3\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}}{60\,\mathrm{s}}
=5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}\\[6pt]
v&=\frac{Q}{A}=\frac{5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}}
{2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}}\\[6pt]
&=2\cdot10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m/s}
=0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Converting the flow rate involves both volume and time. One liter equals $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and one minute equals $60\,\mathrm{s}$, so $3\,\mathrm{L/min}$ corresponds to $\dfrac{3\cdot10^{-3}}{60}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$. Since $\dfrac{3}{60}=0.05=5\cdot10^{-2}$, the flow rate is $5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

The cross section has the dimension of area and requires the square of the linear conversion factor, so $1\,\mathrm{cm^2}=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $A=2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

In the quotient, $\dfrac{5}{2.5}=2$ and $\dfrac{10^{-5}}{10^{-4}}=10^{-1}$, so the speed is $0.20$. Two of the three factors of meters cancel in the final ratio, leaving meters per second.

The formula $v=\dfrac{Q}{A}$ has a geometric interpretation. In one second a cylinder of fluid with base area $A$ and length $v\cdot1\,\mathrm{s}$ crosses the section. Its volume is the volume delivered by the flow in one second. The same calculation in centimeters gives $Q=\dfrac{3000\,\mathrm{cm^3}}{60\,\mathrm{s}}=50\,\mathrm{cm^3/s}$ and $v=\dfrac{50}{2.5}\,\mathrm{cm/s}=20\,\mathrm{cm/s}$, or $0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

The average speed of the fluid is **$0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$**.

</div>
