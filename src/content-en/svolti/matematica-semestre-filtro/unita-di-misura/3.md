## Exercise 1

Express $1.2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ in kilograms per cubic meter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{g}&=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
\rho&=1.2\,\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\mathrm{cm^3}}\cdot
\frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}}{1\,\mathrm{g}}\cdot
\frac{1\,\mathrm{cm^3}}{10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}}\\[6pt]
&=1.2\cdot\frac{10^{-3}}{10^{-6}}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\\[6pt]
&=1.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
=1200\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density is the ratio of mass to volume, so convert the two units separately. A gram is one thousandth of a kilogram, and a cubic centimeter is one millionth of a cubic meter because the linear factor $10^{-2}$ must be cubed.

The mass conversion factor has grams in its denominator, canceling the grams in the density. The volume conversion factor has cubic centimeters in its numerator because the density has them in its denominator. After cancellation, kilograms per cubic meter remain.

The ratio of the factors is one thousand. Indeed, the quotient rule for powers with the same base gives $\dfrac{10^{-3}}{10^{-6}}=10^{-3-(-6)}=10^{3}$. Multiplying $1.2$ by $10^3$ gives $1200$.

Check the result using the definition of density. One cubic meter contains $10^6$ cubic centimeters, each with a mass of $1.2\,\mathrm{g}$, so the mass of one cubic meter is $1.2\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{g}=1200\,\mathrm{kg}$.

The density is **$1200\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 2

A solution contains $0.1\,\mathrm{mol}$ of solute in a final volume of $250\,\mathrm{mL}$. Calculate the molar concentration in moles per liter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=250\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{L}=0.25\,\mathrm{L}\\[6pt]
c&=\frac{n}{V}=\frac{0.1\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.25\,\mathrm{L}}\\[6pt]
&=0.1\cdot4\,\mathrm{mol/L}\\[6pt]
&=0.4\,\mathrm{mol/L}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **molar concentration** is the ratio $c=\dfrac{n}{V}$ of the amount of solute in moles to the final volume of the solution. Convert the final volume to liters to express the ratio in moles per liter.

The prefix milli corresponds to $10^{-3}$, so $250\,\mathrm{mL}=0.25\,\mathrm{L}$, or one-quarter of a liter. Dividing by $0.25$ is equivalent to multiplying by $4$ because $0.25=\dfrac{1}{4}$. The result is therefore $0.1\cdot4=0.4$.

Check the value by considering one liter of solution. The solution is homogeneous, so one liter, which is four times $250\,\mathrm{mL}$, contains four times the amount of solute, or $0.4\,\mathrm{mol}$. This is precisely what $0.4$ moles per liter means.

Dividing by the volume in milliliters would give $4\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol/mL}$, an equivalent value expressed in a different unit from the one required.

The molar concentration is **$0.4\,\mathrm{mol/L}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 3

A solution contains $3.6\,\mathrm{g}$ of solute and occupies $120\,\mathrm{mL}$. Calculate the mass concentration in grams per liter and in kilograms per cubic meter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=120\,\mathrm{mL}=0.120\,\mathrm{L}\\[6pt]
c_m&=\frac{m_{\mathrm{s}}}{V}=\frac{3.6\,\mathrm{g}}{0.120\,\mathrm{L}}
=30\,\mathrm{g/L}\\[6pt]
&=30\cdot\frac{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}}{10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}}
=30\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.03*x" xmin=0 xmax=200 ymin=0 ymax=6 nomi="V,m (g)" punti="120,3.6" segmenti="120,0,120,3.6;0,3.6,120,3.6" etichette="60,3.85,3.6 g in 120 mL" titolo="Mass of solute in a volume $V$ of the homogeneous solution, with $V$ in milliliters. The slope of the line is the mass concentration, $\dfrac{3.6\,\mathrm{g}}{120\,\mathrm{mL}}=0.030\,\mathrm{g/mL}=30\,\mathrm{g/L}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Divide the solute mass by the total volume of solution. The volume is given in milliliters. Convert it to liters because the concentration is required in grams per liter, giving $120\,\mathrm{mL}=0.120\,\mathrm{L}$. The quotient $\dfrac{3.6}{0.120}$ equals $\dfrac{3600}{120}$, or $30$.

Converting grams to kilograms and liters to cubic meters introduces the same factor in the numerator and denominator, so the numerical value stays the same. Indeed, one gram is $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and one liter is $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, so $1\,\mathrm{g/L}=1\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

In a homogeneous solution, the mass of solute in a portion is proportional to the volume of that portion. The graph of mass against volume is therefore a straight line through the origin, and its slope is the mass concentration. In one liter, or $1000\,\mathrm{mL}$, the mass of solute is $0.030\cdot1000=30\,\mathrm{g}$, in agreement with the calculation.

The mass concentration is **$30\,\mathrm{g/L}$**, equal to **$30\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$**.

</div>
