## Exercise 1

Express $75\,\mathrm{mm}$ in meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{m}&=1000\,\mathrm{mm}\\[6pt]
75\,\mathrm{mm}
&=75\,\mathrm{mm}\cdot\frac{1\,\mathrm{m}}{1000\,\mathrm{mm}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{75}{1000}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=0.075\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The prefix milli denotes one thousandth, so $1\,\mathrm{mm}=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$ and one meter contains $1000$ millimeters. The equality $1\,\mathrm{m}=1000\,\mathrm{mm}$ gives the **conversion factor** $\dfrac{1\,\mathrm{m}}{1000\,\mathrm{mm}}$, which equals $1$ because the numerator and denominator express the same length in two ways. Multiplying the measurement by this factor preserves its physical value.

The conversion factor has millimeters in its denominator, so it cancels the millimeters in the measurement and leaves meters. With the ratio inverted, $\dfrac{1000\,\mathrm{mm}}{1\,\mathrm{m}}$, the unit of the product would be $\mathrm{mm^2/m}$, which differs from the required unit.

Dividing $75$ by $1000$ moves the decimal point three places to the left, giving $0.075$, or $7.5\cdot10^{-2}$ in scientific notation. The meter is a larger unit than the millimeter, so the same length has a smaller numerical value in meters, as the calculation shows.

The length is **$0.075\,\mathrm{m}$**, or $7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>


## Exercise 2

Express $25\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ in square meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{cm}&=10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
1\,\mathrm{cm^2}&=(10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^2=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
25\,\mathrm{cm^2}&=25\cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
&=2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}\\[6pt]
&=0.0025\,\mathrm{m^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One square centimeter is the area of a square with side length $1\,\mathrm{cm}$. Each centimeter is one hundredth of a meter, so that square has side length $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$ and area $(10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^2$. Since a square centimeter contains two factors of length, the overall factor is $10^{-2}\cdot10^{-2}=10^{-4}$, by the rule for **converting a unit raised to a power** with $n=2$.

Multiplying $25$ by $10^{-4}$ moves the decimal point four places to the left, so $25\,\mathrm{cm^2}=0.0025\,\mathrm{m^2}$. In scientific notation we write $25=2.5\cdot10$, so the product becomes $2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

One square meter contains $100\cdot100=10\,000$ square centimeters, so $25\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ amounts to $25$ ten-thousandths of a square meter, in agreement with the result.

Using only the linear factor $10^{-2}$ would give $0.25\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the area of a square with side length $50\,\mathrm{cm}$. The original area is that of a square with side length $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and is smaller by a factor of one hundred.

The area is **$0.0025\,\mathrm{m^2}$**.

</div>


## Exercise 3

A tube consists of three consecutive straight sections of lengths $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$, $375\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $40\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate the total length in meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
375\,\mathrm{mm}&=375\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}
=0.375\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
40\,\mathrm{cm}&=40\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
=0.40\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
\ell_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(2.5+0.375+0.40)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=(2.875+0.40)\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=3.275\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three sections all have the dimension of length, so their lengths can be added. Before adding the numerical values, however, they must be expressed in the same unit because $2.5+375+40$ would mix meters, millimeters, and centimeters. Choose the meter, the unit required for the result.

The prefix milli corresponds to $10^{-3}$, so $375\,\mathrm{mm}=0.375\,\mathrm{m}$. The prefix centi corresponds to $10^{-2}$, so $40\,\mathrm{cm}=0.40\,\mathrm{m}$. Once millimeters and centimeters have been converted to meters, the numerical values can be added directly.

The sum of the first two lengths is $2.875\,\mathrm{m}$. Adding the third gives $3.275\,\mathrm{m}$. The figure shows the sections placed end to end, so the right end of the last section is $3.275\,\mathrm{m}$ from the start of the tube.

The same calculation in centimeters gives $250+37.5+40=327.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, again equivalent to $3.275\,\mathrm{m}$.

The total length of the tube is **$3.275\,\mathrm{m}$**.

</div>
