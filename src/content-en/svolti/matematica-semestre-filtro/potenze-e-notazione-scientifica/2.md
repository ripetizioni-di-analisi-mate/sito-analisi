## Exercise 1

Write $0.00072$ in scientific notation.

$$
10^{-4}=0.0001\le0.00072<0.001=10^{-3}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{0.00072}{10^{-4}}\\[6pt]
&=0.00072\cdot10^{4}\\[6pt]
&=7.2
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
0.00072=72\cdot10^{-5}=7.2\cdot10^{-4}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.5 xmax=11.4 ymin=-1.7 ymax=3 punti="1,1.2,pieno;10,1.2,vuoto;7.2,1.2,pieno" linee="1,1.2 10,1.2" segmenti="1,0,1,1.2;10,0,10,1.2;7.2,0,7.2,1.2" etichette="1,2.05,10⁻⁴;10,2.05,10⁻³;7.2,2.05,x" titolo="One unit on the horizontal axis represents $10^{-4}$. The number $x=0.00072$ corresponds to $7.2$ units and lies between $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-3}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient must be at least one and less than ten. Move the decimal point until the coefficient has just one digit to its left, and use a power of ten to compensate for the shift.

To find the exponent, locate the two consecutive powers of ten that bracket the number. Since $0.0001\le0.00072<0.001$, the number lies in the interval beginning at $10^{-4}$, so its exponent of ten is $n=-4$.

The coefficient is the number divided by $10^{-4}$. Dividing by $10^{-4}$ is equivalent to multiplying by $10^4$, which moves the decimal point four places to the right and gives $a=7.2$. This value satisfies $1\le a<10$, so the expression is normalized.

The intermediate form $72\cdot10^{-5}$ has the same value, but its coefficient exceeds ten. Dividing $72$ by ten and multiplying the power of ten by ten changes $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$. The result is **$0.00072=7.2\cdot10^{-4}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $(3\cdot10^4)(4\cdot10^{-2})$ and give the result in scientific notation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3\cdot10^4)(4\cdot10^{-2})&=(3\cdot4)\cdot10^{4+(-2)}\\[6pt]
&=12\cdot10^2\\[6pt]
&=1.2\cdot10\cdot10^2\\[6pt]
&=1.2\cdot10^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents. Since multiplication is commutative, the two coefficients $3$ and $4$ can be grouped together and the two powers of ten grouped separately. The powers have the same base, so their product is $10^{4+(-2)}=10^2$.

The coefficient of the product exceeds ten, so divide it by ten and increase the exponent by one. Indeed, $12=1.2\cdot10$, and the factor $10$ combines with $10^2$ to give $10^3$. The theorem on the **exponent of a product of normalized numbers** guarantees that one such shift suffices, because the product of two normalized coefficients is less than $100$.

To check, write out the factors. We have $3\cdot10^4=30\,000$ and $4\cdot10^{-2}=0.04$, and their product $30\,000\cdot0.04$ equals $1200$. The product is **$1.2\cdot10^3$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\dfrac{1.8\cdot10^{-7}}{6\cdot10^{-4}}$ and write the result in scientific notation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1.8\cdot10^{-7}}{6\cdot10^{-4}}
&=\frac{1.8}{6}\cdot\frac{10^{-7}}{10^{-4}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1.8}{6}\cdot10^{-7-(-4)}\\[6pt]
&=0.3\cdot10^{-3}\\[6pt]
&=3\cdot10^{-1}\cdot10^{-3}\\[6pt]
&=3\cdot10^{-4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quotient of two products can be written as the product of two quotients, one for the coefficients and one for the powers of ten. For the powers, subtract the exponent of the denominator from that of the numerator. The exponent is therefore $-7-(-4)=-7+4=-3$.

The quotient of the coefficients is $0.3$, since $1.8=0.3\cdot6$. The coefficient in the numerator is smaller than that in the denominator, so their quotient is less than one and the expression $0.3\cdot10^{-3}$ is not normalized.

To normalize the result, multiply the coefficient by ten. The power of ten must therefore be divided by ten. Write $0.3=3\cdot10^{-1}$, so the product $10^{-1}\cdot10^{-3}$ equals $10^{-4}$.

As a check, multiplying $3\cdot10^{-4}$ by the denominator $6\cdot10^{-4}$ gives $18\cdot10^{-8}=1.8\cdot10^{-7}$, the numerator. The quotient is **$3\cdot10^{-4}$**.

</div>
