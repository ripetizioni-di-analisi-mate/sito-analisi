## Exercise 1

Calculate $10^{-3}\cdot10^{5}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
10^{-3}\cdot10^{5}&=10^{-3+5}\\[6pt]
&=10^{2}\\[6pt]
&=100.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
10^{-3}\cdot10^{5}&=\frac{1}{10^{3}}\cdot10^{5}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{100\,000}{1000}\\[6pt]
&=100.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-4.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1 ymax=2.5 punti="-3,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" vettori="-3,1,2,1" segmenti="-3,0,-3,1;2,0,2,1" etichette="-1.5,1.3,+5" nomi="n,y" titolo="The horizontal axis shows the exponent $n$. In the product $10^{-3}\cdot10^{5}$, the exponent $-3$ increases by $5$ to become $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two powers have the same base. To multiply them, add their exponents, keeping each exponent's sign. Both powers have coefficient one. By the rule for the **product of powers with the same base**, the resulting exponent is $-3+5=2$, so the product is $10^2=100$.

The second calculation explains the rule. By the definition of a negative exponent, $10^{-3}$ is the reciprocal of $10^3$, namely $\dfrac{1}{1000}$. Multiplying $10^5$ by $\dfrac{1}{1000}$ therefore amounts to dividing $100\,000$ by $1000$, and the quotient is $100$. The three factors of $10$ in the denominator cancel three of the five factors of $10$ in the numerator, leaving two.

On the number line for the exponents, multiplying by $10^5$ moves the exponent five units to the right, from $-3$ to $2$. The result is **$10^2=100$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\dfrac{10^{-3}}{10^{-5}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{10^{-3}}{10^{-5}}&=10^{-3-(-5)}\\[6pt]
&=10^{-3+5}\\[6pt]
&=10^{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{10^{-3}}{10^{-5}}&=10^{-3}\cdot\frac{1}{10^{-5}}\\[6pt]
&=10^{-3}\cdot10^{5}\\[6pt]
&=10^{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To divide the powers, subtract the exponent of the denominator. Since that exponent is negative, subtracting it amounts to adding its absolute value. By the rule for the **quotient of powers with the same base**, the resulting exponent is therefore $-3-(-5)=-3+5=2$. The entire number $-5$ is subtracted, including its sign.

The second calculation gives the same result using the reciprocal. Dividing by $10^{-5}$ is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal. Since $10^{-5}=\dfrac{1}{10^5}$, the reciprocal of $10^{-5}$ is $10^5$, so the quotient becomes the product in the preceding exercise.

The sizes of the powers provide another check. The numerator $10^{-3}=0.001$ is greater than the denominator $10^{-5}=0.00001$, so the quotient is greater than one. Since $0.001$ is one hundred times $0.00001$, the quotient is **$10^2=100$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\dfrac{\left(3^{-2}\right)^2\cdot3^7}{3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(3^{-2})^2\cdot3^7}{3}
&=\frac{3^{(-2)\cdot2}\cdot3^7}{3^1}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3^{-4}\cdot3^7}{3^1}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3^{-4+7}}{3^1}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3^3}{3^1}\\[6pt]
&=3^{3-1}\\[6pt]
&=3^{2}\\[6pt]
&=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the inner exponent by the outer exponent gives $-4$. Indeed, by the rule for a **power of a power**, $(3^{-2})^2$ is the product of two factors equal to $3^{-2}$, so its exponent is $(-2)\cdot2=-4$.

Therefore, add $7$ to $-4$ to find the exponent in the numerator. The factors $3^{-4}$ and $3^7$ have the same base, so their product is $3^{-4+7}=3^3$.

The denominator is $3^1$, so the last step is to subtract one. The quotient $\dfrac{3^3}{3^1}$ has exponent $3-1=2$, and $3^2=9$.

The result is positive even though the negative exponent $-2$ appears, because $3^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{9}$ is positive and every power of a positive base is positive. The value of the expression is **$9$**.

</div>
