## Exercise 1

Calculate $2\cdot10^{-3}+5\cdot10^{-4}$.

$$
2\cdot10^{-3}=2\cdot10\cdot10^{-4}=20\cdot10^{-4}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\cdot10^{-3}+5\cdot10^{-4}&=20\cdot10^{-4}+5\cdot10^{-4}\\[6pt]
&=(20+5)\cdot10^{-4}\\[6pt]
&=25\cdot10^{-4}\\[6pt]
&=2.5\cdot10^{-3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-2 xmax=29 ymin=-1.2 ymax=5 punti="0,2;20,2;25,2" vettori="0,2,20,2;20,2,25,2" segmenti="20,0,20,2;25,0,25,2" etichette="10,3,20 units;22.5,3,5 units;20.15,0.55,20;25.15,0.55,25" titolo="The two terms are $20$ and $5$ units on the same scale, $10^{-4}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

To add the terms, express them using the same power of ten. Then add their coefficients and write the result in scientific notation.

The power $10^{-4}$ serves as a counting unit. Since $10^{-3}=10\cdot10^{-4}$, the first term $2\cdot10^{-3}$ contains $20$ units of $10^{-4}$, while the second contains $5$. Use the smaller exponent, $-4$, so that both coefficients remain integers.

Factoring out $10^{-4}$ gives $(20+5)\cdot10^{-4}=25\cdot10^{-4}$. The coefficient $25$ exceeds ten, so write $25=2.5\cdot10$ and increase the exponent by one, from $-4$ to $-3$.

In decimal form the terms are $0.002$ and $0.0005$, and their sum is $0.0025$, which equals $2.5\cdot10^{-3}$. The sum is **$2.5\cdot10^{-3}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\sqrt{9\cdot10^{-6}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{9\cdot10^{-6}}&=\sqrt{9}\,\sqrt{10^{-6}}\\[6pt]
&=3\cdot10^{-\frac{6}{2}}\\[6pt]
&=3\cdot10^{-3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\left(3\cdot10^{-3}\right)^2=9\cdot10^{-6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand is positive. Since $9\ge0$ and $10^{-6}>0$, the rule for the **root of a product** applies, so take the roots of the coefficient and of the power of ten separately.

The root of the coefficient is known. For a power of ten with an even exponent, take the square root by halving the exponent. We have $\sqrt{9}=3$ because $3$ is the nonnegative number whose square is $9$. Similarly, $\sqrt{10^{-6}}=10^{-3}$ because $10^{-3}$ is positive and the power-of-a-power rule gives $\left(10^{-3}\right)^2=10^{(-3)\cdot2}=10^{-6}$.

Squaring the result gives the original radicand, so $3\cdot10^{-3}$ has both properties that define the square root of $9\cdot10^{-6}$. The coefficient $3$ lies between $1$ and $10$, so the result is already in scientific notation.

In decimal form, $\sqrt{0.000009}=0.003$. The root is **$3\cdot10^{-3}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\sqrt{2.5\cdot10^{-7}+1.1\cdot10^{-7}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2.5\cdot10^{-7}+1.1\cdot10^{-7}}
&=\sqrt{(2.5+1.1)\cdot10^{-7}}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{3.6\cdot10^{-7}}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{3.6\cdot10\cdot10^{-8}}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{36\cdot10^{-8}}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{36}\,\sqrt{10^{-8}}\\[6pt]
&=6\cdot10^{-4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two terms in the radicand already have the same exponent. The root of a sum generally differs from the sum of the roots, so first add the terms under the radical. Factoring out $10^{-7}$ leaves coefficients whose sum is $2.5+1.1=3.6$.

After adding the coefficients, replace $3.6$ with $36$ and decrease the exponent by one. Indeed, $10^{-7}=10\cdot10^{-8}$, and the factor $10$ combines with $3.6$ to give $36$. The value of the radicand is unchanged, and the square root of the coefficient $36$ is an integer.

The new exponent $-8$ is even, so $\sqrt{10^{-8}}=10^{-4}$. The rule for the root of a product then gives $\sqrt{36}\cdot10^{-4}=6\cdot10^{-4}$. With exponent $-7$, the root of the power would have exponent $-\dfrac{7}{2}$, which is not an integer.

Squaring the result recovers the radicand, since $\left(6\cdot10^{-4}\right)^2=36\cdot10^{-8}=3.6\cdot10^{-7}$. The root is **$6\cdot10^{-4}$**.

</div>
