## Exercise 1

The measured edge length of a cube is $3.0\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$. Calculate its volume and report it to two significant figures.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\ell^3\\[6pt]
&=(3.0\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m})^3\\[6pt]
&=3.0^3\cdot\left(10^{-2}\right)^3\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
&=27\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
&=2.7\cdot10\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[6pt]
&=2.7\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of a cube with edge length $\ell$ is the product of its three equal edge lengths, $V=\ell^3$.

Cubing the edge length raises both the coefficient and the power of ten to the third power. By the rule for the **power of a product**, each factor is cubed. We have $3.0^3=27$ and, by the rule for a power of a power, $\left(10^{-2}\right)^3=10^{(-2)\cdot3}=10^{-6}$. The unit is cubed as well, so meters become cubic meters.

The coefficient $27$ exceeds ten, so write $27=2.7\cdot10$ and increase the exponent by one, from $-6$ to $-5$.

The edge length $3.0\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$ has two significant figures, and the volume is reported with the same number. The normalized value already has the required two significant figures, so no rounding is needed.

The edge length is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$, and a cube with edge length $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ has volume $27\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Since $1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, the check agrees with the calculation. The volume is **$2.7\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample has mass $9.60\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g}$ and molar mass $6.0\cdot10^{1}\,\mathrm{g/mol}$. Calculate the amount of substance and round to two significant figures.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=\frac{m}{M}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{9.60\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g}}
           {6.0\cdot10^{1}\,\mathrm{g/mol}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{9.60}{6.0}\cdot10^{-3-1}\,\mathrm{mol}\\[6pt]
&=1.6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Molar mass gives mass per mole. To find the number of moles, divide the mass by the molar mass. In symbols, $n=\dfrac{m}{M}$, where $m$ is the sample mass and $M$ its molar mass.

The mass units cancel. Indeed, dividing grams by grams per mole gives $\mathrm{g}\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{mol}}{\mathrm{g}}=\mathrm{mol}$, the unit of amount of substance.

Divide the coefficients and the powers of ten separately. The quotient of the coefficients is $\dfrac{9.60}{6.0}=1.6$, since $6\cdot1.6=9.6$. For the powers of ten, subtract the exponent of the denominator from that of the numerator, giving $-3-1=-4$. The coefficient $1.6$ lies between $1$ and $10$, so the result is already normalized.

The mass has three significant figures and the molar mass has two. A quotient retains as many significant figures as the measurement with the fewest, so the result has two. The amount of substance is **$1.6\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol}$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sample has mass $1.26\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and volume $4.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Calculate its density in scientific notation to two significant figures. In an exact halfway case, round up.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m}{V}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1.26\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{kg}}
           {4.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{1.26}{4.0}\cdot10^{-2-(-6)}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\\[6pt]
&=0.315\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\\[6pt]
&=3.15\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\\[6pt]
&\approx3.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density is the ratio of mass to volume. The given units are already SI units, so the density is expressed in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ without any conversion.

Divide the coefficients and powers of ten separately. The quotient of the coefficients is $\dfrac{1.26}{4.0}=0.315$ and the exponent is $-2-(-6)=-2+6=4$.

Normalize the exact coefficient $0.315$ by adjusting the exponent. The coefficient is less than one, so multiply it by ten to get $3.15$ and decrease the exponent by one, from $4$ to $3$.

The volume has two significant figures and the mass has three, so report the result to two significant figures. The third digit of the coefficient is five, and the value is halfway between two results with two significant figures. The possible values are $3.1$ and $3.2$, and $3.15$ is $0.05$ away from each. The convention in the problem selects $3.2$.

The result is about three times the density of water, which is $1.0\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The density is **$3.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$**.

</div>
