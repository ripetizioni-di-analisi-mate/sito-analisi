## Exercise 1

Calculate $\sqrt{(-5)^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(-5)^2}&=\sqrt{(-5)\cdot(-5)}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{25}\\[6pt]
&=5\\[6pt]
&=|-5|.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;25" xmin=-6.5 xmax=6.5 ymin=0 ymax=30 punti="-5,25,pieno,A;5,25,pieno,B" segmenti="-5,0,-5,25;5,0,5,25" legenda="y=x^2;y=25" titolo="The points with vertical coordinate $25$ have horizontal coordinates $-5$ and $5$. The root $\sqrt{25}$ is the nonnegative horizontal coordinate, $5$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

By definition, the square root denotes the nonnegative value. A number and its opposite have the same square, so the result equals the absolute value of the original number.

First calculate the radicand. The product $(-5)\cdot(-5)$ has two negative factors, so it is positive and equals $25$. The equation $y^2=25$ has two solutions, $5$ and $-5$. On the graph of $y=x^2$, these are the horizontal coordinates of the points $A$ and $B$, both of which have vertical coordinate $25$.

The notation $\sqrt{25}$ denotes only the nonnegative solution, so $\sqrt{25}=5$. The result differs from the original number $-5$, in agreement with the identity $\sqrt{a^2}=|a|$, valid for every real number $a$. With $a=-5$ it gives $\sqrt{(-5)^2}=|-5|=5$.

The square root of a square equals the original number only if that number is nonnegative. For a negative number, the result is its absolute value. The value of the expression is **$5$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

How many significant figures does $0.00450$ have?

$$
\begin{aligned} 0.00450 &=4.50\cdot10^{-3},\\[6pt]
N&=3\text{ significant figures.}\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The significant figures begin with the first nonzero digit. In $0.00450$, the first nonzero digit is $4$, in the thousandths place.

The zeros before the first nonzero digit serve only to locate the decimal point. The zeros in the ones, tenths, and hundredths places put $4$ in the thousandths place and do not count. Scientific notation shows this directly. In $4.50\cdot10^{-3}$, these zeros are represented by the power $10^{-3}$ and do not appear in the coefficient.

Trailing zeros after significant decimal digits indicate the precision of the measurement and do count. The final zero in $0.00450$ shows that the measurement is given to the nearest hundred-thousandth and is therefore part of the coefficient $4.50$.

The significant figures are therefore $4$, $5$, and the final zero. The number $0.0045$ has the same value but only two significant figures because it indicates lower precision. The number $0.00450$ has **three significant figures**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The measurements $4.682\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}$ and $4.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}$ are for the full and empty container, respectively. Calculate their difference and round to the coarser of the two decimal places to which the measurements are given.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4.682\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}&=4682\,\mathrm{g},\\[6pt]
4.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}&=4200\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta m
&=(4.682-4.2)\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=0.482\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=482\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&\approx 500\,\mathrm{g}\\[6pt]
&=5\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The full container is heavier, so the difference is positive. The difference between the readings is the mass of the contents.

The two measurements have the same exponent $3$, so subtract their coefficients directly. We have $4.682-4.2=0.482$, and $0.482\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}=482\,\mathrm{g}$.

The measurement $4.2\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{g}$ is given to the nearest hundred grams. Its last significant digit, the $2$ in the coefficient, represents $2\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{g}$. The first measurement is given to the nearest gram. In a difference, retain the last place specified by the less precise measurement, so round the result to the nearest hundred.

First subtract the reported values, then round the result to the nearest hundred. In $482$, the first discarded digit is the $8$ in the tens place. Since it is greater than five, the hundreds digit increases from $4$ to $5$. The mass of the contents is **$5\cdot10^2\,\mathrm{g}$**.

</div>
