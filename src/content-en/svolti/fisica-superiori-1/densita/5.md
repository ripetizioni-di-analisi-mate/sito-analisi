## Exercise 1

Two samples each have volume $17\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ and their densities are $2$ and $10\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. By how much is the second sample's mass greater?

::grafico{f="2*x;10*x" xmin=0 xmax=20 ymin=0 ymax=200 nomi="V (cm³),m (g)" punti="17,34,pieno,A;17,170,pieno,B" segmenti="17,34,17,170" legenda="ρ = 2 g/cm³;ρ = 10 g/cm³" titolo="At equal volumes the vertical gap is the mass difference"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta m &= (10-2)\cdot17\\[6pt]
&= 136\,\mathrm{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two samples have the same volume. Subtract the first density from the second and multiply the difference by the common volume. This is equivalent to finding both masses separately. The positive sign confirms that the second sample has greater mass. The calculation gives $\Delta m=(10-2)\cdot17=136\,\mathrm{g}$.

Calculating the products separately gives $34\,\mathrm{g}$ for the first sample and $170\,\mathrm{g}$ for the second. The second contains eight extra grams per cubic centimetre of the common volume. Their difference would therefore grow by another eight grams if one cubic centimetre were added to both samples.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two bodies each have mass $84\,\mathrm{g}$. Their densities are $7$ and $14\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the volume of the denser body?

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \dfrac{84}{14}\\[6pt]
&= 6\,\mathrm{cm}^3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equal mass, the denser sample contains more mass in each cubic centimetre. It needs fewer cubic centimetres to contain the given mass. Use the greater density in the denominator of $V=\dfrac{m}{\rho}$, and check that the inverse product recovers the mass. For the denser body, $V=\dfrac{84}{14}=6\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

The other sample has density $7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$, so its volume is $\dfrac{84}{7}=12\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Both products $7\cdot12$ and $14\cdot6$ give the same mass in grams. With equal masses, doubling the density halves the volume.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two samples A and B have respective volumes $7$ and $14\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ and masses $21$ and $28\,\mathrm{g}$. Which is denser?

- A is denser than B
- B is denser than A
- They have the same density
- They cannot be compared because the volumes differ

A is denser than B.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two samples differ in both mass and volume. To find which contains more mass in the same volume, divide each mass by its corresponding volume. Sample A has the higher ratio even though B has greater mass overall. This occurs because the second volume increases by a larger factor than the mass. For A the ratio is $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$, and for B it is $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

To make the comparison explicit, scale the second sample to the volume of the first. A $7\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ portion of material B would have mass $14\,\mathrm{g}$, while A has $21\,\mathrm{g}$. The seven-gram difference now compares equal volumes, so it directly shows which material has the greater density.

</div>
