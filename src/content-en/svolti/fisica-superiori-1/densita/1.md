## Exercise 1

A homogeneous sample has mass $210\,\mathrm{g}$ and volume $70\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. What is its density?

::grafico{f="3*x" xmin=0 xmax=80 ymin=0 ymax=240 nomi="V (cm³),m (g)" punti="70,210,pieno,A" titolo="The slope of the line through the sample's point gives its density"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \dfrac{210}{70}\\[6pt]
&= 3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mass measures the amount of matter in the sample and volume gives the space occupied. Divide the first value by the second, because density is the mass per cubic centimetre. As a check, multiply the resulting density by the volume to recover the original mass. The calculation gives $\rho=\dfrac{210}{70}=3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

The proportion can be read for a $10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ portion, which occupies one seventh of the volume. Since the sample is homogeneous, this portion contains one seventh of its total mass, or $30\,\mathrm{g}$. The ratio of these two quantities gives the same three grams per cubic centimetre found from the whole sample.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A material has density $7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the mass of $21\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of this material?

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \rho V\\[6pt]
&= 7\cdot21\\[6pt]
&= 147\,\mathrm{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density gives the mass contained in each cubic centimetre. Volume counts the cubic centimetres present, so multiply density by volume to find the mass. The cubic centimetre in the density denominator cancels the cubic centimetre in the volume, leaving grams. Therefore $m=\rho V=7\cdot21=147\,\mathrm{g}$.

The same mass can be built in two parts. The first $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ contribute $140\,\mathrm{g}$. The remaining cubic centimetre adds $7\,\mathrm{g}$. Their sum shows that density is the coefficient that converts each increase in volume to an increase in mass.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sample has a scale reading of $105\,\mathrm{g}$ and density $7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is its volume?

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \dfrac{m}{\rho}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{105}{7}\\[6pt]
&= 15\,\mathrm{cm}^3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale gives total mass and density tells how many grams belong to a cubic centimetre. Dividing mass by that value gives the number of cubic centimetres in the sample. Multiplying the resulting volume by the mass of each unit volume checks the result. Thus $V=\dfrac{m}{\rho}=\dfrac{105}{7}=15\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

An explicit check is $7\cdot15=105\,\mathrm{g}$. If the balance read $35\,\mathrm{g}$ more for the same material, the volume increase would be $\dfrac{35}{7}=5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. The same relation can therefore also compare two mass readings.

</div>
