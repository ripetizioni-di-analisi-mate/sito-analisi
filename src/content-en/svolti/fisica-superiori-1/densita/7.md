## Exercise 1

A sheet has area $70\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, thickness $1\,\mathrm{mm}$, and density $5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is its mass?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.4 xmax=7 ymin=-0.5 ymax=4.2 poligoni="0.5,1 5,1 6.3,2.3 1.8,2.3;0.5,1 0.5,0.65 5,0.65 5,1;5,1 5,0.65 6.3,1.95 6.3,2.3" etichette="3.4,1.72,A = 70 cm²;3.0,0.23,h = 1 mm" titolo="The sheet's geometric measurements. Diagram not to scale"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= 5V\\[6pt]
&= 35\,\mathrm g
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sheet's volume is its area times its thickness. Because the area is in square centimetres, express the thickness in centimetres first. Multiplying the resulting volume by density converts cubic centimetres to grams. As a check, a thicker sheet of the same area and material would have greater mass. The volume is $V=70\cdot0.1=7\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, so $m=5V=35\,\mathrm g$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous cube has side $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is its mass?

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \rho l^3\\[6pt]
&= 2\cdot7^3\\[6pt]
&= 686\,\mathrm g
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a cube multiply three copies of the edge length. Applying the same operation to the unit gives cubic centimetres. Density is the mass of each cubic centimetre, so its product with volume gives the total mass. Doubling the edge would multiply the mass by eight. It follows that $m=\rho l^3=2\cdot7^3=686\,\mathrm g$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A material has density $8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the mass of $22\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of this material?

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \rho V\\[6pt]
&= 8\cdot22\\[6pt]
&= 176\,\mathrm g
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density gives the mass contained in each cubic centimetre. Volume counts the cubic centimetres present, so multiply density by volume to find the mass. The cubic centimetre in the density denominator cancels the cubic centimetre in the volume, leaving grams. Therefore $m=\rho V=8\cdot22=176\,\mathrm g$.

</div>
