## Exercise 1

A sheet has area $70\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, thickness $1\,\mathrm{mm}$, and density $5\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is its mass?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.4 xmax=7 ymin=-0.5 ymax=4.2 poligoni="0.5,1 5,1 6.3,2.3 1.8,2.3;0.5,1 0.5,0.65 5,0.65 5,1;5,1 5,0.65 6.3,1.95 6.3,2.3" etichette="3.4,1.72,A = 70 cm²;3.0,0.23,h = 1 mm" titolo="The sheet's geometric measurements. Diagram not to scale"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= 5V\\[6pt]
&= 35\,\mathrm{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sheet's volume is its area times its thickness. Because the area is in square centimetres, express the thickness in centimetres first. Multiplying the resulting volume by density converts cubic centimetres to grams. As a check, a thicker sheet of the same area and material would have greater mass. The volume is $V=70\cdot0.1=7\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, so $m=5V=35\,\mathrm{g}$.

As a check, first find the mass per unit area. At a thickness of $0.1\,\mathrm{cm}$, each square centimetre contains $0.1\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of material and has mass $0.5\,\mathrm{g}$. Multiplying by $70\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ gives the same mass of $35\,\mathrm{g}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous cube has side $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is its mass?

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \rho l^3\\[6pt]
&= 2\cdot7^3\\[6pt]
&= 686\,\mathrm{g}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a cube multiply three copies of the edge length. Applying the same operation to the unit gives cubic centimetres. Density is the mass of each cubic centimetre, so its product with volume gives the total mass. Doubling the edge would multiply the mass by eight. It follows that $m=\rho l^3=2\cdot7^3=686\,\mathrm{g}$.

As a check, split the cube into seven layers one centimetre thick with base area $49\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Each has volume $49\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and mass $98\,\mathrm{g}$. Adding their masses gives $7\cdot98=686\,\mathrm{g}$. This makes the product of the three lengths explicit and shows the cancellation of cubic centimetres when finding the mass.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A profile $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ long has a constant external cross-sectional area of $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and a longitudinal hole of uniform cross-section. It is made of material of density $2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and has mass $81\,\mathrm{g}$. Ignoring the mass of the air, find the cross-sectional area of the hole and the percentage of the external section it occupies.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    V_m &= \frac{81}{2.7}\,\mathrm{cm^3}=30\,\mathrm{cm^3},\\[5pt]
    A_m &= \frac{30}{15}\,\mathrm{cm^2}=2\,\mathrm{cm^2},\\[5pt]
    A_{\mathrm f} &= (4-2)\,\mathrm{cm^2}=2\,\mathrm{cm^2},\\[5pt]
    \frac{A_{\mathrm f}}{A_e}&=\frac24=50\,\%.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given density belongs to the solid material, so it determines the volume of the wall alone. The hole and the wall each have constant cross-section along the whole length. Dividing the material volume by the length gives the area of material in one cross-section. The external area includes that material and the hole, so the difference gives the required area. As a check, a solid profile would have volume $4\cdot15=60\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and mass $162\,\mathrm{g}$. The actual mass is half that value. At the same density and length, this means half the section is material and half is the hole.

</div>
