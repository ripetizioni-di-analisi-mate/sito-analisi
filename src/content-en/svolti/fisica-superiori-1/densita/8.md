## Exercise 1

A homogeneous material has masses $24$ and $104\,\mathrm g$ at volumes $3$ and $13\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. What density can be obtained from the mass-volume graph's slope?

::grafico{f="8*x" xmin=0 xmax=16 ymin=0 ymax=128 nomi="V (cm³),m (g)" punti="3,24,pieno,P;13,104,pieno,Q" segmenti="3,24,13,24;13,24,13,104" titolo="Find the slope between P and Q from the two coordinate differences"}

$$
8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a graph of mass against volume, the slope is the change in mass divided by the change in volume. Subtract the masses and subtract the volumes in the same order. The slope gives the number of grams added when the volume increases by one cubic centimetre. The ratio $\rho=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta V}=\dfrac{80}{10}$ gives $8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A liquid has mass $800\,\mathrm g$ and volume $0.8\,\mathrm L$. What is its density in $\mathrm{kg/L}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \dfrac{0.8}{0.8}\\[6pt]
&= 1\,\mathrm{kg/L}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator of the result is in kilograms. Divide the supplied grams by one thousand and keep the volume, already in litres. The ratio gives the mass corresponding to one litre. The litre could also be converted to cubic metres to obtain the SI unit. The calculation gives $\rho=\dfrac{0.8}{0.8}=1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Mix $80\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of liquid with density $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of liquid with density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Assuming additive volumes, what is the mixture density?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \dfrac{80+20}{80+10}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{10}{9}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each liquid's mass is the product of its density and volume. With no loss, add the two masses. The additivity assumption allows the volumes to be added for the denominator. The first liquid occupies more space, so the ratio should be closer to its density. Therefore $\rho=\dfrac{80+20}{80+10}=\dfrac{10}{9}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

</div>
