## Exercise 1

A homogeneous material has masses $24$ and $104\,\mathrm{g}$ at volumes $3$ and $13\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. What density can be obtained from the mass-volume graph's slope?

::grafico{f="8*x" xmin=0 xmax=16 ymin=0 ymax=128 nomi="V (cm³),m (g)" punti="3,24,pieno,P;13,104,pieno,Q" segmenti="3,24,13,24;13,24,13,104" titolo="Find the slope between P and Q from the two coordinate differences"}

$$
8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a graph of mass against volume, the slope is the change in mass divided by the change in volume. Subtract the masses and subtract the volumes in the same order. The slope gives the number of grams added when the volume increases by one cubic centimetre. The ratio $\rho=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta V}=\dfrac{80}{10}$ gives $8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

Check the slope using direct proportionality for the homogeneous material. The first point gives $\dfrac{24}{3}=8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$, and the second ratio $\dfrac{104}{13}$ gives the same value. The equal ratios confirm that in this case the line through the two measurements also passes through the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A graph has liquid volume on the vertical axis and total container mass on the horizontal axis. Two readings are $(80\,\mathrm{g};20\,\mathrm{mL})$ and $(111.5\,\mathrm{g};55\,\mathrm{mL})$. Find the liquid's density and the mass of the empty container.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta m}
       &=\frac{55-20}{111.5-80}\,\mathrm{cm^3/g}
         =\frac{10}{9}\,\mathrm{cm^3/g},\\[5pt]
    \rho &= 0.90\,\mathrm{g/cm^3},\\[5pt]
    m_0 &= (80-0.90\cdot20)\,\mathrm{g}=62\,\mathrm{g}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In this graph, the slope gives the volume increase in cubic centimetres for each gram added. Its units are therefore the reciprocal of the units of density. Inverting the slope gives the density, which converts each volume reading to the mass of the liquid alone. The first point gives $18\,\mathrm{g}$, to be subtracted from the total of $80\,\mathrm{g}$. The second point supplies a separate check. At $55\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ the liquid has mass $49.5\,\mathrm{g}$. The difference $111.5-49.5$ gives the same tare. The mass at zero volume lies on the horizontal axis because the axes of the usual graph have been swapped.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A balance reads $45\,\mathrm{g}$ for an empty container, $65\,\mathrm{g}$ with $10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of liquid and $95\,\mathrm{g}$ with $25\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. Treat these three measurements as exact. A fourth reading pairs $30\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ with a mass of $112\pm0.5\,\mathrm{g}$. Check whether the fourth reading is consistent with the linear model. Then assume the model still holds, the fourth volume was recorded incorrectly, and the mass is exactly $112\,\mathrm{g}$. What volume corresponds to that mass?

::grafico{f="45+2*x" xmin=0 xmax=36 ymin=35 ymax=122 nomi="V (cm³),m (g)" punti="0,45,pieno;10,65,pieno;25,95,pieno;30,112,pieno,P;30,105,vuoto" titolo="The first three readings are on the line. Point P represents the fourth reading."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \rho &= \frac{65-45}{10}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}=2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3},\\[5pt]
    m(25)&=(45+2\cdot25)\,\mathrm{g}=95\,\mathrm{g},\\[5pt]
    m(30)&=(45+2\cdot30)\,\mathrm{g}=105\,\mathrm{g},\\[5pt]
    105\,\mathrm{g}&\notin [111.5;112.5]\,\mathrm{g},\\[5pt]
    V&=\frac{112-45}{2}\,\mathrm{cm^3}=33.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two measurements give the container's mass and the mass increase per cubic centimetre. The third reading is a check, as it lies where the line predicts it. At $30\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ the prediction of $105\,\mathrm{g}$ is even below the lower bound of the fourth reading, $111.5\,\mathrm{g}$. The stated mass uncertainty is too small to account for the discrepancy. The second question assumes that the volume was recorded incorrectly. Subtracting the tare leaves $67\,\mathrm{g}$ of liquid, which corresponds to $33.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. This result depends on the two assumptions, because the discrepancy alone does not identify the cause.

</div>
