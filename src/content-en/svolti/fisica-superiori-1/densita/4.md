## Exercise 1

An object has external volume $12\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, an internal cavity of $2\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, and material density $9\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Neglecting the mass of air, what is its mass?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.9 xmax=8.8 ymin=-0.6 ymax=5.5 poligoni="0,0 8,0 8,5 0,5" linee="2,1.5 6,1.5 6,3.5 2,3.5 2,1.5" etichette="4,2.5,2 cm³;4,4.45,12 cm³" titolo="12 cm³ is the external volume, and 2 cm³ is the internal cavity. Diagram not to scale"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= 9\cdot10\\[6pt]
&= 90\,\mathrm g
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The external volume includes material and cavity. The cavity contains none of the wall, so subtract its volume from the external volume before multiplying. The stated density is the material's density. With negligible air, multiplying it by the wall volume gives the object's entire mass. The material volume is $12-2=10\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, so $m=9\cdot10=90\,\mathrm g$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A hollow object has mass $34\,\mathrm g$ and external volume $22\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. What is its mean density based on external volume?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{media} &= \dfrac{34}{22}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{17}{11}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The question specifies external volume, which includes the cavity. Divide the total mass by that volume. The ratio describes mean mass per cubic centimetre of occupied space and may be considerably below the wall material's density. The latter would require the volume of the wall alone. Thus $\rho_{media}=\dfrac{34}{22}=\dfrac{17}{11}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object has external volume $27\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, mass $66\,\mathrm g$, and material density $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is the cavity volume if the mass of air is negligible?

$$
27-(22) = 5\,\mathrm{cm}^3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given mass comes from the wall material because air is neglected. Divide that mass by the material density to find the volume of the wall. The cavity occupies the difference from the outside volume. The two resulting volumes must add up to the reported external volume. The material volume is $66/3=22\,\mathrm{cm}^3$, so the cavity is $27-(22)=5\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

</div>
