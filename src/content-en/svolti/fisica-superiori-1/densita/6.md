## Exercise 1

Mix $70\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of liquid with density $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of liquid with density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Assuming additive volumes, what is the mixture density?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.5 xmax=85 ymin=-0.8 ymax=1.9 linee="0,0 70,0;70,0 80,0" punti="0,0,pieno;70,0,pieno;80,0,pieno" etichette="31,0.35,70 cm³;75,0.65,10 cm³" titolo="The component at density 1 g/cm³ occupies most of the volume"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \dfrac{70+20}{70+10}\\[6pt]
&= 1.125\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each liquid's mass is the product of its density and volume. With no loss, add the two masses. The additivity assumption allows the volumes to be added for the denominator. The first liquid occupies more space, so the ratio should be closer to its density. Therefore $\rho=\dfrac{70+20}{70+10}=1.125\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample has density $0.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ and volume $27\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. If it is divided into two equal homogeneous parts without changing the material, what is the density of either part?

- $0.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $0.4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $1.6\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$
- $0.2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$

$$
\rho'=\dfrac{m/2}{V/2}=\dfrac{m}{V}=0.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the sample into equal parts multiplies the volume by one half. Since the material is homogeneous, each part also has half the initial mass. The two factors cancel in the ratio, so each part has the same density as the original sample. In the ratio $\rho=m/V$ the two factors of one half cancel.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous material has masses $21$ and $84\,\mathrm g$ at volumes $3$ and $12\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. What density can be obtained from the mass-volume graph's slope?

$$
7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a graph of mass against volume, the slope is the change in mass divided by the change in volume. Subtract the masses and subtract the volumes in the same order. The slope gives the number of grams added when the volume increases by one cubic centimetre. The ratio $\rho=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta V}=\dfrac{63}{9}$ gives $7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

</div>
