## Exercise 1

A material's density is $1.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is it in SI units?

::grafico{f="1000*x" xmin=0 xmax=2.25 ymin=0 ymax=2250 nomi="ρ (g/cm³),ρ (kg/m³)" punti="1.7,1700,pieno,A" titolo="A represents the same density expressed in two units"}

$$
\rho = 1700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density is a ratio, so the conversions act in the numerator and denominator. A gram is one thousandth of a kilogram, and a cubic centimetre is one millionth of a cubic metre. Their conversion factors have a ratio of one thousand, which multiplies the old numerical value. Since $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, the density is $\rho=1700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

As a check, take one litre of the same substance. A litre contains a thousand cubic centimetres, so its mass is $1700\,\mathrm{g}=1.7\,\mathrm{kg}$. A cubic metre contains a thousand litres, giving $1700\,\mathrm{kg}$ per cubic metre. This recovers the SI value without converting both units of the ratio at once.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A liquid has mass $700\,\mathrm{g}$ and volume $0.7\,\mathrm{L}$. What is its density in $\mathrm{kg/L}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \dfrac{0.7}{0.7}\\[6pt]
&= 1\,\mathrm{kg/L}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator of the result is in kilograms. Divide the supplied grams by one thousand and keep the volume, already in litres. The ratio gives the mass corresponding to one litre. The litre could also be converted to cubic metres to obtain the SI unit. The calculation gives $\rho=\dfrac{0.7}{0.7}=1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$.

The full conversion can be checked using $0.7\,\mathrm{L}=7\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The ratio $\dfrac{0.7}{7\cdot10^{-4}}$ is $1000$ in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The number differs from the value in kilograms per litre because the reference volume changes, while the physical density stays the same.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A laboratory sheet gives $\rho=4.8\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{g/mm^3}$. Express the density in SI units and find the mass of $4.5\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of material.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \rho &= 4.8\cdot10^{-3}\cdot1000\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
         = 4.8\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}\\[5pt]
         &=4800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3},\\[5pt]
    m &= 4.8\cdot4.5\,\mathrm{g}=21.6\,\mathrm{g}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One cubic centimetre contains a thousand cubes with sides of one millimetre. To obtain grams per cubic centimetre, multiply the mass of one cubic millimetre by a thousand. Conversion to SI units requires a second factor of a thousand, because both the mass and the volume units change. In the second calculation it is simpler to keep grams and cubic centimetres, since the volume already matches the unit in the denominator. As a check, use $V=4.5\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ with the SI density. The product is $0.0216\,\mathrm{kg}$, the same mass.

</div>
