## Exercise 1

A material's density is $1.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What is it in SI units?

::grafico{f="1000*x" xmin=0 xmax=2.25 ymin=0 ymax=2250 nomi="ρ (g/cm³),ρ (kg/m³)" punti="1.7,1700,pieno,A" titolo="A represents the same density expressed in two units"}

$$
\rho = 1700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density is a ratio, so the conversions act in the numerator and denominator. A gram is one thousandth of a kilogram, and a cubic centimetre is one millionth of a cubic metre. Their conversion factors have a ratio of one thousand, which multiplies the old numerical value. Since $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, the density is $\rho=1700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A liquid has mass $700\,\mathrm g$ and volume $0.7\,\mathrm L$. What is its density in $\mathrm{kg/L}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \dfrac{0.7}{0.7}\\[6pt]
&= 1\,\mathrm{kg/L}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator of the result is in kilograms. Divide the supplied grams by one thousand and keep the volume, already in litres. The ratio gives the mass corresponding to one litre. The litre could also be converted to cubic metres to obtain the SI unit. The calculation gives $\rho=\dfrac{0.7}{0.7}=1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous sample has mass $240\,\mathrm g$ and volume $80\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. What is its density?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \dfrac{240}{80}\\[6pt]
&= 3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mass measures the amount of matter in the sample and volume gives the space occupied. Divide the first value by the second, because density is the mass per cubic centimetre. As a check, multiply the resulting density by the volume to recover the original mass. The calculation gives $\rho=\dfrac{240}{80}=3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

</div>
