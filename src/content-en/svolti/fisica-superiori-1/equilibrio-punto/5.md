## Exercise 1

On an incline, weight contributes $12\,\mathrm N$ downhill and a rope pulls $8\,\mathrm N$ uphill. The friction limit is $5\,\mathrm N$. With uphill positive, what is the actual friction component at rest?

::grafico{f="12-x" punti="8,4,pieno,A" nomi="T (N),fₓ (N)" xmin=0 xmax=17 ymin=-6 ymax=14 titolo="With uphill positive, the 8 N tension requires 4 N of friction in the same direction"}

$$
8\,\mathrm N+f_x-12\,\mathrm N=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_x=4\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight still tends to move the block down the ramp even with the rope. Equilibrium needs four newtons uphill. Because the required magnitude is less than five newtons, the result is compatible with rest. The limit is used for the check, not substituted directly into the balance equation.

With the same normal force, there is a margin of $1\,\mathrm N$. If the pull were reduced to $7\,\mathrm N$, required friction would rise to $5\,\mathrm N$ and the contact would reach its static threshold.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The downhill weight component is $18\,\mathrm N$ and the static limit is $7\,\mathrm N$. What minimum uphill tension in a parallel rope makes rest possible?

$$
T_{\min}=(18-7)\,\mathrm N=11\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A weaker rope would require friction to carry more than seven newtons. The minimum tension reduces the remaining downslope weight to seven newtons. At that threshold, friction acts uphill and its sum with tension equals eighteen newtons.

At the upper limit friction points downhill and the tension balances the weight component plus seven newtons, so $T_{\max}=(18+7)\,\mathrm N=25\,\mathrm N$. Rest is therefore possible for $11\,\mathrm N\leq T\leq25\,\mathrm N$. With a tension greater than $25\,\mathrm N$ static friction is no longer enough and the block slides up the ramp.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An uphill rope pulls a stationary block with $22\,\mathrm N$. Weight contributes $14\,\mathrm N$ downhill, and the normal reaction is $40\,\mathrm N$. What minimum static coefficient allows rest?

$$
f_x=(14-22)\,\mathrm N=-8\,\mathrm N,\qquad\mu_{s,\min}=\frac{8}{40}=0.2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rope outweighs the weight component, so the block would tend to slide uphill. The required friction points downhill and has magnitude eight newtons. To find the minimum coefficient, compare this magnitude, not its negative component, with the normal reaction.

Taking uphill as positive gives $f_x=-8\,\mathrm N$. With $\mu_s=0.20$, the limit equals $8\,\mathrm N$. With a coefficient of $0.25$, the limit would rise to $10\,\mathrm N$ and actual friction would still have magnitude $8\,\mathrm N$.

</div>
