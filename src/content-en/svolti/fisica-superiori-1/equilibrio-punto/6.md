## Exercise 1

Two symmetric cords support a lamp weighing $36\,\mathrm N$. The sine of their angle above horizontal is $3/5$. What is each tension?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,-4,3,T;0,0,4,3;0,0,0,-6,P" etichette="2.4,1.25,T" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-7 ymax=4 titolo="The vertical parts of the two tensions balance the 36 N weight, with 6 N per drawing unit"}

$$
T=\frac{36\,\mathrm N}{2\cdot\frac35}=30\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal components cancel by symmetry. Each cord must therefore provide eighteen newtons vertically. Only three fifths of its tension is vertical, so its magnitude must exceed eighteen. In the diagram each unit of length represents six newtons.

With sine $\dfrac35$, the positive cosine is $\dfrac45$. Each rope therefore pulls horizontally with $24\,\mathrm N$ in its own direction. Vertically, the two $18\,\mathrm N$ contributions add to give the full $36\,\mathrm N$ weight.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two symmetric cords support a $42\,\mathrm N$ load with $35\,\mathrm N$ of tension each. What is the sine of the angle of the cords above horizontal?

$$
\sin\alpha=\frac{42}{2\cdot35}=0.6.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two tensions supply equal upward contributions and together must equal the weight. Divide each contribution, half the weight, by the full tension in that cord to find its vertical fraction. A fraction below one is consistent with cords tilted from vertical.

This gives $\sin\alpha=\dfrac{21}{35}=0.6$, and hence $\cos\alpha=0.8$ for the acute angle. Each rope has a horizontal component of $28\,\mathrm N$. They act in opposite directions, and their cancellation checks the second axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vertical tie and an oblique rope hold a $58\,\mathrm N$ load. A $24\,\mathrm N$ rightward force balances the oblique rope's horizontal component. The vertical tie carries $26\,\mathrm N$. What is the oblique tension?

$$
\begin{aligned}T_{x}&=-24\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]T_y&=(58-26)\,\mathrm N=32\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]T&=\sqrt{24^2+32^2}\,\mathrm N=40\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rope's horizontal component opposes the twenty-four-newton force. Vertically, the oblique rope carries the weight remaining after the vertical tension. The components are the legs of a right triangle, so Pythagoras gives the rope's tension magnitude.

The inclined rope's components are $-24\,\mathrm N$ and $+32\,\mathrm N$. The vertical check gives $(26+32-58)\,\mathrm N=0$, and the horizontal residual is $(24-24)\,\mathrm N=0$. The magnitude of $40\,\mathrm N$ describes a rope with those two components.

</div>
