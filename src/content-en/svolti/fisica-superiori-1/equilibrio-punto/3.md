## Exercise 1

A vertical spring with $k=250\,\mathrm{N/m}$ supports a $20\,\mathrm N$ weight at rest. If it is $34\,\mathrm{cm}$ long under load, what is its natural length?

::grafico{f="2.5*x;20" punti="8,20,pieno,A" nomi="x (cm),F (N)" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=29 titolo="The 20 N load meets the spring line at an extension of 8 cm"}

$$
\begin{aligned}x&=\frac{20}{250}\,\mathrm m=8\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]L_0&=(34-8)\,\mathrm{cm}=26\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring force at rest has the same magnitude as weight. Dividing weight by stiffness determines the extension in metres. Convert it to centimetres before subtracting it from the measured length. The resulting natural length is smaller than the loaded length.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ring on a smooth horizontal guide is pulled $18\,\mathrm N$ to the right and $6\,\mathrm N$ to the left. A spring with $k=300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ balances it. What is the taut spring's extension?

$$
\begin{aligned}F_e&=(18-6)\,\mathrm N=12\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]x&=\frac{12}{300}\,\mathrm m=4\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two pulls have a resultant of twelve newtons to the right. To balance the ring the spring must pull left, assuming it is anchored on that side. The extension is force magnitude divided by stiffness. Substituting $0.04\,\mathrm m$ into the product recovers twelve newtons.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two vertical springs mounted at the same height have equal unloaded lengths and stiffnesses $100$ and $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$. They support a point weighing $24\,\mathrm N$. What is the force of the stiffer spring?

$$
\begin{aligned}x&=\frac{24}{100+300}\,\mathrm m=0.06\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]F_2&=300\cdot0.06\,\mathrm N=18\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mounts impose the same deformation. The two upward forces add to the weight, which lets us solve for $x$. The three-times-stiffer spring exerts three times the force, or three quarters of the total weight. The other spring supplies the remaining six newtons.

</div>
