## Exercise 1

A horizontal push of $80\,\mathrm N$ presses a stationary $20\,\mathrm N$ block against a vertical wall. What minimum static coefficient allows the wall to support the block?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 1,0 1,1 0,1" linee="1,-1 1,2" vettori="0.5,0.5,2.5,0.5,S;0.5,0.5,-1.5,0.5,N;0.5,0.5,0.5,1.5,f;0.5,0.5,0.5,-0.5,P" xmin=-2 xmax=3 ymin=-1.5 ymax=2.3 titolo="Force directions for the block to the left of the wall. Pairs of components balance on each axis"}

$$
N=80\,\mathrm N,\quad f=20\,\mathrm N,\quad\mu_{s,\min}=\frac{20}{80}=0.25.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Horizontally, the normal reaction balances the push. Weight is the only other vertical force, so friction carries all of it. At the threshold, maximum friction reaches the required twenty newtons. The minimum coefficient is their ratio to the normal reaction and has no units.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A crate weighing $42\,\mathrm N$ is pulled upward with $18\,\mathrm N$. A $7\,\mathrm N$ parcel rests on it. What is the table's normal force on the crate when everything is at rest?

$$
N=(42+7-18)\,\mathrm N=31\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parcel transmits a downward force equal to its weight to the crate. The crate's own weight also acts downward. The normal reaction acts upward with the vertical rope. The table supports the combined weights minus the share carried by the rope.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block weighs $24\,\mathrm N$ against a vertical wall. An upward vertical cord carries $9\,\mathrm N$ of the weight. With $\mu_s=0.3$, what minimum horizontal push allows equilibrium?

$$
f=(24-9)\,\mathrm N=15\,\mathrm N,\qquad S_{\min}=\frac{15}{0.3}\,\mathrm N=50\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cord supports part of the weight, leaving fifteen newtons of required friction. The wall supplies a normal reaction equal in magnitude to the horizontal push. A fifty-newton push yields the required fifteen-newton static limit. A smaller push would leave an impossible vertical balance for the available friction.

</div>
