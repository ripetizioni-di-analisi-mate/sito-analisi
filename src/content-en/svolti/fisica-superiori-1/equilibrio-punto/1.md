## Exercise 1

A ring receives $12\,\mathrm N$ to the right and $9\,\mathrm N$ downward. What magnitude must a third force have to balance the ring?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,4,0,12 N;0,0,0,-3,9 N;0,0,-4,3,F" punti="0,0,pieno,O" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 titolo="The balancing force F has leftward and upward components"}

$$
\vec E=(-12;9)\,\mathrm N,\qquad E=\sqrt{12^2+9^2}\,\mathrm N=15\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The resultant of the first two forces has a positive horizontal and negative vertical component. The balancing force reverses both signs. When finding its magnitude, the components are the legs of a right triangle. A magnitude of fifteen newtons is consistent with twelve newtons on one axis and nine on the other.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two ropes pull a point with components $(8;6)\,\mathrm N$ and $(-3;-2)\,\mathrm N$. Which vector balances both forces when added?

$$
\vec E=-[(8;6)+(-3;-2)]\,\mathrm N=(-5;-4)\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Add the horizontal components first and the vertical components separately, since the axes are independent. The resultant has components of five and four newtons, both positive. The new force must oppose each with equal magnitude. As a check, the three components sum to zero on both axes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four forces on a point have components $(4;2)$, $(-7;1)$, $(3;-1)$, and $(1;-2)$, all in $\mathrm N$. On which axis must the resultant be corrected?

$$
\begin{aligned}R_x&=(4-7+3+1)\,\mathrm N=1\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]R_y&=(2+1-1-2)\,\mathrm N=0.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equilibrium imposes two conditions. The vertical sum is already zero, so another vertical force is unnecessary. The horizontal sum leaves one newton to the right. A leftward correction of one newton, if applicable at the point, also satisfies the remaining condition.

</div>
