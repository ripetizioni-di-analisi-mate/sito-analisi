## Exercise 1

A smooth ramp has height $3\,\mathrm m$ and base $4\,\mathrm m$. A rope parallel to the ramp holds a $75\,\mathrm N$ body. What is the normal reaction?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 8,0 8,6" riferimento="4,3,36.87,2,x,y" vettori="4,3,4,-1,P" etichette="4,-0.45,4 m;8.5,3,3 m" xmin=-1 xmax=10 ymin=-2 ymax=7 titolo="The geometric scale is uniform. Weight P stays vertical when the axes are rotated"}

$$
\ell=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}\,\mathrm m=5\,\mathrm m,\qquad N=75\frac45\,\mathrm N=60\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ramp dimensions give the sloping length. The cosine of the angle is base divided by hypotenuse. A rope parallel to the slope has no normal component, so the normal reaction equals the weight component perpendicular to the plane. Its value is smaller than the weight because a downslope component is present.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block has weight component $32\,\mathrm N$ into the plane. A guide also exerts $9\,\mathrm N$ perpendicular to and into the plane. What normal reaction is required for equilibrium?

$$
N-32\,\mathrm N-9\,\mathrm N=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N=41\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the perpendicular axis, weight and the guide both press into the plane. The outward normal reaction balances their sum. The check uses axes rotated with the plane, so any forces parallel to it do not affect the result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A smooth ramp has sloping length $10\,\mathrm m$. A rope parallel to the ramp needs tension $27\,\mathrm N$ to hold a $90\,\mathrm N$ load. How high is the ramp?

$$
\sin\alpha=\frac{T}{P}=\frac{27}{90},\qquad h=(10\,\mathrm m)\frac{27}{90}=3\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A smooth ramp supplies no friction. Along the slope, the rope balances the component of weight, making tension divided by weight the sine. Geometrically the same ratio is height divided by the sloping length. Multiply by ten metres to get the height, which must be less than the sloping length.

</div>
