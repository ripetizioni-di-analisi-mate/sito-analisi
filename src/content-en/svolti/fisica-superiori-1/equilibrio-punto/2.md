## Exercise 1

Two vertical cords support a $72\,\mathrm N$ load. One carries twice the tension of the other. What is the larger tension?

$$
\begin{aligned}2T+T&=72\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]2T&=2\cdot24\,\mathrm N=48\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both tensions act upwards. If $T$ is the smaller tension, the larger is $2T$, so the total weight is shared in three equal parts. The larger tension carries two. As a check, $48+24=72\,\mathrm N$ and the larger tension is twice the smaller.

The second piece of information is the ratio of the tensions. Without it, for example, two vertical forces of $36\,\mathrm N$ would also satisfy the sum. The two-to-one ratio identifies a single admissible pair.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An upper vertical cord holds two loads weighing $28$ and $17\,\mathrm N$ at rest. A lower cord carries only the $17\,\mathrm N$ load. By how much do the tensions differ?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1,4 1,4;0,4 0,3.15;0,2.45 0,1.45" poligoni="-.55,2.45 .55,2.45 .55,3.15 -.55,3.15;-.48,.78 .48,.78 .48,1.45 -.48,1.45" etichette=".73,2.7,28 N;.66,1,17 N" xmin=-1.2 xmax=2.05 ymin=.5 ymax=4.2 titolo="The lower cord holds only the 17 N load. The upper cord supports the pair."}

$$
\begin{aligned}T_{\rm alta}&=(28+17)\,\mathrm N=45\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]T_{\rm bassa}&=17\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]T_{\rm alta}-T_{\rm bassa}&=28\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lower cord only supports the load below it. The upper cord supports both loads, so its tension includes the lower tension plus the upper load's weight. For the upper load alone, its weight and the lower tension act downward and are balanced by the upper tension.

The setup gives $T_{\mathrm{inf}}=17\,\mathrm N$ and $T_{\mathrm{sup}}=(28+17)\,\mathrm N=45\,\mathrm N$. If the internal interaction is eliminated by taking both loads together, the external forces are the upper tension and the weights. That separate balance confirms $T_{\mathrm{sup}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two vertical cords are rated for at most $32$ and $46\,\mathrm N$. The load can be shared as desired. What is the maximum weight supported in the particle model?

$$
P_{\max}=T_{1,\max}+T_{2,\max}=(32+46)\,\mathrm N=78\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two upward forces add. Since the load-sharing can be chosen freely, maximum support uses each cord up to its own limit. Equal sharing would instead restrict each cord to thirty-two newtons and give sixty-four newtons in total.

At $78\,\mathrm N$ both ropes carry their maximum. To support a greater weight, at least one rope would have to exceed its limit, because the sum of the admissible tensions reaches exactly $78\,\mathrm N$.

In the table the first rope always carries $32\,\mathrm N$. The second completes the required support and reaches its own limit in the third row. At $80\,\mathrm N$ the formal solution exceeds the limit, and no other distribution can increase the total support.

| Weight | First rope | Second required | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $68\,\mathrm N$ | $32\,\mathrm N$ | $36\,\mathrm N$ | Admissible |
| $76\,\mathrm N$ | $32\,\mathrm N$ | $44\,\mathrm N$ | Admissible |
| $78\,\mathrm N$ | $32\,\mathrm N$ | $46\,\mathrm N$ | At the limit |
| $80\,\mathrm N$ | $32\,\mathrm N$ | $48\,\mathrm N$ | Inadmissible |

</div>
