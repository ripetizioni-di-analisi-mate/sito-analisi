## Exercise 1

A measurement is $l=(100\pm0.7)\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is its relative uncertainty as a fraction?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=99 xmax=101 ymin=-0.65 ymax=1.4 linee="99.3,0 100.7,0" punti="99.3,0,pieno;100,0,pieno;100.7,0,pieno" etichette="99.3,0.4,99.3 cm;100,0.58,100 cm;100.7,0.4,100.7 cm" titolo="Each endpoint is 0.7 cm from the central reading"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon &= \dfrac{0.7}{100}\\[6pt]
&= 0.007
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Relative uncertainty is the ratio of the interval's half-width to the central value. Both lengths are in centimetres, so the unit cancels. The result gives the fraction of the central value represented by the uncertainty. Expressing it as a percentage would require multiplying by one hundred. The ratio is $\varepsilon=\dfrac{0.7}{100}=0.007$.

In millimetres, the centre is $1000\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the radius $7\,\mathrm{mm}$. The ratio $\dfrac{7}{1000}=0.007$ matches the one in centimetres. Conversion multiplies both terms by ten, and that factor cancels. Report the requested dimensionless number, which is seven thousandths of the central value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mass is $m=(90\pm6.3)\,\mathrm g$. What is its percentage uncertainty?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon &= \dfrac{6.3}{90}\\[6pt]
&= 0.07 = 7\%
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute uncertainty and the central value are both in grams. Dividing the first number by the second gives a dimensionless fraction. Multiply that fraction by one hundred to express how much uncertainty there is per hundred parts of the central value. Grams apply to the measurement and absolute uncertainty. The calculation gives $\varepsilon=\dfrac{6.3}{90}=0.07=7\%$.

The compatible interval goes from $83.7$ to $96.3\,\mathrm g$. Both endpoints are $6.3\,\mathrm g$ from the centre, or $7\%$ of $90\,\mathrm g$. The percentage describes this deviation on one side. Comparing the full width of $12.6\,\mathrm g$ with the centre would instead give $14\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object has mass $45\,\mathrm g$ with relative uncertainty $7\%$. What is the absolute uncertainty?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta m &= 45\cdot\dfrac{7}{100}\\[6pt]
&= 3.15\,\mathrm g
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A percentage counts parts per hundred. Divide the percentage number by one hundred and multiply by the central mass. The fraction is dimensionless and the product is in grams. Dividing the result by the mass recovers the relative uncertainty stated in the question. Therefore $\Delta m=45\cdot\dfrac{7}{100}=3.15\,\mathrm g$.

To check the measurement, subtract and add $3.15\,\mathrm g$ to the central value. This gives endpoints of $41.85$ and $48.15\,\mathrm g$. Both have the same absolute distance from the centre. Dividing that distance by $45\,\mathrm g$ gives $0.07$. The original percentage refers to this radius of the interval.

</div>
