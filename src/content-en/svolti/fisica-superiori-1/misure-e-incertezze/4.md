## Exercise 1

Two separately measured lengths have absolute uncertainties $0.7$ and $0.8\,\mathrm{cm}$. Under the conservative rule for a sum, what is the uncertainty of the sum?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.15 xmax=1.65 ymin=-0.55 ymax=1.35 linee="0,0 0.7,0;0.7,0 1.5,0" punti="0,0,pieno;0.7,0,pieno;1.5,0,pieno" etichette="0.35,0.4,0.7 cm;1.1,0.4,0.8 cm" titolo="In the worst case both deviations in the sum have the same direction"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta L &= 0.7+0.8\\[6pt]
&= 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To obtain the upper bound of the sum, take both inputs at their upper endpoints. The two positive deviations add. The lower bound differs from the central value by the same amount, with both deviations directed downward. The calculation gives $\Delta L=0.7+0.8=1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Writing $a_m$ and $b_m$ for the central values, the maximum is $(a_m+b_m)+1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the minimum $(a_m+b_m)-1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The stated uncertainties alone determine the distances from the centre of the sum. The central lengths would also be needed to locate the two endpoints on an absolute scale.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A frame's side is found by subtracting two readings whose uncertainties are $0.7$ and $0.9\,\mathrm{mm}$. Under the conservative rule, what is the uncertainty of the difference?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta l &= 0.7+0.9\\[6pt]
&= 1.6\,\mathrm{mm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference is greatest when the first reading is above its centre and the second is below its centre. Subtracting the second negative deviation makes it a positive contribution, so the magnitudes add. The same sum gives the deviation toward the lower endpoint. Thus $\Delta l=0.7+0.9=1.6\,\mathrm{mm}$.

If the central difference is $d_m$, its upper endpoint is $d_m+1.6\,\mathrm{mm}$. The first deviation is $+0.7\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the second $-0.9\,\mathrm{mm}$ in this case. The result is higher by $0.7-(-0.9)$, so the two magnitudes add even though the operation on the measured quantities is subtraction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two marks read $32.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $11.1\,\mathrm{mm}$. Both readings share the same unknown offset. Each also has reading uncertainty $0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$, with deviations allowed independently. Find the distance and its conservative uncertainty.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_m &= (32.4-11.1)\,\mathrm{mm}=21.3\,\mathrm{mm},\\
\Delta d &= (0.2+0.2)\,\mathrm{mm}=0.4\,\mathrm{mm},\\
d &=(21.3\pm0.4)\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $b$ be the common offset and $e_1,e_2$ the reading deviations. The readings are $r_1=x_1+b+e_1$ and $r_2=x_2+b+e_2$. Subtracting the second from the first eliminates $b$ exactly. The true distance is $d=r_1-r_2-e_1+e_2$. In this expression the reading deviations may cancel or reinforce one another, depending on their signs.

The largest distance allows $e_1=-0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $e_2=+0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$. The correction to the central value is then $0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Reversing those signs gives the minimum. The result lies between $20.9$ and $21.7\,\mathrm{mm}$. The offset does not enter these bounds because it has the same value in both readings.

</div>
