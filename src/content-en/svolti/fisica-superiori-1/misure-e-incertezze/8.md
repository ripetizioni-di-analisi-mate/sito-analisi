## Exercise 1

Forces from $12$ to $18\,\mathrm N$ must be measured with relative uncertainty of at most $1.5\%$. Force meter A reaches $15\,\mathrm N$ in steps of $0.2\,\mathrm N$. Meter B reaches $24\,\mathrm N$ in steps of $0.3\,\mathrm N$. The protocol assigns half a division. Which instrument meets both requirements for all expected forces?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta F_B&=\dfrac{0.3\,\mathrm N}{2}=0.15\,\mathrm N,\\
\varepsilon_{B,\max}&=\dfrac{0.15\,\mathrm N}{12\,\mathrm N}
=0.0125=1.25\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The range of A excludes all expected forces above $15\,\mathrm N$, so A must be rejected even though its divisions are smaller. The range of B covers the full interval from $12$ to $18\,\mathrm N$. Next check the second instrument's relative uncertainty.

Under the protocol, the absolute uncertainty of B is $0.15\,\mathrm N$ for any reading. As force increases, the denominator of the ratio grows while the numerator stays fixed. The most demanding case is therefore the minimum force of $12\,\mathrm N$. Even there, the ratio is only $1.25\%$, within the required limit. B meets both conditions throughout the expected band.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A specification allows lengths from $58.0$ to $62.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. The measurement is $(60.6\pm1.8)\,\mathrm{mm}$. The conservative rule requires every value in this interval to be acceptable. Is the measurement sufficient? What is the maximum absolute uncertainty if its centre remains the same?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=57.1 xmax=63.3 ymin=0 ymax=2.6 linee="58,1.78 62,1.78;58.8,.63 62.4,.63" punti="58,1.78,pieno;62,1.78,pieno;58.8,.63,pieno;62.4,.63,pieno" segmenti="62,.5,62,1.92" etichette="60,2.15,Specification from 58 to 62 mm;60.6,.27,Measurement from 58.8 to 62.4 mm" titolo="The upper measurement endpoint reaches 62.4 mm and exceeds the limit of 62 mm"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
[60.6-1.8;\,60.6+1.8]\,\mathrm{mm}
&=[58.8;\,62.4]\,\mathrm{mm},\\
\Delta_{\max}&=\min(60.6-58.0,\,62.0-60.6)\,\mathrm{mm}\\
 &=1.4\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The measured interval reaches $62.4\,\mathrm{mm}$, beyond the upper limit of $62.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. Its overlap with the specification shows compatibility, but acceptance requires that both limits be met. The original measurement fails that condition at the upper end.

From the centre at $60.6\,\mathrm{mm}$ to the lower limit there are $2.6\,\mathrm{mm}$. The margin to the upper limit is only $1.4\,\mathrm{mm}$. The radius can be at most the smaller margin because the same distance is applied on each side. With $1.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ the new interval is $[59.2;62.0]\,\mathrm{mm}$. Its top endpoint touches the threshold and its lower endpoint is inside.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The readings in the table measure the same length. The protocol assigns the larger of the half-range and the instrumental uncertainty. Which instrument gives a result with smaller uncertainty?

| Instrument | Readings in mm | Instrumental uncertainty in mm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $9.9$, $10.0$, $10.1$ | $0.4$ |
| B | $9.7$, $10.0$, $10.3$ | $0.1$ |

$$
\begin{aligned}
s_A&=\dfrac{10.1-9.9}{2}\,\mathrm{mm}=0.1\,\mathrm{mm},\\
s_B&=\dfrac{10.3-9.7}{2}\,\mathrm{mm}=0.3\,\mathrm{mm},\\
\Delta_A&=\max(0.1,0.4)\,\mathrm{mm}=0.4\,\mathrm{mm},\\
\Delta_B&=\max(0.3,0.1)\,\mathrm{mm}=0.3\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In both sets the two extremes are equally far from the reading of $10.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, so each mean equals that value. The next step compares two different quantities within each set. A has readings that are closer together, but its stated instrumental uncertainty exceeds its half-range. For B, the spread exceeds the instrumental uncertainty.

The reported results are therefore $(10.0\pm0.4)\,\mathrm{mm}$ with A and $(10.0\pm0.3)\,\mathrm{mm}$ with B. Although the readings of B vary more, its result has smaller uncertainty under the stated protocol. Compare the uncertainty assigned to each result here, taking into account both the instrument's limitation and the observed spread.

</div>
