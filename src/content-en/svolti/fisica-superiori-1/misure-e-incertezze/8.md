## Exercise 1

An object has mass $50\,\mathrm g$ with relative uncertainty $8\%$. What is the absolute uncertainty?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta m &= 50\cdot\dfrac{8}{100}\\[6pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm g
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A percentage counts parts per hundred. Divide the percentage number by one hundred and multiply by the central mass. The fraction is dimensionless and the product is in grams. Dividing the result by the mass recovers the relative uncertainty stated in the question. Therefore $\Delta m=50\cdot\dfrac{8}{100}=4\,\mathrm g$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A measurement is $l=(19\pm0.9)\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the lower endpoint of the associated interval?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=17.6 xmax=20.5 ymin=-0.65 ymax=1.45 linee="18.1,0 19.9,0" punti="18.1,0,pieno;19,0,pieno;19.9,0,pieno" etichette="18.1,0.35,18.1 cm;19,0.55,19 cm;19.9,0.35,19.9 cm" titolo="The reading uncertainty establishes the endpoints around the centre"}

$$
19-0.9 = 18.1\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The central value is halfway along the symmetric interval. The absolute uncertainty gives the distance from the centre to each endpoint. For the lower endpoint, subtract it from the centre to obtain the position further left on the scale. The upper endpoint would be obtained by adding it. The lower endpoint is $19-0.9=18.1\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The half-range of a measurement series is $0.08\,\mathrm{{cm}}$ and the reading uncertainty is $0.8\,\mathrm{{cm}}$. If the larger of the two is used, what uncertainty is assigned?

$$
\Delta l = 0.8\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stated method uses the larger of the observed spread and the reading uncertainty. Both values share a unit, so compare their numbers. A small spread across readings does not allow an uncertainty below the reading uncertainty under this rule. Since $0.8>0.08$, the assigned uncertainty is $\Delta l=0.8\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
