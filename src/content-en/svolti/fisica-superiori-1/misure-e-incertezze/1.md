## Exercise 1

Three readings of a length are $23$, $30$, and $37\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is their arithmetic mean?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=21 xmax=39 ymin=-0.8 ymax=1.6 linee="23,0 37,0" punti="23,0,pieno;30,0,pieno;37,0,pieno" etichette="23,0.42,23 cm;30,0.65,30 cm;37,0.42,37 cm" titolo="The two extreme readings are equidistant from the central reading"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar l &= \dfrac{23+30+37}3\\[6pt]
&= 30\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each reading has equal weight. Add the three readings and divide by three. The extremes are equally far from the middle reading, one above it and one below it, so their shifts cancel and the mean equals the middle reading. It follows that $\bar l=\dfrac{23+30+37}3=30\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Check the centre by finding how far the three readings are from $30\,\mathrm{cm}$. Their deviations are $-7$, $0$ and $+7\,\mathrm{cm}$. The deviations sum to zero, as they must when the chosen reference is the mean. Changing only one extreme reading would break the symmetry and could shift the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The extreme readings of a length are $40$ and $54\,\mathrm{mm}$. Estimate the uncertainty with the half-range.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta l &= \dfrac{54-40}2\\[6pt]
&= 7\,\mathrm{mm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Consider the distance between the extreme readings. The requested rule takes half this distance, the radius of the interval with those endpoints. The result uses the same unit as the measurements. Additional uncertainty sources, such as resolution, would require separate data. The calculation gives $\Delta l=\dfrac{54-40}2=7\,\mathrm{mm}$.

The midpoint of the extremes is $\dfrac{40+54}{2}=47\,\mathrm{mm}$. The endpoints can therefore be written as $47-7$ and $47+7\,\mathrm{mm}$. This check recovers the observed span. If other readings lie between the extremes, they contribute to the mean while the half-range still depends only on $40$ and $54\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The half-range of a measurement series is $0.07\,\mathrm{{cm}}$ and the reading uncertainty is $0.7\,\mathrm{{cm}}$. If the larger of the two is used, what uncertainty is assigned?

$$
\Delta l = 0.7\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stated method uses the larger of the observed spread and the reading uncertainty. Both values share a unit, so compare their numbers. A small spread across readings does not allow an uncertainty below the reading uncertainty under this rule. Since $0.7>0.07$, the assigned uncertainty is $\Delta l=0.7\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The comparison is unchanged if we work in millimetres. The half-range becomes $0.7\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the reading uncertainty $7\,\mathrm{mm}$. The second is ten times the first in either unit. Under the protocol the reported uncertainty is therefore $7\,\mathrm{mm}$, equivalent to the $0.7\,\mathrm{cm}$ found earlier.

</div>
