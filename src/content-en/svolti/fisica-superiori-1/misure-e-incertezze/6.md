## Exercise 1

Round $17.45\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the nearest tenth, rounding upward in a tie when the following digit is 5.

$$
17.5\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Keep one digit after the decimal point. The first discarded digit is in the hundredths place. It is five and later digits are zero, so the value is halfway between adjacent tenths. The specified rule selects the higher tenth. Since it is $5$, the rounded value is $17.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A measurement is $l=(110\pm0.8)\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is its relative uncertainty as a fraction?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon &= \dfrac{0.8}{110}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{2}{275}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Relative uncertainty is the ratio of the interval's half-width to the central value. Both lengths are in centimetres, so the unit cancels. The result gives the fraction of the central value represented by the uncertainty. Expressing it as a percentage would require multiplying by one hundred. The ratio is $\varepsilon=\dfrac{0.8}{110}=\dfrac{2}{275}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A mass is $m=(100\pm8)\,\mathrm g$. What is its percentage uncertainty?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon &= \dfrac{8}{100}\\[6pt]
&= 0.08 = 8\%
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute uncertainty and the central value are both in grams. Dividing the first number by the second gives a dimensionless fraction. Multiply that fraction by one hundred to express how much uncertainty there is per hundred parts of the central value. Grams apply to the measurement and absolute uncertainty. The calculation gives $\varepsilon=\dfrac{8}{100}=0.08=8\%$.

</div>
