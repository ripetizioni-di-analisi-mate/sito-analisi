## Exercise 1

Round $17.45\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the nearest tenth, rounding upward in a tie when the following digit is 5.

$$
17.5\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Keep one digit after the decimal point. The first discarded digit is in the hundredths place. It is five and later digits are zero, so the value is halfway between adjacent tenths. The specified rule selects the higher tenth. Since it is $5$, the rounded value is $17.5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The distances to $17.4$ and $17.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ are both $0.05\,\mathrm{cm}$. A distance comparison alone therefore cannot choose between them. The stated rule resolves that tie in favour of $17.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Keep the original value for later calculations if the rounded one is used only to present the measurement.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The estimated interval for a length goes from $7.16$ to $7.58\,\mathrm{cm}$. Report a symmetric interval with the centre rounded to the nearest tenth and the smallest possible uncertainty in tenths. The result must include the entire estimated interval. How should the measurement be written?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=7 xmax=7.8 ymin=0 ymax=2.5 linee="7.16,1.7 7.58,1.7;7.1,.65 7.7,.65" punti="7.16,1.7,pieno;7.58,1.7,pieno;7.1,.65,pieno;7.4,.65,pieno;7.7,.65,pieno" etichette="7.16,2.03,7.16 cm;7.58,2.03,7.58 cm;7.1,.25,7.1 cm;7.4,.25,7.4 cm;7.7,.25,7.7 cm" titolo="The final interval contains both endpoints of the original estimate"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_m &= \dfrac{7.16+7.58}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}=7.37\,\mathrm{cm},\\
x_{\mathrm{r}}&=7.4\,\mathrm{cm},\\
x_{\mathrm{r}}-7.16\,\mathrm{cm}&=0.24\,\mathrm{cm},\\
7.58\,\mathrm{cm}-x_{\mathrm{r}}&=0.18\,\mathrm{cm},\\
x &=(7.4\pm0.3)\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exact midpoint is $7.37\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the original interval has radius $0.21\,\mathrm{cm}$. The reported centre shifts to $7.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The left endpoint is then farther from the centre than the right one. To include both, the new radius must reach at least the greater distance, $0.24\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The allowed value in tenths immediately above $0.24\,\mathrm{cm}$ is $0.3\,\mathrm{cm}$, which gives $[7.1;7.7]\,\mathrm{cm}$. Check minimality by trying the previous tenth. With $0.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ the interval would start at $7.2\,\mathrm{cm}$, losing the portion from $7.16$ to $7.2\,\mathrm{cm}$. Centre and uncertainty rounding therefore have to be checked together.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Add two nominal masses of $18\,\mathrm g$ and $12\,\mathrm g$, with relative uncertainties of $1\%$ and $2\%$ respectively. The deviations may independently take any value within their intervals. Calculate the relative uncertainty of their sum and report the result with centre and uncertainty in hundredths of a gram.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta m_1&=0.01\cdot18\,\mathrm g=0.18\,\mathrm g,\\
\Delta m_2&=0.02\cdot12\,\mathrm g=0.24\,\mathrm g,\\
m&=(30.00\pm0.42)\,\mathrm g,\\
\varepsilon&=\dfrac{0.42\,\mathrm g}{30\,\mathrm g}=0.014=1.4\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each percentage refers to its own mass. First find the absolute deviations, then combine them. In this model the readings can reach their upper endpoints at the same time. The largest increase in their sum is therefore $0.18+0.24=0.42\,\mathrm g$. The central total is $30\,\mathrm g$, written as $30.00\,\mathrm g$ to report it to the same decimal place as its uncertainty.

To convert to a percentage, compare this new deviation with the whole mass, giving $1.4\%$. This lies between $1\%$ and $2\%$. Each percentage in fact contributes with a weight proportional to the mass it refers to. Adding the two percentages directly would give $3\%$ without accounting for their different denominators.

</div>
