## Exercise 1

A full vessel overflows by $3.2\,\mathrm L$ of water as a body is immersed. With $\rho_f=1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is the buoyant force?

::grafico{f="10*x" punti="3.2,32,pieno,A" nomi="V raccolto (L),F_A (N)" xmin=0 xmax=4.3 ymin=0 ymax=45 titolo="The vessel was full. The 3.2 L collected measure the displaced volume"}

$$
F_A=\rho_f V_i g=1\cdot3.2\cdot10\,\mathrm N=32\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vessel was full, so the overflow measures all the volume displaced by the immersed part. The collected mass is three point two kilograms. Buoyancy equals what the displaced fluid would have weighed in the vessel. The graph gives ten newtons per litre of water.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample of mass $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ causes $2\,\mathrm L$ overflow from a full vessel when completely immersed without contact. What is its mean density in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$?

$$
\rho_c=\frac{5\,\mathrm{kg}}{2\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m^3}=2500\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Complete immersion makes the collected volume equal to the sample's excluded outer volume. Divide its mass by that volume after converting litres to cubic metres. Its density exceeds water density, consistent with the need to hold it off the bottom.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two tests use the same immersed volume $0.004\,\mathrm m^3$. Buoyancy in the second liquid exceeds the first by $12\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, how much denser is the second liquid?

$$
\rho_2-\rho_1=\frac{12}{10\cdot0.004}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=300\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the buoyant-force magnitudes leaves volume times field times density difference. The vessels' absolute surface pressures are not needed. The positive result identifies the second liquid as denser.

</div>
