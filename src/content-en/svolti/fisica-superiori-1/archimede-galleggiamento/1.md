## Exercise 1

A body displaces $2.5\,\mathrm L$ of oil with density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is the buoyant force?

::grafico{f="8*x" punti="2.5,20,pieno,A" nomi="Vᵢ (L),F_A (N)" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=35 titolo="The slope of 8 N per litre is the oil's unit weight. A shows 2.5 L of displaced volume"}

$$
\begin{aligned}V_i&=2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m^3\\[6pt]F_A&=800\cdot10\cdot2.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm N=20\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find the weight of the oil that would occupy the submerged volume. The two and a half litres correspond to two kilograms of oil. The ten-newton-per-kilogram field gives twenty newtons of buoyancy. Point A lies on a straight line because each litre always adds eight newtons.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two watertight bodies are immersed in one uniform fluid. The second receives $36\,\mathrm N$ of buoyancy and displaces $3\,\mathrm L$ more than the first, which receives $9\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is the fluid density?

$$
\rho_f=\frac{36-9}{10\cdot0.003}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=900\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first volume is unknown, but subtracting the buoyancy equations uses the change in volume directly. Twenty-seven extra newtons correspond to three extra litres. After converting to cubic metres, divide by volume change and field strength. Neither total body volume is needed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A submerged prism has horizontal faces of area $0.03\,\mathrm m^2$. The top pressure is $104000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ and the bottom pressure is $108000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. The sides are vertical. What is the buoyant force?

$$
F_A=(108000-104000)\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot0.03\,\mathrm m^2=120\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lower face gets an upward force and the upper face a downward force. With equal areas, the pressures can be subtracted before multiplying. The sides contribute no vertical force. The one-hundred-and-twenty-newton resultant is upward because pressure is greater at the deeper face.

</div>
