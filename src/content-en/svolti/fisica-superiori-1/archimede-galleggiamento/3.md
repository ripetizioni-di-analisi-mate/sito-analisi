## Exercise 1

A float weighs $70\,\mathrm N$ in water where each displaced litre weighs $10\,\mathrm N$. What volume is submerged in free equilibrium?

::grafico{f="10*x;70" punti="7,70,pieno,E" nomi="Vᵢ (L),F (N)" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=110 titolo="In free flotation E is the intersection of the weight and buoyancy curves"}

$$
V_i=\frac{70\,\mathrm N}{10\,\mathrm{N/L}}=7\,\mathrm L.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Without ropes or supports, buoyancy must equal weight. Divide seventy newtons by the contribution per litre. The graph intersection at seven litres gives exactly the required force. The result is admissible if at least seven litres of displacement are available.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object weighing $54\,\mathrm N$ can displace at most $5\,\mathrm L$ of water. Each litre of water weighs $10\,\mathrm N$. Is any submerged volume in equilibrium without constraints?

$$
F_{A,\max}=5\cdot10\,\mathrm N=50\,\mathrm N<54\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before accepting a calculated volume, check the maximum available. The volume formally needed would be five point four litres, beyond the external volume. Even fully submerged the body lacks four newtons of support, so those forces alone cannot maintain rest.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a uniform liquid of density $850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ an object of volume $0.012\,\mathrm m^3$ stays suspended fully submerged without ropes. What mass has it in the ideal model?

$$
m=\rho_f V_i=(850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3})(0.012\,\mathrm m^3)=10.2\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Free suspension requires weight and buoyancy magnitudes to match. In full immersion the displaced volume is the whole external volume. Dividing the equality by field strength makes the mass equal to the displaced fluid mass. The body's mean density equals the liquid density.

</div>
