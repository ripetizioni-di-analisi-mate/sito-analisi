## Exercise 1

A submerged body without bottom contact has buoyancy $30\,\mathrm N$ and weight $20\,\mathrm N$. A cord to the bottom holds it at rest. What is its tension?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-1,0 1,0 1,0.8 -1,0.8" linee="-3,-1.1 3,-1.1;0,-1.1 0,0" vettori="0,0.4,0,3.1,F_A;-0.6,0.4,-0.6,-1.4,P;0,0.4,0,-0.5,T" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-2.1 ymax=3.45 titolo="Buoyancy acts upward. Weight and tension act downward. The arrows are offset so each force can be seen"}

$$
F_A-P-T=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T=(30-20)\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Buoyancy tends to lift the body. The bottom cord adds downward tension to weight. The line supplies only the ten-newton difference. Weight plus tension reaches thirty newtons and balances buoyancy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body in water weighs $100\,\mathrm N$ and receives buoyancy $65\,\mathrm N$. An upper vertical line already carries tension $20\,\mathrm N$. What additional upward force holds the body still at this tension?

$$
F_{\rm add}=100\,\mathrm N-65\,\mathrm N-20\,\mathrm N=15\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Buoyancy and upward tension total eighty-five newtons. The downward weight exceeds them by fifteen. Equilibrium needs an upward force of that magnitude. An oblique support would need the same vertical contribution and a separate check along the other axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A calculation for a cord from above a submerged body gives $T=-6\,\mathrm N$ with upward pull assumed. What constraint could keep the same equilibrium?

$$
F_A-P=6\,\mathrm N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_{\downarrow}=6\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Negative tension signals that the missing force acts opposite to the assumed direction. A line can pull toward its anchor. Anchoring it underneath supplies the downward force required when the body is lighter than the fluid it displaces.

</div>
