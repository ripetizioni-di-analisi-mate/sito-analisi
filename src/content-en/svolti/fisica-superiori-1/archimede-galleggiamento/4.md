## Exercise 1

A prism of volume $10\,\mathrm L$ and mean density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ floats in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. How much volume is above the water?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-2,-1.8 3,-1.8 3,0 -2,0;0,-1.2 1.3,-1.2 1.3,0.8 0,0.8" linee="-2,0 3,0" etichette="0.65,0.42,4 L;0.65,-0.62,6 L" xmin=-2.7 xmax=3.6 ymin=-2.1 ymax=1.35 titolo="The liquid surface separates the exposed and submerged parts"}

$$
\begin{aligned}V_i&=10\frac{600}{1000}\,\mathrm L=6\,\mathrm L\\[6pt]V_e&=(10-6)\,\mathrm L=4\,\mathrm L.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The density ratio gives six tenths immersed. The question asks for the other portion, four tenths. Apply that fraction to the ten-litre total. The volume check adds six submerged litres to four exposed litres to recover the prism's total.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An upright cylinder of constant cross-section is $25\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall and $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ protrudes above water. With $\rho_f=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, what is its mean density?

$$
\rho_c=\rho_f\frac{h_i}{h}=1000\frac{25-9}{25}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=640\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In an upright cylinder, heights are proportional to volumes because they multiply the same area. Subtract the exposed height to obtain sixteen immersed centimetres. Mean density is water density times the immersed fraction of sixteen twenty-fifths.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The same block is $72\%$ submerged in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $90\%$ submerged in oil. What is the oil density?

$$
\rho_{\rm olio}=\frac{0.72\cdot1000}{0.90}\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body's mean density is fluid density times immersed fraction in each test. Equate the water product with the oil product. A greater fraction must be immersed in oil, so its calculated density is below water density.

</div>
