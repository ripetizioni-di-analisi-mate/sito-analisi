## Exercise 1

A platform of total mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ floats first in liquid of density $1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ and then in liquid of density $1.2\,\mathrm{kg/L}$. How much does immersed volume decrease?

::grafico{f="60/x" punti="1,60,pieno,A;1.2,50,pieno,B" nomi="ρf (kg/L),Vᵢ (L)" xmin=0.8 xmax=1.45 ymin=0 ymax=78 titolo="The supported mass is 60 kg. At A the density is 1 kg/L and the submerged volume is 60 L. At B they are 1.2 kg/L and 50 L, respectively."}

$$
\Delta V_i=\left(\frac{60}{1}-\frac{60}{1.2}\right)\mathrm L=10\,\mathrm L.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The displaced liquid mass is sixty kilograms in both tests. In the denser liquid the same mass occupies less space. The first test gives sixty litres and the second fifty. The graph shows inverse dependence on density when mass stays fixed.

The new volume is five sixths of the old one because the volume ratio is the inverse of the density ratio. The decrease is therefore one sixth of $60\,\mathrm L$. A twenty per cent rise in density does not give a decrease in volume by the same percentage.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A watertight object of volume $5\,\mathrm L$ is held fully submerged first in water of density $1\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ and then in oil of density $0.8\,\mathrm{kg/L}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, how much does buoyancy decrease?

$$
\Delta F_A=5\,\mathrm L\cdot10\,\mathrm{N/kg}\cdot(1-0.8)\,\mathrm{kg/L}=10\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Full immersion displaces the same volume in both tests. The mass of displaced fluid changes, falling by one kilogram here. Multiply by the field to find the buoyancy change. During free flotation volume could adjust, whereas here it is fixed.

The buoyant forces are $50\,\mathrm N$ in water and $40\,\mathrm N$ in oil. If the restraint were an upper string that stayed taut in both tests, its reading would rise by $10\,\mathrm N$ in oil. The body's weight would stay the same.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sealed hollow body and a solid body of equal outer volume are fully submerged in one liquid. The solid body weighs more. How do buoyancy and weight minus buoyancy compare?

Buoyancy is equal and weight minus buoyancy is greater for the solid.

<div class="spiegazione">

The outer boundary excludes equal volumes for both objects, giving the same buoyancy magnitude. Subtracting a common value from unequal weights gives a larger difference for the heavier body. The comparison holds while the hollow body stays sealed.

The difference between the two weight-minus-buoyancy values is exactly the difference between the weights. Check each value's sign separately. If the shell weighs less than the buoyant force, it needs a downward restraint. If the solid body weighs more than the buoyant force, it needs upward support.

</div>
