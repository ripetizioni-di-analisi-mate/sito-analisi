## Exercise 1

The right end of a spring is moved $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ left from its resting position, with positive axis to the right. For $k=150\,\mathrm{N/m}$, what is the elastic force component?

::grafico{f="-1.5*x" punti="-4,6,pieno,C" nomi="x (cm),Fₑ (N)" xmin=-6 xmax=3 ymin=-5 ymax=10 titolo="C shows the 4 cm compression and the rightward restoring force"}

$$
F_{e,x}=-kx=-150\left(-0.04\right)\,\mathrm N=6\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The leftward displacement is a compression with component $x=-0.04\,\mathrm m$. Within the linear range, the minus sign in Hooke's law reverses the direction of the deformation. The positive result is a push to the right. Its arrow leads back toward the position from which the spring end was moved.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a linear spring, the force magnitude rises from $4$ to $10\,\mathrm N$ during extension. The spring extends a further $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is $k$?

$$
k=\frac{\Delta F}{\Delta x}=\frac{(10-4)\,\mathrm N}{0.02\,\mathrm m}=300\,\mathrm{N/m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the law to the differences between measurements. The same spring constant applies in both states, so the change in force is $k$ times the change in extension. The $4\,\mathrm N$ initial load was already present. The $6\,\mathrm N$ increase is the quantity associated with the extra two centimetres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An ideal spring with $k=200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ extends by $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ at its allowed maximum load. Starting from a load of $3\,\mathrm N$, by how much can the load still increase?

$$
\begin{aligned}F_{\max}&=200\cdot0.05\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]\Delta F&=(10-3)\,\mathrm N=7\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ measurement is taken from the natural length, so it gives the total permitted load. The initial load already uses part of that range. Subtract $3\,\mathrm N$ from the $10\,\mathrm N$ limit. As a check using lengths, the starting extension is $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and another $3.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ remains available.

</div>
