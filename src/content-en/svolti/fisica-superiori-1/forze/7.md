## Exercise 1

A $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ sample is first in a field of $8\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ and then in a field of $2\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. By how much does its weight decrease?

::grafico{f="8*x;2*x" punti="6,48,pieno,A;6,12,pieno,B" nomi="m (kg),P (N)" xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=75 titolo="The mass is the same at both sites, with the weights given by points A and B"}

$$
\begin{aligned}P_A&=(6)(8)\,\mathrm N=48\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]P_B&=(6)(2)\,\mathrm N=12\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]\Delta P&=(48-12)\,\mathrm N=36\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two slopes represent different gravitational fields. The common mass of $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ fixes the same horizontal coordinate for the two points. Increasing or decreasing $g$ changes the weight in proportion. The decrease is the initial weight minus the final weight, giving a positive result.

At the second site the field is one quarter of the original field. The final weight must also be one quarter of $48\,\mathrm N$, which is $12\,\mathrm N$. The $36\,\mathrm N$ difference confirms the direct calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The same object weighs $54\,\mathrm N$ at A and $18\,\mathrm N$ at B. What fraction of the field at A is the field at B?

$$
\frac{g_B}{g_A}=\frac{P_B}{P_A}=\frac{18}{54}=\frac13.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same mass appears in the numerator and denominator of the weight ratio, so it cancels. To find the field at B as a fraction of the field at A, put the weight at B in the numerator. The resulting field is smaller than the reference field, consistent with the smaller weight.

The ratio $\dfrac13$ means that, in the same uniform-local-field model, another sample would also weigh one third as much in B as it does in A. The comparison always uses the same sample for both readings.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A spring scale reads $42\,\mathrm N$ for a sample on Earth with $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. A two-pan balance compares the sample with known masses. The measurements are repeated where $g=3\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. What are the readings there?

$$
\begin{aligned}m&=\frac{42\,\mathrm N}{10\,\mathrm{N/kg}}=4.2\,\mathrm{kg}\\[6pt]P_B&=4.2\cdot3\,\mathrm N=12.6\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initially the spring scale gives the weight on Earth. Divide by Earth's field to obtain the sample's mass. The scale in newtons records a new weight at the second location. In the two-pan balance, the local field multiplies both the sample mass and the reference masses, so the balance condition selects the same mass at both sites.

Check the comparison by placing a reference mass of $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ on the other pan. At the second site it weighs $12\,\mathrm N$, against $12.6\,\mathrm N$ for the sample. The pans balance only with a reference mass of $4.2\,\mathrm{kg}$.

</div>
