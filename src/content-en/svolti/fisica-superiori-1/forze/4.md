## Exercise 1

A block starts at rest with $N=35\,\mathrm N$ and $\mu_s=0.4$. A horizontal cord pulls it with $9\,\mathrm N$. How many newtons of margin remain before slipping?

::grafico{f="x" punti="9,9,pieno,A;14,14,pieno,B" nomi="F (N),fₛ (N)" xmin=0 xmax=14 ymin=0 ymax=16 titolo="A shows the static state under a 9 N pull. B marks the 14 N static limit"}

$$
\begin{aligned}f_{s,\max}&=0.4\cdot35\,\mathrm N=14\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]\Delta F&=(14-9)\,\mathrm N=5\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The static limit depends on the contact and the normal load. Actual friction balances the $9\,\mathrm N$ pull because $9<14$. Reaching the threshold requires another $5\,\mathrm N$ of pull. The normal reaction stays unchanged because the cord is horizontal.

If the pull rose to $12\,\mathrm N$ and the block stayed at rest, the required friction would rise to $12\,\mathrm N$. The limit would still be $14\,\mathrm N$, so the margin would fall from $5\,\mathrm N$ to $2\,\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A stationary block is acted on by opposite tangential forces of $13$ and $8\,\mathrm N$. The normal reaction is $25\,\mathrm N$. What minimum static coefficient allows the block to remain at rest?

$$
\begin{aligned}f_{\mathrm{rich}}&=(13-8)\,\mathrm N=5\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]\mu_{s,\min}&=\frac{5}{25}=0.2.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find the required friction by adding the forces parallel to the surface with signs. The applied forces give a resultant of $5\,\mathrm N$ in the direction of the larger force. Friction must be equal and opposite. At the threshold, the normal reaction times the coefficient equals the required magnitude. The coefficient is dimensionless.

Substituting the minimum coefficient gives $0.20\cdot25\,\mathrm N=5\,\mathrm N$, exactly the force required. A value of $0.19$ would give at most $4.75\,\mathrm N$. An unbalanced contribution would remain in the direction of the larger pull.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A stationary block with $N=50\,\mathrm N$ has $\mu_s=0.36$. It is pulled right with $24\,\mathrm N$. What minimum horizontal force to the left allows it to stay at rest?

$$
\begin{aligned}f_{s,\max}&=0.36\cdot50\,\mathrm N=18\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]F_{\min}&=(24-18)\,\mathrm N=6\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Without a leftward force, equilibrium would require $24\,\mathrm N$ of friction, above the limit. A leftward applied force reduces the resultant that friction must balance. The minimum occurs when the remaining force is exactly $18\,\mathrm N$. A force greater than $6\,\mathrm N$ may leave a static margin, provided the absolute remaining force stays within the limit.

Denoting the magnitude of the leftward force by $S$, rest requires $\lvert24\,\mathrm N-S\rvert\leq18\,\mathrm N$. The full range is from $6\,\mathrm N$ to $42\,\mathrm N$. For $S>24\,\mathrm N$ the required friction changes direction.

The table gives some leftward pulls with right taken as positive. In the first and last cases, the required force exceeds the $18\,\mathrm N$ limit. In the third case it is positive because the leftward pull is stronger.

| Pull $S$ | Required friction | Rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $4\,\mathrm N$ | $-20\,\mathrm N$ | No |
| $16\,\mathrm N$ | $-8\,\mathrm N$ | Yes |
| $30\,\mathrm N$ | $+6\,\mathrm N$ | Yes |
| $44\,\mathrm N$ | $+20\,\mathrm N$ | No |

</div>
