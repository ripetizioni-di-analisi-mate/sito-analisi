## Exercise 1

Two springs extend by $6$ and $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ respectively under a force of $6\,\mathrm N$. What is the ratio $k_2/k_1$?

::grafico{f="x;3*x" punti="6,6,pieno,A;2,6,pieno,B" nomi="x (cm),F (N)" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=0 ymax=15 titolo="Under a force of 6 N the spring represented by the steeper line extends by 2 cm, the other by 6 cm"}

$$
\frac{k_2}{k_1}=\frac{F/x_2}{F/x_1}=\frac{x_1}{x_2}=\frac{6}{2}=3.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For equal force, the spring that extends less must be stiffer. The stiffness ratio is the inverse of the extension ratio because each stiffness is force divided by extension. The lengths already use the same unit, which cancels in the ratio.

To check using the absolute values, convert the extensions to $0.06\,\mathrm m$ and $0.02\,\mathrm m$. Dividing $6\,\mathrm N$ by each gives $100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$. Their ratio is three.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two parallel springs attached to the same mounts have $k_1=180\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $k_2=120\,\mathrm{N/m}$. What is the total force at a common extension of $3\,\mathrm{cm}$?

$$
F=(k_1+k_2)x=(180+120)(0.03)\,\mathrm N=9\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The shared mounts impose the same increase in length on the two springs. Both forces contribute to the total pull in the same direction, so add their magnitudes. The individual products give $5.4\,\mathrm N$ and $3.6\,\mathrm N$. The stiffness measured at the mounts is the sum of the two spring constants.

The combined stiffness is $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and, multiplied by $0.03\,\mathrm m$, gives back $9\,\mathrm N$. Using the same deformation requires both springs to be engaged between the supports throughout the travel considered.

At the same displacement, removing the second spring leaves only the first spring's $5.4\,\mathrm N$. The loss of $3.6\,\mathrm N$ equals the individual contribution removed. The displacement remains the same because it is imposed by the supports.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two springs of stiffnesses $200$ and $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ are connected in series with a connector of negligible weight. How far does the pair extend under a static pull of $12\,\mathrm N$?

$$
\begin{aligned}x&=\frac{12}{200}\,\mathrm m+\frac{12}{300}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]&=(0.06+0.04)\,\mathrm m=10\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stationary connector is pulled from both sides with the same magnitude. Thus both springs carry a tension of $12\,\mathrm N$. Their extensions differ because their stiffnesses differ. The distance between the assembly's ends increases by the sum of the extensions. The result is greater than either individual extension, as expected.

The individual extensions are $0.06\,\mathrm m$ and $0.04\,\mathrm m$. The equivalent stiffness measured between the ends is then $\dfrac{12\,\mathrm N}{0.10\,\mathrm m}=120\,\mathrm{N/m}$. It is smaller than either individual stiffness because the same tension stretches both elements.

</div>
