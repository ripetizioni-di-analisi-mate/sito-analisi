## Exercise 1

A stationary crate weighing $75\,\mathrm N$ rests on a table and is pulled vertically upward with $30\,\mathrm N$. What is the normal reaction?

::grafico{f="75-x" punti="0,75,pieno;30,45,pieno,A;75,0,pieno" nomi="T (N),N (N)" xmin=0 xmax=84 ymin=0 ymax=80 titolo="Under an upward pull of 30 N, the table's normal reaction is 45 N"}

$$
N+30\,\mathrm N-75\,\mathrm N=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N=45\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolate the forces acting on the crate. The table pushes and the cord pulls upward, whereas weight acts in the opposite direction. The table supports the part of the load not carried by the cord. The positive normal reaction makes the assumed contact admissible.

The $45\,\mathrm N$ normal force is the contact contribution that could be measured beneath the box. Adding it to the rope's $30\,\mathrm N$ gives back the $75\,\mathrm N$ weight, with both supporting forces pointing upwards.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A stationary box weighing $90\,\mathrm N$ is pushed down by $18\,\mathrm N$ and pulled up by $30\,\mathrm N$. What is the normal reaction of the horizontal surface?

$$
N+30\,\mathrm N-18\,\mathrm N-90\,\mathrm N=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N=78\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The push and weight have the same sign because both act downward. The cord supports $30\,\mathrm N$ of the total downward load of $108\,\mathrm N$. The surface supports the rest. Adding the $78\,\mathrm N$ normal reaction to the $30\,\mathrm N$ tension checks that the upward forces total $108\,\mathrm N$.

If the rope were removed while the downward push stayed the same, the surface would have to support all $108\,\mathrm N$. The difference from the present reading equals the tension that was removed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object weighing $40\,\mathrm N$ is assumed to remain stationary on a non-adhesive horizontal plane while a cord pulls it vertically upward with $45\,\mathrm N$. What does the check show?

$$
N=(40-45)\,\mathrm N=-5\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For an upward normal reaction, the equation demands a negative value. A non-adhesive surface can only push outward from the surface. A five-newton downward force would require another constraint. The stated forces do not allow equilibrium with support contact.

With zero normal force, weight and tension already leave an upward resultant of $5\,\mathrm N$. A normal force that the table can actually exert would increase this resultant. Equilibrium in this setup would require a restraint that could hold the box down.

</div>
