## Exercise 1

A spring is $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ long under a $6\,\mathrm N$ pull and $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ long under a $15\,\mathrm N$ pull. In the linear range, what is the natural length?

::grafico{f="3*(x-10)" punti="10,0,pieno;12,6,pieno,A;15,15,pieno,B" nomi="L (cm),F (N)" xmin=10 xmax=16 ymin=0 ymax=19 titolo="Zero deformation occurs at 10 cm. Segment AB determines the slope"}

$$
\begin{aligned}k&=\frac{15-6}{15-12}\,\mathrm{N/cm}=3\,\mathrm{N/cm}\\[6pt]L_0&=\left(12-\frac{6}{3}\right)\,\mathrm{cm}=10\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the force increases by $9\,\mathrm N$, the length increases by $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. The gradient is therefore $3\,\mathrm{N/cm}$. The first measurement corresponds to a $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ extension, which is subtracted from the measured length. The second measurement gives an extension of $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and confirms the same starting length.

On the graph with $L$ in centimetres, the line is described by $F=(3\,\mathrm{N/cm})(L-10\,\mathrm{cm})$ over the range considered. A reading at $13\,\mathrm{cm}$ should therefore give $9\,\mathrm N$. That third point would test the model between the two initial readings.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A spring with natural length $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ and spring constant $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is compressed by a force of magnitude $8\,\mathrm N$. What is its length?

$$
\begin{aligned}\lvert x\rvert &=\frac{8}{200}\,\mathrm m=0.04\,\mathrm m=4\,\mathrm{cm}\\[6pt]L&=(18-4)\,\mathrm{cm}=14\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force determines the magnitude of the deformation in the linear range. The length decreases because the load compresses the spring. Convert metres to centimetres before subtracting. The result is smaller than the natural length, as expected for compression.

Taking the direction from the fixed end to the moving end as positive, the deformation is $-4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The spring exerts a positive component of $8\,\mathrm N$, while the push holding it compressed has the same magnitude in the opposite direction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A slowly applied load yields extension–force pairs $(2\,\mathrm{cm},4\,\mathrm N)$, $(4\,\mathrm{cm},8\,\mathrm N)$, $(6\,\mathrm{cm},15\,\mathrm N)$. Which conclusion is consistent with the exact readings?

$$
\frac{8-4}{4-2}\,\mathrm{N/cm}=2\,\mathrm{N/cm},\quad \frac{15-8}{6-4}\,\mathrm{N/cm}=3.5\,\mathrm{N/cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A linear spring has one value of the slope. The extension interval is two centimetres in both comparisons. Over the last interval, the force increases by $7\,\mathrm N$ rather than $4\,\mathrm N$. The three exact measurements do not lie on a single straight line. The linear model with slope $2\,\mathrm{N/cm}$ describes the first two measurements.

The second slope is $\dfrac{7\,\mathrm N}{2\,\mathrm{cm}}=3.5\,\mathrm{N/cm}$. Extending the model through the first two points, at $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ the predicted force is $12\,\mathrm N$. The $15\,\mathrm N$ reading quantifies the difference from that prediction.

</div>
