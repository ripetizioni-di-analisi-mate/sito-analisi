## Exercise 1

A lever needs a moment of $60\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The effort's moment arm increases from $1$ to $2\,\mathrm m$. By how many newtons does the required force decrease?

::grafico{f="60/x" nomi="b (m),F (N)" punti="0.5,120,pieno;1,60,pieno,A;2,30,pieno,B" xmin=0.4 xmax=2.6 ymin=0 ymax=165 titolo="At a fixed moment of 60 N m, doubling the moment arm halves the required force"}

$$
\Delta F=\left(\frac{60}{1}-\frac{60}{2}\right)\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For fixed moment, force and arm are inversely proportional. Doubling the arm halves the force from sixty to thirty newtons. Subtract the two readings to obtain the decrease. They are two points on the curve of the same moment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A crank of length $0.25\,\mathrm m$ must produce a moment of magnitude $15\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. What is the minimum force if the direction is freely chosen?

$$
F_{\min}=\frac{M}{r}=\frac{15\,\mathrm{N\,m}}{0.25\,\mathrm m}=60\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Minimize the force by using the greatest available moment arm. The entire crank length is the arm only when force is perpendicular. An oblique force would shorten the arm and require a greater magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An ideal plier arm has effort arm $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ and load arm $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its handle receives $12\,\mathrm N$. With the forces normal to the lever, what force does that half apply to the object?

$$
F_r=\frac{12\cdot18}{3}\,\mathrm N=72\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolate one half and balance the effort and load moments about the hinge. The effort arm is six times the load arm, so the force that half exerts on the object has six times the magnitude of the handle force.

</div>
