## Exercise 1

A light lever carries $60\,\mathrm N$ at $0.2\,\mathrm m$ left of O and a downward force $0.6\,\mathrm m$ to the right adjusted for equilibrium. What is the vertical pivot reaction?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-1.5,0 4,0;-0.25,-0.5 0,0 0.25,-0.5 -0.25,-0.5" vettori="-1,0,-1,-2,60 N;3,0,3,-0.667,F;0,0,0,2.667,R" punti="0,0,pieno,O" etichette="-0.55,0.4,0.2 m;1.8,0.4,0.6 m" xmin=-2 xmax=4.5 ymin=-2.8 ymax=3.3 titolo="Lever in equilibrium with both loads downward and an upward pivot reaction"}

$$
\begin{aligned}F&=\frac{60\cdot0.2}{0.6}\,\mathrm N=20\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]R&=(60+20)\,\mathrm N=80\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First balance the moments about the pivot to obtain the force on the right. Then the pivot balances both downward vertical forces. The upward reaction exceeds either load and still gives zero moment about the pivot.

To check, take moments about the left-hand load. The $80\,\mathrm N$ reaction produces $80\cdot0.2=16\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ anticlockwise, and the $20\,\mathrm N$ force on the right produces $20\cdot0.8=16\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ clockwise. The two contributions also cancel about this second point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $30\,\mathrm N$ weight acts $0.4\,\mathrm m$ to the right of a pivot. An upward rope acts farther right at $0.6\,\mathrm m$. What is the balancing tension, neglecting lever weight?

$$
T\cdot0.6=30\cdot0.4\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T=20\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Forces on the same side of a pivot can produce opposite moments when their directions differ. Weight tends to lower the right side. The rope tends to lift it and has the longer arm, so it needs fewer newtons than the weight.

With the calculated $20\,\mathrm N$ tension, vertical balance requires an upward pivot reaction of $10\,\mathrm N$. Taking moments about the cable attachment gives $30\cdot0.2=6\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ from the load and $-10\cdot0.6=-6\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ from the reaction. Their sum confirms equilibrium.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A lever carries $24\,\mathrm N$ at $0.5\,\mathrm m$ right of the pivot and its own weight $10\,\mathrm N$ acts $0.2\,\mathrm m$ to the right. What downward force $0.4\,\mathrm m$ left of the pivot balances it?

$$
F=\frac{24\cdot0.5+10\cdot0.2}{0.4}\,\mathrm N=35\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both weights on the right turn the lever clockwise, so their moment magnitudes add. The required load is on the other side and produces an anticlockwise moment. The lever's own weight contributes two newton metres, so omitting it would underestimate the required force by five newtons.

The clockwise contributions are $24\cdot0.5=12\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ and $10\cdot0.2=2\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The $35\,\mathrm N$ force on the left gives exactly $35\cdot0.4=14\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ in the opposite direction. The pivot must also support all three vertical loads, totalling $24+10+35=69\,\mathrm N$.

</div>
