## Exercise 1

A block weighs $120\,\mathrm N$, has base width $0.4\,\mathrm m$, and has a centred centre of gravity. A rightward horizontal push acts at height $0.5\,\mathrm m$. With enough friction, what push reaches the tipping threshold?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-0.7,0 3.8,0;0,0 0,3 2,3 2,0" vettori="1,1.5,1,-0.5,P;0,2.5,2.4,2.5,F" punti="2,0,pieno,O" etichette="0.25,-0.37,0.4 m;2.55,1.25,0.5 m" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=3.8 titolo="Edge O is the reference at the tipping threshold. The base is 0.4 m wide and the force is applied 0.5 m above it."}

$$
Fh=P\frac b2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F=120\frac{0.2}{0.5}\,\mathrm N=48\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the threshold, take moments about the right-hand edge. Weight acts vertically through the centre, so its arm is half the base width. The push acts horizontally with arm equal to its height. Equating the two opposing moment magnitudes gives the limit.

While the push is $24\,\mathrm N$, its moment about the centre of the base is $12\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The line of action of the normal reaction shifts $\dfrac{12}{120}\,\mathrm m=0.10\,\mathrm m$ from the centre. At $48\,\mathrm N$ the shift reaches $0.20\,\mathrm m$, so the normal reaction acts at the edge.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block weighs $100\,\mathrm N$ with a centred centre of gravity and a base $0.4\,\mathrm m$ wide. A horizontal push acts at height $0.4\,\mathrm m$ and the static coefficient is $0.3$. Which threshold does an increasing push reach first?

$$
\begin{aligned}F_{\mathrm{sl}}&=0.3\cdot100\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]F_{\mathrm{tip}}&=\frac{100\cdot0.2}{0.4}\,\mathrm N=50\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Check the friction and moment limits separately while contact is at rest. The horizontal push leaves the normal reaction equal to weight. The friction limit is reached before the moments permit tipping from rest.

The coefficient that would make the two thresholds simultaneous can be found as a check. To balance a $50\,\mathrm N$ push, the maximum friction would have to reach $50\,\mathrm N$, requiring $\mu_s=\dfrac{50}{100}=0.50$. The actual coefficient $0.30$ is lower, so the limit of static friction is reached first.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body weighs $80\,\mathrm N$ and its centre of gravity is $0.15\,\mathrm m$ horizontally from its tipping edge. The horizontal push available is $30\,\mathrm N$ and friction suffices. What minimum height reaches the threshold?

$$
h_{\min}=\frac{80\,\mathrm N\cdot0.15\,\mathrm m}{30\,\mathrm N}=0.4\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The weight's restoring moment is twelve newton metres. With fixed horizontal force, overturning moment grows with height. The threshold is where the two products are equal. A lower point of application would produce insufficient moment.

At $0.4\,\mathrm m$ the push produces $30\cdot0.4=12\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. To check friction separately, the normal force is $80\,\mathrm N$, so $\mu_s\geq\dfrac{30}{80}=0.375$ would be required to reach the calculated threshold without sliding first. The problem assumes this condition holds.

</div>
