## Exercise 1

A vertical force of $25\,\mathrm N$ acts at end A of a horizontal spanner. Bolt O is $0.3\,\mathrm m$ from A. What is the moment magnitude?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3,0" vettori="3,0,3,2,F" punti="0,0,pieno,O;3,0,pieno,A" etichette="1.5,-0.35,0.3 m" xmin=-1 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1 ymax=2.8 titolo="The 0.3 m moment arm is perpendicular to the vertical force"}

$$
M=25\,\mathrm N\cdot0.3\,\mathrm m=7.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line of action is vertical and OA is horizontal. Thus OA is the perpendicular moment arm, as shown. Multiply distance by force. The product of newtons and metres gives the unit of moment.

The diagram shows the force pointing upwards to the right of O. With anticlockwise positive, this gives $M_O=+7.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. A vertical downward force of the same magnitude gives $M_O=-7.5\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The problem asks for the magnitude, which is the same in both cases.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At a point $0.4\,\mathrm m$ from a lever's pivot, a force of $70\,\mathrm N$ acts. The component perpendicular to the lever is three fifths of the force magnitude. What is the magnitude of the moment about the pivot?

$$
M=rF_\perp=0.4\cdot70\cdot\frac35\,\mathrm{N\,m}=16.8\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line of action of the component along the lever passes through the pivot, so its moment is zero. The perpendicular component is forty-two newtons. The moment is the product of this component and the four-decimetre distance. The result is smaller than it would be if the entire force were perpendicular.

The perpendicular component is $\dfrac35\cdot70=42\,\mathrm N$, so the product is $0.40\cdot42=16.8\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. To check the scale, the greatest moment attainable with that force is $0.40\cdot70=28\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The ratio of the actual result to the maximum is exactly $\dfrac35$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A horizontal force of $18\,\mathrm N$ acts at a point $0.4\,\mathrm m$ to the right and $0.3\,\mathrm m$ above the pivot. What is the moment magnitude?

$$
M=(18\,\mathrm N)(0.3\,\mathrm m)=5.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The arm is the distance from the pivot to the horizontal line through the point where the force is applied. Thus it is the vertical separation. Neither the horizontal separation nor the straight-line distance to the application point is the arm for this force.

If the force points to the right, in $M_O=xF_y-yF_x$ we have $F_y=0$, leaving $-0.3\cdot18=-5.4\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. The clockwise sign reverses if the force points leftwards. Thus moving the point horizontally at the same height leaves the requested magnitude unchanged.

</div>
