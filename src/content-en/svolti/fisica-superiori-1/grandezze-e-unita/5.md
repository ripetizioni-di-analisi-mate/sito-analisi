## Exercise 1

An experiment lasts 7 hours and 40 minutes. How long does it last in seconds?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-4 xmax=490 ymin=-1 ymax=1.9 segmenti="0,0,420,0;420,0,460,0" punti="0,0;420,0;460,0" etichette="210,0.48,7 hours;440,0.86,40 min" titolo="Full hours precede the additional minutes on the timeline"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= 7\cdot3600+40\cdot60\\[6pt]
&= 27600\,\mathrm s
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An hour contains sixty minutes and every minute lasts sixty seconds. The first term therefore uses the factor $60^2=3600$ and the second uses $60$. Both terms count seconds, so their sum covers the whole duration. Converting the two contributions back separately recovers the units in the question. The total duration is $t=7\cdot3600+40\cdot60=27600\,\mathrm s$.

The result can also be found entirely through minutes. The seven hours take $7\cdot60=420\,\mathrm{min}$ and the remaining forty minutes give $460\,\mathrm{min}$. Multiplying by $60$ recovers $27600\,\mathrm s$. Hours and minutes are separate units, so the forty minutes count as forty sixtieths of an hour.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sensor records an interval of $27\,\mathrm{{ms}}$. How long is it in seconds?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= 27\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
&= 0.027\,\mathrm s
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symbol $\mathrm{ms}$ means a second with the prefix milli. One second contains a thousand one-millisecond intervals, so an interval of one millisecond represents $10^{-3}\,\mathrm s$. Multiply this value by the number of milliseconds to find the recorded time. The quantity remains a duration. It follows that $t=27\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm s=0.027\,\mathrm s$.

The equivalence moves the value exactly onto a different scale. If an available readout showed only hundredths of a second, it could display the nearest value of $0.03\,\mathrm s$. That number differs from the converted one by $0.003\,\mathrm s$, or $3\,\mathrm{ms}$. The difference comes from rounding on the readout after changing units.

</div>

## Exercise 3

After the start command an instrument waits for $160\,\mathrm{ms}$. It then makes four acquisitions of $250\,\mathrm{ms}$ each, with a $40\,\mathrm{ms}$ pause between consecutive acquisitions. How many seconds after the command does the last acquisition end?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=t_{\mathrm{attesa}}+4t_{\mathrm{acq}}+3t_{\mathrm{pausa}}\\
 &=160\,\mathrm{ms}+4\cdot250\,\mathrm{ms}+3\cdot40\,\mathrm{ms}\\
 &=1280\,\mathrm{ms}=1.280\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem starts at the command and ends as the last acquisition finishes. The wait before the first reading therefore occupies the first $160\,\mathrm{ms}$. The acquisitions take $1000\,\mathrm{ms}$ in total. Between four successive acquisitions there are three pauses, one in each internal gap, adding $120\,\mathrm{ms}$. A pause after the last acquisition would come after the requested end time.

Check the sum using the end time of each acquisition. The first ends at $160+250=410\,\mathrm{ms}$. Each later acquisition finishes $40+250=290\,\mathrm{ms}$ after the previous one, so the last ends at $410+3\cdot290=1280\,\mathrm{ms}$. Convert the total to seconds in a single step.

</div>
