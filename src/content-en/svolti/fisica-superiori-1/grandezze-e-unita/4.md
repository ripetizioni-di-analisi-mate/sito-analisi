## Exercise 1

A scale reads $625\,\mathrm g$. What is the mass in kilograms?

::grafico{f="0.001*x" xmin=0 xmax=1000 ymin=0 ymax=1 nomi="m (g),m (kg)" punti="625,0.625,pieno,A" segmenti="0,0.625,625,0.625;625,0,625,0.625" titolo="Point A represents a mass of 625 g, equal to 0.625 kg"}

$$
0.625\,\mathrm{kg}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A kilogram contains a thousand grams. Divide the value in grams by one thousand because each gram is a thousandth of a kilogram. The scale gives a mass reading. A weight force would be expressed in newtons and would also require the strength of the gravitational field. Since $1000\,\mathrm g=1\,\mathrm{kg}$, the mass is $0.625\,\mathrm{kg}$.

One scale check compares the reading with a one-kilogram reference mass. Their difference is $1000-625=375\,\mathrm g$. In kilograms it is $1-0.625=0.375\,\mathrm{kg}$. The conversion therefore preserves the comparison with the reference even though the number used to express it changes by a factor of one thousand.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A quantity is defined as the ratio of a mass of $9\,\mathrm{kg}$ to a volume in $\mathrm m^3$. What is its SI unit?

- $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $\mathrm{kg\,m^3}$
- $\mathrm{m^3/kg}$
- $\mathrm{kg/m^2}$

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A derived unit follows the operation defining the quantity. The mass stays in the numerator, in kilograms, and the volume goes in the denominator, in cubic metres. The ratio gives a mass per unit volume. Changing the sample's mass would change the number while keeping the same unit. Dividing mass by volume gives the unit $\dfrac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm m^3}$.

The exact volume is unspecified, which does not prevent identifying the unit. If it were $1\,\mathrm m^3$, for example, the numerical ratio would be $9$. For $2\,\mathrm m^3$ it would be $4.5$. In both cases the numerator is measured in kilograms and the denominator in cubic metres, so the result is in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A container holds $65\,\mathrm g$ of material in $7\,\mathrm L$. Express the mass-to-volume ratio in $\mathrm{kg/L}$.

$$
\dfrac{0.065}{7} = \dfrac{13}{1400}\,\mathrm{kg/L}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The requested units are kilograms in the numerator and litres in the denominator. The volume already has the desired unit, so only the mass needs conversion, by dividing its value in grams by a thousand. Divide that mass by the number of litres. The ratio measures how many kilograms are present in one litre at the same concentration. The ratio is $\dfrac{0.065}{7}=\dfrac{13}{1400}\,\mathrm{kg/L}$.

Check the ratio by multiplying it by the stated $7\,\mathrm L$. The litres cancel, leaving $0.065\,\mathrm{kg}$, which equals the original $65\,\mathrm g$. With the volume expressed in cubic metres, the ratio's numerical value would be one thousand times larger because that new reference unit contains one thousand litres.

</div>
