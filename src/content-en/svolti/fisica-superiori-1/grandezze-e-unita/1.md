## Exercise 1

A tape is $295\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. Express its length in metres.

::grafico{assi=0 unita=0 xmin=-10 xmax=340 ymin=-1 ymax=2 segmenti="0,0,295,0" punti="0,0;295,0" etichette="32,0.35,0;265,0.35,295 cm;170,-0.47,2.95 m" titolo="Two ways to express the same length"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
l &= 295\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
&= 2.95\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The aim is to describe the same length in metres. Each centimetre represents one hundredth of a metre, so the number of centimetres is multiplied by $10^{-2}$. The value in metres is smaller because a metre contains one hundred centimetres. Multiplying the new value by one hundred recovers the original reading. It follows that $l=295\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm m=2.95\,\mathrm m$.

The reading can also be split into $200\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $95\,\mathrm{cm}$. The first two groups of one hundred cover $2\,\mathrm m$, and the remaining section covers $0.95\,\mathrm m$. The result is between two and three metres. This scale comparison rules out values such as $29.5\,\mathrm m$ before rechecking the arithmetic.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A road is $4\,\mathrm{km}$ long. How many metres long is it?

$$
\begin{aligned}
l &= 4\cdot1000\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
&= 4000\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The prefix kilo stands for $10^3$. The road is a thousand metres long for every kilometre in the given value, including any fraction of a kilometre. Therefore the numerical value is multiplied by a thousand. The length stays the same because the same road is counted in smaller units. The result is $l=4\cdot1000\,\mathrm m=4000\,\mathrm m$.

Using the units explicitly, write $4\,\mathrm{km}\cdot\dfrac{1000\,\mathrm m}{1\,\mathrm{km}}$. The ratio represents the same length in its numerator and denominator. Kilometres cancel and metres remain. The reciprocal factor would be used if the initial value were in metres and the requested value in kilometres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The factor $10^{-3}$ appears in the prefix of the measurement $8\,\mathrm{mm}$. How many metres does it represent?

- $0.008\,\mathrm m$
- $0.08\,\mathrm m$
- $8000\,\mathrm m$
- $0.000008\,\mathrm m$

$$
8\,\mathrm{mm} = 0.008\,\mathrm m
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the symbol for millimetres the first letter is the prefix and the second denotes the metre. Each unit represents $10^{-3}$ metres, so multiply the given number by that factor. A micrometre would require the factor $10^{-6}$, which is a thousand times smaller. Each millimetre is $10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$, so $8\,\mathrm{mm}=0.008\,\mathrm m$.

Check the relationship between prefixes by expressing the segment in micrometres. The $8\,\mathrm{mm}$ equal $8000\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Multiplying this new count by $10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$ again gives $0.008\,\mathrm m$. The conversion chain shows why applying the micro prefix directly to the number eight would shrink the length by a factor of one thousand.

</div>
