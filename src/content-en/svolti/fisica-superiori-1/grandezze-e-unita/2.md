## Exercise 1

A sheet of card has area $45\,\mathrm{{cm}}^2$. What is the area in square metres?

$$
A = 0.0045\,\mathrm m^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A square centimetre is the area of a square with side $10^{-2}\,\mathrm m$. The conversion factor occurs twice in the product of the sides, giving $10^{-4}$. That factor applies to the entire area measurement. Expressing it in square metres tells how many unit squares with a one-metre side would have the same area as the card. Since $1\,\mathrm{cm}^2=10^{-4}\,\mathrm m^2$, the result is $A=0.0045\,\mathrm m^2$.

A reverse check starts from $0.0045\,\mathrm m^2$ and uses $1\,\mathrm m^2=10000\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. The result must return to $45\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. If the factor of one hundred had been applied only once, converting back would not recover the given area. That error would confuse a cell's side length with the area it covers.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample occupies $55\,\mathrm{{cm}}^3$. What is its volume in cubic metres?

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 55\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m^3\\[6pt]
&= 0.000055\,\mathrm m^3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A cubic centimetre is the volume of a cube with side $10^{-2}\,\mathrm m$. Multiplying the three sides gives three factors of $10^{-2}$, yielding $10^{-6}$. The value in cubic metres therefore contains one millionth of a cubic metre for each cubic centimetre of the sample. Therefore $V=55\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m^3=0.000055\,\mathrm m^3$.

The volume can also be converted using the capacity scale. The $55\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ are $55\,\mathrm{mL}=0.055\,\mathrm L$. Since one litre is $10^{-3}\,\mathrm m^3$, this again gives $0.000055\,\mathrm m^3$. In this second chain the cubed factor is already incorporated in the equivalences among millilitres, litres and cubic metres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A square panel has a side of $7\,\mathrm m$. What is its area in square centimetres?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-1 ymax=8 poligoni="0,0 7,0 7,7 0,7" etichette="3.5,-0.6,700 cm;7.6,3.5,700 cm" titolo="Sides of the square panel after conversion to centimetres"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= (700)^2\\[6pt]
&= 490000\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The question asks for an area in square centimetres. First convert the side using a hundred centimetres per metre. Then multiply the converted length by itself, including its unit. This gives a factor of $100^2$ compared with the numerical value of the area in square metres. The side measures $700\,\mathrm{cm}$, so $A=(700)^2=490000\,\mathrm{cm}^2$.

The same area can be calculated immediately in square metres as $7^2=49$. A square metre contains $100\cdot100=10000$ square centimetres, so the required number is also $49\cdot10000$. The methods agree because converting each side before multiplying is equivalent to converting the square unit of the final product.

</div>
