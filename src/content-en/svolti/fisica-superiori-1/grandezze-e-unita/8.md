## Exercise 1

Two rods of lengths $7\,\mathrm m$ and $400\,\mathrm{{cm}}$ are joined without overlap. What is their total length in metres?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-1 xmax=12 ymin=-1 ymax=2 segmenti="0,0,7,0;7,0,11,0" punti="0,0;7,0;11,0" etichette="3.5,0.5,first rod 7 m;9,0.5,second rod 4 m" titolo="Two rods placed end to end and measured on the same metre scale"}

$$
7+4 = 11\,\mathrm m
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rods are placed end to end without overlap, so their lengths are added. The value in metres can be used directly, and the centimetre value is divided by one hundred. Both terms now count metres. The resulting length must be greater than either of the individual lengths. The second rod measures $4\,\mathrm m$, so the total is $7+4=11\,\mathrm m$.

Changing the common unit does not change the total length. In centimetres the first rod is $700\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the second $400\,\mathrm{cm}$. Their sum is $1100\,\mathrm{cm}$, which converts back to $11\,\mathrm m$. Both calculations rely on the junction having no length of its own, so every part is counted once.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare $75\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $7.5\,\mathrm{dm}$.

- The lengths are equal
- The first is ten times the second
- The second is ten times the first
- The units describe different quantities

$$
7.5\cdot10 = 75\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A decimetre contains ten centimetres. Converting the second measurement shows how many one-centimetre intervals make up the same length. This number matches the first measurement. The two expressions therefore represent the same length, even though the original numbers differ. Converting the second measurement gives $7.5\cdot10=75\,\mathrm{cm}$.

A third unit can provide a check. The $75\,\mathrm{cm}$ are $0.75\,\mathrm m$. A decimetre is one tenth of a metre, so $7.5\,\mathrm{dm}$ also become $0.75\,\mathrm m$. The original numbers cannot be ordered without considering their scales, because each unit covers a different part of the same length.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two strips of lengths $1.20\,\mathrm m$ and $85\,\mathrm{cm}$ are joined in line, overlapping their end sections by $45\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find the resulting length and say how much longer or shorter it is than a $2\,\mathrm m$ section. Treat the stated nominal values as exact.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L&=1.20\,\mathrm m+0.85\,\mathrm m-0.045\,\mathrm m\\
 &=2.005\,\mathrm m,\\
L-2\,\mathrm m&=0.005\,\mathrm m=5\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The joint leaves a section shared by both strips. Adding their two lengths would count this section twice, so subtract one copy. The value $85\,\mathrm{cm}$ becomes $0.85\,\mathrm m$ and $45\,\mathrm{mm}$ becomes $0.045\,\mathrm m$. The three terms are now lengths in the same unit.

Another check uses the positions of the endpoints. With the origin at the beginning of the first strip, the second begins at $1.20-0.045=1.155\,\mathrm m$. It ends $0.85\,\mathrm m$ farther on, at $2.005\,\mathrm m$. The result exceeds the required length by $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ when the stated data are treated as exact.

</div>
