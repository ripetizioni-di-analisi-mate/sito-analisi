## Exercise 1

Two rods of lengths $7\,\mathrm m$ and $400\,\mathrm{{cm}}$ are joined without overlap. What is their total length in metres?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-1 xmax=12 ymin=-1 ymax=2 segmenti="0,0,7,0;7,0,11,0" punti="0,0;7,0;11,0" etichette="3.5,0.5,first rod 7 m;9,0.5,second rod 4 m" titolo="Two rods placed end to end and measured on the same metre scale"}

$$
7+4 = 11\,\mathrm m
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rods are placed end to end without overlap, so their lengths are added. The value in metres can be used directly, and the centimetre value is divided by one hundred. Both terms now count metres. The resulting length must be greater than either of the individual lengths. The second rod measures $4\,\mathrm m$, so the total is $7+4=11\,\mathrm m$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare $75\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $7.5\,\mathrm{dm}$.

- The lengths are equal
- The first is ten times the second
- The second is ten times the first
- The units describe different quantities

$$
7.5\cdot10 = 75\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A decimetre contains ten centimetres. Converting the second measurement shows how many one-centimetre intervals make up the same length. This number matches the first measurement. The two expressions therefore represent the same length, even though the original numbers differ. Converting the second measurement gives $7.5\cdot10=75\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A square panel has a side of $8\,\mathrm m$. What is its area in square centimetres?

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= (800)^2\\[6pt]
&= 640000\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The question asks for an area in square centimetres. First convert the side using a hundred centimetres per metre. Then multiply the converted length by itself, including its unit. This gives a factor of $100^2$ compared with the numerical value of the area in square metres. The side measures $800\,\mathrm{cm}$, so $A=(800)^2=640000\,\mathrm{cm}^2$.

</div>
