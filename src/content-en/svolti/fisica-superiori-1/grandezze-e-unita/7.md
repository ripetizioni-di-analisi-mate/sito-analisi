## Exercise 1

A quantity is defined as the ratio of a mass of $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ to a volume in $\mathrm m^3$. What is its SI unit?

- $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- $\mathrm{kg\,m^3}$
- $\mathrm{m^3/kg}$
- $\mathrm{kg/m^2}$

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm m^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A derived unit follows the operation defining the quantity. The mass stays in the numerator, in kilograms, and the volume goes in the denominator, in cubic metres. The ratio gives a mass per unit volume. Changing the sample's mass would change the number while keeping the same unit. Dividing mass by volume gives the unit $\dfrac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm m^3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has base $8\,\mathrm m$. Which expression has the dimensions of an area?

- $(8\,\mathrm m)(2\,\mathrm m)$
- $(8\,\mathrm m)+(2\,\mathrm m)$
- $(8\,\mathrm m)\div(2\,\mathrm m)$
- $(8\,\mathrm m)(2\,\mathrm s)$

$$
(8\,\mathrm m)(2\,\mathrm m)=16\,\mathrm m^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a rectangle requires a base and a height, both lengths. Multiplying two factors measured in metres gives square metres. Adding two lengths gives metres, while taking their ratio cancels the length units. Multiplying a length by a time would give a quantity with dimensions different from area. The product of the units is $\mathrm m\cdot\mathrm m=\mathrm m^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A container holds $70\,\mathrm g$ of material in $8\,\mathrm L$. Express the mass-to-volume ratio in $\mathrm{kg/L}$.

$$
\dfrac{0.07}{8} = 0.00875\,\mathrm{kg/L}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The requested units are kilograms in the numerator and litres in the denominator. The volume already has the desired unit, so only the mass needs conversion, by dividing its value in grams by a thousand. Divide that mass by the number of litres. The ratio measures how many kilograms are present in one litre at the same concentration. The ratio is $\dfrac{0.07}{8}=0.00875\,\mathrm{kg/L}$.

</div>
