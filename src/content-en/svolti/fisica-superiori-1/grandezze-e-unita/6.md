## Exercise 1

What is the number $0.00007$ in normalized scientific notation?

- $7\cdot10^{-5}$
- $7\cdot10^{5}$
- $7\cdot10^{-4}$
- $7\cdot10^{-6}$

$$
7\cdot10^{-5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The normalized coefficient must be at least $1$ and less than $10$. Move the decimal point five places to obtain a coefficient between $1$ and $10$. The change multiplies the number by $10^5$, so it is compensated by the factor $10^{-5}$. Their product gives the original value, which is less than one. Moving the decimal point five places to the right produces $7$, so the exponent is $-5$.

The form $0.7\cdot10^{-4}$ would still represent the stated number because dividing the coefficient by ten and increasing the power by ten compensate. However, $0.7$ is below the lower bound for normalized form. Writing $7$ in front of the power satisfies both the numerical equality and the condition on the coefficient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $(5\cdot10^3)(8\cdot10^2)$ and choose its value.

- $40\cdot10^5$
- $13\cdot10^5$
- $40\cdot10^6$
- $40\cdot10^1$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5\cdot10^3)(8\cdot10^2) &= (5\cdot8)\cdot10^{3+2}\\[6pt]
&=40\cdot10^5=4\cdot10^6
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two coefficients and the two powers of ten can be grouped separately. Powers with the same base are multiplied by adding the exponents, because the factors of ten accumulate. The question asks for the product's value. For normalized notation, move the coefficient back into the range from $1$ to $10$ and compensate by adjusting the exponent. The coefficient is $5\cdot8=40$ and the exponent is $3+2=5$.

The product can be written as $4\cdot10^6$ by shifting the decimal point one place in the coefficient $40$ and increasing the exponent from five to six. As a check, the original factors are five thousand and eight hundred. Their product is four million. Thus both power-of-ten forms represent the same exact value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Thicknesses measured before and after machining are $8.12\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$ and $7.97\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$. Find the thickness removed in micrometres. Compare it with the value obtained by rounding the coefficients to tenths first.

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=785 xmax=824 ymin=0 ymax=1.65 segmenti="797,.5,812,.5;797,.27,797,.72;812,.27,812,.72" punti="797,.5,pieno;812,.5,pieno" etichette="797,.95,797 µm;812,.95,812 µm;805,.20,15 µm" titolo="The thickness removed is the distance between the two readings on the micrometre scale"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta s
&=(8.12-7.97)\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m\\
&=1.5\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm m=15\,\mathrm{\mu m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the thickness after machining from the thickness before, since the material removed is the decrease in thickness. The readings already share a power of ten, so the coefficients can be subtracted directly. Write the difference $0.15\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$ in normalized form before separating out the factor $10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$ for one micrometre.

If the coefficients are first rounded to $8.1$ and $8.0$, the difference is $0.1\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m=10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Rounding early loses $5\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ from the difference. To check the original difference, convert both readings to micrometres first, obtaining $812$ and $797$, and subtract afterwards.

</div>
