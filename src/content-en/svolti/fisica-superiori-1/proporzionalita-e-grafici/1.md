## Exercise 1

Two quantities follow $y=cx$. A measurement gives $x=7$ and $y=63$. What is the value of $c$?

::grafico{f="9*x" xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=82 punti="7,63,pieno,P" nomi="x,y" segmenti="7,0,7,63;0,63,7,63" titolo="Point P determines the coordinate ratio"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= \dfrac{y}{x}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{63}{7}\\[6pt]
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the direct model, the vertical value is the horizontal value times a coefficient. The given nonzero horizontal value allows that coefficient to be found by division. The ratio measures the vertical increase for a single unit on the horizontal axis. Using it in the law recovers the observed pair. The calculation gives $c=\dfrac{y}{x}=\dfrac{63}{7}=9$.

To check the coefficient, substitute it into the law at horizontal coordinate seven. The product $9\cdot7$ reproduces the vertical coordinate sixty-three. If the relation had a nonzero constant term, one pair would not suffice to determine the slope. The ratio works here because direct proportion is given.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Collected liquid volume follows $V=qt$ at a constant flow rate $q=7\,\mathrm{L/min}$. What volume is collected in $9\,\mathrm{min}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 7\cdot9\\[6pt]
&= 63\,\mathrm L
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Flow rate gives the number of litres collected each minute and stays constant. To find the total, take the same contribution for every minute, so multiply. The time in minutes cancels the minutes in the denominator of the rate. The origin represents no time elapsed and no volume collected. The law gives $V=7\cdot9=63\,\mathrm L$.

Nine minutes make nine one-minute intervals. Each adds the same seven litres, so the total added is sixty-three. Check the units as $\mathrm{L/min}\cdot\mathrm{min}=\mathrm L$. This is the volume collected during the nine minutes. Finding the total in the vessel would also require knowing any volume already present.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A test spring follows $F=kx$ with $k=70\,\mathrm{N/m}$. What extension does a force $14\,\mathrm N$ produce?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \dfrac{F}{k}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{14}{70}\\[6pt]
&= 0.2\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring law is supplied for its proportional range, with coefficient force per unit length. Extension is the quantity multiplied by that coefficient under these conditions. Divide the force by the coefficient to isolate it; metres remain when the units cancel. Substituting the extension recovers the stated force. It follows that $x=\dfrac{F}{k}=\dfrac{14}{70}=0.2\,\mathrm m$.

Dividing by the coefficient cancels newtons and leaves metres because the coefficient is given in newtons per metre. The result is two tenths of a metre, or twenty centimetres. Substituting $0.2\,\mathrm m$ into the law gives $70\cdot0.2$, which returns fourteen newtons. The law applies over the range where force and extension are proportional.

</div>
