## Exercise 1

A container initially has $9\,\mathrm L$ and takes in $7\,\mathrm{L/min}$. How much liquid does it contain after $3\,\mathrm{min}$, with no losses?

::grafico{f="7*x+9" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=37 nomi="t (min),V (L)" punti="0,9,pieno;3,30,pieno,P" titolo="The container starts with 9 litres and reaches 30 after three minutes"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 9+7\cdot3\\[6pt]
&= 30\,\mathrm L
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original volume is the constant term in the model. The product of flow rate and time gives only the added liquid. Add the two quantities because both are volumes and there are no losses. The result exceeds the initial amount because the incoming flow rate is positive. The result is $V=9+7\cdot3=30\,\mathrm L$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a positive direct proportionality, $x$ increases by $70\%$. By what percentage does $y$ increase?

$$
\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{y_1}
=\dfrac{y_2}{y_1}-1
=\dfrac{70}{100}
=70\%
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A percentage increase gives a factor greater than one for the horizontal quantity. The direct law keeps the same coefficient after the change. The factor carries over to the vertical quantity and gives the same percentage change. Each percentage is measured relative to its own initial value. The ratio of the new value to the initial one is $1+\dfrac{70}{100}$ for both quantities.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two models are $y=7x$ and $y=7x+9$. Which statement correctly distinguishes their graphs?

- They have the same slope and different intercepts
- They always have the same intercept
- The second has greater slope
- The first is a quadratic curve

They have the same slope and different intercepts.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two affine laws have the same horizontal coefficient. The graphs have equal slopes, so their vertical gap stays fixed. Their intercepts differ because only the second law includes a nonzero constant term. The lines are therefore distinct and parallel. Both slopes are $7$, while the values at $x=0$ are $0$ and $9$.

</div>
