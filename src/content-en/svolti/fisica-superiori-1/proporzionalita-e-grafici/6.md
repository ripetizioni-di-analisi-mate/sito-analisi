## Exercise 1

A quantity follows $y=cx^2$ with $c>0$ and positive initial $x$. If $x$ becomes $7$ times its initial value, by what factor does $y$ increase?

::grafico{f="x^2" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=0 ymax=65 nomi="x/x₁,y/y₁" punti="1,1,pieno;7,49,pieno,P" titolo="In relative coordinates, multiplying x by seven multiplies y by forty-nine"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_2 &= c(7x_1)^2\\[6pt]
&= 49y_1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal quantity is squared. Substituting its new value gives two copies of the multiplying factor in the square. The law's coefficient is the same before and after, so the ratio of vertical values is the square of the given factor. Indeed $y_2=c(7x_1)^2=49y_1$.

The ratio $\dfrac{y_2}{y_1}=\dfrac{c(7x_1)^2}{cx_1^2}$ isolates the change without requiring the coefficient. The coefficient and the starting square cancel, leaving forty-nine. A concrete example is the area of a square: seven copies of the side in each direction produce a grid of forty-nine original-sized squares.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A quantity follows $y=c/x^2$ with $c>0$ and positive $x$. If $x$ is multiplied by $7$, what is the ratio $y_2/y_1$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{y_2}{y_1} &= \dfrac1{7^2}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{1}{49}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The new horizontal value is squared in the denominator. The denominator is multiplied by the square of the factor, while the numerator is unchanged. The new vertical value is the previous one divided by that square. The ratio is less than one because the positive horizontal value increased. Therefore $\dfrac{y_2}{y_1}=\dfrac1{7^2}=\dfrac{1}{49}$.

In the ratio of the vertical values the positive coefficient cancels, leaving the square of the reciprocal horizontal ratio. The new value is therefore one forty-ninth of the original. Dividing by seven alone would apply to a first-power denominator, whereas the squared denominator changes the scaling here.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two uniform layers of different materials have the same mass. The base area of the second is three times that of the first, and its density is half that of the first. Find the ratio of the second layer's thickness to the first layer's thickness.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_1 &= \rho_1 A_1h_1,\qquad m_2=\rho_2 A_2h_2,\\[4pt]
\rho_1 A_1h_1 &= \dfrac{\rho_1}{2}\cdot 3A_1h_2,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{h_2}{h_1} &= \dfrac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use density times volume, which for a uniform layer is area times thickness. The masses agree, but the volumes differ because density changes. At half the density, the second layer occupies twice the volume for the same mass. Spreading that double volume over triple the area requires multiplying the thickness by two thirds. Check the product of all three changes: $\dfrac12\cdot3\cdot\dfrac23=1$. Taking one third of the thickness would keep the volume unchanged and would instead halve the mass.

</div>
