## Exercise 1

A test spring follows $F=kx$ with $k=80\,\mathrm{N/m}$. What extension does a force $16\,\mathrm N$ produce?

::grafico{f="80*x" xmin=0 xmax=0.36 ymin=0 ymax=29 nomi="x (m),F (N)" punti="0.2,16,pieno,P" segmenti="0,16,0.2,16;0.2,0,0.2,16" titolo="The point with vertical value 16 N has horizontal value 0.2 m"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \dfrac{F}{k}\\[6pt]
&= \dfrac{16}{80}\\[6pt]
&= 0.2\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring law is supplied for its proportional range, with coefficient force per unit length. Extension is the quantity multiplied by that coefficient under these conditions. Divide the force by the coefficient to isolate it; metres remain when the units cancel. Substituting the extension recovers the stated force. It follows that $x=\dfrac{F}{k}=\dfrac{16}{80}=0.2\,\mathrm m$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The law is $xy=96$ with $x,y>0$. What value of $x$ corresponds to $y=4$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \dfrac{96}4\\[6pt]
&= 24
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation gives the constant product and one of its factors. Since the supplied vertical value is positive, division by it does not lose any solutions. It gives the horizontal factor which, multiplied by the vertical one, recovers the required product. Therefore $x=\dfrac{96}4=24$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A container initially has $10\,\mathrm L$ and takes in $8\,\mathrm{L/min}$. How much liquid does it contain after $3\,\mathrm{min}$, with no losses?

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 10+8\cdot3\\[6pt]
&= 34\,\mathrm L
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original volume is the constant term in the model. The product of flow rate and time gives only the added liquid. Add the two quantities because both are volumes and there are no losses. The result exceeds the initial amount because the incoming flow rate is positive. The result is $V=10+8\cdot3=34\,\mathrm L$.

</div>
