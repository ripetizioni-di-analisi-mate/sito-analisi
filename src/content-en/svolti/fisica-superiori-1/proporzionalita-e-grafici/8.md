## Exercise 1

A line passes through $(1;9)$ and $(4;39)$. What is its slope?

::grafico{f="10*x-1" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=50 punti="1,9,pieno,P;4,39,pieno,Q" segmenti="1,9,4,9;4,9,4,39" titolo="The difference triangle has base 3 and height 30"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \dfrac{39-9}{4-1}\\[6pt]
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Slope is the increase in vertical value divided by the increase in horizontal value. Subtract the first vertical value from the second and keep the same order for the horizontal values. This describes the same move in both directions. A positive result describes an increasing line and gives its rise per horizontal unit. The calculation gives $m=\dfrac{39-9}{4-1}=10$.

The slope gives a vertical increase of ten units per horizontal unit. Subtracting the vertical readings of the two points eliminates the intercept. Check by predicting the vertical coordinate after three steps: starting from nine, add $3\cdot10=30$ to reach the observed value thirty-nine.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A force gauge reads $9\,\mathrm N$ at $x=1\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $21\,\mathrm N$ at $x=4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Assume an affine relation and treat those two readings as exact. A third reading at $x=3\,\mathrm{cm}$ is $(16.8\pm0.3)\,\mathrm N$. Find the law and check whether the third reading is compatible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=\dfrac{21-9}{4-1}\,\mathrm{N/cm}=4\,\mathrm{N/cm},\\[4pt]
q&=9\,\mathrm N-4\,\mathrm{N/cm}\cdot1\,\mathrm{cm}=5\,\mathrm N,\\[4pt]
F(3\,\mathrm{cm})&=4\,\mathrm{N/cm}\cdot3\,\mathrm{cm}+5\,\mathrm N=17\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio of differences gives the slope because the intercept cancels. To obtain the intercept, return to the first point and subtract the slope contribution. The second point checks the same law, since $4\cdot4+5=21$. The third reading spans $16.5$ to $17.1\,\mathrm N$, which includes the prediction of seventeen. The central value differs from the prediction by $0.2\,\mathrm N$. This gap is smaller than the stated uncertainty, so the third reading does not contradict the line. Requiring its central value to match the model exactly would be inappropriate.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A linear volume prediction is $V=2t+4$, with time in minutes and volume in litres. At $t=2\,\mathrm{min}$, the reading is $9\,\mathrm L$, and at $t=5\,\mathrm{min}$ it is $18\,\mathrm L$. Find both observed-minus-predicted residuals and the slope through the readings. Is a constant correction to the predictions enough to describe both points?

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=(9-8)\,\mathrm L=1\,\mathrm L,\\[4pt]
r_2&=(18-14)\,\mathrm L=4\,\mathrm L,\\[4pt]
m_{\mathrm{obs}}&=\dfrac{18-9}{5-2}\,\mathrm{L/min}=3\,\mathrm{L/min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find each residual by subtracting the prediction from the reading at the same time. The residuals differ, so a vertical shift is insufficient: the same correction cannot remove both a one-litre gap and a four-litre gap. The readings differ by nine litres over three minutes, or three litres per minute. The initial model predicted two litres per minute. The larger second residual is the consequence of this greater slope. In the revised affine model, the intercept is $9-3\cdot2=3\,\mathrm L$, and the fifth minute gives eighteen litres again.

</div>
