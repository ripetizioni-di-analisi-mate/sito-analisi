## Exercise 1

A line passes through $(1;9)$ and $(4;39)$. What is its slope?

::grafico{f="10*x-1" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=50 punti="1,9,pieno,P;4,39,pieno,Q" segmenti="1,9,4,9;4,9,4,39" titolo="The difference triangle has base 3 and height 30"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \dfrac{39-9}{4-1}\\[6pt]
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Slope is the increase in vertical value divided by the increase in horizontal value. Subtract the first vertical value from the second and keep the same order for the horizontal values. This describes the same move in both directions. A positive result describes an increasing line and gives its rise per horizontal unit. The calculation gives $m=\dfrac{39-9}{4-1}=10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Each horizontal grid square represents $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and each vertical square represents $10\,\mathrm N$. A line rises 3 squares while going right 2 squares. What is its slope in $\mathrm{N/cm}$?

$$
\dfrac{3\cdot10}{2\cdot8} = 1.875\,\mathrm{N/cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find the vertical increase in newtons by multiplying the number of squares by the vertical scale. Find the horizontal increase in centimetres using its own scale. The ratio of the two physical changes gives the slope. Using the ratio of squares alone would omit the different scales. The slope is $\dfrac{3\cdot10}{2\cdot8}=1.875\,\mathrm{N/cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two models are $y=8x$ and $y=8x+10$. Which statement correctly distinguishes their graphs?

- They have the same slope and different intercepts
- They always have the same intercept
- The second has greater slope
- The first is a quadratic curve

They have the same slope and different intercepts.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two affine laws have the same horizontal coefficient. The graphs have equal slopes, so their vertical gap stays fixed. Their intercepts differ because only the second law includes a nonzero constant term. The lines are therefore distinct and parallel. Both slopes are $8$, while the values at $x=0$ are $0$ and $10$.

</div>
