## Exercise 1

Two vessels of different shapes contain water open to the same atmosphere. Point A in the narrow vessel and B in the flared vessel are both $1.35\,\mathrm m$ below the surface. How do their pressures compare?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 1.5,0 1.5,2 0,2;2.7,0 4.6,0 5.4,2 2.2,2" punti="0.75,0.65,pieno,A;3.6,0.65,pieno,B" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="A and B are at the same depth below the two surfaces. The vessel profiles differ"}

$$
p_A-p_B=\rho g(h_A-h_B)=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The column determining local pressure is counted vertically from the point to its reference surface. The heights match, as do the liquid and surface pressure. Vessel shape changes the total liquid quantity, but that quantity is not in the local pressure law.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two water points are separated along an inclined $5\,\mathrm m$ pipe rising $3\,\mathrm m$ from A to B. With $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is $p_A-p_B$?

$$
p_A-p_B=1000\cdot10\cdot3\,\mathrm{Pa}=30\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressure difference follows from the vertical separation along gravity. The diagonal length describes the pipe, but the three-metre rise is the elevation difference. A is deeper, so A minus B is positive.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A tank contains a lower layer $0.6\,\mathrm m$ deep with density $850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and an upper layer $0.4\,\mathrm m$ deep with density $750\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, by how much does the pressure at the bottom exceed that at the surface?

$$
\Delta p=(850\cdot0.6+750\cdot0.4)10\,\mathrm{Pa}=8.1\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving down through two layers adds each layer's weight per unit area. The upper liquid adds three kilopascals and the lower one adds five point one. Both contributions are positive because the path descends along gravity.

</div>
