## Exercise 1

At a free water surface the absolute pressure is $96\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At depth $1.8\,\mathrm m$, with $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what absolute pressure is measured?

::grafico{f="96+10*x" punti="0,96,pieno,S;1.8,114,pieno,A" nomi="h (m),p (kPa)" xmin=0 xmax=2.3 ymin=92 ymax=123 titolo="S is the surface pressure of 96 kPa. A is the absolute pressure at 1.8 m"}

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta p&=1000\cdot10\cdot1.8\,\mathrm{Pa}=18\,\mathrm{kPa}\\[6pt]p_{\rm ass}&=(96+18)\,\mathrm{kPa}=114\,\mathrm{kPa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hydrostatic law adds ten kilopascals per metre to surface pressure. At one point eight metres the column contributes eighteen kilopascals. The absolute sensor starts from the surface value, shown by the graph intercept, and reads their sum.

If the same instrument were zeroed at the surface, it would read only the $18\,\mathrm{kPa}$ increase. The gradient would remain the same and the intercept would become zero. The $114\,\mathrm{kPa}$ answers the question because the absolute sensor is referenced to a vacuum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform pressure of $14\,\mathrm{kPa}$ acts on a pad of area $25\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. What normal force does it produce?

$$
F=(14\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{Pa})(25\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm m^2)=35\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert kilopascals to pascals by multiplying by a thousand and square centimetres to square metres by multiplying by one ten-thousandth. Pressure and area now use the same area units. The square metres cancel in the product, leaving thirty-five newtons.

The calculation can be repeated in another pressure unit. Since $1\,\mathrm{N/cm^2}$ is equivalent to $10\,\mathrm{kPa}$, the given pressure is $1.4\,\mathrm{N/cm^2}$. Using the area of $25\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ directly gives the same product, $35\,\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An absolute sensor reads $82\,\mathrm{kPa}$ in a vessel and ambient pressure is $101\,\mathrm{kPa}$. What is the gauge pressure relative to the surroundings?

$$
p_{\rm rel}=p_{\rm ass}-p_{\rm amb}=(82-101)\,\mathrm{kPa}=-19\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The reference for the gauge reading is ambient pressure. The negative sign means the absolute reading is below the external pressure. Both absolute pressures are positive and the sign arises from their difference.

Check by returning to the absolute scale, $(101-19)\,\mathrm{kPa}=82\,\mathrm{kPa}$. If the ambient pressure rose to $103\,\mathrm{kPa}$ and the absolute value inside stayed the same, the new relative reading would be $-21\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The reference from which the difference is measured has changed.

</div>
