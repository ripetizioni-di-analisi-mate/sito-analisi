## Exercise 1

An extra $48\,\mathrm N$ acts on a piston of area $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. What extra force is obtained at the $16\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ piston at the same elevation in an ideal press?

::grafico{f="12*x" punti="4,48,pieno,A;16,192,pieno,B" nomi="A (cm²),F (N)" xmin=0 xmax=18 ymin=0 ymax=220 titolo="A is the input piston and B the output piston at the same pressure increase"}

$$
F_2=48\,\mathrm N\cdot\frac{16}{4}=192\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The input force gives a pressure change. That change is the same on the second piston, so its force grows with area. The areas have ratio four and common units cancel. In the graph, the output is the point with four times the horizontal coordinate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an ideal press, a piston of area $6\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ advances by $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. How far does the piston of area $15\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ move if the liquid is incompressible?

$$
x_2=\frac{A_1x_1}{A_2}=\frac{6\cdot5}{15}\,\mathrm{cm}=2\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first piston displaces thirty cubic centimetres. The second must make room for the same volume. Because its area is larger it needs a smaller displacement. The product fifteen times two is thirty, checking the balance.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The circular pistons of an ideal level press have diameters $4$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. An added $125\,\mathrm N$ is needed on the large piston. What force is applied to the small one?

$$
F_1=125\left(\frac{4}{10}\right)^2\,\mathrm N=20\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equal pressure increase, forces are in the ratio of the areas. Circle areas are proportional to the diameters squared, since each radius is half the diameter. The small piston needs less than one fifth of the output force, consistent with the area ratio.

</div>
