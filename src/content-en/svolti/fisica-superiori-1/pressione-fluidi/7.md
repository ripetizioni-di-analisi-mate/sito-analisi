## Exercise 1

A water manometer is connected to two gases. Surface A is $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ lower than B. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ and $\rho=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, how do their pressures compare?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-2,-0.6 -1,-0.6 -1,-1 1,-1 1,0.6 2,0.6 2,-2 -2,-2" linee="-2,2 -2,-2 2,-2 2,2;-1,2 -1,-1 1,-1 1,2;-2,-0.6 -1,-0.6;1,0.6 2,0.6" segmenti="-1,-0.6,0.2,-0.6;0.2,0.6,1,0.6;0.2,-0.6,0.2,0.6" vettori="-1.5,1.5,-1.5,-0.35,p_A;1.5,1.5,1.5,0.85,p_B" etichette="0.35,0,h" xmin=-2.8 xmax=3 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.7 titolo="The lower surface is on side A, so gas pressure there is higher. The level difference is h"}

$$
p_A=p_B+\rho g\Delta h=p_B+1000\cdot10\cdot0.12\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Start at surface B and descend in the liquid to elevation A. Pressure rises along this path, so gas A must exert the higher pressure. Converting twelve centimetres to metres gives twelve hundred pascals of difference. The comparison uses the full separation of the surfaces.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal barometer has negligible pressure above the column and liquid of density $12000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is its column height when outside pressure is $96000\,\mathrm{Pa}$?

$$
h=\frac{96000}{12000\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=0.8\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Atmospheric pressure balances the hydrostatic contribution of the whole column. Pressure above it is negligible, so there is no further appreciable amount to subtract. The dense liquid supplies one hundred and twenty thousand pascals per metre, so less than one metre suffices.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A U-tube with unequal arm cross-sections initially has both surfaces at the same height. The left then rises $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the right falls $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. With $\rho=850\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is the pressure-difference magnitude?

$$
\Delta p=(850)(10)(0.03+0.05)\,\mathrm{Pa}=680\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motions go in opposite senses, so add their magnitudes to obtain the new height gap. Density and field multiply those eight centimetres after conversion to metres. Pressure is higher on the right because its surface is lower.

</div>
