## Exercise 1

An open tank contains $0.4\,\mathrm m$ of oil at $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ over $0.8\,\mathrm m$ of water at $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$, what is gauge pressure at the bottom?

::grafico{linee="0,0 0.4,3.2 1.2,11.2" punti="0.4,3.2,pieno,I;1.2,11.2,pieno,F" nomi="h (m),Δp (kPa)" xmin=0 xmax=1.4 ymin=0 ymax=13 titolo="The first segment is in oil. Below I, pressure rises with the larger slope for water"}

$$
\Delta p=(800\cdot0.4+1000\cdot0.8)10\,\mathrm{Pa}=11.2\,\mathrm{kPa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Gauge pressure is zero at the open surface. Descending through the oil reaches three point two kilopascals at the interface. Water adds another eight. The density change alters the graph's slope while pressure remains continuous.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an open U-tube a liquid column of density $750\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ is $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall above its interface with water. Water density is $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. How high is the opposite water surface above the interface?

$$
750\,g\,(20\,\mathrm{cm})=1000\,g\,h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=15\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare two points at the same elevation in the connected water. The two atmospheric pressures cancel. So does $g$, leaving the density ratio. The water column is shorter because water weighs more per unit height for equal area.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two open communicating arms have constant areas $10$ and $30\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ and contain water. After slowly adding $80\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of water, by how much do both surfaces rise?

$$
80\,\mathrm{cm^3}=(10+30)\,\mathrm{cm^2}\cdot\Delta h\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta h=2\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final surfaces stay at one height because both liquid and outside pressure match. The added volume fills both arms up to the new level. The effective area is the sum of the two cross-sections, so the rise is smaller than in either single isolated arm.

</div>
