## Exercise 1

A $6\,\mathrm N$ normal load is spread first over $0.02\,\mathrm m^2$ and then over $0.06\,\mathrm m^2$. How much does pressure decrease?

::grafico{f="600/x" punti="2,300,pieno,A;6,100,pieno,B" nomi="A (dm²),p (Pa)" xmin=1 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=700 titolo="The areas of 0.02 and 0.06 m² correspond to 2 and 6 dm². The 6 N force produces 300 and 100 Pa respectively"}

$$
\begin{aligned}p_A&=\frac{6}{0.02}\,\mathrm{Pa}=300\,\mathrm{Pa}\\[6pt]p_B&=\frac{6}{0.06}\,\mathrm{Pa}=100\,\mathrm{Pa}\\[6pt]p_A-p_B&=200\,\mathrm{Pa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total force stays fixed. In the second case each square metre receives a smaller share of force, so pressure falls. The graph shows both values on the inverse curve. Tripling area divides pressure by three, and the difference gives the requested drop.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $50\,\mathrm N$ force pushes on a face of area $0.04\,\mathrm m^2$. Its normal component is four fifths of its magnitude. What average pressure is due to the force?

$$
p=\frac{(4/5)\cdot50\,\mathrm N}{0.04\,\mathrm m^2}=1000\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Pressure uses the part of the force that presses through the surface. Parallel parts contribute to tangential stress, so the numerator is forty newtons. This is lower than the twelve hundred fifty pascals that would result from treating all fifty newtons as normal.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A flat base has regions of area $0.03$ and $0.01\,\mathrm m^2$. Their respective uniform pressures are $2000$ and $6000\,\mathrm{Pa}$, acting the same way. What is the average pressure on the base?

$$
p_{\rm media}=\frac{2000\cdot0.03+6000\cdot0.01}{0.04}\,\mathrm{Pa}=3000\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In each region its own area links force and pressure. The forces are both sixty newtons and point the same way. Divide the total force of one hundred and twenty newtons by total area for the mean pressure. It is closer to two thousand pascals, the pressure on the larger region.

</div>
