## Exercise 1

A flat wall separates gases at $120$ and $106\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Its area is $0.015\,\mathrm m^2$. What is the magnitude of the net normal force?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,-1 0,1" vettori="-2,0,0,0,120 kPa;1.8,0,0,0,106 kPa;0,-0.45,1.1,-0.45,R" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1.8 ymax=1.7 titolo="Pressure on either wall face. Force R points rightward from the higher-pressure side"}

$$
F=(120-106)10^3\cdot0.015\,\mathrm N=210\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressures act on opposite faces and produce opposite forces. Subtract the lower pressure from the higher one before multiplying by the common area. The resultant points toward the one-hundred-and-six-kilopascal side as shown by the resultant arrow.

Check by calculating the forces separately. The first is $1800\,\mathrm N$ and the second is $1590\,\mathrm N$. Their difference is $210\,\mathrm N$. Expressing both pressures relative to the same reference would leave the numerical difference and the direction of the resultant unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A circular membrane has uniform pressure difference $500\,\mathrm{Pa}$ across it. Its initial area is $0.02\,\mathrm m^2$. If the radius doubles, what is the new resultant force?

$$
F=500\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot(4\cdot0.02\,\mathrm m^2)=40\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pressure difference is fixed. The new area is four times the old one because it depends on the square of the radius. The resultant force also grows by a factor of four. This distinguishes doubling a length from doubling an area.

At the original radius the result would be $500\cdot0.02\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N$. The new area is $0.08\,\mathrm m^2$ and the force rises to $40\,\mathrm N$. Its direction remains from the higher-pressure side towards the lower-pressure side because the pressure difference is maintained.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A plate of area $0.04\,\mathrm m^2$ separates gas at absolute pressure $98\,\mathrm{kPa}$ from atmosphere at $100\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Taking the atmosphere-to-gas direction as positive, what net normal component is produced?

$$
F_x=(100-98)10^3\,\mathrm{Pa}\cdot0.04\,\mathrm m^2=+80\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Atmospheric pressure is higher and its force points toward the gas. The gas pushes the other way. Subtracting the two products gives eighty newtons in the chosen positive sense. The gas's negative gauge pressure gives a net force from outside to inside.

Separating the contributions gives $+4000\,\mathrm N$ from the atmosphere and $-3920\,\mathrm N$ from the gas. Adding the forces directly checks the value obtained from the pressure difference. Reversing the reference direction would change the sign of the component while leaving the physical direction unchanged.

</div>
