## Exercise 1

Which description fits vectors $u=(7;0)\,\mathrm N$ and $v=(0;7)\,\mathrm N$?

- Perpendicular with equal magnitudes
- Equal
- Opposite
- Parallel and pointing the same way

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-1 ymax=8 vettori="0,0,7,0,u;0,0,0,7,v" nomi="x (N),y (N)" punti="7,0,pieno;0,7,pieno" titolo="Both arrows are 7 N long along perpendicular axes"}

Perpendicular with equal magnitudes.

<div class="spiegazione">

The first vector has only a nonzero horizontal component. The second has only a nonzero vertical component. Their lines are perpendicular. The nonzero components have equal sizes, which give their respective magnitudes. Equal magnitudes can therefore occur for differently oriented arrows. Both have magnitude $7\,\mathrm N$ and their directions differ.

The first arrow has magnitude $\sqrt{7^2+0^2}\,\mathrm N=7\,\mathrm N$ and the second has magnitude $\sqrt{0^2+7^2}\,\mathrm N=7\,\mathrm N$. Equal lengths do not make the vectors equal. Their tips lie on different axes, and the second direction is at right angles to the first.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In centimetres, $A=(1;2)$, $B=(7;6)$, $C=(2;5)$ and $D=(8;1)$. Compare the magnitudes of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{CD}$ and decide whether the arrows represent the same vector, opposite vectors or neither.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB} &= (7-1;6-2)\,\mathrm{cm}=(6;4)\,\mathrm{cm},\\[4pt]
\overrightarrow{CD} &= (8-2;1-5)\,\mathrm{cm}=(6;-4)\,\mathrm{cm},\\[4pt]
\lVert \overrightarrow{AB}\rVert^2
&=\lVert \overrightarrow{CD}\rVert^2=(36+16)\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare vectors, subtract the endpoints, since the tails may be in different places. Here both arrows point to the right. One rises and the other falls, so they are different. The opposite of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ would have horizontal component $-6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and therefore is not $\overrightarrow{CD}$. Despite the difference in orientation, both sums of squared components are $52\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, giving the same magnitude $2\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{cm}$. With a common tail, the arrows would be mirror images across the horizontal axis. Reflection leaves the length unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A map uses $3\,\mathrm m$ per horizontal unit and $2\,\mathrm m$ per vertical unit. An arrow advances by two units and drops by four. Compare the magnitude and direction of this displacement with those of the vector $(6;8)\,\mathrm m$. Take right and up as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec u &= (2\cdot3;-4\cdot2)\,\mathrm m=(6;-8)\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
\lVert \vec u\rVert &= \sqrt{6^2+(-8)^2}\,\mathrm m=10\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
\lVert \vec v\rVert &= \sqrt{6^2+8^2}\,\mathrm m=10\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert the units on each drawn axis separately. The horizontal count means six metres to the right and the vertical count means eight metres downwards. Calculate the magnitude in metres after conversion, leaving aside distances recorded in unequal drawing units. Here changing the sign of eight does not alter its square, so the magnitudes agree. However, the vertical projections point in opposite directions while the horizontal projections both point right. The two arrows are therefore different and neither is the opposite of the other. A single half-turn would reverse both components.

</div>
