## Exercise 1

Two vectors have zero sum and the first is $u=(8;10)\,\mathrm N$. What is the second?

- $(-8;-10)\,\mathrm N$
- $(8;10)\,\mathrm N$
- $(8;-10)\,\mathrm N$
- $(-8;10)\,\mathrm N$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-10 xmax=10 ymin=-12 ymax=12 vettori="0,0,8,10;0,0,-8,-10" etichette="4.6,5.1,u;-3.75,-6.15,v" nomi="x (N),y (N)" punti="8,10,pieno;-8,-10,pieno" titolo="The equilibrant reverses both the horizontal and vertical components"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= -u\\[6pt]
&= (-8;-10)\,\mathrm N
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A resultant is zero when both components vanish. To satisfy the horizontal condition, take the opposite of the first vector's horizontal component. For the vertical condition do the same with its other component. The new vector is the opposite, of equal length and pointing the other way along the same line. The second vector must be $v=-u=(-8;-10)\,\mathrm N$.

Choices with one sign changed cancel one component but double the other. For example, $(-8;10)\,\mathrm N$ would give a sum of $(0;20)\,\mathrm N$. The separate checks along both axes distinguish the equilibrant from the other choices. Equal magnitude, which all options preserve, is not enough to guarantee equilibrium.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ring is subject to two forces, $(8;-1)\,\mathrm N$ and $(-3;-5)\,\mathrm N$. Two additional cables can pull only leftwards and upwards, respectively. Find the magnitude of the force each cable must exert to keep the ring at rest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec R &= (8-3;-1-5)\,\mathrm N=(5;-6)\,\mathrm N,\\[4pt]
\vec F_{\mathrm{cables}} &= -\vec R=(-5;6)\,\mathrm N,\\[4pt]
T_1 &= 5\,\mathrm N,\qquad T_2=6\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two given forces produce a resultant pointing rightwards and downwards. The horizontal cable affects only the first component, so it must pull left with five newtons. The vertical cable cancels the downward contribution by pulling up with six newtons. The two tensions are positive magnitudes and match the directions in which the cables are actually arranged. Their combined effect is a single vector with components $(-5;6)\,\mathrm N$, even though two forces produce it. Adding along each axis gives $5-5=0$ and $-6+6=0$. A cable pointing the other way could not provide the required contribution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A resultant with components $(7;-4)\,\mathrm N$ acts on a point. A device can add a leftward horizontal force with magnitude at most $5\,\mathrm N$. Which choice minimises the new resultant's magnitude? Can equilibrium be reached under these conditions?

$$
\begin{aligned}
0\leq T&\leq5\,\mathrm N,\\[4pt]
\vec R_{\mathrm{new}}&=(7\,\mathrm N-T;-4\,\mathrm N),\\[4pt]
\lVert \vec R_{\mathrm{new}}\rVert_{\min}
&=\sqrt{(7-5)^2+(-4)^2}\,\mathrm N
=2\sqrt{5}\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The device subtracts its force magnitude from the horizontal component and leaves the vertical component unchanged. Since the limit is below seven newtons, the horizontal component stays positive. Its square decreases as the device pulls harder to the left. The minimum is therefore at the maximum pull of five newtons, leaving two newtons to the right and four downwards. Equilibrium requires both components to vanish, while the second one stays fixed. Even if the pull could be increased to seven newtons, the vertical contribution would remain and the point would not be in equilibrium.

</div>
