## Exercise 1

Two vectors have zero sum and the first is $u=(8;10)\,\mathrm N$. What is the second?

- $(-8;-10)\,\mathrm N$
- $(8;10)\,\mathrm N$
- $(8;-10)\,\mathrm N$
- $(-8;10)\,\mathrm N$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-10 xmax=10 ymin=-12 ymax=12 vettori="0,0,8,10,u;0,0,-8,-10,v" nomi="x (N),y (N)" punti="8,10,pieno;-8,-10,pieno" titolo="The equilibrant reverses both the horizontal and vertical components"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= -u\\[6pt]
&= (-8;-10)\,\mathrm N
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A resultant is zero when both components vanish. To satisfy the horizontal condition, take the opposite of the first vector's horizontal component. For the vertical condition do the same with its other component. The new vector is the opposite, of equal length and pointing the other way along the same line. The second vector must be $v=-u=(-8;-10)\,\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two vectors have horizontal components $u_x=8\,\mathrm N$ and $v_x=10\,\mathrm N$. What is the horizontal component of their sum?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(u+v)_x &= u_x+v_x\\[6pt]
&= 8+10\\[6pt]
&= 18\,\mathrm N
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Follow only the motion along the horizontal axis. The first vector contributes its horizontal component, and the second is added to it. Both are positive, so the sum lies in the positive sense. The vertical components would be needed for the other component or the whole vector's magnitude. Thus $(u+v)_x=u_x+v_x=8+10=18\,\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given $u_x=8\,\mathrm m$ and $v_x=-10\,\mathrm m$, what is the $x$ component of $u-v$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(u-v)_x &= 8-(-10)\\[6pt]
&= 18\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting a vector means adding its opposite. On the horizontal axis alone, reverse the sign of the second vector's component and add it to the first. The originally negative component becomes positive. Adding the second vector's component to the result checks that the first component is recovered. Therefore $(u-v)_x=8-(-10)=18\,\mathrm m$.

</div>
