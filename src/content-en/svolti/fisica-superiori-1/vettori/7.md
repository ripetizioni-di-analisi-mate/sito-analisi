## Exercise 1

Two vectors have magnitudes $35$ and $14\,\mathrm N$. What is the smallest possible magnitude of their sum as their relative directions vary?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-2 xmax=41 ymin=-1 ymax=2.6 vettori="0,1.2,35,1.2,u;35,1.2,21,1.2,v;0,0.15,21,0.15,R" etichette="15,1.8,35 N;29,2.1,14 N;10,0.62,21 N" titolo="Opposite senses give the minimum magnitude, equal to the difference"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{min} &= |35-14|\\[6pt]
&= 21\,\mathrm N
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the tip-to-tail construction one arrow cancels the other as much as possible when both are on the same line pointing opposite ways. The residual magnitude is the positive difference of the two lengths. Zero would also require equal magnitudes. A nonaligned arrangement leaves a longer resultant. The lower bound is $R_{min}=|35-14|=21\,\mathrm N$.

To check the result, consider moving the tip of the second vector without changing its length. When opposite, it subtracts all fourteen newtons along the first vector. In any other direction the opposing component is smaller or a perpendicular component appears as well. The magnitude cannot fall below twenty-one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two vectors have magnitudes $7$ and $9\,\mathrm N$. What is the greatest possible magnitude of their sum?

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{max} &= 7+9\\[6pt]
&= 16\,\mathrm N
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The resultant is the direct segment from start to finish along the two-arrow path. It reaches the sum of their lengths only if the whole path advances on one line in the same sense. Then the contributions add without any components cancelling. The upper bound is $R_{max}=7+9=16\,\mathrm N$.

If the seven- and nine-newton forces were perpendicular, the magnitude would be $\sqrt{7^2+9^2}\,\mathrm N=\sqrt{130}\,\mathrm N$, which is less than sixteen. If they pointed in opposite directions, only two newtons would remain. These comparisons show why adding the magnitudes works only in the configuration required for the maximum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two arrows have magnitudes $a\geq b$ and can rotate freely. The largest possible magnitude of their sum is $20\,\mathrm N$ and the smallest is $4\,\mathrm N$. Find the two magnitudes and decide whether their sum can have magnitude $3\,\mathrm N$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a+b &= 20\,\mathrm N,\qquad a-b=4\,\mathrm N,\\[4pt]
2a &= 24\,\mathrm N,\qquad a=12\,\mathrm N,\\[4pt]
b &= 20\,\mathrm N-12\,\mathrm N=8\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The maximum occurs when both vectors point the same way. The minimum occurs when the shorter arrow points directly opposite the longer one. These two configurations give two equations for two unknowns. Adding the equations eliminates the shorter magnitude and leaves twice the larger one. Check by calculating $12+8=20$ and $12-8=4$ separately. A resultant of three newtons is impossible because it would require cancelling more than eight newtons of the twelve-newton arrow. Turning the smaller arrow away from the line would reduce its component opposing the larger vector.

</div>
