## Exercise 1

A parallel-plate capacitor has plates of area $A = 0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at a distance $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. Between the plates, parallel to them, there is a dielectric slab of area $A$, thickness $s = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and constant $\varepsilon_r = 4.0$; the rest of the space is empty. The capacitor is connected to a $\Delta V = 100\,\mathrm{V}$ source. Compute the capacitance, the charge, the field in vacuum and in the slab and the potential difference across each region.

::grafico{unicolore=1 assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="2,0 4,0 4,3 2,3" linee="1,0 1,3;5,0 5,3" vettori="1.08,0.3,1.92,0.3;1.08,0.9,1.92,0.9;1.08,1.5,1.92,1.5;1.08,2.1,1.92,2.1;2.55,1.5,3.45,1.5,E;4.08,0.3,4.92,0.3;4.08,0.9,4.92,0.9;4.08,1.5,4.92,1.5;4.08,2.1,4.92,2.1" segmenti="2,-0.35,4,-0.35;1,3.35,5,3.35" etichette="1.3,2.6,E₀;4.3,2.6,E₀;2.85,-0.8,s;2.85,3.55,d;2.55,0.3,εᵣ = 4;0.65,1.4,+;5.15,1.4,−" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3.9 titolo="Slab of thickness $s = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ between plates $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ apart: in vacuum $E_0 = 8.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m}$, in the slab $E = 2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V/m}$; the field lines are four times denser in vacuum than in the slab"}

$$
C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - s + \dfrac{s}{\varepsilon_r}} , \qquad Q = C\,\Delta V , \qquad E_0 = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A} , \qquad E = \frac{E_0}{\varepsilon_r}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.040}{(1.0 + 0.25) \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 2.8 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F} = 283\,\mathrm{pF} \\[6pt]
Q &= 2.83 \cdot 10^{-10} \cdot 100\,\mathrm{C} \approx 28\,\mathrm{nC} \\[6pt]
E_0 &= \frac{2.832 \cdot 10^{-8}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.040}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m} , \qquad E = \frac{8.0 \cdot 10^{4}}{4.0}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_{\text{vac}} &= E_0\,(d - s) = 80\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad \Delta V_{\text{slab}} = E\,s = 20\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slab is parallel to the plates and has the same area, therefore the theorem on the **dielectric slab parallel to the plates** applies. The capacitance, $283\,\mathrm{pF}$, lies between that of the empty capacitor, $177\,\mathrm{pF}$, and that of the completely filled capacitor, $708\,\mathrm{pF}$; the position of the slab between the plates has no effect.

In the empty region the field is the one produced by the free charge, $\dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$, while in the slab it is $\varepsilon_r$ times smaller. The two potential differences, $80\,\mathrm{V}$ across the millimeter of vacuum and $20\,\mathrm{V}$ across the millimeter of dielectric, add up to the $100\,\mathrm{V}$ of the source, as a check. The capacitor is equivalent to the **series** of an empty capacitor of thickness $d - s$ and a filled one of thickness $s$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A parallel-plate capacitor has plates of area $A = 0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at a distance $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. Half of the area is occupied, through the whole thickness, by a dielectric with $\varepsilon_r = 3.0$, and the other half is empty. The capacitor is connected to a $\Delta V = 100\,\mathrm{V}$ source. Compute the capacitance, the field and the free charge density in the two halves, and the total charge.

$$
C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \left( A_2 + \varepsilon_r A_1 \right)}{d} , \qquad A_1 = A_2 = \frac{A}{2} , \qquad E = \frac{\Delta V}{d} , \qquad \sigma_{\text{vac}} = \varepsilon_0 E , \qquad \sigma_{\text{diel}} = \varepsilon_r \varepsilon_0 E
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot (0.020 + 3.0 \cdot 0.020)}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 3.5 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F} = 354\,\mathrm{pF} \\[6pt]
E &= \frac{100}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
\sigma_{\text{vac}} &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} , \qquad \sigma_{\text{diel}} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \\[6pt]
Q &= C\,\Delta V \approx 3.5 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C} = 35\,\mathrm{nC}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two halves share the plates, therefore they have the same potential difference, and the capacitor is equivalent to the **parallel combination** of two capacitors of area $\dfrac{A}{2}$, one empty and one filled; the theorem on the **dielectric occupying part of the area** applies. The capacitance is $\dfrac{1 + \varepsilon_r}{2} = 2$ times that of the empty capacitor, $177\,\mathrm{pF}$.

In both halves the field is uniform and equals $\dfrac{\Delta V}{d}$. In the half with the dielectric, for the same field, the free charge on the plates must be $\varepsilon_r$ times larger, because part of its field is compensated by the polarization charges. The charges of the two halves are $8.9\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $27\,\mathrm{nC}$, and their sum is $35\,\mathrm{nC}$, as a check. The free charge is therefore distributed nonuniformly on the plates, which nevertheless remain equipotential.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Between the plates of a parallel-plate capacitor of area $A = 0.030\,\mathrm{m^2}$, at a distance $d = 3.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, an uncharged copper slab is inserted, parallel to the plates, of area $A$ and thickness $s = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, at $0.80\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the positive plate. Compute the capacitance before and after the insertion. With the capacitor connected to $\Delta V = 60\,\mathrm{V}$, compute the field in the empty regions and the induced charge density on the faces of the slab.

$$
C_0 = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} , \qquad
\frac{1}{C} = \frac{x_1}{\varepsilon_0 A} + \frac{x_2}{\varepsilon_0 A} = \frac{d - s}{\varepsilon_0 A} , \qquad x_1 + x_2 = d - s
$$
$$
E = \frac{\Delta V}{d - s} , \qquad \sigma_{\text{ind}} = \varepsilon_0 E
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_0 &= \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.030}{3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} = 88.5\,\mathrm{pF} \\[6pt]
C &= \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.030}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 133\,\mathrm{pF} \\[6pt]
E &= \frac{60}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
\sigma_{\text{ind}} &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \approx 2.7 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slab is a conductor in equilibrium, therefore inside it the field is zero and charges opposite to those of the facing plates are induced on its faces, that is, $-Q$ on the face toward the positive plate and $+Q$ on the opposite face. The system is hence equivalent to two vacuum capacitors in **series**, of thicknesses $x_1 = 0.80\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $x_2 = 1.2\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the capacitance depends only on the sum $x_1 + x_2 = d - s$, as in the remark on the **conducting slab between the plates**. The datum on the position of the slab is not needed; the capacitance goes from $88.5\,\mathrm{pF}$ to $133\,\mathrm{pF}$.

With the source connected, the voltage of $60\,\mathrm{V}$ drops only across the empty regions, of total thickness $2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, because the field in the slab is zero. The field is the same in the two regions, since the charges have the same density in absolute value, and the density induced on the slab is equal, in absolute value, to that on the plates, $\varepsilon_0 E$, by Coulomb's theorem.

</div>
