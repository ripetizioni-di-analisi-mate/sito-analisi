## Exercise 1

The flash capacitor of a camera has capacitance $C = 150\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and is charged to $\Delta V = 300\,\mathrm{V}$. Compute the charge and the stored energy, and the average power delivered if the capacitor discharges completely in $\Delta t = 1.0\,\mathrm{ms}$.

$$
Q = C\,\Delta V , \qquad U = \frac{1}{2} C\,\Delta V^2 , \qquad P_m = \frac{U}{\Delta t}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= 150 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 300\,\mathrm{C} = 4.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
U &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 150 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 300^2\,\mathrm{J} = 6.75\,\mathrm{J} \approx 6.8\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
P_m &= \frac{6.75}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{W} \approx 6.8\,\mathrm{kW}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The capacitance and the voltage are given, therefore for the **energy of a charged capacitor** the form $\dfrac{1}{2} C\,\Delta V^2$ is the convenient one. During the discharge all the stored energy is delivered to the flash circuit. The average power is the energy divided by the duration of the discharge.

The energy is modest, a few joules, but it is released in a thousandth of a second, and the average power exceeds $6\,\mathrm{kW}$. For this reason a capacitor is used in the flash: it accumulates energy slowly from a low-power battery and returns it in a very short time.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has plates of area $A = 0.050\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at a distance $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, is charged to $\Delta V = 200\,\mathrm{V}$ and then disconnected from the source. Compute the stored energy and the energy density of the field, verifying that the product of the density and the volume gives the energy. Then compute the force between the plates and the work needed to bring them to the distance $2d$.

$$
C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} , \qquad U = \frac{1}{2} C\,\Delta V^2 , \qquad E = \frac{\Delta V}{d} , \qquad u = \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_0 E^2
$$
$$
F = \frac{Q^2}{2\varepsilon_0 A} = u\,A , \qquad W = F\,d
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.050}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F} \\[6pt]
U &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.425 \cdot 10^{-10} \cdot 200^2\,\mathrm{J} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
E &= \frac{200}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{V/m} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
u &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \left( 2.0 \cdot 10^{5} \right)^2\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \approx 0.18\,\mathrm{J/m^3} \\[6pt]
u\,A\,d &= 0.177 \cdot 0.050 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
F &= 0.177 \cdot 0.050\,\mathrm{N} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
W &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The capacitance follows from the formula of the parallel-plate capacitor, and the energy from the form $\dfrac{1}{2} C\,\Delta V^2$. The field between the plates is uniform and is zero outside, therefore the energy is distributed with **uniform density** $u$ in the volume $A\,d$, and the product $u\,A\,d$ gives back $U$, as predicted by the theorem on the energy density.

The capacitor is isolated, hence during the displacement the charge $Q = C\,\Delta V$ stays constant, and with it the field $E = \dfrac{Q}{\varepsilon_0 A}$ and the force $\dfrac{Q^2}{2\varepsilon_0 A}$. The force is therefore constant and the work to move the plate away by $d$ is $F\,d$. The work equals the initial energy, and indeed at constant charge the energy $\dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$ doubles when $C$ is halved; the external work remains stored in the field, which now occupies the volume $2A\,d$ with the same density $u$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A capacitor of capacitance $C_1 = 4.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is charged to $\Delta V_0 = 100\,\mathrm{V}$, disconnected from the source and connected to an uncharged capacitor of capacitance $C_2 = 6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$, joining the plates in pairs. Compute the final potential difference, the final charge of each capacitor and the energy dissipated in the connection.

$$
Q_0 = C_1\,\Delta V_0 , \qquad \Delta V = \frac{Q_0}{C_1 + C_2} , \qquad U_i = \frac{1}{2} C_1\,\Delta V_0^2 , \qquad U_f = \frac{1}{2} (C_1 + C_2)\,\Delta V^2
$$
$$
U_i - U_f = \frac{C_2}{C_1 + C_2}\,U_i
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_0 &= 4.0 \cdot 100\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 400\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
\Delta V &= \frac{400}{10}\,\mathrm{V} = 40\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad Q_1 = 4.0 \cdot 40\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 160\,\mu\mathrm{C} , \qquad Q_2 = 6.0 \cdot 40\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 240\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
U_i &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 100^2\,\mathrm{J} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
U_f &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 40^2\,\mathrm{J} = 8.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
U_i - U_f &= 1.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After the connection the joined plates form two conductors, therefore the two capacitors have the same potential difference and are in parallel. The system is isolated, hence by **conservation of charge** the charge $Q_0$ is shared between the two capacitors, in proportion to the capacitances, and the final voltage is $\dfrac{Q_0}{C_1 + C_2}$.

The final energy is smaller than the initial one. Substituting $\Delta V$ gives $U_f = \dfrac{C_1}{C_1 + C_2}\,U_i$, therefore $60\,\%$ of the energy is lost, the fraction $\dfrac{C_2}{C_1 + C_2}$. The **dissipated** energy does not depend on the resistance of the connecting wires. It is converted into heat by the Joule effect in the wires and part of it is radiated during the rapid transfer of charge; for the Joule effect see the topic on electric current and direct-current circuits.

</div>
