## Exercise 1

A parallel-plate capacitor has plates of area $A = 0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at a distance $d = 0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$, and is filled by a dielectric with $\varepsilon_r = 4.0$ and dielectric strength $E_{\max} = 1.5 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{V/m}$. Compute the capacitance, the maximum potential difference that can be applied and the corresponding charge. Compare with the same capacitor in air, with dielectric strength $3.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{V/m}$.

$$
C = \varepsilon_r\,\frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} , \qquad \Delta V_{\max} = E_{\max}\,d , \qquad Q_{\max} = C\,\Delta V_{\max}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_0 &= \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.010}{0.50 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} = 177\,\mathrm{pF} , \qquad C = 4.0 \cdot 177\,\mathrm{pF} \approx 710\,\mathrm{pF} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_{\max} &= 1.5 \cdot 10^{7} \cdot 0.50 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} = 7.5\,\mathrm{kV} \\[6pt]
Q_{\max} &= 7.08 \cdot 10^{-10} \cdot 7.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 5.3\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
\text{air:} \quad \Delta V_{\max} &= 3.0 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot 0.50 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} = 1.5\,\mathrm{kV} , \qquad Q_{\max} = 177 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 1.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 0.27\,\mu\mathrm{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The dielectric fills all the space between the plates, therefore by the law of the **capacitor with a dielectric** the capacitance is $\varepsilon_r$ times that in vacuum. The field between the plates is uniform and equals $\dfrac{\Delta V}{d}$, hence the condition $E \leq E_{\max}$ is equivalent to $\Delta V \leq E_{\max}\,d$, by the definition of **dielectric strength**.

Compared with air, the dielectric quadruples the capacitance and quintuples the maximum voltage, therefore the maximum charge becomes $4 \cdot 5 = 20$ times larger, that is, $5.3\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ against $0.27\,\mu\mathrm{C}$. For this reason real capacitors contain a solid dielectric.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A capacitor of capacitance $C_0 = 50\,\mathrm{pF}$ in vacuum is charged to $\Delta V_0 = 200\,\mathrm{V}$ and disconnected from the source. Then the space between the plates is filled with a dielectric of constant $\varepsilon_r = 2.5$. Compute the charge, the final potential difference, the energy before and after the insertion and the work done by the external agent that slowly inserts the dielectric.

$$
Q = C_0\,\Delta V_0 , \qquad \Delta V = \frac{Q}{\varepsilon_r C_0} = \frac{\Delta V_0}{\varepsilon_r} , \qquad U_0 = \frac{Q^2}{2C_0} , \qquad U = \frac{Q^2}{2\varepsilon_r C_0} = \frac{U_0}{\varepsilon_r}
$$
$$
W_{\text{ext}} = U - U_0 = -\left( 1 - \frac{1}{\varepsilon_r} \right) U_0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= 50 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 200\,\mathrm{C} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
\Delta V &= \frac{200}{2.5}\,\mathrm{V} = 80\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
U_0 &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 50 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 200^2\,\mathrm{J} = 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{J} , \qquad U = \frac{1.0}{2.5}\,\mu\mathrm{J} = 0.40\,\mu\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
W_{\text{ext}} &= (0.40 - 1.0)\,\mu\mathrm{J} = -0.60\,\mu\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The capacitor is isolated, therefore the charge remains $1.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C}$. The capacitance becomes $\varepsilon_r C_0$, hence the potential difference and the field become $\varepsilon_r$ times smaller. Since the charge is constant, for the energy the convenient form is $\dfrac{Q^2}{2C}$, which shows that the energy **decreases** by a factor $\varepsilon_r$.

The external agent moves the dielectric slowly, therefore the kinetic energy of the dielectric remains negligible and the external work equals the change in energy of the capacitor. The work is negative, because the field pulls the dielectric inward and the agent must hold it back; the field does the positive work $0.60\,\mu\mathrm{J}$ on the dielectric.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A capacitor of capacitance $C_0 = 50\,\mathrm{pF}$ in vacuum remains connected to the $\Delta V = 200\,\mathrm{V}$ source while it is filled with the dielectric of constant $\varepsilon_r = 2.5$. Compute the final charge and energy, the work done by the source and the work done by the electric forces on the dielectric.

$$
Q_0 = C_0\,\Delta V , \qquad Q = \varepsilon_r C_0\,\Delta V , \qquad U_0 = \frac{1}{2} C_0\,\Delta V^2 , \qquad U = \frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_r C_0\,\Delta V^2
$$
$$
W_{\text{gen}} = \Delta V\,(Q - Q_0) , \qquad W_{\text{el}} = W_{\text{gen}} - (U - U_0)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= 2.5 \cdot 50 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 200\,\mathrm{C} = 2.5 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
U &= 2.5 \cdot 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{J} = 2.5\,\mu\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
W_{\text{gen}} &= 200 \cdot (2.5 - 1.0) \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{J} = 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
W_{\text{el}} &= 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{J} - (2.5 - 1.0)\,\mu\mathrm{J} = 1.5\,\mu\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The source maintains the potential difference, therefore the field $\dfrac{\Delta V}{d}$ does not change, and the charge becomes $\varepsilon_r$ times larger. Since the voltage is constant, for the energy the form $\dfrac{1}{2} C\,\Delta V^2$ is used, which **increases** by a factor $\varepsilon_r$, from $1.0\,\mu\mathrm{J}$ to $2.5\,\mu\mathrm{J}$.

The source moves the charge $Q - Q_0 = 1.5 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C}$ through the constant potential difference $\Delta V$, and does the work $3.0\,\mu\mathrm{J}$. By conservation of energy this work equals the increase in the energy of the capacitor plus the work done by the electric forces on the dielectric, which enters pulled by the field; the two parts are equal, $1.5\,\mu\mathrm{J}$ each, as predicted by the remark on inserting the dielectric at constant voltage.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A parallel-plate capacitor with plates of area $A = 0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at a distance $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries the charge $Q = 30\,\mathrm{nC}$ and is filled by a dielectric with $\varepsilon_r = 3.0$. Compute the field that would exist in vacuum with the same charge, the field in the dielectric, the polarization density and charge on the faces of the dielectric, the potential difference and the capacitance.

$$
\sigma = \frac{Q}{A} , \qquad E_0 = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0} , \qquad E = \frac{E_0}{\varepsilon_r} , \qquad \sigma_p = \sigma \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\varepsilon_r} \right) , \qquad \Delta V = E\,d , \qquad C = \frac{Q}{\Delta V}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma &= \frac{30 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.020}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} = 1.5 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \\[6pt]
E_0 &= \frac{1.5 \cdot 10^{-6}}{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^{5}\,\mathrm{V/m} , \qquad E = \frac{1.69 \cdot 10^{5}}{3.0}\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 5.6 \cdot 10^{4}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
\sigma_p &= 1.5 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \frac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C/m^2} , \qquad Q_p = \sigma_p\,A = 20\,\mathrm{nC} \\[6pt]
\Delta V &= 5.65 \cdot 10^{4} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 56\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad C = \frac{30 \cdot 10^{-9}}{56.5}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 5.3 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The free charge on the plates is given, therefore the density $\sigma$ and the field in vacuum follow from Coulomb's theorem. In the dielectric the field is $\varepsilon_r$ times smaller, by the theorem on **field and polarization charges**. On the face of the dielectric facing the positive plate there is the polarization charge $-20\,\mathrm{nC}$ and on the opposite one $+20\,\mathrm{nC}$; the net charge facing each plate is hence $30 - 20 = 10\,\mathrm{nC}$, one third of the free charge, and the field is one third of $E_0$.

The potential difference is the product of the uniform field and the distance. As a check, the capacitance coincides with $\varepsilon_r\,\dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} = 3.0 \cdot \dfrac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.020}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 5.3 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F}$.

</div>
