## Exercise 1

Two capacitors of capacitances $C_1 = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and $C_2 = 3.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$, initially uncharged, are connected to a $\Delta V = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ source, first in parallel and then, after being discharged, in series. In both cases compute the equivalent capacitance, the charge and the potential difference of each capacitor.

$$
\text{parallel:} \quad C_{\text{eq}} = C_1 + C_2 , \qquad \Delta V_1 = \Delta V_2 = \Delta V , \qquad Q_i = C_i\,\Delta V
$$
$$
\text{series:} \quad C_{\text{eq}} = \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2} , \qquad Q_1 = Q_2 = C_{\text{eq}}\,\Delta V , \qquad \Delta V_i = \frac{Q_1}{C_i}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{parallel:} \quad C_{\text{eq}} &= 5.0\,\mu\mathrm{F} , \qquad Q_1 = 2.0 \cdot 12\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 24\,\mu\mathrm{C} , \qquad Q_2 = 3.0 \cdot 12\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 36\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
\text{series:} \quad C_{\text{eq}} &= \frac{2.0 \cdot 3.0}{5.0}\,\mu\mathrm{F} = 1.2\,\mu\mathrm{F} , \qquad Q_1 = Q_2 = 1.2 \cdot 12\,\mu\mathrm{C} \approx 14\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_1 &= \frac{14.4}{2.0}\,\mathrm{V} = 7.2\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad \Delta V_2 = \frac{14.4}{3.0}\,\mathrm{V} = 4.8\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In parallel the plates connected to the same wire are at the same potential, therefore both capacitors have the potential difference of the source, and by the theorem on **capacitors in parallel** the capacitances add. The charge of each is proportional to its capacitance, and the total charge $60\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ equals $C_{\text{eq}}\,\Delta V$.

In series the inner plates form an isolated neutral conductor, hence the two capacitors have the same charge, and by the theorem on **capacitors in series** the reciprocals of the capacitances add. The equivalent capacitance, $1.2\,\mu\mathrm{F}$, is smaller than both. The capacitor with the smaller capacitance has the larger voltage, and the two voltages add up to $12\,\mathrm{V}$, as required by the sum of the potential differences along the series.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the network in the figure $C_1 = 4.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is connected in series with the parallel combination of $C_2 = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and $C_3 = 6.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$, and the network is connected to a $\Delta V = 24\,\mathrm{V}$ source. The capacitors are initially uncharged. Compute the equivalent capacitance, the charge and the potential difference of each capacitor.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,1.35;-0.2,1.35 0.2,1.35;-0.4,1.65 0.4,1.65;0,1.65 0,3 1.85,3;1.85,2.6 1.85,3.4;2.15,2.6 2.15,3.4;2.15,3 6,3 6,1.65;5.6,1.65 6.4,1.65;5.6,1.35 6.4,1.35;6,1.35 6,0 0,0;3.5,3 3.5,1.65;3.1,1.65 3.9,1.65;3.1,1.35 3.9,1.35;3.5,1.35 3.5,0" punti="3.5,3,pieno;3.5,0,pieno" etichette="0.5,1.75,+;0.35,1.05,−;-1.05,1.4,ΔV;1.75,3.65,C₁;4.05,1.4,C₂;6.55,1.4,C₃" xmin=-1.4 xmax=7.4 ymin=-0.5 ymax=4.1 titolo="Capacitor network: $C_1$ is in series with the parallel combination of $C_2$ and $C_3$"}

$$
C_{23} = C_2 + C_3 , \qquad C_{\text{eq}} = \frac{C_1 C_{23}}{C_1 + C_{23}} , \qquad Q_1 = C_{\text{eq}}\,\Delta V
$$
$$
\Delta V_1 = \frac{Q_1}{C_1} , \qquad \Delta V_{23} = \frac{Q_1}{C_{23}} , \qquad Q_2 = C_2\,\Delta V_{23} , \qquad Q_3 = C_3\,\Delta V_{23}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{23} &= (2.0 + 6.0)\,\mu\mathrm{F} = 8.0\,\mu\mathrm{F} \\[6pt]
C_{\text{eq}} &= \frac{4.0 \cdot 8.0}{12}\,\mu\mathrm{F} \approx 2.7\,\mu\mathrm{F} \\[6pt]
Q_1 &= \frac{8}{3} \cdot 24\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 64\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_1 &= \frac{64}{4.0}\,\mathrm{V} = 16\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad \Delta V_{23} = \frac{64}{8.0}\,\mathrm{V} = 8.0\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
Q_2 &= 2.0 \cdot 8.0\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 16\,\mu\mathrm{C} , \qquad Q_3 = 6.0 \cdot 8.0\,\mu\mathrm{C} = 48\,\mu\mathrm{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

$C_2$ and $C_3$ have their plates connected to the same two nodes, therefore they are in parallel and are replaced by $C_{23} = 8.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$. This equivalent capacitor is in series with $C_1$, because between the two there is a single wire, which does not lead to other components; the capacitance of the network is hence $\dfrac{8}{3}\,\mu\mathrm{F}$.

The network is then traversed backward. The charge delivered by the source is that of the equivalent capacitor, and because of the series connection it is also the charge of $C_1$ and of the parallel combination. From the charge the voltages are obtained, which add up to $24\,\mathrm{V}$. Finally $C_2$ and $C_3$ have the same voltage $8.0\,\mathrm{V}$, and their charges, $16\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ and $48\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, add up again to $64\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, as a check.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two capacitors of capacitances $C_1 = 1.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and $C_2 = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ can each withstand a maximum potential difference of $100\,\mathrm{V}$. Compute the maximum potential difference that can be applied to the two capacitors connected in series, and the one that can be applied to their parallel connection.

$$
Q_1 = Q_2 = Q , \qquad \Delta V_1 = \frac{Q}{C_1} , \qquad \Delta V_2 = \frac{Q}{C_2} = \frac{C_1}{C_2}\,\Delta V_1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V_1 &= 100\,\mathrm{V} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q = C_1 \cdot 100\,\mathrm{V} = 100\,\mu\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_2 &= \frac{100}{2.0}\,\mathrm{V} = 50\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_{\max}^{\text{series}} &= \Delta V_1 + \Delta V_2 = 150\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad \Delta V_{\max}^{\text{parallel}} = 100\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In series the two capacitors have the same charge, therefore the larger voltage belongs to the capacitor with the smaller capacitance, $C_1$, and at every instant $\Delta V_1 = 2\,\Delta V_2$. As the applied voltage increases, the first capacitor to reach the limit is hence $C_1$, and this happens with the charge $Q = 100\,\mu\mathrm{C}$, when $C_2$ is at $50\,\mathrm{V}$. The maximum voltage that can be applied to the series is the sum, $150\,\mathrm{V}$; as a check, $C_{\text{eq}} = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ and $C_{\text{eq}} \cdot 150\,\mathrm{V} = 100\,\mu\mathrm{C}$.

The result is smaller than $200\,\mathrm{V}$, the sum of the maximum voltages, because the voltages are shared unequally. In parallel both capacitors have the applied voltage, therefore the limit is $100\,\mathrm{V}$.

</div>
