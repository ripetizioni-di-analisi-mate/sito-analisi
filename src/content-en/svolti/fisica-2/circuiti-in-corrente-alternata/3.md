## Exercise 1

A real coil, connected to a direct voltage of $12\,\mathrm{V}$, carries in steady state the current $0.50\,\mathrm{A}$. Connected to an alternating voltage of rms value $12\,\mathrm{V}$ and frequency $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$, it carries the rms current $0.30\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the resistance and the inductance of the coil and the phase shift between voltage and current in AC.

$$
R = \frac{V}{I} ,
\qquad
Z = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{I_{\text{rms}}} = \sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
X_L = \sqrt{Z^2 - R^2} ,
\qquad
L = \frac{X_L}{2\pi f} ,
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{X_L}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \frac{12}{0.50}\,\Omega = 24\,\Omega \\[6pt]
Z &= \frac{12}{0.30}\,\Omega = 40\,\Omega \\[6pt]
X_L &= \sqrt{40^2 - 24^2}\,\Omega = 32\,\Omega \\[6pt]
L &= \frac{32}{2\pi \cdot 50}\,\mathrm{H} \approx 0.10\,\mathrm{H} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= \arctan\frac{32}{24} \approx 53^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A real coil is modeled as a resistor $R$, the resistance of the wire, in series with an ideal inductor $L$. In direct current in steady state the current is constant, so the voltage across the inductor $L\,\dfrac{di}{dt}$ is zero and the coil reduces to the resistor; by Ohm's law we obtain $R$.

In AC the theorem on the **impedance of the series RLC circuit** applies with $X_C = 0$, that is, to an $RL$ circuit. The ratio of the rms values gives the impedance, and the impedance is the hypotenuse of the right triangle with legs $R$ and $X_L$; therefore $X_L = \sqrt{Z^2 - R^2}$, and dividing by the angular frequency we obtain $L$. The phase shift is positive, so the current **lags** the voltage by about $53^\circ$, as in every inductive circuit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A resistor of resistance $R = 100\,\Omega$ and a capacitor of capacitance $C = 20\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ are connected in series to the grid, with $V_{\text{rms}} = 230\,\mathrm{V}$ and $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Compute the impedance, the rms current, the phase shift and the rms voltages across the two components.

$$
X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi f C} ,
\qquad
Z = \sqrt{R^2 + X_C^2} ,
\qquad
I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{Z} ,
\qquad
\tan\varphi = -\frac{X_C}{R} ,
\qquad
V_R = R I_{\text{rms}} , \quad V_C = X_C I_{\text{rms}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_C &= \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 20 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\Omega \approx 159\,\Omega \\[6pt]
Z &= \sqrt{100^2 + 159^2}\,\Omega \approx 188\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{rms}} &= \frac{230}{188}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 1.22\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= -\arctan\frac{159}{100} \approx -58^\circ \\[6pt]
V_R &= 100 \cdot 1.22\,\mathrm{V} \approx 122\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad V_C = 159 \cdot 1.22\,\mathrm{V} \approx 195\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circuit is a series $RLC$ circuit without an inductor, so in the theorem on the **impedance of the series RLC circuit** we set $X_L = 0$. The phase shift $\varphi$ is negative, therefore the current **leads** the source voltage by about $58^\circ$ and the circuit is capacitive.

The sum $V_R + V_C \approx 317\,\mathrm{V}$ exceeds the source voltage, and there is no contradiction. The two voltages are out of phase by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, because the one across the resistor is in phase with the current and the one across the capacitor lags by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$; their phasors are perpendicular and add as vectors. Indeed $\sqrt{122^2 + 195^2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 230\,\mathrm{V}$, which is the rms voltage of the source.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A series $RLC$ circuit with $R = 30\,\Omega$, $L = 0.25\,\mathrm{H}$ and $C = 60\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is supplied by the voltage $v(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$, with $V_{\text{rms}} = 120\,\mathrm{V}$ and $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Compute the impedance, the rms current, the phase shift, the rms voltages across the three components and the expression of $i(t)$.

$$
Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} ,
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{X_L - X_C}{R} ,
\qquad
i(t) = \sqrt{2}\,\frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{Z} \sin(\omega t - \varphi)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_L &= 2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 0.25\,\Omega \approx 78.5\,\Omega \\[6pt]
X_C &= \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 50 \cdot 60 \cdot 10^{-6}}\,\Omega \approx 53.1\,\Omega \\[6pt]
Z &= \sqrt{30^2 + 25.5^2}\,\Omega \approx 39.4\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{rms}} &= \frac{120}{39.4}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 3.05\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= \arctan\frac{25.5}{30} \approx 0.70\,\mathrm{rad} \approx 40^\circ \\[6pt]
V_R &\approx 91\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad V_L \approx 239\,\mathrm{V} , \qquad V_C \approx 162\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i(t) &\approx 4.3 \sin(314\,t - 0.70)\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We first compute the reactances at the frequency of the source and then apply the theorem on the **impedance of the series RLC circuit**. The inductive reactance exceeds the capacitive one by $25.5\,\Omega$, so the phase shift is positive and the current lags the voltage by about $40^\circ$; the circuit is inductive. The current amplitude is $\sqrt{2} \cdot 3.05\,\mathrm{A} \approx 4.3\,\mathrm{A}$.

The rms voltages across the components are the products of the rms current with $R$, $X_L$ and $X_C$. The voltage across the inductor, about $239\,\mathrm{V}$, is twice that of the source. In the phasor diagram the voltages across the inductor and the capacitor are opposite and their sum has magnitude $V_L - V_C \approx 78\,\mathrm{V}$; together with $V_R$ it forms the right triangle whose hypotenuse is $\sqrt{91^2 + 78^2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 120\,\mathrm{V}$, the source voltage.

</div>
