## Exercise 1

A series $RLC$ circuit with $R = 60\,\Omega$, inductive reactance $X_L = 120\,\Omega$ and capacitive reactance $X_C = 40\,\Omega$ at the operating frequency is supplied with $V_{\text{rms}} = 230\,\mathrm{V}$. Compute the impedance, the rms current, the power factor and the average power absorbed.

$$
Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} , \qquad I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{Z} , \qquad \cos\varphi = \frac{R}{Z} , \qquad P = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}} \cos\varphi = R\, I_{\text{rms}}^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z &= \sqrt{60^2 + 80^2}\,\Omega = 100\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{\text{rms}} &= \frac{230}{100}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.3\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\cos\varphi &= \frac{60}{100} = 0.60 \\[6pt]
P &= 230 \cdot 2.3 \cdot 0.60\,\mathrm{W} \approx 3.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The impedance follows from the theorem on the **impedance of the series RLC circuit**, with $X_L - X_C = 80\,\Omega$, and the rms current from the ratio of the rms voltage to the impedance. In the phasor triangle the cosine of the phase shift is the ratio of the leg $R$ to the hypotenuse $Z$, so the power factor is $0.60$.

By the theorem on the **average power**, $P = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}} \cos\varphi \approx 317\,\mathrm{W}$. The result coincides with the power dissipated in the resistor, $R\,I_{\text{rms}}^2 = 60 \cdot 2.3^2\,\mathrm{W} \approx 317\,\mathrm{W}$, because the inductor and the capacitor absorb no average power. The product $V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}} = 529\,\mathrm{V \cdot A}$ is larger than the average power, and the difference is due to the phase shift.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electric motor supplied by the grid, with $V_{\text{rms}} = 230\,\mathrm{V}$ and $f = 50\,\mathrm{Hz}$, absorbs the average power $P = 1.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ and carries the rms current $I_{\text{rms}} = 9.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the power factor and the phase shift. Modeling the motor as a resistor in series with an inductor, compute $R$ and $L$.

$$
\cos\varphi = \frac{P}{V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}}} ,
\qquad
Z = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{I_{\text{rms}}} ,
\qquad
R = \frac{P}{I_{\text{rms}}^2} ,
\qquad
X_L = \sqrt{Z^2 - R^2} ,
\qquad
L = \frac{X_L}{2\pi f}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos\varphi &= \frac{1500}{230 \cdot 9.0} \approx 0.72 , \qquad \varphi \approx 44^\circ \\[6pt]
Z &= \frac{230}{9.0}\,\Omega \approx 25.6\,\Omega \\[6pt]
R &= \frac{1500}{9.0^2}\,\Omega \approx 18.5\,\Omega \\[6pt]
X_L &= \sqrt{25.6^2 - 18.5^2}\,\Omega \approx 17.6\,\Omega \\[6pt]
L &= \frac{17.6}{2\pi \cdot 50}\,\mathrm{H} \approx 56\,\mathrm{mH}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From the theorem on the **average power** we obtain the power factor as the ratio of the average power to the product of the rms values; the phase shift is its arccosine, positive because the motor is an inductive load and the current lags.

In the series $RL$ model the average power is entirely dissipated in the resistor, so $P = R\,I_{\text{rms}}^2$ and we obtain $R$. The impedance is the ratio of the rms values, and the inductive reactance is the second leg of the right triangle with hypotenuse $Z$. As a check, $\dfrac{R}{Z} = \dfrac{18.5}{25.6} \approx 0.72$, which is the power factor found. In the real motor the resistor represents both the power converted into mechanical work and the losses.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The motor of the previous exercise, which absorbs $P = 1.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ with $I_{1,\text{rms}} = 9.0\,\mathrm{A}$ and $\cos\varphi \approx 0.72$ from the grid at $230\,\mathrm{V}$ and $50\,\mathrm{Hz}$, is power-factor corrected by connecting a capacitor in parallel. Compute the capacitance that brings the power factor of the motor-capacitor system to $1$, and the rms current in the line after the correction.

$$
\tilde{I} = \tilde{I}_1 + \tilde{I}_2 ,
\qquad
I_{2,\text{rms}} = I_{1,\text{rms}} \sin\varphi = \omega C\, V_{\text{rms}}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
C = \frac{I_{1,\text{rms}} \sin\varphi}{\omega V_{\text{rms}}} ,
\qquad
I_{\text{rms}} = I_{1,\text{rms}} \cos\varphi = \frac{P}{V_{\text{rms}}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\varphi &= \sqrt{1 - 0.725^2} \approx 0.689 \\[6pt]
I_{2,\text{rms}} &= 9.0 \cdot 0.689\,\mathrm{A} \approx 6.2\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
C &= \frac{6.20}{314 \cdot 230}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 8.6 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{F} = 86\,\mu\mathrm{F} \\[6pt]
I_{\text{rms}} &= \frac{1500}{230}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 6.5\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 segmenti="0,0,4.6,0" vettori="0,0,2.61,-2.48,I₁;2.61,-2.48,2.61,0,I₂;0,0,2.61,0,I" etichette="4.7,-0.1,V;0.75,-0.3,φ" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5.2 ymin=-3.0 ymax=0.9 titolo="Phasors of the rms currents: the capacitor current $I_2$, leading the voltage by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, compensates for the component of $I_1$ perpendicular to $V$, and the line current $I$ is in phase with the voltage"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The motor and the capacitor are in parallel, so they have the same grid voltage across them, and by the junction rule, which also holds for phasors, the line current is the vector sum of the two currents. The motor current $\tilde{I}_1$ lags the voltage by $\varphi$ and decomposes into a component $I_{1,\text{rms}} \cos\varphi$ parallel to the voltage and a component $I_{1,\text{rms}} \sin\varphi$ perpendicular to it, lagging. The capacitor current $\tilde{I}_2$ leads by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and has rms value $\omega C V_{\text{rms}}$, by the theorem on the capacitor in AC.

The power factor of the system is $1$ when the line current is in phase with the voltage, that is, when $\tilde{I}_2$ cancels the perpendicular component of $\tilde{I}_1$; from this condition we obtain $C \approx 86\,\mu\mathrm{F}$. The line current then reduces to the parallel component, about $6.5\,\mathrm{A}$ instead of $9.0\,\mathrm{A}$. The average power absorbed remains $1.5\,\mathrm{kW}$, because the capacitor absorbs no average power, while the losses in the line, proportional to $I_{\text{rms}}^2$, are reduced to about half, since $\left( \dfrac{6.5}{9.0} \right)^2 \approx 0.52$.

</div>
