## Exercise 1

A series $RLC$ circuit with $R = 8.0\,\Omega$, $L = 40\,\mathrm{mH}$ and $C = 0.40\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is supplied by a source of rms voltage $V_{\text{rms}} = 4.0\,\mathrm{V}$ and adjustable frequency. Compute the resonant frequency, the rms current at resonance, the quality factor and the rms voltage across the capacitor at resonance.

$$
\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} , \qquad f_0 = \frac{\omega_0}{2\pi} , \qquad I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{R} , \qquad Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} , \qquad V_C = X_C I_{\text{rms}} = Q\,V_{\text{rms}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_0 &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{40 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 0.40 \cdot 10^{-6}}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1.6 \cdot 10^{-8}}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 7.91 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
f_0 &= \frac{7906}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{Hz} \approx 1.26\,\mathrm{kHz} \\[6pt]
I_{\text{rms}} &= \frac{4.0}{8.0}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.50\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
Q &= \frac{7906 \cdot 40 \cdot 10^{-3}}{8.0} \approx 40 \\[6pt]
V_C &= 316 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.6 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on the **resonance of the series RLC circuit** the current is maximum when the angular frequency of the source coincides with the natural angular frequency $\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$. In that condition the two reactances are equal, $X_L = X_C = \omega_0 L \approx 316\,\Omega$, the impedance reduces to $R$ and the rms current is $\dfrac{V_{\text{rms}}}{R}$.

At resonance the voltage across the capacitor is $X_C I_{\text{rms}} = \dfrac{\omega_0 L}{R}\,V_{\text{rms}}$, that is, $Q$ times the source voltage. With a quality factor close to $40$ the capacitor is subjected to about $160\,\mathrm{V}$ rms, even though the source supplies only $4.0$; the voltage across the inductor has the same value and opposite phase, so the two cancel out.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The tuning circuit of a radio receiver has an inductor of inductance $L = 0.30\,\mathrm{mH}$ and a capacitor of adjustable capacitance. Compute the range over which the capacitance must vary for the resonant frequency to cover the band between $540\,\mathrm{kHz}$ and $1600\,\mathrm{kHz}$.

$$
f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 f_0^2 L}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\max} &= \frac{1}{4\pi^2 \cdot (540 \cdot 10^3)^2 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 2.9 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{F} = 290\,\mathrm{pF} \\[6pt]
C_{\min} &= \frac{1}{4\pi^2 \cdot (1600 \cdot 10^3)^2 \cdot 0.30 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 3.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{F} = 33\,\mathrm{pF}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The receiver selects the station whose frequency coincides with the **resonant** frequency of the circuit, so $C$ is obtained from the formula for $f_0$. The capacitance is inversely proportional to the square of the frequency, therefore the maximum capacitance corresponds to the minimum frequency and vice versa. The ratio of the extreme capacitances is $\left( \dfrac{1600}{540} \right)^2 \approx 8.8$, the square of the ratio of the frequencies, and the capacitance must vary between about $33\,\mathrm{pF}$ and $290\,\mathrm{pF}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A series $RLC$ circuit with $R = 5.0\,\Omega$, $L = 50\,\mathrm{mH}$ and $C = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is supplied by a source with $V_{\text{rms}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{V}$ and adjustable angular frequency. Compute the resonant angular frequency, the quality factor, the endpoints of the bandwidth and the rms current at the endpoints of the band.

$$
\omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} , \qquad Q = \frac{\omega_0 L}{R} , \qquad \omega_\pm = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + \frac{R^2}{4L^2}} \pm \frac{R}{2L} , \qquad I_{\text{rms}}(\omega_\pm) = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{\sqrt{2}\,R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_0 &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{50 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1.0 \cdot 10^{-7}}}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 3162\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
Q &= \frac{3162 \cdot 0.050}{5.0} \approx 32 \\[6pt]
\frac{R}{2L} &= \frac{5.0}{2 \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} = 50\,\mathrm{s^{-1}} \\[6pt]
\omega_\pm &= \left( \sqrt{3162^2 + 50^2} \pm 50 \right)\mathrm{rad/s} \approx (3163 \pm 50)\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
\omega_- &\approx 3113\,\mathrm{rad/s} , \qquad \omega_+ \approx 3213\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
I_{\text{rms}}(\omega_\pm) &= \frac{2.0}{\sqrt{2} \cdot 5.0}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.28\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The resonant angular frequency is the natural one of the $LC$ circuit, and the quality factor follows from its definition. The endpoints of the **bandwidth** are the angular frequencies at which the impedance equals $\sqrt{2}\,R$, that is, $\omega L - \dfrac{1}{\omega C} = \pm R$; solving the two quadratic equations and taking the positive solutions we obtain the formula for $\omega_\pm$.

The band has width $\omega_+ - \omega_- = \dfrac{R}{L} = 100\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, in agreement with $\dfrac{\omega_0}{Q} \approx \dfrac{3162}{31.6}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The endpoints are almost symmetric about $\omega_0$, because $\dfrac{R}{2L}$ is small compared with $\omega_0$. At resonance the rms current is $\dfrac{2.0}{5.0}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.40\,\mathrm{A}$, and at the endpoints of the band it is $\sqrt{2}$ times smaller, so the power dissipated in the resistor is half.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A series $RLC$ circuit with $R = 50\,\Omega$ is supplied by a source with $V_{\text{rms}} = 10\,\mathrm{V}$ and adjustable frequency. The rms current is maximum at the frequency $f_0 = 800\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and at the frequency $400\,\mathrm{Hz}$ it is half the maximum. Compute the maximum current, the inductance and the capacitance.

$$
I_{\max} = \frac{V_{\text{rms}}}{R} , \qquad \omega = \frac{\omega_0}{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
X_L = \frac{\omega_0 L}{2} , \quad X_C = \frac{2}{\omega_0 C} = 2\,\omega_0 L
$$
$$
Z = 2R
\;\Longrightarrow\;
(X_L - X_C)^2 = 3R^2
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{3}{2}\,\omega_0 L = \sqrt{3}\, R
\;\Longrightarrow\;
L = \frac{2R}{\sqrt{3}\,\omega_0} , \qquad C = \frac{1}{\omega_0^2 L}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{\max} &= \frac{10}{50}\,\mathrm{A} = 0.20\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\omega_0 &= 2\pi \cdot 800\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 5027\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
L &= \frac{2 \cdot 50}{\sqrt{3} \cdot 5027}\,\mathrm{H} \approx 1.15 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{H} \approx 11\,\mathrm{mH} \\[6pt]
C &= \frac{1}{5027^2 \cdot 1.15 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 3.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{F} = 3.4\,\mu\mathrm{F}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="100/sqrt(2500+(7.2169*x-461.88/x)^2)+0*sqrt(x)" punti="8,2,pieno;4,1,pieno" segmenti="4,0,4,1;8,0,8,2;0,1,4,1;0,2,8,2" xmin=-0.4 xmax=19.5 ymin=-0.3 ymax=2.6 nomi="f,I" legenda=0 titolo="Rms current as a function of frequency ($f$ in hundreds of hertz, $I_{\text{rms}}$ in tenths of an ampere): the maximum $0.20\,\mathrm{A}$ is at $800\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and at $400\,\mathrm{Hz}$ the current is half"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The frequency at which the current is maximum is the **resonant** frequency, where the impedance equals $R$; from this follows $I_{\max}$. At the frequency $400\,\mathrm{Hz}$, that is, for $\omega = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$, the inductive reactance is half of that at resonance and the capacitive reactance is twice as large. At resonance $\dfrac{1}{\omega_0 C} = \omega_0 L$, so $X_C = 2\,\omega_0 L$ and the difference of the reactances equals $-\dfrac{3}{2}\,\omega_0 L$; the negative sign indicates that below resonance the circuit is capacitive.

The current is half the maximum, therefore the impedance is twice as large, $Z = 2R$. Squaring, $R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2 = 4R^2$, from which $|X_L - X_C| = \sqrt{3}\,R$ and hence $\omega_0 L = \dfrac{2R}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 57.7\,\Omega$. Dividing by $\omega_0$ we obtain $L$, and the resonance condition $\omega_0^2 LC = 1$ gives $C$. As a check, at $400\,\mathrm{Hz}$ we have $X_L \approx 28.9\,\Omega$ and $X_C \approx 115.5\,\Omega$, so $Z = \sqrt{50^2 + 86.6^2}\,\Omega \approx 100\,\Omega$ and the current equals $0.10\,\mathrm{A}$.

</div>
