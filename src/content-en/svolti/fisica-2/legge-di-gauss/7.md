## Exercise 1

In a region of space the electric field is $\vec{E} = b\,x\,\hat{\imath}$, with $b = 3.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N/(C \cdot m)}$. Compute the charge density in the region and check the result with the outward flux through a cube of edge $a = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ with one vertex at the origin and its edges along the positive semi-axes.

$$
\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\partial (b\,x)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial\, 0}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial\, 0}{\partial z} = b ,
\qquad
\rho = \varepsilon_0\, \nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \varepsilon_0\, b
$$
$$
\Phi_E = E_x(a)\,a^2 - E_x(0)\,a^2 = b\,a^3 ,
\qquad
q_{\text{int}} = \varepsilon_0\, b\, a^3 = \rho\, a^3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \approx 2.7 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \\[6pt]
\Phi_E &= 3.0 \cdot 10^4 \cdot 0.10^3\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} = 30\,\mathrm{N \cdot m^2/C} \\[6pt]
q_{\text{int}} &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 30\,\mathrm{C} \approx 2.7 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is of class $C^1$, so the **local form of Gauss's law** applies, which gives the density as $\varepsilon_0$ times the divergence. The field has only the $x$ component, which depends only on $x$, therefore the divergence is the derivative of $b\,x$ with respect to $x$, that is, the constant $b$, and the density is **uniform**.

For the check the integral form is used. The field is parallel to the four faces of the cube perpendicular to the $y$ and $z$ axes, so on those faces the flux is zero; on the face $x = 0$ the field is zero, and on the face $x = a$ it equals $b\,a$ and points outward. The enclosed charge is $\varepsilon_0$ times the flux, and it coincides with the product of the density and the volume of the cube, $2.7 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, as it must for a uniform density.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Inside a sphere of radius $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ the electric field is $\vec{E} = b\,r^2\,\hat{r}$, where $r$ is the distance from the center, $\hat{r}$ the outward radial unit vector and $b = 2.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{N/(C \cdot m^2)}$. Compute the charge density as a function of $r$, its value at the surface and the total charge contained in the sphere.

$$
\vec{E} = b\,r\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} + z\,\hat{k}) ,
\qquad
r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial r}{\partial x} = \frac{x}{r}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial (b\,r\,x)}{\partial x} = b \left( \frac{x^2}{r} + r \right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = b \left( \frac{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}{r} + 3r \right) = 4\,b\,r
$$
$$
\rho(r) = 4\,\varepsilon_0\, b\, r ,
\qquad
Q = \oint_S \varepsilon_0\, \vec{E} \cdot \hat{n}\, dA = \varepsilon_0\, b R^2 \cdot 4\pi R^2 = 4\pi\varepsilon_0\, b R^4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(R) &= 4 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \approx 7.1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \\[6pt]
Q &= 4\pi \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.10^4\,\mathrm{C} \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compute the divergence, the field is written in Cartesian coordinates, recalling that $r\,\hat{r}$ is the position vector $x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath} + z\,\hat{k}$. The $x$ component is the product $b\,r\,x$, and by the product rule, with $\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial x} = \dfrac{x}{r}$, its derivative with respect to $x$ is $b\left( \dfrac{x^2}{r} + r \right)$; the derivatives of the $y$ and $z$ components are analogous. Summing, the numerator $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ is $r^2$, so the divergence is $4br$. By the **local form of Gauss's law** the density grows linearly from the center to the surface.

The total charge is obtained from Gauss's law in integral form, applied to the surface of the sphere, where the field is radial with constant magnitude $b R^2$. As a check, integrating the density over the spherical shells gives the same value, because $\displaystyle\int_0^R 4\varepsilon_0 b\, s \cdot 4\pi s^2\, ds = 4\pi\varepsilon_0\, b R^4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a slab between the planes $x = 0$ and $x = a$, with $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, the electric field is $\vec{E} = E_0 \left( \dfrac{x}{a} \right)^2 \hat{\imath}$, with $E_0 = 1.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N/C}$. Compute the charge density in the slab as a function of $x$, its maximum value and the charge per unit area contained in the slab.

$$
\rho(x) = \varepsilon_0\, \frac{dE_x}{dx} = \frac{2\,\varepsilon_0 E_0\, x}{a^2} ,
\qquad
\rho_{\max} = \rho(a) = \frac{2\,\varepsilon_0 E_0}{a}
$$
$$
\frac{q}{A} = \int_0^a \rho(x)\, dx = \frac{2\,\varepsilon_0 E_0}{a^2} \cdot \frac{a^2}{2} = \varepsilon_0 E_0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_E = E_x(a)\,A - E_x(0)\,A = E_0 A = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\max} &= \frac{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^4}{0.020}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C/m^3} \\[6pt]
\frac{q}{A} &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{C/m^2} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field has only the $x$ component and depends only on $x$, so the divergence reduces to the derivative $\dfrac{dE_x}{dx}$, and by the **local form of Gauss's law** the density is $\varepsilon_0$ times this derivative. The density is zero on the plane $x = 0$ and grows linearly up to its maximum value on the plane $x = a$.

The charge contained in a portion of slab of area $A$ is obtained by integrating the density over the thin layers of thickness $dx$, which have volume $A\,dx$. The result is checked with Gauss's law in integral form, applied to the cylinder with bases of area $A$ on the planes $x = 0$ and $x = a$. The flux through the lateral surface is zero, the field is zero on the base $x = 0$, and on the base $x = a$ it equals $E_0$ and points outward. The flux $E_0 A$, multiplied by $\varepsilon_0$, gives the same charge $\varepsilon_0 E_0 A$.

</div>
