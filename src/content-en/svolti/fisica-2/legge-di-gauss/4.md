## Exercise 1

An insulating sphere of radius $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ is charged with volume density $\rho(r) = \dfrac{A}{r}$ for $0 < r \leq R$, where $r$ is the distance from the center and $A = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{C/m^2}$. Compute the electric field inside the sphere, the total charge and the magnitude of the field at $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$ from the center.

$$
q_{\text{int}}(r) = \int_0^r \frac{A}{s}\, 4\pi s^2\, ds = 4\pi A \int_0^r s\, ds = 2\pi A\, r^2
$$
$$
E(r) \cdot 4\pi r^2 = \frac{2\pi A\, r^2}{\varepsilon_0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E(r) = \frac{A}{2\varepsilon_0} \quad (r \leq R)
$$
$$
Q = 2\pi A R^2 ,
\qquad
E(r) = \frac{kQ}{r^2} \quad (r \geq R)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{int}} &= \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-8}}{2 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C} \\[6pt]
Q &= 2\pi \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot 0.10^2\,\mathrm{C} \approx 1.9 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
E(0.20\,\mathrm{m}) &= \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 1.88 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.20^2}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 4.2 \cdot 10^2\,\mathrm{N/C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The density depends only on $r$, so the distribution has spherical symmetry and on a concentric Gaussian sphere of radius $r$ the flux is $E(r) \cdot 4\pi r^2$. The enclosed charge is obtained by summing the spherical shells of radius $s$ and thickness $ds$, which have volume $4\pi s^2\,ds$ and charge $\rho(s)\,4\pi s^2\,ds$. The density tends to infinity near the center, but the product $\dfrac{A}{s} \cdot s^2 = A s$ is continuous, so the integral is finite.

By **Gauss's law** the factor $r^2$ cancels, and inside the field has **constant magnitude**, independent of the distance from the center. Outside, the sphere produces the field of a point charge $Q$ at the center. As a check, at the surface the two expressions coincide, because $\dfrac{kQ}{R^2} = \dfrac{2\pi A R^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^2} = \dfrac{A}{2\varepsilon_0}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An insulating sphere of radius $R = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is charged with density $\rho(r) = \rho_0 \left( 1 - \dfrac{r}{R} \right)$ for $r \leq R$, with $\rho_0 = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C/m^3}$. Compute the total charge, the electric field as a function of $r$ inside the sphere, the distance from the center at which the field is maximum and the maximum value.

::grafico{f="x<0 ? NaN : x<=6 ? 3.3898*(x/3-x^2/24) : 61.0169/x^2" xmin=-0.6 xmax=13 ymin=-0.3 ymax=2.8 nomi="r,E" segmenti="4,0,4,2.26;6,0,6,1.695" punti="4,2.260,pieno" legenda="E(r)" titolo="Magnitude of the field of the sphere with $\rho = \rho_0 \left( 1 - \dfrac{r}{R} \right)$, with $r$ in centimeters and $E$ in $10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$; the maximum is at $r = \dfrac{2R}{3} = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
q_{\text{int}}(r) = \int_0^r \rho_0 \left( 1 - \frac{s}{R} \right) 4\pi s^2\, ds = 4\pi\rho_0 \left( \frac{r^3}{3} - \frac{r^4}{4R} \right) ,
\qquad
Q = q_{\text{int}}(R) = \frac{\pi \rho_0 R^3}{3}
$$
$$
E(r) = \frac{q_{\text{int}}(r)}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2} = \frac{\rho_0}{\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{r}{3} - \frac{r^2}{4R} \right)
$$
$$
\frac{dE}{dr} = \frac{\rho_0}{\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{1}{3} - \frac{r}{2R} \right) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r_{\max} = \frac{2R}{3}
$$
$$
E_{\max} = \frac{\rho_0}{\varepsilon_0} \left( \frac{2R}{9} - \frac{R}{9} \right) = \frac{\rho_0 R}{9\varepsilon_0}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= \frac{\pi \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 0.060^3}{3}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 6.8 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{C} \\[6pt]
r_{\max} &= \frac{2 \cdot 6.0}{3}\,\mathrm{cm} = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
E_{\max} &= \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 0.060}{9 \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12}}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distribution has spherical symmetry, and the charge enclosed in the sphere of radius $r$ is computed by integrating the density over the spherical shells of volume $4\pi s^2\,ds$. By **Gauss's law** the field inside is the enclosed charge divided by $4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2$; the first term, $\dfrac{\rho_0 r}{3\varepsilon_0}$, is the field of a sphere with uniform density $\rho_0$, and the second accounts for the decrease of the density toward the surface.

The function $E(r)$ inside is a polynomial of degree two with negative coefficient of $r^2$, so its maximum is at the point where the derivative vanishes, $r = \dfrac{2R}{3}$, which belongs to the interval $[0, R]$. Outside, the field is $\dfrac{kQ}{r^2}$ and decreases, therefore the point found is the **absolute maximum**. As a check, at $r = R$ the interior formula gives $\dfrac{\rho_0 R}{12\varepsilon_0} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$, equal to $\dfrac{kQ}{R^2} = \dfrac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 6.8 \cdot 10^{-10}}{0.060^2}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 1.7 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An insulating sphere of radius $a = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, with charge $Q_1 = +4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ distributed uniformly throughout its volume, is surrounded by a thin concentric spherical shell of radius $b = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ with charge $Q_2 = -6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ distributed uniformly. Compute the electric field at distances $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, $4.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $9.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center. Determine what charge the shell should have for the field outside the shell to be zero.

$$
E(r) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{k Q_1\, r}{a^3} & \text{if } r \leq a \\[12pt]
\dfrac{k Q_1}{r^2} & \text{if } a \leq r < b \\[12pt]
\dfrac{k\,(Q_1 + Q_2)}{r^2} & \text{if } r > b
\end{cases}
\qquad
E = 0 \text{ for } r > b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Q_2 = -Q_1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
E(1.5\,\mathrm{cm}) &= \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot 0.015}{0.030^3}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 2.0 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N/C} \\[6pt]
E(4.5\,\mathrm{cm}) &= \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.045^2}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx 1.8 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{N/C} \\[6pt]
E(9.0\,\mathrm{cm}) &= \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot (-2.0 \cdot 10^{-9})}{0.090^2}\,\mathrm{N/C} \approx -2.2 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{N/C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sphere and the shell are concentric, so the overall distribution has spherical symmetry and $E(r)$ denotes the component of the field along the outward radial unit vector. In each region one applies **Gauss's law** to the sphere of radius $r$, counting the enclosed charge. At $1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ the Gaussian sphere encloses the fraction $\left( \dfrac{1.5}{3.0} \right)^3 = \dfrac{1}{8}$ of the charge $Q_1$. At $4.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ it encloses the whole sphere and not the shell, and the shell produces no field inside it. At $9.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ it encloses the total charge $-2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$, so the radial component is negative and the field is **directed toward the center**.

Outside the shell the field is proportional to the total charge $Q_1 + Q_2$, therefore it is zero at every outside point if and only if the shell has the charge $Q_2 = -4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$. Inside the shell the field does not depend on $Q_2$.

</div>
