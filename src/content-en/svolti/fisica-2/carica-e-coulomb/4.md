## Exercise 1

In vacuum the charge $q_0 = +2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ is at the origin, the charge $q_1 = -4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at the point $(0;\,0.30\,\mathrm{m})$ and the charge $q_2 = -3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at the point $(0.40\,\mathrm{m};\,0)$. Compute the magnitude and direction of the total force on $q_0$.

$$
\vec{F}_1 = k\,\frac{|q_1|\,q_0}{0.30^2}\,\hat{\jmath} , \qquad
\vec{F}_2 = k\,\frac{|q_2|\,q_0}{0.40^2}\,\hat{\imath} , \qquad
F = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2} , \qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{F_1}{F_2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1 &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.090}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.799\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_2 &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.160}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.337\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F &= \sqrt{0.799^2 + 0.337^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.87\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
\varphi &= \arctan \frac{0.799}{0.337} \approx 67^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno;0,3,pieno,q₁;4,0,pieno,q₂" vettori="0,0,0,2.4,F₁;0,0,1.01,0,F₂;0,0,1.01,2.4,F" segmenti="0,2.4,1.01,2.4;1.01,0,1.01,2.4" etichette="-0.45,-0.45,q₀" xmin=-0.9 xmax=4.6 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.6 titolo="The two negative charges attract $q_0$: $\vec{F}_1$ toward $q_1$ along the $y$-axis, $\vec{F}_2$ toward $q_2$ along the $x$-axis, and the resultant $\vec{F}$ forms about $67^\circ$ with the $x$-axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **superposition principle** the force on $q_0$ is the sum of the forces of $q_1$ and $q_2$. Both charges are negative and $q_0$ is positive, so both forces are attractive. The force of $q_1$ is directed from $q_0$ toward $q_1$, that is in the positive direction of the $y$-axis; the force of $q_2$ is directed toward $q_2$, that is in the positive direction of the $x$-axis.

The two forces are perpendicular, therefore the magnitude of the resultant is obtained with the Pythagorean theorem, and the angle $\varphi$ with the $x$-axis has tangent equal to the ratio between the $y$ component and the $x$ component. Both components are positive, so the angle lies in the first quadrant; therefore the total force has magnitude about $0.87\,\mathrm{N}$ and forms about $67^\circ$ with the positive direction of the $x$-axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three point charges are at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side $a = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, in vacuum: $q_A = q_B = +1.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at the base vertices $A$ and $B$, and $q_C = -1.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at the opposite vertex $C$. Compute the total force on $q_C$ and on $q_A$.

$$
F_0 = k\,\frac{q^2}{a^2} = 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{\left( 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \right)^2}{0.20^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.225\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
F_C = 2F_0\cos 30^\circ = \sqrt{3}\,F_0 \approx 1.732 \cdot 0.225\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.39\,\mathrm{N}
$$
$$
\vec{F}_A = F_0\,(-\hat{\imath}) + F_0\,(\cos 60^\circ\,\hat{\imath} + \sin 60^\circ\,\hat{\jmath}) = F_0\,\left( -\frac{1}{2}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\hat{\jmath} \right) ,
\qquad
F_A = F_0 \approx 0.22\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three pairs of charges have the same distance $a$ and the same product of absolute values, so each force has the same magnitude $F_0$, given by **Coulomb's law**. Only the directions and orientations change.

The charge $q_C$ is attracted by $q_A$ and by $q_B$, and the two forces each form an angle of $30^\circ$ with the altitude of the triangle drawn from $C$. The triangle is symmetric with respect to that altitude, therefore the components perpendicular to the altitude cancel, as in the **force on the axis of two equal charges**, and the two components $F_0\cos 30^\circ$ remain. The force on $q_C$ is directed toward the midpoint of the base and has magnitude about $0.39\,\mathrm{N}$.

With the $x$-axis along the base, from $A$ toward $B$, the charge $q_A$ is repelled by $q_B$ to the left and attracted by $q_C$ along the side $AC$, which forms $60^\circ$ with the base. Adding the components gives a vector of magnitude $F_0$ that forms $120^\circ$ with the $x$-axis, that is parallel to the side $BC$ and oriented from $B$ toward $C$. Indeed two forces of equal magnitude forming an angle of $120^\circ$ have a resultant of the **same magnitude**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four equal point charges $q = +0.50\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ are at the vertices of a square of side $a = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$, in vacuum. Compute the total force on one of the charges, and the charge $Q$ to be placed at the center of the square so that all five charges are in equilibrium.

$$
F_0 = k\,\frac{q^2}{a^2} , \qquad
F = \sqrt{2}\,F_0 + k\,\frac{q^2}{\left( \sqrt{2}\,a \right)^2} = \left( \sqrt{2} + \frac{1}{2} \right) F_0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_0 &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{\left( 0.50 \cdot 10^{-6} \right)^2}{0.10^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.225\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F &\approx 1.914 \cdot 0.225\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.43\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
k\,\frac{q\,|Q|}{\left( \dfrac{a}{\sqrt{2}} \right)^2} = \left( \sqrt{2} + \frac{1}{2} \right) k\,\frac{q^2}{a^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
|Q| = \frac{2\sqrt{2} + 1}{4}\,q
$$
$$
Q = -\frac{2\sqrt{2} + 1}{4} \cdot 0.50\,\mu\mathrm{C} \approx -0.957 \cdot 0.50\,\mu\mathrm{C} \approx -0.48\,\mu\mathrm{C}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a charge act the two charges at the adjacent vertices, at distance $a$, and the charge at the opposite vertex, at distance $\sqrt{2}\,a$; all are repulsive. The forces of the two adjacent charges have magnitude $F_0$ and are perpendicular, so their sum has magnitude $\sqrt{2}\,F_0$ and is directed along the diagonal, outward from the square. The force of the opposite charge is also directed along the diagonal outward, with magnitude $\dfrac{F_0}{2}$ because the squared distance is $2a^2$. The forces along the diagonal add up, and the total force has magnitude about $0.43\,\mathrm{N}$, directed along the diagonal outward.

The charge $Q$ at the center is at distance $\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$ from each vertex and exerts on each charge $q$ a force along the diagonal. To balance the outward repulsion it must attract, so $Q$ is negative, and the magnitude of its force must equal $F$. The charge $Q$ is in turn in equilibrium, because the forces of the four equal charges at the vertices cancel in pairs by symmetry. The definition of **equilibrium of a charge** is therefore satisfied for all five charges with $Q \approx -0.48\,\mu\mathrm{C}$.

</div>
