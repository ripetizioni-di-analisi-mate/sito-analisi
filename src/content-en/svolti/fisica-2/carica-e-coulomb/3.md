## Exercise 1

On the $x$-axis, in vacuum, there are the point charges $q_1 = +2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at $x_1 = 0$ and $q_2 = -4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at $x_2 = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the total force on the charge $q_0 = +1.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ placed at $x_0 = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
F_x = k\,\frac{q_1 q_0}{(x_0 - x_1)^2} + k\,\frac{|q_2|\,q_0}{(x_2 - x_0)^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1 &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.10^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.80\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_2 &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{4.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.20^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.90\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_x &= F_1 + F_2 \approx 2.7\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **superposition principle** the total force on $q_0$ is the sum of the forces that $q_1$ and $q_2$ would exert alone, and all the forces are directed along the $x$-axis. The charge $q_1$ is positive like $q_0$ and lies to its left, so it repels it to the right. The charge $q_2$ is negative and lies to the right of $q_0$, so it attracts it to the right. The two contributions have the same orientation and add up.

The total force has magnitude $2.7\,\mathrm{N}$ and is **directed to the right**, that is toward $q_2$. The contribution of $q_2$ is half that of $q_1$, because $q_2$ has twice the absolute value and twice the distance from $q_0$, so its contribution is multiplied by $\dfrac{2}{2^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On the $x$-axis, in vacuum, there are the point charges $q_1 = +3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at $x_1 = 0$ and $q_2 = -3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at $x_2 = 0.20\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the total force on the charge $q_0 = +2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ placed at $x_0 = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
F_x = k\,\frac{q_1 q_0}{(x_0 - x_1)^2} - k\,\frac{|q_2|\,q_0}{(x_0 - x_2)^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_1 &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.40^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.337\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_2 &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}}{0.20^2}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.349\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_x &= F_1 - F_2 \approx -1.0\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Now $q_0$ lies to the right of both charges. The charge $q_1$, positive, repels it to the right; the charge $q_2$, negative, attracts it to the left. By the rule for **charges on a line** the first contribution is positive and the second negative.

The two charges have the same absolute value, and $q_2$ is at half the distance of $q_1$, so its contribution is four times larger. The total force has a negative component, that is it is **directed to the left**, with magnitude about $1.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On the $x$-axis, in vacuum, there are the point charges $q_1 = +1.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at $x_1 = 0$, $q_2 = +2.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at $x_2 = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $q_3 = -3.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ at $x_3 = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the total force on each charge and verify that their sum is zero.

$$
F_{12} = k\,\frac{|q_1 q_2|}{0.10^2} , \qquad
F_{13} = k\,\frac{|q_1 q_3|}{0.30^2} , \qquad
F_{23} = k\,\frac{|q_2 q_3|}{0.20^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{12} &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{-12}}{0.010}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.798\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_{13} &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{3.0 \cdot 10^{-12}}{0.090}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.300\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_{23} &= 8.99 \cdot 10^{9} \cdot \frac{6.0 \cdot 10^{-12}}{0.040}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.349\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{1,x} &= -F_{12} + F_{13} \approx (-1.798 + 0.300)\,\mathrm{N} \approx -1.50\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_{2,x} &= +F_{12} + F_{23} \approx (1.798 + 1.349)\,\mathrm{N} \approx +3.15\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_{3,x} &= -F_{13} - F_{23} \approx (-0.300 - 1.349)\,\mathrm{N} \approx -1.65\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_{1,x} + F_{2,x} + F_{3,x} &\approx (-1.50 + 3.15 - 1.65)\,\mathrm{N} = 0
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno;1,0,pieno;3,0,pieno" vettori="0,0,-0.45,0,F₁;1,0,1.94,0,F₂;3,0,2.5,0,F₃" etichette="-0.2,-0.45,q₁;0.8,-0.45,q₂;2.8,-0.45,q₃" xmin=-1 xmax=3.6 ymin=-0.9 ymax=0.7 titolo="Total forces on the three charges, drawn to scale: the sum of the three forces is zero"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The three charges form three pairs, and for each pair the magnitude of the force is computed only once with **Coulomb's law**, because by action and reaction the two charges of the pair experience forces of equal magnitude and opposite orientations. The pair $q_1$, $q_2$ is repulsive; the pairs $q_1$, $q_3$ and $q_2$, $q_3$ are attractive.

On $q_1$ the charge $q_2$ pushes to the left and $q_3$ pulls to the right; on $q_2$ the charge $q_1$ pushes to the right and $q_3$ also pulls to the right; on $q_3$ both positive charges pull to the left. The total forces are therefore $1.50\,\mathrm{N}$ to the left on $q_1$, $3.15\,\mathrm{N}$ to the right on $q_2$ and $1.65\,\mathrm{N}$ to the left on $q_3$.

The sum is zero, as predicted by the **sum of the internal forces**: each magnitude $F_{ij}$ appears twice, with opposite signs. The check confirms the calculations.

</div>
