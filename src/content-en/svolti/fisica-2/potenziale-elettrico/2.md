## Exercise 1

In a region of space there is a uniform electric field $\vec{E} = E\,\hat{\imath}$ with $E = 2.5 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m}$. A charge $q = -4.0\,\mu\mathrm{C}$ moves from the point $A = (0,\ 0)$ to the point $B = (0.40\,\mathrm{m},\ 0.30\,\mathrm{m})$. Compute the potential difference $V_B - V_A$, the work done by the electric force and the work done by an external agent that moves the charge slowly.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="-0.6,-0.8,0.6,-0.8,E;1.8,-0.8,3,-0.8;-0.6,3.8,0.6,3.8;1.8,3.8,3,3.8" linee="0,0 4,0 4,3" segmenti="0,0,4,3" punti="0,0,pieno,A;4,3,pieno,B;4,0,pieno,C" xmin=-1 xmax=5.2 ymin=-1.3 ymax=4.3 titolo="In the uniform field directed along $x$ the work from $A$ to $B$ is the same along the segment $AB$ and along the broken line $ACB$, where only the segment $AC$, parallel to the field, contributes"}

$$
V_B - V_A = -\vec{E} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = -E\, \Delta x ,
\qquad
W_{A \to B} = -q\,(V_B - V_A) ,
\qquad
W_{\text{ext}} = -W_{A \to B}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_B - V_A &= -2.5 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{V} = -1.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
W_{A \to B} &= -(-4.0 \cdot 10^{-6}) \cdot (-1.0 \cdot 10^3)\,\mathrm{J} = -4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
W_{\text{ext}} &= +4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform, so the **potential difference in a uniform field** applies: only the projection of the displacement onto the direction of the field matters, which is $\Delta x = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$, while the displacement $\Delta y = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ is perpendicular to the field and does not change the potential. The potential decreases in the direction of the field, therefore $V_B < V_A$. The work done by the field is $-q\,\Delta V$; the charge is negative and moves toward lower potentials, so its potential energy increases and the work done by the field is **negative**. The same result is obtained along the broken line $ACB$: on the segment $AC$ the force $q\vec{E}$ is opposite to the displacement and does the work $qE \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{m} = -4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J}$, on the segment $CB$ it is perpendicular and does no work, as predicted by the **zero circulation of the electrostatic field**. If the charge moves slowly, the kinetic energy does not change and the total work is zero, so the external agent does the work opposite to that of the field.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point charge $Q = 5.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is fixed. A charge $q = -2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ is brought slowly from the point $A$, at distance $r_A = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from $Q$, to the point $B$, at distance $r_B = 25\,\mathrm{cm}$. Compute the potentials at $A$ and at $B$, the work done by the electric force and the external work.

$$
V_A = \frac{kQ}{r_A} , \qquad V_B = \frac{kQ}{r_B} ,
\qquad
W_{A \to B} = q\,(V_A - V_B) = k Q q \left( \frac{1}{r_A} - \frac{1}{r_B} \right) ,
\qquad
W_{\text{ext}} = -W_{A \to B}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_A &= \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.10}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 450\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
V_B &= \frac{8.99 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-9}}{0.25}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 180\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
W_{A \to B} &= -2.0 \cdot 10^{-9} \cdot (449.5 - 179.8)\,\mathrm{J} \approx -5.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
W_{\text{ext}} &\approx +5.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The charge $Q$ is pointlike, so the potentials are computed with the **potential of a point charge**, with the reference at infinity. The work done by the field depends only on the initial and final potentials, as guaranteed by the **work in the field of a point charge**, and the path followed is not needed. The charges have opposite signs, so they attract each other; when $q$ is moved away from $Q$ the electric force is opposite to the radial displacement and does **negative** work. Equivalently, the negative charge moves toward lower potentials and its potential energy $qV$ increases. The displacement is slow, therefore the external agent supplies the positive work $5.4 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{J}$, which becomes potential energy of the system.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a region of space the electric field is directed along the $x$ axis and has component $E_x(x) = b\,x^2$, with $b = 6.0 \cdot 10^3\,\mathrm{V/m^3}$. Compute the potential difference $V(2.0\,\mathrm{m}) - V(0)$, the work done by the field on an electron that goes from $x = 0$ to $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and the minimum kinetic energy, in electronvolts, that the electron must have at $x = 0$ to reach $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
V(x_B) - V(x_A) = -\int_{x_A}^{x_B} E_x(x)\, dx = -\frac{b}{3}\left( x_B^3 - x_A^3 \right)
$$
$$
W = -q\,\Delta V = e\, \Delta V ,
\qquad
K_{\min} = -W = -e\, \Delta V
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta V &= -\frac{6.0 \cdot 10^3}{3} \cdot 2.0^3\,\mathrm{V} = -1.6 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
W &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot (-1.6 \cdot 10^4)\,\mathrm{J} \approx -2.6 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
K_{\min} &= 1.6 \cdot 10^4\,\mathrm{eV} = 16\,\mathrm{keV}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field varies with position, so the potential difference is computed with the integral of the **potential difference and field**. One integrates along the $x$ axis, where $d\vec{\ell} = dx\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{E} \cdot d\vec{\ell} = E_x\,dx$; an antiderivative of $b x^2$ is $\dfrac{b x^3}{3}$. The field points toward increasing $x$, therefore the potential **decreases** from $x = 0$ to $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The electron has charge $q = -e$ and moves toward lower potentials, so its potential energy increases and the field does negative work, that is, it slows the electron down. By the work-energy theorem the electron reaches $x = 2.0\,\mathrm{m}$ only if its initial kinetic energy is at least equal to the resistive work, $16\,\mathrm{keV}$; in electronvolts the result is read directly from $\Delta V$, because the charge is $e$.

</div>
