## Exercise 1

A solenoid of length $\ell = 40\,\mathrm{cm}$, with a radius much smaller than its length, consists of $N = 800$ turns carrying the current $I = 2.5\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the magnetic field inside the solenoid and the current needed for the field to equal $B' = 0.010\,\mathrm{T}$.

$$
n = \frac{N}{\ell} ,
\qquad
B = \mu_0\, n I ,
\qquad
I' = \frac{B'}{\mu_0\, n}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= \frac{800}{0.40\,\mathrm{m}} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} \\[6pt]
B &= 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{T} \approx 6.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
I' &= \frac{0.010}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 4.0\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solenoid is long compared with its radius, so far from the ends the theorem on the **field of an ideal solenoid** applies: the field inside is uniform, parallel to the axis, and depends only on the number of turns per unit length and on the current, not on the radius. With $2000$ turns per meter and $2.5\,\mathrm{A}$ the field is about $6.3\,\mathrm{mT}$, more than one hundred times the Earth's field. The field is proportional to the current, therefore to obtain $0.010\,\mathrm{T}$ the current must be increased by the ratio $\dfrac{0.010}{6.3 \cdot 10^{-3}} \approx 1.6$, that is brought to about $4.0\,\mathrm{A}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A toroid with rectangular cross section has inner radius $a = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, outer radius $b = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $h = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$; it consists of $N = 500$ turns carrying the current $I = 3.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the magnetic field at the inner edge and at the outer edge of the ring and the flux of the field through a cross section of the ring.

$$
B(r) = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r} , \qquad a < r < b
$$
$$
\Phi_B = \int_a^b \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r}\,h\,dr = \frac{\mu_0 N I h}{2\pi}\,\ln\frac{b}{a}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B(a) &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 500 \cdot 3.0}{0.10}\,\mathrm{T} = 3.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(b) &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 500 \cdot 3.0}{0.15}\,\mathrm{T} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\Phi_B &= 2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 500 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 0.020 \cdot \ln 1.5\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on the **field of a toroid**, inside the ring the field is tangent to the coaxial circles and inversely proportional to the distance $r$ from the axis; hence it is maximum at the inner edge and minimum at the outer one, with ratio $\dfrac{b}{a} = 1.5$.

A cross section of the ring is a rectangle with sides $b - a$ and $h$, perpendicular to the field lines. The field is not uniform over the cross section, therefore the flux is computed by dividing the rectangle into strips of width $dr$ and area $h\,dr$, on each of which $B$ is constant, and summing the contributions $B(r)\,h\,dr$. As a check, the average field over the cross section is $\dfrac{\Phi_B}{h\,(b - a)} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$, between the values at the two edges.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A long solenoid, of radius $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, has $n = 1.0 \cdot 10^{3}$ turns per meter carrying the current $I = 4.0\,\mathrm{A}$. An electron is near the axis, inside the solenoid, and has velocity $v = 2.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ perpendicular to the axis. Compute the radius of the trajectory of the electron and the period of its motion.

$$
B = \mu_0\, n I ,
\qquad
e v B = \frac{m_e v^2}{r}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r = \frac{m_e v}{e B} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m_e}{e B}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B &= 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{T} \approx 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
r &= \frac{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{6}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.03 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 7.1 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Inside the solenoid the field is uniform and parallel to the axis, by the theorem on the **field of an ideal solenoid**. The velocity of the electron is perpendicular to the field, so the Lorentz force has magnitude $evB$, is perpendicular to the velocity and does not change its magnitude; the electron therefore moves along a circle in a plane perpendicular to the axis, and the Lorentz force acts as the centripetal force. For the motion of a charge in a uniform field see the topic on the magnetic field and the Lorentz force.

The radius is about $2.3\,\mathrm{mm}$, so the trajectory, with a diameter of about $4.5\,\mathrm{mm}$, remains inside the solenoid, of radius $3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, and far from the turns; the hypothesis of a uniform field is satisfied along the whole trajectory. The period does not depend on the speed and is about $7.1\,\mathrm{ns}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Two long coaxial solenoids are one inside the other. The inner solenoid has $n_1 = 2.0 \cdot 10^{3}$ turns per meter carrying the current $I_1 = 1.5\,\mathrm{A}$; the outer solenoid, of larger radius, has $n_2 = 1.0 \cdot 10^{3}$ turns per meter carrying the current $I_2 = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the opposite sense. Compute the magnetic field inside the inner solenoid, in the region between the two solenoids and outside both.

$$
\vec{B} = \vec{B}_1 + \vec{B}_2 ,
\qquad
B_1 =
\begin{cases}
\mu_0\, n_1 I_1 & \text{inside solenoid } 1 \\[4pt]
0 & \text{outside}
\end{cases}
\qquad
B_2 =
\begin{cases}
\mu_0\, n_2 I_2 & \text{inside solenoid } 2 \\[4pt]
0 & \text{outside}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{\text{in}} &= \mu_0\,(n_1 I_1 - n_2 I_2) = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot (3000 - 2000)\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_{\text{between}} &= \mu_0\, n_2 I_2 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2000\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_{\text{out}} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the superposition principle the field is the sum of the fields of the two solenoids, and by the theorem on the **field of an ideal solenoid** each produces a uniform field $\mu_0 n I$ inside itself and zero outside. The currents circulate in opposite senses, so by the right-hand rule the two fields have opposite senses where both are present.

Inside the inner solenoid both fields act, and the resultant field has the magnitude of the difference, $1.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$, with the sense of the field of the inner solenoid, because $n_1 I_1 > n_2 I_2$. Between the two solenoids we are outside the inner solenoid and inside the outer one, therefore the field is only that of the outer solenoid, $2.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$, with sense **opposite** to that of the central region. Outside both the field is zero.

</div>
