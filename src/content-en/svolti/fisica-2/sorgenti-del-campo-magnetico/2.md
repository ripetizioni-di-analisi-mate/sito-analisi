## Exercise 1

A circular loop of radius $R = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the current $I = 4.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the magnitude of the magnetic field at the center of the loop and at the point on its axis at distance $z = 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center.

$$
B_0 = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R} ,
\qquad
B(z) = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2\,(R^2 + z^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}} = B_0\,\frac{R^3}{(R^2 + z^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_0 &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 4.0}{2 \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 5.03 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\sqrt{R^2 + z^2} &= \sqrt{5.0^2 + 12^2}\,\mathrm{cm} = 13\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
B(z) &= 5.03 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot \frac{5^3}{13^3}\,\mathrm{T} = 5.03 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot \frac{125}{2197}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.9 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We apply the theorem on the **field of a circular loop on its axis**. At the center the formula reduces to $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$, and the field is about $5.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$. Writing the field on the axis as $B_0$ times a dimensionless factor simplifies the calculation: with $R = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $z = 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ the distance of the point from the loop is $13\,\mathrm{cm}$, because $5$, $12$, $13$ form a Pythagorean triple. The factor equals $\dfrac{125}{2197} \approx 0.057$, so at $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the center the field is about $6\,\%$ of the field at the center.

As a check, for $z \gg R$ the field should approach $\dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2 z^3} \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T}$; here $z$ is only $2.4$ times $R$, and the exact value is still noticeably smaller.

</div>

$$
B_{\text{out}} = \frac{\mu_0 I \pi}{4\pi b} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4b} \ (\odot) ,
\qquad
B_{\text{in}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4a} \ (\otimes) ,
\qquad
B_{\text{rad}} = 0
$$

$$

B = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 6.0}{4} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{0.040} - \frac{1}{0.080} \right)\mathrm{T} = 1.885 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 12.5\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circuit consists of arcs centered at $C$ and of radial segments, so we apply the theorem on the **field at the center of a circular arc** to each semicircle, with $\varphi = \pi$. The straight segments lie on the diameter, that is they are aligned with $C$; for them $d\vec{l}$ is parallel to $\vec{r}$, and their contribution is zero.

The current travels the closed circuit in a single sense: if it flows counterclockwise on the outer arc, on the left radial segment it goes toward the center and on the inner arc it flows clockwise. By the right-hand rule the outer arc produces at $C$ a field out of the page and the inner arc a field into the page, and the two contributions subtract. The inner arc is closer to the center, therefore its contribution prevails: the field at $C$ is about $2.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$ and **points into the page**.

## Exercise 3

A very long wire is bent into a hairpin: two parallel semi-infinite straight segments are connected by a semicircle of radius $R = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the straight segments are tangent to the semicircle at the endpoints of its diameter. The wire carries the current $I = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the magnetic field at the center $C$ of the semicircle.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0.000,1.000 -0.087,0.996 -0.174,0.985 -0.259,0.966 -0.342,0.940 -0.423,0.906 -0.500,0.866 -0.574,0.819 -0.643,0.766 -0.707,0.707 -0.766,0.643 -0.819,0.574 -0.866,0.500 -0.906,0.423 -0.940,0.342 -0.966,0.259 -0.985,0.174 -0.996,0.087 -1.000,0.000 -0.996,-0.087 -0.985,-0.174 -0.966,-0.259 -0.940,-0.342 -0.906,-0.423 -0.866,-0.500 -0.819,-0.574 -0.766,-0.643 -0.707,-0.707 -0.643,-0.766 -0.574,-0.819 -0.500,-0.866 -0.423,-0.906 -0.342,-0.940 -0.259,-0.966 -0.174,-0.985 -0.087,-0.996 -0.000,-1.000;0,1 4.5,1;0,-1 4.5,-1;0.653,0.350 0.645,0.389 0.623,0.423 0.589,0.445 0.550,0.453 0.511,0.445 0.477,0.423 0.455,0.389 0.447,0.350 0.455,0.311 0.477,0.277 0.511,0.255 0.550,0.247 0.589,0.255 0.623,0.277 0.645,0.311 0.653,0.350" vettori="3.2,1,2.2,1,I;2.2,-1,3.2,-1;-1,0.15,-1,-0.25" punti="0.550,0.350,pieno;0,0,pieno,C" segmenti="0,0,0,1" etichette="0.12,0.45,R;0.8,0.2,B" xmin=-1.6 xmax=4.8 ymin=-1.6 ymax=1.6 titolo="Hairpin wire: the two semi-infinite segments and the semicircle produce at $C$ fields out of the page"}

$$
B_{\text{arc}} = \frac{\mu_0 I \pi}{4\pi R} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4R} ,
\qquad
B_{\text{top}} = B_{\text{bottom}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R}
$$
$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4R} + 2\,\frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4R} \left( 1 + \frac{2}{\pi} \right)
$$
$$
B = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 5.0}{4 \cdot 0.020} \cdot (1 + 0.637)\,\mathrm{T} = 7.85 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 1.637\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire is divided into three parts, and by the superposition principle the field at $C$ is the sum of their contributions. The semicircle has center $C$ and angle $\pi$, so by the theorem on the **field at the center of an arc** it gives $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4R}$. Each straight segment is a semi-infinite wire ending at the foot of the perpendicular drawn from $C$, because it is tangent to the semicircle at an endpoint of the diameter; by the theorem on the field of a straight wire its contribution is $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi R}$, half that of an infinite wire.

It remains to compare the senses. On the upper segment the current goes to the left and $C$ is below the wire; on the lower segment the current goes to the right and $C$ is above it; on the semicircle the current circulates counterclockwise. By the right-hand rule all three contributions **point out of the page**, so the magnitudes add up and the field is about $1.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$. Without the straight segments the field would be $61\,\%$ of this value.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Two coaxial circular coils, each of $N = 50$ turns of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, are placed at distance $R$ from each other and carry the current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the same sense (Helmholtz coils). Compute the magnetic field at the point on the axis equidistant from the two coils and compare it with the field at the center of a single coil.

$$
B = 2 \cdot \frac{N \mu_0 I R^2}{2\,\left( R^2 + \frac{R^2}{4} \right)^{\frac{3}{2}}} = \frac{N \mu_0 I}{R} \left( \frac{4}{5} \right)^{\frac{3}{2}} ,
\qquad
B_1 = \frac{N \mu_0 I}{2R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{N \mu_0 I}{R} &= \frac{50 \cdot 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0}{0.10}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.257 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B &= 1.257 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 0.7155\,\mathrm{T} \approx 9.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_1 &= \frac{1.257 \cdot 10^{-3}}{2}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 6.3 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equidistant point is on the axis at distance $z = \dfrac{R}{2}$ from each coil. By the theorem on the **field of a circular loop on its axis**, multiplied by the number of turns, each coil produces there a field directed along the axis; the currents have the same sense, so the two fields have the same sense and add up. The denominator contains $R^2 + \dfrac{R^2}{4} = \dfrac{5}{4} R^2$, and simplifying we obtain the factor $\left( \dfrac{4}{5} \right)^{\frac{3}{2}} \approx 0.716$.

The field at the midpoint is about $9.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{T}$, that is $1.43$ times the field at the center of a single coil. The distance $R$ between the coils is chosen so that the field near the midpoint is **nearly uniform**, and for this reason Helmholtz coils are used to produce uniform fields in the laboratory.

</div>
