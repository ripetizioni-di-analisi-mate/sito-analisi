## Exercise 1

A straight segment of wire of length $\ell = 40\,\mathrm{cm}$, part of a circuit whose rest is far away, carries the current $I = 10\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the magnetic field produced by the segment at the point $P$ located at distance $d = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from its midpoint, on the perpendicular to the segment, and compare it with the field of an infinite wire at the same distance.

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}\,(\sin\theta_2 - \sin\theta_1) ,
\qquad
\sin\theta_2 = -\sin\theta_1 = \frac{\frac{\ell}{2}}{\sqrt{\frac{\ell^2}{4} + d^2}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_2 &= \frac{0.20}{\sqrt{0.20^2 + 0.15^2}} = \frac{0.20}{0.25} = 0.80 \\[6pt]
B &= \frac{10^{-7} \cdot 10}{0.15} \cdot 2 \cdot 0.80\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.07 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_\infty &= \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 10}{0.15}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 1.33 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We apply the theorem on the **field of a straight segment**. The foot $H$ of the perpendicular from $P$ is the midpoint of the segment, so the endpoints are at $s_1 = -\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and $s_2 = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$, and the angles $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ are opposite. The triangle formed by $P$, $H$ and an endpoint has legs of $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and hypotenuse of $25\,\mathrm{cm}$, therefore $\sin\theta_2 = 0.80$.

The field equals $1.07 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$, that is $80\,\%$ of that of an infinite wire: the ratio is $\dfrac{2\sin\theta_2}{2} = \sin\theta_2$. A segment of wire can therefore be treated as infinite only when $\sin\theta_2$ is close to $1$, that is when the distance $d$ is small compared with the distance of the point from the endpoints.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A square loop of side $a = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the current $I = 3.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the magnetic field at the center of the loop and compare it with the field at the center of the circular loop inscribed in the square, carrying the same current.

$$
B_{\text{side}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi\,\frac{a}{2}} \cdot 2\sin 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,\mu_0 I}{2\pi a} ,
\qquad
B = 4 B_{\text{side}} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}\,\mu_0 I}{\pi a}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B &= \frac{2\sqrt{2} \cdot 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 3.0}{\pi \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{T} = 2\sqrt{2} \cdot 1.2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.4 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_{\circ} &= \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 \cdot \frac{a}{2}} = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 3.0}{0.10}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 3.8 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\frac{B}{B_{\circ}} &= \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\pi} \approx 0.90
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center of the square is at distance $\dfrac{a}{2}$ from each side, and the foot of the perpendicular is the midpoint of the side; the lines joining the center to the endpoints form angles of $45^\circ$ with the perpendicular. By the theorem on the **field of a straight segment** each side therefore produces the same field $B_{\text{side}}$. The current always circulates around the center in the same sense, therefore by the right-hand rule the four contributions have the same sense and are perpendicular to the plane of the loop, and the total field is four times that of one side.

The inscribed loop has radius $\dfrac{a}{2}$, and by the theorem on the field of a circular loop its field at the center is $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{a}$. The square produces $90\,\%$ of that field: its sides are longer than the inscribed circle, but its points, except the midpoints, are farther from the center.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular loop with sides $a = 8.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $b = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ carries the current $I = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Compute the magnetic field at the center of the loop.

$$
\delta = \sqrt{\frac{a^2}{4} + \frac{b^2}{4}} = \frac{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}{2} = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
B_a = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi\,\frac{b}{2}} \cdot 2\,\frac{\frac{a}{2}}{\delta} ,
\qquad
B_b = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi\,\frac{a}{2}} \cdot 2\,\frac{\frac{b}{2}}{\delta} ,
\qquad
B = 2 B_a + 2 B_b = \frac{2 \mu_0 I \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}{\pi a b}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_a &= \frac{10^{-7} \cdot 5.0}{0.030} \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{4.0}{5.0}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 2.67 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_b &= \frac{10^{-7} \cdot 5.0}{0.040} \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{3.0}{5.0}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.50 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B &= 2 \cdot (2.67 + 1.50) \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 8.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center of the rectangle is $\dfrac{b}{2} = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the two sides of length $a$ and $\dfrac{a}{2} = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the two sides of length $b$, while its distance $\delta$ from the vertices is half the diagonal, $5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. For each side the foot of the perpendicular is the midpoint, so in the theorem on the **field of a straight segment** the angles are opposite and $\sin\theta_2$ is the ratio of half the side to $\delta$.

The sides of length $a$ are closer to the center and give the larger contribution, $B_a$; the sides of length $b$ give $B_b$. All four contributions have the same sense, because the current circulates around the center in a single sense, and the total field is about $8.3 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$. As a check, for $a = b$ the final formula gives $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}\,\mu_0 I}{\pi a}$, that is the result for the square loop.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A very long wire is bent at a right angle at the point $O$: one semi-infinite segment lies on the negative $x$ semi-axis and carries the current $I = 8.0\,\mathrm{A}$ toward $O$, the other lies on the positive $y$ semi-axis and the current travels along it away from $O$. Compute the magnetic field at the point $P = (-a;\ a)$, with $a = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-4.3,0 0,0 0,4.3;-1.007,1.900 -1.014,1.936 -1.034,1.966 -1.064,1.986 -1.100,1.993 -1.136,1.986 -1.166,1.966 -1.186,1.936 -1.193,1.900 -1.186,1.864 -1.166,1.834 -1.136,1.814 -1.100,1.807 -1.064,1.814 -1.034,1.834 -1.014,1.864 -1.007,1.900" vettori="-3.4,0,-2.4,0,I;0,2.6,0,3.6,I" segmenti="-1.5,1.5,-1.5,0;-1.5,1.5,0,1.5" punti="-1.100,1.900,pieno;-1.5,1.5,pieno,P;0,0,pieno" etichette="-1.85,0.7,a;-0.85,1.62,a;0.15,-0.35,O;-0.85,2.0,B" xmin=-4.6 xmax=1.2 ymin=-0.7 ymax=4.4 titolo="Wire bent at a right angle: both semi-infinite segments produce at $P$ a field out of the page"}

$$
B_{\text{hor}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi a} \left( \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}\,a} - (-1) \right) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi a} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right) ,
\qquad
B_{\text{vert}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi a} \left( 1 - \frac{-a}{\sqrt{2}\,a} \right) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi a} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right)
$$
$$
B = B_{\text{hor}} + B_{\text{vert}} = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi a} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right)
$$
$$
B = \frac{2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 8.0}{0.050} \cdot 1.707\,\mathrm{T} = 3.2 \cdot 10^{-5} \cdot 1.707\,\mathrm{T} \approx 5.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field at $P$ is the sum of the contributions of the two segments, and to each we apply the theorem on the **field of a straight segment**, with the coordinate $s$ measured from the foot of the perpendicular in the sense of the current. For the horizontal segment the foot is $(-a;\ 0)$, the distance is $a$, and the segment goes from $s_1 \to -\infty$ to $s_2 = a$, that is up to $O$; therefore $\sin\theta_1 = -1$ and $\sin\theta_2 = \dfrac{a}{\sqrt{2}\,a}$. For the vertical segment the foot is $(0;\ a)$, the distance is again $a$, and the segment goes from $s_1 = -a$, that is from $O$, to $s_2 \to +\infty$.

The two contributions have the same magnitude, by the symmetry of the figure with respect to the bisector of the second quadrant. On the horizontal segment the current goes to the right and $P$ is above the wire; on the vertical segment the current goes upward and $P$ is to the left of the wire; by the right-hand rule both fields **point out of the page**. The total field is about $5.5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}$, about $85\,\%$ of the field $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{\pi a}$ of two infinite wires at the same distance with contributions in the same sense.

</div>
