## Exercise 1

A plane circular loop of radius $r = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is in a uniform magnetic field of magnitude $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$, which forms an angle $\theta = 60^\circ$ with the normal to the plane of the loop. Compute the flux of the field through the loop and the flux linked with a coil of $N = 50$ identical turns, in the same position.

$$
\Phi_B = B\,\pi r^2 \cos\theta ,
\qquad
\Phi_N = N\,\Phi_B
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_B &= 0.40 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.060^2 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
\Phi_N &= 50 \cdot 2.26 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 0.11\,\mathrm{Wb}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform and the loop is plane, so by the definition of **flux of the magnetic field** the flux is the product of the magnitude of the field, the area of the loop and the cosine of the angle between the field and the normal. The given angle is measured with respect to the normal, therefore the factor is $\cos 60^\circ = 0.50$; if the angle were measured with respect to the plane of the loop, the factor would be the sine. The flux linked with the coil is the sum of the fluxes through the $N$ turns, which are equal, and it is about $0.11\,\mathrm{Wb}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An infinite straight wire, carrying the current $I = 20\,\mathrm{A}$, lies in the plane of a rectangular loop. The sides of the loop parallel to the wire are $\ell = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ long and are at distances $a = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $b = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire. Compute the flux of the magnetic field of the wire through the loop.

$$
\Phi_B = \int_a^b \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}\,\ell\,dr = \frac{\mu_0 I \ell}{2\pi}\,\ln\frac{b}{a}
$$
$$
\Phi_B = 2 \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 20 \cdot 0.30 \cdot \ln 3\,\mathrm{Wb} = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 1.099\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on the **field of an infinite straight wire** the field of the wire is perpendicular to the plane containing the wire, that is to the plane of the loop, and its magnitude $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$ depends on the distance $r$. The field is not uniform over the loop, so the flux is computed with an integral: the loop is divided into strips parallel to the wire, of width $dr$ and area $\ell\,dr$, on each of which the distance from the wire, and hence the field, is constant. The result depends on the ratio $\dfrac{b}{a} = 3$ and not on the two distances separately.

As a check, the flux lies between the fluxes that a uniform field equal to the value at the far side and at the near side would give: with the area $\ell\,(b - a) = 0.030\,\mathrm{m^2}$ one obtains $8.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{Wb}$ and $2.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb}$, and the value found lies between the two.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A hemispherical surface of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ has its circular base in the $xy$ plane and lies in the region $z \geq 0$, in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = B\,\hat{k}$ with $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$. Compute the flux of the field through the hemispherical surface, oriented with the normal pointing outward.

::grafico{unicolore=1 assi=0 uguale=1 linee="2.000,0.000 1.992,0.174 1.970,0.347 1.932,0.518 1.879,0.684 1.813,0.845 1.732,1.000 1.638,1.147 1.532,1.286 1.414,1.414 1.286,1.532 1.147,1.638 1.000,1.732 0.845,1.813 0.684,1.879 0.518,1.932 0.347,1.970 0.174,1.992 0.000,2.000 -0.174,1.992 -0.347,1.970 -0.518,1.932 -0.684,1.879 -0.845,1.813 -1.000,1.732 -1.147,1.638 -1.286,1.532 -1.414,1.414 -1.532,1.286 -1.638,1.147 -1.732,1.000 -1.813,0.845 -1.879,0.684 -1.932,0.518 -1.970,0.347 -1.992,0.174 -2.000,0.000;-2,0 2,0" vettori="-2.6,-0.8,-2.6,0.9,B;-1.2,-0.8,-1.2,0.9;0,-0.8,0,0.9;1.2,-0.8,1.2,0.9;2.6,-0.8,2.6,0.9;1.41,1.41,1.98,1.98,n;0.4,0,0.4,-0.6,n" etichette="-0.45,2.25,S;-0.6,-0.95,disk" xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-1.3 ymax=2.6 titolo="The hemisphere and the base disk form a closed surface: the flux that enters through the disk leaves through the hemisphere"}

$$
\Phi_S + \Phi_{\text{disk}} = 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_{\text{disk}} = \vec{B} \cdot (-\hat{k})\,\pi R^2 = -B\pi R^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Phi_S = B\pi R^2
$$
$$
\Phi_S = 0.20 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.10^2\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 6.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The direct calculation would require a surface integral over the hemisphere, on which the angle between the field and the normal changes from point to point. It is more convenient to close the surface with the base disk: the hemisphere and the disk form a closed surface, and by **Gauss's law for the magnetic field** the total outward flux is zero. On the disk the outward normal is $-\hat{k}$, opposite to the field, so the flux through the disk is $-B\pi R^2$; therefore the outward flux through the hemisphere is $+B\pi R^2$, about $6.3 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Wb}$. The field lines that enter through the disk all leave through the hemisphere.

As a check one can compute the direct integral. In spherical coordinates, with $\vartheta$ the angle from the positive $z$ semi-axis, we have $\vec{B} \cdot \hat{n} = B\cos\vartheta$ and $dA = R^2 \sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta\,d\phi$, therefore
$$
\Phi_S = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} B\cos\vartheta\,R^2 \sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta\,d\phi = 2\pi B R^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = B\pi R^2 .
$$

</div>

## Exercise 4

A long solenoid, of radius $r_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, has $n = 3.0 \cdot 10^{3}$ turns per meter carrying the current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Around its central part a coaxial coil of $N_2 = 20$ circular turns of radius $r_2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is wound. Compute the flux of the field of the solenoid linked with the coil.

$$
\Phi_1 = \mu_0\, n I\,\pi r_1^2 ,
\qquad
\Phi_2 = N_2\,\Phi_1 = N_2\, \mu_0\, n I\,\pi r_1^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_0\, n I &= 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 2.0\,\mathrm{T} \approx 7.54 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\Phi_1 &= 7.54 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.020^2\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 9.47 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb} \\[6pt]
\Phi_2 &= 20 \cdot 9.47 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Wb} \approx 1.9 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Wb}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on the **field of an ideal solenoid** the field of the solenoid is uniform inside and zero outside. A turn of the coil encloses a disk of radius $r_2$, but the field is nonzero only in the inner disk of radius $r_1$, so the flux through the turn is the product of the field and the area $\pi r_1^2$ of the cross section of the solenoid. The flux linked with the coil is $N_2$ times the flux through one turn. The result does not depend on the radius of the coil, provided the coil surrounds the solenoid far from its ends; the flux linked between two circuits is the quantity that determines mutual inductance, for which see the topic on electromagnetic induction.

</div>
