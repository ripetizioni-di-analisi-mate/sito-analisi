## Exercise 1

A harmonic plane radio wave of frequency $f = 100\,\mathrm{MHz}$ propagates in vacuum, and the amplitude of its electric field is $E_0 = 0.30\,\mathrm{V/m}$. Compute the wavelength, the period, the angular frequency, the wave number and the amplitude of the magnetic field.

$$
\lambda = \frac{c}{f} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{f} ,
\qquad
\omega = 2\pi f ,
\qquad
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} ,
\qquad
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{1.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{m} = 3.00\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{1}{1.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\omega &= 2\pi \cdot 1.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 6.28 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
k &= \frac{2\pi}{3.00}\,\mathrm{rad/m} \approx 2.09\,\mathrm{rad/m} \\[6pt]
B_0 &= \frac{0.30}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In vacuum all electromagnetic waves propagate with speed $c$, and for a **harmonic wave** $c = \lambda f$, so the wavelength is the ratio of $c$ to the frequency. The period is the reciprocal of the frequency, the angular frequency is $2\pi f$ and the wave number is $\dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}$; as a check, $\dfrac{\omega}{k} = \dfrac{6.28 \cdot 10^{8}}{2.09}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which is exactly $c$. By the **relation between the electric field and the magnetic field** of a plane wave, the amplitudes satisfy $E_0 = c B_0$, hence the magnetic field has an amplitude of only $1.0\,\mathrm{nT}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A harmonic plane wave of green light, with wavelength $\lambda = 500\,\mathrm{nm}$, propagates in vacuum in the positive direction of the $x$ axis. Its electric field is directed along the $z$ axis and is
$$
\vec{E}(x,t) = E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)\,\hat{k} , \qquad E_0 = 6.0\,\mathrm{V/m} .
$$
Compute the frequency, angular frequency and wave number, and write the expression of the magnetic field $\vec{B}(x,t)$.

$$
f = \frac{c}{\lambda} ,
\qquad
\omega = 2\pi f ,
\qquad
k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} ,
\qquad
B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}
$$
$$
\hat{k} \times \left( -\hat{\jmath} \right) = \hat{\imath}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{B}(x,t) = -B_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{5.00 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{Hz} = 6.00 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
\omega &= 2\pi \cdot 6.00 \cdot 10^{14}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 3.77 \cdot 10^{15}\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
k &= \frac{2\pi}{5.00 \cdot 10^{-7}}\,\mathrm{rad/m} \approx 1.26 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{rad/m} \\[6pt]
B_0 &= \frac{6.0}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\mathrm{T} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\vec{B}(x,t) = -2.0 \cdot 10^{-8} \sin\left( 1.26 \cdot 10^{7}\, x - 3.77 \cdot 10^{15}\, t \right)\hat{\jmath}\ \mathrm{T}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Frequency, angular frequency and wave number follow from $c = \lambda f$ and from the definitions of the harmonic wave. For the magnetic field we use the **relation between the electric field and the magnetic field**: in a plane traveling wave $\vec{B}$ oscillates in phase with $\vec{E}$, has magnitude $\dfrac{E}{c}$, is perpendicular both to $\vec{E}$ and to the direction of propagation, and $\vec{E} \times \vec{B}$ points in the direction of propagation. The electric field is along $\hat{k}$ and the wave travels toward $\hat{\imath}$, so $\vec{B}$ is along the $y$ axis. Since $\hat{k} \times \hat{\jmath} = -\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{k} \times \left( -\hat{\jmath} \right) = \hat{\imath}$, when $E_z$ is positive the magnetic field must point toward $-\hat{\jmath}$; hence $B_y = -\dfrac{E_z}{c}$ and the magnetic field **points along** $-\hat{\jmath}$ when $\sin(kx - \omega t) > 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A harmonic plane wave propagates in vacuum in the positive direction of the $x$ axis, with the electric field directed along the $y$ axis. At the instant $t = 0$ the electric field is $E_y(x,0) = E_0 \sin(kx)$, with $E_0 = 12\,\mathrm{V/m}$, and two consecutive maxima are located at $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and at $x = 5.0\,\mathrm{m}$. Compute the wavelength and the frequency, the magnetic field vector at the point $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ at the instant $t = 0$, and the first instant $t > 0$ at which the electric field at that point vanishes.

::grafico{f="12*sin(pi*x/2)" punti="1,12,pieno;5,12,pieno" segmenti="1,14,5,14;1,0,1,12;5,0,5,12" etichette="2.85,14.6,λ" xmin=-0.5 xmax=9.3 ymin=-16 ymax=16 nomi="x,E" titolo="Electric field $E_y$ (in $\mathrm{V/m}$) as a function of $x$ (in meters) at the instant $t = 0$: two consecutive maxima are one wavelength apart"}

$$
\lambda = 5.0\,\mathrm{m} - 1.0\,\mathrm{m} = 4.0\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}{4.0}\,\mathrm{Hz} = 7.5 \cdot 10^{7}\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
$$
E_y(x,t) = E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t) ,
\qquad
B_z(x,t) = \frac{E_0}{c}\,\sin(kx - \omega t)
$$
$$
k \cdot 1.0\,\mathrm{m} = \frac{2\pi}{4.0} \cdot 1.0 = \frac{\pi}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{B}(1.0\,\mathrm{m}, 0) = \frac{E_0}{c}\,\hat{k} = \frac{12}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{T} = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\hat{k}\ \mathrm{T}
$$
$$
\frac{\pi}{2} - \omega t = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{\pi}{2\omega} = \frac{T}{4} = \frac{1}{4f} = \frac{1}{4 \cdot 7.5 \cdot 10^{7}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 3.3 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the instant $t = 0$ the field is periodic in space with period $\lambda$, so the distance between two consecutive maxima is the wavelength, $4.0\,\mathrm{m}$, and from $c = \lambda f$ it follows that $f = 75\,\mathrm{MHz}$. The wave travels in the positive direction of the $x$ axis, hence its expression is $E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)$, which for $t = 0$ gives back the given profile.

By the **relation between the electric field and the magnetic field**, with $\vec{E}$ along $\hat{\jmath}$ and propagation toward $\hat{\imath}$, the magnetic field is $B_z = \dfrac{E_y}{c}$ along $\hat{k}$. At the point $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$, at the instant $t = 0$, the argument is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the electric field is maximum; since the two fields are in phase, the magnetic field is also maximum and equals $4.0 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}$ in the direction of $\hat{k}$.

At the same point the electric field vanishes for the first time when the argument goes from $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to $0$, that is, after a quarter of a period, **about 3.3 ns**. The same result is obtained by observing that in that time the profile is translated by $c\,\dfrac{T}{4} = \dfrac{\lambda}{4} = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$, so the point of the profile that at $t = 0$ was at the origin, where the field is zero, arrives at $x = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>
