## Exercise 1

A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum, with circular plates of radius $R = 4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, is charged with a constant current $i = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Neglecting edge effects, compute the magnitude of the magnetic field in the region between the planes of the plates at the distances $r_1 = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $r_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis, the maximum value of the field, and the distance from the axis, greater than $R$, at which the field is equal to the field at $r_1$.

$$
B(r) = \frac{\mu_0\, i\, r}{2\pi R^2} \quad (r \le R) ,
\qquad
B(r) = \frac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi r} \quad (r \ge R)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B(r_1) &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 0.020}{2\pi \cdot 0.040^2}\,\mathrm{T} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(r_2) &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0}{2\pi \cdot 0.060}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 6.7 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_{\max} &= B(R) = \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0}{2\pi \cdot 0.040}\,\mathrm{T} = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{\mu_0\, i}{2\pi r_3} = \frac{\mu_0\, i\, r_1}{2\pi R^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r_3 = \frac{R^2}{r_1} = \frac{0.040^2}{0.020}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.080\,\mathrm{m}
$$

::grafico{f="min(2.5*x,40/x)+0*sqrt(x)" punti="2,5,pieno;6,6.667,pieno;4,10,pieno;8,5,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,5;8,0,8,5;2,5,8,5" xmin=-0.6 xmax=12.5 ymin=-0.8 ymax=12 nomi="r,B" titolo="Magnetic field $B$ (in $\mu\mathrm{T}$) as a function of the distance $r$ from the axis (in centimeters): maximum at the edge of the plates, at $r = 4\,\mathrm{cm}$, and equal values at $r_1 = 2\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $r_3 = 8\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The plates are circular and edge effects are negligible, so the electric field between the plates is uniform and the theorem on the **magnetic field between the plates of a capacitor** applies; it follows from the Ampère-Maxwell law applied to coaxial circles. The point $r_1$ is inside, where the electric flux through the circle is proportional to $r^2$ and the field grows linearly; the point $r_2$ is outside, where the circle encloses all the flux and the field is that of a wire carrying the current $i$.

The field grows up to $r = R$ and then decreases, so the maximum is at the edge of the plates and equals $10\,\mu\mathrm{T}$. Equating the outer expression to the value at $r_1$ gives $r_3 = \dfrac{R^2}{r_1}$, that is, **8.0 cm**, as the graph shows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Between two large flat plates, in a cylindrical region of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$, there is a uniform electric field parallel to the axis of the cylinder, which grows in time; outside the region the electric field is zero and there are no conduction currents. At distance $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis a magnetic field of magnitude $B = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T}$ is measured. Compute the rate of change of the electric field and the displacement current through the entire cross section of the region.

$$
2\pi r\, B = \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{d}{dt} \left( E\, \pi r^2 \right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{2B}{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, r}
$$
$$
i_s = \varepsilon_0\, \pi R^2\, \frac{dE}{dt}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dE}{dt} &= \frac{2 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-7}}{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)} \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)} \\[6pt]
i_s &= 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.10^2 \cdot 3.60 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.10\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The electric field has rotational symmetry about the axis, so the magnetic field is tangent to the coaxial circles and has constant magnitude on each of them. Apply the **Ampère-Maxwell law** to the circle of radius $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, inside the region, with the disk as the surface: there are no conduction currents and the electric flux is $E\, \pi r^2$, hence the circulation $2\pi r B$ is equal to $\mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \pi r^2 \dfrac{dE}{dt}$. Solving gives about $3.6 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)}$. A magnetic field of only $0.1\,\mu\mathrm{T}$ therefore requires an electric field that varies very rapidly, because the product $\mu_0 \varepsilon_0 = \dfrac{1}{c^2}$ is very small.

The displacement current through the entire cross section is $\varepsilon_0$ times the derivative of the total flux $E\, \pi R^2$, that is, **0.10 A**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has circular plates of radius $R = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at a distance $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, driven by the voltage $v(t) = V_0 \sin(\omega t)$ with $V_0 = 100\,\mathrm{V}$ and frequency $f = 1.0\,\mathrm{MHz}$. Neglecting edge effects, compute the amplitude of the magnetic field at the edge of the plates and at half the radius.

$$
E(t) = \frac{V_0}{d}\,\sin(\omega t)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{V_0\,\omega}{d}\,\cos(\omega t)
$$
$$
2\pi r\, B = \mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, \pi r^2\, \frac{dE}{dt}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B(r,t) = \frac{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0\, r\, V_0\, \omega}{2d}\,\cos(\omega t) \qquad (r \le R)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{V_0\,\omega}{d} &= \frac{100 \cdot 2\pi \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{6}}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)} \approx 3.14 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{V/(m\cdot s)} \\[6pt]
B_0(R) &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot 0.050}{2} \cdot 3.14 \cdot 10^{11}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 8.7 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B_0\!\left( \frac{R}{2} \right) &= \frac{B_0(R)}{2} \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wavelength corresponding to the frequency of $1\,\mathrm{MHz}$ is $\dfrac{c}{f} = 300\,\mathrm{m}$, much larger than the dimensions of the capacitor; so at every instant the field between the plates is uniform and equal to $\dfrac{v}{d}$, as in a charged capacitor in equilibrium. Its derivative oscillates as $\cos(\omega t)$ with amplitude $\dfrac{V_0\,\omega}{d}$.

The **Ampère-Maxwell law** applied to a coaxial circle of radius $r \le R$ gives a magnetic field proportional to $r$ and to the derivative of the electric field, hence also oscillating as $\cos(\omega t)$. At the edge the amplitude is about $87\,\mathrm{nT}$; at half the radius it is half of that, because inside the field grows linearly with the distance from the axis. As a check, the displacement current has amplitude $\dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \pi R^2 V_0\, \omega}{d} \approx 21.8\,\mathrm{mA}$, and the field of a wire carrying this current at the distance $R$ is $\dfrac{\mu_0 \cdot 21.8 \cdot 10^{-3}}{2\pi \cdot 0.050}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 8.7 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}$, the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum, with circular plates of radius $a = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ at a distance $d = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$, is charged to the voltage $V_0 = 200\,\mathrm{V}$ and at the instant $t = 0$ is connected to a resistor of resistance $R = 10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$, through which it discharges. Neglecting edge effects, compute the time constant of the discharge, the magnitude of the magnetic field between the plates at distance $r = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis at the instants $t = 0$ and $t = \tau$, and determine the direction of its field lines.

$$
C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 \pi a^2}{d} ,
\qquad
\tau = RC ,
\qquad
i(t) = \frac{V_0}{R}\, e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}
$$
$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0 \pi a^2}\,\frac{dq}{dt} = -\frac{i(t)}{\varepsilon_0 \pi a^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B(r,t) = \frac{\mu_0\, i(t)\, r}{2\pi a^2} \qquad (r \le a)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{8.85 \cdot 10^{-12} \cdot \pi \cdot 0.030^2}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 2.50 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{F} \\[6pt]
\tau &= 1.0 \cdot 10^{4} \cdot 2.50 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
i(0) &= \frac{200}{1.0 \cdot 10^{4}}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
B(r, 0) &= \frac{4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 0.020}{2\pi \cdot 0.030^2}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
B(r, \tau) &= B(r, 0)\, e^{-1} \approx 3.3 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{T}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the discharge of an RC circuit the charge and the current decrease exponentially with time constant $\tau = RC$, and the initial current is $\dfrac{V_0}{R}$; for these results see the topic on electric current and direct-current circuits. The capacitance of the parallel-plate capacitor is about $25\,\mathrm{pF}$, so the discharge takes place over times of the order of $0.25\,\mu\mathrm{s}$.

The field between the plates is $\dfrac{q}{\varepsilon_0 \pi a^2}$ and decreases, because the charge decreases. By the **Ampère-Maxwell law** on an inner coaxial circle, the magnitude of the magnetic field is given by the same formula derived for the charging capacitor, with the current $i(t)$ of the discharge, and therefore decreases as $e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}$: from about $89\,\mathrm{nT}$ at the initial instant to about $33\,\mathrm{nT}$ after one time constant.

The electric field decreases, so the displacement current points opposite to $\vec{E}$, that is, from the negative plate to the positive one, and it continues between the plates the current that in the external circuit goes from the positive plate, through the resistor, to the negative plate. The magnetic field lines circulate around the axis according to the right-hand rule with respect to the direction of the displacement current, hence they have the **opposite direction** to the one they had during charging.

</div>
