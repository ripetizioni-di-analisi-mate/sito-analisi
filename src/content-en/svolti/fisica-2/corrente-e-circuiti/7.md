## Exercise 1

An uncharged capacitor of capacitance $C = 50\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is connected in series with a resistor $R = 20\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and an ideal source with $\mathcal{E} = 9.0\,\mathrm{V}$. Compute the time constant, the time after which the charge has reached $90\,\%$ of the final value, and at that instant the charge and the current.

$$
\tau = RC ,
\qquad
q(t) = C\mathcal{E} \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \right) = 0.90\, C\mathcal{E}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} = 0.10
\;\Longrightarrow\;
t = \tau \ln 10 ,
\qquad
i(t) = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R}\, e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &= 20 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 50 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.0\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
t = 1.0 \cdot \ln 10\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.3\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
q &= 0.90 \cdot 50 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 9.0\,\mathrm{C} = 4.05 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{C} ,
\qquad
i = 0.10 \cdot \frac{9.0}{20 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{A} = 45\,\mu\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x>=0?1:NaN)*9*(1-exp(-x))" asintoti="y=9" punti="2.303,8.1,pieno" segmenti="2.303,0,2.303,8.1" xmin=-0.4 xmax=5.6 ymin=-0.8 ymax=10.5 nomi="t,V" legenda="\Delta V_C = \mathcal{E}\,\big( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \big)" titolo="Voltage across the capacitor, in volts, with $\mathcal{E} = 9.0\,\mathrm{V}$ and $\tau = 1.0\,\mathrm{s}$: $90\,\%$ of the final charge, that is, $8.1\,\mathrm{V}$, is reached at $t = \tau \ln 10 \approx 2.3\,\mathrm{s}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The circuit is the one in the theorem on **charging a capacitor**: ideal source, resistor and initially uncharged capacitor in series. The time constant is the product $RC$, with International System units, and equals one second. The $90\,\%$ condition is equivalent to requiring that the exponential term equal $0.10$, and taking logarithms gives $t = \tau \ln 10$.

The current at the same instant contains the same exponential factor, therefore it is $10\,\%$ of the initial current $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R} = 450\,\mu\mathrm{A}$. At that instant the voltage across the capacitor is $0.90\,\mathcal{E} = 8.1\,\mathrm{V}$, and $0.90\,\mathrm{V}$ remains across the resistor, in agreement with $Ri = 20 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 45 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{V}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A capacitor of capacitance $C = 2.0\,\mu\mathrm{F}$, charged to the voltage $\Delta V_0 = 100\,\mathrm{V}$, discharges through a resistor $R = 500\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. Compute the initial energy of the capacitor, the time after which the voltage has halved, the time after which the energy has halved and the total energy dissipated in the resistor.

$$
U_0 = \frac{1}{2} C\, \Delta V_0^2 ,
\qquad
\Delta V(t) = \Delta V_0\, e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} ,
\qquad
U(t) = \frac{1}{2} C\, \Delta V(t)^2 = U_0\, e^{-\frac{2t}{\tau}}
$$
$$
e^{-\frac{t_V}{\tau}} = \frac{1}{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
t_V = \tau \ln 2 ,
\qquad
e^{-\frac{2 t_U}{\tau}} = \frac{1}{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
t_U = \frac{\tau \ln 2}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &= 500 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s} = 1.0\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
U_0 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 100^2\,\mathrm{J} = 10\,\mathrm{mJ} \\[6pt]
t_V &\approx 0.69\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
t_U \approx 0.35\,\mathrm{s} ,
\qquad
W_{\text{diss}} = U_0 = 10\,\mathrm{mJ}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the theorem on **discharging a capacitor** the charge decreases exponentially with time constant $\tau = RC$, and with it the voltage $\Delta V = \dfrac{q}{C}$. The stored energy is proportional to the square of the voltage, therefore it decreases with twice the exponent and halves in half the time compared with the voltage: when the voltage has halved, the energy has dropped to one quarter.

During the discharge the resistor is the only component in which energy is converted, and at the end the capacitor is uncharged; hence by conservation of energy the resistor dissipates by the Joule effect all the initial energy, $10\,\mathrm{mJ}$, whatever the value of $R$. The same result is obtained by integrating $R\,i^2$ between $0$ and $+\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

To measure the capacitance of a capacitor, it is charged to $12\,\mathrm{V}$ and discharged through a resistor $R = 10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$; after $\Delta t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}$ the voltage across it is $3.0\,\mathrm{V}$. Compute the time constant and the capacitance.

$$
\Delta V(t) = \Delta V_0\, e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\tau = \frac{\Delta t}{\ln \dfrac{\Delta V_0}{\Delta V}} ,
\qquad
C = \frac{\tau}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &= \frac{4.0}{\ln 4}\,\mathrm{s} \approx \frac{4.0}{1.386}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.9\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
C &= \frac{2.885}{10 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{F} \approx 2.9 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{F} = 2.9 \cdot 10^{2}\,\mu\mathrm{F}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During the discharge the voltage follows the exponential law of the theorem on **discharging a capacitor**. Dividing the initial voltage by the measured one and taking logarithms gives $\dfrac{\Delta t}{\tau} = \ln \dfrac{\Delta V_0}{\Delta V} = \ln 4$, from which the time constant follows. The voltage has gone from $12\,\mathrm{V}$ to $3.0\,\mathrm{V}$, that is, it has halved twice, therefore $\Delta t$ equals two half-lives, $2\tau \ln 2$: this is the same result written in another form. With the resistance known, the capacitance is $\dfrac{\tau}{R}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

An ideal source with $\mathcal{E} = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ supplies, through a switch, the resistor $R_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ in series with the resistor $R_2 = 12\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$; an uncharged capacitor of capacitance $C = 100\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ is connected in parallel with $R_2$. At the instant $t = 0$ the switch is closed. Determine the voltage $v(t)$ across the capacitor, the time constant, the current delivered by the source immediately after closing and at steady state, and the voltage across the capacitor at $t = 0.30\,\mathrm{s}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,1.41;0,1.59 0,3;-0.36,1.59 0.36,1.59;-0.19,1.41 0.19,1.41;0,3 0.7,3 1.22,3.32;1.3,3 2,3;2,3 2.75,3 2.833,3.17 3,2.83 3.167,3.17 3.333,2.83 3.5,3.17 3.667,2.83 3.75,3 4.5,3;4.5,3 7,3;4.5,3 4.5,2 4.67,1.917 4.33,1.75 4.67,1.583 4.33,1.417 4.67,1.25 4.33,1.083 4.5,1 4.5,0;7,3 7,1.6;7,1.4 7,0;6.68,1.4 7.32,1.4;6.68,1.6 7.32,1.6;7,0 0,0" vettori="3.3,-0.35,2.3,-0.35;4.1,2.7,4.1,2" punti="0.7,3,vuoto;1.3,3,vuoto;4.5,3,pieno;4.5,0,pieno" etichette="0.45,1.69,+;0.45,1.09,−;-0.65,1.43,ℰ;0.9,3.55,T;3.03,3.38,R₁;4.8,1.43,R₂;7.47,1.43,C;2.7,-0.85,i₁;4.24,2.28,i₂" xmin=-1.4 xmax=8.2 ymin=-1.2 ymax=4.0 titolo="The capacitor is in parallel with $R_2$; after $T$ is closed it charges with time constant $\tau = \dfrac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}\, C$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
i_1 = i_2 + i_C \\[4pt]
\mathcal{E} = R_1 i_1 + v \\[4pt]
v = R_2 i_2 , \quad i_C = C\, \dfrac{dv}{dt}
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal{E} = R_1 \left( \frac{v}{R_2} + C\, \frac{dv}{dt} \right) + v
$$
$$
\frac{dv}{dt} + \frac{R_1 + R_2}{R_1 R_2 C}\, v = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_1 C}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
v(t) = V_\infty \left( 1 - e^{-\frac{t}{\tau}} \right) ,
\qquad
V_\infty = \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}\, \mathcal{E} ,
\qquad
\tau = \frac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}\, C
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_\infty &= \frac{12}{16} \cdot 12\,\mathrm{V} = 9.0\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
\tau = 3.0 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 100 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s} = 0.30\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
i_1(0) &= \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_1} = \frac{12}{4.0 \cdot 10^{3}}\,\mathrm{A} = 3.0\,\mathrm{mA} ,
\qquad
i_1(\infty) = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_1 + R_2} = 0.75\,\mathrm{mA} \\[6pt]
v(0.30\,\mathrm{s}) &= 9.0 \cdot \left( 1 - e^{-1} \right) \mathrm{V} \approx 5.7\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Denote by $i_1$ the current in $R_1$, by $i_2$ the one in $R_2$ and by $i_C$ the one charging the capacitor. At the node where $R_1$ connects to the parallel combination, the **junction rule** gives $i_1 = i_2 + i_C$; the loop formed by the source, $R_1$ and the capacitor gives $\mathcal{E} = R_1 i_1 + v$; the capacitor and $R_2$ are in parallel, therefore they have the same voltage $v$, and the current of the capacitor is the derivative of its charge $Cv$. Substituting gives a first-order linear equation in $v$, with coefficient $\dfrac{R_1 + R_2}{R_1 R_2 C} = \dfrac{1}{\tau}$, and the solution with $v(0) = 0$ has the same form as charging through a single resistor.

The time constant contains the parallel combination of $R_1$ and $R_2$, which equals $3.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$. At the initial instant the capacitor is uncharged and has zero voltage, therefore it behaves like a wire that short-circuits $R_2$, and the current is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R_1}$. At steady state no current flows in the branch of the capacitor, and by the remark on the **capacitor at steady state** the circuit reduces to the series of $R_1$ and $R_2$, with $v = V_\infty$ given by the voltage divider. At $t = \tau$ the voltage has reached $63\,\%$ of the final value.

</div>
