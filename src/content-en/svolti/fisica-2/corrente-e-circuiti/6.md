## Exercise 1

Two ideal sources, with $\mathcal{E}_1 = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ and $\mathcal{E}_2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{V}$, are in series with $R_1 = 4.0\,\Omega$ and $R_2 = 2.0\,\Omega$ respectively; the two branches are connected between the nodes $A$ and $B$ with the positive terminals toward $A$, and $R_3 = 2.0\,\Omega$ is connected between the same nodes. Compute the currents in the three branches.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,0.91;0,1.09 0,2;-0.36,1.09 0.36,1.09;-0.19,0.91 0.19,0.91;0,2 0,2.5 0.17,2.583 -0.17,2.75 0.17,2.917 -0.17,3.083 0.17,3.25 -0.17,3.417 0,3.5 0,4;3.5,0 3.5,1.5 3.67,1.583 3.33,1.75 3.67,1.917 3.33,2.083 3.67,2.25 3.33,2.417 3.5,2.5 3.5,4;7,0 7,0.91;7,1.09 7,2;6.64,1.09 7.36,1.09;6.81,0.91 7.19,0.91;7,2 7,2.5 7.17,2.583 6.83,2.75 7.17,2.917 6.83,3.083 7.17,3.25 6.83,3.417 7,3.5 7,4;0,4 7,4;0,0 7,0" vettori="0.8,4.35,1.9,4.35;6.2,4.35,5.1,4.35;3.05,3.6,3.05,2.8" punti="3.5,4,pieno,A;3.5,0,pieno,B" etichette="0.45,1.19,+;0.45,0.59,−;-0.8,0.93,ℰ₁;0.3,2.93,R₁;3.8,1.93,R₃;6.38,1.19,+;6.4,0.59,−;7.5,0.93,ℰ₂;6.44,2.93,R₂;1.23,4.57,I₁;5.53,4.57,I₂;3.19,3.13,I₃" xmin=-1.5 xmax=8.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=5.0 titolo="Two sources with the positive terminals toward node $A$; the directions chosen for the currents are shown by the arrows"}

$$
\begin{cases}
I_1 + I_2 = I_3 \\[4pt]
\mathcal{E}_1 = R_1 I_1 + R_3 I_3 \\[4pt]
\mathcal{E}_2 = R_2 I_2 + R_3 I_3
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
I_1 + I_2 - I_3 = 0 \\[4pt]
4 I_1 + 2 I_3 = 12 \\[4pt]
2 I_2 + 2 I_3 = 6
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1 &= 3 - \frac{1}{2}\, I_3 ,
\qquad
I_2 = 3 - I_3 \\[6pt]
\left( 3 - \frac{1}{2}\, I_3 \right) + (3 - I_3) &= I_3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I_3 = \frac{12}{5} = 2.4\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
I_1 &= 1.8\,\mathrm{A} ,
\qquad
I_2 = 0.60\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circuit has two nodes and three branches, therefore one junction equation and two loop equations are needed. The direction of each current is chosen: $I_1$ and $I_2$ toward $A$ in the branches of the sources, $I_3$ from $A$ to $B$. By the **junction rule**, $I_1$ and $I_2$ enter $A$ and $I_3$ leaves it.

The first loop contains $\mathcal{E}_1$, $R_1$ and $R_3$; going around it in the direction of $I_1$, the source is traversed from the negative to the positive terminal, and $R_1$ and $R_3$ are traversed in the direction of their respective currents, hence by the **loop rule** $\mathcal{E}_1 = R_1 I_1 + R_3 I_3$. The second loop, with $\mathcal{E}_2$, $R_2$ and $R_3$, is analogous. The last two equations give $I_1$ and $I_2$ as functions of $I_3$, and substituting into the junction equation gives $I_3$.

The three currents are positive, therefore they flow in the chosen directions, and both sources deliver power. As a check, the voltage between $A$ and $B$ equals $R_3 I_3 = 4.8\,\mathrm{V}$, and it coincides with $\mathcal{E}_1 - R_1 I_1$ and with $\mathcal{E}_2 - R_2 I_2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal source with $\mathcal{E}_1 = 24\,\mathrm{V}$ in series with $R_1 = 2.0\,\Omega$ and an ideal source with $\mathcal{E}_2 = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ in series with $R_2 = 1.0\,\Omega$ are connected between the nodes $A$ and $B$ with the positive terminals toward $A$; $R_3 = 6.0\,\Omega$ is connected between the same nodes. Compute the currents in the three branches and verify the power balance.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 0,0.91;0,1.09 0,2;-0.36,1.09 0.36,1.09;-0.19,0.91 0.19,0.91;0,2 0,2.5 0.17,2.583 -0.17,2.75 0.17,2.917 -0.17,3.083 0.17,3.25 -0.17,3.417 0,3.5 0,4;3.5,0 3.5,1.5 3.67,1.583 3.33,1.75 3.67,1.917 3.33,2.083 3.67,2.25 3.33,2.417 3.5,2.5 3.5,4;7,0 7,0.91;7,1.09 7,2;6.64,1.09 7.36,1.09;6.81,0.91 7.19,0.91;7,2 7,2.5 7.17,2.583 6.83,2.75 7.17,2.917 6.83,3.083 7.17,3.25 6.83,3.417 7,3.5 7,4;0,4 7,4;0,0 7,0" vettori="0.8,4.35,1.9,4.35;6.2,4.35,5.1,4.35;3.05,3.6,3.05,2.8" punti="3.5,4,pieno,A;3.5,0,pieno,B" etichette="0.45,1.19,+;0.45,0.59,−;-0.8,0.93,ℰ₁;0.3,2.93,R₁;3.8,1.93,R₃;6.38,1.19,+;6.4,0.59,−;7.5,0.93,ℰ₂;6.44,2.93,R₂;1.23,4.57,I₁;5.53,4.57,I₂;3.19,3.13,I₃" xmin=-1.5 xmax=8.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=5.0 titolo="With $\mathcal{E}_1 = 24\,\mathrm{V}$ and $\mathcal{E}_2 = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ the current $I_2$ turns out negative: in the branch of $\mathcal{E}_2$ the current flows from $A$ to $B$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
I_1 + I_2 = I_3 \\[4pt]
\mathcal{E}_1 = R_1 I_1 + R_3 I_3 \\[4pt]
\mathcal{E}_2 = R_2 I_2 + R_3 I_3
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
I_1 = 12 - 3 I_3 \\[4pt]
I_2 = 12 - 6 I_3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(12 - 3 I_3) + (12 - 6 I_3) &= I_3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I_3 = 2.4\,\mathrm{A} ,
\qquad
I_1 = 4.8\,\mathrm{A} ,
\qquad
I_2 = -2.4\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E}_1 I_1 &= 24 \cdot 4.8\,\mathrm{W} = 115.2\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
R_1 I_1^2 + R_2 I_2^2 + R_3 I_3^2 + \mathcal{E}_2\,|I_2| &= (46.08 + 5.76 + 34.56 + 28.8)\,\mathrm{W} = 115.2\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The currents $I_1$ and $I_2$ are chosen toward $A$ in the branches of the sources and $I_3$ from $A$ to $B$; the **junction rule** at $A$ and the **loop rule** for the two loops containing $R_3$ give the system. From the two loop equations $I_1$ and $I_2$ are obtained as functions of $I_3$, and the junction equation gives $I_3$. The current $I_2$ turns out **negative**, therefore in the branch of $\mathcal{E}_2$ the current flows from $A$ to $B$ and enters the source at the positive terminal. Source 2 absorbs the power $\mathcal{E}_2\,|I_2|$, like a battery being charged, because the voltage between $A$ and $B$, $R_3 I_3 = 14.4\,\mathrm{V}$, exceeds its electromotive force.

By the power balance, the power delivered by source 1 must equal the sum of the powers dissipated in the three resistors by the Joule effect and of the power absorbed by source 2. The two sums coincide, and the check confirms the currents found.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a Wheatstone bridge the resistor $R_1 = 150\,\Omega$ connects the node $A$ to the node $C$, the resistor $R_2 = 300\,\Omega$ connects $C$ to the node $B$, the resistor $R_3 = 220\,\Omega$ connects $A$ to the node $D$ and the unknown resistor $R_x$ connects $D$ to $B$. An ideal source with $\mathcal{E} = 12\,\mathrm{V}$ is connected between $A$ and $B$, and a galvanometer connected between $C$ and $D$ shows no current. Compute $R_x$ and the currents in the two branches $ACB$ and $ADB$.

$$
I_G = 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
V_C = V_D
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\begin{cases}
R_1 I_{ACB} = R_3 I_{ADB} \\[4pt]
R_2 I_{ACB} = R_x I_{ADB}
\end{cases}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \frac{R_3}{R_x}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
R_x = \frac{R_2 R_3}{R_1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= \frac{300 \cdot 220}{150}\,\Omega = 440\,\Omega \\[6pt]
I_{ACB} &= \frac{12}{150 + 300}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 26.7\,\mathrm{mA} ,
\qquad
I_{ADB} = \frac{12}{220 + 440}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 18.2\,\mathrm{mA}
\end{aligned}

<div class="spiegazione">

No current flows through the galvanometer, therefore the voltage across it is zero and the nodes $C$ and $D$ are at the same potential. By the **junction rule** at $C$ and at $D$, with no current in the galvanometer, $R_1$ and $R_2$ carry the same current $I_{ACB}$, and $R_3$ and $R_x$ the same current $I_{ADB}$. Since $V_C = V_D$, the voltage drop from $A$ to $C$ equals that from $A$ to $D$, and the drop from $C$ to $B$ equals that from $D$ to $B$: the two conditions hold together, and dividing side by side the currents cancel.

The balance condition $R_1 R_x = R_2 R_3$ does not depend on $\mathcal{E}$; for this reason the bridge makes it possible to measure a resistance by comparing it with known resistances, without knowing the supply voltage precisely. The currents in the branches follow from Ohm's law, because each branch is a series connected directly to the terminals of the source.

</div>

## Exercise 4

In the bridge formed by the resistors $R_1 = 3.0\,\Omega$ between $M$ and $C$, $R_2 = 2.0\,\Omega$ between $C$ and $N$, $R_3 = 6.0\,\Omega$ between $M$ and $D$ and $R_4 = 2.0\,\Omega$ between $D$ and $N$, supplied between $M$ and $N$ by an ideal source with $\mathcal{E} = 12\,\mathrm{V}$, an ideal ammeter is connected between $C$ and $D$. Compute the current read by the ammeter and its direction.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="1,4 1.75,4 1.833,4.17 2,3.83 2.167,4.17 2.333,3.83 2.5,4.17 2.667,3.83 2.75,4 3.5,4;3.5,4 4.25,4 4.333,4.17 4.5,3.83 4.667,4.17 4.833,3.83 5,4.17 5.167,3.83 5.25,4 6,4;1,0 1.75,0 1.833,0.17 2,-0.17 2.167,0.17 2.333,-0.17 2.5,0.17 2.667,-0.17 2.75,0 3.5,0;3.5,0 4.25,0 4.333,0.17 4.5,-0.17 4.667,0.17 4.833,-0.17 5,0.17 5.167,-0.17 5.25,0 6,0;1,0 1,4;6,0 6,4;3.5,4 3.5,2.28;3.5,1.72 3.5,0;3.78,2 3.77,2.072 3.742,2.14 3.698,2.198 3.64,2.242 3.572,2.27 3.5,2.28 3.428,2.27 3.36,2.242 3.302,2.198 3.258,2.14 3.23,2.072 3.22,2 3.23,1.928 3.258,1.86 3.302,1.802 3.36,1.758 3.428,1.73 3.5,1.72 3.572,1.73 3.64,1.758 3.698,1.802 3.742,1.86 3.77,1.928 3.78,2;1,2 -0.3,2 -0.3,-1.4 2.5,-1.4;2.5,-1.4 3.41,-1.4;3.59,-1.4 4.5,-1.4;3.41,-1.76 3.41,-1.04;3.59,-1.59 3.59,-1.21;4.5,-1.4 7.3,-1.4 7.3,2 6,2" vettori="3.9,3,3.9,2.4" punti="1,2,pieno,M;6,2,pieno,N;3.5,4,pieno,C;3.5,0,pieno,D" etichette="2.03,4.38,R₁;4.53,4.38,R₂;2.03,-0.56,R₃;4.53,-0.56,R₄;3.4,1.9,A;3.11,-0.98,+;3.67,-0.98,−;3.38,-2.2,ℰ" xmin=-0.9 xmax=8.0 ymin=-2.4 ymax=4.8 titolo="Unbalanced bridge with an ideal ammeter between $C$ and $D$: the current in the ammeter goes from $C$ to $D$"}

$$
V_C = V_D
\;\Longrightarrow\;
R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{R_1 R_3}{R_1 + R_3} + \frac{R_2 R_4}{R_2 + R_4} ,
\qquad
I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R_{\text{eq}}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\text{eq}} &= (2.0 + 1.0)\,\Omega = 3.0\,\Omega ,
\qquad
I = 4.0\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_{MC} &= 2.0 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{V} = 8.0\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
I_1 = \frac{8.0}{3.0}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 2.67\,\mathrm{A} ,
\qquad
I_3 = \frac{8.0}{6.0}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 1.33\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_{CN} &= 1.0 \cdot 4.0\,\mathrm{V} = 4.0\,\mathrm{V} ,
\qquad
I_2 = I_4 = \frac{4.0}{2.0}\,\mathrm{A} = 2.0\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
I_A &= I_1 - I_2 = \frac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.67\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ideal ammeter has zero resistance, therefore it connects $C$ and $D$ like a wire and the two nodes are at the same potential. Then $R_1$ and $R_3$ have the same voltage across them and are **in parallel**, and the same holds for $R_2$ and $R_4$; the two parallel combinations are in series, and the network reduces to $3.0\,\Omega$. The source voltage is split into $8.0\,\mathrm{V}$ across the first parallel combination and $4.0\,\mathrm{V}$ across the second, and Ohm's law gives the currents in the four resistors.

The current $I_1$ from $R_1$ enters node $C$ and the current $I_2$ toward $R_2$ leaves it; since $I_1 > I_2$, by the **junction rule** the difference $I_1 - I_2$ leaves $C$ through the ammeter, which therefore carries $0.67\,\mathrm{A}$ **from $C$ to $D$**. At node $D$ the ammeter current adds to $I_3$ and gives $I_4 = 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$, in agreement with the direct calculation. The bridge is not balanced, since $R_1 R_4 = 6.0\,\Omega^2$ is different from $R_2 R_3 = 12\,\Omega^2$.

</div>
