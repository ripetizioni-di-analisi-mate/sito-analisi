## Exercise 1

A straight wire of length $\ell = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, placed along the $x$ axis, carries the current $I = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in the positive sense of the axis. The magnetic field is uniform, equals $B = 0.25\,\mathrm{T}$ and lies in the $xy$ plane, forming an angle of $30^\circ$ with the $x$ axis. Compute the force on the wire and the maximum value the force would take by rotating the wire in the $xy$ plane.

$$
\vec{\ell} = \ell\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
\vec{B} = B \left( \cos 30^\circ\,\hat{\imath} + \sin 30^\circ\,\hat{\jmath} \right) ,
\qquad
\vec{F} = I\,\vec{\ell} \times \vec{B} = I \ell B \sin 30^\circ\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 5.0 \cdot 0.80 \cdot 0.25 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} = 0.50\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F_{\max} &= I \ell B = 5.0 \cdot 0.80 \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{N} = 1.0\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is uniform and the wire is straight, so the **force on a straight wire** applies. In the cross product the component of the field along the wire gives zero contribution, because $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{\imath} = \vec{0}$, and only the component $B\sin 30^\circ\,\hat{\jmath}$ remains; since $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{\jmath} = \hat{k}$, the force is directed along the $z$ axis, perpendicular both to the wire and to the field, and equals $0.50\,\mathrm{N}$. The force is maximum when the wire is perpendicular to the field, that is after a rotation of the wire by $60^\circ$, and it then equals $1.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal conducting rod, of length $\ell = 0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ and mass $m = 50\,\mathrm{g}$, hangs from two flexible insulating vertical threads, in a uniform horizontal magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to the rod. Compute the current that must flow in the rod for the tension in the threads to vanish. Then compute the total tension in the threads when the current of $1.5\,\mathrm{A}$ flows in the rod in the opposite sense.

$$
I \ell B - m g = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{m g}{\ell B} ,
\qquad
T_1 + T_2 = m g + I' \ell B
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{0.050 \cdot 9.81}{0.40 \cdot 0.50}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 2.5\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
T_1 + T_2 &= \left( 0.050 \cdot 9.81 + 1.5 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 0.50 \right)\mathrm{N} = (0.49 + 0.30)\,\mathrm{N} \approx 0.79\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is perpendicular to the field, so by the **force on a straight wire** the magnetic force has magnitude $I \ell B$ and is perpendicular both to the rod and to the field; since the field is horizontal, the force is vertical. The tension in the threads vanishes when the magnetic force is directed upward and balances the weight, that is for $I \approx 2.5\,\mathrm{A}$. The correct sense of the current is found with the right-hand rule: with the rod along $\hat{\imath}$ and the field into the page, $-B\,\hat{k}$, the current in the sense of $\hat{\imath}$ gives $\hat{\imath} \times (-\hat{k}) = \hat{\jmath}$, that is an upward force.

Reversing the current reverses the force, which becomes directed downward and adds to the weight. The rod is in equilibrium, therefore the sum of the tensions in the two threads balances the weight and the magnetic force, and it equals about $0.79\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A conducting rod of mass $m = 0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ and length $\ell = 0.30\,\mathrm{m}$ rests perpendicularly on two smooth parallel rails, inclined at $\alpha = 20^\circ$ to the horizontal, in a uniform vertical magnetic field, pointing upward, of magnitude $B = 0.60\,\mathrm{T}$. Compute the current that must flow in the rod for it to remain in equilibrium, its sense, and the total normal force of the rails.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,2.184" vettori="2.959,1.205,2.959,-1.738,mg;2.959,1.205,1.888,4.148,N;2.959,1.205,4.03,1.205,F;0.6,1.6,0.6,3.4,B" punti="2.959,1.205,pieno" etichette="1.3,0.18,α;2.209,1.005,⊗ I" xmin=-0.4 xmax=6.6 ymin=-2.2 ymax=4.6 titolo="Cross section of the rails: the rod, carrying a current into the page, is in equilibrium under the weight, the normal force $\vec{N}$ and the horizontal magnetic force $\vec{F}$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
I \ell B \cos\alpha - m g \sin\alpha = 0 \\[4pt]
N - m g \cos\alpha - I \ell B \sin\alpha = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{m g \tan\alpha}{\ell B} ,
\qquad
N = \frac{m g}{\cos\alpha}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{0.10 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.364}{0.30 \cdot 0.60}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
N &= \frac{0.10 \cdot 9.81}{0.940}\,\mathrm{N} \approx 1.0\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is vertical and the rod is horizontal, so by the **force on a straight wire** the magnetic force has magnitude $I \ell B$ and is horizontal, perpendicular to the rod. For equilibrium it must have a component along the incline pointing upward, which balances the component of the weight $mg\sin\alpha$; in the cross section of the drawing it must therefore be directed toward the incline, that is to the right. With $\vec{B}$ upward, a current into the page, $-\hat{k}$, gives $(-\hat{k}) \times \hat{\jmath} = \hat{\imath}$, that is a force to the right: the current must be **into** the page.

We decompose the forces along the incline and perpendicular to it. Along the incline the magnetic force contributes $I \ell B\cos\alpha$, and from the first equation it follows that $I \approx 2.0\,\mathrm{A}$. Perpendicular to the incline the magnetic force presses the rod against the rails with the component $I \ell B\sin\alpha$, which adds to $mg\cos\alpha$; substituting the current, $N = mg\cos\alpha + mg \dfrac{\sin^2\alpha}{\cos\alpha} = \dfrac{mg}{\cos\alpha} \approx 1.0\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A wire in the shape of a semicircle of radius $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ lies in the $xy$ plane, with its center at the origin and its endpoints at $P = (R, 0)$ and $Q = (-R, 0)$, and passes through the points with $y > 0$. It carries the current $I = 4.0\,\mathrm{A}$ from $P$ to $Q$, in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ directed into the page. Compute the force on the wire by direct integration, check the result with the force on a wire of arbitrary shape, and compute the total force on the closed loop obtained by adding the diameter from $Q$ to $P$.

$$
\vec{r}(\phi) = R \left( \cos\phi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\phi\,\hat{\jmath} \right) , \quad \phi \in [0, \pi] ,
\qquad
d\vec{\ell} = R \left( -\sin\phi\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\phi\,\hat{\jmath} \right) d\phi
$$
$$
d\vec{\ell} \times \left( -B\,\hat{k} \right) = -R B \left( -\sin\phi\,\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k} + \cos\phi\,\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{k} \right) d\phi = -R B \left( \cos\phi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\phi\,\hat{\jmath} \right) d\phi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{F}_{\text{semi}} &= -I R B \int_0^\pi \left( \cos\phi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\phi\,\hat{\jmath} \right) d\phi = -I R B \left( 0\,\hat{\imath} + 2\,\hat{\jmath} \right) = -2 I R B\,\hat{\jmath} \\[6pt]
\vec{F}_{\text{semi}} &= I\,\overrightarrow{PQ} \times \vec{B} = I\,(-2R\,\hat{\imath}) \times (-B\,\hat{k}) = 2 I R B\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k}) = -2 I R B\,\hat{\jmath} \\[6pt]
\vec{F}_{\text{diam}} &= I\,(2R\,\hat{\imath}) \times (-B\,\hat{k}) = 2 I R B\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{F}_{\text{semi}} + \vec{F}_{\text{diam}} = \vec{0}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F_{\text{semi}} = 2 \cdot 4.0 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N} = 0.40\,\mathrm{N}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **force on a wire of arbitrary shape** the force is the integral of $I\,d\vec{\ell} \times \vec{B}$ along the wire. We parametrize the semicircle by the angle $\phi$, which goes from $0$ at $P$ to $\pi$ at $Q$, and the infinitesimal displacement is the derivative of $\vec{r}$ times $d\phi$. In the cross product we use $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k} = -\hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{k} = \hat{\imath}$. The integral of $\cos\phi$ over $[0, \pi]$ is zero, because the horizontal contributions of the two quarter circles cancel, while the integral of $\sin\phi$ equals $2$; the force is therefore directed downward and has magnitude $2IRB = 0.40\,\mathrm{N}$.

The field is uniform, therefore the force coincides with the force on the straight segment from $P$ to $Q$, of length $2R$, and the second calculation confirms the result. The diameter from $Q$ to $P$ carries the current in the opposite sense and receives the opposite force, so the total force on the closed loop is **zero**, as expected for every loop in a uniform field.

</div>
