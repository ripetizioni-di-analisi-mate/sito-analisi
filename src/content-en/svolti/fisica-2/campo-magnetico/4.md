## Exercise 1

An electron enters a uniform magnetic field $B = 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T}$ with velocity $v = 5.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ forming an angle $\varphi = 30^\circ$ with the direction of the field. Compute the radius of the helix described by the electron, the period and the pitch.

$$
v_\perp = v \sin\varphi ,
\qquad
v_\parallel = v \cos\varphi ,
\qquad
r = \frac{m_e v_\perp}{e B} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi m_e}{e B} ,
\qquad
p = v_\parallel T
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_\perp &= 5.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{m/s} = 2.5 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s} ,
\qquad
v_\parallel = 5.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.866\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 4.33 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
r &= \frac{9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^6}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 3.6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
T &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 4.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 8.9 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
p &= 4.33 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 8.94 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 3.9 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity forms an angle different from $0$ and from $90^\circ$ with the field, so by **helical motion** it is decomposed into a component parallel and a component perpendicular to $\vec{B}$. The perpendicular component determines the circular motion around the field line, with radius about $3.6\,\mathrm{mm}$; the period does not depend on the speed and is the same as that of an electron with velocity perpendicular to the field.

The parallel component remains constant, because the magnetic force has no component along the field, so in each period the electron advances along the axis by $v_\parallel T$, about $3.9\,\mathrm{cm}$. The pitch is more than ten times the radius, and the helix turns out to be very elongated.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A proton moves with speed $v = 1.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and enters perpendicularly a strip of width $d = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in which there is a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.10\,\mathrm{T}$, directed into the page and parallel to the edges of the strip. Compute the angle by which the proton is deflected when it leaves the strip and the lateral displacement of the exit point with respect to the direction of entry. The mass of the proton is $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,-2 0,5.6;5,-2 5,5.6;0,0 0.13,0.001 0.261,0.003 0.391,0.007 0.521,0.013 0.651,0.02 0.781,0.029 0.911,0.04 1.041,0.052 1.171,0.066 1.3,0.081 1.429,0.098 1.558,0.117 1.687,0.137 1.816,0.159 1.944,0.183 2.072,0.208 2.199,0.234 2.327,0.263 2.454,0.292 2.58,0.324 2.706,0.357 2.832,0.391 2.957,0.428 3.082,0.465 3.206,0.505 3.33,0.545 3.453,0.588 3.576,0.632 3.698,0.677 3.82,0.724 3.941,0.772 4.061,0.822 4.181,0.874 4.3,0.927 4.419,0.981 4.536,1.037 4.653,1.095 4.77,1.153 4.885,1.214 5,1.275;5,1.275 8.95,3.431" segmenti="-3,0,9.5,0;5,1.275,9.5,1.275" vettori="-2.8,0,-0.8,0,v" punti="0,0,pieno;5,1.275,pieno" etichette="6.7,1.5,α;2.3,-1.85,d;0.83,-1.17,⊗;0.83,2.43,⊗;0.83,4.03,⊗;2.33,-1.17,⊗;2.33,2.43,⊗;2.33,4.03,⊗;3.83,-1.17,⊗;3.83,2.43,⊗;3.83,4.03,⊗" xmin=-3.2 xmax=9.8 ymin=-2.2 ymax=5.8 titolo="The proton enters perpendicularly the field strip of width $d$, travels along a circular arc and leaves deflected by the angle $\alpha$"}

$$
r = \frac{m_p v}{e B} ,
\qquad
\sin\alpha = \frac{d}{r} ,
\qquad
y = r\,(1 - \cos\alpha)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \frac{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^6}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 0.10}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.104\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\sin\alpha &= \frac{0.050}{0.104} \approx 0.479
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha \approx 29^\circ \\[6pt]
y &= 0.104 \cdot (1 - 0.878)\,\mathrm{m} \approx 1.3 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Inside the strip, by **circular motion in a uniform magnetic field**, the proton travels along a circular arc of radius $r \approx 10.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The radius is greater than the width of the strip, so the proton crosses the strip and leaves from the opposite edge. The proton enters perpendicularly to the edge, therefore the center of the circle lies on the line of the entry edge, at distance $r$ from the entry point; in the drawing it is above the entry point, because the initial force $e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ is directed upward.

At the exit the radius going from the center to the exit point has rotated by the angle $\alpha$, and the velocity, perpendicular to the radius, has rotated by the same angle. The horizontal distance traveled is $d = r\sin\alpha$, from which $\alpha \approx 29^\circ$, and the height of the exit point with respect to the line of entry is $r - r\cos\alpha$, about $1.3\,\mathrm{cm}$. After leaving, the proton continues in a straight line with the deflected velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An electron with kinetic energy $K = 2.0\,\mathrm{keV}$ enters perpendicularly a strip of width $d = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ in which there is a uniform magnetic field parallel to the edges. Compute the minimum value of the field for which the electron does not cross the strip and, with this field, the time the electron spends in the strip.

$$
r = \frac{\sqrt{2 m_e K}}{e B} \leq d
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B \geq B_{\min} = \frac{\sqrt{2 m_e K}}{e\,d} ,
\qquad
t = \frac{T}{2} = \frac{\pi m_e}{e B_{\min}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
K &= 2.0 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} = 3.2 \cdot 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
B_{\min} &= \frac{\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31} \cdot 3.2 \cdot 10^{-16}}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2}}\,\mathrm{T} = \frac{2.41 \cdot 10^{-23}}{3.2 \cdot 10^{-21}}\,\mathrm{T} \approx 7.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
t &= \frac{\pi \cdot 9.11 \cdot 10^{-31}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 7.55 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The electron enters perpendicularly to the edge, so the center of its circular trajectory lies on the entry edge and the point of the circle farthest from the edge is at distance $r$ from it. If $r \leq d$ the electron does not reach the opposite edge, it completes half a turn and comes back; the required condition is therefore $r \leq d$. By the relation between **radius, momentum and energy**, $r$ is inversely proportional to $B$, therefore the condition is equivalent to $B \geq B_{\min}$, with $B_{\min} \approx 7.5\,\mathrm{mT}$.

In the strip the electron travels half a circle, and the time taken is half the period, which does not depend on the speed. The speed of the electron is $\sqrt{\dfrac{2K}{m_e}} \approx 2.7 \cdot 10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}$, less than $9\,\%$ of $c$, so the nonrelativistic treatment gives an accurate result.

</div>
