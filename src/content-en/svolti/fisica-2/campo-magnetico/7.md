## Exercise 1

A rectangular coil of $N = 100$ turns, with sides $4.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, carries the current $I = 0.50\,\mathrm{A}$ and is placed in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$. The normal to the coil forms an angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ with the field. Compute the magnetic moment of the coil, the torque acting on it and the maximum value of this torque.

$$
\mu = N I A ,
\qquad
\tau = \mu B \sin\theta ,
\qquad
\tau_{\max} = \mu B
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu &= 100 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 0.040 \cdot 0.060\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} = 0.12\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} \\[6pt]
\tau &= 0.12 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 1.2 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} \\[6pt]
\tau_{\max} &= 0.12 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N\cdot m} = 2.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $N$ turns are identical and carry the same current, so by the definition of **magnetic moment** their moments add up, and $\mu = NIA$. The torque is $\vec{\mu} \times \vec{B}$, with magnitude $\mu B \sin\theta$; with $\theta = 30^\circ$ it equals half the maximum. The maximum occurs for $\theta = 90^\circ$, that is when the field lies in the plane of the coil, and it equals $2.4 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. The torque rotates the coil so as to reduce $\theta$, toward the position in which $\vec{\mu}$ has the same sense as $\vec{B}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A circular loop of radius $r = 5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$, carrying the current $I = 3.0\,\mathrm{A}$, can rotate about one of its diameters perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$. Compute the potential energy of the loop in the position of stable equilibrium and the work that must be done to rotate it slowly from this position to $\theta = 90^\circ$ and to $\theta = 180^\circ$.

$$
\mu = I \pi r^2 ,
\qquad
U(\theta) = -\mu B \cos\theta ,
\qquad
W_{0 \to \theta} = U(\theta) - U(0) = \mu B\,(1 - \cos\theta)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu &= 3.0 \cdot \pi \cdot 0.050^2\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} \approx 2.36 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{A\cdot m^2} \\[6pt]
U(0) &= -2.36 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 0.40\,\mathrm{J} \approx -9.4 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
W_{0 \to 90^\circ} &= \mu B \approx 9.4 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{J} ,
\qquad
W_{0 \to 180^\circ} = 2 \mu B \approx 1.9 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The loop is circular, and the formula for the **magnetic moment** holds for plane loops of arbitrary shape, with the area $\pi r^2$. The axis of rotation is perpendicular to the field, so the loop can take any angle $\theta$ between $\hat{n}$ and $\vec{B}$. By the **energy of a magnetic moment in a uniform field** the position of stable equilibrium is $\theta = 0$, where the energy is minimum and equals $-\mu B$.

The rotation is slow, so the kinetic energy does not change and the work of the external agent equals the change in potential energy. Up to $90^\circ$ the energy goes from $-\mu B$ to $0$, up to $180^\circ$ it goes from $-\mu B$ to $\mu B$, so the second work is twice the first.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A moving-coil galvanometer has a coil of $N = 200$ turns of area $A = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, in a radial magnetic field of magnitude $B = 0.25\,\mathrm{T}$; the spiral spring has torsion constant $\kappa = 1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{N\cdot m/rad}$. Compute the current that rotates the pointer by $90^\circ$, that is the full-scale current, and the angle of rotation for the current $I = 0.10\,\mathrm{mA}$.

$$
N I A B = \kappa\,\phi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I = \frac{\kappa\,\phi}{N A B} ,
\qquad
\phi = \frac{N A B}{\kappa}\, I
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
N A B &= 200 \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 0.25\,\mathrm{m^2\cdot T} = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2\cdot T} \\[6pt]
I_{\text{fs}} &= \frac{1.0 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2}}{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 3.1 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
\phi &= \frac{5.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-6}} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{rad} = 0.50\,\mathrm{rad} \approx 29^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the **galvanometer** the field is radial, so the plane of the coil always contains the direction of the field, the angle between $\vec{\mu}$ and $\vec{B}$ remains $90^\circ$ and the torque of the magnetic forces has the maximum value $NIAB$ in every position. At equilibrium this torque equals the restoring torque of the spring, $\kappa\,\phi$, with the angle in radians. The full-scale angle is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{rad}$, from which the full-scale current follows, about $0.31\,\mathrm{mA}$.

The angle is proportional to the current, therefore the scale of the instrument is linear; $0.10\,\mathrm{mA}$ is about one third of the full-scale current and produces a rotation of $0.50\,\mathrm{rad}$, about one third of $90^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

A rigid square loop of side $a = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and mass $m = 20\,\mathrm{g}$, uniformly distributed, hangs from a smooth horizontal hinge along one of its sides, in a uniform vertical magnetic field, pointing upward, of magnitude $B = 0.10\,\mathrm{T}$. When the current $I = 5.0\,\mathrm{A}$ circulates in the loop, the loop moves away from the vertical and remains in equilibrium inclined at an angle $\theta$. Compute $\theta$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 1.816,-3.564;-0.8,0 0.8,0" segmenti="0,0,0,-4.3" vettori="0.908,-1.782,0.908,-3.182;1.816,-3.564,3.316,-3.564,F;-2,-4,-2,-1.8,B" punti="0,0,pieno,O;0.908,-1.782,pieno" etichette="0.158,-2.682,mg;0.12,-1.25,θ;1.066,-3.764,⊗" xmin=-2.6 xmax=4 ymin=-4.6 ymax=0.6 titolo="View along the hinge $O$: the loop forms the angle $\theta$ with the vertical; the weight acts at the center and the magnetic force $\vec{F}$ on the side opposite the hinge"}

$$
F = I a B ,
\qquad
\tau_F = I a B \cdot a\cos\theta ,
\qquad
\tau_P = m g\,\frac{a}{2}\sin\theta
$$
$$
I a^2 B \cos\theta = m g\,\frac{a}{2} \sin\theta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\tan\theta = \frac{2 I a B}{m g}
$$
$$
\tan\theta = \frac{2 \cdot 5.0 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 0.10}{0.020 \cdot 9.81} = \frac{0.10}{0.196} \approx 0.51
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta \approx 27^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We compute the torques about the axis of the hinge, which lies on the upper side of the loop. By the **force on a straight wire**, the lower side, horizontal and perpendicular to the field, receives a horizontal force of magnitude $IaB$; with the current into the page, as in the drawing, the force is directed to the right. The two slanted sides lie in vertical planes perpendicular to the hinge and carry opposite currents, so they receive opposite forces parallel to the hinge, which have no torque about its axis; the force on the hinge side is applied on the axis and has zero torque.

The lever arm of the horizontal force $IaB$ is the vertical distance between the hinge and the lower side, $a\cos\theta$. The weight acts at the center of the loop, at distance $\dfrac{a}{2}$ from the hinge, with lever arm $\dfrac{a}{2}\sin\theta$. At equilibrium the two torques have the same magnitude and opposite senses, and we obtain $\theta \approx 27^\circ$. The same result follows from the formula $\tau = \mu B \sin\theta'$ with $\mu = I a^2$, because the normal to the loop forms with the vertical field the angle $\theta' = 90^\circ - \theta$, and $\sin\theta' = \cos\theta$.

</div>
