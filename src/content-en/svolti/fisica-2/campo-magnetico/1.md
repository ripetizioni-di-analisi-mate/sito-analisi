## Exercise 1

A proton moves in the plane of the page along the $x$ axis, in the positive sense, with speed $v = 3.0 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, in a uniform magnetic field of magnitude $B = 0.20\,\mathrm{T}$ directed into the page. Compute the magnetic force on the proton and its acceleration. The mass of the proton is $m_p = 1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,2.2,0,v;0,0,0,1.8,F" punti="0,0,pieno,p" riferimento="-2.4,-2.2,0,1,x,y" etichette="-2.17,-1.57,⊗;-2.17,0.03,⊗;-2.17,1.43,⊗;-0.97,-1.57,⊗;-0.97,0.03,⊗;-0.97,1.43,⊗;1.03,-1.57,⊗;1.03,1.43,⊗;2.23,-1.57,⊗;2.23,0.03,⊗;2.23,1.43,⊗" xmin=-2.8 xmax=3.2 ymin=-2.5 ymax=2.3 titolo="The proton moves along the $x$ axis in a field into the page; the force $\vec{F} = e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ is directed along the $y$ axis"}

$$
\vec{v} = v\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
\vec{B} = -B\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\vec{F} = e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}
$$
$$
\vec{F} = e\,v\,\hat{\imath} \times \left( -B\,\hat{k} \right) = -e v B\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k}) = e v B\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
a = \frac{e v B}{m_p}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^5 \cdot 0.20\,\mathrm{N} = 9.6 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
a &= \frac{9.6 \cdot 10^{-15}}{1.67 \cdot 10^{-27}}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \approx 5.7 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the page in the $xy$ plane and the $z$ axis pointing toward the reader, a field into the page is $-B\,\hat{k}$. The proton has charge $e$, so by the **Lorentz force** the force is $e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$. Since $\hat{k} \times \hat{\imath} = \hat{\jmath}$, swapping the factors gives $\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k} = -\hat{\jmath}$, and the minus sign of the field makes the force directed along $+\hat{\jmath}$, that is upward in the drawing. Velocity and field are perpendicular, therefore the magnitude of the force is the largest possible, $evB$. The acceleration is perpendicular to the velocity and has magnitude $5.7 \cdot 10^{12}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The value is much larger than the accelerations of macroscopic bodies, because the ratio of the charge to the mass of the proton is very large.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An electron has velocity $\vec{v} = \left( 2.0\,\hat{\imath} + 3.0\,\hat{\jmath} \right) \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = \left( 0.040\,\hat{\imath} - 0.020\,\hat{k} \right)\mathrm{T}$. Compute the magnetic force on the electron in components and its magnitude.

$$
\vec{F} = -e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{v} \times \vec{B}
&= \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\imath} & \hat{\jmath} & \hat{k} \\ 2.0 \cdot 10^6 & 3.0 \cdot 10^6 & 0 \\ 0.040 & 0 & -0.020 \end{vmatrix}\,\mathrm{m\cdot T/s} \\[6pt]
&= \left[ 3.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot (-0.020)\,\hat{\imath} - 2.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot (-0.020)\,\hat{\jmath} - 3.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.040\,\hat{k} \right] \mathrm{m\cdot T/s} \\[6pt]
&= \left( -6.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\imath} + 4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\jmath} - 1.2 \cdot 10^5\,\hat{k} \right) \mathrm{m\cdot T/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{F} &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot \left( 6.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\imath} - 4.0 \cdot 10^4\,\hat{\jmath} + 1.2 \cdot 10^5\,\hat{k} \right) \mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
&= \left( 9.6\,\hat{\imath} - 6.4\,\hat{\jmath} + 19.2\,\hat{k} \right) \cdot 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
F &= 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot \sqrt{3.6 \cdot 10^9 + 1.6 \cdot 10^9 + 14.4 \cdot 10^9}\,\mathrm{N} = 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.4 \cdot 10^5\,\mathrm{N} \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity and the field are not perpendicular, so it is convenient to compute the cross product in components, with the symbolic determinant. In the determinant the term in $\hat{\jmath}$ has a minus sign in front, and its minor is $v_x B_z - v_z B_x = 2.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot (-0.020)$; the term in $\hat{k}$ equals $v_x B_y - v_y B_x = -3.0 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.040$. The unit of the product is $\mathrm{m/s}$ times tesla, and multiplied by a charge in coulombs it gives newtons.

The electron has charge $-e$, so by the **Lorentz force** the force is the opposite of $e\,\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$, and all the components change sign. As a check, the dot product $\vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$ is proportional to $9.6 \cdot 2.0 - 6.4 \cdot 3.0 + 19.2 \cdot 0 = 0$, so the force is perpendicular to the velocity, as it must be. The magnitude of the force is about $2.2 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A proton moving with speed $v = 2.0 \cdot 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ along the $x$ axis experiences no force. When it moves with the same speed along the $y$ axis, in the positive sense, it experiences a force of magnitude $6.4 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}$ directed in the negative sense of the $z$ axis. Determine the magnetic field, assumed uniform, and the force on the proton when its velocity, still of magnitude $v$, lies in the $xy$ plane and forms an angle of $60^\circ$ with the $x$ axis.

$$
\vec{F}_x = \vec{0} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \vec{B} = B_x\,\hat{\imath}
$$
$$
\vec{F}_y = e\,v\,\hat{\jmath} \times B_x\,\hat{\imath} = -e v B_x\,\hat{k} = -6.4 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B_x = \frac{6.4 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}}{e v}
$$
$$
\vec{v}\,' = v \left( \cos 60^\circ\,\hat{\imath} + \sin 60^\circ\,\hat{\jmath} \right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vec{F}\,' = e\,\vec{v}\,' \times B_x\,\hat{\imath} = -e v B_x \sin 60^\circ\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_x &= \frac{6.4 \cdot 10^{-14}}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^6}\,\mathrm{T} = 0.20\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\vec{F}\,' &= -6.4 \cdot 10^{-14} \cdot 0.866\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N} \approx -5.5 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\hat{k}\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **Lorentz force** the magnetic force is zero when the velocity is parallel to the field; since the proton moving along the $x$ axis experiences no force, the field is parallel to the $x$ axis, that is $\vec{B} = B_x\,\hat{\imath}$, with $B_x$ positive or negative. For the velocity along the $y$ axis we use $\hat{\jmath} \times \hat{\imath} = -\hat{k}$; the measured force is directed along $-\hat{k}$, so $B_x$ is positive, and the field is $\vec{B} = 0.20\,\hat{\imath}\,\mathrm{T}$. The measurement with two velocities thus determines the direction, sense and magnitude of the field, as the definition of the **magnetic field** prescribes.

With the velocity at $60^\circ$ from the $x$ axis, the component $v\cos 60^\circ\,\hat{\imath}$ is parallel to the field and does not contribute to the force; only the perpendicular component $v\sin 60^\circ\,\hat{\jmath}$ contributes. The force therefore has the same sense as in the previous case and its magnitude is reduced by the factor $\sin 60^\circ$, that is about $5.5 \cdot 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>
