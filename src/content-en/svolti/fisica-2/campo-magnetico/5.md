## Exercise 1

A copper strip of width $w = 2.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ and thickness $s = 0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries the current $I = 20\,\mathrm{A}$ and is placed in a uniform magnetic field $B = 1.5\,\mathrm{T}$ perpendicular to its wide faces. Copper has $n = 8.5 \cdot 10^{28}$ conduction electrons per cubic meter. Compute the drift velocity of the electrons, the Hall field and the Hall voltage.

$$
v_d = \frac{I}{n e\, w s} ,
\qquad
E_H = v_d B ,
\qquad
\Delta V_H = E_H\, w = \frac{I B}{n e\, s}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v_d &= \frac{20}{8.5 \cdot 10^{28} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 1.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
E_H &= 1.47 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{V/m} \approx 2.2 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{V/m} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_H &= \frac{20 \cdot 1.5}{8.5 \cdot 10^{28} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 5.0 \cdot 10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 4.4 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross section of the strip, perpendicular to the current, has area $w s$; from the relation between current and drift velocity, $I = n e\, v_d\, w s$, the drift velocity follows, about $0.15\,\mathrm{mm/s}$. In the steady state of the **Hall effect** the electric force of the Hall field balances the magnetic force, so $E_H = v_d B$.

The Hall field is uniform across the width, therefore the voltage between the edges is $E_H\, w$; the formula $\dfrac{IB}{n e s}$ gives the same value, about $4.4\,\mu\mathrm{V}$, and provides a check of the calculation. The voltage is very small, because in copper the electron density is very large and the drift velocity very small.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A semiconductor strip of thickness $s = 0.20\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries the current $I = 10\,\mathrm{mA}$ along the $x$ axis, in the positive sense, and is placed in a magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ out of the page, perpendicular to the strip. Between the upper edge and the lower edge of the strip the Hall voltage $\Delta V_H = 6.0\,\mathrm{mV}$ is measured, with the upper edge at the higher potential. Determine the density of the charge carriers and their sign, assuming a single type of carrier, of charge $\pm e$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,-1.1 6.4,-1.1 6.4,1.1 0,1.1" linee="-1.4,0 0,0;6.4,0 7.6,0" vettori="-1.3,0.25,-0.2,0.25,I;3,0,1.8,0,v;3,0,3,-0.75,F" punti="3,0,pieno" etichette="3.2,0.3,e⁻;0.6,-0.95,−;1.6,-0.95,−;2.6,-0.95,−;3.6,-0.95,−;4.6,-0.95,−;5.6,-0.95,−;0.6,0.65,+;1.6,0.65,+;2.6,0.65,+;3.6,0.65,+;4.6,0.65,+;5.6,0.65,+;0.33,1.53,⊙;0.33,-1.87,⊙;1.83,1.53,⊙;1.83,-1.87,⊙;3.33,1.53,⊙;3.33,-1.87,⊙;4.83,1.53,⊙;4.83,-1.87,⊙" xmin=-1.6 xmax=7.8 ymin=-2.1 ymax=2.1 titolo="With negative carriers the electrons move opposite to the current, are pushed toward the lower edge and charge it negatively: the upper edge is at the higher potential"}

$$
\Delta V_H = \frac{I B}{n e\, s}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
n = \frac{I B}{e\, s\, \Delta V_H}
$$
$$
n = \frac{1.0 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 0.50}{1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 6.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}} \approx 2.6 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}
$$
$$
q > 0 : \quad \vec{v}_d = v_d\,\hat{\imath} ,
\quad
q\,\vec{v}_d \times \vec{B} = e\, v_d B\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k}) = -e\, v_d B\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
q < 0 : \quad \vec{v}_d = -v_d\,\hat{\imath} ,
\quad
q\,\vec{v}_d \times \vec{B} = (-e)(-v_d B)\,(\hat{\imath} \times \hat{k}) = -e\, v_d B\,\hat{\jmath}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The carrier density is obtained from the formula of the **Hall effect**, and it is about $2.6 \cdot 10^{22}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, a few million times less than in copper; for this reason the Hall voltage is a few millivolts rather than a few microvolts.

For the sign we compare the two possible cases. With positive carriers the drift velocity has the sense of the current, with negative carriers the opposite sense; in both cases the magnetic force is directed along $-\hat{\jmath}$, so the carriers accumulate on the lower edge, as stated in the remark on the **sign of the carriers**. If the carriers were positive, the lower edge would be positively charged and at the higher potential. The upper edge is instead at the higher potential, so negative charge accumulates on the lower edge, and the carriers are **negative**; the semiconductor is therefore $n$-type.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A Hall probe consists of a semiconductor strip of thickness $s = 0.10\,\mathrm{mm}$, with $n = 2.0 \cdot 10^{21}$ carriers of charge $-e$ per cubic meter, carrying the current $I = 2.0\,\mathrm{mA}$. Placed in a magnetic field perpendicular to the strip, the probe measures the Hall voltage $\Delta V_H = 25\,\mathrm{mV}$. Compute the magnitude of the field and the Hall voltage that a copper strip of the same thickness, with $n_{\mathrm{Cu}} = 8.5 \cdot 10^{28}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, would measure under the same conditions.

$$
B = \frac{n e\, s\, \Delta V_H}{I} ,
\qquad
\Delta V_{H,\mathrm{Cu}} = \frac{I B}{n_{\mathrm{Cu}}\, e\, s} = \frac{n}{n_{\mathrm{Cu}}}\,\Delta V_H
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
B &= \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{21} \cdot 1.60 \cdot 10^{-19} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}}{2.0 \cdot 10^{-3}}\,\mathrm{T} = 0.40\,\mathrm{T} \\[6pt]
\Delta V_{H,\mathrm{Cu}} &= \frac{2.0 \cdot 10^{21}}{8.5 \cdot 10^{28}} \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 5.9 \cdot 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the formula of the **Hall effect** the current, the carrier density, the thickness and the voltage are known, so the field is obtained, $0.40\,\mathrm{T}$. For a probe already built the ratio $\dfrac{n e s}{I}$ is a constant, and the probe measures a field proportional to the voltage read; the sign of the voltage moreover indicates the sense of the field.

For equal current, field and thickness, the Hall voltage is inversely proportional to the carrier density, therefore the copper strip would give a voltage smaller by the factor $\dfrac{n}{n_{\mathrm{Cu}}} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{-8}$, less than a nanovolt, which would require much more sensitive instruments. For this reason Hall probes are made with semiconductors.

</div>
