## Exercise 1

An object of height $y = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ is at $p = 75\,\mathrm{cm}$ from a concave mirror with radius of curvature $R = 50\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine the position, height and characteristics of the image.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="-4+4*cos(t),4*sin(t),-0.3,0.3" linee="-6.8,0 0.5,0;-6,1 0,1 -3,-0.5;-6,1 -2,0 0,-0.5 -3,-0.5;-6,1 0,0 -3,-0.5" vettori="-6,0,-6,1;-3,0,-3,-0.5" punti="-4,0,pieno,C;-2,0,pieno,F;0,0,pieno,V" xmin=-6.8 xmax=0.8 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.5 titolo="Concave mirror with $f = 25\,\mathrm{cm}$ and object at $p = 75\,\mathrm{cm}$: the real, inverted image forms between $F$ and $C$, at $37.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex; the heights are drawn on a larger scale than the distances"}

$$
f = \frac{R}{2} , \qquad
\frac{1}{q} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{p} , \qquad
m = -\frac{q}{p}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= 25\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
\frac{1}{q} &= \frac{1}{25\,\mathrm{cm}} - \frac{1}{75\,\mathrm{cm}} = \frac{2}{75\,\mathrm{cm}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q = 37.5\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
m &= -\frac{37.5}{75} = -0.50 , \qquad y' = -0.50 \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{cm} = -1.5\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mirror is concave, so the focal length is positive and equal to half the radius of curvature. The **spherical mirror equation** gives $q = 37.5\,\mathrm{cm}$, positive, hence the image is **real**: it forms in front of the mirror, where the reflected rays actually meet.

The magnification $-0.50$ indicates an inverted image half as tall as the object. The result agrees with the ray diagram: the object is beyond the center of curvature, at $75\,\mathrm{cm} > R$, and in this case the image falls between the focus and the center; indeed $25\,\mathrm{cm} < q < 50\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The side rearview mirror of a car is a convex mirror with a radius of curvature of magnitude $2.0\,\mathrm{m}$. A car $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ tall is $10\,\mathrm{m}$ behind the mirror. Determine the position and the height of the image.

$$
f = \frac{R}{2} = -1.0\,\mathrm{m} , \qquad
\frac{1}{q} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{p} , \qquad
y' = -\frac{q}{p}\, y
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{q} &= -\frac{1}{1.0\,\mathrm{m}} - \frac{1}{10\,\mathrm{m}} = -\frac{11}{10\,\mathrm{m}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q = -\frac{10}{11}\,\mathrm{m} \approx -0.91\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
m &= -\frac{q}{p} = \frac{1}{11} \approx 0.091 , \qquad
y' = \frac{1.5}{11}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 0.14\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a convex mirror, in the sign convention, the radius of curvature and the focal length are **negative**, so $f = -1.0\,\mathrm{m}$. The car is a real object with $p = 10\,\mathrm{m}$, and the spherical mirror equation gives $q \approx -0.91\,\mathrm{m}$: the image is virtual, behind the mirror, between the mirror and the focus, which is at $1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the vertex.

The magnification is positive and small, hence the image is upright and about $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ tall. A plane mirror would instead give an image $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ tall, $10\,\mathrm{m}$ behind the mirror. The convex mirror reduces images and brings them closer, and for this reason it shows a wide area of the road behind the car in a small space.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A concave mirror is used to project onto a screen the image of a small light bulb, magnified four times. The screen is $3.0\,\mathrm{m}$ from the bulb, on the same side of the mirror, on its axis. Compute the distance of the bulb from the mirror and the radius of curvature of the mirror.

$$
\begin{cases}
m = -\dfrac{q}{p} = -4 \\[8pt]
q - p = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
q = 4p \\[4pt]
3p = 3.0\,\mathrm{m}
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p = 1.0\,\mathrm{m} , \quad q = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = \frac{1}{1.0\,\mathrm{m}} + \frac{1}{4.0\,\mathrm{m}} = \frac{5}{4.0\,\mathrm{m}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f = 0.80\,\mathrm{m} , \qquad R = 2f = 1.6\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An image captured on a screen is **real**, so $q > 0$, and for a mirror a real image is inverted: the magnification is $m = -4$, which gives $q = 4p$. Bulb and screen are in front of the mirror on the same axis, and the screen is farther away, because $q > p$; the distance between the two is therefore $q - p$. The two conditions must be satisfied together, and the system gives $p = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}$ and $q = 4.0\,\mathrm{m}$.

The **spherical mirror equation** then gives the focal length, $0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, and the radius of curvature is twice that, $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$. As a check, the bulb is between the focus and the center of curvature, $0.80\,\mathrm{m} < p < 1.6\,\mathrm{m}$, which is the position in which a concave mirror gives a real, enlarged image.

</div>
