## Exercise 1

A coil of $N = 100$ rectangular turns with sides $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ rotates with frequency $f = 10\,\mathrm{Hz}$ about an axis perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.50\,\mathrm{T}$. At time $t = 0$ the normal to the turns is parallel to the field. Write the law $\mathcal{E}(t)$, and compute the maximum value of the emf and its value at time $t_1 = 0.020\,\mathrm{s}$.

$$
\Phi_B(t) = N B A\cos(\omega t) , \qquad
\mathcal{E}(t) = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = N B A \omega \sin(\omega t) , \qquad
\omega = 2\pi f
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 0.20 \cdot 0.10\,\mathrm{m^2} = 0.020\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
\omega &= 2\pi \cdot 10\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 62.8\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E}_{\max} &= 100 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 0.020 \cdot 62.8\,\mathrm{V} \approx 63\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E}(t_1) &= 62.8 \cdot \sin(62.8 \cdot 0.020)\,\mathrm{V} = 62.8 \cdot \sin(0.4\pi)\,\mathrm{V} \approx 60\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="6.283*sin(0.6283*x)" punti="2,5.976,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,5.976" xmin=-0.5 xmax=10.8 ymin=-7.5 ymax=8 nomi="t,ℰ" legenda="\mathcal{E}(t) = \mathcal{E}_{\max} \sin(\omega t)" titolo="Emf of the coil over one period, with time in hundredths of a second and emf in tens of volts: at time $t_1 = 0.020\,\mathrm{s}$ the emf is about $60\,\mathrm{V}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At time $t$ the normal to the turns makes the angle $\omega t$ with the field, so the flux linkage is $N B A\cos(\omega t)$. Differentiating we obtain the law of the theorem on the **emf in a rotating loop**, with maximum value $N B A \omega$. The angular frequency is $2\pi$ times the rotation frequency, and the emf has the same frequency as the rotation, $10\,\mathrm{Hz}$, that is, period $0.10\,\mathrm{s}$.

At time $t_1$ the coil has completed one fifth of a revolution, because $t_1$ is one fifth of the period, and the angle $\omega t_1$ equals $0.4\pi$, that is, $72^\circ$. Since $\sin 72^\circ \approx 0.951$, the emf is $95\,\%$ of the maximum value. For the dimensional check, $\mathrm{T \cdot m^2 \cdot rad/s} = \mathrm{Wb/s} = \mathrm{V}$, since the radian is dimensionless.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An alternator consists of a coil of $N = 50$ turns of area $A = 0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$, which rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega = 100\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ in a uniform field $B = 0.40\,\mathrm{T}$, perpendicular to the axis of rotation. The coil, of negligible resistance, supplies a resistor $R = 20\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. Neglecting self-induction, compute the maximum current, the average power delivered, and the maximum and average values of the torque that the motor must apply to the coil.

$$
\mathcal{E}_{\max} = N B A \omega , \qquad
I_{\max} = \frac{\mathcal{E}_{\max}}{R} , \qquad
P_m = \frac{\mathcal{E}_{\max}^2}{2 R}
$$
$$
P(t) = M(t)\,\omega = \frac{\mathcal{E}_{\max}^2}{R}\sin^2(\omega t)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M_{\max} = \frac{\mathcal{E}_{\max} I_{\max}}{\omega} , \qquad
M_m = \frac{P_m}{\omega}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E}_{\max} &= 50 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 0.010 \cdot 100\,\mathrm{V} = 20\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
I_{\max} &= \frac{20}{20}\,\mathrm{A} = 1.0\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
P_m &= \frac{20^2}{2 \cdot 20}\,\mathrm{W} = 10\,\mathrm{W} \\[6pt]
M_{\max} &= \frac{20 \cdot 1.0}{100}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m} = 0.20\,\mathrm{N \cdot m} , \qquad
M_m = \frac{10}{100}\,\mathrm{N \cdot m} = 0.10\,\mathrm{N \cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The emf is sinusoidal with maximum value $N B A \omega$, and the current in the resistor, in the absence of self-induction, is $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}}{R}$ at every instant. The instantaneous power $\dfrac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R}$ is proportional to $\sin^2(\omega t)$, which has average value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ over one period, so the **average power** is half the maximum power.

The magnetic forces on the current-carrying coil oppose the rotation, by Lenz's law; the angular velocity is constant, therefore the motor applies a torque equal and opposite to the braking torque. By the conservation of energy the mechanical power $M\omega$ equals the electrical power at every instant, so the torque varies in time as $\sin^2(\omega t)$, with maximum value $0.20\,\mathrm{N \cdot m}$ and average value $0.10\,\mathrm{N \cdot m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A conducting disk of radius $r = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$ rotates with frequency $f = 20\,\mathrm{Hz}$ about its own axis, in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.30\,\mathrm{T}$ parallel to the axis. Two sliding contacts, one on the axis and one on the rim of the disk, connect the disk to a resistor $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{\Omega}$. Neglecting the resistance of the disk and the contacts, compute the emf between the center and the rim, the current, and the power that the motor must supply to keep the rotation constant.

$$
\mathcal{E} = \int_0^r \left| \vec{v} \times \vec{B} \right| ds = \int_0^r B\,\omega s\,ds = \frac{1}{2} B \omega r^2 , \qquad
I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} , \qquad
P = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega &= 2\pi \cdot 20\,\mathrm{rad/s} \approx 126\,\mathrm{rad/s} \\[6pt]
\mathcal{E} &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 0.30 \cdot 126 \cdot 0.15^2\,\mathrm{V} \approx 0.42\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
I &= \frac{0.424}{0.10}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 4.2\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
P &= \frac{0.424^2}{0.10}\,\mathrm{W} \approx 1.8\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the disk the flux linkage cannot be computed directly, because the current spreads through the disk and the circuit has no definite path; it is therefore convenient to use the **motional emf in general**, as the circulation of $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$. Along a radius of the disk, the point at distance $s$ from the axis has speed $\omega s$, perpendicular to the radius and to the field; the Lorentz force per unit charge has magnitude $B \omega s$ and is directed along the radius. Integrating from the center to the rim we obtain $\dfrac{1}{2} B \omega r^2$; the rest of the circuit is at rest and does not contribute.

The emf is constant, because the disk always presents the same geometry to the field, and the generator therefore produces a direct current. The electrical power dissipated in the resistor must be supplied by the motor, since the magnetic force on the radial current in the disk opposes the rotation. The emf is small, and obtaining a significant current requires a circuit of very low resistance.

</div>
