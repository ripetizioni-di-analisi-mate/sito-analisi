## Exercise 1

A coil of $N = 200$ circular turns of radius $r = 3.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ has its axis parallel to a uniform magnetic field, whose magnitude increases uniformly from $0$ to $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ during the interval $\Delta t = 0.10\,\mathrm{s}$. The coil is closed on a circuit of total resistance $R = 4.0\,\mathrm{\Omega}$, and its self-induction is negligible. Compute the magnitude of the induced emf and of the current.

$$
\Phi_B = N B\,\pi r^2 , \qquad
\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} , \qquad
i = \frac{|\mathcal{E}|}{R}
$$
$$
|\mathcal{E}| = N \pi r^2\,\frac{\Delta B}{\Delta t} , \qquad
i = \frac{N \pi r^2\,\Delta B}{R\,\Delta t}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi r^2 &= \pi \cdot 0.030^2\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 2.83 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}| &= 200 \cdot 2.83 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot \frac{0.50}{0.10}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 2.8\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
i &= \frac{2.83}{4.0}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.71\,\mathrm{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis of the coil is parallel to the field, so the normal to the turns makes a zero angle with $\vec{B}$ and the **flux linkage** is $N B$ times the area of one turn. Only the magnitude of the field varies, and it varies uniformly, therefore the derivative of the flux coincides with the ratio $\dfrac{\Delta\Phi_B}{\Delta t}$ and the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** gives the constant emf $N \pi r^2\,\dfrac{\Delta B}{\Delta t}$. The current is obtained by dividing by the resistance, since the self-induction is negligible.

Dimensional check: $\mathrm{T \cdot m^2/s} = \mathrm{Wb/s}$, and one weber per second is one volt, in agreement with Faraday's law. The induced current stops when the field stops increasing.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A square loop of side $\ell = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and resistance $R = 0.50\,\mathrm{\Omega}$ is at rest in a uniform magnetic field, with its normal inclined at $\theta = 60^\circ$ to the field. The magnitude of the field varies according to the law $B(t) = \alpha t^2$, with $\alpha = 0.40\,\mathrm{T/s^2}$. Neglecting self-induction, compute the induced current at time $t_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and the charge that passes through the loop between $t = 0$ and $t_1$.

$$
\Phi_B(t) = \alpha t^2\,\ell^2 \cos\theta , \qquad
\mathcal{E}(t) = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -2 \alpha t\,\ell^2 \cos\theta
$$
$$
|i(t_1)| = \frac{2 \alpha t_1\,\ell^2 \cos\theta}{R} , \qquad
|q| = \frac{|\Phi_B(t_1) - \Phi_B(0)|}{R} = \frac{\alpha t_1^2\,\ell^2 \cos\theta}{R}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal{E}(t_1)| &= 2 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 3.0 \cdot 0.20^2 \cdot 0.50\,\mathrm{V} = 0.048\,\mathrm{V} \\[6pt]
|i(t_1)| &= \frac{0.048}{0.50}\,\mathrm{A} \approx 0.096\,\mathrm{A} \\[6pt]
|q| &= \frac{0.40 \cdot 3.0^2 \cdot 0.20^2 \cdot 0.50}{0.50}\,\mathrm{C} \approx 0.14\,\mathrm{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The loop is flat and the field is uniform, so the flux is the product of the field, the area $\ell^2$ and $\cos\theta$. The area and the angle are constant, therefore the flux varies only through $B(t)$, and its derivative is $2\alpha t\,\ell^2\cos\theta$. The emf grows linearly in time, and at time $t_1$ it equals $48\,\mathrm{mV}$.

For the charge we apply **Felici's law**: the charge that passes through the loop depends only on the change in flux between the initial instant, at which the flux is zero, and the instant $t_1$. The same result is obtained by integrating $|i(t)| = \dfrac{2\alpha t\,\ell^2\cos\theta}{R}$ between $0$ and $t_1$, since $\displaystyle\int_0^{t_1} 2t\,dt = t_1^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A long solenoid, with $n = 2000$ turns per meter, contains inside it, far from the ends, a small coaxial coil of $N = 100$ turns of radius $r = 1.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The current in the solenoid increases uniformly from $0$ to $3.0\,\mathrm{A}$ in $50\,\mathrm{ms}$. Compute the emf induced in the coil and determine the direction of the induced current relative to that of the current in the solenoid.

$$
B = \mu_0 n\,i_s , \qquad
\Phi_B = N B\,\pi r^2 = N \mu_0 n\,\pi r^2\,i_s , \qquad
|\mathcal{E}| = N \mu_0 n\,\pi r^2\,\frac{\Delta i_s}{\Delta t}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_0 n &= 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \cdot 2000\,\mathrm{T/A} \approx 2.51 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{T/A} \\[6pt]
\pi r^2 &= \pi \cdot 0.015^2\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2} \\[6pt]
|\mathcal{E}| &= 100 \cdot 2.51 \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot 7.07 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot \frac{3.0}{0.050}\,\mathrm{V} \approx 1.1 \cdot 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Inside a long solenoid the field is uniform, parallel to the axis and equal to $\mu_0 n\,i_s$, as seen in the topic on sources of the magnetic field. The coil is coaxial, so its turns are perpendicular to the field and the flux linkage is $N B \pi r^2$, proportional to the current $i_s$. The current increases uniformly by $60\,\mathrm{A}$ per second, and the **Faraday-Neumann-Lenz law** gives a constant emf of about $11\,\mathrm{mV}$.

The flux in the coil increases, therefore by **Lenz's law** the induced current generates a field opposite to that of the solenoid; hence the current in the coil flows in the direction **opposite** to that of the solenoid. During the increase the field in the solenoid reaches $\mu_0 n \cdot 3.0\,\mathrm{A} \approx 7.5\,\mathrm{mT}$, a typical value for laboratory solenoids.

</div>
