## Exercise 1

A $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ hits a stationary $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass elastically. Find the first body's signed final velocity.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="0,1,4,1,u_1;0,0,-2,0,v_1;0,-1,2,-1,v_2" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=2 titolo="The lighter mass rebounds while the heavier one moves forward"}

$$
v_1=\frac{m_1-m_2}{m_1+m_2}u_1,\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_1&=\left(1 - 3\right) \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{1}{1 + 3}\\[4pt]&=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first mass is lighter, so the coefficient is negative. Its rebound leaves the second mass moving positively.

The negative value of magnitude two means the first body rebounds. The formula gives the second body two metres per second in the positive direction. Final momenta are minus two and plus six, summing to the initial four. The energies, two and six joules, also sum to the initial eight joules.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ carts collide elastically on a track at initial velocities $6$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the first cart's final velocity.

$$
v_1=u_2,\qquad
v_1=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a one-dimensional elastic collision of equal masses, the velocities exchange. This conserves both total momentum and total energy.

The first mass goes from positive to negative motion because it takes on the other's signed velocity. The other gets a positive six metres per second. Relative separation speed is eight metres per second, matching the relative approach speed before contact.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ collides elastically in one dimension with a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the second cart's final velocity.

$$
v_2=\frac{2m_1u_1+(m_2-m_1)u_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_2&=\frac{\left(1 - 3\right) \cdot \left(-2\right) + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}{1 + 3}\\[4pt]&=7\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initial momentum is ten kilogram metres per second, and relative separation after impact is six metres per second. Solving both constraints gives the second velocity.

The first final velocity is seven minus six, or one metre per second. The final momenta are $3\cdot1$ and $1\cdot7$ in SI units, summing to ten. The energy check gives $\dfrac12(3\cdot1^2+1\cdot7^2)\,\mathrm J=26\,\mathrm J$, the same total as before impact.

</div>
