## Exercise 1

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass moves at $+6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and strikes a stationary $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass. The collision is head-on and elastic, with negligible external impulse. Find both final velocities and check the energy balance.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="0,1.2,6,1.2,u₁;0,.35,-2,.35,v₁;0,-.45,4,-.45,v₂" xmin=-3.2 xmax=7.1 ymin=-1.1 ymax=1.9 titolo="The initial velocity appears above. Below, the first mass rebounds and the second moves forwards."}

$$
\begin{aligned}v_1&=\frac{2-4}{2+4}6\,\mathrm{m/s}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad
v_2=\frac{2\cdot2}{2+4}6\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s},\\
K_i&=\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot6^2\,\mathrm J=36\,\mathrm J,\qquad K_f=\tfrac12[2(-2)^2+4\cdot4^2]\,\mathrm J=36\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stationary-target formula applies because the second cart starts from rest. The first rebounds: the numerator of its mass ratio is negative. Check the signed momentum too. The final contributions are minus four and plus sixteen in SI units, for a total of twelve, matching the initial momentum. In the energy balance the negative velocity is squared.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass approaches at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hits a stationary body elastically and rebounds at $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the second body's mass, neglecting external impulse.

$$
\begin{aligned}v_2&=v_1+u_1-u_2=(-1+5-0)\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s},\\ m_2&=\frac{m_1(u_1-v_1)}{v_2}=\frac{2(5-(-1))}{4}\,\mathrm{kg}=3\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use relative velocity first to find the final velocity of the body with unknown mass. The bodies separate at the same relative speed of five metres per second as they approached. The first body loses twelve kilogram metres per second, which the target gains. Dividing by its final velocity gives its mass. The energy check gives twenty-five joules before contact and one plus twenty-four afterwards.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two masses of $2$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ approach along a track at $+5$ and $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The collision is isolated and elastic. Use the centre-of-mass frame to find the velocities after contact.

$$
\begin{aligned}V&=\frac{2\cdot5+3(-1)}{2+3}\,\mathrm{m/s}=1.4\,\mathrm{m/s},\\
v_1&=2V-u_1=-2.2\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad v_2=2V-u_2=3.8\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the centre-of-mass frame the initial velocities are $+3.6$ and $-2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Both reverse in this head-on elastic collision. Add the same centre-of-mass velocity to each to return to the lab. The final difference of six metres per second is the separation speed. Total kinetic energy is $26.5\,\mathrm J$ before and after, while the centre continues rightwards.

</div>
