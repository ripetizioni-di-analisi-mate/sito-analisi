## Exercise 1

Masses of $1$, $2$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ sit on a track at $-2$, $1$ and $5\,\mathrm m$, respectively. Find the centre-of-mass coordinate.

$$
x_{CM}=\frac{1(-2)+2\cdot1+3\cdot5}{1+2+3}\,\mathrm m=2.5\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two mass–coordinate products cancel. The third contributes three times its coordinate of five metres. The denominator still includes all three masses. To check, group the first two together. Their centre is at the origin and their combined mass equals the third mass. The overall centre therefore lies halfway between the origin and the third mass.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On a horizontal board, three masses of $1$, $2$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ occupy $(0;0)$, $(3;0)$ and $(0;2)$ in metres. Find the centre of mass and determine whether it lies inside the triangle.

::grafico{linee="0,0 3,0 0,2 0,0" punti="0,0,pieno,A;3,0,pieno,B;0,2,pieno,C;1,1,pieno,CM" uguale=1 xmin=-.6 xmax=3.8 ymin=-.6 ymax=2.7 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="The weighted mean lies inside the triangle formed by the three masses."}

$$
x_{CM}=\frac{1\cdot0+2\cdot3+3\cdot0}{6}\,\mathrm m=1\,\mathrm m,\qquad
y_{CM}=\frac{1\cdot0+2\cdot0+3\cdot2}{6}\,\mathrm m=1\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Calculate each weighted average separately with the same total mass in the denominator. The mass at the origin contributes to that denominator and moves both averages towards zero. The point with both coordinates equal to one metre lies below the sloping side, which is four thirds of a metre high at the same horizontal coordinate. The centre is inside the triangle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Between two frames $0.50\,\mathrm s$ apart, a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ marker moves by $+0.80\,\mathrm m$ and a $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ marker by $-0.40\,\mathrm m$. Find the displacement of the centre of mass and its average velocity.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta x_{CM}&=\frac{1\cdot0.80+3(-0.40)}{1+3}\,\mathrm m=-0.10\,\mathrm m,\\
\overline v_{CM}&=\frac{-0.10}{0.50}\,\mathrm{m/s}=-0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two position averages share the same denominator. Subtract them to work with the measured displacements without reconstructing the absolute positions. The heavier marker covers half the first one's distance, but its contribution is one and a half times as large and determines the sign. Two frames give an average velocity. Intermediate frames could reveal how the velocity changed.

</div>
