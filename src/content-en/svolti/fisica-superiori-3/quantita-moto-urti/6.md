## Exercise 1

Masses of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ are at $0$ and $4\,\mathrm m$. Find their centre of mass.

::grafico{linee="0,0 4,0" punti="0,0,pieno,m_1;3,0,pieno,CM;4,0,pieno,m_2" xmin=-1 xmax=5.5 ymin=-1 ymax=1 nomi="x (m)," titolo="The weighted average lies closer to the mass at four metres"}

$$
x_{CM}=\frac{\sum_jm_jx_j}{\sum_jm_j},\qquad
\begin{aligned}x_{CM}&=\frac{2 \cdot 0 + 6 \cdot 4}{2 + 6}\\[4pt]&=3\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The four-metre coordinate has three times the weight of the other. The result is one metre to the left of the heavier mass and three metres from the lighter one.

Taking the centre at $x=3\,\mathrm m$ puts the two-kilogram mass three metres away and the six-kilogram mass one metre away. Each mass–distance product is six numerically. Their opposite signs make them cancel, as expected about the centre of mass.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass is at $-2\,\mathrm m$ and a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass at $5\,\mathrm m$. Find the centre-of-mass coordinate.

$$
x_{CM}=\frac{m_1x_1+m_2x_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}x_{CM}&=\frac{3 \cdot \left(-2\right) + 2 \cdot 5}{2 + 3}\\[4pt]&=0.8\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first product is negative because that mass is left of the origin. The positive sum places the centre to the right of the origin.

The numerator is four kilogram-metres, from contributions minus six and plus ten. Divide by five kilograms to get $0.8\,\mathrm m$. It lies between $-2$ and $5\,\mathrm m$, closer to the heavier mass.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass is at $-4\,\mathrm m$ and a second mass at $6\,\mathrm m$. The centre is at $x=0$. Find the second mass.

$$
m_2=-\frac{m_1x_1}{x_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}m_2&=3 \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{1}{6}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean coordinate is zero when the numerator is zero. Equating the two product magnitudes gives a smaller mass on the more distant side.

The left side has mass–distance product of magnitude twelve kilogram-metres. Two kilograms at six metres give the same magnitude with the opposite sign. This checks the weighted-average condition with all masses positive.

</div>
