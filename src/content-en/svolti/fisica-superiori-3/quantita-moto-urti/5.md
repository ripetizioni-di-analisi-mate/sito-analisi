## Exercise 1

An internal spring separates blocks of $0.60$ and $1.40\,\mathrm{kg}$ initially at rest on an ideal track. The first leaves at $+3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the second block's velocity and the total kinetic energy.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="0,1,3.5,1,v₁;0,0,-1.5,0,v₂" xmin=-2.7 xmax=4.5 ymin=-.65 ymax=1.7 titolo="Arrow lengths are proportional to the speeds in the laboratory."}

$$
\begin{aligned}v_2&=-\frac{0.60\cdot3.5}{1.40}\,\mathrm{m/s}=-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s},\\ K_f&=\frac{0.60(3.5)^2+1.40(-1.5)^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=5.25\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The centre of mass stays at rest, so the two momentum magnitudes both reach $2.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The energies are positive even though the velocities have opposite signs. The spring releases stored energy to produce this motion. The lighter block receives the greater share, as shown by the inverse dependence on mass when momentum magnitude is fixed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $54\,\mathrm{kg}$ person and the $36\,\mathrm{kg}$ platform they are standing on are both initially at rest. The platform is free to slide without horizontal friction. At one instant the person walks right at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the platform. Find both velocities relative to the floor.

$$
\begin{aligned}54(v_d+2\,\mathrm{m/s})+36v_d&=0,\\ v_d&=-\frac{54\cdot2}{54+36}\,\mathrm{m/s}=-1.2\,\mathrm{m/s},\\ v_{\mathrm{p}}&=(-1.2+2)\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.8\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The walking speed is measured on the moving platform. Add the platform's signed velocity to obtain the person's velocity relative to the floor. Total horizontal momentum stays zero. Relative to the floor, the person moves more slowly than an observer on the platform would measure because the platform moves left.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On an ideal track, two attached carts of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ travel at $+1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. They separate and an internal spring pushes the leading $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart to $+4.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the other velocity and the gain in kinetic energy.

$$
\begin{aligned}v_2&=\frac{(2+6)1.5-2\cdot4.5}{6}\,\mathrm{m/s}=0.5\,\mathrm{m/s},\\ K_f-K_i&=\frac{2(4.5)^2+6(0.5)^2-8(1.5)^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=12\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial total already includes the motion of both carts. The second keeps moving right because some positive momentum remains even after subtracting the first cart's contribution. They still move apart because the leading cart is faster. The twelve-joule gain comes from stored spring energy while total momentum stays constant.

</div>
