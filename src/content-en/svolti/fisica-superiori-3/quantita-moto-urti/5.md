## Exercise 1

Two blocks of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ initially at rest separate due to an internal spring. The first moves right at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the second velocity component?

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="0,1,3,1,v_1;0,0,-1,0,v_2" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=2 titolo="The lighter body has the higher speed"}

$$
v_2=-\frac{m_1v_1}{m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_2&=\left(-2\right) \cdot 3 \cdot \frac{1}{6}\\[4pt]&=-1\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Total momentum is zero before and after. The second velocity points the other way and has smaller magnitude because the second mass is greater.

The first block has momentum $+6\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The second must have $-6\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. Dividing by six kilograms gives one metre per second backwards and the sum checks to zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ skater initially at rest pushes a $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ package along the ice. The package moves away at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the recoil speed.

$$
v_r=\frac{m_pv_p}{m_s},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_r&=10 \cdot 5 \cdot \frac{1}{50}\\[4pt]&=1\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Neglect the impulse from the ice along the motion during the push. The skater's final momentum has the same magnitude as the package's.

In the horizontal balance, fifty kilograms at one metre per second gives a momentum magnitude of fifty, matching ten kilograms at five metres per second. Their directions are opposite. The skater's push and the reaction from the package are internal when both bodies belong to the system.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two fragments of $3$ and $9\,\mathrm{kg}$ separate from rest. Find the ratio of their kinetic energies, first to second.

$$
K_1/K_2=\frac{m_2}{m_1},\qquad
\begin{aligned}K_1/K_2&=\frac{9}{3}\\[4pt]&=3.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The momentum magnitudes agree. Writing energy as momentum squared divided by twice the mass shows that the greater mass has lower energy.

The lighter fragment travels three times as fast as the heavier one. The squared speed gives a factor of nine, and its mass gives a factor of one third. Its energy is therefore three times as great.

</div>
