## Exercise 1

A $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart at $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ sticks to a stationary $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart. External impulse is negligible. Find the kinetic energy lost and its fraction of the initial energy.

$$
\begin{aligned}v_f&=\frac{1\cdot8}{1+3}\,\mathrm{m/s}=2\,\mathrm{m/s},\\
K_i&=\tfrac12\cdot1\cdot8^2\,\mathrm J=32\,\mathrm J,\qquad K_f=\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot2^2\,\mathrm J=8\,\mathrm J,\\ \Delta K_{\mathrm{lost}}&=24\,\mathrm J,\qquad \frac{\Delta K_{\mathrm{lost}}}{K_i}=\tfrac34.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final-to-initial speed ratio is one quarter: the momentum is now shared by four times the original moving mass. In the energy, squaring the new speed contributes a factor of one sixteenth, while mass adds a factor of four. One quarter of the original kinetic energy remains, with the rest converted to other forms. Use the initial energy as the denominator because the question asks for a fraction of it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two carts of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ each approach with velocities $+4$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. On an ideal track they leave at $-1$ and $+3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Check conservation of momentum and classify the collision.

$$
\begin{aligned}P_i&=2(4-2)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\qquad P_f=2(-1+3)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\\
K_i&=(4^2+(-2)^2)\,\mathrm J=20\,\mathrm J,\qquad K_f=((-1)^2+3^2)\,\mathrm J=10\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The signed sums of the velocities match. With equal masses this verifies the momentum balance. The sums of the squares show that the kinetic energy halves. That decrease makes the collision inelastic. After contact the two velocities differ and point in opposite directions, so the carts separate. The measurements satisfy the horizontal momentum balance of the model.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ sticks to a stationary body. With negligible external impulse the kinetic energy loss is $15\,\mathrm J$. Find the mass of the second body.

$$
\begin{aligned}K_f&=\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot5^2\,\mathrm J-15\,\mathrm J=10\,\mathrm J,\qquad P=10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\\
M&=\frac{P^2}{2K_f}=5\,\mathrm{kg},\qquad m_2=5\,\mathrm{kg}-2\,\mathrm{kg}=3\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After sticking, treat the pair as one body of unknown mass. Momentum follows from the initial data and final kinetic energy follows by subtracting the loss. Substitute $v_f=P/M$ into the kinetic energy formula to obtain one equation for the combined mass. The resulting final velocity of two metres per second reproduces both ten joules and the momentum balance.

</div>
