## Exercise 1

Two $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ carts stick on an isolated track. The first moves at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the second is stationary. Find the kinetic energy lost.

$$
E_{\mathrm{loss}}=K_i-K_f,\qquad
\begin{aligned}E_{\mathrm{loss}}&=- \frac{3^{2} \cdot \left(4 + 4\right)}{2} + \frac{4 \cdot 6^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=36\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Momentum conservation gives a shared speed of three metres per second. Compare the energy of the single moving mass before impact with the energy of both together after.

Initial kinetic energy is seventy-two joules. After sticking, twice the mass moves at half the speed, giving thirty-six joules. The difference goes to other forms of energy, consistently with conserved total momentum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ masses arrive at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in opposite directions and stick. Find the kinetic energy transferred to other forms if external impulse is negligible.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="-6,0,-1,0,v_1;6,0,1,0,v_2" xmin=-7 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=2 titolo="The two vectors have opposite directions and equal magnitudes"}

$$
E_{\mathrm{loss}}=K_{1i}+K_{2i},\qquad
\begin{aligned}E_{\mathrm{loss}}&=2 \cdot 2 \cdot 5^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\\[4pt]&=50\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initial momenta cancel, so the shared final velocity is zero. Both initial energies are positive and their sum is transferred.

Each mass brings twenty-five joules into the collision regardless of direction. The energies sum to fifty joules even though the momenta sum to zero. After the bodies stick at zero speed, all this kinetic energy has been transferred.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ body at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ catches and sticks to a $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ body travelling at $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the same direction. How much kinetic energy is lost on an ideal track?

$$
E_{\mathrm{loss}}=K_i-\frac12(m_1+m_2)v_f^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}E_{\mathrm{loss}}&=- \frac{\left(\frac{3 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 6}{2 + 3}\right)^{2} \cdot \left(2 + 3\right)}{2} + \frac{3 \cdot 1^{2} + 2 \cdot 6^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=15\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both bodies contribute to initial kinetic energy and momentum. Momentum gives the shared speed of three metres per second used in the final energy.

The faster body brings twelve kilogram metres per second of momentum and the slower one three. The total of fifteen spread over five kilograms gives the shared velocity. Energy goes from $37.5$ to $22.5\,\mathrm J$, with the fifteen-joule loss coming from the initial relative motion.

</div>
