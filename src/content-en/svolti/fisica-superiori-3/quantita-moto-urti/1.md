## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart travels left at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With right positive find $p_x$.

$$
p_x=mv_x,\qquad
\begin{aligned}p_x&=\left(-4\right) \cdot 3\\[4pt]&=-12\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity component is negative and mass is positive. The momentum has the same sign as the velocity.

The minus sign denotes direction; momentum magnitude is not negative. Dividing $-12\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ by the positive four-kilogram mass recovers the $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ component, consistent with motion to the left.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ object has velocity components $3$ and $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find its momentum magnitude.

::grafico{uguale=1 vettori="0,0,3,0,v_x;3,0,3,4,v_y;0,0,3,4,v" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5 titolo="The reference directions are perpendicular"}

$$
p=m\sqrt{v_x^2+v_y^2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}p&=2 \cdot \sqrt{3^{2} + 4^{2}}\\[4pt]&=10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Speed squared is the sum of squared perpendicular components. Multiply the resulting speed by mass.

One may instead start from momentum components $6$ and $8\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. Magnitude squared is $36+64=100$ in SI units, giving magnitude ten. Adding six and eight directly would incorrectly treat the two components as aligned.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cyclist and bicycle have total mass $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ and velocity $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. An observer travels in the same direction at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the momentum in the observer’s frame.

$$
p=m(v-u),\qquad
\begin{aligned}p&=60 \cdot \left(8 - 5\right)\\[4pt]&=180\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the observer's velocity from the ground velocity first, because the required frame is moving.

In the road frame momentum would be $480\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The two values differ by mass times observer speed, $300\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. This difference provides a check on the transformation between the two measurements.

</div>
