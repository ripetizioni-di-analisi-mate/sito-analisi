## Exercise 1

A model bicycle and its load have a combined mass of $6\,\mathrm{kg}$. It travels west at $2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Take east as positive and find the component $p_x$ and its magnitude.

$$
p_x=mv_x=6(-2.5)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}=-15\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\qquad |\vec p|=15\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load belongs to the moving system and contributes through its mass. Fix the axis first, then give $v_x$ a negative sign because the motion is westwards. The component $p_x$ encodes both direction and magnitude. Describing the same observation with west as positive gives a positive component. The magnitude is $15\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$ for either axis orientation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ puck has velocity $(-4;3)\,\mathrm{m/s}$, with east and north positive. Calculate both components of momentum, its magnitude and its quadrant.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,-3.2,0,pₓ;-3.2,0,-3.2,2.4,pᵧ;0,0,-3.2,2.4,p" uguale=1 xmin=-4.3 xmax=1 ymin=-.9 ymax=3.4 nomi="pₓ,pᵧ" titolo="Momentum components and resultant in kg·m/s."}

$$
\vec p=\big(-3.2;2.4\big)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\qquad |\vec p|=0.80\sqrt{4^2+3^2}\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}=4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mass multiplies each component without changing the angles. The horizontal component is negative and the vertical one positive, so the vector points north-west. It is closer to west because the horizontal component is greater in magnitude. The component triangle has perpendicular legs. Pythagoras gives a magnitude larger than either leg and smaller than their sum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $12\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart moves right at $3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the floor. A camera on a second cart moves left at $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the first cart's momentum measured by the camera and its difference from the ground-frame value.

$$
\begin{aligned}v'&=[3.5-(-1.5)]\,\mathrm{m/s}=5\,\mathrm{m/s},\\ p'&=12\cdot5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}=60\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\\ p'-p&=12(5-3.5)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}=18\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With right positive, the camera has a negative velocity. Subtracting it increases the observed speed, as expected for carts approaching from opposite directions. The difference between the two momentum measurements depends only on the moving cart's mass and the camera's velocity. It compares simultaneous measurements in different frames.

</div>
