## Exercise 1

A constant $12\,\mathrm N$ force acts in the negative direction for $0.25\,\mathrm s$. Find its impulse component.

$$
J_x=F_x\Delta t,\qquad
\begin{aligned}J_x&=\left(-12\right) \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{4}\\[4pt]&=-3\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The elapsed time is positive and the force component is negative. The impulse points in the same direction as the force.

On the force–time graph the force stays below zero and height times duration gives negative signed area. The magnitude is three newton-seconds, equivalent to three kilogram metres per second. For a body of constant mass this is the momentum-change magnitude if the force is the net force.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A force increases linearly from zero to $40\,\mathrm N$ in $0.20\,\mathrm s$ and keeps its direction. Find the impulse magnitude.

::grafico{linee="0,0 .2,40" area="200*x;0;0;.2" xmin=0 xmax=.25 ymin=0 ymax=50 nomi="t (s),F (N)" titolo="Impulse is the triangular area below the graph"}

$$
J=\frac12 F_{\mathrm{max}}\Delta t,\qquad
\begin{aligned}J&=40 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force–time graph is a triangle. The time-average force is half the final value.

Mean force is $20\,\mathrm N$ because the graph is linear between zero and forty. Multiplying that equivalent constant force by $0.20\,\mathrm s$ again gives $4\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$. Using the final force for the whole interval counts the full rectangle and doubles the result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A force is $10\,\mathrm N$ for $0.40\,\mathrm s$ and then $-4\,\mathrm N$ for $0.50\,\mathrm s$. Find the total impulse along the axis.

$$
J_x=F_1\Delta t_1+F_2\Delta t_2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}J_x&=- \frac{1 \cdot 4}{2} + 10 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{5}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each stage gives a rectangle on the graph. Add the signed areas to find their net contribution.

The first stage gives $+4\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ and the second $-2\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$. The positive balance means final momentum is greater along the chosen axis. The second stage still reduces it, as its contribution shows, even though the overall balance is positive.

</div>
