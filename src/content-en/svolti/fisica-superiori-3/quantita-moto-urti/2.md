## Exercise 1

A sensor measures a force component $F_x$. Its reading varies linearly from $-15\,\mathrm N$ to $+9\,\mathrm N$ in $0.40\,\mathrm s$. Find the signed impulse and the time when the force reverses.

::grafico{linee="0,-15 400,9" area="0;.06*x-15;0;250|.06*x-15;0;250;400" punti="250,0,pieno,Z" xmin=0 xmax=500 ymin=-18 ymax=12 nomi="t (ms),Fₓ (N)" titolo="Before Z the area contributes negatively. After Z the contribution is positive."}

$$
\begin{aligned}F_x(t)&=-15\,\mathrm N+(60\,\mathrm{N/s})t,\qquad t_0=\frac{15\,\mathrm N}{60\,\mathrm{N/s}}=0.25\,\mathrm s,\\ J_x&=\frac{-15+9}{2}\cdot0.40\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}=-1.2\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope is the twenty-four-newton change divided by four tenths of a second. The zero occurs after halfway, as expected since the starting force has the greater magnitude. For a linear change, the mean is the average of the endpoints. Adding the two signed triangular areas gives the same result: the negative contribution dominates.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A push rises linearly from zero to $100\,\mathrm N$ between zero and $0.03\,\mathrm s$, stays constant until $0.08\,\mathrm s$, then falls linearly to zero at $0.12\,\mathrm s$. Find the impulse and average force.

$$
\begin{aligned}J&=100\left(\frac{0.03}{2}+0.05+\frac{0.04}{2}\right)\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}=8.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot s},\\ F_m&=\frac{8.5}{0.12}\,\mathrm{N}\simeq70.8\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two ramps are triangles with different widths. The middle segment stays at the peak for five hundredths of a second and contributes the full area of a rectangle. Divide the total by the full duration, including both ramps. The result is below the peak and above either ramp's average because the middle segment stays high.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body experiences a force of $+8\,\mathrm N$ for $0.20\,\mathrm s$ and then a force of $-6\,\mathrm N$. A sensor measures a total impulse of $+1\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$. How long does the second phase last?

$$
\begin{aligned}J_1&=8\cdot0.20\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}=1.6\,\mathrm{N\cdot s},\\ \Delta t_2&=\frac{1-1.6}{-6}\,\mathrm s=0.10\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second impulse is the measured total minus the contribution already transferred. It is negative, as is its force. Dividing two negative values gives a positive duration. For a check, the second phase must subtract six tenths of a newton second from the initial sixteen tenths. Twice the duration at the same force magnitude would leave a smaller impulse than measured.

</div>
