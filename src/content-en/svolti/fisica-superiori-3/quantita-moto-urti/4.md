## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart moving at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ sticks to a stationary $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart without external impulse. Find their common speed.

$$
v_f=\frac{m_1v_1}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_f&=4 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{1}{2 + 4}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first cart supplies all initial momentum. Once the carts stick together, both masses move.

Before impact total momentum is $24\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. After sticking, six kilograms moving at four metres per second give the same value. Kinetic energy has changed: sticking conserves momentum without external impulse, while energy transfers to other forms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ meets and sticks to a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass at $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neglect external impulse and find their shared velocity.

$$
v_f=\frac{m_1v_1+m_2v_2}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_f&=\frac{- 1 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 4}{2 + 3}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second mass contributes negative momentum. The net value is positive, so the combined body moves in the first cart's direction.

Momentum contributes twelve in the positive direction and two in the opposite direction, in $\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. Their balance is ten and total mass is five kilograms. With a common convention, the shared two metres per second lies between the two initial velocities.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart travels east at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart travels north at $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. They stick. Find their speed if horizontal external impulse is zero.

::grafico{uguale=1 vettori="0,0,6,0,p_1;6,0,6,8,p_2;0,0,6,8,P" xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-1 ymax=9 nomi="p_x,p_y" titolo="The initial momentum has two nonzero components"}

$$
v_f=\frac{\sqrt{P_x^2+P_y^2}}{m_1+m_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_f&=\frac{\sqrt{\left(2 \cdot 3\right)^{2} + \left(1 \cdot 8\right)^{2}}}{1 + 2}\\[4pt]&\simeq3.33333\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each momentum component is conserved. Find the total magnitude with the Pythagorean theorem, then divide by the combined mass.

Dividing each momentum component by the system's three kilograms gives $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ east and $8/3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ north. Combining them also gives magnitude $10/3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The vector method avoids adding initially perpendicular speeds as though they were aligned.

</div>
