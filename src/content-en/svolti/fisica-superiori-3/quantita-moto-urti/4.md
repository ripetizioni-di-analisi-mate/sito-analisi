## Exercise 1

Two carts of $3$ and $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ travel along a track in the same direction at $2$ and $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and stick. An external actuator gives the pair $-1\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$ during the collision. Find the signed velocity after contact.

$$
\begin{aligned}P_i&=(3\cdot2+2\cdot1)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}=8\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\\ v_f&=\frac{8-1}{3+2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=1.4\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Add the actuator's impulse to the momentum of the whole pair. Its negative direction reduces the numerator by one unit. The contact impulses between the carts are internal and cancel in the sum. The initial eight kilogram metres per second thus become seven after the collision, shared by all five kilograms, which keep moving forwards.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart travels at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the positive direction. A second cart approaches at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the opposite direction. They stick and move at $+1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with negligible external impulse. Find the second cart's mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}m_1(u_1-v_f)&=m_2(v_f-u_2),\\ m_2&=4\frac{3-1}{1-(-3)}\,\mathrm{kg}=2\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Put the change for the first cart on one side and the change for the second cart on the other. Going from a negative velocity to a positive one changes the second cart's velocity by four metres per second. The first loses two metres per second. The momentum changes must cancel, so the cart with twice the velocity change has half the mass.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ puck has velocity $(2;0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a $1\,\mathrm{kg}$ puck has velocity $(0;-3)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. They stick and receive an external impulse of $(-1;0.6)\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$. Find their common velocity and its magnitude.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,4,-3,Pᵢ;4,-3,3,-2.4,J;0,0,3,-2.4,P_f" uguale=1 xmin=-.9 xmax=5.3 ymin=-4.1 ymax=.8 nomi="Pₓ,Pᵧ" titolo="The impulse is added at the tip of the initial vector. Momentum units are kg·m/s."}

$$
\begin{aligned}\vec P_f&=\big((4;-3)+(-1;0.6)\big)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}=(3;-2.4)\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s},\\ \vec v_f&=(1;-0.8)\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad |\vec v_f|=\frac{\sqrt{41}}5\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq1.28\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First construct the initial momentum from the two masses. Add the impulse component by component. Its westward contribution reduces the horizontal component. Its northward one reduces the magnitude of the negative vertical component. Dividing by the total mass still gives a vector pointing south-east, as shown in the diagram.

</div>
