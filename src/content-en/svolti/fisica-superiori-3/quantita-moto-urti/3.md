## Exercise 1

A $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ ball arrives at $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and stops in $0.20\,\mathrm s$. With the incoming direction positive find the mean net force.

$$
F_m=\frac{m(v_f-v_i)}{\Delta t},\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_m&=- \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot \frac{1}{1 \cdot \frac{1}{5}}\\[4pt]&=-20\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initial momentum is positive and final momentum is zero. The change is negative and remains negative after division by time.

A mean force of $-20\,\mathrm N$ times two tenths of a second gives $-4\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}$. This cancels the initial momentum of $4\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The check also shows that finding an average does not require constant force during contact.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ ball arrives at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and rebounds at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the opposite direction in $0.050\,\mathrm s$. Find the mean net force magnitude.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="0,1,6,1,v_i;0,0,-4,0,v_f" xmin=-5 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=2 titolo="The rebound reverses the direction of velocity"}

$$
F_m=\frac{m(v_{\mathrm{in}}+v_{\mathrm{out}})}{\Delta t},\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_m&=1 \cdot \frac{1}{5} \cdot \left(4 + 6\right) \cdot \frac{1}{1 \cdot \frac{1}{20}}\\[4pt]&=40\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocities point in opposite directions. The magnitude of the momentum change therefore uses the sum of the two speeds.

Taking the incoming direction as positive, velocity changes by $-4-6=-10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Momentum therefore changes by $-2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. The mean force points backwards and has magnitude $40\,\mathrm N$, greater than needed for a mere stop over the same interval.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ object stops from $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ under a mean net force of magnitude $30\,\mathrm N$. Find the braking duration.

$$
\Delta t=\frac{mv}{F_m},\qquad
\begin{aligned}\Delta t&=3 \cdot 5 \cdot \frac{1}{30}\\[4pt]&=0.5\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required impulse cancels the initial momentum. Divide its magnitude by the magnitude of the mean force to obtain time.

Initial momentum is $15\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m/s}$. Thirty newtons for half a second gives the needed impulse magnitude fifteen. A shorter duration would not be enough to produce the same change at that mean force.

</div>
