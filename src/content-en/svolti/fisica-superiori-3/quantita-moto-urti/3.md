## Exercise 1

A $0.75\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart hits a horizontal bumper at $2.4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and rebounds at $0.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after $0.040\,\mathrm s$. Neglect track drag and find the average contact-force component, with the incoming direction positive.

$$
\overline F_x=\frac{m(v_f-v_i)}{\Delta t}=\frac{0.75(-0.8-2.4)}{0.040}\,\mathrm N=-60\,\mathrm N.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Contact reverses the motion, so the final component has the opposite sign to the incoming one. The impulse inferred from the velocity change is horizontal. In this model the bumper supplies all of it. Sixty newtons is the magnitude of the average. Finding the peak would require the force history during contact, for example from a sensor with closely spaced readings.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ ball falls at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and rebounds at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. It is in contact with the floor for $0.10\,\mathrm s$. Neglect air resistance and use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the magnitude of the floor's average force on the ball.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="-.9,1,-.9,-1.4,vᵢ;.9,0,.9,0.8,v_f" linee="-2,-1.6 2,-1.6" xmin=-2.3 xmax=2.3 ymin=-1.9 ymax=1.55 titolo="The incoming arrow points towards the floor. The outgoing arrow points up."}

$$
\begin{aligned}\overline{F}_{\mathrm{net}}&=0.30\frac{2-(-6)}{0.10}\,\mathrm N=24\,\mathrm N,\\ \overline N&=\overline{F}_{\mathrm{net}}+mg=(24+0.30\cdot10)\,\mathrm N=27\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive vertical axis points upwards. The velocity change gives the average net force from both forces together. Weight keeps acting downwards throughout the interval. The floor must supply three newtons more than the average net force. Only weight acts immediately before and after contact in the model, so those instants bound the averaging interval.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two devices accelerate the same body from rest to $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its mass is $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$. The first supplies a constant net force for $0.12\,\mathrm s$. The second force profile is triangular, lasting $0.08\,\mathrm s$ and reaching zero at both ends. Compare the mean forces and find the peak force of the second pulse.

$$
\begin{aligned}J&=1.2\cdot3\,\mathrm{N\cdot s}=3.6\,\mathrm{N\cdot s},\\ \overline F_1&=3.6/0.12\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N,\qquad \overline F_2=3.6/0.08\,\mathrm N=45\,\mathrm N,\\ F_{2,\max}&=2\overline F_2=90\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity change fixes the area for both devices. The second compresses it into a shorter interval, so its mean force is greater. For a triangular profile the base is the full duration, even if the peak is off-centre. Its height is twice the mean. The peak forces are therefore thirty newtons for the first device and ninety for the second.

</div>
