## Exercise 1

Velocity changes from $-3$ to $-9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $2\,\mathrm s$. Find the average acceleration.

::grafico{segmenti="0,-3,2,-9" punti="0,-3,pieno;2,-9,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=2.4 ymin=-10 ymax=1 nomi="t (s),v (m/s)" titolo="The mean slope is −3 m/s²"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{-9-(-3)}2\\[4pt]
&= -3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both velocities point in the negative direction. The final value is more negative, so the change is $-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Division by two seconds gives a negative acceleration. Here the speed grows even though the acceleration is negative.

The speed starts at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and ends at $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Average acceleration is negative because the signed velocity decreases. If the graph is linear between the readings, velocity and acceleration point in the negative direction throughout the interval.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object goes from $v=-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $v=+8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm s$. What is its average acceleration?

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{8-(-4)}3\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity changes from negative to positive. Subtracting the negative initial velocity adds four to the final value, giving a change of $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Dividing by three seconds gives a positive signed result.

If velocity is continuous, it reaches zero at least once between the readings. If acceleration is also constant, that zero occurs after $1\,\mathrm s$, when the increase is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The average acceleration calculation only needs the initial and final readings.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A velocity–time graph reads $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=0$, $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=3\,\mathrm s$ and again $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=5\,\mathrm s$. Velocity varies linearly between readings. Decide whether acceleration is constant over the entire interval, calculate its mean and find the time of reversal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1&=\frac{4-(-2)}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
a_2&=\frac{4-4}{5-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=0,\\[6pt]
a_m&=\frac{4-(-2)}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[6pt]
-2+2t&=0\ \Longrightarrow\ t=1\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Acceleration is the slope of each segment. The first slope is positive and the second is zero, so a single constant acceleration cannot reproduce the three readings. The mean for the full time uses the total change of velocity. It can also be reconstructed by weighting the two slopes by three and two seconds. The first segment starts at negative velocity and ends positive, so find the zero there. The graph crosses the axis after one second. The velocity stays positive and constant on the second segment.

</div>
