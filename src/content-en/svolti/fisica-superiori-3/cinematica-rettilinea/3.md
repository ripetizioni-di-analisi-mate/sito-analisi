## Exercise 1

Velocity changes from $-3$ to $-9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $2\,\mathrm s$. Find the average acceleration.

::grafico{segmenti="0,-3,2,-9" xmin=0 xmax=2.4 ymin=-10 ymax=1 nomi="t,v" titolo="The mean slope is −3 m/s²"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{-9-(-3)}2\\[4pt]
&= -3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both velocities point in the negative direction. The final value is more negative, so the change is $-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Division by two seconds gives a negative acceleration. Here the speed grows even though the acceleration is negative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object goes from $v=-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $v=+8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm s$. What is its average acceleration?

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{8-(-4)}3\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity changes from negative to positive. Subtracting the negative initial velocity adds four to the final value, giving a change of $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Dividing by three seconds gives a positive signed result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body has velocity $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=2\,\mathrm s$, $11\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=5\,\mathrm s$ and $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $t=6\,\mathrm s$. Find its mean acceleration between the first and last readings.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{9-5}{6-2}\\[4pt]
&= 1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The intermediate changes are $+6$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Add them to get $+4\,\mathrm{m/s}$, then divide by the total four seconds. A simple mean of stage accelerations would assign equal weight to unequal durations.

</div>
