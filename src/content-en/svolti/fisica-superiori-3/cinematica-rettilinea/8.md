## Exercise 1

Points $A$ and $B$ start at $3$ and $31\,\mathrm m$. Their constant velocities are $+5$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what coordinate do they meet?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{31-3}{5-(-2)}=4\,\mathrm s\\[4pt]x&=3+5\cdot4\\[4pt]
&= 23\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Point $A$ advances and $B$ approaches from higher coordinates. Their distance closes by seven metres per second. After four seconds either equation gives the meeting coordinate. For $B$ it is $31-2\cdot4=23\,\mathrm m$, confirming the intersection.

The points start $28\,\mathrm m$ apart. Every second their separation decreases by the sum of their speeds, $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$. This recovers the meeting time and verifies that B, starting farther ahead, travels eight metres in the negative direction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cyclist starts at $x=0$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Another starts from $x=40\,\mathrm m$ towards her at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after a $4\,\mathrm s$ delay. How many seconds after the second cyclist starts do they meet?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{40-4\cdot4}{4+2}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During the delay only the first cyclist moves, reducing the gap from forty to twenty-four metres. Once the second starts, their speeds close the gap by six metres each second. The requested time is measured from the second event, not from the first start.

When the second cyclist starts, the first is at $16\,\mathrm m$, leaving a gap of $24\,\mathrm m$. Four seconds after the second start, both are at $32\,\mathrm m$. Eight seconds have passed since the first start, while the question uses time since the second.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two trolleys on parallel tracks use the same coordinate $x$. A starts at $0$ and moves at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ until $t=2\,\mathrm s$, then turns back at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. B starts at the same instant at $4\,\mathrm m$ and moves at $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find all times in the first $3\,\mathrm s$ when they are side by side, and the corresponding coordinates.

::grafico{f="4*x-3*((x-2)+abs(x-2));4+x" punti="1.3333333,5.3333333,pieno;2,8,pieno;2.6666667,6.6666667,pieno" etichette="1.1,5.65,I₁;2.62,7.19,I₂" legenda="x_A;x_B" xmin=0 xmax=3.22 ymin=0 ymax=9 nomi="t (s),x (m)" titolo="The trolleys are side by side at I₁ as A moves forward and at I₂ after it reverses."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_B(t)&=4+t,\\[4pt]
4t=4+t&\ \Longrightarrow\ t_1=\tfrac43\,\mathrm s,\quad x_1=\tfrac{16}3\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
x_A(t)&=8-2(t-2)=12-2t\quad\text{for }2\leq t\leq3,\\[4pt]
12-2t=4+t&\ \Longrightarrow\ t_2=\tfrac83\,\mathrm s,\quad x_2=\tfrac{20}3\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the first leg A catches B because its position–time graph has a steeper slope. The first time is less than two seconds and lies on that branch. At its reversal A is at eight metres, which is the initial coordinate of the second leg. The new equation gives a second time between two and three seconds, so the second solution is physical as well. B is still behind when A reverses. The slopes now have opposite signs, so the trolleys approach each other again and their coordinates become equal a second time.

</div>
