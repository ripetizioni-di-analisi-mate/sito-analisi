## Exercise 1

A vehicle travelling at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ continues for $1\,\mathrm s$ during reaction, then brakes at constant deceleration magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its total stopping distance?

::grafico{curve="t,18,0,1;t,21-3*t,1,7" area="18;0;0;1|21-3*x;0;1;7" segmenti="1,0,1,18" punti="1,18,pieno;7,0,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=7.5 ymin=0 ymax=20 nomi="t (s),v (m/s)" titolo="Total distance includes a rectangle and a triangle"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=v_0t_r+\frac{v_0^2}{2d}=18+\frac{18^2}{2\cdot3}\\[4pt]
&= 72\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During reaction the speed is steady and covers eighteen metres. During braking it falls from eighteen to zero in six seconds: the velocity–time triangle has area fifty-four metres. The two areas add because velocity remains along the initial direction.

The change occurs at one second. Braking takes another $6\,\mathrm s$, so the stop occurs at total time seven seconds. The triangle with base six gives $54\,\mathrm m$, to be added to the $18\,\mathrm m$ travelled before braking starts.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A car at $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ brakes without delay and stops in $50\,\mathrm m$. What is the magnitude of the constant deceleration?

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{20^2}{2\cdot50}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The time-free equation relates zero final velocity to the drop in $v^2$. Divide the square of the initial speed by twice the distance to obtain the magnitude of deceleration. The result implies five seconds of braking at mean speed $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $10\cdot5=50\,\mathrm m$.

With the positive axis along the motion, acceleration is $-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Velocity falls from twenty to zero in five seconds. Average velocity during braking is $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$, which again gives fifty metres. The sign and magnitude answer different questions.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A trolley enters a $24\,\mathrm m$ section with positive velocity. It keeps its initial speed for $0.5\,\mathrm s$ and then brakes with constant acceleration of magnitude $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the greatest initial speed that lets it stop within the section?

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{tot}}&=v_0t_r+\frac{v_0^2}{2|a|},\\[4pt]
0.5v_0+\frac{v_0^2}{8}&=24\iff(v_0-12)(v_0+16)=0,\\[6pt]
v_{0,\max}&=12\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[4pt]
d_r&=(0.5\cdot12)\,\mathrm m=6\,\mathrm m,\qquad
d_f=\frac{12^2}{8}\,\mathrm m=18\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The trolley covers an area given by a rectangle in the velocity–time graph before braking begins. During braking, the distance follows from the equation with zero final velocity. Both distances increase with positive initial speed, so the maximum speed uses all twenty-four metres. The negative root of the equation does not represent a speed. At the positive root the trolley travels for half a second before braking and for another three seconds before stopping. The two distances add up to the stated limit.

</div>
