## Exercise 1

An object has $v_0=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $a=-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its velocity after $2\,\mathrm s$ if the same acceleration persists beyond the reversal?

::grafico{f="6-4*x" punti="1.5,0,pieno;2,-2,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=2.4 ymin=-4 ymax=7 nomi="t,v" titolo="Velocity crosses zero before 2 s"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=v_0+at=6-4\cdot2\\[4pt]
&= -2\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The change in velocity is $-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its magnitude exceeds the initial speed, so the body passes through $v=0$ at $1.5\,\mathrm s$ and moves in the negative direction. The requested quantity is signed velocity and retains that sign.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a constant-acceleration stage, velocity rises from $2$ to $14\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over $6\,\mathrm s$. What distance is covered?

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\frac{v_0+v_f}{2}t=\frac{2+14}{2}\cdot6\\[4pt]
&= 48\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity–time graph is a straight line above the axis. Its trapezium area uses the mean of the two endpoint velocities as its average height. This is $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and covers forty-eight metres in six seconds.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cart starts at $v_0=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and accelerates at $+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $3\,\mathrm s$. What is its displacement?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=v_0t+\tfrac12at^2=-2\cdot3+\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot3^2\\[4pt]
&= 3\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity vanishes at one second, after a displacement of $-1\,\mathrm m$. During the next two seconds the object accelerates in the positive direction and covers $4\,\mathrm m$. Their signed sum is $3\,\mathrm m$, matching the position equation.

</div>
