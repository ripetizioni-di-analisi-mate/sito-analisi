## Exercise 1

An object falls from rest from $20\,\mathrm m$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How many seconds pass before it reaches the ground? Neglect air resistance.

::grafico{curve="t,20-5*t^2,0,2" punti="0,20,pieno;2,0,pieno" etichette="0.09,20.73,L (20 m)" xmin=0 xmax=2.25 ymin=-0.5 ymax=22 nomi="t (s),y (m)" titolo="The parabola reaches the ground at 2 s"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot20}{10}}\\[4pt]
&= 2\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical axis points up and height is $y=20-5t^2$. Contact occurs when height reaches zero. Of the two roots, only the positive one describes an event after release. The graph ends at contact because the model changes after impact.

With the vertical axis positive upwards, the velocity at contact is $-20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The triangular area in the velocity graph is $-20\,\mathrm m$. Adding the displacement to the initial height gives zero, consistent with the endpoint in the graph.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object is dropped from a tower. It reaches the ground $3\,\mathrm s$ after the release is recorded. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How high is the tower? Neglect air resistance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=\tfrac12gt^2=\tfrac12\cdot10\cdot3^2\\[4pt]
&= 45\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interval starts at release, so initial velocity is zero. The fall increases speed to $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Average speed is $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for the three seconds, giving $45\,\mathrm m$.

At contact the speed is $30\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Half that value times three seconds gives the distance found. Starting the recording at release fixes both the elapsed time and the initial velocity of zero. A later recording would instead begin at a nonzero velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object is released from rest and travels $35\,\mathrm m$ in the last second before reaching the ground. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. How long does the fall last and from what height was it released?

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(t)&=5t^2,\\[4pt]
d(T)-d(T-1)&=5\bigl[T^2-(T-1)^2\bigr]=10T-5,\\[4pt]
10T-5&=35\ \Longrightarrow\ T=4\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
h&=(5\cdot4^2)\,\mathrm m=80\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Measure the drop from the release height. The final second is the interval that ends at total time $T$ and starts one second earlier. The given distance is therefore the difference between two cumulative drops. That interval does not start from rest, so a duration of one second alone cannot be inserted into the formula for a fall from rest. With $T=4\,\mathrm s$, check using the average velocity in the final second. The speeds of $30$ and $40\,\mathrm{m/s}$ vary linearly, so their mean times one second gives thirty-five metres.

</div>
