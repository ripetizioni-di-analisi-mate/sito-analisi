## Exercise 1

A cart accelerates from rest at $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ for $2\,\mathrm s$ and then travels at constant speed for another $3\,\mathrm s$. How far does it travel?

::grafico{curve="t,3*t,0,2;t,6,2,5" area="3*x;0;0;2|6;0;2;5" segmenti="2,0,2,6" punti="2,6,pieno;5,6,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=5.4 ymin=-0.6 ymax=7 nomi="t (s),v (m/s)" titolo="The triangle and rectangle both contribute to distance travelled"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\tfrac12\cdot3\cdot2^2+(3\cdot2)\cdot3\\[4pt]
&= 24\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During acceleration the cart goes from zero to $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and covers six metres. At the transition it retains $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the steady stage adds eighteen metres. The graph shows a triangle followed by a rectangle.

The second leg starts at the $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ speed reached at the transition, so the graph is continuous. The rectangular area gives $18\,\mathrm m$ and the initial triangle gives $6\,\mathrm m$. Extending the acceleration across all five seconds would increase velocity during the stated uniform leg.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart from rest travels $18\,\mathrm m$ during its first $3\,\mathrm s$ at constant acceleration. What is its acceleration?

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{2s}{t^2}=\frac{2\cdot18}{3^2}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Distance from rest depends on acceleration and squared duration. Solve $\frac12at^2=s$ for the unknown. At $a=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ the final speed is $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the mean is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, consistent with eighteen metres.

At the end of the interval the velocity is $a t=12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The average velocity is half the final value because the endpoints are joined by a straight line on the velocity–time graph. Multiplying the average by three seconds recovers the measured eighteen metres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A trolley starts from rest with constant positive acceleration. Two video frames, at laboratory clock times $4\,\mathrm s$ and $6\,\mathrm s$, show it $9\,\mathrm m$ and $25\,\mathrm m$ from the start. Release occurs before both frames. Find the acceleration and the clock reading at release.

Set $x=0$ at the release point and denote the release time by $t_0$. The intermediate steps use numerical values in metres and seconds.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t)&=\tfrac12a(t-t_0)^2,\\[4pt]
3&=\sqrt{\tfrac12a}\,(4-t_0),\qquad
5=\sqrt{\tfrac12a}\,(6-t_0),\\[4pt]
2&=2\sqrt{\tfrac12a}\ \Longrightarrow\ a=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[4pt]
t_0&=(4-3)\,\mathrm s=1\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The elapsed time is the difference between a reading and the release reading. Both frames come after release, so that difference is positive and is the one obtained by taking the square roots of the distances. Subtract the equations to eliminate the unknown release reading. This leaves the slope of the straight line for square root of distance against time, which determines acceleration. With a release reading of one second, five seconds have passed at the second frame and the distance is indeed $25\,\mathrm m$.

</div>
