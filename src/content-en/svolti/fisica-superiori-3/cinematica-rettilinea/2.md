## Exercise 1

A runner goes from $x=0$ to $x=16\,\mathrm m$ and back to $x=4\,\mathrm m$ in a total of $8\,\mathrm s$. What is the average speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{\mathrm{rap}}&=\frac{16+(16-4)}{8}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The route includes $16\,\mathrm m$ outward and $12\,\mathrm m$ on the return. Add both lengths as positive and divide by the total time. Average velocity would be $0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ because it would use only the final displacement of four metres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sensor records $x=10\,\mathrm m$ at $t=1\,\mathrm s$ and $x=-5\,\mathrm m$ at $t=6\,\mathrm s$. Find the average velocity.

::grafico{segmenti="1,10,6,-5" punti="1,10,pieno;6,-5,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-7 ymax=12 nomi="t,x" titolo="The secant through the two readings has slope −3 m/s"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_m&=\frac{x_f-x_i}{t_f-t_i}\\[4pt]&=\frac{-5-10}{6-1}\\[4pt]
&= -3\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final coordinate is fifteen metres below the first. Five seconds pass between the readings. The negative sign describes the net displacement direction even if the motion was not uniform.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At a transition, the mean velocities immediately before and after are $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The position graph is straight on each side. What is the slope after minus the slope before?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta v&=(-2)-3\\[4pt]
&= -5\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slopes give the two signed velocities. The later one is negative and the earlier one positive, so their difference is more negative than either one alone. The corner marks the transition between the two behaviours and gives no unique instantaneous velocity at the change.

</div>
