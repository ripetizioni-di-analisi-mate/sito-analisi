## Exercise 1

A hoist lifts $30\,\mathrm{kg}$ through $5\,\mathrm m$ in $6\,\mathrm s$ at constant speed. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find useful average power.

::grafico{f="300*x" punti="5,1500,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=1800 nomi="h (m),W (J)" titolo="Useful work versus height gain at constant speed"}

$$
P_u=\frac{mgh}{\Delta t},\qquad
\begin{aligned}P_u&=30 \cdot 10 \cdot 5 \cdot \frac{1}{6}\\[4pt]&=250\,\mathrm W.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Speed is constant, so the mass gains no kinetic energy. The hoist supplies fifteen hundred joules over six seconds.

The useful output can also be checked through force and speed. The motor pulls with $300\,\mathrm N$ and raises the load at $5/6\,\mathrm{m/s}$, giving $250\,\mathrm W$. Gravity has the opposite power, so speed stays constant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A lift raises $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ by $7\,\mathrm m$ in $5\,\mathrm s$ with efficiency $0.80$. At constant speed and with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find average input power.

$$
P_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{mgh}{\eta\Delta t},\qquad
\begin{aligned}P_{\mathrm{in}}&=40 \cdot 10 \cdot 7 \cdot \frac{1}{4 \cdot 5 \cdot \frac{1}{5}}\\[4pt]&=700\,\mathrm W.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find useful power from the height gain. It equals eighty per cent of the input power, so divide useful power by efficiency.

Weight times rise gives $2800\,\mathrm J$. Divide by five seconds to get $560\,\mathrm W$ of useful output. Multiplying $700\,\mathrm W$ of input by $0.80$ recovers that value, so the powers are consistent.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A machine raises a $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ load by $3\,\mathrm m$ in $4\,\mathrm s$. The load starts from rest and arrives at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and no other losses, find the average power delivered to the load.

$$
P_m=\frac{mg\Delta h+\Delta K}{\Delta t},\qquad
\begin{aligned}P_m&=\frac{\frac{20 \cdot 2^{2}}{2} + 20 \cdot 10 \cdot 3}{4}\\[4pt]&=160\,\mathrm W.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load gains potential and kinetic energy. Add both gains and divide by the time interval.

The gravitational gain is $600\,\mathrm J$ and the kinetic gain is $40\,\mathrm J$. Omitting the second would amount to assuming equal initial and final speed. Here average power uses the work done in four seconds, while instantaneous power can vary during the lift.

</div>
