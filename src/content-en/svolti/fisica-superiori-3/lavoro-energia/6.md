## Exercise 1

A cart enters a frictionless descent at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and drops $2\,\mathrm m$. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and find the speed at the bottom.

::grafico{linee="0,2 3,0" punti="0,2,pieno,A;3,0,pieno,B" xmin=-.5 xmax=4 ymin=-.5 ymax=3 nomi="s,h (m)" titolo="Schematic descent with a vertical drop of two metres"}


$$
v_f=\sqrt{v_i^2+2g(h_i-h_f)}
=\sqrt{3^2+2\cdot10\cdot2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=7\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There is kinetic energy at entry as well. After cancelling the mass in the balance, add the descent term to the initial squared speed.

The height drop contributes $40\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ to squared speed, while entry contributes $9\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Their sum is forty-nine, giving seven metres per second. Adding speeds directly would not represent an energy balance.

The drop contributes to the squared speed. Adding the initial speed directly to the speed from rest would predict too much speed because the terms that add in this balance are energies.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object enters a smooth stationary ramp upwards at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find the maximum height above the entry point, where the object stops before returning.


$$
\Delta h_{\rm max}=\dfrac{v_i^2}{2g}
=\dfrac{10^2}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=5\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the turning point the kinetic energy is zero. All the kinetic energy at entry has become gravitational potential energy.

At ten metres per second initial energy is $50\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. Each metre of ascent adds $10\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ of potential energy, so the available energy gives a five-metre rise if the ramp extends that far.

The result is a vertical height above the entry point. If the angle of a straight ramp were also known, the distance along the slope would follow by dividing by the sine of its angle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A block starts from rest at height $1.0\,\mathrm m$ constrained to a fixed, smooth track that drops to height $0$. It then climbs towards a second crest at $1.10\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find its speed as it rises through height $0.20\,\mathrm m$ and decide whether it reaches the second crest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac12v^2&=g(h_i-h)=10(1.0-0.20)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}
=8\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}\\[4pt]
v&=4\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad h_{\rm max}=h_i=1.0\,\mathrm m<1.10\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With a fixed, smooth track, the normal force does no work while contact is maintained. The block gains kinetic energy downhill, then loses some of it during the climb. At $0.20\,\mathrm m$ it retains the energy corresponding to a height drop of $0.80\,\mathrm m$ from release. Higher up, the greatest height accessible by energy is the starting height, where speed reaches zero. The crest at $1.10\,\mathrm m$ would require more energy than is available. Crossing it would require an energy input or a nonzero initial speed.

</div>
