## Exercise 1

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart slows from $8$ to $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the net work?

::grafico{f="2.5*x^2" punti="6,90,pieno,B;8,160,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=185 nomi="v (m/s),K (J)" titolo="The ordinates at A and B are the energies before and after slowing"}

$$
W=K_f-K_i,\qquad
\begin{aligned}W&=\frac{5 \cdot \left(- 8^{2} + 6^{2}\right)}{2}\\[4pt]&=-70\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two kinetic energies are one hundred sixty and ninety joules. Their change is negative because the body loses kinetic energy.

Use two squared speeds because the comparison involves two states. Squaring the speed difference alone would always give a positive value and miss the seventy-joule loss. On the graph the final point lies below the initial one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ sled moves at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A push does $70\,\mathrm J$ of work and resistance does $-25\,\mathrm J$. Find its final speed.

$$
v_f=\sqrt{v_i^2+2W_{\mathrm{tot}}/m},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_f&=\sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot \left(70 - 25\right)}{10} + 4^{2}}\\[4pt]&=5\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The algebraic sum of work is forty-five joules. Add it to the initial kinetic energy and take the positive root for speed.

At entry the sled has $80\,\mathrm J$. The positive net work brings this to $125\,\mathrm J$. Using the final speed of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives $\dfrac12\cdot10\cdot5^2\,\mathrm J=125\,\mathrm J$. Both energy and speed have increased.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $600\,\mathrm{kg}$ vehicle travels at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The brake exerts a constant resisting force of $2400\,\mathrm N$. What distance is needed to stop it on level ground?

$$
d=\frac{mv_i^2}{2f},\qquad
\begin{aligned}d&=\frac{600 \cdot 12^{2}}{2 \cdot 2400}\\[4pt]&=18\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At rest, kinetic energy is zero. Resisting work equals $-fd$, so equating it to minus the initial energy determines the distance.

Weight and normal force are perpendicular to the horizontal motion. Braking is therefore the remaining work term. Multiplying $2400\,\mathrm N$ by $18\,\mathrm m$ recovers $43200\,\mathrm J$, the whole initial kinetic energy.

</div>
