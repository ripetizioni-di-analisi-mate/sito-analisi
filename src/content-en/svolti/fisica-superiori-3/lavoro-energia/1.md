## Exercise 1

A crate moves $5\,\mathrm m$ under a $24\,\mathrm N$ pull at $60^\circ$ to its displacement. Find the work done by the pull.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,5,0,s;0,0,2,3.464,F" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=4 titolo="The force and displacement make a $60^\circ$ angle"}


$$
W=Fs\cos\theta=24\cdot5\cdot0.5\,\mathrm J=60\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cosine gives the force component along the displacement. At sixty degrees it equals one half, so the forward component is twelve newtons.

The vertical component can affect the normal force. The point of application moves horizontally, so the vertical component does zero work.

A useful check starts with an entirely horizontal pull, which would give $120\,\mathrm J$. At sixty degrees the projection is exactly half as large, giving $60\,\mathrm J$.

The pull also has a perpendicular component, which could affect contact with the surface. The work of the pull requested here comes only from its horizontal projection.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The wind exerts an $18\,\mathrm N$ force at $120^\circ$ to a boat's $6\,\mathrm m$ displacement. Find its work.


$$
W=Fs\cos\theta=18\cdot6\left(-\dfrac12\right)\,\mathrm J=-54\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cosine of an obtuse angle is negative. It equals minus one half here, so the wind has a nine-newton component opposing the displacement.

The sign gives the direction of energy transfer. Dividing the work by the displacement magnitude recovers the signed component.

To interpret the sign, replace the wind by its $9\,\mathrm N$ opposing horizontal component. Over six metres it removes $54\,\mathrm J$. The transverse component is perpendicular and does not change the work.

In the frame of the dock the wind removes mechanical energy from the boat over those six metres. The wind's work alone cannot predict the final speed while other forces act.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A trailer moves $8\,\mathrm m$. The tow rope pulls with $50\,\mathrm N$ at $60^\circ$ to the displacement, and horizontal resistance is $15\,\mathrm N$. Find the net work.


$$
W_{\rm net}=(50\cos60^\circ-15)\cdot8\,\mathrm J=80\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal component of the pull is twenty-five newtons. Resistance acts in the opposite direction, so the net component along the displacement is ten newtons.

Weight and the normal force are vertical and do zero work. Adding two hundred joules and minus one hundred and twenty joules separately gives the same result.

As a check, $80\,\mathrm J$ over eight metres is equivalent to $10\,\mathrm N$ along the motion. If resistance reached $25\,\mathrm N$, it would cancel the pull's projection and the total work would be zero.

Calculating the two contributions separately gives $+200\,\mathrm J$ from the rope and $-120\,\mathrm J$ from resistance. They would cancel if the resistance were $25\,\mathrm N$, equal to the projected pull.

</div>
