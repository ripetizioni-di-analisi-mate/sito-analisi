## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart passes a sensor at $18\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Find its kinetic energy in the laboratory frame.

::grafico{f="2*x^2" punti="5,50,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=76 nomi="v (m/s),K (J)" titolo="Kinetic energy of the cart versus speed"}


$$
v=\dfrac{18}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s}=5\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad K=\dfrac12\cdot4\cdot5^2\,\mathrm J=50\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert speed before squaring it. Eighteen kilometres in one hour is eighteen thousand metres in thirty-six hundred seconds, giving five metres per second.

The resulting units are kilogram metres squared per second squared. This combination equals a joule.

The curve shows a fifty-joule ordinate at five metres per second. Using eighteen without conversion would treat the speed as $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and overestimate energy by a factor of $3.6^2$.

A sensor reporting twice the speed after conversion would give four times the energy. The square of the speed in base units sets how much an error in conversion affects the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ body has velocity components $-4$ and $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find its kinetic energy.


$$
v^2=v_x^2+v_y^2=((-4)^2+3^2)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}=25\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2},
\qquad K=\dfrac12\cdot3\cdot25\,\mathrm J=37.5\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axes are perpendicular, so add the squares of the components. The squared speed is twenty-five, and the sign of the first component does not affect the energy.

The speed is five metres per second. Adding the two components directly would incorrectly treat them as lying on the same axis.

The same result follows from adding $24\,\mathrm J$ from the horizontal component and $13.5\,\mathrm J$ from the vertical one. This equals using speed because on perpendicular axes $v^2=v_x^2+v_y^2$.

Reversing the horizontal axis makes the first component positive. The speed and kinetic energy stay the same because the frame remains at rest and only the orientation of its axes changes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cyclist and bicycle have combined mass $80\,\mathrm{kg}$ and travel east at $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the road. An observer moves north at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the road without rotating the axes. Find the cyclist's velocity and the kinetic energy measured in the observer's frame.

$$
\vec v'=(9,-4)\,\mathrm{m/s},
\qquad K'=\dfrac12\cdot80(9^2+4^2)\,\mathrm J=3880\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take east and north as positive. Find relative velocity by subtracting the observer's velocity vector from the cyclist's ground velocity. The eastwards component stays fixed and the northwards component becomes negative. The cyclist moves towards the south-east in the new frame. The components are perpendicular, so the squared speed is $97\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. On the road, the eastwards component alone would give $3240\,\mathrm J$. Moving with the observer transversely adds a component to the relative motion. Moving east at the same speed would instead subtract from the eastwards component.

</div>
