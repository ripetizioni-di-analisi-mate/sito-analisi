## Exercise 1

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ crate travels $4\,\mathrm m$ along a ramp inclined at $30^\circ$. Taking $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the rise in potential energy.

::grafico{uguale=1 assi=0 linee="0,0 3.464,2 3.464,0 0,0" punti="0,0,pieno,A;3.464,2,pieno,B" xmin=-.6 xmax=4.5 ymin=-.5 ymax=2.8 titolo="A four-metre ramp gives a rise of two metres"}


$$
h=s\sin30^\circ=4\cdot0.5\,\mathrm m=2\,\mathrm m,
\qquad \Delta U_g=mgh=5\cdot10\cdot2\,\mathrm J=100\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The height change is the vertical component of displacement along the ramp. The sine of thirty degrees is one half, giving a rise of two metres.

Gravitational potential depends on height, so the sloping length cannot be counted as vertical rise. Weight of $50\,\mathrm N$ through $2\,\mathrm m$ of rise gives a $100\,\mathrm J$ gain. Gravity does the opposite work.

Gravity therefore does $-100\,\mathrm J$ of work. The work done by the pull would also depend on friction and any gain in kinetic energy. Height alone determines the potential energy change.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ parcel is $2\,\mathrm m$ below the chosen zero level, with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the potential energy of the parcel–Earth system.


$$
U_g=mgh=3\cdot10\cdot(-2)\,\mathrm J=-60\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The height is negative relative to the chosen level. The negative potential energy is consistent with that choice of zero.

The negative sign comes from the chosen zero. Bring the parcel to that level without changing speed and it requires $60\,\mathrm J$, the difference between the level's energy and the initial value. Adding the same constant to both values leaves this interpretation unchanged.

Lifting the parcel to the reference plane raises its potential energy by $60\,\mathrm J$. Moving the zero lower shifts both values by the same constant and leaves this difference unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ object first rises $5\,\mathrm m$ and then drops $8\,\mathrm m$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and find the total work done by weight.


$$
W_g=-mg\Delta h=-4\cdot10(5-8)\,\mathrm J=120\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object ends three metres lower down. Weight is conservative and its work equals the negative of the potential energy change.

Equivalently, calculate each segment separately. Gravity does $-200\,\mathrm J$ on the ascent and $320\,\mathrm J$ on the descent. The net $120\,\mathrm J$ agrees with using only the endpoint heights.

The upwards leg contributes $-200\,\mathrm J$ and the downward leg $+320\,\mathrm J$. Adding the work on both legs checks the result directly without tracking the potential value at the turning point.

</div>
