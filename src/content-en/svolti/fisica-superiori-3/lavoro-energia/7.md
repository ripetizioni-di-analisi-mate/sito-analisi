## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ block slides $5\,\mathrm m$ on a stationary horizontal floor with $\mu_d=0.20$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the work done by friction.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,5,0,s;0,0,-1.5,0,f" assi=0 xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=2 titolo="Kinetic friction points opposite to displacement"}

$$
W_f=-\mu_d mgs,\qquad
\begin{aligned}W_f&=- \frac{1}{5} \cdot 4 \cdot 10 \cdot 5\\[4pt]&=-40\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forty-newton normal force follows from vertical equilibrium. Friction has magnitude eight newtons and opposes displacement.

Each metre dissipates eight joules under these conditions. Multiply by five for five metres and give the work a negative sign because friction opposes motion. Weight has no component along the path and affects friction through the normal force.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ crate is pulled along a floor by a force with an upward component of $20\,\mathrm N$. It remains in contact and travels $3\,\mathrm m$, with $\mu_d=0.25$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the work of friction.

$$
W_f=-\mu_d(mg-F_y)s,\qquad
\begin{aligned}W_f&=- \frac{1}{4} \cdot \left(\left(-1\right) \cdot 20 + 6 \cdot 10\right) \cdot 3\\[4pt]&=-30\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The upward pull supports part of the weight. Vertical equilibrium gives a forty-newton normal force, so friction has magnitude ten newtons.

With no vertical pull component, the normal force would be sixty newtons and friction fifteen newtons. This explains the lower work magnitude in the given case. Work stays negative because the stationary floor resists the direction of sliding.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ sled enters a rough level section at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $\mu_d=0.20$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find its stopping distance.

$$
d=\frac{v_i^2}{2\mu_d g},\qquad
\begin{aligned}d&=\frac{4^{2}}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 10 \cdot \frac{1}{5}}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Friction takes kinetic energy from forty joules to zero. The resisting force is ten newtons, so distance is the ratio of initial energy to resistance.

Mass cancels because it multiplies both initial energy and friction. With the given values each metre removes ten joules. Four metres remove exactly forty joules, and any period after the stop requires a separate balance.

</div>
