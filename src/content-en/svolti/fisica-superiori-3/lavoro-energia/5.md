## Exercise 1

A spring of stiffness $400\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is stretched by $0.10\,\mathrm m$. Find the elastic energy.

::grafico{f="400*x" area="400*x;0;0;.1" punti=".1,40,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=.13 ymin=0 ymax=52 nomi="x (m),F (N)" titolo="The area below the line is the stored energy"}

$$
U_e=\frac12 kx^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}U_e&=\frac{400 \cdot \left(1 \cdot \frac{1}{10}\right)^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the final deformation the elastic force magnitude is forty newtons. The displacement average is twenty newtons because the graph starts at zero and is a straight line.

Mean force with respect to displacement is half the final force because the graph is a line from the origin. Twenty newtons across a tenth of a metre gives two joules. Using the final force over the whole deformation would double the energy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A spring of stiffness $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ changes from a compression of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ to one of $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the increase in elastic energy.

$$
\Delta U_e=\frac12 k(x_f^2-x_i^2),\qquad
\begin{aligned}\Delta U_e&=\frac{200 \cdot \left(- \left(\frac{4}{100}\right)^{2} + \left(\frac{12}{100}\right)^{2}\right)}{2}\\[4pt]&=1.28\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert both deformations to metres. Subtract the energies at the endpoints because the spring was already compressed at the start.

At four centimetres the energy is $0.16\,\mathrm J$, and at twelve centimetres it is $1.44\,\mathrm J$. Their difference is $1.28\,\mathrm J$. The factor of nine agrees with a factor of three in deformation and a square dependence.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart starts from rest and is launched horizontally by a $400\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring compressed by $0.15\,\mathrm m$. Neglect friction and spring mass. Find the speed as the spring reaches its natural length and releases the cart.

$$
v=x\sqrt{\frac km},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v&=\sqrt{\frac{400}{2}} \cdot 15 \cdot \frac{1}{100}\\[4pt]&\simeq2.12132\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initial elastic energy becomes kinetic energy. The spring has zero deformation at release, so the balance contains only the cart's kinetic energy in the final state.

Initially the spring stores $4.50\,\mathrm J$. After release the two-kilogram cart's kinetic energy in joules is numerically equal to $v^2$ in SI units. The square root of $4.50$ is about $2.12\,\mathrm{m/s}$, confirming the direct calculation.

</div>
