## Exercise 1

A pump keeps $4\,\mathrm{L/s}$ for $10\,\mathrm s$. Find transported volume.

$$
\Delta V=Q\Delta t=(4\,\mathrm{L/s})(10\,\mathrm s)=40\,\mathrm L.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A constant rate assigns the same volume to each one-second interval. Here there are ten contributions and each transfers four litres. On a graph of $Q$ against $t$, the rectangle's area gives their sum. Litres per second times seconds leaves litres. The same pump would transfer twice the volume in twice the time if the rate remained unchanged. If only the rate at the end of the interval were known, with no information about its constancy, multiplying it by the duration would be unjustified.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pump starts at zero flow and rises linearly to $4\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in the first $6\,\mathrm s$. The rate then stays constant. How many seconds after startup has the pump transferred $32\,\mathrm L$?

::grafico{linee="0,0 6,4 13,4" area="2*x/3;0;0;6|4;0;6;11" punti="6,4,pieno;11,4,pieno" segmenti="6,0,6,4;11,0,11,4" xmin=0 xmax=13.5 ymin=0 ymax=5.35 nomi="t (s),Q (L/s)" titolo="The target volume is the combined area of the triangle and rectangle."}

$$
\begin{aligned}V_1&=\frac{6\cdot4}{2}\,\mathrm L=12\,\mathrm L,\\ \Delta t_2&=\frac{(32-12)\,\mathrm L}{4\,\mathrm{L/s}}=5\,\mathrm s,\\ t_f&=(6+5)\,\mathrm s=11\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before finding the time, identify which part of the graph reaches the required volume. The triangular area over the first six seconds is twelve litres, below the target. Twenty litres remain to be pumped at four litres per second. This gives the duration of the second stage alone. Add the first six seconds to find the time since startup. On the graph the result is a triangle plus a rectangle, whose areas add to $32\,\mathrm L$. Dividing the full volume by the final rate would treat the startup phase as constant and give a time that is too short.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A tank holds $15\,\mathrm L$ and receives a constant flow of $3\,\mathrm{L/s}$ while its only outlet releases $1\,\mathrm{L/s}$. Find its volume after $8\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\begin{aligned}Q_{\mathrm{net}}&=(3-1)\,\mathrm{L/s}=2\,\mathrm{L/s},\\ V_f&=(15+2\cdot8)\,\mathrm L=31\,\mathrm L.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the liquid inside the tank as the system. Inflow adds to its volume and outflow reduces it. In the first eight seconds, $24\,\mathrm L$ enters and $8\,\mathrm L$ leaves. The difference of $16\,\mathrm L$ is the change and must be added to the initial volume. Taking the net rate first and multiplying by the duration gives the same result because both rates apply throughout. With equal rates, the tank would retain its initial volume even while liquid kept flowing through.

</div>
