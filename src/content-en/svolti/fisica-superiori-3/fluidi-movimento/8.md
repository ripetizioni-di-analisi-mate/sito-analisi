## Exercise 1

A small sphere with $r=0.001\,\mathrm m$ moves at $0.002\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a liquid of viscosity $0.10\,\mathrm{Pa\cdot s}$. At very low Reynolds number find the Stokes drag magnitude.

$$
F_v=6\pi\eta rv,\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_v&=6 \cdot \pi \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{10} \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{1000} \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{500}\\[4pt]&\simeq3.76991\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The linear law applies at the stated low Reynolds number. All factors are in SI units, giving newtons.

The speed is measured relative to the liquid far from the sphere. The product of units is $(\mathrm{Pa}\cdot\mathrm s)\mathrm m(\mathrm{m/s})=\mathrm{Pa}\cdot\mathrm{m^2}=\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In Stokes flow the same sphere doubles its relative speed and moves into a liquid of triple viscosity. Find the drag force ratio.

$$
F_2/F_1=\eta_2v_2/(\eta_1v_1),\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_2/F_1&=2 \cdot 3\\[4pt]&=6.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Radius is unchanged and cancels. Speed and viscosity both enter linearly, so their factors multiply.

In each situation drag opposes motion relative to the liquid. The ratio $6$ compares magnitudes and holds while both situations remain in the Stokes regime.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At terminal downward speed, drag is $0.006\,\mathrm N$ and buoyancy is $0.004\,\mathrm N$. What is the sphere's weight?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve=".48*cos(t),.48*sin(t),0,6.283185" vettori="0,0,0,-2,P;-.3,0,-.3,1.2,F_v;.3,0,.3,.8,F_A" xmin=-1.4 xmax=1.4 ymin=-2.4 ymax=1.8 titolo="Buoyancy and drag balance weight at terminal speed"}

$$
P=F_v+F_A,\qquad
\begin{aligned}P&=\frac{4 + 6}{1000}\\[4pt]&=0.01\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At terminal speed acceleration is zero. Drag and buoyancy both point upward and their sum balances weight.

Taking upward as positive, the check is $+0.006+0.004-0.010=0$ in newtons. Before reaching terminal speed the drag would be smaller with the other forces the same.

</div>
