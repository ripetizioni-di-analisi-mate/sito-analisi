## Exercise 1

Water in an ideal horizontal pipe speeds up from $2$ to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the pressure drop.

$$
\Delta p=\frac12\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2),\qquad
\begin{aligned}\Delta p&=\frac{1000 \cdot \left(- 2^{2} + 4^{2}\right)}{2}\\[4pt]&=6000\,\mathrm{Pa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equal elevation the pressure drop supplies the gain in kinetic energy per volume. Subtract the squared speeds.

Along a streamline the dynamic pressure $\rho v^2/2$ rises from $2$ to $8\,\mathrm{kPa}$. At constant elevation the static pressure drop is therefore $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The inlet of an ideal horizontal Venturi is twice the throat area. Its water pressure drop is $6000\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Find inlet speed.

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-4,1.4 -1,1.4 1,1 4,1;-4,-1.4 -1,-1.4 1,-1 4,-1" vettori="-3.3,0,-1.9,0,v_1;1,0,3.8,0,v_2" xmin=-4.6 xmax=4.9 ymin=-2.2 ymax=2.3 titolo="Ideal flow rate is conserved through the throat"}

$$
v_1=\sqrt{\frac{2\Delta p}{\rho[(A_1/A_2)^2-1]}},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_1&=\sqrt{2 \cdot 6000 \cdot \frac{1}{1000 \cdot \left(\left(-1\right) \cdot 1 + 2^{2}\right)}}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Throat speed is twice inlet speed by continuity. Substituting in the squared-speed difference leaves three times the squared upstream speed.

Substitution gives $p_1-p_2=(3\rho/2)v_1^2$. The positive root gives $v_1=2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_2=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. These values return $6000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ in Bernoulli’s equation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In an ideal horizontal pipe, water slows from $5$ to $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find $p_2-p_1$.

$$
p_2-p_1=\frac12\rho(v_1^2-v_2^2),\qquad
\begin{aligned}p_2-p_1&=\frac{1000 \cdot \left(- 3^{2} + 5^{2}\right)}{2}\\[4pt]&=8000\,\mathrm{Pa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Kinetic energy per volume decreases. Part of that energy reappears as static pressure, which rises.

With both points at the same elevation, the ideal-fluid balance is $p_2-p_1=\tfrac12\rho(5^2-3^2)=8\,\mathrm{kPa}$. In a real pipe some mechanical energy would be dissipated and pressure recovery could be smaller.

</div>
