## Exercise 1

A hole in a large open reservoir lies $0.80\,\mathrm m$ below the surface. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find ideal exit speed.

::grafico{f="sqrt(20*x)" punti=".8,4,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=1.05 ymin=0 ymax=5 nomi="h (m),v (m/s)" titolo="The marker shows a depth of eighty centimetres"}

$$
v=\sqrt{2gh},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v&=\sqrt{2 \cdot 10 \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{1}{5}}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The common atmospheric pressure cancels. The height drop supplies the jet motion, so take the positive root.

Because the free surface area is much greater than the hole area, it moves very slowly at the same flow rate. The loss of $8\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ of gravitational energy therefore matches $\tfrac12v^2$ for the jet.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A jet from a large open reservoir exits at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what depth is the hole with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

$$
h=v^2/(2g),\qquad
\begin{aligned}h&=\frac{3^{2}}{2 \cdot 10}\\[4pt]&=0.45\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Square speed before solving for depth. The resulting unit is length.

The broad surface allows its speed to be neglected and exposure to air makes the two pressures equal. Substituting the computed depth gives $\sqrt{2\cdot10\cdot0.45}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}$, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Water exits at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ through an opening of effective area $0.0005\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find flow rate in litres per second.

$$
Q=1000Av,\qquad
\begin{aligned}Q&=4 \cdot 5 \cdot \frac{1}{10000} \cdot 1000\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{L/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert exit speed into volume per time using jet area. Convert cubic metres to litres.

The effective area is the area to associate with the jet when finding how much volume passes through the section. The product $0.0005\cdot4$ gives $0.002\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

</div>
