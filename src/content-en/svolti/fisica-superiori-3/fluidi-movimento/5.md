## Exercise 1

A hole in a large open reservoir lies $0.80\,\mathrm m$ below the surface. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find ideal exit speed.

$$
v=\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2\cdot10\cdot0.80}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the two points at the free surface and in the emerging jet. Both are at atmospheric pressure. Since the surface is much larger than the small hole, the water there moves slowly and its kinetic term is negligible. The $8\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ of energy per mass lost on descending becomes the jet's kinetic energy. Take the positive root for speed. A hole closer to the surface would still have equal boundary pressures but a smaller available height difference. The emerging jet would be slower too.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A jet from a large open reservoir exits at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what depth is the hole with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

$$
h=\frac{v^2}{2g}=\frac{3^2}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=0.45\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The problem gives the jet's speed and asks for the height difference that produced it. To invert Torricelli's law, square the speed before dividing by twice gravitational acceleration. The units reduce to metres. The wide tank lets the motion of the surface be ignored. Putting the calculated depth back into the formula gives $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ again. The value is a vertical distance measured from the free surface. Finding the hole's height above the bottom would also require the total liquid depth.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Water leaves a wide open tank through a hole of constant effective area $0.0005\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Initially the hole lies $1.25\,\mathrm m$ below the free surface. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and negligible losses. How far must the surface fall for the flow rate to become $1.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$?

::grafico{f="0.5*sqrt(20*x)" punti=".45,1.5,pieno,F;1.25,2.5,pieno,I" segmenti=".45,0,.45,1.5;0,1.5,.45,1.5;1.25,0,1.25,2.5;0,2.5,1.25,2.5" etichette=".40,.20,0.45;1.19,.20,1.25" xmin=0 xmax=1.5 ymin=0 ymax=3.25 nomi="h (m),Q (L/s)" titolo="The final flow rate on the curve gives the remaining depth at F."}

$$
\begin{aligned}v_f&=\frac{0.0015}{0.0005}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3\,\mathrm{m/s},\\ h_f&=\frac{v_f^2}{2g}=\frac{3^2}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=0.45\,\mathrm m,\\ h_i-h_f&=(1.25-0.45)\,\mathrm m=0.80\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the surface height, start with the final flow rate converted to cubic metres per second. Divide by the effective area to get the outlet speed. In Torricelli's model, speed squared sets the remaining depth of the hole. Subtract that depth from the initial depth to find how far the surface falls. Check with the initial rate of $2.5\,\mathrm{L/s}$. The final rate is three fifths as large, so the final depth must be nine twenty-fifths of the initial depth. The hole remains submerged, with a positive outflow in the model.

</div>
