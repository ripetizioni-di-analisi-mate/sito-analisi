## Exercise 1

An incompressible liquid flows steadily in a pipe with no branches or leaks. The cross-sectional area decreases from $0.010$ to $0.004\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The upstream mean speed is $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the mean speed at the smaller section.

$$
v_2=A_1v_1/A_2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_2&=10 \cdot \frac{1}{1000} \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{4 \cdot \frac{1}{1000}}\\[4pt]&=5\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At fixed flow, area times speed is conserved. Speed increases inversely with area.

Check the computed speed by calculating the rate separately at each section. Upstream $0.010\cdot2=0.020\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ and downstream $0.004\cdot5=0.020\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Water flows steadily through a circular tube whose radius halves downstream. The upstream mean speed is $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the downstream mean speed.

$$
v_2=v_1(r_1/r_2)^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_2&=\frac{3 \cdot 2^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=6\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Circular area depends on radius squared. Halving the radius gives an area ratio of four to one.

The downstream section has area $A_1/4$. The speed quadruples to $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the product with area returns $A_1(1.5\,\mathrm{m/s})$. The flow is steady because at each section the speed is constant in time, even though it varies between sections.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A branch receives $10\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. Branch 2 discharges $6\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and branch 3 has area $0.002\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find speed through branch 3 with no accumulation.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="-3,0,-.4,0,10;.2,.3,2.7,1.4,6;.2,-.3,2.4,-1.3,4" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=2.6 titolo="Arrow labels show flows in litres per second"}

$$
v_3=(Q_1-Q_2)/A_3,\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_3&=\frac{10 - 6}{1000 \cdot \frac{2}{1000}}\\[4pt]&=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Volume balance gives four litres per second in branch 3. Convert to cubic metres per second and divide by section area.

Branch 3 has to carry away the $4\,\mathrm{L/s}$ left after branch 2. Dividing $0.004\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$ by $0.002\,\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the two outgoing rates add back up to the incoming rate.

</div>
