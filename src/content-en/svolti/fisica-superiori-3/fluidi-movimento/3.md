## Exercise 1

An incompressible liquid flows steadily in a pipe with no branches or leaks. The cross-sectional area decreases from $0.010$ to $0.004\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The upstream mean speed is $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the mean speed at the smaller section.

$$
\begin{aligned}Q&=(0.010\cdot2)\,\mathrm{m^3/s}=0.020\,\mathrm{m^3/s},\\v_2&=\frac{0.020}{0.004}\,\mathrm{m/s}=5\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The flow is steady and the liquid is incompressible. The region between the sections stores no volume and has no other outlets. Find the flow rate once using the upstream data. At the smaller section, dividing by area gives the new mean speed. The downstream area is $40\%$ of the upstream area, so speed must be two and a half times larger to keep the product. Check that $0.004\cdot5=0.020\,\mathrm{m^3/s}$, the upstream rate. For a gas with changing density, the mass flow rates would have to be equated instead.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A gas flows steadily in a pipe with no leaks or branches. Upstream its density is $1.2\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, area is $0.006\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and mean normal speed is $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Downstream its density is $0.8\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and area is $0.003\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find the downstream speed and volume flow rate.

$$
\begin{aligned}\dot m&=(1.2\cdot0.006\cdot5)\,\mathrm{kg/s}=0.036\,\mathrm{kg/s},\\ Q_2&=\frac{\dot m}{\rho_2}=\frac{0.036}{0.8}\,\mathrm{m^3/s}=0.045\,\mathrm{m^3/s},\\ v_2&=\frac{Q_2}{A_2}=\frac{0.045}{0.003}\,\mathrm{m/s}=15\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gas that crosses the first section must continue to the second because there are no branches and no mass accumulates. Kilograms per second are conserved. After computing them upstream, divide by downstream density to find the volume carried. The smaller section and lower density both increase speed. Halving the area alone would give a factor of two, and the density ratio is $1.5$. Multiplying those factors explains the fifteen metres per second. The upstream and downstream volume flow rates differ, as expected from the density change.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A branch receives $10\,\mathrm{L/s}$ of water. Branch 2 discharges $6\,\mathrm{L/s}$ and branch 3 has area $0.002\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find speed through branch 3 with no accumulation.

::grafico{assi=0 vettori="-3,0,-.4,0,10;.2,.3,2.7,1.4,6;.2,-.3,2.4,-1.3,4" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=2.6 titolo="Arrow labels show flows in litres per second"}

$$
\begin{aligned}Q_3&=(10-6)\,\mathrm{L/s}=0.004\,\mathrm{m^3/s},\\v_3&=\frac{0.004}{0.002}\,\mathrm{m/s}=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First identify which arrows in the diagram enter the junction and which leave. For water with no accumulation, the incoming rate equals the sum of the branch rates. Subtracting the six litres per second in branch 2 gives the four in branch 3. To find speed in branch 3, divide its flow rate in cubic metres per second by its area. The area of branch 2 is unnecessary. If its rate changed with the inlet rate fixed, branch 3 would have to offset the change to maintain the balance.

</div>
