## Exercise 1

In a long pipe the radius doubles at fixed viscosity, length and pressure drop. If both flows satisfy Poiseuille's law, by what factor does the flow rate increase?

::grafico{f="x^4" punti="1,1,pieno,A;2,16,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=2.2 ymin=0 ymax=22 nomi="r/r_1,Q/Q_1" titolo="With the other data fixed, the flow ratio is the fourth power of the radius ratio"}

$$
\frac{Q_2}{Q_1}=\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^4=2^4=16.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare two versions of Poiseuille's law. Length, viscosity and pressure drop agree, so they cancel in the ratio. This leaves the fourth power of the ratio of the radii, shown by the curve. Area alone grows fourfold. For the rate to grow sixteenfold, mean speed must also quadruple. At fixed density and viscosity, Reynolds number then rises eightfold because the diameter doubles too. The factor of sixteen therefore applies when the wider pipe also stays in the specified laminar regime.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cylindrical pipe is replaced by one twice as long, with the same Newtonian liquid and pressure drop. The flow rate must stay the same and both pipes operate in the Poiseuille regime. By what factor must the radius be multiplied?

$$
\begin{aligned}1=\frac{Q_2}{Q_1}&=\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^4\frac{L_1}{L_2},\\ \frac{r_2}{r_1}&=\sqrt[4]{2}\simeq1.19.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At a fixed pressure drop a longer tube offers more resistance, so the radius must grow to compensate. Taking the ratio of the two Poiseuille expressions cancels viscosity and pressure drop because they remain the same. For the flow rate ratio to be one, doubling length must be balanced by doubling the fourth power of the radius. Take a fourth root. As a check, a ten percent radius increase would give a fourth power about $1.46$ times the original, too small to compensate for doubling the length. The result increases the radius by about nineteen percent.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a pipe, a liquid has flow rate $0.40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in the Poiseuille regime. A liquid with twice the viscosity is then used. The pipe geometry and pressure difference stay the same and the flow remains in the Poiseuille regime. Find the new flow rate.

$$
Q_2=Q_1\frac{\eta_1}{\eta_2}=\frac{0.40}{2}\,\mathrm{L/s}=0.20\,\mathrm{L/s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In Poiseuille's law viscosity and rate are inversely proportional at fixed geometry and pressure drop. Taking the ratio removes those unchanged quantities and lets the calculation stay in litres per second. With twice the viscosity, the tube carries half as many litres per second. The original rate could be recovered with the second liquid by increasing the pressure drop by the same factor as the viscosity, while the model remains applicable. The relation makes clear which quantity is held fixed in each prediction.

</div>
