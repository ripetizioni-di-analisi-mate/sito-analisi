## Exercise 1

In a long pipe the radius doubles at fixed viscosity, length and pressure drop. If both flows satisfy Poiseuille's law, by what factor does the flow rate increase?

::grafico{f="x^4" punti="1,1,pieno,A;2,16,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=2.2 ymin=0 ymax=22 nomi="r/r_1,Q/Q_1" titolo="With the other data fixed, the flow ratio is the fourth power of the radius ratio"}

$$
Q_2/Q_1=(r_2/r_1)^4,\qquad
\begin{aligned}Q_2/Q_1&=2^{4}\\[4pt]&=16.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In Poiseuille flow, area alone does not describe the radius effect. Radius is raised to the fourth power, giving sixteen.

The area grows fourfold, so the mean speed grows fourfold given the factor of sixteen in the flow rate. At the same density the Reynolds number increases eightfold because the diameter doubles as well. This explains why the model requires the flow to remain laminar after the change.

</div>

## Exercise 2

To hold the flow rate fixed, replace a tube with one three times as long and with the same radius. Find the factor for pressure drop under Poiseuille flow.

$$
\Delta p_2/\Delta p_1=L_2/L_1,\qquad
\Delta p_2/\Delta p_1=3.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At fixed flow, pressure drop grows linearly with length. Each added length contributes resistance.

With $Q$, radius and viscosity fixed, Poiseuille’s law keeps $\Delta p/L$ constant. Carrying the same amount per second over three times the distance therefore requires three times the pressure difference.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a pipe, a liquid has flow rate $0.40\,\mathrm{L/s}$ in the Poiseuille regime. A liquid with twice the viscosity is then used. The pipe geometry and pressure difference stay the same and the flow remains in the Poiseuille regime. Find the new flow rate.

$$
Q_2=Q_1\eta_1/\eta_2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}Q_2&=\frac{2}{2 \cdot 5}\\[4pt]&=0.2\,\mathrm{L/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At fixed pressure difference, flow rate is inversely proportional to viscosity. Doubling viscosity halves the throughput.

The hydraulic resistance $\Delta p/Q$ doubles. With the same pressure difference, $0.40/2=0.20\,\mathrm{L/s}$ provided both flows follow the Poiseuille model.

</div>
