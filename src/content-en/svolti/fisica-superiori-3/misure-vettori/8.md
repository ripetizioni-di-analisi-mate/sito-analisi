## Exercise 1

A constant force $\vec F=(-4,3)\,\mathrm N$ acts during a displacement $\vec s=(2,0)\,\mathrm m$. What work does the force do?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,-4,3,F;0,0,-4,0,Fₓ" segmenti="-4,0,-4,3" etichette="0.6,0.48,direction of s →" xmin=-5 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=4 nomi="Fₓ (N),Fᵧ (N)" uguale=1 titolo="The positive horizontal axis points along the displacement. The projection points to the left."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=F_xs_x+F_ys_y\\[4pt]&=(-4)\cdot2+3\cdot0\\[4pt]
&= -8\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Work is a dot product. This displacement has no vertical part, so work depends on the force's $x$-component. Its projection acts against the displacement, giving the negative sign. The force can affect other aspects of motion even if one component does not contribute to the work calculated here.

As a check, the force has magnitude $5\,\mathrm N$ and the cosine of its angle with the displacement is $-\dfrac45$. The product $Fs\cos\theta$ again gives $-8\,\mathrm J$. Multiplying the magnitudes alone would give $10\,\mathrm J$ and would imply that the whole force acts along the motion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For what component $b$ are $(2,3)$ and $(6,b)$ perpendicular?

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=2\cdot6+3b\\[4pt] b&=-\frac{12}{3}\\[4pt]
&= -4
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set the dot product to zero because both vectors are nonzero. The unknown component must contribute $-12$, balancing the positive horizontal contribution. With $b=-4$, the vertical contribution is $3(-4)$, so the sum is indeed zero.

A perpendicular direction can be constructed by rotating $(2,3)$ through $90^\circ$ clockwise. This gives $(3,-2)$. Doubling it gives $(6,-4)$, which has the required horizontal component. This geometric check determines the sign of the missing component even before numerical substitution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A constant force $\vec F=(4,2)\,\mathrm N$ is projected along the dimensionless vector $\vec d=(3,4)$. What is its signed scalar component along $\vec d$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\parallel}&=\frac{4\cdot3+2\cdot4}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The projection uses the unit vector $\vec d/d$, which specifies the direction without bringing in the arbitrary magnitude of $\vec d$. Its dot product with the given direction vector is $20\,\mathrm N$, but it must be divided by $d=5$ to give the projection. The result, $4\,\mathrm N$, is below the magnitude of the force.

The unit vector has components $\left(\dfrac35,\dfrac45\right)$. The part of the force along the line would be the vector $4\hat d\,\mathrm N$, while the question asks for its signed coefficient. Specifying the direction with $(6,8)$ would double both the dot product and the norm. The component would stay $4\,\mathrm N$.

The vector projection has coordinates $(2.4,3.2)\,\mathrm N$. Subtracting it from the original force gives $(1.6,-1.2)\,\mathrm N$. The dot product of this remainder with $\vec d$ is zero because $1.6\cdot3$ and $-1.2\cdot4$ have the same numerical magnitude and opposite signs.

</div>
