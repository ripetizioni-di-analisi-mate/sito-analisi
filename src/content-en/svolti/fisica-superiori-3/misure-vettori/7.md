## Exercise 1

A robot makes successive displacements $(2,1)$, $(-4,2)$ and $(2,-3)$, in metres. What is its total displacement magnitude?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,2,1,A;2,1,-2,3,B;-2,3,0,0,C" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=4 nomi="x (m),y (m)" uguale=1 titolo="The last displacement ends back at the origin"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=\sqrt{(2-4+2)^2+(1+2-3)^2}\\[4pt]
&= 0\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The head coordinates after each step are found by adding the components of the next arrow. The net horizontal and vertical components are both zero. The robot returns to its starting point even though all three segments have positive length.

After the first segment the robot is at $(2,1)\,\mathrm m$. After the second it is at $(-2,3)\,\mathrm m$. The last displacement, $(2,-3)\,\mathrm m$, is opposite to the vector from the origin to that point. The path in the diagram closes, while the distance travelled is the sum of the three lengths.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two displacements have resultant $(6,-4)\,\mathrm m$. The first is $(2,-1)\,\mathrm m$. Calculate the magnitude of the second.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,2,-1;2,-1,6,-4;0,0,6,-4" etichette="1.37,-0.29,A;4.4,-2.45,B;2.55,-1.98,R" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-5 ymax=2 nomi="x (m),y (m)" uguale=1 titolo="The second arrow joins the first vector's head to the resultant's head"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
B&=\sqrt{(6-2)^2+(-4-(-1))^2}\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To recover the missing displacement, subtract the first from the resultant, one axis at a time. The components are $4\,\mathrm m$ rightwards and $3\,\mathrm m$ downwards. Its magnitude is the length of the line from the first head to the resultant's head.

Adding back gives $(2,-1)+(4,-3)=(6,-4)$ in metres. Subtracting the magnitudes would not recover the second displacement, because the vectors in the subtraction point in different directions. Their individual components account for how much remains along each coordinate direction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two displacements of magnitudes $7\,\mathrm m$ and $4\,\mathrm m$ can make any angle. What is the smallest magnitude of the resultant?

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\min}&=7-4\\[4pt]
&= 3\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With fixed magnitudes, the resultant is shortest when the arrows point in opposite directions. The smaller one can cancel at most $4\,\mathrm m$ of the larger one, leaving $3\,\mathrm m$. Any other angle adds a transverse component or reduces the cancellation along the first arrow.

The dot product gives $R^2=7^2+4^2+2\cdot7\cdot4\cos\theta$ with lengths expressed in metres. The cosine reaches its minimum at $180^\circ$, giving $R^2=9\,\mathrm{m^2}$. With the arrows pointing the same way the magnitude would be $11\,\mathrm m$. These two alignments bound all possible resultants.

For perpendicular vectors the cross term vanishes. The magnitude is $\sqrt{65}\,\mathrm m$, about $8.06\,\mathrm m$. The magnitude at the midpoint of the angle range is therefore different from the mean of its extreme magnitudes, which is $7\,\mathrm m$.

</div>
