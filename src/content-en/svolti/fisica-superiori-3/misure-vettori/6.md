## Exercise 1

A force of magnitude $12\,\mathrm N$ makes $120^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis. Find $F_x$.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,-6,10.3923,F" segmenti="-6,0,-6,10.3923" xmin=-8 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=12 uguale=1 titolo="The horizontal projection points left"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_x&=12\cos(120^\circ)\\[4pt]&=12\left(-\frac12\right)\\[4pt]
&= -6\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal projection uses the cosine because the angle is measured from the horizontal axis. At $120^\circ$ the head of the force lies to the left of its tail. The component is therefore negative and its magnitude is half of $12\,\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A force $(5,0)\,\mathrm N$ is expressed in axes rotated anticlockwise by $90^\circ$. What is its new $y'$-component?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{y'}&=5\cos(180^\circ)\\[4pt]
&= -5\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The new $x'$-axis points along the old positive $y$-axis. The new $y'$ points along the old negative $x$-axis, opposite the force. The whole magnitude therefore projects onto $y'$ with a negative sign. The frame rotates while the force preserves its physical orientation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A force has vertical component $6\,\mathrm N$ and makes $30^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis. What is its magnitude?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=\frac{F_y}{\sin(30^\circ)}=\frac{6}{1/2}\\[4pt]
&= 12\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Start with the vertical projection, which uses the sine of the angle from the horizontal axis. This sine is one half, so the component is half the force. As a check, the magnitude is at least as large as either component.

</div>
