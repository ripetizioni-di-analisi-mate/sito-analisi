## Exercise 1

A displacement goes from $P=(-2,1)\,\mathrm m$ to $Q=(2,-2)\,\mathrm m$. Find the magnitude of $\overrightarrow{PQ}$.

::grafico{vettori="-2,1,2,-2,s" punti="-2,1,pieno,P;2,-2,pieno,Q" segmenti="-2,1,2,1;2,1,2,-2" xmin=-3.5 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=2 nomi="x (m),y (m)" uguale=1 titolo="From tail to head, the components are 4 and −3 m."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\sqrt{(2-(-2))^2+(-2-1)^2}\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tail is at $P$ and the head is at $Q$, so the displacement has a horizontal leg of $4\,\mathrm m$ rightwards and a vertical leg of $3\,\mathrm m$ downwards. The right triangle has hypotenuse $5\,\mathrm m$. Translating the arrow to the origin leaves the geometry unchanged.

To check, add the components to the starting point. Starting at $(-2,1)\,\mathrm m$ gives exactly $(2,-2)\,\mathrm m$. Reversing the journey changes both signs. The square of each component stays the same, and so does the magnitude of $5\,\mathrm m$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A displacement of magnitude $13\,\mathrm m$ has horizontal component $5\,\mathrm m$ and points into the fourth quadrant. What is its vertical component?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,5,-12,s" punti="5,-12,pieno" segmenti="5,0,5,-12;0,-12,5,-12" xmin=-5 xmax=13 ymin=-14 ymax=2 nomi="x (m),y (m)" uguale=1 titolo="In the fourth quadrant the vertical component is negative."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
s_y&=-\sqrt{13^2-5^2}\\[4pt]
&= -12\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The squared magnitude is the sum of the squares of its components. Subtract the horizontal contribution to obtain the square of the vertical component, $144\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Both signs are possible at this point. In the fourth quadrant the vertical component is negative, so take $-12\,\mathrm m$.

Pythagoras also allows $+12\,\mathrm m$. That value would place the tip above the horizontal axis, in the first quadrant. Substituting the selected component gives $5^2+(-12)^2=169$. Taking the square root recovers the given magnitude, and the sign places the tip in the stated region.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vector of magnitude $10\,\mathrm m$ has vertical component $8\,\mathrm m$. It lies in the second quadrant. What is the magnitude of its horizontal projection?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert s_x\rvert&=\sqrt{10^2-8^2}\\[4pt]
&= 6\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product of the components does not appear because the axes are perpendicular. Subtract the square of the vertical component from the squared magnitude. The second quadrant gives a negative horizontal component, but the requested magnitude is its absolute value.

The components of the complete vector would be $(-6,8)\,\mathrm m$. Reflecting the arrow in the vertical axis would give $(6,8)\,\mathrm m$ with the same horizontal magnitude. The quadrant distinguishes the two arrows, while the question asks only for the length of the horizontal leg.

The ratio of the horizontal component to the magnitude is $-0.6$. This is the cosine of the angle from the positive $x$-axis. Its sign agrees with the second quadrant and the full arrow magnitude follows from $(-6)^2+8^2=100$.

</div>
