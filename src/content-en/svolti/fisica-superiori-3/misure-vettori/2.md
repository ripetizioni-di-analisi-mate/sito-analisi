## Exercise 1

The empty container gives a scale reading of $80\,\mathrm{g}$. Holding $45\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of liquid, the reading becomes $170\,\mathrm{g}$. What is the liquid's density?

::grafico{linee="0,80 50,180" punti="0,80,pieno;45,170,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=54 ymin=0 ymax=205 nomi="V (cm³),m (g)" titolo="The line passes through the empty and filled container readings."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho&=\frac{m_{\mathrm{tot}}-m_{\mathrm{tara}}}{V}\\[4pt]&=\frac{170-80}{45}\\[4pt]
&= 2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale measures the mass of the entire container and liquid. To isolate the mass of the liquid, subtract the empty reading. Density is the slope joining the two readings as a function of volume. The zero-volume point represents the tare.

Along the line, each increase of $10\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ adds $20\,\mathrm g$ under the homogeneous-liquid model. Dividing $170\,\mathrm g$ directly by $45\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ would give a ratio that depends on the volume poured because of the tare.

Any additive error common to both readings cancels with the tare. If the error depends on the load, further reference readings are needed to check the slope.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous sample has mass $54\,\mathrm{g}$ and density $2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. What volume does it occupy?

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\frac m\rho\\[4pt]&=\frac{54}{2.7}\\[4pt]
&= 20\,\mathrm{cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Density specifies the mass of each cubic centimetre. The volume is the number of portions of $2.7\,\mathrm g$ contained in the total mass. Dividing grams by grams per cubic centimetre leaves cubic centimetres. As a check, $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ of the material has mass $54\,\mathrm g$.

The same check can be done in SI units. The mass is $0.054\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the density is $2700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Their ratio is $2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. There are a million cubic centimetres in a cubic metre, so the order of magnitude agrees with the result in cubic centimetres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ piece of density $2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ is joined to a $30\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ piece of density $4\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Their volumes add. What is the combined object's mean density?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\frac{20\cdot2+30\cdot4}{20+30}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 3.2\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find the masses separately, $40$ and $120\,\mathrm g$, because each piece has its own density. The ratio of total mass to total volume is a volume-weighted mean. The denser material occupies the larger share, so the mean exceeds $3\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$.

The volume fractions are $\dfrac25$ and $\dfrac35$. Increasing the second fraction would bring the density even closer to the denser material's value. The calculation uses additive volumes. Any voids between the materials would increase the total volume and lower the apparent density.

</div>
