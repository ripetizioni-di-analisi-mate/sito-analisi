## Exercise 1

A sensor records a straight-line displacement of $450\,\mathrm{m}$ in $30\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the average velocity in kilometres per hour?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{\mathrm{km/h}}&=\frac{450}{30}\cdot\frac{3600}{1000}\\[4pt]
&= 54\,\mathrm{km/h}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First find how many metres are covered per second. At that steady rate, an hour would cover $3600$ times that distance. Divide by $1000$ to express the result in kilometres. The numerical value is therefore larger when expressed in kilometres per hour.

The calculation gives $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ as an intermediate value. The conversion also works backwards. Multiplying $54$ by $\dfrac5{18}$ gives $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ again. Here the sensor supplies only the net displacement and the total time. The velocity may therefore have varied during the thirty seconds.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vehicle maintains $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$ for $12\,\mathrm{s}$. How many metres does it travel?

::grafico{f="20" area="20;0;0;12" punti="12,20,pieno" segmenti="12,0,12,20" xmin=0 xmax=14 ymin=0 ymax=24 nomi="t (s),v (m/s)" legenda=0 titolo="The rectangle up to 12 s has area 240 m."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=vt\\[4pt]&=\frac{72\cdot1000}{3600}\cdot12\\[4pt]
&= 240\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Distance is the area of the rectangle under the speed–time graph. Its height is $20\,\mathrm{m/s}$ after conversion and its base is $12\,\mathrm{s}$. The product has the dimensions of a length because the seconds in the denominator cancel.

The velocity is constant, so every second adds $20\,\mathrm m$. To check using the starting unit, twelve seconds are $\dfrac1{300}$ of an hour. The vehicle covers $0.24\,\mathrm{km}$ in that time, which gives the same distance in metres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vehicle covers one straight segment for $10\,\mathrm{s}$ at $36\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and another for $20\,\mathrm{s}$ at $18\,\mathrm{km/h}$ in the same direction. Find its average velocity in $\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_m&=\frac{10\cdot10+5\cdot20}{10+20}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 6.667\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The speeds correspond to $10$ and $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Each segment contributes its own displacement, here $100\,\mathrm{m}$ in both cases. Total time is $30\,\mathrm{s}$. The weighted mean is closer to $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ because that speed is maintained longer.

A constant velocity of $6.667\,\mathrm{m/s}$, maintained for $30\,\mathrm s$, gives about $200\,\mathrm m$, the same displacement as the two stages. Giving the velocities equal weights would give $7.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. That value would describe motion in which the two velocities last for equal times.

Here the two sections have the same length. In SI values, two divided by $\dfrac1{10}+\dfrac15$ also gives the answer, because each time equals the common length divided by the velocity on that section.

</div>
