## Exercise 1

Three readings are $12.0$, $12.1$ and $12.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the difference between the greatest reading and the exact mean?

::grafico{punti="120,1,pieno;121,1,pieno;125,1,pieno;122,2,pieno" segmenti="120,1,125,1;122,1,122,2" xmin=119 xmax=127 ymin=0 ymax=3 nomi="ℓ (mm),row" titolo="The mean at 122 mm is above the readings at 120, 121 and 125 mm."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{\max}-\bar x&=12.5-\frac{12.0+12.1+12.5}{3}\\[4pt]
&= 0.3\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean gives all readings the same weight, so it is $12.2\,\mathrm{cm}$. The upper endpoint is $0.3\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the estimated centre. The half-range is instead $0.25\,\mathrm{cm}$ because it only uses the separation between the endpoints, divided by two.

The lower endpoint is $0.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ below the mean. The upper deviation is therefore the larger of the two. A symmetric interval centred on the mean must have a margin of at least $0.3\,\mathrm{cm}$ to contain all three readings. The graph shows the same distances in millimetres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four readings of a length have mean $16\,\mathrm{cm}$. Three are $15$, $16$ and $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the fourth?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_4&=4\bar x-(x_1+x_2+x_3)\\[4pt]&=4\cdot16-(15+16+18)\\[4pt]
&= 15\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Start from the definition of the mean and recover the full sum of $64\,\mathrm{cm}$. Subtracting the three known readings leaves the missing one. Substitution verifies that two readings of $15\,\mathrm{cm}$, one of $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ and one of $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ have mean $16\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The missing reading also follows from the deviations from $16\,\mathrm{cm}$. The three known deviations are $-1$, $0$ and $+2\,\mathrm{cm}$, totalling $+1\,\mathrm{cm}$. The deviations from the mean sum to zero, so the final deviation must be $-1\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A group of three readings has mean $12\,\mathrm{cm}$, and a group of five has mean $16\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the mean of all eight readings.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{3\cdot12+5\cdot16}{3+5}\\[4pt]
&= 14.5\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original means describe groups of different sizes. Multiply each mean by its own count to reconstruct its group's sum. Add the two totals and divide by eight. The final estimate is closer to $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ because the second group contains more readings.

One check uses the deviations from the combined mean. Together, the first three readings contribute $3(12-14.5)\,\mathrm{cm}=-7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The second group contributes $5(16-14.5)\,\mathrm{cm}=+7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The deviations balance, as the definition of the mean of all readings requires.

The two group means suffice to recover the overall mean because they determine the sums. The group means alone give no individual reading endpoints, which are needed to calculate the half-range.

</div>
