## Exercise 1

Compare $(20.0\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $(5.0\pm0.1)\,\mathrm{cm}$. By what factor is the second relative uncertainty larger than the first?

::grafico{linee="0,0 20,0.2;0,0 5,0.1" punti="20,0.2,pieno,1;5,0.1,pieno,2" etichette="5.3,0.076,0.1;19,0.223,0.2" xmin=0 xmax=24 ymin=0 ymax=0.25 nomi="ℓ (cm),Δℓ (cm)" titolo="The line through point 2 has twice the slope."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\varepsilon_2}{\varepsilon_1}&=\frac{2\%}{1\%}\\[4pt]
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute uncertainties have the same unit but apply to different central values. The first measurement allows a one-percent deviation. The second allows two parts per hundred because its central value is four times smaller while its absolute margin is only twice as small.

The two absolute margins are plotted at different horizontal positions. Dividing the height of each point by its horizontal coordinate gives the slope of the line from the origin. These two slopes are the relative uncertainties, compared on the same scale.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A reading is $0.860\,\mathrm m$. Its relative uncertainty must be at most $0.5\%$. What is the maximum absolute uncertainty in millimetres?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=\frac{0.5}{100}\cdot860\\[4pt]
&= 4.3\,\mathrm{mm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The requirement applies to the ratio of the margin to the central reading. Multiply the reading by $0.005$. Using $860\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the start gives the margin in the requested unit. A larger margin would exceed the allowed percentage.

For example, a margin of $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ would give a fraction of $\dfrac5{860}$, about $0.58\%$. That value fails the requirement. A margin of $4\,\mathrm{mm}$ would give about $0.47\%$, which meets it. These two checks place the upper limit between $4$ and $5\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An area is computed from a square side of $(10.0\pm0.2)\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is the first-order worst-case percentage uncertainty in the area?

$$
\begin{aligned}
100\frac{\Delta A}{A}&\simeq 100\,\frac{2\ell\Delta\ell}{\ell^2}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{\%}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Changing the side by $\delta\ell$ changes its square by a linear term $2\ell\delta\ell$ and a quadratic term $\delta\ell^2$. The requested model neglects the quadratic term, only $0.04\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The side has a $2\%$ relative margin, so to first order the area has a $4\%$ relative margin.

The same side appears twice, so both occurrences increase or decrease together. At the exact endpoints, the area ranges from $96.04$ to $104.04\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The deviations from $100\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ are slightly asymmetric. The first-order result uses one common margin of $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.

A requirement of at most $4\%$ deviation calls for the exact endpoints. The upper endpoint exceeds the nominal area by $4.04\%$, so the rounded first-order estimate alone cannot certify the requirement.

</div>
