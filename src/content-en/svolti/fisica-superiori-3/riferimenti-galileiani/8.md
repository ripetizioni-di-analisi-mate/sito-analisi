## Exercise 1

A carriage moves right and brakes with acceleration $A=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. With right positive, find the apparent-force component on a $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ bag.

::grafico{f="8-2*x;8" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=0 ymax=10 nomi="t (s),v (m/s)" legenda="\text{carriage};\text{bag}" titolo="The initial velocity of 8 m/s is an example value. The carriage slows while the free bag keeps that ground velocity."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{app},x}&=-mA=-4\cdot(-2)\\[5pt]&=8\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

While braking, the carriage velocity points right but its acceleration points left. The apparent term is determined by the acceleration, so it points right. Multiplying the acceleration magnitude by the mass gives eight newtons. In the graph the ground velocities start at the same value and the carriage velocity falls. If the bag is horizontally free, it keeps its ground velocity and moves forwards relative to the carriage. This observation agrees with the positive sign of the apparent term in the moving frame.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The horizontal resultant of the real forces on a body is $18\,\mathrm N$ to the right. The body has mass $m=3\,\mathrm{kg}$. The carriage accelerates right at $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the relative acceleration component with right positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{app},x}&=-3\cdot4=-12\,\mathrm N\\[5pt]a'&=\dfrac{18-12}{3}\\[5pt]&=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the carriage frame, the real force acts to the right and the translation term points left. Six newtons remain in the positive direction. Divide by three kilograms to obtain two metres per second squared relative to the carriage. In the ground frame the real horizontal force gives the body six metres per second squared. Subtracting the carriage's four gives the same answer. The two calculations use different force balances to describe the same motion.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A lift accelerates downwards at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A person of mass $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at rest relative to the scale. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what force does the scale read?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N-mg+m\cdot2&=0\\[5pt]N&=40(10-2)\\[5pt]&=320\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take upwards as positive. The weight is four hundred newtons downwards. In the lift an apparent term of eighty newtons points up because the lift accelerates down. The person is at rest relative to the scale, so the upward contributions add to the weight. The normal force is three hundred and twenty newtons. In the ground frame, the same normal force minus the weight gives minus eighty newtons, exactly the mass multiplied by the lift acceleration.

</div>
