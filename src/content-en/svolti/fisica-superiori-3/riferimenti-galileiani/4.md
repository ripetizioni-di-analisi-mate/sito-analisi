## Exercise 1

Two cyclists travel in the same direction at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The faster cyclist starts $36\,\mathrm m$ behind. How long does it take to catch the other?

::grafico{f="12*x;36+8*x" xmin=0 xmax=11 ymin=0 ymax=143 nomi="t (s),x (m)" punti="9,108,pieno,I" legenda="\text{cyclist behind};\text{cyclist ahead}" titolo="The cyclists meet at 108 m after 9 s"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(t)&=36+(8-12)t\\[5pt]t&=\dfrac{36}{12-8}\\[5pt]&=9\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the faster cyclist's starting point as the origin. The other starts thirty-six metres ahead and keeps moving, so the position to be reached changes during the pursuit. Each second the cyclist behind travels twelve metres and the one ahead travels eight. The gap closes by four metres per second and reaches zero in nine seconds. Substitution into both position equations gives the same coordinate, one hundred and eight metres. On the graph the crossing point represents exactly this time-position pair.

</div>

## Exercise 2

From $t=0$, runner $B$ travels at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from $20\,\mathrm m$. $A$ leaves the origin at $t=2\,\mathrm s$ and runs at $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At what time on the original clock does the catch occur?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_A&=7(t-2),\qquad x_B=20+3t\\[5pt]4t&=20+14=34\\[5pt]t&=\dfrac{34}{4}\\[5pt]&=\dfrac{17}{2}\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare the two runners' positions at a common instant. On the first clock, $B$ has moved for time $t$ and $A$ for $t-2$. The equation for $A$ is valid from two seconds onward, and the solution lies after that instant. The same result follows by noting that the gap when $A$ starts is twenty-six metres. At a closing speed of four metres per second, it takes $6.5\,\mathrm s$ from the delayed start, corresponding to $8.5\,\mathrm s$ on the requested clock.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A runner starts from zero at $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Another starts $30\,\mathrm m$ ahead at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long until one catches the other if both continue at constant velocity beyond the $78\,\mathrm m$ finish?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\frac{30}{9-6}\,\mathrm s=10\,\mathrm s,\\[5pt]
x_A&=(9\cdot10)\,\mathrm m=90\,\mathrm m,\\[5pt]
x_B&=(30+6\cdot10)\,\mathrm m=90\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The meeting calculation uses the stated condition that both continue beyond the finish. The runner behind gains three metres each second and closes thirty metres in ten seconds. The common coordinate is ninety metres, twelve metres beyond the finish. The runner ahead reaches the finish in eight seconds, with only forty-eight metres to run. The finish thus provides a check on the order of events. A model in which the runners stop at the line would need to specify their motion after crossing.

</div>
