## Exercise 1

A train travels at $54\,\mathrm{km/h}$. A passenger walks towards the front at $7.2\,\mathrm{km/h}$ relative to the carriage. Find the ground velocity in metres per second.

::grafico{f="15*x;17*x" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=70 nomi="t (s),x (m)" legenda="\text{train};\text{passenger}" titolo="The passenger's ground position increases faster"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=\dfrac{54}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s}=15\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v'&=\dfrac{7.2}{3.6}\,\mathrm{m/s}=2\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v&=15+2\\[5pt]&=17\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocities link the train to the ground and then the passenger to the train. To add them in metres per second, convert both by dividing by $3.6$. Take the train's direction as positive. Since the passenger walks forwards, the second component is also positive. Every second, the carriage advances fifteen metres and the passenger gains another two metres inside it. The ground therefore measures seventeen metres covered in one second, as the steeper slope in the graph shows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A station measures $19\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for a passenger walking towards the front and $17\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for the train. Both ground-frame velocities remain constant for the next $6\,\mathrm s$. How far does the passenger walk inside the carriage in that interval?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v'&=v-u=19-17=2\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]\Delta x'&=v'\Delta t=2\cdot6\\[5pt]&=12\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The station measures both values in the same frame. To find the velocity along the carriage floor, subtract the common contribution, the train velocity. The positive result confirms that the passenger walks towards the front. In six seconds the passenger advances twelve metres in the carriage. The same result follows by comparing the ground displacements, one hundred and fourteen metres for the passenger and one hundred and two for the train. Their difference is twelve metres again.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A train travels at $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a passenger walks towards the front at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the carriage. How long does the passenger take to pass between two ground markers $90\,\mathrm m$ apart?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=16+2=18\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]\Delta t&=\dfrac{\Delta x}{v}=\dfrac{90}{18}\\[5pt]&=5\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ninety metres are measured between two points fixed to the station. To divide the distance by a velocity, use the passenger's velocity relative to the ground. The two components in the same direction give eighteen metres per second, and division gives five seconds. During that time, the train covers eighty metres and the passenger covers another ten by walking. Their sum is the separation of the two markers, with both displacements measured over the same interval.

</div>
