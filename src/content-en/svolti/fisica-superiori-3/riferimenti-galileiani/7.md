## Exercise 1

A carriage has $A=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and a body has $a=-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ in the ground frame along the same axis. Initially both are at rest and their positions coincide. Find the acceleration component relative to the carriage.

::grafico{f="x^2;-1.5*x^2" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=-14 ymax=10 nomi="t (s),x (m)" legenda="\text{carriage on ground};\text{body in carriage}" titolo="Carriage position on the ground and body position in the carriage"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a'&=a-A=-1-2\\[5pt]&=-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relative coordinate is the body's position minus the carriage's position. The carriage's velocity change per unit time is therefore subtracted as well. The body already accelerates in the negative direction on the ground, while the carriage accelerates in the positive direction. Their difference gives a more negative relative acceleration. In the graph the carriage's coordinate is positive and increasing, while the body's relative coordinate decreases quadratically. The coefficient of $t^2$ in the second curve is half the relative acceleration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A non-rotating carriage has ground acceleration $\vec A=(0,-2)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A body has $\vec a=(3,0)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the magnitude of $\vec a'$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec a'&=(3,0)-(0,-2)=(3,2)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[5pt]a'&=\sqrt{3^2+2^2}\\[5pt]&=\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The transformation applies component by component along the parallel axes. The carriage has no acceleration along the first axis, so the relative value remains three in that direction. Along the second axis the body has zero ground acceleration and the carriage accelerates negatively. Subtracting that component gives two in the positive direction in the moving frame. The magnitude is the length of the vector with components three and two. Subtracting magnitudes would treat the motion as one-dimensional and lose the information on the second axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In $4\,\mathrm s$ the ground velocity of a body changes from $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ while the carriage velocity stays $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the body's mean acceleration in the carriage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v'_i&=5-(-1)=6\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v'_f&=7-(-1)=8\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]\overline a'&=\dfrac{8-6}{4}\\[5pt]&=\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the same carriage velocity at the start and end of the period. The relative velocities are a metre per second greater because the carriage moves in the opposite direction, but their increase is still two metres per second. Divide by the elapsed time to obtain half a metre per second squared. The same result follows by subtracting the mean acceleration of the carriage, zero throughout this interval, from the ground-frame mean acceleration. A negative carriage velocity alone gives no negative acceleration.

</div>
