## Exercise 1

A trolley starts at $X_0=-12\,\mathrm m$ and travels at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A parcel is fixed at $x'=-3\,\mathrm m$ in the trolley frame, with parallel axes. What is the parcel's ground coordinate after $5\,\mathrm s$?

::grafico{f="-12+4*x;-15+4*x" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=-17 ymax=15 nomi="t (s),x (m)" punti="5,8,pieno,O′;5,5,pieno,P" legenda="\text{trolley};\text{parcel}" titolo="The parcel is always three metres behind the moving origin"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
X(5)&=-12+4\cdot5=8\,\mathrm m\\[5pt]x&=X(5)+x'=8-3\\[5pt]&=5\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the initial instant, the trolley has a negative coordinate. In five seconds it travels twenty metres in the positive direction and its origin reaches eight metres from the ground origin. Measure the parcel's position from this new origin. The negative relative coordinate means three metres towards decreasing coordinates, so add the component $-3\,\mathrm m$. The graph shows parallel lines because the parcel and trolley share their ground velocity. The parcel remains three metres behind the moving origin at every instant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A moving frame starts at $70\,\mathrm m$ and has velocity $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A marker is fixed at $50\,\mathrm m$ on the ground. What is its coordinate in the moving frame after $3\,\mathrm s$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
X(3)&=70-4\cdot3=58\,\mathrm m\\[5pt]x'&=50-58\\[5pt]&=-8\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The marker keeps its ground coordinate, while the moving origin travels twelve metres in the negative direction. After three seconds it is at $58\,\mathrm m$. The relative coordinate is the difference between the vector's endpoint, the marker, and its starting point, the moving origin. The negative sign means the marker lies on the side of smaller coordinates. The separation is eight metres. As the moving origin approaches the marker, that separation decreases until it passes the marker.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At $t=4\,\mathrm s$ a particle has $x=11\,\mathrm m$ on the ground and $x'=-3\,\mathrm m$ in a frame translating at $u=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the initial coordinate of the moving origin.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X(4)&=x-x'=11-(-3)=14\,\mathrm m\\[5pt]X_0&=X(4)-u\cdot4=14-(-2)\cdot4\\[5pt]&=22\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quantity $x'$ describes the particle, while the unknown describes the moving origin. First reconstruct the origin's position at four seconds. The particle is three metres on the negative side, so the origin is three metres farther in the positive direction, at $14\,\mathrm m$. During the four seconds the origin moved eight metres towards the negative side. To return to the initial instant, add those eight metres. At the fourth second, the given velocity and the sum $X(4)+x'$ recover the observed eleven metres.

</div>
