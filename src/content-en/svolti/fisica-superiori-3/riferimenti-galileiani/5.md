## Exercise 1

A channel is $48\,\mathrm m$ wide. A boat points straight towards the opposite bank at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water, and the current flows at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the drift.

::grafico{linee="0,0 36,48" segmenti="36,0,36,48;0,48,36,48" xmin=0 xmax=40 ymin=0 ymax=54 uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" punti="36,48,pieno,A" titolo="Ground-frame path to the landing point A"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t&=\dfrac{48}{4}\,\mathrm s=12\,\mathrm s\\[5pt]x&=3\cdot12\\[5pt]&=36\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Point one axis downstream and one towards the other bank. The motor provides a transverse component of four metres per second and the current provides a longitudinal component of three. The boat takes twelve seconds to cross the width. During each second the water shifts it three metres downstream. The diagram shows a straight path because both velocities are constant. Its slope is the ratio of their components, four thirds, and gives the landing point with coordinates thirty-six and forty-eight metres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a $30\,\mathrm m$ wide channel the horizontal axis points east. The current is $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and the boat velocity relative to the water has components $v'_x=1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v'_y=3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ towards the other bank. Find the displacement along the eastward axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_x&=1-2=-1\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]t&=\dfrac{30}{3}\,\mathrm s=10\,\mathrm s\\[5pt]\Delta x&=-1\cdot10\\[5pt]&=-10\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive direction is east, so the current flows west. The boat moves east relative to the water, but the westward contribution is larger. Add the two horizontal components for the value observed from the bank. The three metres per second towards the opposite bank stays the same because the current is horizontal. Use the ten-second interval in the east–west direction as well. The negative result places the landing ten metres west of the starting point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A river is $60\,\mathrm m$ wide. A boat maintains $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water perpendicular to the banks. The current flows at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over the first half of the width and $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over the second. Find the total drift.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_1&=t_2=\dfrac{30}{5}\,\mathrm s=6\,\mathrm s\\[5pt]x&=2\cdot6+4\cdot6\\[5pt]&=36\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The current changes when the boat reaches midstream. The model uses two segments in place of a single horizontal velocity. In the first segment the boat covers thirty metres across in six seconds and drifts twelve metres. In the second segment the transverse motor-driven speed is unchanged, so the time is again six seconds, while the drift increases to twenty-four metres. Add the displacements from the two segments. The arithmetic mean of the current speeds works here because the elapsed times are equal.

</div>
