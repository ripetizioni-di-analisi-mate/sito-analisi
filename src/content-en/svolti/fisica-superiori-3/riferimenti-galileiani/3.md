## Exercise 1

A moving walkway travels at $1.2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. A person walks in the opposite direction at $1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to it. Find the ground component, taking the walkway's direction as positive.

::grafico{f="1.2*x;-0.6*x" xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=7 nomi="t (s),x (m)" legenda="\text{walkway};\text{person}" titolo="The ground-frame slopes have opposite signs"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v'&=-1.8\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v&=u+v'=1.2-1.8\\[5pt]&=- \dfrac{3}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive speeds give magnitudes. Adding velocities requires a signed component. The walkway carries the person in the positive direction, while the walk takes them towards its rear. In one second the walkway advances $1.2\,\mathrm m$ and the person moves back $1.8\,\mathrm m$ relative to it. The ground balance leaves $0.6\,\mathrm m$ towards the negative side. The component is negative, with speed $0.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The graph expresses the two directions as slopes of opposite signs.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A girl stays in front of the same ground sign while walking on a walkway moving at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find her velocity component relative to the walkway, taking its direction as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=0\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v'&=v-u=0-2\\[5pt]&=-2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The position relative to the sign is constant, so the velocity in the ground frame is zero. Subtract the walkway's velocity from zero to obtain the relative velocity. The negative sign shows that the girl walks opposite to the walkway's motion. Each second she moves two metres towards its rear while the surface she walks on advances two metres relative to the ground. The displacements cancel over the same interval and the girl stays aligned with the sign.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A man walks against a walkway with component $-2.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to it. In four seconds his ground position decreases by $2\,\mathrm m$. Find the constant walkway velocity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=\dfrac{-2}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s}=-0.5\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]u&=v-v'=-0.5-(-2.5)\\[5pt]&=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ground positions decrease, so the displacement is negative. Dividing minus two metres by four seconds gives the ground component. To isolate the walkway contribution, subtract the walking component, which is already negative. A positive velocity results. In four seconds the walkway advances eight metres and the man walks ten in the opposite direction relative to it. The sum is minus two metres, matching the ground measurement.

</div>
