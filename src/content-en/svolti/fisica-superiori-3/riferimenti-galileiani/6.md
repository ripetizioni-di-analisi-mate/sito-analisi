## Exercise 1

A boat's velocity relative to the water is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ across the river, perpendicular to a $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ current. Find the speed relative to the bank.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,3,0,u;3,0,3,4,v′;0,0,3,4,v" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5 uguale=1 nomi="v_x (m/s),v_y (m/s)" titolo="The sum of the two perpendicular contributions has magnitude 5 m/s"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec v&=(3,4)\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v&=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}\\[5pt]&=5\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An observer on the bank sees both the motion produced by the motor and the transport due to the water. The components lie on perpendicular axes, so the magnitude is the hypotenuse of the triangle. Five is greater than either leg and less than their sum, as required for a right triangle with positive legs. Adding the two speeds directly would give seven metres per second and would apply to components pointing in the same direction. The drawing shows instead that the endpoint of the sum has both coordinates nonzero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A boat can travel at $13\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the water. The current is $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is its speed relative to the bank if it must land directly opposite?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v'_x&=-5\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v_y&=v'_y=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}\\[5pt]&=12\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Landing directly opposite requires a wholly transverse ground velocity. Use a five-metres-per-second component of the relative velocity to cancel the current. Thirteen metres per second is the magnitude of the water-relative velocity and is therefore the hypotenuse. The transverse component, left unchanged by the water, is the square root of one hundred and sixty-nine minus twenty-five. Twelve metres per second remain available for crossing. Check that the longitudinal ground component is $5-5=0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The bank measures boat components $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ downstream and $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ across. The current is $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ downstream. Find the boat speed relative to the water.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec v'&=(4-8,3)=(-4,3)\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[5pt]v'&=\sqrt{(-4)^2+3^2}\\[5pt]&=5\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total velocity observed from the bank is known, and the target is the velocity relative to the water. Subtract the current from the along-bank component to obtain four metres per second upstream. The across-river component stays at three metres per second. The negative sign shows that the pilot points partly upstream even though the ground-frame path heads downstream. The hypotenuse of the relative components gives five metres per second. Adding the current back recovers both components measured from the bank.

</div>
