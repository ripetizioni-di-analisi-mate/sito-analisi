## Exercise 1

A uniform disc starts from rest and rolls without slipping through a vertical drop of $1.20\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and no dissipation, find its centre speed.

::grafico{linee="0,1.2 2.5,0" punti="0,1.2,pieno,A;2.5,0,pieno,B" xmin=-.5 xmax=3 ymin=-.5 ymax=1.7 nomi="s,h (m)" titolo="The vertical drop from A to B is $1.20\,\mathrm m$"}

$$
v_{CM}=\sqrt{4gh/3},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_{CM}&=\sqrt{4 \cdot 10 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{3}}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Potential energy becomes translation and rotation. For a disc the rotational term is half the translational term, so $mv^2$ carries a coefficient of three quarters.

The drop releases $gh=12\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ per unit mass. At four metres per second, eight are translational and four rotational, because $I=\tfrac12mR^2$ and $v=R\omega$. Their sum recovers the twelve joules per kilogram initially available.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A thin ring starts from rest and rolls without slipping through a vertical drop of $1.6\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and no dissipation, find its final centre speed.

$$
v_{CM}=\sqrt{gh},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_{CM}&=\sqrt{10 \cdot 8 \cdot \frac{1}{5}}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ring has inertia $mR^2$. Using the rolling constraint gives equal rotational and translational kinetic energies.

All mass of a thin ring is at one distance from the axis, so $I=mR^2$. With $v=R\omega$, both kinetic terms are $\tfrac12mv^2$. At four metres per second they sum to $16\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ per unit mass, exactly $10\cdot1.6$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform solid disc rolls without slipping from rest to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ without dissipation. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the vertical drop.

$$
h=\frac{3v^2}{4g},\qquad
\begin{aligned}h&=\frac{3 \cdot 4^{2}}{4 \cdot 10}\\[4pt]&=1.2\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set the lost potential energy equal to all the energy at the bottom. Include rotational energy before solving for vertical drop.

Translation alone at four metres per second would require eight joules per kilogram. The disc also rotates, requiring four more joules per kilogram. Dividing the total twelve per kilogram by $g$ gives $1.2\,\mathrm m$, greater than the $0.8\,\mathrm m$ that would suffice for a sliding particle without rotation.

</div>
