## Exercise 1

A uniform disc starts from rest and rolls without slipping through a vertical drop of $1.20\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and no dissipation, find its centre speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
mgh&=\frac12mv_{CM}^2+\frac14mv_{CM}^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac34mv_{CM}^2\\[6pt]
v_{CM}&=\sqrt{\frac{4\cdot10\cdot1.20}{3}}
=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(20/3)*x;(10/3)*x" punti="1.2,8,pieno;1.2,4,pieno" etichette=".56,5.12,K_t/m;.56,1.48,K_r/m" xmin=0 xmax=1.37 ymin=0 ymax=10 nomi="h (m),K/m (J/kg)" titolo="After a drop of 1.20 m the disc gains 8 J/kg in translation and 4 J/kg in rotation"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The central inertia of the uniform disc is half the mass times the radius squared. The rolling constraint lets us express the angular velocity as the centre speed divided by the radius. The radius cancels in the rotational energy, leaving a quarter of the mass times the centre speed squared.

The total kinetic energy comes from the decrease in potential energy. On the graph, after a vertical drop of one metre and twenty centimetres, the ordinates give eight joules per kilogram for translation and four for rotation. They add to the twelve joules per kilogram released by gravity. Static friction maintains the constraint on the fixed surface.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A thin ring starts from rest and rolls without slipping through a vertical drop of $1.6\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and no dissipation, find its final centre speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
mgh&=\frac12mv_{CM}^2+\frac12mR^2
    \left(\frac{v_{CM}}{R}\right)^2\\[6pt]
&=mv_{CM}^2\\[6pt]
v_{CM}&=\sqrt{gh}=\sqrt{10\cdot1.6}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the thin ring the mass lies at distance $R$ from the axis. The moment of inertia is therefore the mass times the radius squared. With the rolling constraint the radius cancels, and the rotational term equals the translational term. The decrease in potential energy supplies the two contributions equally.

At four metres per second each contribution is eight joules per kilogram. The total per unit mass is sixteen joules per kilogram, the product of $g$ and the height lost. The ring reaches the same speed as the disc in the preceding exercise with a larger drop. At that speed it has to gain more rotational energy per unit mass.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform solid disc rolls without slipping from rest to $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ without dissipation. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the vertical drop.

$$
\begin{aligned}
gh&=\frac12v_{CM}^2+\frac14v_{CM}^2
=\frac34v_{CM}^2\\[6pt]
h&=\frac{3v_{CM}^2}{4g}=\frac{3\cdot4^2}{4\cdot10}\\[6pt]
&=1.2\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quantity sought is the vertical change in height. Since the motion starts from rest and the descent is lossless, gravity must supply all the final kinetic energy. The mass occurs in each term. Dividing by it leaves eight joules per kilogram for translation and four more for rotation.

Each metre of descent releases ten joules per kilogram at the given value of $g$. The combined contributions therefore require a vertical drop of one metre and twenty centimetres. A point mass gaining the same speed without rotating would require eight tenths of a metre. The rotational energy accounts for the difference.

</div>
