## Exercise 1

A body with $I=0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ experiences a $3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ torque. Find angular acceleration with positive in the sense of the torque.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_z&=I\alpha\\[6pt]
\alpha&=\frac{M_z}{I}=\frac{3}{0.50}\\[6pt]
&=6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The net torque along the axis and the inertia about that axis determine the angular acceleration. The positive direction is chosen to agree with the torque, so the acceleration is positive. The ratio of a torque in newton metres to an inertia in kilogram metres squared has the units of angular acceleration.

The acceleration gives the signed change in velocity each second. If the body initially turned at six radians per second in the negative direction, the same torque would decrease the speed to zero in one second. The initial direction is what distinguishes slowing down from gaining speed under the same positive acceleration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A lever of length $0.30\,\mathrm m$ receives a $20\,\mathrm N$ force at $30^\circ$ to the arm. Find the torque magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_t&=F\sin30^\circ=20\cdot\frac12=10\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
|M|&=rF_t=0.30\cdot10=3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3,0" segmenti="3,0,5,0" curve="3+.72*cos(t),.72*sin(t),0,.52359878" punti="0,0,pieno,O;3,0,pieno,A" vettori="3,0,5.598,1.5,F" etichette="3.95,.21,30°" xmin=-1 xmax=6.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="The force makes thirty degrees with the arm r"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the diagram the radius runs from the pivot to the point where the force acts. The component parallel to the radius has a line of action through the pivot. The perpendicular component has a moment arm of the full thirty centimetres. The perpendicular projection is half the force because the angle with the radius is thirty degrees.

The same result comes from using the full force and the distance from the pivot to its line of action. That distance is $0.30\sin30^\circ=0.15\,\mathrm m$. The two methods give the same magnitude. With the positive axis towards the reader, the force in the diagram tends to turn the lever anticlockwise.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pulley of radius $0.20\,\mathrm m$ and inertia $0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ is pulled by two downward tangential tensions. The tension on the left is $12\,\mathrm N$ and on the right it is $7\,\mathrm N$. Neglecting axle friction, find the magnitude of $\alpha$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_z&=T_1R-T_2R\\[6pt]
&=(12-7)\cdot0.20=1.0\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}\\[6pt]
|\alpha|&=\frac{|M_z|}{I}=\frac{1.0}{0.10}
=10\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left tension points downwards and produces an anticlockwise torque. The right tension also points downwards, but acts on the other side of the axis and produces a clockwise torque. Choose anticlockwise as positive. The two contributions then subtract even though the forces point in the same direction.

Both tensions are tangential, so their moment arm is the pulley radius. The signed contributions are $+2.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ for the first and $-1.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ for the second. The positive net torque produces an anticlockwise angular acceleration. The requested magnitude depends on the inertia of the entire pulley about its axis.

</div>
