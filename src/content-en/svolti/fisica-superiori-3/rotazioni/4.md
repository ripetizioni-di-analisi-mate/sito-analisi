## Exercise 1

A body with $I=0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ experiences a $3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ torque. Find angular acceleration with positive in the sense of the torque.

$$
\alpha=\frac MI,\qquad
\begin{aligned}\alpha&=\frac{3}{1 \cdot \frac{1}{2}}\\[4pt]&=6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a fixed axis the net torque equals inertia times acceleration. This relation does not require the initial velocity.

Torque and inertia already refer to the same axis. Inertia is positive, so the angular acceleration sign follows that of torque. Substituting $6\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ gives back $0.50\cdot6=3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ without needing initial angular velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A lever of length $0.30\,\mathrm m$ receives a $20\,\mathrm N$ force at $30^\circ$ to the arm. Find the torque magnitude.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 3,0" punti="0,0,pieno,O" vettori="3,0,5.598,1.5,F" xmin=-1 xmax=6.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3 titolo="The force makes thirty degrees with the arm r"}

$$
M=rF\sin\phi,\qquad
\begin{aligned}M&=3 \cdot \frac{1}{10} \cdot 20 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\\[4pt]&=3\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Only the component perpendicular to the lever contributes. The sine of thirty degrees is one half, so the effective moment arm is half the length.

The force component perpendicular to the lever is $20\sin30^\circ=10\,\mathrm N$. Multiplying it by the $0.30\,\mathrm m$ radius gives the same result as full force times the $0.15\,\mathrm m$ moment arm. The parallel component's line runs through the pivot and contributes no torque.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pulley of radius $0.20\,\mathrm m$ and inertia $0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ has opposing tangential tensions of $12$ and $7\,\mathrm N$. Find the magnitude of $\alpha$.

$$
\alpha=\frac{(T_1-T_2)R}{I},\qquad
\begin{aligned}\alpha&=\left(12 - 7\right) \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{1 \cdot \frac{1}{10}}\\[4pt]&=10\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tension torques are opposite and have equal moment arms. Divide their difference by the inertia.

The two tensions give torques of magnitudes $2.4$ and $1.4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. Subtract them because they turn the pulley in opposite directions. Their difference is one newton metre, giving ten radians per second squared for inertia $0.10\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$.

</div>
