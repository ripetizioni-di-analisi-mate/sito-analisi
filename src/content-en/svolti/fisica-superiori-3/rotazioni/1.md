## Exercise 1

A wheel starts at $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and accelerates at $2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ for $3\,\mathrm s$. Find its final angular velocity.

::grafico{linee="0,4 3,10" punti="0,4,pieno;3,10,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=3.8 ymin=0 ymax=12 nomi="t (s),ω (rad/s)" titolo="The segment slope is two radians per second squared"}

$$
\omega=\omega_0+\alpha t,\qquad
\begin{aligned}\omega&=4 + 2 \cdot 3\\[4pt]&=10\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gain over three seconds is six radians per second. Add it to the initial velocity because the acceleration is in the same sense.

The $2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ value is the change of angular velocity in one second. Three seconds accumulate $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, added to the initial four. Check backwards: the slope of the segment from $4$ to $10\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is $6/3=2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, consistent with the prescribed acceleration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wheel at $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ slows with $\alpha=-3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. Find when it first stops.

$$
t=-\frac{\omega_0}{\alpha},\qquad
\begin{aligned}t&=\frac{8}{3}\\[4pt]&\simeq2.66667\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set the final angular velocity to zero to cancel the full initial value. Negative acceleration gives a positive stopping time.

Stopping requires losing the full initial $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The loss rate has magnitude $3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$, so the time is $\dfrac83\,\mathrm s$. Substitution into the angular velocity line gives $8-3\cdot\dfrac83=0$. The line implies reverse rotation after that point only if the same braking torque is maintained.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel changes from $-3$ to $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm s$ at constant acceleration. Find $\alpha$.

$$
\alpha=\frac{\omega_f-\omega_0}{t},\qquad
\begin{aligned}\alpha&=\frac{6 - -3}{3}\\[4pt]&=3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initial velocity is negative, so the change is nine radians per second. Crossing zero indicates a reversal during the interval.

The initial value is negative, so the signed change is $6-(-3)=9\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. After one second angular velocity reaches zero from below. The remaining two seconds add $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ in the positive direction. Taking just the difference between magnitudes would count only three radians per second and miss the reversal.

</div>
