## Exercise 1

A wheel starts at $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and accelerates at $2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ for $3\,\mathrm s$. Find its final angular velocity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\omega&=\alpha t=2\cdot3=6\,\mathrm{rad/s}\\[6pt]
\omega_f&=\omega_0+\Delta\omega\\[6pt]
&=4+6=10\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,4 3,10" punti="0,4,pieno;3,10,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=3.8 ymin=0 ymax=12 nomi="t (s),ω (rad/s)" titolo="The segment slope is two radians per second squared"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Angular velocity and angular acceleration are components about the same axis. In one second the latter raises the former by two radians per second. Since the rate of change is constant, three equal increments add to the initial four over the three seconds. The positive result means that the direction of rotation has remained the chosen positive one.

On the graph the initial value is the intercept at $t=0$. The slope is the gain of six radians per second divided by three seconds. The line segment describes the time for which we know the acceleration. To reconstruct the motion beyond the final reading, we would need to know how long the cause of that acceleration acts.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wheel at $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ slows with $\alpha=-3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. Find when it first stops.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=\omega_0+\alpha t_{\mathrm a}\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm a}&=-\frac{\omega_0}{\alpha}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{8}{3}\,\mathrm s\simeq2.67\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The disc stops when its angular velocity reaches zero. Because the acceleration is negative, each second removes three radians per second. Divide the initial velocity by the magnitude of the rate of decrease. The ratio of the angular velocity and angular acceleration units gives seconds.

Substituting the exact time into the velocity equation gives $8-3\cdot\dfrac83=0$. The line would cross the time axis if the same torque continued to act after the stop. A brake that applies a torque opposing rotation behaves differently once the disc comes to rest.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel changes from $-3$ to $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm s$ at constant acceleration. Find $\alpha$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\omega&=6-(-3)=9\,\mathrm{rad/s}\\[6pt]
\alpha&=\frac{\Delta\omega}{\Delta t}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{9}{3}=3\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The readings at the two ends of the interval are signed. The first describes rotation in the negative direction and the second in the positive direction. Since the change is the final value minus the initial one, subtracting the negative number increases the result. Using the magnitudes of the readings would give only the difference between the angular speeds.

At the calculated rate, one second and a gain of three radians per second are enough to reach zero. Over the remaining two seconds the velocity becomes positive and reaches the stated value. The change in direction during the interval is consistent with a single constant positive acceleration.

</div>
