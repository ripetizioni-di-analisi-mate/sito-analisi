## Exercise 1

A uniform disc of $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ has radius $0.50\,\mathrm m$. Find its moment of inertia about the axis through its centre perpendicular to the disc.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{\mathrm{CM}}&=\frac12mR^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac12\cdot4\cdot(0.50)^2\\[6pt]
&=0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis passes through the centre of the disc and is normal to its plane. For this configuration, the factor of one half describes how the mass of a uniform disc is distributed between the centre and the rim. The radius, expressed in metres, is squared because distance enters each part's contribution as a square.

A thin ring of the same mass and radius would have a moment of inertia of one kilogram metre squared. All its mass would lie at the rim. The smaller value for the disc agrees with the presence of material close to the axis. The disc formula requires both the stated axis and a uniform distribution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ disc of radius $0.50\,\mathrm m$ rotates about an axis through its rim perpendicular to the disc. Find its moment of inertia about that axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{\mathrm{CM}}&=\frac12\cdot4\cdot(0.50)^2
=0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}\\[6pt]
md^2&=4\cdot(0.50)^2=1.00\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}\\[6pt]
I_B&=I_{\mathrm{CM}}+md^2=1.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve=".5*cos(t),.5*sin(t),0,6.283185" linee="0,0 .5,0" punti="0,0,pieno,C;.5,0,pieno,B" xmin=-.7 xmax=.8 ymin=-.7 ymax=.7 titolo="Moving the axis from centre C to rim B increases inertia"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both axes are perpendicular to the face of the disc, so they are parallel. The first passes through the centre of mass and the second through the point on the rim. Their separation is the distance from the centre to the rim, one radius. These conditions satisfy the parallel-axis theorem.

For the disc the central moment of inertia is half a kilogram metre squared. The shift term is twice as large because it equals the mass times the radius squared. The total inertia about the rim is therefore three times the central value. This comparison applies to these two axes and this uniform disc.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ disc of radius $0.40\,\mathrm m$ carries a $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ point mass on its rim. Find the combined moment of inertia about the axis through the disc's centre, perpendicular to the disc.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_d&=\frac12\cdot2\cdot(0.40)^2
=0.16\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}\\[6pt]
I_p&=0.50\cdot(0.40)^2=0.08\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}\\[6pt]
I&=I_d+I_p=0.24\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axle passes through the geometric centre of the disc. The disc therefore contributes its central inertia. Adding a point mass shifts the centre of mass of the assembly, while the question keeps the same axis. The added mass is all forty centimetres from that axis and contributes its mass times that distance squared.

The two contributions add because they refer to the same line. The point on the rim accounts for one fifth of the total mass and adds one third of the final inertia. This comparison shows the effect of placing part of the mass as far from the axle as possible.

</div>
