## Exercise 1

A uniform disc of $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ has radius $0.50\,\mathrm m$. Find its moment of inertia about the axis through its centre perpendicular to the disc.

$$
I=\frac12mR^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}I&=\frac{4 \cdot \left(1 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=0.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Inertia reflects radial mass distribution. The solid-disc coefficient is half that of a ring with the same mass and radius.

The result must be below $mR^2=1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, the value if all mass were concentrated at the rim. Inner portions of a uniform disc lie closer to the axis and contribute less to inertia. Do not use the diameter here: each distance is measured from the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ disc of radius $0.50\,\mathrm m$ rotates about an axis through its rim perpendicular to the disc. Find its moment of inertia about that axis.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve=".5*cos(t),.5*sin(t),0,6.283185" linee="0,0 .5,0" punti="0,0,pieno,C;.5,0,pieno,B" xmin=-.7 xmax=.8 ymin=-.7 ymax=.7 titolo="Moving the axis from centre C to rim B increases inertia"}

$$
I=I_{CM}+mR^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}I&=\frac{4 \cdot \left(1 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}{2} + 4 \cdot \left(1 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\\[4pt]&=1.5\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving the axis by one radius adds mass times distance squared. The central contribution remains in the result.

The central and rim axes are $0.50\,\mathrm m$ apart. Thus the transfer term is $4\cdot0.50^2=1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, added to the centroidal $0.50$. This is a positive change, and replacing radius by diameter in the disc formula would not give the rim-axis inertia.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ disc of radius $0.40\,\mathrm m$ carries a $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ point mass on its rim. Find the combined moment of inertia about the axis through the disc's centre, perpendicular to the disc.

$$
I=\frac12m_dR^2+m_pR^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}I&=1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^{2} + \frac{2 \cdot \left(\frac{2}{5}\right)^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=0.24\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both contributions are about the disc axis. The mass on the rim lies exactly one radius from that axis.

The uniform disc contributes $0.16\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. All of the added point mass lies $0.40\,\mathrm m$ from the axis and contributes $0.50\cdot0.40^2=0.08\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$. Add them because both contributions refer to the same axis, that of the disc.

</div>
