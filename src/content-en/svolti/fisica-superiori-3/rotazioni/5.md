## Exercise 1

A flywheel of inertia $2\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ rotates at $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Find its kinetic energy.

$$
K=\frac12I\omega^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}K&=\frac{2 \cdot 6^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=36\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Angular velocity enters squared. Rotation in the opposite sense at equal speed gives the same energy.

In $\tfrac12I\omega^2$, the square is $36\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$ because radians are dimensionless. Multiply by half the inertia, $1\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$, to obtain $36\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s^2}=36\,\mathrm J$. Reversing the sense of rotation would leave this energy unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rotor of inertia $0.50\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ changes from $4$ to $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Find the work done by net torque.

::grafico{f=".25*x^2" punti="4,4,pieno;8,16,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=21 nomi="ω (rad/s),K (J)" titolo="Net work is the difference between the two ordinates"}

$$
W=\frac12I(\omega_f^2-\omega_i^2),\qquad
\begin{aligned}W&=1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \left(- 4^{2} + 8^{2}\right) \cdot \frac{1}{2}\\[4pt]&=12\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the initial energy. Doubling speed makes the final energy four times larger, and work equals the gain.

The rotor has $4\,\mathrm J$ before the change and $16\,\mathrm J$ afterwards. Doubling angular velocity quadruples energy at constant inertia. The required work is their twelve-joule difference and is positive because the rotor gains energy.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A flywheel with inertia $3\,\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2}$ starts from rest and receives a constant torque of $6\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$ through $4\,\mathrm{rad}$. Find its final angular speed.

$$
\omega_f=\sqrt{2M\Delta\theta/I},\qquad
\begin{aligned}\omega_f&=\sqrt{2 \cdot 6 \cdot 4 \cdot \frac{1}{3}}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{rad/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The torque increases energy from zero to twenty-four joules. Take the positive magnitude when finding angular speed from energy.

The energy method gives the magnitude. Starting from rest under a positive torque also fixes the positive sign. As a check use $\alpha=M/I=2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$ and $\omega_f^2=2\alpha\Delta\theta=16\,\mathrm{rad^2/s^2}$. Both approaches give four radians per second.

</div>
