## Exercise 1

A disc starts from rest at $\alpha=4\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. How far does it turn in its first $3\,\mathrm s$?

::grafico{linee="0,0 3,12" area="4*x;0;0;3" xmin=0 xmax=3.5 ymin=0 ymax=15 nomi="t (s),ω (rad/s)" titolo="The triangular area gives the angle swept"}

$$
\Delta\theta=\frac12\alpha t^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}\Delta\theta&=\frac{4 \cdot 3^{2}}{2}\\[4pt]&=18\,\mathrm{rad}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Starting from zero gives a triangular graph. Its final height is twelve radians per second and the base is three seconds.

The angular velocity graph starts at zero and reaches $12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ after three seconds. The triangular area is twelve times three divided by two, giving eighteen radians. Multiplying twelve directly by three would assign the final speed to the entire interval.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A disc starts from rest at $\alpha=2\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}$. How far does it turn from $t=2\,\mathrm s$ to $t=4\,\mathrm s$?

$$
\Delta\theta=\frac12\alpha(t_f^2-t_i^2),\qquad
\begin{aligned}\Delta\theta&=\frac{2 \cdot \left(- 2^{2} + 4^{2}\right)}{2}\\[4pt]&=12\,\mathrm{rad}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wheel is already moving at two seconds. Subtracting its angle at two seconds from its angle at four isolates the requested interval.

Between two and four seconds the disc is already turning: $\omega$ rises from $4$ to $8\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The trapezium has mean height $6\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and duration $2\,\mathrm s$, giving $12\,\mathrm{rad}$. Applying the from-rest equation to a duration of two seconds would describe the initial stage instead.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A blade starts at rest and turns through $18\,\mathrm{rad}$ in $3\,\mathrm s$ at constant angular acceleration. Find that acceleration.

$$
\alpha=\frac{2\Delta\theta}{t^2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}\alpha&=2 \cdot 18 \cdot \frac{1}{3^{2}}\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{rad/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Rearrange the angular law to divide twice the displacement by squared time. The result is positive because the angle is positive.

Check by finding the final angular velocity $4\cdot3=12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Its mean is six radians per second because the increase is linear and starts at zero. Over three seconds that mean gives the specified eighteen radians exactly.

</div>
