## Exercise 1

A wheel of radius $0.40\,\mathrm m$ rolls without slipping on a stationary surface with $|\omega|=5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Find the speed of its centre.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=v_{CM}-|\omega|R\\[6pt]
v_{CM}&=|\omega|R=5\cdot0.40\\[6pt]
&=2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 assi=0 curve=".4*cos(t),.4+.4*sin(t),0,6.283185" linee="-.6,0 1.05,0" vettori="0,.4,.3,.4,v_CM;0,.8,.6,.8,v" punti="0,0,pieno,C" xmin=-.65 xmax=1.1 ymin=-.2 ymax=1.05 titolo="Contact point C is instantaneously at rest"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The bottom point of the wheel shares the centre's forward velocity. Rotation about the centre adds a backward tangential velocity there. The surface is stationary and the wheel rolls without slipping, so the contributions cancel at that point.

The magnitude of the rotation contribution is the angular speed times the radius. It is two metres per second here and determines the speed of the centre. The arrows also show that at the top the two contributions add. The contact point has zero velocity at this instant. The material point occupying it leaves the surface as the wheel moves forward.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A roller of radius $0.25\,\mathrm m$ moves at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ without slipping on a stationary surface. Find the speed of its top point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\omega|&=\frac{v_{CM}}{R}=\frac{3}{0.25}=12\,\mathrm{rad/s}\\[6pt]
v_{\mathrm{top}}&=v_{CM}+|\omega|R\\[6pt]
&=3+(12\cdot0.25)=6\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The roller moves forwards and its top point moves forwards relative to the centre. There the velocity due to rotation is parallel to the centre's velocity and points the same way. The tangential speed equals the centre's speed because the roller satisfies the rolling constraint on a stationary surface.

The radius gives the angular speed, twelve radians per second. Multiplying by the radius again recovers three metres per second to add to the translation's three. The radius affects the angular velocity required. At a fixed speed of the centre, the speed at the top is always twice that speed in this model.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel rolls without slipping on fixed ground and moves forward at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the speed of the frontmost rim point level with the centre?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_x&=v_{CM}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
v_y&=-|\omega|R=-4\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
|\vec v|&=\sqrt{4^2+(-4)^2}
=4\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq5.66\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the wheel to move rightwards. The front point is at the same height as the centre, so its tangential velocity is vertical. Clockwise rotation points it downwards. The rolling constraint makes the magnitude of this component equal to the speed of the centre.

In the velocity sum one term gives the horizontal component and the other the vertical one. The four metres per second of each form the two legs of the velocity triangle. Their resultant is the hypotenuse and points forwards and downwards at forty-five degrees. The speed doubles at the top, where the velocities point the same way.

</div>
