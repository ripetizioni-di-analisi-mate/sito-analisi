## Exercise 1

A rotor completes $30$ revolutions per minute at a constant rate. What is its angular speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega&=2\pi f=2\pi\cdot\frac{30}{60}\\[4pt]
&= \pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Divide the revolutions by sixty to express them per second. Each revolution includes $2\pi$ radians. Multiplying half a revolution per second by the radians in one turn gives $\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

A frequency of half a hertz corresponds to a period of $2\,\mathrm s$. In one second the rotor covers half a turn and sweeps $\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$. Starting from revolutions per minute or from the angle of a half turn therefore gives the same angular speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point sweeps a quarter revolution at constant rate in $0.5\,\mathrm s$. What is $\omega$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega&=\frac{(2\pi)/4}{0.5}\\[4pt]
&= \pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A quarter revolution is $\dfrac\pi2$ radians. The given duration applies to that quarter only, so divide its angle by half a second. At the same rate a full turn would take two seconds.

As a check, applying $\omega=\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ for $0.5\,\mathrm s$ gives the angle of a quarter turn. On a circle of radius $r$, the distance along the path is $r$ times that angle. The arc length depends on the radius, while the number of radians is set by the fraction of a revolution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

There are two distinct marks on the rim of a uniformly rotating disk. A fixed sensor records a pulse whenever either mark passes. Intervals between pulses alternate between $0.12\,\mathrm s$ and $0.28\,\mathrm s$. Find the rotation period, the angular speed and the angle subtended by the shorter arc between the marks.

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.283185307;0.55*cos(t),0.55*sin(t),0,1.884955592" punti="1,0,pieno;-0.309017,0.951057,pieno" segmenti="0,0,1,0;0,0,-0.309017,0.951057" etichette="1.06,0.13,A;-0.47,1.08,B;0.19,0.61,108°" xmin=-1.25 xmax=1.35 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.26 uguale=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="The shorter arc from A to B corresponds to the interval of 0.12 s."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=(0.12+0.28)\,\mathrm s=0.40\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
\omega&=\frac{2\pi}{0.40}\,\mathrm{rad/s}=5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s},\\[6pt]
\Delta\theta_{\min}&=5\pi\cdot0.12\,\mathrm{rad}
=\frac{3\pi}{5}\,\mathrm{rad}=108^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two marks pass alternately. From the passage of one mark, add the wait for the other and the wait to see the first again. This gives the time for one revolution. The intervals differ because the arcs between the marks have different lengths. The shorter arc takes the fraction $\dfrac{0.12}{0.40}=0.30$ of a revolution. Multiplying this fraction by $360^\circ$ checks the angle obtained in radians. There are two pulses per revolution, with two different interval lengths.

</div>
