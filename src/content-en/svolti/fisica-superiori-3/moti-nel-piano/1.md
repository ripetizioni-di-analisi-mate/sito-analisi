## Exercise 1

A drone moves $24\,\mathrm m$ east and $-18\,\mathrm m$ along the north-pointing axis in $6\,\mathrm s$. What is the magnitude of its mean velocity?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,24,-18,s" segmenti="0,0,24,0;24,0,24,-18" xmin=-2 xmax=26 ymin=-20 ymax=2 uguale=1 titolo="Displacement forms a right triangle with its components"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_m&=\sqrt{(24/6)^2+(-18/6)^2}\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mean velocity is $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ east and $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ south. Divide the two signed displacements by the same elapsed time. The directions are perpendicular, so add their squares.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object has speed $13\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_x=-5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find $v_y$ if it rises.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_y&=+\sqrt{13^2-(-5)^2}\\[4pt]
&= 12\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the squared horizontal contribution from the squared speed, regardless of its sign. Two possibilities of magnitude twelve remain. Rising motion imposes the positive sign on an upward axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In $2\,\mathrm s$, velocity changes from $(0,6)$ to $(8,0)\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the magnitude of average acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_m&=\frac{\sqrt{(8-0)^2+(0-6)^2}}2\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the velocities component by component. The difference has components $8$ and $-6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and magnitude ten. Division by two seconds gives five metres per second squared. Subtracting just the speeds would lose the change in direction.

</div>
