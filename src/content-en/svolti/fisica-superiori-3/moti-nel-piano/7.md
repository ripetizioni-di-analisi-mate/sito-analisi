## Exercise 1

An object curves at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ around a radius of $9\,\mathrm m$. Find the magnitude of $a_c$.

::grafico{curve="9*cos(t),9*sin(t),0,1.5708" vettori="9,0,5,0,a_c;9,0,9,6,v" xmin=-1 xmax=11 ymin=-1 ymax=10.5 uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="The v and a_c arrows are perpendicular at the position shown. The arrows show the directions."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_c&=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{6^2}{9}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity changes direction while retaining its magnitude. Radial acceleration is speed squared divided by radius and points towards the centre. The units are $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ divided by metres. This value describes change of the velocity vector without implying an increase of speed.

Dividing the speed by the radius gives $\omega=\dfrac23\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. The formula $\omega^2r$ gives the same acceleration. If the radius doubled at the same speed, both angular speed and centripetal acceleration would halve.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point on a disc rotates at $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and is $0.25\,\mathrm m$ from the centre. Find the magnitude of $a_c$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_c&=\omega^2r=4^2\cdot0.25\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angular data give $\omega^2r$ directly. This is different from $\omega r$, the one-metre-per-second tangential speed. Substituting that speed in $v^2/r$ confirms four metres per second squared.

Because the point is fixed to the disk, doubling the distance from the centre also doubles tangential speed. Substituting in $v^2/r$ quadruples the numerator and doubles the denominator. This recovers the proportionality to the radius when $\omega$ is held fixed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At the same bend, a sensor records two pairs of speed and radial acceleration. At $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ it reads $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ it reads $4.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Estimate the radius from each pair. A third run at $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ reads $9.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ with a stated uncertainty of $0.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Assess whether the last reading is consistent with the estimated radius.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=\frac{4^2}{2}\,\mathrm m=8\,\mathrm m,\qquad
r_2=\frac{6^2}{4.5}\,\mathrm m=8\,\mathrm m,\\[6pt]
a_{c3,\mathrm{mod}}&=\frac{8^2}{8}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=8\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\[4pt]
a_{c3,\mathrm{obs}}&\in[9.0;9.4]\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relation $a_c=\dfrac{v^2}{r}$ gives a radius from each pair. The estimates agree. On the same arc, doubling the speed from the first reading must quadruple the radial acceleration, consistent with the prediction of eight metres per second squared. The third reading's uncertainty allows values from $9.0$ to $9.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The prediction lies outside that interval. If the first two pairs establish the radius and the third speed is known, the third reading is inconsistent with the model.

</div>
