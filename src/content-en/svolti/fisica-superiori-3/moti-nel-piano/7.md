## Exercise 1

An object curves at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ around a radius of $9\,\mathrm m$. Find the magnitude of $a_c$.

::grafico{curve="9*cos(t),9*sin(t),0,1.5708" vettori="9,0,5,0,a_c;9,0,9,6,v" xmin=-1 xmax=11 ymin=-1 ymax=10.5 uguale=1 titolo="The v and a_c arrows are perpendicular at the position shown"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_c&=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{6^2}{9}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity changes direction while retaining its magnitude. Radial acceleration is speed squared divided by radius and points towards the centre. The units are $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ divided by metres. This value describes change of the velocity vector without implying an increase of speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point on a disc rotates at $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and is $0.25\,\mathrm m$ from the centre. Find its centripetal acceleration magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_c&=\omega^2r=4^2\cdot0.25\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angular data give $\omega^2r$ directly. This is different from $\omega r$, the one-metre-per-second tangential speed. Substituting that speed in $v^2/r$ confirms four metres per second squared.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At $v=8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the required $a_c$ is $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What radius must the circle have?

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{v^2}{a_c}=\frac{8^2}{4}\\[4pt]
&= 16\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At a fixed speed, a broader curve changes direction more slowly. Solve $a_c=v^2/r$ for radius. Sixty-four square metres per square second divided by four metres per square second leaves sixteen metres.

</div>
