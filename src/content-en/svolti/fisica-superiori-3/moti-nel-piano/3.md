## Exercise 1

A projectile has $v_{0x}=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_{0y}=10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, how far does it rise above launch?

::grafico{curve="6*t,10*t-5*t^2,0,2" punti="6,5,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=13 ymin=-0.5 ymax=6 titolo="Maximum height occurs above horizontal coordinate 6 m"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=\frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g}=\frac{10^2}{2\cdot10}\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the top the vertical component vanishes, while the horizontal component remains six. The time-free equation applies along $y$ and uses only the initial vertical velocity. The vertex is reached after one second and six horizontal metres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A projectile rises at most $20\,\mathrm m$ above launch. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what was its initial vertical velocity?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{0y}&=\sqrt{2gH}=\sqrt{2\cdot10\cdot20}\\[4pt]
&= 20\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set vertical velocity to zero at maximum height. The equation $0=v_{0y}^2-2gH$ has two algebraic roots, but initial rise requires the positive one. The recovered component drops by ten metres per second each second and vanishes after two seconds.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body is launched from a roof $12\,\mathrm m$ above the ground with $v_{0y}=8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Use $g=8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its greatest height above the ground?

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{\max}&=h+\frac{v_{0y}^2}{2g}=12+\frac{8^2}{2\cdot8}\\[4pt]
&= 16\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rise above the roof is four metres. The ground is twelve metres below launch, so the requested elevation includes both terms. After one second vertical velocity vanishes and the equation confirms $12+8-4=16\,\mathrm m$.

</div>
