## Exercise 1

A sphere is launched horizontally at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from $20\,\mathrm m$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and ignore drag. What horizontal distance does it cover?

::grafico{curve="4*t,20-5*t^2,0,2" punti="0,20,pieno;8,0,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=10 ymin=-1 ymax=22 titolo="Flight time 2 s and range 8 m"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_f&=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot20}{10}}=2\,\mathrm s\\[4pt]R&=4\cdot2\\[4pt]
&= 8\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Time comes from vertical free fall, which starts with zero vertical velocity. During those two seconds the horizontal component remains four metres per second. Their product gives the range, with impact at the end of the displayed arc.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object launched horizontally from $5\,\mathrm m$ lands $7\,\mathrm m$ away. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect drag. What was its launch speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=\frac{R}{\sqrt{2h/g}}=\frac7{\sqrt{2\cdot5/10}}\\[4pt]
&= 7\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From five metres the vertical time is one second. Horizontal distance is seven metres and horizontal acceleration is zero, so constant velocity covers that distance in one second. The ratio is in metres per second.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball launched horizontally at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is directly above a point $6\,\mathrm m$ away horizontally. How far has it dropped from launch? Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and ignore drag.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta h&=\tfrac12g(x/v_0)^2=\tfrac12\cdot10(6/3)^2\\[4pt]
&= 20\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the horizontal datum determines the time. At three metres per second it takes two seconds to advance six metres. During that time vertical motion starts from rest and grows with the square of duration, giving twenty metres of fall.

</div>
