## Exercise 1

A sphere is launched horizontally at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from $20\,\mathrm m$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and ignore drag. What horizontal distance does it cover?

::grafico{curve="4*t,20-5*t^2,0,2" punti="0,20,pieno;8,0,pieno" etichette="0.30,20.43,L (20 m);8.17,0.81,I" xmin=0 xmax=9.4 ymin=0 ymax=22 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="Flight time 2 s and range 8 m"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_f&=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot20}{10}}=2\,\mathrm s\\[4pt]R&=4\cdot2\\[4pt]
&= 8\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Time comes from vertical free fall, which starts with zero vertical velocity. During those two seconds the horizontal component remains four metres per second. Their product gives the range, with impact at the end of the displayed arc.

Halfway through the flight the ball has travelled $4\,\mathrm m$ horizontally and dropped $5\,\mathrm m$. In the second interval it moves another four metres horizontally and drops fifteen metres. The increasing magnitude of its vertical velocity explains why the path gets steeper.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object launched horizontally from $5\,\mathrm m$ lands $7\,\mathrm m$ away. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect drag. What was its launch speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=\frac{R}{\sqrt{2h/g}}=\frac7{\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot5}{10}}}\\[4pt]
&= 7\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From five metres the vertical time is one second. Horizontal distance is seven metres and horizontal acceleration is zero, so constant velocity covers that distance in one second. The ratio is in metres per second.

Check the speed using the path $y=5-\dfrac{5}{49}x^2$ in SI units. At horizontal coordinate $7\,\mathrm m$ the height is zero. The horizontal coordinate has to be measured from the launch vertical because the distance in the calculation is the horizontal displacement.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The path of a ball launched horizontally is $y=11-\dfrac{x^2}{20}$ in SI units, with $x$ measured from the launch vertical in the direction of motion and $y=0$ at floor level. Neglect air resistance and use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find its initial speed and its horizontal coordinate when it passes a height of $2\,\mathrm m$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{g}{2v_0^2}&=\frac1{20}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},\\[6pt]
v_0&=\sqrt{\frac{10\cdot 20}{2}}\,\mathrm{m/s}
=10\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
11-\frac{x^2}{20}&=2,\qquad x^2=180,\\[4pt]
x&=6\sqrt5\,\mathrm m\simeq13.42\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With no air resistance the horizontal component stays constant. Substitute $t=\dfrac{x}{v_0}$ in the vertical equation to identify the coefficient of $x^2$, which depends on the launch speed. The ball travels to the right from the horizontal origin, so take the positive root for the second part. It reaches the required height before impact because it is still above the floor. As a check, the elapsed time is $t=\dfrac{6\sqrt5}{10}\,\mathrm s$ and the height lost is $9\,\mathrm m$, equal to the difference between the initial and requested heights.

</div>
