## Exercise 1

A point at $r=0.50\,\mathrm m$ completes a revolution every $2\,\mathrm s$. Find its tangential speed.

::grafico{curve="0.5*cos(t),0.5*sin(t),0,6.2832" vettori="0,0,0.5,0,r;0.5,0,0.5,0.35,v" etichette="0.47,-0.065,0.5" xmin=-0.7 xmax=0.8 ymin=-0.7 ymax=0.7 uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="Velocity is tangent at the point half a metre from the axis"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_t&=\frac{2\pi r}{T}=\frac{2\pi\cdot0.50}{2}\\[4pt]
&= \frac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In two seconds the point covers a circumference of length $\pi\,\mathrm m$. Divide that distance by duration for the speed. The same value follows from $\omega=\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $v_t=r\omega$.

After the first second the point is on the opposite radius. It has travelled half the circumference, a distance of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm m$. The displacement magnitude over that same second is the diameter, $1\,\mathrm m$. Tangential speed uses the distance travelled along the circle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wheel has angular speed $12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. At what radius is tangential speed $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{v_t}{\omega}=\frac3{12}\\[4pt]
&=0.25\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In one second the point travels an arc of three metres and sweeps through twelve radians. Their ratio is the radius because one radian spans an arc one radius long. A quarter of a metre times twelve recovers the original speed.

A second point at half that radius would sweep the same twelve radians in one second and travel half the arc length. Its speed would therefore be $1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The comparison also gives a physical interpretation of the unknown radius. It identifies which points on the disk travel three metres each second.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vertical disk of radius $0.50\,\mathrm m$ is carried by a trolley. Viewed from the front, its centre moves to the right at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the disk turns anticlockwise at $4\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Find the ground-relative velocities of the top and bottom points on its rim.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_t&=r\omega=(0.50\cdot4)\,\mathrm{m/s}=2\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[4pt]
\vec v_{\mathrm{A}}&=(4-2;0)\,\mathrm{m/s}=(2;0)\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[4pt]
\vec v_{\mathrm{B}}&=(4+2;0)\,\mathrm{m/s}=(6;0)\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Relative to the centre, A is the top point and moves left, while B is the bottom point and moves right. At both points the relative motion is horizontal because the radius is vertical. Add the common translational velocity to the motion about the centre. At the top the rotational contribution reduces the rightward component. At the bottom it increases it. Both ground-relative velocities still point right because the trolley's four metres per second exceed the rotation's two metres per second. The mean of the horizontal components is the centre's velocity.

</div>
