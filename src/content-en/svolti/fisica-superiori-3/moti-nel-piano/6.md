## Exercise 1

A point at $r=0.50\,\mathrm m$ completes a revolution every $2\,\mathrm s$. Find its tangential speed.

::grafico{curve="0.5*cos(t),0.5*sin(t),0,6.2832" vettori="0,0,0.5,0,r;0.5,0,0.5,0.35,v" xmin=-0.7 xmax=0.8 ymin=-0.7 ymax=0.7 uguale=1 titolo="Velocity is tangent at the point half a metre from the axis"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_t&=\frac{2\pi r}{T}=\frac{2\pi\cdot0.50}{2}\\[4pt]
&= \frac{\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In two seconds the point covers a circumference of length $\pi\,\mathrm m$. Divide that distance by duration for the speed. The same value follows from $\omega=\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $v_t=r\omega$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wheel has angular speed $12\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. At what radius is tangential speed $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=\frac{v_t}{\omega}=\frac3{12}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 0.25\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In one second the point travels an arc of three metres and sweeps through twelve radians. Their ratio is the radius because one radian spans an arc one radius long. A quarter of a metre times twelve recovers the original speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rigid wheel rotates uniformly. For points $0.20\,\mathrm m$ and $0.50\,\mathrm m$ from the axis, what is the second tangential speed divided by the first?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{v_2}{v_1}&=\frac{\omega r_2}{\omega r_1}=\frac{0.50}{0.20}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 2.5
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The points share angular speed. The outer point travels a longer arc in the same interval, in proportion to its radius. The ratio is therefore two and a half.

</div>
