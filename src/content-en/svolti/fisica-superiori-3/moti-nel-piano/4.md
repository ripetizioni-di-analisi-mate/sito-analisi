## Exercise 1

A $45^\circ$ launch starts and lands at the same height. With $v_0=10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the range? Neglect air resistance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=\frac{v_0^2\sin(2\theta)}g=\frac{10^2\cdot1}{10}\\[4pt]
&= 10\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial components are equal and positive. Ascent and descent take the same time because the heights match. In the range expression $\sin(90^\circ)=1$, and speed squared divided by $g$ gives a length.

The launch angle gives two initial components of $5\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The vertical motion takes $\sqrt2\,\mathrm s$ to return to the launch height. Its product with the horizontal component confirms the range. At the apex half this time has elapsed and the horizontal coordinate is halfway along the range.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two launches with the same speed and equal launch and landing elevations use $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. What is the ratio $R_{60}/R_{30}$? Neglect air resistance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{R_{60}}{R_{30}}&=\frac{\sin(120^\circ)}{\sin(60^\circ)}\\[4pt]
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $v_0^2/g$ is shared. The doubled angles are supplementary and have equal sines, so the ranges agree. Flight times differ because they depend on the sine of the launch angle itself, without doubling it.

You can interpret the interchange of sine and cosine through the components. The steeper launch has a larger vertical component and stays in the air longer. Its horizontal component is smaller by the same factor. This interchange preserves the product of flight time and horizontal speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball is launched from the origin with $v_{0x}=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_{0y}=8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The ground slopes upwards to the right according to $y=\dfrac x2$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. Find the flight time, the impact coordinates and the distance measured along the ground from launch to impact.

::grafico{curve="6*t,8*t-5*t^2,0,1" f="x/2" punti="0,0,pieno;6,3,pieno" segmenti="6,0,6,3" etichette="5.81,3.49,I" xmin=0 xmax=7.4 ymin=-0.2 ymax=4.1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" legenda="\text{Ground};\text{Trajectory}" titolo="I is the intersection after launch, at 6 m horizontally and 3 m vertically."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t)&=6t,\qquad y(t)=8t-5t^2,\\[4pt]
y(t)&=\frac{x(t)}2 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ 5t(1-t)=0,\\[4pt]
t_f&=1\,\mathrm s,\qquad (x_f;y_f)=(6;3)\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
d&=\sqrt{6^2+3^2}\,\mathrm m=3\sqrt5\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Contact occurs where the parabola meets the ground. At each time, compare the ball's height with the ground height at the same horizontal coordinate. One solution is the launch itself. The vertical gap $5t(1-t)$ is positive for $0<t<1$, so the first later intersection really is the impact. The equal-height range formula would give the return to the initial height after $1.6\,\mathrm s$. On the slope, the requested distance is the hypotenuse of the triangle with horizontal and vertical sides of six and three metres.

</div>
