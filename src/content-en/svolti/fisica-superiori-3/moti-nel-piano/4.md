## Exercise 1

A $45^\circ$ launch starts and lands at the same height. With $v_0=10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the range?

::grafico{f="x-0.1*x^2" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.1 punti="0,0,pieno;5,2.5,pieno;10,0,pieno" titolo="The vertex halfway along the range when launch and landing heights agree"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=\frac{v_0^2\sin(2\theta)}g=\frac{10^2\cdot1}{10}\\[4pt]
&= 10\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial components are equal and positive. Ascent and descent take the same time because the heights match. In the range expression $\sin(90^\circ)=1$, and speed squared divided by $g$ gives a length.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two launches with the same speed and equal launch and landing elevations use $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. What is the ratio $R_{60}/R_{30}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{R_{60}}{R_{30}}&=\frac{\sin(120^\circ)}{\sin(60^\circ)}\\[4pt]
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $v_0^2/g$ is shared. The doubled angles are supplementary and have equal sines, so the ranges agree. Flight times differ because they depend on the sine of the launch angle itself, without doubling it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $45^\circ$ launch must cover $20\,\mathrm m$ between points of equal elevation. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What launch speed is needed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=\sqrt{gR}=\sqrt{10\cdot20}\\[4pt]
&= 10 \sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At forty-five degrees range becomes $R=v_0^2/g$. Isolate speed squared and take the positive root because a magnitude is requested. The unit of $gR$ is $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, so its square root is a speed.

</div>
