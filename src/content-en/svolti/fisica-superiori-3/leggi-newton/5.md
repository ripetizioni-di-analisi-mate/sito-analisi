## Exercise 1

Two carts of $2$ and $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ are connected on a smooth track. A $24\,\mathrm N$ horizontal pull acts on the front cart. Find the common acceleration.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-2,0 -1,0 -1,0.8 -2,0.8;0.4,0 1.9,0 1.9,0.8 0.4,0.8" segmenti="-1,0.4,0.4,0.4;-2.4,0,3.5,0" vettori="1.9,0.4,3.1,0.4,F" etichette="-1.5,0.4,2;1.1,0.4,4" xmin=-2.5 xmax=3.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=1.3 titolo="The external pull accelerates both masses, labelled in kilograms"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{24}{2+4}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the two-cart system tension cancels because it acts between members of the chosen system. The only horizontal external force supplies twenty-four newtons to the full six kilograms. A taut connection gives both carts the same acceleration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two connected carts accelerate at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ on a smooth track under $14\,\mathrm N$. One has mass $3\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the other mass?

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_2&=\frac{14}{2}-3\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The net force divided by acceleration gives the total mass of seven kilograms. Subtract the three kilograms of the first cart. As a check, $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ on four kilograms requires eight newtons, leaving six for the three-kilogram cart.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Connected carts of total mass $10\,\mathrm{kg}$ are pulled rightwards by $35\,\mathrm N$. A total external resistance of $15\,\mathrm N$ points left. Find their acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{35-15}{10}\\[4pt]
&= 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Include both external forces with their opposite directions. Internal tensions do not change the balance for the two carts together. The resultant is twenty newtons and the accelerated inertia is the full ten kilograms.

</div>
