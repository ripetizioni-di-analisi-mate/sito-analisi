## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart experiences net force $(12,-16)\,\mathrm N$. Find its acceleration magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\sqrt{\left(\frac{12}{4}\right)^2+\left(\frac{-16}{4}\right)^2}\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,3,-4,a" segmenti="0,0,3,0;3,0,3,-4" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-5 ymax=1 uguale=1 nomi="aₓ (m/s²),aᵧ (m/s²)" titolo="Acceleration points in the same direction as the net force"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the second law independently along both axes. Division by mass yields $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to the right and $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ downwards. These directions are perpendicular, giving magnitude five. As a check, the net force has magnitude twenty newtons, equal to mass times five.

Both components are read on the same axes. The vertical component has the larger magnitude. The vector points into the fourth quadrant and is inclined more than $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. This geometric check confirms the signs in the diagram.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart is pulled with a horizontal force of $10\,\mathrm N$ and accelerates at $1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ in the direction of the pull. In a second trial the force is $22\,\mathrm N$ and the acceleration is $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ in the same direction. A force opposing motion has the same magnitude $R$ in both trials. Find the mass and the resistance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
10\,\mathrm N-R&=m\cdot1\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\qquad
22\,\mathrm N-R=m\cdot4\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\
m&=\frac{22-10}{4-1}\,\mathrm{kg}=4\,\mathrm{kg},\\
R&=(10-4\cdot1)\,\mathrm N=6\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the direction of the pull as positive, so the resistance is subtracted in both equations. The difference between trials cancels $R$. The mass therefore comes from the slope of applied force against acceleration, even when the line does not pass through the origin. Resistance is the intercept in $F=ma+R$.

In the second trial, the net force is $22-6=16\,\mathrm N$. Dividing by $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ recovers the measured acceleration. If resistance changed between trials, subtracting would no longer eliminate it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass changes velocity from $-2$ to $+4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm s$ under constant net force. Find that signed force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=m\frac{v_f-v_i}{\Delta t}=3\frac{4-(-2)}3\\[4pt]
&= 6\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity changes by six metres per second and acceleration is positive. The object initially moves in the negative direction, so the force first reduces its speed. After passing through zero, the same force increases speed in the positive direction. The resultant depends on acceleration rather than on velocity direction.

Using the two speed magnitudes would give a different change. The initial velocity is negative, so the increase is $4-(-2)=6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the first part of the interval the cart slows despite positive acceleration, then moves in the positive direction.

</div>
