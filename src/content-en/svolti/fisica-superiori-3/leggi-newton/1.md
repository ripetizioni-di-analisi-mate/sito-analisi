## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart experiences net force $(12,-16)\,\mathrm N$. Find its acceleration magnitude.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,3,-4,a" segmenti="0,0,3,0;3,0,3,-4" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-5 ymax=1 uguale=1 titolo="Acceleration points in the same direction as the net force"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\sqrt{\left(\frac{12}{4}\right)^2+\left(\frac{-16}{4}\right)^2}\\[4pt]
&= 5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the second law independently along both axes. Division by mass yields $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ to the right and $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ downwards. These directions are perpendicular, giving magnitude five. As a check, the net force has magnitude twenty newtons, equal to mass times five.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A constant resultant of $18\,\mathrm N$ produces acceleration magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=\frac Fa=\frac{18}{3}\\[4pt]
&= 6\,\mathrm{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Solve Newton's law for mass. A resultant of eighteen newtons changes velocity by three metres per second per second for a mass of six kilograms. Dividing newtons by $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ leaves kilograms. Substituting $m=6$ recovers the specified force.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass changes velocity from $-2$ to $+4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm s$ under constant net force. Find that signed force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F&=m\frac{v_f-v_i}{\Delta t}=3\frac{4-(-2)}3\\[4pt]
&= 6\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity changes by six metres per second and acceleration is positive. The object initially moves in the negative direction, so the force first reduces its speed. After passing through zero, the same force increases speed in the positive direction. The resultant depends on acceleration rather than on velocity direction.

</div>
