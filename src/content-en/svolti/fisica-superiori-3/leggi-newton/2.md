## Exercise 1

A body of mass $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ experiences $20\,\mathrm N$ to the right and $8\,\mathrm N$ to the left. What is its rightward acceleration?

::grafico{vettori="0,0,5,0,F_1;0,0,-2,0,F_2" xmin=-3 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=1 nomi="x,y" titolo="Arrow lengths 5 and 2 match the ratio 20 to 8"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{20-8}{6}\\[4pt]
&= 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two forces lie along one line but point oppositely. With right positive, their signed sum is twelve newtons. Normal force and weight, if balanced vertically, do not enter the horizontal sum. Dividing twelve by six gives two metres per second squared.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ body accelerates leftwards at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. A $12\,\mathrm N$ force pulls right. What is the magnitude of the only leftward force?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{sin}}&=F_{\mathrm{des}}-ma\\[4pt]&=12-3(-2)\\[4pt]
&= 18\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With right positive, acceleration is minus two and the net force is minus six newtons. The leftward force must balance twelve newtons to the right and supply six more to the left. Its magnitude is eighteen while its signed component is negative.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three signed horizontal forces $+12$, $-3$ and $-4\,\mathrm N$ act on a cart. What additional force gives zero net force?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\mathrm{add}}&=-(12-3-4)\\[4pt]
&= -5\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rightward contribution exceeds the two leftward contributions by five newtons. Zero net force requires adding the opposite of that signed total. If the cart is already moving, this condition leaves velocity constant.

</div>
