## Exercise 1

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart takes a bend of radius $8\,\mathrm m$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of its radial resultant?

::grafico{curve="8*cos(t),8*sin(t),0,1.5708" vettori="8,0,4,0,F_r;8,0,8,4,v" xmin=-1 xmax=10 ymin=-1 ymax=9 uguale=1 titolo="The resultant is perpendicular to velocity in this uniform motion"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_r&=\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{2\cdot4^2}{8}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Acceleration points towards the centre at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The resultant also points towards the centre and has magnitude two kilograms times that acceleration. Radial force means the radial sum of the real forces present.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart passes the bottom of a dip of radius $4\,\mathrm m$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the normal force?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=mg+\frac{mv^2}{r}=3\left(10+\frac{4^2}{4}\right)\\[4pt]
&= 42\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the bottom the curve centre is above. Weight acts down and the normal force up, so $N-mg$ must be positive. The normal force must provide twelve newtons of resultant as well as balancing the thirty of weight, giving forty-two newtons.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object crosses the crest of a hill of radius $9\,\mathrm m$. Use $g=9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At the contact limit with $N=0$, what is its speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=\sqrt{gr}=\sqrt{9\cdot9}\\[4pt]
&= 9\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the crest the centre is below. When the normal force vanishes, weight supplies the entire radial resultant, so $mg=mv^2/r$. Cancel mass and take the positive square root. At a greater speed the contact model would require a negative normal force.

</div>
