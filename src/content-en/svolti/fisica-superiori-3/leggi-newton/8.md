## Exercise 1

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart takes a bend of radius $8\,\mathrm m$ at a constant speed of $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of its radial resultant?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_r&=\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{2\cdot4^2}{8}\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="8*cos(t),8*sin(t),0,1.5708" vettori="8,0,4,0,F_r;8,0,8,4,v" xmin=-1 xmax=10 ymin=-1 ymax=9 uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="The resultant is perpendicular to velocity in this uniform motion"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Acceleration points towards the centre at $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The resultant also points towards the centre and has magnitude two kilograms times that acceleration. Radial force means the radial sum of the real forces present.

The velocity arrow is tangent to the track. The radial resultant is perpendicular to it, changing the direction of velocity while keeping its magnitude. The physical force that provides the resultant depends on the constraint, such as a rail or friction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The same cart passes the bottom of a dip and the crest of a hill at equal speed. Both have local radius of curvature $5\,\mathrm m$. The normal forces are $65$ and $35\,\mathrm N$, respectively. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the cart’s mass and speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_1&=mg+\frac{mv^2}{r},\qquad N_2=mg-\frac{mv^2}{r},\\
m&=\frac{65+35}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm{kg}=5\,\mathrm{kg},\\
\frac{mv^2}{r}&=\frac{65-35}{2}\,\mathrm N=15\,\mathrm N,\qquad
v=\sqrt{15}\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the bottom the centre of curvature is above the cart and the normal exceeds weight. At the crest the centre is below, so the normal is smaller. Speed and radius are equal. The same radial component is added to weight in the first reading and subtracted in the second.

The mean reading gives weight and half the difference gives the radial component’s magnitude. Multiply that component by the radius and divide by the mass to find the square of the speed. The crest reading is positive, so the cart is still in contact.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object crosses the crest of a hill of radius $9\,\mathrm m$. Use $g=9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At the contact limit with $N=0$, what is its speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=\sqrt{gr}=\sqrt{9\cdot9}\\[4pt]
&= 9\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the crest the centre is below. When the normal force vanishes, weight supplies the entire radial resultant, so $mg=mv^2/r$. Cancel mass and take the positive square root. At a greater speed the contact model would require a negative normal force.

The limit is independent of mass, which appears in both weight and the required radial component. At a lower speed, the road can still supply a positive normal force. Above the limit, weight alone cannot produce the required inward acceleration, and the body leaves the surface.

</div>
