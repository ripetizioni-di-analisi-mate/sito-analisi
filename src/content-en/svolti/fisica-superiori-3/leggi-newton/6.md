## Exercise 1

A $30\,\mathrm N$ pull acts on a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart that tows a $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart on a smooth surface. Find the tension.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{30}{2+3}=6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[4pt]T&=3\cdot6\\[4pt]
&= 18\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-2,0 -0.7,0 -0.7,0.8 -2,0.8;0.7,0 1.6,0 1.6,0.8 0.7,0.8" segmenti="-0.7,0.4,0.7,0.4" vettori="1.6,0.4,2.7,0.4,F;-0.7,0.4,0.1,0.4,T" etichette="-1.3,0.4,3;1.1,0.4,2" xmin=-2.4 xmax=3 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.4 titolo="The 3 kg cart receives the pull through the string"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The pull accelerates five kilograms at six metres per second squared. Tension is the only horizontal force on the rear cart, so it must accelerate three kilograms at the same rate. The front cart has $30-18=12\,\mathrm N$, consistent with two kilograms times six.

Tension points right on the rear cart. The string pulls the front cart left, and an independent check is $30-18=12\,\mathrm N$. This produces $6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ on the $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three carts of mass $2$, $3$ and $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ are arranged in that order from front to back and move rightwards. Their strings are ideal and taut. A $40\,\mathrm N$ force pulls the first to the right. Resistive forces on the carts are $2$, $3$ and $5\,\mathrm N$, respectively. Find the tensions in the two strings.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{40-2-3-5}{2+3+5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\
T_{23}&=(5\cdot3+5)\,\mathrm N=20\,\mathrm N,\\
T_{12}&=((3+5)\cdot3+3+5)\,\mathrm N=32\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find acceleration, include all three carts and their strings. The pull must overcome the sum of the resistances. Isolate the third cart for the rear tension. For the front tension, enclose the second and third carts. Their string becomes internal, so add both resistances to the force required to accelerate them.

For the first cart, the check is $40-32-2=6\,\mathrm N$, which is its mass times the acceleration. Both tensions are positive, as expected for strings that pull.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two carts of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ tow each other on a smooth track. The same pull is applied once to the $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart and once to the $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart, each time the leading one. What is $T_2/T_6$, with the subscript denoting the cart directly pulled?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{T_2}{T_6}&=\frac{6a}{2a}\\[4pt]
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Total force and total mass are unchanged, so both trials have the same common acceleration. Isolate the cart behind: when two kilograms are directly pulled, the string accelerates six kilograms. In the other trial it accelerates two. Their load ratio is three.

The index labels the mass being pulled directly. When the two-kilogram cart is pulled, the six-kilogram cart receives only tension. For the same acceleration, the tension ratio is therefore the ratio of the masses left behind.

</div>
