## Exercise 1

A $30\,\mathrm N$ pull acts on a $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart that tows a $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart on a smooth surface. Find the tension.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-2,0 -0.7,0 -0.7,0.8 -2,0.8;0.7,0 1.6,0 1.6,0.8 0.7,0.8" segmenti="-0.7,0.4,0.7,0.4" vettori="1.6,0.4,2.7,0.4,F;-0.7,0.4,0.1,0.4,T" etichette="-1.3,0.4,3;1.1,0.4,2" xmin=-2.4 xmax=3 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.4 titolo="The 3 kg cart receives the pull through the string"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{30}{2+3}=6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[4pt]T&=3\cdot6\\[4pt]
&= 18\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pull accelerates five kilograms at six metres per second squared. Tension is the only horizontal force on the rear cart, so it must accelerate three kilograms at the same rate. The front cart has $30-18=12\,\mathrm N$, consistent with two kilograms times six.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart is pulled horizontally by a string at tension $18\,\mathrm N$. Resistance is $3\,\mathrm N$. Find its acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{T-R}{m}=\frac{18-3}{5}\\[4pt]
&= 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the isolated cart tension is external. Subtract resistance before dividing by mass. The fifteen-newton net force is less than tension, with their difference exactly matching resistance.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two carts of $2$ and $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ tow each other on a smooth track. The same pull is applied once to the $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart and once to the $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart, each time the leading one. What is $T_2/T_6$, with the subscript denoting the cart directly pulled?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{T_2}{T_6}&=\frac{6a}{2a}\\[4pt]
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Total force and total mass are unchanged, so both trials have the same common acceleration. Isolate the cart behind: when two kilograms are directly pulled, the string accelerates six kilograms. In the other trial it accelerates two. Their load ratio is three.

</div>
