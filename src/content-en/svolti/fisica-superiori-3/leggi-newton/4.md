## Exercise 1

A $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ person rises in a lift that slows at acceleration magnitude $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what normal force does the scale read?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=m(g+a)=60(10-2)\\[4pt]
&= 480\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="60*(10+x)" punti="-2,480,pieno;0,600,pieno" xmin=-5 xmax=3 ymin=250 ymax=820 nomi="a (m/s²),N (N)" titolo="The reading is below weight for negative acceleration"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity points upwards but decreases, so acceleration points downwards. With an upward axis, weight is negative and the normal force positive. The required vertical resultant is minus one hundred and twenty newtons. The normal force must therefore be one hundred and twenty newtons below weight.

The graph shows the vertical difference between the reading at the stated acceleration and at zero acceleration. It is $120\,\mathrm N$, the mass times the acceleration magnitude. The floor still pushes upwards because the scale reading remains positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ person stands on a scale. The lift cabin starts accelerating downwards at $12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. What does the scale read immediately afterwards, before any contact with the ceiling? What is the person’s acceleration relative to the ground?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{hyp}}&=50(10-12)\,\mathrm N=-100\,\mathrm N,\\
N&=0\,\mathrm N,\qquad a_y=-g=-10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take the vertical axis as positive upwards. As a first test, impose the cabin’s acceleration on the person. The equation requires a downward normal force, as if the scale could hold the person’s feet down. Contact cannot supply that force. The floor accelerates down faster than the person, and contact opens.

With no normal force, weight alone acts, and the person falls at acceleration $-g$. Relative to the cabin, their acceleration is instead $(-10)-(-12)=+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. This description lasts until another contact occurs.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ person descends at constant velocity on a scale. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what does it read?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=mg=40\cdot10\\[4pt]
&= 400\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lift moves downwards, but velocity is constant and acceleration is zero. Weight and normal force balance just as at rest. Downward motion does not subtract any force from the normal force.

The reading would be the same if the cabin rose at constant velocity. The scale responds to the forces producing the person’s acceleration. Descending tells you the direction of velocity, and its constancy gives zero acceleration.

</div>
