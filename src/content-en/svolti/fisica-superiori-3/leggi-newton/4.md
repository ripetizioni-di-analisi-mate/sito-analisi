## Exercise 1

A $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ person rises in a lift that slows at acceleration magnitude $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what normal force does the scale read?

::grafico{f="60*(10+x)" punti="-2,480,pieno;0,600,pieno" xmin=-5 xmax=3 ymin=250 ymax=820 nomi="a,N" titolo="The reading is below weight for negative acceleration"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=m(g+a)=60(10-2)\\[4pt]
&= 480\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Velocity points upwards but decreases, so acceleration points downwards. With an upward axis, weight is negative and the normal force positive. The required vertical resultant is minus one hundred and twenty newtons. The normal force must therefore be one hundred and twenty newtons below weight.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A person of mass $50\,\mathrm{kg}$ reads $650\,\mathrm N$ on the scale. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the lift acceleration with upwards positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac Nm-g=\frac{650}{50}-10\\[4pt]
&= 3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight is five hundred newtons and the reading exceeds it by one hundred and fifty. That difference is the upward net force. Dividing by fifty kilograms gives acceleration; it does not show whether the lift is moving up or down at that instant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ person descends at constant velocity on a scale. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what does it read?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=mg=40\cdot10\\[4pt]
&= 400\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lift moves downwards, but velocity is constant and acceleration is zero. Weight and normal force balance just as at rest. Downward motion does not subtract any force from the normal force.

</div>
