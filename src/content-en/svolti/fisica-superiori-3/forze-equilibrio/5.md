## Exercise 1

A sled weighing $60\,\mathrm N$ moves on a smooth $30^\circ$ slope. A supplementary $18\,\mathrm N$ pull acts perpendicular to the surface and outwards. Find the two weight-component magnitudes and the normal force. Check that contact still holds.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_\parallel&=60\sin30^\circ\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N,\qquad
P_\perp=60\cos30^\circ\,\mathrm N=30\sqrt3\,\mathrm N,\\
N&=(30\sqrt3-18)\,\mathrm N\simeq34.0\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight is vertical and its components depend on the slope. The extra pull leaves those components unchanged. It enters the perpendicular balance alongside the normal force. With outwards positive, add those two forces and subtract the weight projection into the slope. The positive result confirms that the surface can still provide the reaction. The added pull makes no direct contribution along the slope.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ body is on a slope with an acute incline angle. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, the magnitude of its weight component along the slope is $40\,\mathrm N$. Find the slope angle and the magnitude of the perpendicular component.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta&=\frac{40}{50}=\frac45,\qquad
\theta=\arcsin(4/5)\simeq53.1^\circ,\\
P_\perp&=\sqrt{50^2-40^2}\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass gives the weight magnitude, which is the hypotenuse of the component triangle. The ratio of the parallel component to weight gives the sine. The acute-angle condition selects the relevant solution. Find the perpendicular component directly from Pythagoras, without rounding the angle first. Identifying those thirty newtons with the normal force also requires no other perpendicular force and continued contact.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On a smooth slope with $\sin\theta=0.60$ and $\cos\theta=0.80$, a package weighs $45\,\mathrm N$. An actuator applies another $15\,\mathrm N$ vertically downwards. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the normal force and the acceleration along the slope.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=(45+15)\cdot0.80\,\mathrm N=48\,\mathrm N,\\
\sum F_x&=(45+15)\cdot0.60\,\mathrm N=36\,\mathrm N,\\
a_x&=\frac{36}{45/10}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight and the push point in the same direction, so add them before projecting onto the axes of the slope. The normal force balances the forty-eight perpendicular newtons. With no friction, thirty-six newtons remain downhill. Use gravitational weight alone to find the mass. The actuator changes the net force without adding matter to the package. Using its push in the mass calculation would underestimate the acceleration.

</div>
