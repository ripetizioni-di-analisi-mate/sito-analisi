## Exercise 1

For a $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ block on a slope with $\sin\theta=0.6$, $\cos\theta=0.8$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the downslope component of weight.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 segmenti="0,0,4,0;4,0,4,3;0,0,4,3" vettori="2.4,1.8,2.4,-0.7,P;2.4,1.8,1.2,0.9,P_p;2.4,1.8,3.6,0.2,P_n" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3.4 titolo="The parallel projection points down the slope"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_\parallel&=mg\sin\theta=5\cdot10\cdot0.6\\[4pt]
&= 30\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight is fifty newtons and points vertically. In its projection triangle, the downslope component is opposite the angle between the vertical and the inward normal. Use the sine to obtain thirty newtons. The cosine would give forty newtons along the normal to the plane.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On a $60^\circ$ slope the body weighs $80\,\mathrm N$ and no other normal forces act. Find the normal force while contact lasts.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=P\cos(60^\circ)=80\cdot\frac12\\[4pt]
&= 40\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body has no acceleration perpendicular to the plane. The normal force balances only the component of weight pushing into the surface. The cosine of sixty degrees is one half, so the normal force equals half the weight rather than all of it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body on a plane with $\sin\theta=0.8$ has a parallel weight component of $64\,\mathrm N$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find its mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=\frac{P_\parallel}{g\sin\theta}=\frac{64}{10\cdot0.8}\\[4pt]
&= 8\,\mathrm{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given component is only eighty percent of weight, so full weight is eighty newtons. Divide by ten newtons per kilogram. Checking forwards, eight kilograms gives eighty newtons of weight and sixty-four down the slope.

</div>
