## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ block moves downhill at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ on a slope with $\sin\theta=0.60$ and $\cos\theta=0.80$. A string pulls uphill with $22\,\mathrm N$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $\mu_d=0.25$ and $\mu_s=0.40$. Find the acceleration and stopping time, and check whether the block stays at rest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_x&=\frac{24-0.25\cdot32-22}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\
t&=\frac{3}{1.5}\,\mathrm s=2\,\mathrm s,\qquad
f_s=(24-22)\,\mathrm N=2\,\mathrm N<0.40\cdot32\,\mathrm N=12.8\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2.2,-2.15 2.25,1.1875" vettori="0,0,0,-2,P;0,0,-.96,1.28,N;0,0,1.2,.9,T+f_d" punti="0,0,pieno" xmin=-2.57 xmax=2.65 ymin=-2.43 ymax=1.79 titolo="Tension and kinetic friction both point uphill during the descent."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Take downhill as positive. While the block slides, friction and tension both act uphill. The string is parallel to the plane, so the normal force is weight times the cosine, or thirty-two newtons. The resultant points uphill and slows the block. Kinetic friction ends when it stops. The weight component then exceeds tension by only two newtons, and the contact can provide that amount of static friction uphill to keep the block still.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A runner enters a downhill slope at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The slope has $\sin\theta=0.60$ and $\cos\theta=0.80$. It travels $1.50\,\mathrm m$ with $\mu_d=0.25$ and then $1.25\,\mathrm m$ with $\mu_d=0.50$. Using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find its speeds at the boundary and at the end.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1&=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\qquad a_2=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\
v_1^2&=(2^2+2\cdot4\cdot1.50)\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}=16\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2},\qquad v_1=4\,\mathrm{m/s},\\
v_2&=\sqrt{4^2+2\cdot2\cdot1.25}\,\mathrm{m/s}=\sqrt{21}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq4.58\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope and hence the normal force are the same in the two sections. The friction coefficient changes, so a single acceleration cannot describe the whole ramp. Acceleration remains positive downhill even on the rougher section. Velocity does not jump at the boundary because the change in force supplies no instantaneous impulse. Use the exit speed of the first section as the entry speed of the second.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On a slope with $\sin\theta=0.60$ and $\cos\theta=0.80$, a sliding block accelerates downhill at $1.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and assume only weight, the normal force and friction act. Find $\mu_d$ and the downhill acceleration component if the block is then launched uphill.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_d&=\frac{0.60-1.6/10}{0.80}=0.55,\\
a_{x,2}&=10(0.60+0.55\cdot0.80)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=10.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first section, friction subtracts part of the weight component pointing downhill. Solving that equation gives the dimensionless coefficient. In the uphill trial the axis still points downhill, but friction reverses to oppose the sliding. It adds to the weight component. Acceleration can exceed the magnitude of gravity because it comes from two forces, and during ascent it reduces speed.

</div>
