## Exercise 1

A body slides down a slope with $\sin\theta=3/5$, $\cos\theta=4/5$ and $\mu_d=0.25$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find acceleration signed downslope.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 segmenti="0,0,4,0;4,0,4,3;0,0,4,3" punti="4,3,pieno" xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.7 titolo="The slope triangle has legs 4 and 3 and hypotenuse 5"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=10\left(\frac35-0.25\cdot\frac45\right)\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight contributes six metres per second squared down the slope. Opposing friction depends on the normal force through the cosine and reduces acceleration by two. The positive result means downslope speed grows. Mass cancels from the slope balance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On a slope with $\sin\theta=3/5$ and $\cos\theta=4/5$, a body slides down at constant velocity. Find $\mu_d$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
 \mu_d&=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\frac{3/5}{4/5}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 0.75
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Constant velocity implies zero parallel resultant. The downslope weight component equals the coefficient times normal force. Cancelling $mg$ gives sine divided by cosine, which is the dimensionless slope tangent.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An object moves uphill on a plane with $\sin\theta=3/5$, $\cos\theta=4/5$, $\mu_d=0.25$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find acceleration signed positive downhill.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=10\left(\frac35+0.25\cdot\frac45\right)\\[4pt]
&= 8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight still has a downslope component. Since the body is sliding uphill, kinetic friction also points down the slope. The two parts add instead of subtracting. Positive acceleration opposes the current velocity, so the body slows down.

</div>
