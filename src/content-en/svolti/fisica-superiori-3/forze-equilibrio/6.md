## Exercise 1

A junction at rest supports a $50\,\mathrm N$ weight and receives a $10\,\mathrm N$ horizontal force to the right. Two strings pull left and right, both at $45^\circ$ above horizontal. Find their tensions and identify which string pulls harder.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=T_L/\sqrt2,\qquad B=T_R/\sqrt2,\\
A+B&=50\,\mathrm N,\qquad A-B=10\,\mathrm N,\\
T_L&=30\sqrt2\,\mathrm N,\qquad T_R=20\sqrt2\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each string has two components of equal magnitude because its angle is forty-five degrees. Vertically the components add to support the weight. Horizontally the left string must balance both the right string and the extra rightward force. The two balances give thirty and twenty newtons as component magnitudes. To recover the full tensions, divide by the sine or cosine.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two strings support a vertical load. The left string makes $30^\circ$ with horizontal and can withstand $48\,\mathrm N$; the right one makes $60^\circ$ and can withstand $60\,\mathrm N$. Find the maximum load weight for equilibrium and identify the limiting string.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_L&=P/2,\qquad T_R=\sqrt3 P/2,\\
P&\leq96\,\mathrm N,\qquad P\leq\frac{120}{\sqrt3}\,\mathrm N,\\
P_{\max}&=40\sqrt3\,\mathrm N\simeq69.3\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Horizontal balance makes the tensions proportional. Substituting that relation in the vertical balance gives both tensions in terms of the weight. Each tensile limit now produces a separate inequality. The right string can carry more newtons, but its tension rises faster as weight increases. Its limit is reached first. At the maximum load, the left string carries about thirty-five newtons, still below its own limit.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A junction is attached to two strings rising to opposite sides at $45^\circ$. It supports a $40\,\mathrm N$ weight and is pulled horizontally rightwards by $50\,\mathrm N$. Decide whether it can remain at rest with both strings taut.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=T_L/\sqrt2,\qquad B=T_R/\sqrt2,\\
A+B&=40\,\mathrm N,\qquad A-B=50\,\mathrm N,\\
A&=45\,\mathrm N,\qquad B=-5\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With both strings taut, their vertical components should be positive. The junction equations instead give a negative component for the right string. Holding that equilibrium configuration would require the string to push the junction left and down. A string can only pull towards its anchor. If the right string is removed at the same geometry, the left one has equal components and cannot balance fifty horizontal newtons and forty vertical newtons at once.

</div>
