## Exercise 1

Two strings at equal inclination make $30^\circ$ above horizontal and support $50\,\mathrm N$. Find the tension in each.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,0,-4.33,2.5,T;0,0,4.33,2.5,T;0,0,0,-5,P" xmin=-5.1 xmax=5.1 ymin=-5.5 ymax=3.6 titolo="The two vertical components support a weight equal to one tension magnitude"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=\frac{50}{2\sin(30^\circ)}\\[4pt]
&= 50\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal projections have equal magnitudes and opposite directions. Each string provides a vertical component equal to half its own magnitude, since the sine of thirty degrees is one half. Together they provide one full tension magnitude, which must support fifty newtons.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two symmetric strings inclined $60^\circ$ above horizontal have $40\,\mathrm N$ of tension each. What weight do they support?

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=2T\sin(60^\circ)=2\cdot40\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\[4pt]
&= 40 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Horizontal forces cancel in pairs. Add the vertical projections of the tensions rather than their full magnitudes. Supported weight is less than eighty newtons because each string is oblique.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two strings support a $60\,\mathrm N$ weight, at angles above horizontal of $30^\circ$ on the left and $60^\circ$ on the right. Find left tension.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_R&=T_L\frac{\cos 30^\circ}{\cos60^\circ}=\sqrt3\,T_L\\[4pt]60&=\tfrac12T_L+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}T_R=2T_L\\[4pt]T_L&=\frac{60}{2}\\[4pt]
&= 30\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Horizontal balance gives the right tension in terms of the left because their components must cancel. Substitution into the vertical balance leaves one unknown. The left carries thirty newtons and the right $30\sqrt3\,\mathrm N$. Their vertical contributions are fifteen and forty-five newtons and add to the weight.

</div>
