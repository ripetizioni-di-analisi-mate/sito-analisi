## Exercise 1

The end of a $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring is compressed by $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Take extension as positive. What is the spring force component?

::grafico{f="-200*x" punti="-0.03,6,pieno" xmin=-0.045 xmax=0.045 ymin=-10 ymax=10 nomi="x,F_e" titolo="The restoring force is positive in compression"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_e&=-kx=-200(-0.03)\\[4pt]
&= 6\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Displacement from natural length is negative because the spring is compressed. Convert three centimetres to three hundredths of a metre before multiplying. Hooke's minus sign reverses the sign of displacement, so force points towards extension.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal spring at $x=2\,\mathrm{cm}$ requires $5\,\mathrm N$ to remain extended. Find its spring constant.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac F{x}=\frac5{0.02}\\[4pt]
&= 250\,\mathrm{N/m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The applied force balances the restoring force at equal magnitude. Dividing five newtons by $0.02\,\mathrm m$ gives the slope in newtons per metre. It predicts another five newtons for each additional two centimetres within the elastic range.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring is held in equilibrium first by $6$ and then by $15\,\mathrm N$. By how much does the extension increase?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=\frac{\Delta F}{k}=\frac{15-6}{300}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 0.03\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both extensions are measured from the same natural state. Apply Hooke's law in both equilibria and subtract, leaving a nine-newton increase in the numerator. The first extension is $0.02\,\mathrm m$ and the second $0.05\,\mathrm m$, a difference of three centimetres.

</div>
