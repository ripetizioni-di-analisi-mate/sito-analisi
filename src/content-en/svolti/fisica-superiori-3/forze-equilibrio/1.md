## Exercise 1

A spring of stiffness $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ has natural length $24\,\mathrm{cm}$. In trial A it is $21\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, in trial B $26.5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Take extension as positive and find the signed spring force in each trial. Which magnitude is greater?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_A&=-0.030\,\mathrm m,\qquad x_B=+0.025\,\mathrm m,\\
F_A&=-200(-0.030)\,\mathrm N=+6\,\mathrm N,\qquad
F_B=-200(0.025)\,\mathrm N=-5\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-2*x" punti="-3,6,pieno,A;2.5,-5,pieno,B" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-8.5 ymax=8.5 nomi="x (cm),Fₑ (N)" titolo="Point A represents compression. B represents extension. The slope is −2 N/cm."}

<div class="spiegazione">

First subtract the natural length from each reading and convert to metres because stiffness is in newtons per metre. The sign gives the direction opposite the deformation: in the first trial the spring pushes towards extension, and in the second it pulls towards shortening. The points lie on opposite sides of the origin. The compression has the larger magnitude, so its spring force does too.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Holding a spring extended to $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ takes a pull of $4\,\mathrm N$. Holding it extended to $22\,\mathrm{cm}$ takes a pull of $12\,\mathrm N$. In the ideal model, find its stiffness, its natural length and the force required at $26\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac{12-4}{0.22-0.18}\,\mathrm{N/m}=200\,\mathrm{N/m},\\
\ell_0&=0.18\,\mathrm m-\frac{4\,\mathrm N}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}}=0.16\,\mathrm m,\\
F&=200(0.26-0.16)\,\mathrm N=20\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both forces are the applied forces holding the spring at rest. Their difference is proportional to the difference in length because the natural length cancels between the two equations. Once the slope is known, extrapolate to the zero-force intercept. The prediction at twenty-six centimetres adds the same increase in force as between the first two readings. That check assumes the spring remains in its linear range.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two springs of stiffness $200$ and $300\,\mathrm{N/m}$ are connected in series on a smooth track by a negligible-mass joint. The free end is pulled with $12\,\mathrm N$ and the system settles. Find the extensions and the stiffness of a single spring giving the same total extension.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1&=12/200\,\mathrm m=0.060\,\mathrm m,\qquad x_2=12/300\,\mathrm m=0.040\,\mathrm m,\\
x&=0.100\,\mathrm m,\qquad k_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{12\,\mathrm N}{0.100\,\mathrm m}=120\,\mathrm{N/m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The joint is at rest, so the springs pull it in opposite directions with equal magnitudes. Each spring transmits the full applied force. The displacement of the free end includes both extensions because the springs are in series. The equivalent stiffness is lower than either individual stiffness: at a fixed force the assembly stretches more than either spring alone. Adding the stiffnesses would predict the opposite.

</div>
