## Exercise 1

On a light lever, $40\,\mathrm N$ act downwards $0.30\,\mathrm m$ left of the pivot. What downward force $0.60\,\mathrm m$ to the right gives rotational equilibrium?

::grafico{assi=0 segmenti="-0.30,0,0.60,0" vettori="-0.30,0,-0.30,-0.30,F_L;0.60,0,0.60,-0.15,F_R" punti="0,0,pieno,O" xmin=-0.41 xmax=0.74 ymin=-0.37 ymax=0.15 titolo="The right-hand force has twice the moment arm"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_R&=\frac{40\cdot0.30}{0.60}\\[4pt]
&= 20\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both loads point down but act on opposite sides of the pivot, so their torques have opposite signs. The first gives twelve newton metres anticlockwise. A twice-longer arm balances it with half the force. The pivot's vertical reaction then supports the sum of both loads.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A light horizontal lever carries $30\,\mathrm N$ at $0.20\,\mathrm m$ left of a pivot and $15\,\mathrm N$ at $0.40\,\mathrm m$ right of it. Find the vertical reaction at the pivot.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_O&=30+15\\[4pt]
&= 45\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First check that the torques about the pivot both have magnitude six newton metres and opposite signs. Then require zero vertical resultant. Both loads point down, so their magnitudes add and the reaction balances them upwards. The pivot has zero moment arm about itself but still exerts a translational force.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A board weighs $20\,\mathrm N$, with its centre of mass $0.10\,\mathrm m$ right of a pivot. It also carries $30\,\mathrm N$ at $0.20\,\mathrm m$ on that side. What downward force $0.40\,\mathrm m$ to the left balances the torques?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_L&=\frac{20\cdot0.10+30\cdot0.20}{0.40}\\[4pt]
&= 20\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The board's weight acts at its centre of mass and creates two newton metres clockwise. The right load adds six more. The force on the left must create eight newton metres in the other sense, so divide by an arm of four tenths. Omitting the board's weight would leave an imbalance of two newton metres.

</div>
