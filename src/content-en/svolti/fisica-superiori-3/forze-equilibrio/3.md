## Exercise 1

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ body slides on a horizontal plane with $\mu_d=0.20$. A rope also pulls upward with component $10\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the friction magnitude.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-0.6,0 0.6,0 0.6,0.7 -0.6,0.7" segmenti="-2,0,2,0" vettori="0,0.3,0,-1.2,P;0,0.3,0,1.5,N;0,0.3,0,0.65,T_y;0,0.3,-0.6,0.3,f" xmin=-2.2 xmax=2.2 ymin=-1.5 ymax=2 titolo="Weight, normal force and vertical tension enter the normal balance"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=5\cdot10-10=40\,\mathrm N\\[4pt]f_d&=0.20\cdot40\\[4pt]
&= 8\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body has no normal acceleration. The rope supports ten of the fifty newtons of weight, leaving forty newtons of normal force. Friction depends on this normal force rather than directly on weight. Its direction follows from the sliding relative to the surface.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sliding box has normal force $30\,\mathrm N$ and kinetic friction $9\,\mathrm N$. What is $\mu_d$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_d&=\frac{f_d}{N}=\frac9{30}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 0.3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The kinetic model makes friction proportional to the normal force. Divide the two forces, both in newtons, to find the constant of proportionality. The coefficient is dimensionless. Multiplying it by thirty recovers nine newtons.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ crate slides rightwards. The normal force is $40\,\mathrm N$, kinetic coefficient is $0.25$ and a horizontal pull is $6\,\mathrm N$ rightwards. Find acceleration with right positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{6-0.25\cdot40}{4}\\[4pt]
&= -1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Kinetic friction points left because the crate slides right on the surface. Its ten-newton magnitude exceeds the pull. Signed net force is minus four newtons. Dividing by four gives leftward acceleration, so the crate slows while it continues sliding right.

</div>
