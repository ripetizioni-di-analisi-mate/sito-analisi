## Exercise 1

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ crate is at rest on a floor. A $20\,\mathrm N$ pull acts upwards and to the right with $\cos\alpha=0.80$ and $\sin\alpha=0.60$. Given $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $\mu_s=0.40$ and $\mu_d=0.25$, determine whether it slides and, if so, find its acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=(50-20\cdot0.60)\,\mathrm N=38\,\mathrm N,\\
F_x&=16\,\mathrm N,\qquad f_{s,\max}=0.40\cdot38\,\mathrm N=15.2\,\mathrm N,\\
f_d&=0.25\cdot38\,\mathrm N=9.5\,\mathrm N,\qquad a_x=\frac{16-9.5}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1.3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

During sliding, friction points left."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Vertically the crate remains in contact, so the normal force and the vertical part of the pull balance weight. Keeping it still along the floor would require sixteen newtons of friction, above the static limit. It begins moving right. Only then use the kinetic coefficient, multiplying it by the normal force already found. Using weight directly in the friction formula would incorrectly predict that rest is possible because it gives a larger static limit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A crate weighing $40\,\mathrm N$ is at rest on a floor with $\mu_s=0.50$. A push $F$ acts rightwards and downwards, with component magnitudes $4F/5$ and $3F/5$. Find the largest push compatible with rest and the normal force at that value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=40\,\mathrm N+\frac35 F,\qquad
\frac45F\leq\frac12\left(40\,\mathrm N+\frac35F\right),\\
\frac12F&\leq20\,\mathrm N,\qquad F_{\max}=40\,\mathrm N,\qquad N(F_{\max})=64\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical component presses the crate against the floor. Increasing the push also raises the maximum static friction, so one normal force cannot be used for all push values. Compare the required horizontal component with the limit at the same push. At the threshold the required thirty-two newtons equals half the normal force. The crate can remain at rest up to that value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ parcel initially at rest relative to the floor is set onto a belt moving steadily rightwards at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With $\mu_d=0.20$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the time until it stops slipping relative to the belt and the distance travelled relative to the floor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_d&=0.20\cdot4\cdot10\,\mathrm N=8\,\mathrm N,\qquad a_x=8/4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\\
t&=3/2\,\mathrm s=1.5\,\mathrm s,\qquad \Delta x=\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot(1.5)^2\,\mathrm m=2.25\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initially the parcel slides left relative to the belt even though it is at rest relative to the floor. Friction therefore points right and speeds up the parcel in that direction. Sliding ends when the parcel’s ground velocity reaches the belt velocity. From then on there is no need to accelerate the parcel because the belt moves steadily, so the static friction required is zero.

</div>
