## Exercise 1

Relative to pivot $O$, a force with components $(6;-8)\,\mathrm N$ acts at point $(0.30;0.40)\,\mathrm m$. Take anticlockwise as positive. Find the signed torque and the distance from $O$ to the line of action.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_O&=xF_y-yF_x=(0.30(-8)-0.40\cdot6)\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}=-4.8\,\mathrm{N\cdot m},\\
|\vec F|&=\sqrt{6^2+(-8)^2}\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N,\qquad b=4.8/10\,\mathrm m=0.48\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 linee="0,0 .3,.4" segmenti="0,0,.384,.288;.3,0,.3,.4" vettori=".3,.4,.63,-.04,F" punti="0,0,pieno;.3,.4,pieno,A;.384,.288,pieno,H" nomi="x (m),y (m)" xmin=-.13 xmax=.76 ymin=-.18 ymax=.54 titolo="The lever arm OH is perpendicular to the line of action of the force at A."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical component gives a clockwise effect because it points down to the right of the pivot. The horizontal component also acts clockwise because it points right above the pivot. Thus both torques are negative. To find the lever arm, divide the torque magnitude by the full force. It is smaller than the half-metre distance to the application point because the lever arm is the shortest distance to the line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $10\,\mathrm N$ downward force acts along the line $x=0.45\,\mathrm m$. Find the signed torque about $O$ at $x=0$ and about $B$ at $x=0.20\,\mathrm m$, with anticlockwise positive. What changes if the application point is moved upwards along the same line?

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_O&=-10\cdot0.45\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}=-4.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m},\\
M_B&=-10(0.45-0.20)\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}=-2.5\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perpendicular distance to a vertical line is horizontal. The second pivot is closer to the line of action, so the torque magnitude decreases. The force still acts downwards to the right in both cases, so the sign stays clockwise. Moving only the application point along the line leaves the horizontal distances unchanged. Both torques therefore keep their calculated values.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $12\,\mathrm N$ force points upwards $0.20\,\mathrm m$ to the left of the pivot. A second $12\,\mathrm N$ force points downwards $0.30\,\mathrm m$ to the right. What vertical force should be added $0.50\,\mathrm m$ to the right to cancel the torque, and what resultant remains?

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{12}&=-12(0.20+0.30)\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}=-6\,\mathrm{N\cdot m},\\
F_y&=\frac{6\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}}{0.50\,\mathrm m}=+12\,\mathrm N,\qquad
\sum F_y=+12\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two forces form a couple: their resultant is zero but both torques are clockwise. The third force must act anticlockwise, so on the right it needs to point upwards. Cancelling the torque leaves a new resultant. Force balance would also require, for example, an opposite force whose line of action passes through the pivot and therefore leaves the torque balance unchanged.

</div>
