## Exercise 1

A $20\,\mathrm N$ force is perpendicular to a $0.30\,\mathrm m$ arm. What is the torque magnitude?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 segmenti="0,0,0.30,0" vettori="0.30,0,0.30,0.20,F" punti="0,0,pieno,O" xmin=-0.06 xmax=0.37 ymin=-0.05 ymax=0.26 titolo="The force acts 0.30 m from the pivot"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=bF=0.30\cdot20\\[4pt]
&= 6\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Perpendicularity makes distance along the rod equal to the shortest distance between pivot and line of action. Thirty centimetres is $0.30$ metres. Multiplying by twenty newtons gives six newton metres. The sign would depend on rotational sense, but the magnitude is requested.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $20\,\mathrm N$ force acts $0.40\,\mathrm m$ from a pivot at $30^\circ$ to the position from the pivot. What is the torque magnitude?

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=rF\sin\theta=0.40\cdot20\cdot\frac12\\[4pt]
&= 4\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Radial distance differs from moment arm when force is oblique. The arm is the component of radius perpendicular to the line of action, here $0.20\,\mathrm m$. Equivalently multiply the full radial distance by the ten-newton transverse component. Both methods give the same torque.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A perpendicular force of $40\,\mathrm N$ produces torque $12\,\mathrm{N\cdot m}$. How far is its point of application from the pivot?

$$
\begin{aligned}
b&=\frac{M}{F}=\frac{12}{40}\\[4pt]
&\simeq 0.3\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a perpendicular force, moving the application point along the lever directly increases the moment arm. Find it from torque divided by force. Newton metres divided by newtons leave metres, and thirty centimetres produces the required torque.

</div>
