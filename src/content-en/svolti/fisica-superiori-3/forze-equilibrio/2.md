## Exercise 1

A $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass rests suspended from a vertical $200\,\mathrm{N/m}$ spring. An auxiliary support exerts $6\,\mathrm N$ upwards on the mass. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the extension and the auxiliary force that would reduce it to zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=0.80\cdot10\,\mathrm N=8\,\mathrm N,\qquad F_e=(8-6)\,\mathrm N=2\,\mathrm N,\\
x&=\frac{2\,\mathrm N}{200\,\mathrm{N/m}}=0.010\,\mathrm m,\qquad
F_{\mathrm{aux},0}=8\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the mass diagram, weight points downwards. For rest, the support and spring together must supply an equally large upward force. Extension corresponds to the two newtons still supplied by the spring. At the natural length the spring force vanishes and the support carries the entire weight. A larger auxiliary force would require the spring to act downwards, possible only if the connection can transmit compression.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a lift, a $0.90\,\mathrm{kg}$ mass is at rest relative to the cabin, suspended from a spring of stiffness $150\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and natural length $0.200\,\mathrm m$. The spring is $0.272\,\mathrm m$ long. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the magnitude and direction of the lift’s acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=(0.272-0.200)\,\mathrm m=0.072\,\mathrm m,\qquad
F_e=150\cdot0.072\,\mathrm N=10.8\,\mathrm N,\\
a_y&=\frac{10.8-0.90\cdot10}{0.90}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=+2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass and cabin have the same acceleration relative to the ground. In that frame compare the upward spring force and downward weight. The first exceeds the second, so acceleration is upwards. If the cabin is descending, this acceleration reduces its speed. The length reading and force balance alone do not give the direction of the cabin’s velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two vertical springs of equal natural length, with stiffnesses $200$ and $400\,\mathrm{N/m}$, connect two bars guided to remain parallel. The lower bar and its load weigh $30\,\mathrm N$ and are at rest. The smooth guide supplies no vertical force. Find the shared extension and the force from each spring.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=\frac{30\,\mathrm N}{(200+400)\,\mathrm{N/m}}=0.050\,\mathrm m,\\
F_1&=200\cdot0.050\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N,\qquad F_2=400\cdot0.050\,\mathrm N=20\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the parallel connection both lower ends move down by the same distance because the guide keeps the bars parallel. Equal natural lengths therefore give equal extensions. The vertical spring forces add. The stiffer spring supplies twice the force for the same extension. Check by adding ten and twenty newtons. Either spring alone would need a larger extension to carry the same load.

</div>
