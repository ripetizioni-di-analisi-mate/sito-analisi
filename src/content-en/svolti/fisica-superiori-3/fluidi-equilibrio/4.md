## Exercise 1

Two ideal pistons at the same height have a large-to-small area ratio of five. An $80\,\mathrm N$ force is applied at the small one. Find the balanced force at the large one.

$$
F_2=F_1\frac{A_2}{A_1},\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_2&=80 \cdot 5\\[4pt]&=400\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same added pressure is transmitted. Five times the area gives five times the force.

At equal heights the ideal liquid transmits the same pressure increase to both pistons. Force on each is that pressure times its own area. Five times the piston area gives five times the force. For the same volume transfer its travel is one fifth as large, consistent with conservation of work.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal hydraulic press supports $1800\,\mathrm N$ on its larger piston of area $90\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. How much force is needed on the small piston of $15\,\mathrm{cm^2}$?

$$
F_1=F_2\frac{A_1}{A_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_1&=1800 \cdot 15 \cdot \frac{1}{90}\\[4pt]&=300\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the smaller-to-larger area ratio. Square centimetres cancel between numerator and denominator.

Check the ratio with both areas in square centimetres. The large piston has $1800/90=20\,\mathrm{N/cm^2}$, so the small one needs $20\cdot15=300\,\mathrm N$. Converting only one area would leave different units in the ratio and change its numerical value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An ideal press has piston areas $0.010$ and $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The small piston drops $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the other piston travel in centimetres.

::grafico{assi=0 linee="0,1.6 0,0 4,0 4,1.6;.6,1.6 .6,.45 1.4,.45 1.4,1.6;0,1.2 .6,1.2;1.4,1.2 4,1.2" vettori=".3,1.85,.3,1.35,s_1;2.6,1.4,2.6,1.525,s_2" xmin=-.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-.3 ymax=2.1 titolo="The pistons move in opposite directions with a four-to-one travel ratio"}

$$
s_2=s_1\frac{A_1}{A_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}s_2&=12 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{100} \cdot \frac{1}{4 \cdot \frac{1}{100}}\\[4pt]&=3\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Displaced volume equals area times travel and is conserved. Quadruple area gives a quarter of the travel.

The small piston displaces $0.010\cdot0.12=0.0012\,\mathrm{m^3}$. If the liquid is incompressible and stays in the press, the large piston receives that volume. Division by $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $0.03\,\mathrm m$, a rise of three centimetres. The larger piston's travel is inversely proportional to area.

</div>
