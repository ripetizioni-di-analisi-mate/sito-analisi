## Exercise 1

Two ideal pistons at the same height have a large-to-small area ratio of five. An $80\,\mathrm N$ force is applied at the small one. Find the balanced force at the large one.

$$
F_2=F_1\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}=80\cdot5\,\mathrm{N}=400\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The same added pressure is transmitted. Five times the area gives five times the force.

At equal heights the ideal liquid transmits the same pressure increase to both pistons. Force on each is that pressure times its own area. Five times the piston area gives five times the force. For the same volume transfer its travel is one fifth as large, consistent with conservation of work.

The relation $F_1/A_1=F_2/A_2$ compares the load per unit area above the common outside pressure. At unequal heights the weight of the liquid column between the pistons would also enter the balance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal hydraulic press contains oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. The large piston of area $90\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ supports a $1800\,\mathrm N$ load and has an outside pressure of $108\,\mathrm{kPa}$. The smaller piston has area $15\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, is $0.50\,\mathrm m$ lower and is exposed to $98\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Neglecting piston weights, find the applied downward force on the smaller piston using $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\rm int,2}&=p_{\rm ext,2}+\dfrac{F_2}{A_2}
=108\,\mathrm{kPa}+\dfrac{1800}{0.0090}\,\mathrm{Pa}=308\,\mathrm{kPa}\\[4pt]
p_{\rm int,1}&=p_{\rm int,2}+\rho g\Delta z
=308\,\mathrm{kPa}+4\,\mathrm{kPa}=312\,\mathrm{kPa}\\[4pt]
F_1&=(p_{\rm int,1}-p_{\rm ext,1})A_1
=(312000-98000)\cdot0.0015\,\mathrm{N}=321\,\mathrm{N}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load and external gas pressure on the larger piston both act towards the oil. The liquid must support them together, so the load adds $200\,\mathrm{kPa}$ to the outside pressure. Moving down from that piston to the smaller one raises internal pressure by another $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Only then subtract the outside pressure acting on the smaller piston. Convert the area to square metres because the pressure is in pascals. With equal heights and outside pressures the force would be $300\,\mathrm N$. Here both the descent through the oil and the lower outside pressure demand a larger applied force.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An ideal press has piston areas $0.010$ and $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The small piston drops $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the other piston travel in centimetres.

::grafico{assi=0 linee="0,1.6 0,0 4,0 4,1.6;.6,1.6 .6,.45 1.4,.45 1.4,1.6;0,1.2 .6,1.2;1.4,1.2 4,1.2" vettori=".3,1.85,.3,1.35,s₁;2.6,1.4,2.6,1.525,s₂" xmin=-.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-.3 ymax=2.1 titolo="The pistons move in opposite directions with a four-to-one travel ratio"}

$$
s_2=s_1\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}=12\cdot\dfrac{0.010}{0.040}\,\mathrm{cm}=3\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Displaced volume equals area times travel and is conserved. Quadruple area gives a quarter of the travel.

The small piston displaces $0.010\cdot0.12=0.0012\,\mathrm{m^3}$. If the liquid is incompressible and stays in the press, the large piston receives that volume. Division by $0.040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ gives $0.03\,\mathrm m$, a rise of three centimetres. The larger piston's travel is inversely proportional to area.

The travel can be calculated directly in centimetres because the area ratio is dimensionless. If the transferred volume is needed, convert both travel distances to metres.

</div>
