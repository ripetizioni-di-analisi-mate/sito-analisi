## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ body with fully submerged volume $0.001\,\mathrm{m^3}$ hangs in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find the tension.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-.7,-.65 .7,-.65 .7,.65 -.7,.65" linee="-2,1 2,1;0,.65 0,2.45" vettori="-.35,0,-.35,.5,F_A;0,0,0,-2,P;.33,0,.33,1.5,T" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2.5 ymax=2.8 titolo="Buoyancy and tension act against weight"}

$$
T=mg-\rho_f gV=(4-1000\cdot0.001)\cdot10\,\mathrm{N}=30\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ten-newton buoyancy balances part of the forty-newton weight. Tension supplies the rest.

Weight is forty newtons. The displaced litre of water has mass one kilogram, so buoyancy is ten newtons. The string supplies the missing thirty newtons. Vertical net force is $30+10-40=0$, consistent with a stationary body and positive tension from above.

Changing the depth leaves this value unchanged if the body remains fully immersed and the liquid is uniform. As long as displaced volume and weight stay fixed, the line must still supply $30\,\mathrm{N}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A body weighs $32\,\mathrm N$ in air, neglecting air buoyancy, and its scale reads $20\,\mathrm N$ in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find submerged volume.

$$
V=\dfrac{P-T}{\rho_f g}=\dfrac{32-20}{1000\cdot10}\,\mathrm{m^3}=0.0012\,\mathrm{m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference of the readings is the increase in buoyancy. Divide it by water weight per unit volume to find volume.

The reading decreases by twelve newtons because the liquid supports part of the weight. Twelve newtons of buoyancy corresponds to the weight of $1.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At ordinary density that mass fills $1.2$ litres, or $0.0012\,\mathrm{m^3}$.

The result is the wetted volume that excludes water. If the sample is only partly submerged, finding its mean density also requires a measurement of its total outside volume.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $0.6\,\mathrm{kg}$ body of volume $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is held fully submerged in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ by a line attached to the bottom. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find tension.

$$
T=\rho_f gV-mg=(1000\cdot0.001-0.60)\cdot10\,\mathrm{N}=4\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Ten newtons of buoyancy outweigh six newtons of weight. Here the line pulls down, supplying the difference in the opposite direction to a line from above.

One displaced litre of water gives ten newtons of buoyancy while the body weighs six newtons. Four newtons upwards remain to be balanced. The string is anchored below and pulls down, so its tension joins weight in $10-6-T=0$. An upper anchor would be incompatible with this equilibrium.

If the line snapped, Newton’s second law would apply to the initial $4\,\mathrm{N}$ upward resultant. The body would start to rise and could later break the surface.

</div>
