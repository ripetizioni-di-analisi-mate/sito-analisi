## Exercise 1

A block of density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ floats in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. What percentage of its volume is submerged?

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-1,-.6 1,-.6 1,.4 -1,.4" linee="-2.5,0 2.5,0" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1.35 ymax=1.15 titolo="The submerged part is sixty percent of the block of uniform cross section"}

$$
f_{\%}=\dfrac{\rho_c}{\rho_f}\cdot100\%=\dfrac{600}{1000}\cdot100\%=60\%.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equilibrium the displaced fluid mass is the block's mass. The density ratio equals the submerged-to-exterior volume ratio.

Each cubic metre of the body has mass $600\,\mathrm{kg}$. For equal water weight, it must displace $600/1000=0.60\,\mathrm{m^3}$ for each cubic metre of its own volume. The result lies between zero and one hundred percent and leaves forty percent of its volume above water.

In the diagram the horizontal cross-section is constant, so the same ratio can be read from the height. With a varying cross-section, the submerged fraction still refers to volume.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block floats in oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ with $25\%$ of its volume above the surface. Find the block's mean density.

$$
\rho_c=(1-f_{\rm emersa})\rho_f=0.75\cdot800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the submerged part, the complement of one quarter. Mean density is three quarters of the oil density.

Twenty-five percent above the liquid means seventy-five percent submerged. Displaced oil mass per unit of the block's full volume is $0.75\cdot800=600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. In equilibrium this is also the block's mass per unit volume because $g$ is common to both weights.

As a check, each cubic metre of block displaces $0.75\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of oil, whose weight is that of $600\,\mathrm{kg}$. The block mass must match it for equilibrium.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A platform floats in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ with its upper edge $3.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ above the water. Its horizontal cross-section stays at $2.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ up to the edge. A $40\,\mathrm{kg}$ load is distributed so the platform remains level. How far does it sink and how much more mass can it support at the threshold where water reaches the edge?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta h&=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\rho_f A}=\dfrac{40}{1000\cdot2.0}\,\mathrm{m}
=0.020\,\mathrm{m}\\[4pt]
h_{\rm margine}&=(0.035-0.020)\,\mathrm m=0.015\,\mathrm m\\[4pt]
\Delta m_{\rm rimasta}&=\rho_f A h_{\rm margine}
=1000\cdot2.0\cdot0.015\,\mathrm{kg}=30\,\mathrm{kg}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first load requires an extra $0.040\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of displaced volume. With constant cross-section, that amounts to a two-centimetre drop, leaving one and a half centimetres of the edge above water. The platform can then submerge only another layer of that thickness, or $0.030\,\mathrm{m^3}$. The weight of this water balances the extra thirty kilograms. An independent check starts with the original margin: all three and a half centimetres give an extra volume of $0.070\,\mathrm{m^3}$, enough for seventy kilograms in total. Once water reaches the edge, the submerged geometry and possible flooding require a different balance.

</div>
