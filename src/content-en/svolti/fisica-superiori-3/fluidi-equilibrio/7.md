## Exercise 1

A block of density $600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ floats in water. What percentage of its volume is submerged?

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-1,-.6 1,-.6 1,.4 -1,.4" linee="-2.5,0 2.5,0" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1.35 ymax=1.15 titolo="The submerged part is sixty percent of the block of uniform cross section"}

$$
f_{\%}=100\frac{\rho_c}{\rho_f},\qquad
\begin{aligned}f_{\%}&=100 \cdot 600 \cdot \frac{1}{1000}\\[4pt]&=60\,\%.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At equilibrium the displaced fluid mass is the block's mass. The density ratio equals the submerged-to-exterior volume ratio.

Each cubic metre of the body has mass $600\,\mathrm{kg}$. For equal water weight, it must displace $600/1000=0.60\,\mathrm{m^3}$ for each cubic metre of its own volume. The result lies between zero and one hundred percent and leaves forty percent of its volume above water.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block floats in oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ with $25\%$ of its volume above the surface. Find the block's mean density.

$$
\rho_c=\rho_f(1-f_{\mathrm{emerged}}),\qquad
\begin{aligned}\rho_c&=3 \cdot \frac{1}{4} \cdot 800\\[4pt]&=600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the submerged part, the complement of one quarter. Mean density is three quarters of the oil density.

Twenty-five percent above the liquid means seventy-five percent submerged. Displaced oil mass per unit of the block's full volume is $0.75\cdot800=600\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. In equilibrium this is also the block's mass per unit volume because $g$ is common to both weights.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A platform floats in water and receives an extra $40\,\mathrm{kg}$. Its horizontal area is constant at $2\,\mathrm{m^2}$. How far does it sink, keeping its upper edge above water?

$$
\Delta h=\frac{\Delta m}{\rho_f A},\qquad
\begin{aligned}\Delta h&=\frac{40}{2 \cdot 1000}\\[4pt]&=0.02\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The added mass requires that much extra displaced water mass. Dividing by density and area gives the newly immersed thickness.

The load adds the weight of forty kilograms, so forty extra kilograms of water must be displaced, or $0.04\,\mathrm{m^3}$. At constant horizontal area this additional volume is area times sinking depth. Dividing by two square metres gives two centimetres. This calculation applies while the rim stays above water.

</div>
