## Exercise 1

A sealed body of volume $0.0030\,\mathrm{m^3}$ is completely submerged across an oil–water interface. Of its volume, $60\%$ is in oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and the rest in water of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Neglecting surface tension, what mean density gives vertical equilibrium without a support?

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_o&=0.60 V=0.0018\,\mathrm{m^3},
\qquad V_a=0.40 V=0.0012\,\mathrm{m^3}\\[4pt]
\rho_c Vg&=\rho_o V_o g+\rho_a V_a g\\[4pt]
\rho_c&=\dfrac{\rho_o V_o+\rho_a V_a}{V}
=(0.60\cdot800+0.40\cdot1000)\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}=880\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Buoyancy is the weight of the oil and water that would fill the space occupied by the body. Equilibrium equates this displaced weight with the weight of the body, so $g$ cancels. The answer lies between the two liquid densities and closer to the oil density because more volume is displaced there. If the body is raised slightly while keeping its orientation and staying fully immersed, it displaces less water and its buoyancy decreases. The resultant vertical force then tends to return it towards its original position.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sphere of volume $1.5\,\mathrm L$ is half submerged in oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find buoyancy.

$$
F_A=\rho_f gV_{\rm imm}=800\cdot10\cdot0.00075\,\mathrm{N}=6\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use only the submerged half and convert litres to cubic metres. The displaced volume holds six hundred grams of oil.

The submerged half is $0.75\,\mathrm L=0.00075\,\mathrm{m^3}$. At density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ this is $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ of displaced oil with weight six newtons. Using the full sphere volume would double the answer by assigning liquid buoyancy to the part above the surface.

The model neglects the air buoyancy on the exposed half. In the same oil, if the submerged volume is unchanged, the buoyant force stays at $6\,\mathrm{N}$ regardless of the depth of the bottom point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A fully submerged prism has horizontal base $0.05\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and height $0.40\,\mathrm m$. Pressure on its lower face exceeds upper pressure by $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Find buoyancy.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-.5,-.4 .5,-.4 .5,.4 -.5,.4" vettori="0,.8,0,.45;0,-1,0,-.47" linee="-1.5,1.15 1.5,1.15" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.5 titolo="The deeper face receives the larger force magnitude"}

$$
F_A=(p_{\rm bottom}-p_{\rm top})A=4000\cdot0.05\,\mathrm{N}=200\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a straight prism, side pressures have no vertical component. The difference between vertical forces equals pressure difference times base area.

Side pressures on the right prism cancel in pairs. Bottom pressure acts upwards and top pressure downwards. The vertical residual is $4000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ on the $0.05\,\mathrm{m^2}$ base. Another check multiplies the mean pressure gradient $10000\,\mathrm{Pa/m}$ by the $0.020\,\mathrm{m^3}$ volume.

The $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ is already the difference between the faces. Raising both pressures by the same amount increases each normal force, while leaving their resultant unchanged.

</div>
