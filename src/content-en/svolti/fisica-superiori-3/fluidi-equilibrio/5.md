## Exercise 1

An immersed object displaces $0.002\,\mathrm{m^3}$ of water with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find buoyancy.

$$
F_A=\rho_f gV,\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_A&=1000 \cdot 10 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{1000}\\[4pt]&=20\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

That volume holds two kilograms of water. Buoyancy is their weight and is upward.

The displaced volume corresponds to two litres. At density $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ that much water has mass two kilograms. Its weight is twenty newtons, matching the upward buoyancy force in magnitude. The body's own density is irrelevant here because the excluded volume is given.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sphere of volume $1.5\,\mathrm L$ is half submerged in oil of density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ find buoyancy.

$$
F_A=\rho_f g\frac{V_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_A&=800 \cdot 10 \cdot 15 \cdot \frac{1}{10000} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\\[4pt]&=6\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use only the submerged half and convert litres to cubic metres. The displaced volume holds six hundred grams of oil.

The submerged half is $0.75\,\mathrm L=0.00075\,\mathrm{m^3}$. At density $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ this is $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ of displaced oil with weight six newtons. Using the full sphere volume would double the answer by assigning liquid buoyancy to the part above the surface.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A fully submerged prism has horizontal base $0.05\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and height $0.40\,\mathrm m$. Pressure on its lower face exceeds upper pressure by $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$. Find buoyancy.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="-.5,-.4 .5,-.4 .5,.4 -.5,.4" vettori="0,.8,0,.45;0,-1,0,-.47" linee="-1.5,1.15 1.5,1.15" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.5 titolo="The deeper face receives the larger force magnitude"}

$$
F_A=(p_{\mathrm{bottom}}-p_{\mathrm{top}})A,\qquad
\begin{aligned}F_A&=4000 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{20}\\[4pt]&=200\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a straight prism, side pressures have no vertical component. The difference between vertical forces equals pressure difference times base area.

Side pressures on the right prism cancel in pairs. Bottom pressure acts upwards and top pressure downwards. The vertical residual is $4000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ on the $0.05\,\mathrm{m^2}$ base. Another check multiplies the mean pressure gradient $10000\,\mathrm{Pa/m}$ by the $0.020\,\mathrm{m^3}$ volume.

</div>
