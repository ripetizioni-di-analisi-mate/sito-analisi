## Exercise 1

A normal force of $120\,\mathrm N$ is uniform over $30\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Find the pressure.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,.4 0,.4" vettori=".5,1,.5,.4,F;1.5,1,1.5,.4;2.5,1,2.5,.4" xmin=-.5 xmax=3.6 ymin=-.3 ymax=1.4 titolo="The force is distributed over the face and points normally to it"}

$$
p=\frac{F_\perp}{A},\qquad
\begin{aligned}p&=\frac{120}{30 \cdot \frac{1}{10000}}\\[4pt]&=40000\,\mathrm{Pa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Converting area squares the centimetre-to-metre factor. Thirty square centimetres are three thousandths of a square metre.

One square centimetre is $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ because both sides of the square are converted. The full area is $0.003\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Check backwards: $40000$ pascals over that area gives $40000\cdot0.003=120\,\mathrm N$, with square metres cancelling.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.20\,\mathrm{m^2}$ plate has uniform pressure of $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ on one side and $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ on the other. Find the net force magnitude.

$$
F=(p_1-p_2)A,\qquad
\begin{aligned}F&=\left(6000 - 2000\right) \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{1}{5}\\[4pt]&=800\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both forces are normal to the same plate and point oppositely. Convert their pressure difference to pascals before multiplying by area.

The wall receives $6000\cdot0.20=1200\,\mathrm N$ from one side and $2000\cdot0.20=400\,\mathrm N$ from the other. Both act normal to the wall in opposite directions, so subtract them. The resultant points from the higher-pressure side towards the lower-pressure side.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A force of $80\,\mathrm N$ acts at $60^\circ$ to the normal of a face of area $0.10\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Find the mean pressure due to the force.

$$
p=\frac{F\cos\theta}{A},\qquad
\begin{aligned}p&=\frac{80}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{1}{10}}\\[4pt]&=400\,\mathrm{Pa}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the projection along the normal, forty newtons. The tangential component does not increase normal pressure.

The projection onto the normal is $80\cos60^\circ=40\,\mathrm N$. The tangential part creates a shear load and does not enter pressure. Applying the full $80\,\mathrm N$ to the area would overestimate the compressive contribution by a factor of two.

</div>
