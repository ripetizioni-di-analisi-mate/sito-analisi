## Exercise 1

A normal force of $120\,\mathrm N$ is uniform over $30\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. Find the pressure.

::grafico{assi=0 poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,.4 0,.4" vettori=".5,1,.5,.4;1.5,1,1.5,.4;2.5,1,2.5,.4" xmin=-.5 xmax=3.6 ymin=-.3 ymax=1.4 titolo="The force is distributed over the face and points normally to it"}

$$
p=\dfrac{F_\perp}{A}=\dfrac{120}{30\cdot10^{-4}}\,\mathrm{Pa}=40000\,\mathrm{Pa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Converting area squares the centimetre-to-metre factor. Thirty square centimetres are three thousandths of a square metre.

One square centimetre is $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ because both sides of the square are converted. The full area is $0.003\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Check backwards: $40000$ pascals over that area gives $40000\cdot0.003=120\,\mathrm N$, with square metres cancelling.

The arrows in the diagram are equal because equal portions of the face carry the same force. Measuring over a smaller portion would give the same ratio of force to area.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.20\,\mathrm{m^2}$ plate has uniform pressure of $6\,\mathrm{kPa}$ on one side and $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ on the other. Find the net force magnitude.

$$
F=(p_1-p_2)A=(6000-2000)\cdot0.20\,\mathrm{N}=800\,\mathrm{N}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both forces are normal to the same plate and point oppositely. Convert their pressure difference to pascals before multiplying by area.

The wall receives $6000\cdot0.20=1200\,\mathrm N$ from one side and $2000\cdot0.20=400\,\mathrm N$ from the other. Both act normal to the wall in opposite directions, so subtract them. The resultant points from the higher-pressure side towards the lower-pressure side.

Taking the $2\,\mathrm{kPa}$ side as the reference gives the same result. Its contribution is zero in that reference, and the opposite side exerts $4\,\mathrm{kPa}$ over the full area.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A vertical rectangular gate $0.70\,\mathrm m$ wide extends from the free surface to a depth of $0.90\,\mathrm m$. Air on the dry side is at the same atmospheric pressure as at the free surface. With a water density of $1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the mean pressure difference, the resultant force and its torque about the upper edge.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{\Delta p}&=\dfrac{\rho gH}{2}=\dfrac{1000\cdot10\cdot0.90}{2}\,\mathrm{Pa}=4500\,\mathrm{Pa}\\[4pt]
F&=\overline{\Delta p}\, bH=4500\cdot0.70\cdot0.90\,\mathrm{N}=2835\,\mathrm{N}\\[4pt]
h_R&=\dfrac{2H}{3}=0.60\,\mathrm{m},
\qquad M=Fh_R=2835\cdot0.60\,\mathrm{N\,m}=1701\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Outside air pressure cancels the atmospheric contribution on the water side. The remaining pressure difference is zero at the surface and $9000\,\mathrm{Pa}$ at the bottom. Because the width is constant, horizontal strips have area proportional to their height and the mean is half the sum of the endpoints. The resultant pushes the gate towards the dry side. For torque, replace the triangular distribution with a force acting through its centroid, closer to the bottom. Using the mean depth $0.45\,\mathrm m$ as the lever arm would underestimate torque even with the correct force magnitude.

</div>
