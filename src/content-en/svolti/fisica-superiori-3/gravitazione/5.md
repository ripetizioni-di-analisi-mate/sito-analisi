## Exercise 1

At a radius, $\mu/r=4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Find minimum escape speed with no atmosphere.

$$
v_e=\sqrt{2\mu/r},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_e&=\sqrt{2 \cdot 4 \cdot 10^{6}}\\[4pt]&\simeq2828.43\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Kinetic energy per unit mass must match the magnitude of potential energy per unit mass. The factor of two comes from the one-half kinetic-energy coefficient.

Since $\mu/r$ has units of energy per mass, twice that value has units $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$ and its square root is a speed. At about $2828\,\mathrm{m/s}$, kinetic energy per kilogram balances the negative potential of four million joules per kilogram.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Circular speed at the departure radius is $5000\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find minimum escape speed from that radius.

$$
v_e=\sqrt2v_c,\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_e&=5000 \cdot \sqrt{2}\\[4pt]&\simeq7071.07\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Escape requires twice the circular kinetic energy at the same radius. Multiply the speed by the square root of two.

Circular kinetic energy offsets part of the negative potential energy, leaving a negative total. At the same position kinetic energy must increase while potential is unchanged. The speed of about $7071\,\mathrm{m/s}$ has twice the squared circular speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body is launched outward with kinetic energy $13\,\mathrm{MJ}$ and potential energy $-9\,\mathrm{MJ}$, with no further work. What kinetic energy remains at infinity?

$$
K_\infty=K_i+U_i,\qquad
\begin{aligned}K_\infty&=13 - 9\\[4pt]&=4\,\mathrm{MJ}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Potential energy tends to zero at infinity. The initial total remains, positive relative to the escape threshold.

As the body recedes, potential energy rises by nine megajoules and kinetic energy drops by the same amount. The positive total leaves four megajoules at infinity. If initial kinetic energy equalled the magnitude of potential, the limiting value would instead be zero.

</div>
