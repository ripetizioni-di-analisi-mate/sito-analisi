## Exercise 1

A satellite has period $2\,\mathrm h$. A second satellite around the same source has triple the semimajor axis. Find its period.

$$
T_2=T_1(a_2/a_1)^{3/2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}T_2&=2 \cdot 3^{\frac{3}{2}}\\[4pt]&\simeq10.3923\,\mathrm h.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Period grows with the three-halves power of the semimajor axis. Both periods must use the same unit.

The common constant cancels when the two third-law equations are divided. Three to the three-halves power is $3\sqrt3$, so the period is $6\sqrt3\,\mathrm h$, about $10.39\,\mathrm h$. It grows faster than the semimajor axis because the exponent exceeds one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An elliptical orbit reaches distances $2R$ and $8R$ from the focus. Find the semimajor axis in units of $R$.

::grafico{uguale=1 assi=0 curve="5*cos(t),4*sin(t),0,6.283185" punti="3,0,pieno,F;5,0,pieno,A;-5,0,pieno,B" xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-5 ymax=5 titolo="F is 2R from A and 8R from B"}

$$
a/R=\frac{r_{\min}+r_{\max}}{2R},\qquad
\begin{aligned}a/R&=\frac{2 + 8}{2}\\[4pt]&=5.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The extreme radii add to the length of the major axis. Its semimajor axis is half that length.

The focus divides the major axis into segments of two and eight radii. Their sum is the full axis length, while the semimajor axis runs from the centre to a vertex. Five radii lies between the two extreme orbital radii.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two orbits about the same source have a period ratio of eight to one. Find the semimajor-axis ratio.

$$
a_2/a_1=(T_2/T_1)^{2/3},\qquad
\begin{aligned}a_2/a_1&=8^{\frac{2}{3}}\\[4pt]&=4.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Invert the power in Kepler's third law by taking the two-thirds power. The cube root of eight is two and its square is four.

As a check, putting the ratio four into Kepler's third law gives $4^3=64$. This is the ratio of squared periods, so the period ratio is $\sqrt{64}=8$, the observed value.

</div>
