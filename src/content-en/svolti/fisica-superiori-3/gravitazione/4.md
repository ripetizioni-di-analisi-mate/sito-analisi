## Exercise 1

A satellite travels around a circle of radius $5\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m$ at $5000\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find its period.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=\frac{2\pi r}{v}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{2\pi\cdot5\cdot10^6}{5000}
=2000\pi\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
&\simeq6283.19\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One turn takes circumference divided by speed. The metre-to-metre-per-second ratio leaves seconds.

Before multiplying by $2\pi$, the radius-to-speed ratio is one thousand seconds. A full turn covers more than six radii, so the period exceeds six thousand seconds. Multiplying the result by five thousand metres per second checks the circumference.

This relation uses the whole circumference and the constant speed. If two position readings indicated diametrically opposite points, half a period would separate them. Speed varies on an elliptical orbit, so a single local value would be insufficient for this calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A probe has orbital period $6000\,\mathrm s$ at radius $6\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m$ on a circular orbit. Find orbital speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega&=\frac{2\pi}{T}\\[6pt]
v&=\omega r=\frac{2\pi\cdot6\cdot10^6}{6000}\\[6pt]
&=2000\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq6283.19\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One period covers a full circumference. Speed stays constant on that orbit.

The ratio $\dfrac rT$ is one thousand metres per second, while one circuit is $2\pi$ radii. The speed of about $6283\,\mathrm{m/s}$ therefore exceeds that ratio. Six thousand seconds is the duration of a full turn, rather than of one radian.

The same value follows from angular speed, which measures angle swept per second, multiplied by the radius. The factor of two pi converts one full revolution to radians. Here the radius already measures distance from the centre and can be used directly.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two circular orbits of radii $r$ and $4r$ are around the same planet. The first takes $1000\,\mathrm s$. How long does the second take?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{T_2}{T_1}&=\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^{\frac32}
=4^{\frac32}=8\\[6pt]
T_2&=8\cdot1000=8000\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The path is four times longer and the speed is half as large. The period is eight times greater.

Check the exponent by squaring the period ratio. Eight squared is sixty-four, equal to four cubed. The comparison uses the same source so the constant in Kepler's ratio is shared.

The graph shows that an increase in radius changes the period more when the orbit is already large. If the two orbits belonged to different planets, the gravitational parameter ratio would also enter. Here the shared source makes the radius ratio sufficient.

</div>
