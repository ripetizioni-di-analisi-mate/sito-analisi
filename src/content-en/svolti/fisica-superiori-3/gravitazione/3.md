## Exercise 1

A planet's parameter is $5\cdot10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$. Find circular speed $5\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m$ from its centre.

$$
v=\sqrt{\mu/r},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v&=\sqrt{\frac{5 \cdot 10^{12}}{5 \cdot 10^{6}}}\\[4pt]&=1000\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parameter divided by radius gives one million square metres per second squared. Take its square root for speed.

The centripetal check gives $v^2/r=(10^6)/(5\cdot10^6)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=0.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. This matches the local field, with velocity tangent to the orbit. At that radius the same speed directed radially would not describe a circular orbit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Circular speed at radius $r$ is $6000\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the speed at radius $4r$ around the same source.

::grafico{f="6000/sqrt(x)" punti="1,6000,pieno;4,3000,pieno" xmin=.8 xmax=5 ymin=0 ymax=7000 nomi="r/r_1,v (m/s)" titolo="Circular speed decreases inversely as the square root of radius"}

$$
v_2=v_1\sqrt{r_1/r_2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}v_2&=\frac{6000}{\sqrt{4}}\\[4pt]&=3000\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting both radii in the circular law cancels the parameter. Divide by the square root of four.

The field on the second orbit is one sixteenth of the first, while radius is four times larger. Field times radius, equal to squared speed, is therefore one quarter as large. Speed is consequently one half.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular satellite travels at $3000\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at distance $4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m$. Find the source's gravitational parameter.

$$
\mu=v^2r,\qquad
\begin{aligned}\mu&=3000^{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 10^{6}\\[4pt]&=3.6\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equate centripetal acceleration with the gravitational field and solve for the parameter. Multiply squared speed by the radius.

Independently calculate centripetal acceleration from speed as $9\cdot10^6/(4\cdot10^6)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2.25\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Multiplying by the radius squared gives $3.6\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$, as in the direct calculation. The units confirm the result is $\mu$, distinct from mass.

</div>
