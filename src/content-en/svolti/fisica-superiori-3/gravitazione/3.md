## Exercise 1

A planet's parameter is $5\cdot10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$. Find circular speed $5\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m$ from its centre.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{v^2}{r}&=\frac{\mu}{r^2}\\[6pt]
v&=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}}
 =\sqrt{\frac{5\cdot10^{12}}{5\cdot10^6}}
 =1000\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parameter divided by radius gives one million square metres per second squared. Take its square root for speed.

The centripetal check gives $\dfrac{v^2}{r}=\dfrac{10^6}{5\cdot10^6}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=0.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. This matches the local field, with velocity tangent to the orbit. At that radius the same speed directed radially would not describe a circular orbit.

The satellite mass cancels because the force and its inertia increase by the same factor. The calculation assumes the source stays practically at rest in the chosen frame. For comparable masses, motion about the centre of mass would require a refinement of the model.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Circular speed at radius $r$ is $6000\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find the speed at radius $4r$ around the same source.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{v_2}{v_1}&=\sqrt{\frac{r_1}{r_2}}=\sqrt{\frac14}\\[6pt]
v_2&=6000\cdot\frac12=3000\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting both radii in the circular law cancels the parameter. Divide by the square root of four.

The field on the second orbit is one sixteenth of the first, while radius is four times larger. Field times radius, equal to squared speed, is therefore one quarter as large. Speed is consequently one half.

The curve joins speeds of different circular orbits in the same field. A probe transferring between the two generally follows an intermediate trajectory. The graph compares the circular states, while tracking the transfer requires the energy balance and the details of the manoeuvre.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular satellite travels at $3000\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at distance $4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m$. Find the source's gravitational parameter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu&=v^2r\\[6pt]
&=3000^2\cdot4\cdot10^6
=3.6\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equate centripetal acceleration with the gravitational field and solve for the parameter. Multiply squared speed by the radius.

Independently calculate centripetal acceleration from speed as $\dfrac{9\cdot10^6}{4\cdot10^6}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=2.25\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Multiplying by the radius squared gives $3.6\cdot10^{13}\,\mathrm{m^3/s^2}$, as in the direct calculation. The units confirm the result is $\mu$, distinct from mass.

The measurement therefore gives information about the source. Dividing the parameter by the gravitational constant estimates its mass. If a second circular satellite is observed around the same body, its measured speed and radius should yield the same parameter within experimental uncertainty.

</div>
