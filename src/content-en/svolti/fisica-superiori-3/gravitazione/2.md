## Exercise 1

A planet has $g_0=12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find its field at altitude $R$, where $R$ is its radius.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&=R+h=2R\\[6pt]
g(r)&=g_0\left(\frac Rr\right)^2\\[6pt]
&=12\left(\frac12\right)^2=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="12/x^2" punti="1,12,pieno;2,3,pieno" xmin=1 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=17 nomi="r/R,g (m/s²)" titolo="The field is plotted against centre distance"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Add the altitude to the radius. The requested point is twice the surface distance from the centre, giving one quarter of the field.

Initial centre distance is the radius, and final distance is twice that, because altitude is measured above the surface. The $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ result matches the graph's drop from twelve to three as relative distance goes from one to two.

The local value is for a point two radii from the centre. A mass released there would initially accelerate towards the centre at three metres per second squared. During a long fall the acceleration would increase as it approached. Here only the initial value at the stated point is required.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ person weighs $600\,\mathrm N$ at the surface. What is their weight at distance $3R$ from the centre?

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_0&=\frac{P_0}{m}=\frac{600}{60}=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}\\[6pt]
P(3R)&=m g_0\left(\frac{R}{3R}\right)^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac{600}{9}\,\mathrm N\simeq66.7\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mass stays fixed, so weight follows the field. Tripling centre distance reduces the field to one ninth.

The person keeps the same mass, so the initial field is $\dfrac{600}{60}=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. At three times the centre distance the field is one ninth as large. Multiplying by sixty kilograms recovers a weight of about $66.7\,\mathrm N$.

The stated weight is the magnitude of the gravitational force, which points towards the centre. A scale on a stationary platform at that distance would measure a support force of equal magnitude. In free fall that support force would disappear while gravity would continue to act.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On a line, one source gives a field of $14\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ left and another gives $8\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ right. Find the net component with right positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_x&=g_{1x}+g_{2x}\\[6pt]
&=(-14+8)\,\mathrm{N/kg}\\[6pt]
&=-6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fields are opposing collinear vectors. Their sum is negative because the leftward component is larger.

Assign $-14\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ to the source attracting to the left and $+8\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ to the rightward source. The negative sum of magnitude six points towards the first. A positive test mass would accelerate in that direction without changing the field found.

Reversing the axis would make the number positive while indicating the same physical direction for the field. Adding the magnitudes directly would give twenty-two and describe contributions pointing the same way. The difference of six is consistent with a point between the two sources.

</div>
