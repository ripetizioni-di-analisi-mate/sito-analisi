## Exercise 1

A planet has $g_0=12\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find its field at altitude $R$, where $R$ is its radius.

::grafico{f="12/x^2" punti="1,12,pieno;2,3,pieno" xmin=.8 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=17 nomi="r/R,g (m/s²)" titolo="The field is plotted against centre distance"}

$$
g=g_0\left(\frac{R}{R+h}\right)^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}g&=\frac{12}{2^{2}}\\[4pt]&=3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Add the altitude to the radius. The requested point is twice the surface distance from the centre, giving one quarter of the field.

Initial centre distance is the radius, and final distance is twice that, because altitude is measured above the surface. The $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ result matches the graph's drop from twelve to three as relative distance goes from one to two.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $60\,\mathrm{kg}$ person weighs $600\,\mathrm N$ at the surface. What is their weight at distance $3R$ from the centre?

$$
P=P_0\left(\frac Rr\right)^2,\qquad
\begin{aligned}P&=\frac{600}{3^{2}}\\[4pt]&\simeq66.6667\,\mathrm N.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mass stays fixed, so weight follows the field. Tripling centre distance reduces the field to one ninth.

The person keeps the same mass, so the initial field is $600/60=10\,\mathrm{N/kg}$. At three times the centre distance the field is one ninth as large. Multiplying by sixty kilograms recovers a weight of about $66.7\,\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On a line, one source gives a field of $14\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ left and another gives $8\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ right. Find the net component with right positive.

$$
g_x=g_{\mathrm{right}}-g_{\mathrm{left}},\qquad
\begin{aligned}g_x&=8 - 14\\[4pt]&=-6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fields are opposing collinear vectors. Their sum is negative because the leftward component is larger.

Assign $-14\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ to the source attracting to the left and $+8\,\mathrm{N/kg}$ to the rightward source. The negative sum of magnitude six points towards the first. A positive test mass would accelerate in that direction without changing the field found.

</div>
