## Exercise 1

A circular satellite has potential energy $-16\,\mathrm{MJ}$. Find its total orbital mechanical energy.

$$
\begin{aligned}
K&=-\frac U2=8\,\mathrm{MJ}\\[6pt]
E&=K+U=(8-16)\,\mathrm{MJ}=-8\,\mathrm{MJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Circular kinetic energy is half the magnitude of potential energy, so their sum is half the potential energy.

The kinetic contribution is positive eight megajoules. Add potential energy of minus sixteen to get a negative total. That sign is consistent with a bound orbit: the system needs extra energy to reach the threshold relative to infinity.

The relationship between kinetic and potential energy follows from the centripetal condition at constant radius. On an elongated orbit, speed and radius vary during the revolution, so the same local potential energy would leave kinetic energy undetermined. The circular orbit is the information that fixes it here.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A circular satellite has mechanical energy $-10\,\mathrm{MJ}$. How much work must be supplied ideally to reach the escape threshold?

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_f&=0\\[6pt]
W&=E_f-E_i=(0-(-10))\,\mathrm{MJ}\\[6pt]
&=10\,\mathrm{MJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At threshold the total energy is zero relative to infinity. Positive work supplies the difference from the initial orbit.

Supply ten megajoules to bring the motion to threshold. With less energy input, the total would stay negative. The known initial total suffices because the balance compares endpoints without recalculating kinetic and potential energy separately.

The manoeuvre is an ideal transfer of mechanical energy to the satellite of fixed mass. The thrust must also put it on an unobstructed trajectory outwards. With exactly zero total energy, speed tends to zero as the distance grows, with no stop at a finite distance.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circular satellite with energy $-12\,\mathrm{MJ}$ changes to a circular orbit of twice the radius with the same mass. Find the energy change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_f&=E_i\frac{r_i}{r_f}=\frac{-12}{2}\,\mathrm{MJ}=-6\,\mathrm{MJ}\\[6pt]
\Delta E&=E_f-E_i=[-6-(-12)]\,\mathrm{MJ}=6\,\mathrm{MJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Energy is negative and inversely proportional to radius. It becomes less negative at twice the radius, giving a positive change.

In both circular orbits energy has the form of a negative constant divided by radius. Doubling radius brings the total to minus six megajoules. The positive difference of six megajoules compares the two circular states, distinct from gravity's zero work over a single orbit.

In the transfer to the wider orbit, potential energy becomes less negative and circular kinetic energy halves. The changes in the two terms have opposite signs. The total rises because the potential energy increase exceeds the kinetic energy decrease. The graph compares the two balances after the manoeuvres.

</div>
