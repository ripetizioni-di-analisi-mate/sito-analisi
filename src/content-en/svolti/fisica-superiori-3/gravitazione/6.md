## Exercise 1

A $100\,\mathrm{kg}$ body is where $\mu/r=2\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Find its potential energy with zero at infinity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U&=-m\frac{\mu}{r}\\[6pt]
&=-100\cdot2\cdot10^6\,\mathrm J\\[6pt]
&=-2\cdot10^8\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given quantity is the potential energy magnitude per unit mass. Multiply by mass and assign the negative sign.

The magnitude of two hundred megajoules scales with mass and is negative relative to zero at infinity. The same change with positive sign is the minimum energy needed to separate the body from the source when starting from rest at this radius.

The value is in joules because square metres per square second times kilograms give energy. The sign follows from the reference at infinity. If the body were already moving at this distance, deciding whether it is bound would require comparing the potential energy magnitude with the kinetic energy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Potential energy at radius $r$ is $-30\,\mathrm{MJ}$. Find its change on moving the body to $3r$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_f&=U_i\frac{r_i}{r_f}=\frac{-30}{3}\,\mathrm{MJ}=-10\,\mathrm{MJ}\\[6pt]
\Delta U&=U_f-U_i=[-10-(-30)]\,\mathrm{MJ}\\[6pt]
&=20\,\mathrm{MJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-30/x" punti="1,-30,pieno;3,-10,pieno" xmin=.8 xmax=4 ymin=-40 ymax=5 nomi="r/r_1,U (MJ)" titolo="Potential energy is less negative at greater radius"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The new value is less negative because the distance is three times larger. Subtract the initial value to find the positive energy needed to move outward.

The graph shows that tripling radius reduces magnitude from thirty to ten megajoules. The new value is less negative. The change is positive twenty megajoules, so gravity alone would do minus twenty megajoules of work.

The change depends only on the initial and final distances. A transfer along a curved path would give the same value between the same endpoints in a static field. Velocity can change during the transfer, but its value does not enter the potential energy difference.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A body falls from a position at potential energy $-20\,\mathrm{MJ}$ to one at $-28\,\mathrm{MJ}$. Find the work done by gravity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&=[-28-(-20)]\,\mathrm{MJ}=-8\,\mathrm{MJ}\\[6pt]
W_g&=-\Delta U=8\,\mathrm{MJ}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Gravity does positive work during approach. Take the opposite of the negative potential energy change.

The potential difference is $-8\,\mathrm{MJ}$. Attraction points along the inward displacement, so positive work is consistent with the motion. If gravity were the only force, kinetic energy would increase by eight megajoules.

A transfer in the opposite direction would give the opposite potential energy change. Gravitational work would also reverse its sign because gravity would oppose the outward motion. If other forces act, their work must be included in the change in kinetic energy.

</div>
