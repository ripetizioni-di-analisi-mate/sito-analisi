## Exercise 1

Solve $u_t+2u_x=t$ for $t\ge0$, $x\in\mathbb R$, with $u(0,x)=\sin x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X'(s)&=2,\qquad X(t)=x, \\
X(s)&=x-2(t-s), \\
\frac{d}{ds}u(s,X(s))&=u_t(s,X(s))+2u_x(s,X(s)), \\
&=s, \\
u(t,x)&=u(0,x-2t)+\int_0^t s\,ds, \\
&=\sin(x-2t)+\left[\frac{s^2}{2}\right]_{0}^{t}, \\
&=\sin(x-2t)+\frac{t^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic has the same constant velocity as for the homogeneous equation and therefore passes through $x-2t$ when $s=0$. The difference is in the transported value. Along the characteristic, the derivative of $u$ equals the source evaluated at the current point. Since the source depends only on time, it equals $s$ and is the same on every characteristic at time $s$.

Integrating from zero to $t$ gives the change from the initial value. The initial sine function and the source are smooth, so the resulting function has the required derivatives and the calculation can be reversed to verify the equation. The sine contributions to $u_t+2u_x$ cancel by the chain rule. The time derivative of the quadratic term equals $t$ and its spatial derivative is zero. It vanishes initially, so the initial data are attained and the increment is the same throughout the profile.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $u_t+xu_x=0$ with $u(0,x)=x^2$ for $x\in\mathbb R$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X'(s)&=X(s),\qquad X(t)=x, \\
\frac{d}{ds}\bigl(e^{-s}X(s)\bigr)&=0, \\
e^{-s}X(s)&=e^{-t}x, \\
X(0)&=e^{-t}x, \\
\frac{d}{ds}u(s,X(s))&=u_t(s,X(s))+X(s)u_x(s,X(s)), \\
&=0, \\
u(t,x)&=u(0,xe^{-t}), \\
&=x^2e^{-2t}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;exp(-2)*x^2" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=0 ymax=4.4 nomi="x,u" legenda="u(0,x);u(1,x)" titolo="With velocity $x$, the profile at time $1$ is obtained by evaluating the initial data at $xe^{-1}$. Points at which the profile has the same positive value move away from the origin."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic velocity depends on the current spatial coordinate. The equation $X'=X$ has a smooth coefficient and a global solution for each initial value. The integrating factor $e^{-s}$ makes its product with $X(s)$ constant and also includes the stationary characteristic $X=0$. Thus the final condition gives the initial coordinate without division by $X$.

Along each characteristic the chain rule gives the left-hand side of the transport equation. Since it is zero, the value at $(t,x)$ equals the square of the initial spatial coordinate. The initial function is smooth, so the composition is a classical solution. Indeed, $u_t=-2x^2e^{-2t}$ and $xu_x=2x^2e^{-2t}$ cancel, and at time zero $x^2$ remains. For $t>0$, a positive initial point moves away from the origin to the right and a negative one to the left. At a fixed position the profile decreases because the initial function is evaluated closer to the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $x\ge0$, $t\ge0$, solve $u_t+u_x=0$ with $u(0,x)=1+x^2$ and $u(t,0)=1+t^3$. Check how the solutions match across $x=t$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X(s)&=x-t+s, \\
X(0)&=x-t, \\
X(s)&=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s=t-x, \\
\frac{d}{ds}u(s,X(s))&=0, \\
\lim_{x\to t^+}u(t,x)&=\lim_{x\to t^-}u(t,x)=1, \\
\lim_{x\to t^\pm}u_x(t,x)&=0,\qquad
\lim_{x\to t^\pm}u_t(t,x)=0, \\
u(t,x)&=
\begin{cases}
1+(x-t)^2,&x\ge t,\\
1+(t-x)^3,&x<t.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{nomi="x,t" uguale=1 xmin=0 xmax=4 ymax=4 ymin=0 linee="0,0 4,4;1,0 4,3;0,1 3,4" punti="1,0,pieno;0,1,pieno;3,2,pieno;2,3,pieno" etichette="2.5,1.1,from t=0;0.95,2.7,from x=0" titolo="The diagonal $x=t$ separates characteristics originating at $t=0$ from those entering through $x=0$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Trace the unit-speed characteristic through $(t,x)$ backward to its first intersection with the boundary of the quadrant. If $x\ge t$, time reaches zero at spatial coordinate $x-t\ge0$, so use the initial data. If $x<t$, the spatial coordinate reaches zero at positive time $t-x$, so use the data prescribed on the side $x=0$. Since the equation is homogeneous, values remain constant on each such line.

On the diagonal both prescriptions originate at the origin and give one. For $x>t$, the spatial derivative is $2(x-t)$ and the time derivative is its negative. For $x<t$, the spatial derivative is $-3(t-x)^2$ and the time derivative is its negative. All four expressions tend to zero on approaching the diagonal. The values and limits of the first derivatives agree, so the match is $C^1$ and the equation also holds on the diagonal.

</div>
