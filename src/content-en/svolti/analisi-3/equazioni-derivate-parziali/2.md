## Exercise 1

Solve $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with zero endpoint values and initial data $u(0,x)=\sin(x)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u(t,x)&=T(t)\sin x, \\
u_t(t,x)&=T'(t)\sin x, \\
u_{xx}(t,x)&=-T(t)\sin x, \\
T'(t)+T(t)&=0,\qquad T(0)=1, \\
\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(e^tT(t)\bigr)&=0, \\
e^tT(t)&=1, \\
T(t)&=e^{-t}, \\
u(t,x)&=e^{-t}\sin x.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sin(x);exp(-1)*sin(x)" xmin=0 xmax=3.14159265 ymin=0 ymax=1.15 nomi="x,u" legenda="u(0,x);u(1,x)" titolo="The mode $\sin x$ retains its shape and its zeros at both endpoints. At time $1$ its amplitude is $e^{-1}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The spatial function $sin x$ vanishes at $0$ and $pi$, so multiplying it by a time coefficient satisfies the boundary conditions at every instant. Its second spatial derivative is the negative of the same profile. The heat equation thus reduces to one equation for the coefficient $T$. It can be obtained by evaluating at $x=dfrac{pi}{2}$, where the sine equals one.

The initial data equal the spatial profile, so the coefficient is one at time zero. Multiplying the equation by $e^t$ gives the derivative of the product $e^tT$ on the left. Evaluate the product at time zero to determine the constant of integration. The solution is smooth and satisfies all the data, which agree at the two corners of the space-time domain. Uniqueness follows by applying the **heat energy identity** to the difference of two classical solutions with the same data.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with zero endpoint values and initial data $u(0,x)=\sin(2x)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u(t,x)&=T(t)\sin(2x), \\
\frac{d}{dx}\sin(2x)&=2\cos(2x), \\
\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\sin(2x)&=-4\sin(2x), \\
T'(t)\sin(2x)&=-4T(t)\sin(2x), \\
T'(t)+4T(t)&=0,\qquad T(0)=1, \\
\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(e^{4t}T(t)\bigr)&=0, \\
e^{4t}T(t)&=1, \\
u(t,x)&=e^{-4t}\sin(2x).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second spatial mode vanishes at both endpoints because $sin 0=sin(2pi)=0$. Each spatial differentiation gives a factor two, so the second derivative multiplies the mode by $-4$. Substitution into the heat equation gives the equality in the fourth line. Evaluating at $x=dfrac{pi}{4}$, where the sine is one, gives the scalar equation without dividing by the sine at its zeros.

The initial amplitude is one, so $T(0)=1$. The integrating factor $e^{4t}$ reduces the equation to a derivative equal to zero and determines the amplitude at every time. The product is smooth and satisfies the initial and boundary data, so uniqueness makes it the classical solution. The exponential factor is positive for every finite $t$, so the profile keeps its initial sign at each point and the interior node stays at $x=dfrac{pi}{2}$.

</div>
