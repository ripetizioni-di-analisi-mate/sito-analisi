## Exercise 1

Solve $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with fixed endpoints, $u(0,x)=\sin(x)$, and $u_t(0,x)=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u(t,x)&=T(t)\sin x, \\
u_{tt}(t,x)&=T''(t)\sin x, \\
u_{xx}(t,x)&=-T(t)\sin x, \\
T''(t)+T(t)&=0, \\
T(t)&=A\cos t+B\sin t, \\
T'(t)&=-A\sin t+B\cos t, \\
T(0)&=A=1, \\
T'(0)&=B=0, \\
u(t,x)&=\cos t\sin x.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sin(x);0;-sin(x)" xmin=0 xmax=3.14159265 ymin=-1.15 ymax=1.15 nomi="x,u" legenda="u(0,x);u(\dfrac{\pi}{2},x);u(\pi,x)" titolo="The first wave mode passes from its initial profile through zero displacement to the negative of the initial profile. The string has nonzero velocity as it passes through zero displacement."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $sin x$ is an eigenfunction of the spatial problem with fixed endpoints. It vanishes at the endpoints and its second derivative is $-sin x$. Thus $u_{tt}$ equals $u_{xx}$ for every $x$ when the time coefficient satisfies the oscillator equation. To obtain that equation, substitute $x=dfrac{pi}{2}$, where the spatial factor is one.

The two independent oscillator solutions are sine and cosine with angular frequency one. Initial displacement determines the cosine coefficient, while the initial derivative of the time factor determines the sine coefficient. Both data are smooth and vanish at the endpoints, so the mode satisfies every condition of the classical problem. Conservation of energy applied to a difference with zero data gives uniqueness. At $t=dfrac{pi}{2}$ the displacement vanishes everywhere, while the velocity is $-sin x$ and is nonzero in the interior.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $u_{tt}=u_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with fixed endpoints, $u(0,x)=\sin(2x)$, and $u_t(0,x)=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u(t,x)&=T(t)\sin(2x), \\
u_{tt}(t,x)&=T''(t)\sin(2x), \\
u_{xx}(t,x)&=-4T(t)\sin(2x), \\
T''(t)+4T(t)&=0, \\
T(t)&=A\cos(2t)+B\sin(2t), \\
T'(t)&=-2A\sin(2t)+2B\cos(2t), \\
T(0)&=A=1, \\
T'(0)&=2B=0, \\
u(t,x)&=\cos(2t)\sin(2x).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The profile $sin(2x)$ satisfies zero displacement at the endpoints. Its second derivative is the profile multiplied by $-4$, so the time factor must satisfy an oscillator equation with angular frequency two. Evaluate the equality of the second derivatives at $x=dfrac{pi}{4}$ to obtain the scalar equation. At that point the spatial factor is one.

The initial displacement has coefficient one, so the cosine coefficient is one. To impose the initial velocity, differentiate the time factor before evaluating at zero. The sine contributes $2B$, which must vanish, and the cosine has zero derivative at zero. The final product is smooth and satisfies the boundary conditions and both initial conditions. Conservation of energy for the difference proves uniqueness among classical solutions. The spatial node at $x=dfrac{pi}{2}$ stays fixed while the other interior points first pass through zero displacement at time $t=dfrac{pi}{4}$.

</div>
