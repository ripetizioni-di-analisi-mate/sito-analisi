## Exercise 1

Classify $u_{xx}+2u_{xy}+3u_{yy}=0$ throughout the plane.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=1,\qquad 2B=2,\qquad C=3, \\
B&=1, \\
M&=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}, \\
\det M&=1\cdot3-1^2, \\
&=2, \\
B^2-AC&=-\det M, \\
&=-2<0, \\
&\Longrightarrow\quad\text{elliptic equation in }\mathbb R^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t)-sin(t)/sqrt(2),sin(t)/sqrt(2),0,6.283185307;cos(t)-sin(t)/sqrt(3),sin(t)/sqrt(3),0,6.283185307" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.2 uguale=1 nomi="ξ,η" legenda="(ξ+η)^2+2η^2=1;(ξ+η)^2+3η^2=1" titolo="Both quadratic forms have elliptical level curves. A larger coefficient of $\eta^2$ narrows the curve in the $\eta$ direction."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Classification depends on the coefficients of the second derivatives. The mixed derivative coefficient is split between the two off-diagonal entries of the symmetric matrix. Since the term is $2u_{xy}$, each such entry is one. The determinant is the product of the diagonal entries minus the square of the off-diagonal entry.

The discriminant $B^2-AC$ is the negative of this determinant. Since it is strictly negative, the equation is elliptic. The associated quadratic form gives the same information. Indeed, $\xi^2+2\xi\eta+3\eta^2$ can be written as $(\xi+\eta)^2+2\eta^2$, which is positive for every nonzero vector $(\xi,\eta)$. The matrix therefore has rank two and the principal part has two principal directions with coefficients of the same sign. Its coefficients are constant, so the classification is the same at every point of the plane.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify $u_{xx}+2u_{xy}+4u_{yy}=0$ throughout the plane.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=1,\qquad B=1,\qquad C=4, \\
q(\xi,\eta)&=\xi^2+2\xi\eta+4\eta^2, \\
&=(\xi+\eta)^2+3\eta^2, \\
B^2-AC&=1^2-1\cdot4, \\
&=-3<0, \\
&\Longrightarrow\quad\text{elliptic equation in }\mathbb R^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To obtain the principal quadratic form, replace $u_{xx}$, $u_{xy}$, and $u_{yy}$ by $\xi^2$, $\xi\eta$, and $\eta^2$, respectively. Completing the square isolates $(\xi+\eta)^2$. That square already contains $\eta^2$, so the coefficient four leaves a contribution $3\eta^2$. If the sum of the squares vanishes, the second term gives $\eta=0$ and the first gives $\xi=0$. The form is therefore positive definite.

The discriminant gives the same type. The mixed term has coefficient $2B=2$, so $B$ equals one and the factors in $AC$ are one and four. Their difference from the squared mixed coefficient gives a negative discriminant independent of position. The equation is therefore elliptic throughout the plane. The two calculations express the same property, since completing the square leaves coefficient $C-B^2$ and here $A=1$.

</div>
