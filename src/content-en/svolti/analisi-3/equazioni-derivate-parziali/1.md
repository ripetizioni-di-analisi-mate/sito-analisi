## Exercise 1

Solve $u_t+u_x=0$ on the real line with $u(0,x)=e^{-x^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X'(s)&=1,\qquad X(t)=x, \\
X(s)-X(t)&=\int_t^s1\,dr, \\
X(s)&=x+s-t, \\
\frac{d}{ds}u(s,X(s))&=u_t(s,X(s))+u_x(s,X(s)), \\
&=0, \\
u(t,x)&=u(0,X(0)), \\
&=\exp\bigl(-X(0)^2\bigr), \\
&=e^{-(x-t)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="exp(-x^2);exp(-(x-1)^2)" xmin=-3 xmax=4 ymin=0 ymax=1.15 nomi="x,u" punti="0,1,pieno;1,1,pieno" legenda="u(0,x);u(1,x)" titolo="At speed $1$, the center of the Gaussian moves from the origin to $x=1$ in one unit of time."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The variable $s$ denotes time along the characteristic through $(t,x)$. The constant transport velocity is one, so the spatial coordinate $X(s)$ increases by one per unit time. The condition $X(t)=x$ selects exactly one line. At time zero its spatial coordinate is $x-t$, where the initial function must therefore be evaluated.

The Gaussian initial function is of class $C^1$ on the entire line, so it can be composed with $x-t$ and the chain rule applies. The derivative of the composition along the characteristic equals the left-hand side of the equation, so the value remains constant. Every classical solution must therefore take the initial value found in the calculation. The function constructed also satisfies the equation because its derivatives in $t$ and $x$ are opposites. At fixed time the maximum is at $x=t$, where the exponent vanishes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $u_t+2u_x=0$ on the real line with $u(0,x)=e^{-x^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X'(s)&=2,\qquad X(t)=x, \\
X(s)&=x+2(s-t), \\
X(0)&=x-2t, \\
\frac{d}{ds}u(s,X(s))&=u_t(s,X(s))+2u_x(s,X(s)), \\
&=0, \\
u(t,x)&=u(0,X(0)), \\
&=e^{-(x-2t)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Fix the point $(t,x)$ at which the solution is sought. The characteristic traced backward has speed two and goes from $x$ to spatial coordinate $x-2t$ over a time interval of length $t$. Since the initial function is defined on the entire line, the starting point lies in its domain even when $x-2t$ is negative.

Let $f(s)=e^{-s^2}$. The function $f$ is smooth and the composition found above has derivatives $u_t=-2f'(x-2t)$ and $u_x=f'(x-2t)$. Thus $2u_x$ cancels $u_t$, while at $t=0$ the argument reduces to $x$. Every characteristic meets the initial line exactly once, and the equation requires constancy along it. This gives uniqueness among classical solutions. The maximum occurs at $x=2t$, and in particular at $x=2$ at time one.

</div>
