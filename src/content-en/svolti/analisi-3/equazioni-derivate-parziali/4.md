## Exercise 1

In the rectangle $(0,\pi)\times(0,1)$, solve $\Delta u=0$ with value $\sin(x)$ on the side $y=1$ and zero on the remaining sides.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u(x,y)&=\sin x\,Y(y), \\
\Delta u(x,y)&=\sin x\bigl(Y''(y)-Y(y)\bigr), \\
Y''(y)-Y(y)&=0, \\
Y(y)&=A\cosh y+B\sinh y, \\
Y(0)&=A=0, \\
Y(1)&=B\sinh 1=1, \\
B&=\frac{1}{\sinh 1}, \\
u(x,y)&=\frac{\sinh y}{\sinh 1}\sin x.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sinh(x)/sinh(1)" xmin=0 xmax=1 ymin=0 ymax=1.15 nomi="y,u" punti="0,0,pieno;1,1,pieno" titolo="The cross section at $x=\dfrac\pi2$ has profile $\dfrac{\sinh y}{\sinh 1}$. It connects zero on the lower boundary to $1$ on the upper boundary."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The prescribed value on the top side is a single sine mode. Its factor $sin x$ vanishes on the vertical sides, so we need a vertical factor equal to zero at the bottom and one at the top. Since the second derivative in $x$ multiplies by $-1$, harmonicity requires the second vertical derivative to equal the vertical factor itself.

The solution of the equation for $Y$ is a combination of hyperbolic cosine and sine. At height zero, hyperbolic cosine is one and hyperbolic sine zero, so the cosine coefficient vanishes. At height one, divide by $sinh 1$, which is positive. The resulting function is $C^2$ in the interior and continuous on the closure, including the corners, because the top boundary function vanishes at both endpoints. The **maximum principle** applied to the difference of two solutions with the same data gives uniqueness.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the rectangle $(0,\pi)\times(0,1)$, solve $\Delta u=0$ with value $\sin(2x)$ on the side $y=1$ and zero on the remaining sides.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u(x,y)&=\sin(2x)\,Y(y), \\
u_{xx}(x,y)&=-4\sin(2x)\,Y(y), \\
u_{yy}(x,y)&=\sin(2x)\,Y''(y), \\
Y''(y)-4Y(y)&=0, \\
Y(y)&=A\cosh(2y)+B\sinh(2y), \\
Y(0)&=A=0, \\
Y(1)&=B\sinh 2=1, \\
B&=\frac{1}{\sinh 2}, \\
u(x,y)&=\frac{\sinh(2y)}{\sinh 2}\sin(2x).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mode $sin(2x)$ in the top boundary value vanishes at both endpoints, so the product automatically satisfies the data on the vertical sides. The second derivative in $x$ introduces the factor $-4$. For the Laplacian to vanish, the vertical factor must satisfy $Y''=4Y$. Evaluation at $x=dfrac{pi}{4}$ suffices to obtain this equation because the spatial mode equals one there.

The characteristic roots are $2$ and $-2$, so $cosh(2y)$ and $sinh(2y)$ form a basis of solutions. The bottom boundary condition eliminates hyperbolic cosine. The top condition normalizes hyperbolic sine by a positive divisor. The product is smooth in the interior, continuous on the closure, and gives the prescribed values even at the corners. The maximum principle gives uniqueness. At every height the profile retains its middle node at $x=dfrac{pi}{2}$.

</div>
