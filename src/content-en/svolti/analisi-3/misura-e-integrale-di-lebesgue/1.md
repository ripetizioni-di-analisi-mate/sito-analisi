## Exercise 1

Find the Lebesgue measure of $[0,3]\cup[2,6]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=[0,3],\qquad B=[2,6] \\
A\cap B&=[2,3] \\
B\setminus A&=(3,6] \\
m(A\cup B)&=m(A)+m(B\setminus A) \\
&=(3-0)+(6-3) \\
&=3+3 \\
&=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,1 3,1;2,2 6,2;0,3 6,3" segmenti="0,0,0,3;2,0,2,2;3,0,3,2;6,0,6,3" etichette="1,1.3,A;3.9,2.3,B;2.8,3.3,A ∪ B" xmin=-0.4 xmax=6.5 ymin=-0.2 ymax=3.8 titolo="The two intervals overlap between $2$ and $3$. The upper segment shows their union."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The intervals $A$ and $B$ are Lebesgue measurable, so additivity applies to a disjoint decomposition of their union. The segment $[2,3]$ belongs to both intervals. If we retain all of $A$, only $(3,6]$ needs to be added. The point $3$ already lies in $A$, so it belongs only to the first piece of the decomposition. The second piece has length $6-3$ even though its left endpoint is excluded, since a singleton has measure zero.

The same decomposition identifies $A\cup B$ with the full interval $[0,6]$. Indeed, any point between $0$ and $3$ belongs to $A$, while every point greater than $3$ and at most $6$ belongs to $B$. The two resulting pieces each have length $3$, and the sum of their lengths gives the measure of the union.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the Lebesgue measure of $[0,4]\cup[3,8]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=[0,4],\qquad B=[3,8] \\
A\cap B&=[3,4] \\
m(A)+m(B)&=(4-0)+(8-3) \\
&=4+5 \\
m(A\cap B)&=4-3 \\
&=1 \\
m(A\cup B)&=m(A)+m(B)-m(A\cap B) \\
&=9-1 \\
&=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $A$ and $B$ have finite measure, we can subtract the measure of their intersection in the inclusion–exclusion formula. To derive the formula, decompose $B$ into $B\setminus A$ and $A\cap B$. These parts are measurable and disjoint, so their measures sum to $m(B)$. The union of $A$ and $B\setminus A$ is also disjoint and equals $A\cup B$. Substituting $m(B\setminus A)=m(B)-m(A\cap B)$ gives the formula used in the calculation.

Here a point belongs to the intersection if it satisfies both $0\le x\le4$ and $3\le x\le8$. This leaves the points between $3$ and $4$. The common part has measure one and is subtracted once from $4+5$, where it was counted twice. This recovers the length of the interval $[0,8]$.

</div>
