## Exercise 1

For $f_n(x)=n\mathbf1_{(0,\dfrac1n)}(x)$ on $(0,1)$, compute $\lim_n\int f_n$ and $\int\lim_n f_n$, in that order.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_n&=\left(0,\frac1n\right) \\
m(A_n)&=\frac1n \\
\int_0^1f_n(x)\,dx&=n\,m(A_n) \\
&=n\cdot\frac1n \\
&=1 \\
n>\frac1x&\ \Longrightarrow\ x>\frac1n
\ \Longrightarrow\ f_n(x)=0 \\
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x)&=0 && (0<x<1) \\
\left(\lim_n\int_0^1f_n,\ \int_0^1\lim_n f_n\right)&=(1,0).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each $f_n$ is a nonnegative simple function because it is a positive multiple of the indicator of the measurable interval $A_n$. The integral of a simple function here is the height times the base length. As the index increases, the base shrinks in inverse proportion to the height, so the area stays one.

To find the pointwise limit, fix $x$ in $(0,1)$. The threshold $\dfrac1x$ is finite. For every index above that threshold the point lies outside $A_n$, so all subsequent values are zero. The integral of the limit is therefore zero. **Fatou's lemma** applies because the functions are measurable and nonnegative. The inequality is strict here, since the integral of the limit is zero and the limit inferior of the integrals is one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $f_n(x)=2n\mathbf1_{(0,\dfrac1n)}(x)$ on $(0,1)$, compute $\lim_n\int f_n$ and $\int\lim_n f_n$, in that order.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m\left(\left(0,\frac1n\right)\right)&=\frac1n \\
\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx&=2n\cdot\frac1n \\
&=2 \\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^1 f_n(x)\,dx&=2 \\
n>\frac1x&\ \Longrightarrow\ f_n(x)=0 &&(0<x<1) \\
\int_0^1\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x)\,dx&=\int_0^1 0\,dx \\
&=0 \\
\left(\lim_n\int_0^1f_n,\ \int_0^1\lim_n f_n\right)&=(2,0).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,4 0.5,4 0.5,0;0,8 0.25,8 0.25,0" etichette="0.49,4.4,n = 2;0.28,8.35,n = 4" xmin=-0.035 xmax=0.68 ymin=-0.2 ymax=9.4 titolo="In the second exercise the rectangles have height $2n$ and width $\dfrac1n$, so their area stays equal to $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The rectangles in the figure correspond to two different indices. In each case the height is $2n$ and the base has length $\dfrac1n$. The integral formula for the indicator applies because the base is a measurable interval. Thus every term has area two, and the limit of the integrals is also two.

For each fixed $x>0$, we have $\dfrac1n<x$ as soon as $n>\dfrac1x$. From that index onward the indicator vanishes at $x$, so the limit function is identically zero on the domain. If there were a common integrable majorant, the hypotheses of the **dominated convergence theorem** would hold. The integrals would then tend to zero. Since all of them equal two, no such majorant exists. This explains why the two orders of operation give different results.

</div>
