## Exercise 1

Find the measure of $([0,1]\cup[2,4])\setminus\mathbb Q$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=[0,1]\cup[2,4] \\
[0,1]\cap[2,4]&=\varnothing \\
m(E)&=(1-0)+(4-2) \\
&=3 \\
N&=E\cap\mathbb Q \\
m(N)&=0 \\
m(E\setminus\mathbb Q)
&=m(E)-m(N) \\
&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two intervals are disjoint because the right endpoint of the first is less than the left endpoint of the second. Both are measurable, so the measure of their union is the sum of their lengths. The segment between $1$ and $2$ lies outside the union, so its length is excluded.

The set $N$ of rational points in $E$ is countable and measurable, so it has measure zero. To remove the rationals, only those already in $E$ matter. Indeed, $E$ is the disjoint union of $N$ and $E\setminus\mathbb Q$. Since $m(E)$ is finite, additivity permits subtraction of $m(N)$ without taking a difference of infinities. The measure is therefore the same as that of the original set. Infinitely many points are lost in every interval of positive length, but what is removed is a countable union of null singletons.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the integral of $2\mathbf1_{[0,3]}+\mathbf1_{[2,5]}$ by identifying its level sets.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_2&=[0,2),& m(E_2)&=2 \\
E_3&=[2,3],& m(E_3)&=1 \\
E_1&=(3,5],& m(E_1)&=2 \\
f&=2\mathbf1_{E_2}+3\mathbf1_{E_3}+\mathbf1_{E_1} \\
\int_{\mathbb R} f\,dm
&=2m(E_2)+3m(E_3)+m(E_1) \\
&=2\cdot2+3\cdot1+2 \\
&=4+3+2 \\
&=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,2 2,2;2,3 3,3;3,1 5,1" punti="0,2,pieno;2,2,vuoto;2,3,pieno;3,3,pieno;3,1,vuoto;5,1,pieno" poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,2 0,2;2,0 3,0 3,3 2,3;3,0 5,0 5,1 3,1" etichette="1,2.25,f = 2;2.5,3.25,f = 3;4,1.25,f = 1" xmin=-0.35 xmax=5.5 ymin=-0.2 ymax=3.7 titolo="The three rectangles correspond to the levels of $f$ that make a positive contribution to the integral."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both sets in the question are measurable, but they overlap on $[2,3]$. On $[0,2)$ only the first indicator equals one, so $f$ is two. On $[2,3]$ both indicators equal one, so $f$ is three. On $(3,5]$ only the second indicator is nonzero, so the value is one. The value is zero outside these three sets.

The three positive level sets are disjoint because the function has a single value at each point. The endpoints $2$ and $3$ belong to level three under the endpoint conventions in the question. Apply the definition of the integral of a simple function, multiplying each level by the measure of its set. The middle segment in the graph is the shortest but has the greatest height. The areas of the three rectangles sum to nine.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $f=2\mathbf1_{[0,2]}-3\mathbf1_{[1,3]}$, compute $\int f\,dm$ and $\int |f|\,dm$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=2\mathbf1_{[0,1)}-\mathbf1_{[1,2]}-3\mathbf1_{(2,3]} \\
f^+&=2\mathbf1_{[0,1)} \\
f^-&=\mathbf1_{[1,2]}+3\mathbf1_{(2,3]} \\
\int_{\mathbb R} f^+\,dm&=2\cdot1=2 \\
\int_{\mathbb R} f^-\,dm&=1\cdot1+3\cdot1=4 \\
\int_{\mathbb R} f\,dm&=2-4=-2 \\
\int_{\mathbb R}|f|\,dm&=2+4=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The overlap of the intervals is $[1,2]$. There $f$ equals $2-3=-1$ and thus has absolute value one. On the first outer segment $[0,1)$ its value is two. On the second outer segment $(2,3]$ it is minus three. These three measurable disjoint segments all have length one. Outside their union the function vanishes.

The **positive part** $f^+$ equals two on the first segment and zero elsewhere. The **negative part** $f^-$ takes nonnegative values, one on the overlap and three on the right. Both integrals are finite because the sets are finite unions of bounded intervals and the heights are finite. Since $f=f^+-f^-$, subtracting the integrals gives minus two. Since $|f|=f^++f^-$, their sum gives six. On the overlap, evaluate the weighted sum before taking its absolute value.

</div>
