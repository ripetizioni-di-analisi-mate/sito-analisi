## Exercise 1

Compute $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty\dfrac{e^{-x}}{1+\dfrac xn}\,dx$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_n(x)&=\frac{e^{-x}}{1+\frac{x}{n}} \\
1+\frac{x}{n}&\ge1 && (x>0) \\
0\le f_n(x)&\le e^{-x} \\
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x)&=e^{-x} && (x>0) \\
\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}[-e^{-x}]_0^R \\
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}(1-e^{-R}) \\
&=1 \\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty f_n(x)\,dx
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx \\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The functions $f_n$ are continuous for $x>0$ because the denominator is always positive, so they are measurable. For fixed $x$, the ratio $\dfrac{x}{n}$ tends to zero. The numerator is independent of $n$ and the denominator tends to one, so the pointwise limit is $e^{-x}$.

For every $x>0$ and every index $n$ the denominator is at least one. The same function $e^{-x}$ therefore bounds the absolute value of every term. This majorant is integrable on the entire half-line, as can be seen by evaluating its antiderivative between zero and $R$ and then letting $R$ increase. A positive constant could bound all the functions but would have infinite integral on the half-line. Since the exponential majorant is common and integrable and a pointwise limit exists, the **dominated convergence theorem** allows the limit and integral to be interchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute $\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty\dfrac{e^{-2x}}{1+\dfrac xn}\,dx$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_n(x)&=\frac{e^{-2x}}{1+\frac{x}{n}} \\
|g_n(x)|&\le e^{-2x} && (x>0) \\
\lim_{n\to\infty}g_n(x)&=e^{-2x} && (x>0) \\
\int_0^\infty e^{-2x}\,dx
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\left[-\frac{e^{-2x}}{2}\right]_0^R \\
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\left(-\frac{e^{-2R}}{2}+\frac12\right) \\
&=\frac12 \\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_0^\infty g_n(x)\,dx
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-2x}\,dx \\
&=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator of $g_n$ is positive for every $n$ and every $x$ in the domain, so each quotient is continuous and measurable on the half-line. The inequality $1+\dfrac{x}{n}\ge1$ gives a bound uniform in $n$. For fixed $x$, the denominator tends to one, so the majorant also equals the pointwise limit. In the graph, $g_1$ and $g_3$ stay below the limiting curve.

The antiderivative of $e^{-2x}$ contains the factor $\dfrac12$ because the derivative of $-2x$ is $-2$. At the upper endpoint the exponential term tends to zero, so the remaining contribution is minus the antiderivative at zero. This proves finiteness of the integral of the majorant. The **dominated convergence theorem** applies on $(0,\infty)$ and reduces the calculation to the integral of the limit function.

</div>
