## Exercise 1

Find the transform of the signal equal to $3$ for $1<t<4$ and zero elsewhere. Also give the transform value at $s=0$.

The integral is over a finite interval. Evaluate it first for $s\ne0$ and then for $s=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(s)&=3\int_1^4 e^{-st}\,dt\\
&=3\left[-\frac{e^{-st}}s\right]_1^4\\
&=\frac{3(e^{-s}-e^{-4s})}{s},\qquad s\ne0,\\[6pt]
F(0)&=3\int_1^4 1\,dt\\
&=3(4-1)\\
&=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 1,0;1,3 4,3;4,0 5,0" segmenti="1,0,1,3;4,0,4,3" poligoni="1,0 4,0 4,3 1,3" punti="1,3,vuoto;4,3,vuoto;1,0,pieno;4,0,pieno" nomi="t,y" xmin=0 xmax=5.2 ymin=0 ymax=3.6 titolo="For $s=0$ the weight equals one, so the integral equals the area of the rectangle with width $3$ and height $3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The signal vanishes outside the interval between $1$ and $4$, so the exponential weight is integrated only over that interval. The signal is bounded with bounded support, hence the transform exists for every complex $s$. For $s\ne0$ the antiderivative is $-e^{-st}$ divided by $s$. The upper endpoint gives $-e^{-4s}$ and subtracting the lower endpoint gives $e^{-s}$. At $s=0$ the weight is one, so we integrate the constant $3$ over an interval of length $3$. Geometrically this is the area of the shaded rectangle. The numerator in the formula for $s\ne0$ vanishes at zero and its derivative there is $3(-1+4)=9$. Thus the quotient tends to $9$ and has a removable singularity. The assigned values at the pulse endpoints do not affect the integral.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f(t)=t$ for $0\le t\le1$, $f(t)=2-t$ for $1<t\le2$, and $f(t)=0$ for $t>2$. Compute $\mathcal L[f](s)$ using delayed ramps.

Express the signal as three ramps and first work in the half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(t)&=t-2H(t-1)(t-1)+H(t-2)(t-2),\\
F(s)&=\mathcal L[t](s)-2\mathcal L[H(t-1)(t-1)](s)
+\mathcal L[H(t-2)(t-2)](s)\\
&=\frac1{s^2}-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s^2}+\frac{e^{-2s}}{s^2}\\
&=\frac{1-2e^{-s}+e^{-2s}}{s^2}\\
&=\left(\frac{1-e^{-s}}s\right)^2,\qquad s\ne0,\\[4pt]
F(0)&=\int_0^1t\,dt+\int_1^2(2-t)\,dt\\
&=\frac12+\frac12\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,0 1,1 2,0 3,0" poligoni="0,0 1,1 2,0" punti="0,0,pieno;1,1,pieno;2,0,pieno" nomi="t,y" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=0 ymax=1.3 titolo="The slope changes from $1$ to $-1$ at the vertex and from $-1$ to $0$ when the signal switches off."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first ramp gives slope $1$ until time $1$. The slope then becomes $-1$, so the coefficient of the second ramp must be $-2$. At time $2$ the slope returns to zero, requiring coefficient $1$ for the third ramp. Substitution after $t=2$ gives $t-2(t-1)+(t-2)=0$, so the sum also reproduces the zero segment. The three ramp transforms converge absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, where linearity and the delay theorem apply. Their combination has bounded support, making its transform entire. The final formula agrees with the transform in the half-plane and extends by analytic uniqueness to $s\ne0$. At zero we obtain the area of the triangle, the sum of the areas under the two linear pieces. Since $\dfrac{1-e^{-s}}s$ tends to one at zero, the formula has the same extension.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $f$ have period $2$, taking the value one on $[0,1)$ and zero on $[1,2)$. Compute $\mathcal L[f](s)$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$.

In the period indexed by $k$, set $u=t-2k$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_k(s)&=\int_{2k}^{2k+1}e^{-st}\,dt\\
&=e^{-2ks}\int_0^1e^{-su}\,du\\
&=e^{-2ks}\left[-\frac{e^{-su}}s\right]_0^1\\
&=e^{-2ks}\frac{1-e^{-s}}s,\\[4pt]
F(s)&=\sum_{k=0}^\infty I_k(s)\\
&=\frac{1-e^{-s}}s\cdot\frac1{1-e^{-2s}}\\
&=\frac1{s(1+e^{-s})},\qquad \operatorname{Re}s>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="0,1 1,1;1,0 2,0;2,1 3,1;3,0 4,0;4,1 5,1;5,0 6,0" segmenti="1,0,1,1;2,0,2,1;3,0,3,1;4,0,4,1;5,0,5,1" nomi="t,y" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=1.3 titolo="The intervals during which the signal is on have the same shape. In the integral the first three contributions have weights $1$, $e^{-2s}$, and $e^{-4s}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Only the first unit of each period contributes, since the signal vanishes in the second. Substitution gives the same integral over $[0,1]$, weighted by $e^{-2ks}$. For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ the geometric ratio has modulus $e^{-2\operatorname{Re}s}<1$. The sum of the absolute integrals converges by the same geometric bound, so the individual contributions may be summed. The denominator is $1-e^{-2s}$ because the switch-on times are two units apart. It factors as $(1-e^{-s})(1+e^{-s})$. In the given half-plane $|e^{-s}|<1$, so $1-e^{-s}$ is nonzero and cancels. The graph distinguishes the on-time of one from the period of two. The on-time determines the first-period integral, while the period sets the geometric ratio.

</div>
