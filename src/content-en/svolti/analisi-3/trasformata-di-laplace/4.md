## Exercise 1

Compute the transform of $H(t-1)(t-1)^2$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$.

Let $H$ be the unit step and make a substitution beginning at $t=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(s)&=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}H(t-1)(t-1)^2\,dt\\
&=\int_1^\infty e^{-st}(t-1)^2\,dt\\
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-s(u+1)}u^2\,du,\qquad u=t-1,\\
&=e^{-s}\int_0^\infty e^{-su}u^2\,du\\
&=e^{-s}\frac2{s^3}\\
&=\frac{2e^{-s}}{s^3},\qquad \operatorname{Re}s>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The step vanishes before $t=1$, so the integral begins at the switch-on time. The value of the step at $t=1$ does not affect the integral because a single point has measure zero. With $u=t-1$ the lower limit becomes zero, the square becomes $u^2$, and the differential is unchanged. Substituting $t=u+1$ into the weight brings the constant factor $e^{-s}$ outside the integral. The remaining integral is the transform of the power $u^2$, valid because $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ and the weighted polynomial is absolutely integrable. The delay factor appears only outside this integral. For example, at $s=1$ the weighted area of the delayed signal is $2e^{-1}$, compared with $2$ without delay.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the transform of $H(t-2)(t-2)^2$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$.

The function is a quadratic power delayed by two time units.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(s)&=\int_2^\infty e^{-st}(t-2)^2\,dt\\
&=e^{-2s}\int_0^\infty e^{-su}u^2\,du,\qquad u=t-2,\\
\int_0^\infty e^{-su}u^2\,du
&=\left[-\frac{u^2e^{-su}}s\right]_0^\infty+\frac2s\int_0^\infty ue^{-su}\,du\\
&=\frac2s\cdot\frac1{s^2}\\
&=\frac2{s^3},\\
F(s)&=\frac{2e^{-2s}}{s^3},\qquad\operatorname{Re}s>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lower endpoint $t=2$ is determined by the signal's support. The substitution $u=t-2$ moves the origin to the switch-on time, so the polynomial also vanishes at the new lower endpoint. Splitting the weight as $e^{-2s}e^{-su}$ brings the delay outside the integral. In integration by parts the lower term is zero, while the term at infinity vanishes because $u^2e^{-(\operatorname{Re}s)u}$ tends to zero. The remaining integral is that of the first power, already evaluated in the same half-plane. The active signal is exactly $u^2$. If it were $H(t-2)t^2$ instead, the substitution would give $(u+2)^2=u^2+4u+4$, producing two additional terms in the transform with the same delay factor. The difference is already apparent in the polynomial value at switch-on.

</div>
