## Exercise 1

Compute $\mathcal L[t e^{-t}](s)$ on its half-plane of absolute convergence.

For $\operatorname{Re}(s+1)>0$, use the calculation for $\mathcal L[t]$ with parameter $s+1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|te^{-t}e^{-st}|&=t e^{-(1+\operatorname{Re}s)t},\\
\mathcal L[te^{-t}](s)&=\int_0^\infty t e^{-t} e^{-st}\,dt\\
&=\int_0^\infty t e^{-(s+1)t}\,dt\\
&=\left.\mathcal L[t](z)\right|_{z=s+1}\\
&=\frac1{(s+1)^2},\qquad \operatorname{Re}s>-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{nomi="σ,ω" uguale=1 xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=2 asintoti="x=-1" poligoni="-1,-2 3,-2 3,2 -1,2" punti="-1,0,vuoto;0,0,pieno" titolo="The weight $e^{-t}$ moves the boundary of convergence from $\operatorname{Re}s=0$ to $\operatorname{Re}s=-1$. The point $s=0$ now lies in the half-plane of convergence."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the two exponentials adds their exponents, so the parameter in the transform of $t$ is $z=s+1$. The function $t$ is continuous on the half-line, and its integral with weight $e^{-zt}$ converges absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}z>0$. Substitution of $z=s+1$ gives the condition $\operatorname{Re}s>-1$. At $s=0$ the combined weight is $e^{-t}$ and the integral equals $1$, as the formula predicts. On the line $\operatorname{Re}s=-1$ the weight has unit modulus, leaving the integral of $t$ for the absolute integral. To the left of the line the weight grows in modulus, so comparison with $t$ again shows failure of absolute convergence. For example, the rational formula gives $1$ even at $s=-2$, where the absolute integral diverges. The convergence condition is therefore part of the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute $\mathcal L[t e^{-2t}](s)$ on its half-plane of absolute convergence.

The condition on the boundary term in integration by parts is $\operatorname{Re}(s+2)>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_R(s)&=\int_0^R te^{-(s+2)t}\,dt\\
&=\left[-\frac{te^{-(s+2)t}}{s+2}\right]_0^R
+\frac1{s+2}\int_0^R e^{-(s+2)t}\,dt\\
&=-\frac{Re^{-(s+2)R}}{s+2}+\frac{1-e^{-(s+2)R}}{(s+2)^2},\\
\mathcal L[te^{-2t}](s)&=\lim_{R\to+\infty} I_R(s)\\
&=\frac1{(s+2)^2},\qquad \operatorname{Re}s>-2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The effective weight is $e^{-(s+2)t}$, so we may integrate by parts directly on a finite interval. Differentiating $t$ leaves an integral of the exponential alone. Its antiderivative gives a first factor $\dfrac1{s+2}$ in the remaining term and another in evaluating the integral. Since $s+2$ has positive real part, both terms containing $e^{-(s+2)R}$ tend to zero, including the one multiplied by $R$. For example, the real value $s=-1$ meets the condition and gives the integral of $te^{-t}$. At $s=-2$ the exponential factor is one, leaving the integral of $t$. More generally, for $\operatorname{Re}s\le-2$ the modulus is at least $t$. Thus the boundary of the half-plane of absolute convergence is exactly $\operatorname{Re}s=-2$.

</div>
