## Exercise 1

Solve $y''+y=0$ with $y(0)=1$ and $y'(0)=0$ for $t\ge0$.

Transform both terms of the equation using both initial conditions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L[y''](s)+Y(s)&=0,\\
s^2Y(s)-sy(0)-y'(0)+Y(s)&=0,\\
(s^2+1)Y(s)&=s,\\
Y(s)&=\frac{s}{s^2+1},\\
y(t)&=\cos t,\qquad t\ge0.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(x);cos(2*x)" xmin=0 xmax=6.4 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.2 nomi="t,y" legenda="\cos t;\cos(2t)" titolo="Both solutions start at $1$ with a horizontal tangent. The second completes two periods in the time the first takes to complete one."}

<div class="spiegazione">

For solutions of the linear oscillator, $y,y'$ are locally absolutely continuous and the derivatives are of exponential order. The second derivative transform formula therefore applies on a half-plane sufficiently far to the right. The initial value $y(0)=1$ contributes $-s$, while zero initial velocity eliminates the constant term. After solving for $Y$, the denominator $s^2+1$ corresponds to unit angular frequency. The cosine transform has numerator $s$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, giving the quotient found. To check, the cosine has derivative $-\sin t$ and second derivative $-\cos t$. This verifies the differential equation, and at zero the cosine is $1$ and the sine $0$. The horizontal tangent in the graph corresponds to the assigned initial velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $y''+4y=0$ with $y(0)=1$ and $y'(0)=0$ for $t\ge0$.

The coefficient $4$ determines the angular frequency $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^2Y(s)-sy(0)-y'(0)+4Y(s)&=0,\\
(s^2+4)Y(s)&=s,\\
Y(s)&=\frac{s}{(s-2i)(s+2i)}\\
&=\frac12\left(\frac1{s-2i}+\frac1{s+2i}\right),\\[4pt]
y(t)&=\frac12\bigl(e^{2it}+e^{-2it}\bigr)\\
&=\cos(2t),\qquad t\ge0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation has constant coefficients and finite initial data, so the solution is regular and of exponential order and its second derivative can be transformed. Moving $sy(0)$ to the right and using the zero initial velocity leaves the numerator unchanged. The denominator factors as $s-2i$ and $s+2i$. To check the decomposition, put the sum over a common denominator. The imaginary terms in the numerator cancel, and taking half the sum gives $s$. For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$ the inverse transforms of both terms are oscillating exponentials. They are conjugates for real $t$, so their average is the cosine. Its derivative at zero vanishes and its second derivative equals $-4\cos(2t)$ for every $t$, verifying the equation and initial conditions. Doubling the angular frequency halves the period relative to the unit-coefficient case.

</div>
