## Exercise 1

For $f(z)=z^2$, find the local scale factor at $z_0=2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(z)&=2z\\[6pt]
f'(2)&=4\\[6pt]
f(2+h)-f(2)&=(2+h)^2-4\\[6pt]
&=4h+h^2\\[6pt]
\frac{|f(2+h)-f(2)|}{|h|}&=|4+h| \qquad(h\ne0)\\[6pt]
\lim_{h\to0}|4+h|&=4
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is polynomial and hence holomorphic. Its derivative at $2$ is $4$ and is nonzero, so the **local conformality criterion** applies. The differential acts by multiplication by the complex number $4$. Complex multiplication scales vector lengths by the modulus of the multiplier, so the required factor is $4$. Here the multiplier is positive real, making the rotation angle zero.

The increment calculation also explains the scale factor. The term $4h$ gives the linear change, while $h^2$ has modulus $|h|^2$. Dividing the length of the increment by $|h|$ gives $|4+h|$, which tends to $4$ by continuity of the modulus. The estimate holds for complex increments in every direction. At a finite distance the quadratic term can alter the ratio, so $4$ describes the scale in the local limit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $f(z)=z^3$, find the local scale factor at $z_0=2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(z)&=3z^2\\[6pt]
f'(2)&=3\cdot4\\[6pt]
&=12\\[6pt]
f(2+h)-f(2)&=(2+h)^3-8\\[6pt]
&=12h+6h^2+h^3\\[6pt]
\frac{|f(2+h)-f(2)|}{|h|}&=|12+6h+h^2| \qquad(h\ne0)\\[6pt]
\lim_{h\to0}|12+6h+h^2|&=12
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the power rule, $f'(z)=3z^2$ throughout $\mathbb C$. The derivative at $2$ is positive real and nonzero, so the differential is a scaling by $12$ with zero rotation angle. The scale factor applies to increments based at $f(2)=8$. This is why the direct calculation subtracts $8$ from the value of $f(2+h)$.

Expanding the cube of $2+h$ gives the linear contribution $12h$. The higher-degree terms are $6h^2+h^3$, so division by $h$ leaves a remainder $6h+h^2$ that tends to zero. The estimate $|6h+h^2|\le6|h|+|h|^2$ proves convergence in every direction. By continuity of the modulus, the ratio of lengths converges as well. The scale factor is therefore independent of the direction of the increment, as expected for the differential of a holomorphic function at a regular point.

</div>
