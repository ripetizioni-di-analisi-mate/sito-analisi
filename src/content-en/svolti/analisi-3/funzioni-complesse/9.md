## Exercise 1

For $z=-2i$, find the logarithm with argument in $(-\pi,\pi)$ and with argument in $(0,2\pi)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|-2i|&=2\\[6pt]
-2i&=2e^{-\frac{\pi i}{2}}\\[6pt]
&=2e^{\frac{3\pi i}{2}}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Log}(-2i)&=\log 2-\frac{\pi i}{2}\\[6pt]
L(-2i)&=\log 2+\frac{3\pi i}{2}\\[6pt]
L(-2i)-\operatorname{Log}(-2i)&=2\pi i
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.8 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.6 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,4.71238898038469" vettori="0,0,0,-2" punti="0,-2,pieno,−2i" titolo="The long arc gives the positive argument. The short clockwise arc gives the principal argument."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $-2i$ lies on the negative imaginary axis at distance $2$ from the origin. For any branch the real part of the logarithm is $\log|z|$, so both real parts equal $\log 2$. The imaginary part depends on the choice of argument. The interval $(-\pi,\pi)$ contains $-\dfrac\pi2$, which corresponds to a clockwise quarter turn from the positive real axis. The interval $(0,2\pi)$ contains $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, which corresponds to three quarters of a turn counterclockwise.

The point belongs to both domains because it is nonzero and lies off both cuts along the real half-axes. The two rotations lead to the same point on the circle and differ by exactly $2\pi$. The logarithms therefore differ by $2\pi i$. By periodicity of the exponential, applying $\exp$ to either value gives the same number $-2i$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the principal square root of $-3+4i$ and determine the other square root.

$$
\begin{aligned}
w^2&=(a+ib)^2\\[6pt]
&=a^2-b^2+2abi\\[6pt]
a^2-b^2&=-3, \qquad ab=2\\[6pt]
a^2+b^2&=|w^2|\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{(-3)^2+4^2}\\[6pt]
&=5\\[6pt]
a^2&=\frac{5-3}{2}=1\\[6pt]
b^2&=\frac{5+3}{2}=4\\[6pt]
w&\in\{1+2i,-1-2i\}\\[6pt]
\sqrt{-3+4i}\ \text{principal}&=1+2i
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" xmin=-2.4 xmax=2.4 ymin=-2.8 ymax=2.8 vettori="0,0,1,2;0,0,-1,-2" punti="1,2,pieno,1+2i;-1,-2,pieno,−1−2i" titolo="The two roots are negatives of each other. The principal root lies in the right half-plane."}

<div class="spiegazione">

If $w=a+ib$ is a square root, equating the squares requires their real parts to equal $-3$ and their imaginary parts to equal $4$. This determines the difference of the squares of $a$ and $b$ together with their product. Moreover, the modulus is multiplicative, so $|w^2|=|w|^2=a^2+b^2$. Since $|-3+4i|=5$, we know the sum of the squares. Adding and subtracting the sum and difference equations gives each square separately.

The condition $ab=2$ requires the two coordinates to have the same sign. This leaves the pairs $(1,2)$ and $(-1,-2)$. The original number is in the second quadrant, so its principal argument lies between $\dfrac\pi2$ and $\pi$. Halving the angle gives a value between $\dfrac\pi4$ and $\dfrac\pi2$, where both coordinates are positive. The principal square root is therefore $1+2i$. The other root is its negative and has the same square because squaring eliminates the sign.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $z=e^{-\dfrac{2\pi i}{3}}$ and $w=e^{-\dfrac{3\pi i}{4}}$. Compare $\operatorname{Log}(zw)$ with $\operatorname{Log}z+\operatorname{Log}w$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Arg}z+\operatorname{Arg}w
&=-\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{3\pi}{4}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{8\pi}{12}-\frac{9\pi}{12}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{17\pi}{12}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Arg}(zw)&=-\frac{17\pi}{12}+2\pi\\[6pt]
&=\frac{7\pi}{12}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Log}z+\operatorname{Log}w&=-\frac{17\pi i}{12}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Log}(zw)&=\frac{7\pi i}{12}\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Log}(zw)-\operatorname{Log}z-\operatorname{Log}w&=2\pi i
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" xmin=-1.75 xmax=1.55 ymin=-1.55 ymax=1.65 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.283185307179586" punti="-0.5,-0.8660254,pieno,z;-0.7071068,-0.7071068,pieno,w;-0.258819,0.9659258,pieno,zw" titolo="Both factors lie in the third quadrant. Reducing the argument of their product places it in the second quadrant."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The given angles for $z$ and $w$ both belong to $(-\pi,\pi)$, so they are the principal arguments of the factors. Multiplying the two complex exponentials adds the exponents and preserves modulus one. Their angles sum to $-\dfrac{17\pi}{12}$, which is less than $-\pi$. This angle represents the product because the exponential is periodic. For the principal logarithm, however, we need the representative between $-\pi$ and $\pi$.

Adding $2\pi$ gives $\dfrac{7\pi}{12}$, strictly inside the interval. Thus the product belongs to the domain of the principal logarithm, as do both factors. Since the real logarithm of one is zero, all three logarithms are purely imaginary. Their difference accounts exactly for the turn added when choosing the argument of the product. Thus the two sides have the same exponential and differ by $2\pi i$.

</div>
