## Exercise 1

Compute $f'(1)$ for $f(z)=z^2+z$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(1+h)-f(1)&=(1+h)^2+(1+h)-2\\[6pt]
&=1+2h+h^2+1+h-2\\[6pt]
&=3h+h^2\\[6pt]
\frac{f(1+h)-f(1)}{h}&=3+h \qquad(h\ne0)\\[6pt]
f'(1)&=\lim_{h\to0}(3+h)\\[6pt]
&=3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The complex derivative at $1$ is the limit of the difference quotient for complex increments $h$. The polynomial has domain all of $\mathbb C$, so every increment is allowed, and the quotient is defined for $h\ne0$. In the difference $f(1+h)-f(1)$ the constant terms cancel because both equal $f(1)=2$. The square contributes $2h+h^2$ and the linear term adds $h$, so the difference is divisible by $h$.

After division, the difference from $3$ is exactly $h$, whose modulus tends to zero in every direction. Indeed, the distance between the quotient and $3$ is $|h|$, so a radius smaller than $\varepsilon$ ensures an error smaller than $\varepsilon$. Thus the complex limit exists and equals $3$. The power rule gives the same result because nonnegative integer powers are holomorphic throughout the plane. The derivative of the polynomial is $2z+1$, which at $z=1$ equals $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute $f'(1)$ for $f(z)=z^3+2z$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(1+h)-f(1)&=(1+h)^3+2(1+h)-3\\[6pt]
&=1+3h+3h^2+h^3+2+2h-3\\[6pt]
&=5h+3h^2+h^3\\[6pt]
\frac{f(1+h)-f(1)}{h}&=5+3h+h^2 \qquad(h\ne0)\\[6pt]
\left|3h+h^2\right|&\le 3|h|+|h|^2\\[6pt]
f'(1)&=5
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Expanding the cube gives a polynomial identity valid for every complex $h$. The linear term in the cube is $3h$, since in multiplying the three factors we choose $h$ from exactly one factor. The term $2(1+h)$ contributes $2h$, and subtracting $f(1)$ cancels the terms independent of $h$. Thus the coefficient of $h$ in the increment is $5$.

After division by $h$, the terms of degrees two and three become $3h$ and $h^2$. The bound on their moduli applies in every direction of approach to zero, so both tend to zero in the difference quotient. The quotient tends to the same value even if the direction varies with $h$. The power rule also gives $f'(z)=3z^2+2$ throughout the plane because $f$ is a polynomial. At the given point the cubic term contributes $3$ and the linear term contributes $2$.

</div>
