## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\dfrac{e^z}{z^2},0\right)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
e^z&=1+z+\frac{z^2}{2}+O(z^3)\\[6pt]
f(z)&=\frac{e^z}{z^2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac1{z^2}+\frac1z+\frac12+O(z)\\[6pt]
z^2 f(z)&=e^z\\[6pt]
\lim_{z\to0} z^2 f(z)&=1\ne0\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Res}(f,0)&=1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $e^z$ is entire and $e^0=1$. Thus the product $z^2f(z)$ extends holomorphically to zero with a nonzero value. This makes zero a **pole of order two**. The exponential Taylor series converges throughout the plane, so dividing it by $z^2$ gives a Laurent expansion valid in a punctured neighborhood of the origin.

Dividing by $z^2$ reduces every exponent by two. The constant term becomes $z^{-2}$ and determines the pole order. The linear term becomes $z^{-1}$ and its coefficient is the residue. The quadratic and higher terms become regular at zero. The pole order and residue therefore come from different coefficients of the same series.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\operatorname{Res}\!\left(\dfrac{e^{2z}}{z^2},0\right)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(z)&=\frac{e^{2z}}{z^2}\\[6pt]
H(z)&=z^2 f(z)\\[6pt]
&=e^{2z}\\[6pt]
H(0)&=1\ne0\\[6pt]
H'(z)&=2e^{2z}\\[6pt]
H'(0)&=2e^0\\[6pt]
&=2\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Res}(f,0)&=H'(0)\\[6pt]
&=2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying $f$ by $z^2$ gives a function $H$ that extends to the whole plane because it agrees with the exponential $e^{2z}$. Its extension has value $1$ at zero. This makes the singularity a double pole, so the residue formula for a **pole of order two** applies.

To derive the formula, write the linear Taylor term of $H$ as $H'(0)z$. In the expansion of $f$ this term is divided by $z^2$, so the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ is precisely $H'(0)$. The constant term $H(0)$ instead becomes the coefficient of $z^{-2}$. The factor $2$ comes from differentiating the exponent $2z$. At zero the exponential factor equals one.

</div>
