## Exercise 1

Find the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the expansion of $\dfrac1{z(z-2)}$ for $0<|z|<2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
z-2&=-2\left(1-\frac z2\right)\\[6pt]
\left|\frac z2\right|&<1 \qquad (0<|z|<2)\\[6pt]
\frac1{z(z-2)}
&=-\frac1{2z}\cdot\frac1{1-\frac z2}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac1{2z}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\left(\frac z2\right)^n\\[6pt]
&=-\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^{n-1}}{2^{n+1}}\\[6pt]
n-1=-1&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n=0\\[6pt]
c_{-1}&=-\frac1{2^{0+1}}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac12
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the given punctured disk the ratio $\dfrac z2$ has modulus less than one, so the **geometric series** for $\left(1-\dfrac z2\right)^{-1}$ converges. Factoring $-2$ out of $z-2$ determines the sign and the factor $\dfrac12$. The factor $z^{-1}$ from the first term of the denominator also remains.

Multiplication by $z^{-1}$ lowers every exponent in the geometric series by one. The exponent $-1$ therefore comes from index zero, the constant term in the geometric series. Indices one and above give nonnegative exponents. The condition $z\ne0$ justifies the division throughout the annulus, and $z=2$ lies on the outer boundary, where the geometric ratio has modulus one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the expansion of $\dfrac1{z(z-3)}$ for $0<|z|<3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{z(z-3)}&=\frac A z+\frac B{z-3}\\[6pt]
1&=A(z-3)+Bz\\[6pt]
1&=-3A\qquad (z=0)\\[6pt]
1&=3B\qquad (z=3)\\[6pt]
A&=-\frac13\\[6pt]
B&=\frac13
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{z(z-3)}
&=-\frac1{3z}+\frac1{3(z-3)}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac1{3z}-\frac19\sum_{n=0}^\infty
\left(\frac z3\right)^n\\[6pt]
c_{-1}&=-\frac13
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Partial fractions separate the factor vanishing at zero from the one vanishing at three. Multiplying the identity by $z(z-3)$ gives a polynomial identity. We can substitute zero and three into this identity even though both are excluded from the original rational function.

The second partial fraction is holomorphic on $|z|<3$. Since $\left|\dfrac z3\right|<1$, its geometric expansion contains only nonnegative powers. Thus only the first fraction contributes to the coefficient of $z^{-1}$. The radius three equals the distance from the expansion center to the other pole and bounds the disk on which the second fraction has this expansion.

</div>
