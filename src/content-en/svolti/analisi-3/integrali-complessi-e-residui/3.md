## Exercise 1

Find the residue of $f(z)=\dfrac1{z^2+1}$ at $z=i$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(z)&=z^2+1\\[6pt]
&=(z-i)(z+i)\\[6pt]
h'(z)&=2z\\[6pt]
h'(i)&=2i\ne0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}(f,i)&=\lim_{z\to i}(z-i)f(z)\\[6pt]
&=\lim_{z\to i}\frac1{z+i}\\[6pt]
&=\frac1{2i}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac i2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $h$ is a polynomial vanishing at $i$. Its derivative there is $2i$, so the zero is simple. The numerator equals $1$ and does not vanish at $i$. The quotient therefore has a **simple pole** there. The other root of the denominator, $-i$, is distinct from $i$, so the singularity is isolated.

To find the residue, multiply by $z-i$. Factoring the denominator allows cancellation of this factor on a punctured disk around $i$. The remaining factor $z+i$ has value $2i$ at the center, so its reciprocal extends holomorphically and the limit follows by substitution. Since $i^2=-1$, the reciprocal of $2i$ can be written as $-\dfrac i2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the residue of $f(z)=\dfrac1{z^2+4}$ at $z=2i$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(z)&=1\\[6pt]
h(z)&=z^2+4\\[6pt]
h(2i)&=(2i)^2+4\\[6pt]
&=0\\[6pt]
h'(2i)&=2(2i)\\[6pt]
&=4i
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}(f,2i)&=\frac{g(2i)}{h'(2i)}\\[6pt]
&=\frac1{4i}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac i4
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The functions $g$ and $h$ are holomorphic throughout the complex plane. The denominator has a simple zero at $2i$ because $h(2i)=0$ and $h'(2i)=4i\ne0$. Since $g(2i)=1$, the quotient has a simple pole. Thus its residue is the numerator divided by the derivative of the denominator, evaluated at the pole.

This formula follows from the local factorization $h(z)=(z-2i)(z+2i)$. The term $h'(2i)$ is the value of the second factor at $2i$, so the calculation agrees with canceling the factor $z-2i$ and taking the limit. The opposite root $-2i$ is four units from the point under consideration. Its contribution is calculated separately if the integration contour encloses that pole too.

</div>
