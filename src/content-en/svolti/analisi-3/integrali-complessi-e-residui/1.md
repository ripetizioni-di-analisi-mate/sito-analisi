## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\int_\gamma z\,dz$, where $\gamma$ is the segment directed from $0$ to $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(z)&=\frac{z^2}{2}\\[6pt]
F'(z)&=z\\[6pt]
F(0)&=\frac{0^2}{2}\\[6pt]
&=0\\[6pt]
F(2)&=\frac{2^2}{2}\\[6pt]
&=2
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\gamma z\,dz&=F(2)-F(0)\\[6pt]
&=2-0\\[6pt]
&=2
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" xmin=-0.55 xmax=2.65 ymin=-1 ymax=1 linee="0,0 2,0" vettori="0.70,0,1.35,0" punti="0,0,pieno,0;2,0,pieno,2" titolo="The segment is traversed from $0$ toward $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The segment runs from $0$ to $2$, so in the formula for the integral of a derivative the value at $2$ comes first. The polynomial $F$ is defined on the entire complex plane and its derivative equals the integrand. Thus $F$ is an antiderivative on an open set containing the entire segment, as required by the fundamental theorem for contour integrals.

We can also parametrize the segment by $\gamma(t)=2t$ for $0\le t\le1$. By the chain rule the product $\gamma(t)\gamma'(t)$ is the derivative of $F(\gamma(t))$, so integration with respect to $t$ gives the difference of the endpoint values. Every piecewise regular path with the same endpoints would give the same difference because the antiderivative is defined throughout $\mathbb C$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\int_\gamma z^{2}\,dz$, where $\gamma$ is the segment directed from $0$ to $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma(t)&=2t,\quad 0\le t\le1\\[6pt]
\gamma'(t)&=2\\[6pt]
\gamma(t)^2\gamma'(t)&=(2t)^2\cdot2\\[6pt]
&=8t^2
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\gamma z^2\,dz&=\int_0^1 8t^2\,dt\\[6pt]
&=8\left[\frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^1\\[6pt]
&=8\left(\frac13-0\right)\\[6pt]
&=\frac83
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parametrization $\gamma(t)=2t$ reaches $0$ and $2$ in that order and is regular on the entire interval. The integrand is continuous on the segment, so the definition of the complex line integral applies. Substituting $z=\gamma(t)$ gives the square $(2t)^2$. The factor $\gamma'(t)=2$ accounts for the rate at which the point moves along the segment. Their product is $8t^2$.

The integral in the parameter is real because both the segment and the integrand evaluated along it are real-valued. The factor $8$ incorporates the scaling of the segment and the square in the integrand. Reversing orientation interchanges the endpoints and gives $-\dfrac83$. The direct calculation agrees with the endpoint difference for the entire antiderivative $\dfrac{z^3}{3}$.

</div>
