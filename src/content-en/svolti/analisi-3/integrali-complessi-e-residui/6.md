## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{dx}{x^2+1}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}(f,i)
&=\lim_{z\to i}\frac{z-i}{(z-i)(z+i)}\\[6pt]
&=\frac1{2i}\\[6pt]
|z^2+1|&\ge R^2-1\qquad (|z|=R,\ R>1)\\[6pt]
\left|\int_{\Gamma_R}f(z)\,dz\right|&\le\frac{\pi R}{R^2-1}
\underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{-R}^R\frac{dx}{x^2+1}
&=2\pi i\frac1{2i}-\int_{\Gamma_R}f(z)\,dz\\[6pt]
\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{dx}{x^2+1}
&=2\pi i\frac1{2i}\\[6pt]
&=\pi
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Consider $f(z)=(z^2+1)^{-1}$ and close the segment from $-R$ to $R$ with the upper semicircle $\Gamma_R$ directed from $R$ to $-R$. This gives a positively oriented contour. For $R>1$ the pole $i$ is inside and $-i$ outside. Both are simple and neither lies on the contour, so the **residue theorem** applies.

The reverse triangle inequality gives $|z^2+1|\ge|z|^2-1$ on the arc. Its length is $\pi R$, so the modulus of the integral over $\Gamma_R$ is bounded by that length times $\dfrac1{R^2-1}$. The product tends to zero. The real integrand is continuous and its modulus decays like $x^{-2}$ at infinity, so the improper integral converges absolutely. Its value over the entire line therefore equals the limit of the integrals on symmetric segments.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{dx}{x^2+4}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^2+4&=(z-2i)(z+2i)\\[6pt]
\operatorname{Res}(f,2i)
&=\left.\frac1{z+2i}\right|_{z=2i}\\[6pt]
&=\frac1{4i}\\[6pt]
\left|\int_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz\right|
&\le \frac{\pi R}{R^2-4}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{\pi}{R-\frac4R}
\underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{-R}^R\frac{dx}{x^2+4}
&=2\pi i\frac1{4i}-\int_{\Gamma_R} f(z)\,dz\\[6pt]
\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{dx}{x^2+4}
&=2\pi i\frac1{4i}\\[6pt]
&=\frac\pi2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $f(z)=(z^2+4)^{-1}$ has poles at $2i$ and $-2i$. Choose $R>2$ so that the upper semicircle and real segment form a positively oriented contour enclosing only $2i$. The derivative of the denominator at $2i$ is $4i$, so the pole is simple. Its residue is found by evaluating the reciprocal of the factor $z+2i$.

On $\Gamma_R$ the denominator has modulus at least $R^2-4$ by the reverse triangle inequality. Multiplying the reciprocal of this lower bound by the length $\pi R$ gives an upper bound for the arc integral. The denominator of the bound grows quadratically, so the contribution from the arc tends to zero. On the real line the denominator is positive, and the integrand is integrable at infinity because it is of order $x^{-2}$. The limit over the segments is therefore the value of the improper integral.

</div>
