## Exercise 1

Find the transform of $f(x)=e^{-x^2}$.

Write $G$ for the transform of $g(x)=e^{-x^2}$. The derivative of $g$ is $-2xg(x)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
i\xi G(\xi)&=\widehat {g'}(\xi)\\
&=-2\widehat{(xg)}(\xi)\\
&=-2iG'(\xi),\\
G'(\xi)+\frac{\xi}{2}G(\xi)&=0,\\
\frac d{d\xi}\bigl(e^{\frac{\xi^2}{4}}G(\xi)\bigr)&=0,\\
G(\xi)&=G(0)e^{-\frac{\xi^2}4}\\
&=\sqrt\pi\,e^{-\frac{\xi^2}4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Gaussian and its products with polynomials decay rapidly. In particular $g$, $g'$, and $xg$ are integrable, and $g$ tends to zero at both ends of the line. Thus we can both transform the derivative and differentiate under the integral with respect to frequency. The sign in the second operation follows from the kernel, whose derivative with respect to $\xi$ is $-ix$ times the kernel itself. The relation $g'=-2xg$ then gives a differential equation for $G$. The integrating factor $e^{\frac{\xi^2}4}$ makes the left-hand side the derivative of a product, so that product is constant. At zero frequency the Gaussian integral over the line equals $\sqrt\pi$. This fixes the constant without dividing by $G$. The transform is a Gaussian in frequency with quadratic coefficient $\dfrac14$ in the exponent.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the transform of $f(x)=e^{-2x^2}$.

Write $f(x)=g(\sqrt2\,x)$ with $g(x)=e^{-x^2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat f(\xi)&=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-2x^2}e^{-i\xi x}\,dx\\
&=\frac1{\sqrt2}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-t^2}e^{-i\frac{\xi}{\sqrt2}t}\,dt,\qquad t=\sqrt2\,x,\\
&=\frac1{\sqrt2}\widehat g\left(\frac{\xi}{\sqrt2}\right)\\
&=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt2}e^{-\frac{\left(\frac{\xi}{\sqrt2}\right)^2}{4}}\\
&=\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\,e^{-\frac{\xi^2}8}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The transform of $g$ is known, and evaluating $g$ at $\sqrt2\,x$ gives the function in question. Its Gaussian decay parameter is positive, so the integral is finite and the substitution is valid on the entire line. The differential $\dfrac{dt}{\sqrt2}$ accounts for the coefficient outside the integral. The frequency in the kernel is $\dfrac{\xi}{\sqrt2}$ because $x=\dfrac t{\sqrt2}$. Substituting this frequency into the Gaussian formula gives a further factor $\dfrac12$ in the exponent when the argument is squared. Thus the decay rate changes from $\dfrac14$ to $\dfrac18$ and the value at zero from $\sqrt\pi$ to $\sqrt{\dfrac\pi2}$. The original Gaussian is narrower, so it has a smaller integral. In frequency the smaller quadratic coefficient instead gives slower decay.

</div>
