## Exercise 1

For $g=\mathbf1_{[0,1]}$, compute $(g*g)(1)$.

Write down the conditions imposed by the two factors in the integrand.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(g*g)(1)
&=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(1-y)\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(y)\,dy,\\
0\le1-y\le1&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\le y\le1,\\
(g*g)(1)&=\int_{[0,1]\cap[0,1]}1\,dy\\
&=\int_0^1 1\,dy\\
&=\left[y\right]_0^1\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The indicator is bounded with bounded support, so it is integrable. Here the convolution integral exists at every point because the product is bounded and vanishes outside the support of the second factor. The factor $g(y)$ restricts $y$ to $[0,1]$. The first factor also requires $1-y$ to lie in $[0,1]$. Subtracting one on both sides and changing signs gives $0\le y\le1$ again. The two conditions agree, so the integrand is one on their common interval. The integral of one is the length of that interval. Its largest possible value is one, since at every horizontal coordinate the factor $g(y)$ limits the overlap to an interval of length one. At $x=1$ the intervals coincide, so the maximum is attained.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $g=\mathbf1_{[0,2]}$, compute $(g*g)(2)$.

First consider the overlap as $x$ varies.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0\le x-y\le2
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x-2\le y\le x,\\
(g*g)(x)&=\bigl|[x-2,x]\cap[0,2]\bigr|\\
&=\max\bigl(0,\min(x,2)-\max(x-2,0)\bigr),\\[4pt]
(g*g)(2)&=\max\bigl(0,\min(2,2)-\max(0,0)\bigr)\\
&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument $x-y$ of the first indicator must lie between zero and two. Solving for $y$ gives the moving interval $[x-2,x]$. The second indicator requires $y$ to lie in the fixed interval $[0,2]$. The integrand is one exactly where both conditions hold, so the convolution is the length of the intersection. The left endpoint of the intersection is the larger of the two left endpoints, and the right endpoint is the smaller of the two right endpoints. Taking the positive part of their difference also gives zero for disjoint intervals. At $x=2$ the moving endpoints are zero and two and the overlap is complete. Before this point the length grows from zero to two. After the center it decreases to zero at $x=4$. The graph shows these two linear pieces and the maximum at the required point.

</div>
