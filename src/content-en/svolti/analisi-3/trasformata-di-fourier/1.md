## Exercise 1

Using $\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R} f(x)e^{-i\xi x}\,dx$, find the transform of $f(x)=e^{-|x|}$.

Split the integral where the formula for $|x|$ changes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat f(\xi)
&=\int_{-\infty}^0e^{x}e^{-i\xi x}\,dx+\int_0^\infty e^{-x}e^{-i\xi x}\,dx\\
&=\int_0^\infty e^{-(1-i\xi)t}\,dt+\int_0^\infty e^{-(1+i\xi)t}\,dt\\
&=\left[-\frac{e^{-(1-i\xi)t}}{1-i\xi}\right]_0^\infty
+\left[-\frac{e^{-(1+i\xi)t}}{1+i\xi}\right]_0^\infty\\
&=\frac1{1-i\xi}+\frac1{1+i\xi}\\
&=\frac{(1+i\xi)+(1-i\xi)}{(1-i\xi)(1+i\xi)}\\
&=\frac2{1+\xi^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The kernel $e^{-i\xi x}$ has modulus one, so the product is absolutely integrable because $e^{-|x|}$ decays exponentially at both ends of the line. On the negative half-line set $x=-t$. Both the order of the limits and the sign of the differential reverse, so the first integral also runs from zero to infinity. Its kernel becomes $e^{i\xi t}$. Both complex exponents have real part minus one, so the antiderivatives tend to zero at infinity. At zero each antiderivative is the negative reciprocal of its respective decay rate. On taking a common denominator, the imaginary terms in the numerator cancel and the two denominators multiply to give $1+\xi^2$. The transform is therefore real and even, as expected from the symmetry of the original function. At $\xi=0$ its value is two, the sum of the areas of the exponentials on the two half-lines.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Using $\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R} f(x)e^{-i\xi x}\,dx$, find the transform of $f(x)=e^{-2|x|}$.

Use symmetry to reduce the calculation to a cosine integral.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat f(\xi)&=2\int_0^\infty e^{-2x}\cos(\xi x)\,dx\\
&=2\operatorname{Re}\int_0^\infty e^{-(2+i\xi)x}\,dx\\
&=2\operatorname{Re}\frac1{2+i\xi}\\
&=2\operatorname{Re}\frac{2-i\xi}{4+\xi^2}\\
&=\frac4{4+\xi^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original function is even and integrable, so splitting the kernel into cosine and sine gives two absolutely convergent integrals. The sine product is odd and integrates to zero on the line. The cosine product is even, giving twice the integral on the positive half-line. The cosine is the real part of the complex exponential. Since the rate $2+i\xi$ has real part two, the exponential in the antiderivative tends to zero at infinity and the integral is the reciprocal of the rate. To find its real part, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate. The denominator becomes positive real, so only the two in the numerator contributes. At zero frequency the transform equals one because the area on each half-line is one half.

</div>
