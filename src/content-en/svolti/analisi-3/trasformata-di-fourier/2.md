## Exercise 1

Find $\widehat{\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}}(\xi)$ for $\xi\ne0$ using the convention adopted in this chapter.

The integral is restricted to the support of the indicator.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat f(\xi)&=\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-i\xi x}\,dx\\
&=\left[-\frac{e^{-i\xi x}}{i\xi}\right]_{-1}^1\\
&=\frac{-e^{-i\xi}+e^{i\xi}}{i\xi}\\
&=\frac{2i\sin\xi}{i\xi}\\
&=\frac{2\sin\xi}{\xi},\qquad \xi\ne0,\\[4pt]
\widehat f(0)&=\int_{-1}^1 1\,dx=2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*sin(x)/x" punti="0,2,pieno;-3.141592654,0,pieno;3.141592654,0,pieno" nomi="ξ,F" xmin=-9 xmax=9 ymin=-0.65 ymax=2.2 titolo="At zero frequency the integral equals $2$. Its zeros closest to the origin are $\pm\pi$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The indicator is one on the interval and zero elsewhere, so the transform is an integral over $[-1,1]$. For nonzero frequency, divide the exponential by $-i\xi$ to obtain an antiderivative. Evaluating at the endpoints subtracts the value at minus one. The exponent is positive there, and this contribution appears with a plus sign in the numerator. Euler's formula gives $2i\sin\xi$ for the exponential difference, so $i$ cancels. At zero we evaluate the integral directly since the kernel equals one. The result agrees with the limit from nonzero frequencies because the ratio of sine to its argument tends to one. The nonzero zeros are the nonzero integer multiples of $\pi$, as the graph shows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find $\widehat{\mathbf1_{[-2,2]}}(\xi)$ for $\xi\ne0$ using the convention adopted in this chapter.

Integrate the cosine over the positive half of the support.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat f(\xi)
&=\int_{-2}^2\cos(\xi x)\,dx-i\int_{-2}^2\sin(\xi x)\,dx\\
&=2\int_0^2\cos(\xi x)\,dx\\
&=2\left[\frac{\sin(\xi x)}{\xi}\right]_0^2\\
&=\frac{2\sin(2\xi)}{\xi},\qquad \xi\ne0,\\
\widehat f(0)&=\int_{-2}^2 1\,dx=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Split the integrand into real and imaginary parts. The sine is odd in $x$ and the support has opposite endpoints, so its integral is zero. The cosine is even in $x$, so integrate over $[0,2]$ and double the result. At nonzero frequency its antiderivative is the sine divided by $\xi$. The sine argument is zero at the origin and $2\xi$ at the upper endpoint. The nonzero zeros solve $2\xi=k\pi$ for nonzero integers $k$. Compared with the indicator of width two, doubling the support length therefore halves the spacing between consecutive zeros. At zero frequency the kernel is one and the integral is the interval length, four. The limit of the quotient also gives four because $\dfrac{\sin(2\xi)}{2\xi}\to1$.

</div>
