## Exercise 1

In Euclidean $\mathbb R^2$, project $(3,1)$ onto the line spanned by $(1,1)$ and find the distance to the line.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v&=(3,1),\qquad w=(1,1),\qquad M=\operatorname{span}\{w\}. \\[4pt]
\langle v,w\rangle &= 3\cdot1+1\cdot1 \\
&=4. \\[4pt]
\langle w,w\rangle &=1^2+1^2 \\
&=2. \\[4pt]
P_Mv&=\frac{\langle v,w\rangle}{\langle w,w\rangle}\,w \\
&=2(1,1)\\
&=(2,2). \\[4pt]
r&=(3,1)-(2,2)\\
&=(1,-1). \\[4pt]
\langle r,w\rangle&=1-1=0. \\[4pt]
d(v,M)&=\sqrt{1^2+(-1)^2} \\
&=\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x" segmenti="2,2,3,1" vettori="0,0,3,1,v" punti="2,2,pieno,P;3,1,pieno" legenda="M" xmin=-0.1 xmax=3.55 ymin=-0.1 ymax=3.55 uguale=1 titolo="The projection $P=(2,2)$ lies on the line $M$. The residual is the perpendicular segment from $P$ to $v$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The span of $w$ is a one-dimensional linear subspace and is thus closed. The projection theorem gives a closest point characterized by orthogonality of the residual. Writing the point as $cw$, this condition imposes $\langle v,w\rangle-c\langle w,w\rangle=0$. The generator has squared norm two, so division is valid and gives coefficient two.

Subtracting the projected point from $v$ gives the residual $(1,-1)$. Its inner product with $(1,1)$ is zero, as required. In the plane the residual points from the foot of the perpendicular to the given point. Its norm is the distance to the line because any displacement from the foot along the line would add a positive squared term.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Project $x^2$ onto the constants in $L^2(-1,1)$ and compute the squared error.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|1\|_2^2 &=\int_{-1}^1 1\,dx \\
&=2. \\[4pt]
\langle x^2,1\rangle &=\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^1 \\
&=\frac23. \\[4pt]
c &=\frac{\langle x^2,1\rangle}{\|1\|_2^2} \\
&=\frac13. \\[4pt]
\|x^2\|_2^2 &=\left[\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_{-1}^1 \\
&=\frac25. \\[4pt]
\|c\,1\|_2^2 &= 2\left(\frac13\right)^2 \\
&=\frac29. \\[4pt]
d(x^2,M)^2 &=\frac25-\frac29 \\
&=\frac8{45}. \\[4pt]
P_M(x^2)&=\frac13,\qquad d(x^2,M)^2=\frac8{45}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the constant vector $1$ has norm $\sqrt2$ because the interval has length two. Its span is closed, so the **projection theorem** gives the inner product divided by the squared norm of the generator. The numerator is $\dfrac23$ since the antiderivative of $x^2$ takes opposite values at the endpoints. Dividing by two yields the mean $\dfrac13$.

The residual is orthogonal to the constants, so the squared norm of $x^2$ is the sum of the squared norms of the projection and the residual. The squared norm of the original function is the integral of $x^4$. For the projection, integrate the constant $\dfrac19$ on an interval of length two. Subtracting gives the squared error.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In $L^2(-1,1)$, project $x^3+x^2$ onto the subspace spanned by $1$ and $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle 1,x\rangle &=\int_{-1}^1x\,dx \\
&=0. \\[4pt]
\|1\|_2^2 &=2,\qquad \|x\|_2^2=\frac23. \\[4pt]
\langle x^3+x^2,1\rangle &=\int_{-1}^1 x^2\,dx \\
&=\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^1 \\
&=\frac23. \\[4pt]
\langle x^3+x^2,x\rangle &=\int_{-1}^1 x^4\,dx \\
&=\left[\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_{-1}^1 \\
&=\frac25. \\[4pt]
c_0&=\frac{\frac23}{2} \\
&=\frac13. \\[4pt]
c_1&=\frac{\frac25}{\frac23} \\
&=\frac35. \\[4pt]
P_M(x^3+x^2)&=\frac13+\frac35x.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $M$ is spanned by two vectors, it is finite-dimensional and closed. The projection residual must be orthogonal to both generators. The inner product of $1$ and $x$ vanishes since $x$ is odd and the interval is symmetric. Thus the residual equation involving $1$ contains only $c_0$, and the one involving $x$ contains only $c_1$.

In the integral of $x^3+x^2$, the cubic term is odd, leaving the contribution from $x^2$. After multiplication by $x$, the cubic term is again odd and $x^4$ remains. The two equations divide the integrals by the distinct squared norms of the generators. This gives a constant term for the even part and a linear term for the odd part.

</div>
