## Exercise 1

Compute $\|n^{\frac1{2}}\mathbf1_{(0,\dfrac1n)}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}$ for every $n\ge1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_n(x) &= \sqrt n\,\mathbf1_{(0,\frac1n)}(x). \\[5pt]
|f_n(x)|^2 &= n\,\mathbf1_{(0,\frac1n)}(x). \\[5pt]
\|f_n\|_2^2 &= \int_0^1 n\,\mathbf1_{(0,\frac1n)}(x)\,dx \\
&= n\int_0^{\frac1n}1\,dx \\
&= n\left[x\right]_0^{\frac1n} \\
&= n\cdot\frac1n \\
&= 1. \\[5pt]
\|f_n\|_2 &= 1\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{poligoni="0,0 0.25,0 0.25,2 0,2" linee="0,2 0.25,2" punti="0.25,2,vuoto" xmin=-0.06 xmax=0.62 ymin=-0.12 ymax=2.45 titolo="The term with $n=4$ in the first exercise has height $2$ and width $\dfrac14$. To find the squared norm, integrate the square of the height."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The indicator equals one on the given interval and zero elsewhere, so squaring leaves it unchanged. The square of the amplitude $\sqrt n$ is $n$. The integrand for the squared norm is therefore constant over a segment of length $\dfrac1n$. Its integral is that length multiplied by the value $n$ of the squared function.

For $n=1$ the segment is all of $(0,1)$. For $n>1$ there is also a part of the interval where the integrand is zero and contributes nothing. In both cases the area is one, so its square root is one as well. As $n$ increases the length shrinks in exactly the same proportion as the integrand's height grows.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute $\|n^{\frac1{3}}\mathbf1_{(0,\dfrac1n)}\|_{L^{3}(0,1)}$ for every $n\ge1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_n(x)&=n^{\frac13}\mathbf1_{(0,\frac1n)}(x). \\[5pt]
\|f_n\|_3^3 &= \int_0^1 |f_n(x)|^3\,dx \\
&= n\int_0^{\frac1n}1\,dx \\
&= n\cdot\frac1n \\
&= 1. \\[4pt]
\|f_n\|_2^2 &= n^{\frac23}\int_0^{\frac1n}1\,dx \\
&= n^{\frac23}\cdot\frac1n \\
&= n^{-\frac13}. \\[4pt]
\|f_n\|_2 &= \left(n^{-\frac13}\right)^{\frac12} \\
&= n^{-\frac16}. \\[4pt]
\|f_n\|_3 &= \sqrt[3]{1} \\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each $n$ the indicator is measurable and the function is bounded, so its cube is integrable on $(0,1)$. In the $L^3$ norm, cubing the absolute value changes the amplitude from $n^{\frac13}$ to $n$ and leaves the indicator unchanged. The rectangular base has length $\dfrac1n$, so the integral is one. The norm is the cube root of this quantity.

Comparison with $L^2$ clarifies the role of the exponent. Squaring gives the integrand height $n^{\frac23}$, which grows more slowly than $n$. Its product with the same length therefore tends to zero. The sequence can have constant L³ norm and converge to zero in L² because the two norms use different powers on the same support.

</div>
