## Exercise 1

For two orthonormal vectors $e_1,e_2$ in a Hilbert space, compute $\|e_1+2e_2\|^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= e_1+2e_2. \\[4pt]
\langle e_1,e_1\rangle &=1,\qquad \langle e_2,e_2\rangle=1. \\
\langle e_1,e_2\rangle &=0,\qquad \langle e_2,e_1\rangle=0. \\[4pt]
\|v\|^2 &= \langle e_1+2e_2,e_1+2e_2\rangle \\
&= \langle e_1,e_1\rangle+2\langle e_1,e_2\rangle
   +2\langle e_2,e_1\rangle+4\langle e_2,e_2\rangle \\
&= 1+0+0+4 \\
&= 5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Orthonormality determines all four inner products in the expansion. The two inner products of a vector with itself are one and the two between different vectors are zero. Since the coefficients are real, conjugating the second argument in a complex space leaves the factors one and two unchanged.

The coefficient $2$ occurs twice in the contribution from $e_2$, once for each argument of the inner product. This gives the factor $4$. The cross terms vanish, so the squared norm is the sum of the squares of the two coordinates. It suffices that the pair be orthonormal and that the vector belong to its span. The pair may be part of a larger basis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For two orthonormal vectors $e_1,e_2$ in a Hilbert space, compute $\|2e_1+3e_2\|^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=2e_1,\qquad w=3e_2. \\[4pt]
\langle u,w\rangle &= 6\langle e_1,e_2\rangle \\
&=0. \\[4pt]
\|u\|^2&=|2|^2\|e_1\|^2 \\
&=4. \\[4pt]
\|w\|^2&=|3|^2\|e_2\|^2 \\
&=9. \\[4pt]
\|2e_1+3e_2\|^2&=\|u\|^2+\|w\|^2 \\
&=4+9 \\
&=13.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,2,0,2e₁;2,0,2,3,3e₂;0,0,2,3,v" segmenti="0,3,2,3" xmin=-0.3 xmax=2.65 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.55 uguale=1 titolo="The vector $v=2e_1+3e_2$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with perpendicular legs of lengths $2$ and $3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum has contributions in two different directions. Since $e_1$ and $e_2$ are orthogonal, so are their multiples $u=2e_1$ and $w=3e_2$. Their sum therefore satisfies the **Pythagorean identity**. Orthonormality also makes both original vectors unit vectors, so their multiples have lengths two and three.

In the diagram, $w$ is translated to the tip of $u$. Translation preserves length and direction. The segment from the origin to the final tip therefore represents the sum $u+w$ and is the hypotenuse. Since the question asks for the squared length, the final result is the sum $4+9$.

</div>
