## Exercise 1

In $L^2(0,1)$, find the norm of the functional $\ell(f)=\int_0^1 f(x)x\,dx$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell(f) &= \langle f,x\rangle. \\[4pt]
\|x\|_2^2 &= \int_0^1 x^2\,dx \\
&= \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 \\
&= \frac13. \\[4pt]
|\ell(f)| &\le \|f\|_2\|x\|_2. \\[4pt]
\|\sqrt3\,x\|_2 &= \sqrt3\,\|x\|_2 \\
&= 1. \\[4pt]
\ell(\sqrt3\,x)&=\sqrt3\int_0^1 x^2\,dx \\
&= \frac{\sqrt3}{3}. \\[4pt]
\|\ell\|&=\frac1{\sqrt3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inner product on $L^2$ is linear in the first argument and conjugates the second. The weight $x$ is real, so its conjugate is unchanged and the functional is the inner product with the vector $x$. That vector belongs to $L^2$, as the integral of its square shows. Thus **Cauchy–Schwarz** guarantees integrability of the product and continuity of the functional.

The same inequality gives an upper bound for $|\ell(f)|$ on all unit vectors. To identify the norm we also need a vector attaining this bound. Dividing the representing vector by its norm gives the unit vector $\sqrt3\,x$. The functional attains the upper bound on that vector.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In $L^2(0,1)$, find the norm of the functional $\ell(f)=\int_0^1 f(x)x^{2}\,dx$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= x^2,\qquad \ell(f)=\langle f,g\rangle. \\[4pt]
\|g\|_2^2 &= \int_0^1x^4\,dx \\
&= \left[\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1 \\
&= \frac15. \\[4pt]
|\ell(f)| &\le \|f\|_2\|g\|_2. \\[4pt]
f_0 &= \frac{g}{\|g\|_2} \\
&= \sqrt5\,x^2. \\[4pt]
|\ell(f_0)| &= \sqrt5\int_0^1x^4\,dx \\
&= \frac{\sqrt5}{5}. \\[4pt]
\|\ell\| &= \frac1{\sqrt5}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(5)*x^2;x^2" legenda="\sqrt5\,x^2;x^2" xmin=0 xmax=1.06 ymin=0 ymax=2.46 titolo="The representing vector $x^2$ and its multiple $\sqrt5\,x^2$, which has unit norm and attains the norm of the functional."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $g(x)=x^2$ is real, so $\overline{g(x)}$ equals the weight in the integral. Its norm is finite since its square is a polynomial integrable on the interval. The **Cauchy–Schwarz** bound therefore applies to every $f\in L^2$. In particular, for $\|f\|_2\le 1$, the absolute value of $\ell(f)$ is at most $\|g\|_2$.

The graph compares $g$ with $f_0$, the vector obtained by normalizing $g$. The functions are proportional, so equality holds in Cauchy–Schwarz for this choice. Evaluation at $f_0$ confirms that the upper bound is attained. Thus the functional and its representing vector have the same norm, as the Riesz representation theorem states.

</div>
