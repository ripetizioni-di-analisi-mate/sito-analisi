## Exercise 1

Find the orthogonal projection of $f(x)=x$ onto the subspace of constant functions in $L^2(0,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \operatorname{span}\{1\},\qquad P_Mf=c\,1. \\[5pt]
\langle f-c,1\rangle &= \int_0^1(x-c)\,dx \\
&= \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1-c[ x ]_0^1 \\
&= \frac12-c. \\[5pt]
\langle f-c,1\rangle=0 &\iff \frac12-c=0 \\
&\iff c=\frac12. \\[5pt]
P_M f(x) &= \frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constants form a one-dimensional subspace of $L^2(0,1)$. Every finite-dimensional subspace is closed, so the **orthogonal projection theorem** applies. A candidate has the form $c\,1$. To be the projection, its residual must be orthogonal to the generator $1$, since orthogonality to this vector implies orthogonality to all its multiples.

Taking the inner product with $1$ integrates the residual. The constant $c$ contributes $c$ itself since the interval has length one. The contribution from $x$ is $\dfrac12$, obtained by evaluating $\dfrac{x^2}{2}$ at the endpoints. Setting the difference of the contributions to zero determines a unique candidate. The resulting function is constant throughout the interval.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the orthogonal projection of $f(x)=x^{2}$ onto the subspace of constant functions in $L^2(0,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|1\|_2^2 &= \int_0^1 1\,dx \\
&= 1. \\[4pt]
\langle x^2,1\rangle &= \int_0^1 x^2\,dx \\
&= \left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1 \\
&= \frac13. \\[4pt]
\langle x^2-c,1\rangle &= \frac13-c. \\[4pt]
\langle x^2-c,1\rangle=0 &\iff c=\frac13. \\[4pt]
P_M(x^2) &= \frac13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The subspace of constants is spanned by one vector and is closed. The projection theorem therefore gives the best approximation by imposing orthogonality of the residual. The inner product with $1$ equals the integral over $(0,1)$. Since the constant one has squared norm one, the required value is precisely the integral of $x^2$, its mean on this interval.

The curve $y=x^2$ meets the horizontal line $y=\dfrac13$ when $x=\dfrac1{\sqrt3}$. To the left the residual is negative and to the right it is positive. Orthogonality requires its signed integral to vanish, so the two areas shown are equal.

</div>
