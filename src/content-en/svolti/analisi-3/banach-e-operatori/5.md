## Exercise 1

In a complete metric space with metric $d(x,y)=|x-y|$, a contraction has constant $q=\dfrac{1}{3}$ and two consecutive iterates satisfy $|x_n-x_{n-1}|=1$. What a posteriori upper bound does Banach's theorem give for $|x_*-x_n|$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x_{n+1}-x_n|
&=|T(x_n)-T(x_{n-1})|\\
&\le q|x_n-x_{n-1}|\\
&=\frac{1}{3},\\
|x_{n+j}-x_{n+j-1}|&\le 3^{-j}\qquad(j\ge1).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x_*-x_n|&\le\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{n+j}-x_{n+j-1}|\\
&\le\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}3^{-j}\\
&=\frac{\frac13}{1-\frac13}\\
&=\frac{1}{2} .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Banach's estimate concerns the distance from the current iterate to the limit. The available information is the last observed step, so start with the distance between $x_n$ and $x_{n-1}$. Applying $T$ to both gives a bound for the next step by the contraction property. Its length is at most one third. Repeating the argument bounds the $j$th step after $x_n$ by $3^{-j}$. To compare $x_n$ with a later iterate, add the intermediate step lengths using the triangle inequality. The theorem ensures convergence to the fixed point under the given hypotheses, so we can pass to the limit in this inequality. The resulting geometric series has ratio one third and first term one third, since the known step ends at $x_n$. Dividing the first term by one minus the ratio gives an upper error bound of one half.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a complete metric space with metric $d(x,y)=|x-y|$, a contraction has constant $q=\dfrac{1}{4}$ and two consecutive iterates satisfy $|x_n-x_{n-1}|=1$. What a posteriori upper bound does Banach's theorem give for $|x_*-x_n|$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x_{n+1}-x_n|
&\le \frac{1}{4} |x_n-x_{n-1}|\\
&=\frac{1}{4},\\
|x_{n+j}-x_{n+j-1}|&\le 4^{-j}\qquad(j\ge1).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x_{n+m}-x_n|&\le\sum_{j=1}^m |x_{n+j}-x_{n+j-1}|\\
&\le\sum_{j=1}^m 4^{-j}\\
&=\frac{\frac{1}{4}(1-4^{-m})}{1-\frac{1}{4}}\\
&=\frac{1-4^{-m}}3,\\
|x_*-x_n|&\le\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1-4^{-m}}3\\
&=\frac{1}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value one given in the problem measures the step from $x_{n-1}$ to $x_n$. The requested error starts at $x_n$, so the first step to estimate runs from $x_n$ to $x_{n+1}$. The contraction factor of one quarter bounds this step using the preceding one. Repeated application gives bounds of one sixteenth, one sixty-fourth, and the subsequent powers of one quarter. For a finite number of steps these bounds can already be added using the geometric sum formula. The triangle inequality bounds the distance between the endpoints by that sum. The Banach fixed-point theorem gives convergence of the iterates, so the distance from $x_n$ passes to the limit by continuity of the metric. As $m$ tends to infinity, $4^{-m}$ vanishes and the bound is one third. It remains valid even when the actual steps are strictly smaller than their bounds.

</div>
