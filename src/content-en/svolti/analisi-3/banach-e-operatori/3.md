## Exercise 1

On $C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm, find the norm of $\ell(f)=f(0)+2f(1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\ell(f)|&=|f(0)+2f(1)|\\
&\le |f(0)|+2|f(1)|\\
&\le\|f\|_\infty+2\|f\|_\infty\\
&=3\|f\|_\infty,\\
\|\ell\|&\le3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|1\|_\infty&=1,\\
\ell(1)&=1+2\\
&=3,\\
\|\ell\|&=3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Evaluation at a fixed point is linear and bounded in absolute value by the supremum norm. The weighted sum of the two evaluations is therefore a continuous linear functional. To compute its norm, apply the triangle inequality to estimate each evaluation separately. For any function of norm at most one, both endpoint values have absolute value at most one. Hence the first contribution is at most one and the second at most two. The supremum of the absolute value of the functional on the unit ball is therefore at most three. For the reverse inequality, one input must attain both extreme values compatible with the weights. Both weights are positive, so both endpoint values can be one. The constant function one has those values and has norm one. The functional takes the value three on it and attains the upper bound.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On $C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm, find the norm of $\ell(f)=2f(0)+3f(1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\ell(f)|&=|2f(0)+3f(1)|\\
&\le2|f(0)|+3|f(1)|\\
&\le(2+3)\|f\|_\infty\\
&=5\|f\|_\infty,\\
\|\ell\|&\le5 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_0(0)&=f_0(1)=1\qquad (f_0\equiv1),\\
\|f_0\|_\infty&=1,\\
|\ell(f_0)|&=|2+3|\\
&=5,\\
\|\ell\|&=5 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both point evaluations are continuous, since the absolute value at any point is bounded by the maximum absolute value of the function. Their combination with coefficients two and three is linear. For an upper bound, take an arbitrary function in the unit ball. Its values at zero and one both have absolute value at most one. The absolute value of their weighted sum is therefore at most the sum of the positive coefficients, namely five. Equality is possible when the two products have the same sign and both evaluations have absolute value one. The constant function one satisfies these conditions simultaneously. Although the functional uses only two values, the test function must be continuous and have norm one over the entire interval. The constant function also meets these requirements, so the maximum over the unit ball is indeed five.

</div>
