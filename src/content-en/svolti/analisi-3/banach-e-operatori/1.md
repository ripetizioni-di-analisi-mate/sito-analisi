## Exercise 1

On $C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm, find the norm of $Tf(x)=(1+x)f(x)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0\le x\le1 &\Longrightarrow 1\le1+x\le2,\\
|(Tf)(x)|&=(1+x)|f(x)|\\
&\le (1+x)\|f\|_\infty\\
&\le 2\|f\|_\infty,\\
\|Tf\|_\infty &\le 2\|f\|_\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_0(x)&=1,\\
\|f_0\|_\infty&=1,\\
(Tf_0)(1)&=(1+1)f_0(1)\\
&=2,\\
2&\le\|T\|\le2,\\
\|T\|&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product of two continuous functions is continuous, so the multiplier $1+x$ defines an operator from $C([0,1])$ into itself. Multiplication by a fixed function respects linear combinations. It therefore suffices to prove a uniform bound. For each $x$, bound the absolute values of the input and the multiplier separately. The former is at most $\|f\|_\infty$ by definition of the norm. The latter is at most two because $x$ ranges from zero to one. The resulting upper bound holds for every function in the unit ball. For a matching lower bound, choose a unit function whose absolute value is one where the multiplier attains its maximum. The constant function $f_0=1$ meets this condition at $x=1$. Its image takes the value two there, so its supremum norm is at least two. The bounds agree and the operator norm is two.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On $C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm, find the norm of $Tf(x)=(2+x)f(x)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\le 2+x\le3 &\qquad (x\in[0,1]),\\
|(Tf)(x)|&=(2+x)|f(x)|\\
&\le3\|f\|_\infty,\\
\|Tf\|_\infty&\le3\|f\|_\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_0(x)&=1,\\
\|f_0\|_\infty&=1,\\
\|Tf_0\|_\infty&=\max_{x\in[0,1]}|2+x|\\
&=2+1\\
&=3,\\
\|T\|&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both the multiplier and the input are continuous, so their product belongs to the original space. Linearity follows from distributivity of multiplication over function addition. The multiplier is positive on the entire interval, hence its absolute value equals $2+x$. It increases with $x$ and therefore attains its maximum at the right endpoint. Taking the maximum over $x$ in the pointwise estimate gives an upper bound for the operator norm. The constant function one belongs to the unit ball, so the norm of its image is one of the values in the supremum defining $\|T\|$. Its image is the multiplier itself, which equals three at $x=1$. The value at this one point suffices for the lower bound, whereas the upper bound must hold throughout the interval. Thus the supremum is attained and the norm is three.

</div>
