## Exercise 1

On $C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm, find the norm of $Tf(x)=\int_0^x tf(t)\,dt$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|(Tf)(x)|&=\left|\int_0^x t f(t)\,dt\right|\\
&\le\int_0^x t|f(t)|\,dt\\
&\le\|f\|_\infty\int_0^x t\,dt\\
&=\|f\|_\infty\left[\frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^x\\
&=\frac{x^2}{2}\|f\|_\infty\\
&\le\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_\infty .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|1\|_\infty &= 1,\\
(T1)(1)&=\int_0^1 t\,dt\\
&=\frac{1}{2},\\
\|T\|&=\frac{1}{2} .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For every continuous input $f$, the integrand $t f(t)$ is continuous. Its integral with a variable upper limit is therefore continuous in $x$. Linearity of integration makes $T$ linear. At every $x$ in the interval, the triangle inequality bounds the absolute value of the integral by the integral of the absolute value. Since $0\le t\le x$, the factor $t$ is nonnegative and needs no absolute value. Replacing $|f(t)|$ by its maximum on the interval gives a bound uniform over unit inputs. The integral of the weight increases with $x$, so its maximum on the interval occurs at $x=1$. To see that the bound is exact, take the constant unit function one. The product is then nonnegative and its absolute value equals the weight, so both pointwise inequalities are equalities. Taking $x=1$ also gives the maximum over the upper limits of integration. This yields the exact norm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On $C([0,1])$ with the supremum norm, find the norm of $Tf(x)=\int_0^x t^{2}f(t)\,dt$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|(Tf)(x)|&\le\int_0^x t^2|f(t)|\,dt\\
&\le\|f\|_\infty\int_0^x t^2\,dt\\
&=\|f\|_\infty\left[\frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^x\\
&=\frac{x^3}{3}\|f\|_\infty,\\
\|Tf\|_\infty&\le\frac{1}{3}\|f\|_\infty .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(T1)(x)&=\frac{x^3}{3},\\
\|T1\|_\infty&=\max_{x\in[0,1]}\frac{x^3}{3}\\
&=\frac{1}{3},\\
\|1\|_\infty &= 1,\\
\|T\|&=\frac{1}{3} .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The operator is linear by linearity of integration and takes values in $C([0,1])$ because it integrates a continuous function. At every point $x$ the absolute value of the output is bounded by the integral of $t^2|f(t)|$. For any input in the unit ball, the inequality $|f(t)|\le1$ holds simultaneously for every $t$. Thus comparison with the integral of $t^2$ gives an upper bound for the entire ball. This integral depends on $x$ through $x^3$. Since the latter is increasing on $[0,1]$, the largest value is obtained by integrating over the whole interval. To show the bound is attained, compute the full image of the constant function one. Its value at $x=1$ is exactly one third. The input has norm one, so this image gives the required lower bound in the definition of the norm.

</div>
