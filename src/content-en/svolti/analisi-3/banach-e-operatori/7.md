## Exercise 1

On $\ell^2$, is the operator $(Tx)_n=\dfrac{1}{n}x_n$ compact? What is its norm?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|Tx\|_2^2&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|x_n|^2}{n^2}\\
&\le\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x_n|^2\\
&=\|x\|_2^2,\\
\|Te_1\|_2&=\|e_1\|_2=1,\\
\|T\|&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $N\ge1$, let $T_Nx=(x_1,\dfrac{x_2}{2},\ldots,\dfrac{x_N}{N},0,\ldots)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|(T-T_N)x\|_2^2&=\sum_{n>N}\frac{|x_n|^2}{n^2}\\
&\le\frac{\|x\|_2^2}{(N+1)^2},\\
\|(T-T_N)e_{N+1}\|_2&=\frac{1}{N+1},\\
\|T-T_N\|&=\frac{1}{N+1}\longrightarrow0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
T_N\text{ of finite rank},\quad T_N\to T\text{ in norm}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T\text{ compact}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each output coordinate is the corresponding input coordinate multiplied by the reciprocal of its index. Since all coefficients have absolute value at most one, the series of squared output magnitudes converges. The operator is therefore well defined, linear in each coordinate, and has norm at most one. The standard basis vector $e_1$ has norm one and is fixed, so it attains the upper bound. For compactness, truncate the operator after the first $N$ coordinates. The same estimate bounds the truncation, whose range lies in the subspace spanned by $e_1,\ldots,e_N$. It is compact because its rank is finite. The difference between the two operators involves only coordinates beyond $N$. Its largest coefficient has index $N+1$ and the corresponding bound is attained at $e_{N+1}$. The norm of the difference therefore tends to zero. The codomain $\ell^2$ is complete, so an operator norm limit of compact operators is compact.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On $\ell^2$, is the operator $(Tx)_n=\dfrac1{n^{2}}x_n$ compact? What is its norm?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|Tx\|_2^2&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|x_n|^2}{n^4}\\
&\le\|x\|_2^2,\\
Te_1&=e_1,\\
\|T\|&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $P_N$ denote truncation to the first $N$ coordinates, and set $T_N=P_NT$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|(T-T_N)x\|_2^2&=\sum_{n>N}\frac{|x_n|^2}{n^4}\\
&\le\frac{\|x\|_2^2}{(N+1)^4},\\
(T-T_N)e_{N+1}&=\frac{1}{(N+1)^2}e_{N+1},\\
\|T-T_N\|&=\frac{1}{(N+1)^2}\longrightarrow0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
T_N\text{ of finite rank},\quad T_N\to T\text{ in norm}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T\text{ compact}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The norm of $\ell^2$ is the square root of the sum of the squared absolute values of its coordinates. The coefficient $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ is squared in this sum, so the denominator is $n^4$. All coefficients have absolute value at most one, so the norm of $T$ is at most one. The first coefficient is one and the vector $e_1$ has norm one, so the upper bound is exact. To show compactness, approximate $T$ by $T_N$, whose range lies in the subspace of the first $N$ coordinates. It is bounded and has finite rank, so it is compact. In the difference $T-T_N$ the first $N$ coordinates vanish. The remaining coefficients decrease and are therefore all bounded above by $(N+1)^{-2}$. The vector $e_{N+1}$ attains equality in the resulting norm estimate. The operator norm distance tends to zero and the codomain is complete, so the limit $T$ is compact.

</div>
