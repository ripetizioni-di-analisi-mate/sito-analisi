## Exercise 1

Find the fixed point of $T(x)=\dfrac{x}{3}+1$ on $\mathbb R$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|T(x)-T(y)|&=\left|\frac{x}{3}+1-\frac{y}{3}-1\right|\\
&=\frac{1}{3}|x-y|.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(x_*)&=x_*,\\
\frac{x_*}{3}+1&=x_*,\\
x_*-\frac{x_*}{3}&=1,\\
\frac{2}{3} x_*&=1,\\
x_*&=\frac{3}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)&=\frac{1}{2}+1\\
&=\frac{3}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x;x/3+1" punti="1.5,1.5,pieno" uguale=1 xmin=-0.1 xmax=2.2 ymin=-0.1 ymax=2.2 legenda="y=x;y=T(x)" titolo="The horizontal coordinate of the intersection is $\dfrac{3}{2}$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The map takes real values for every real argument, and its domain $\mathbb R$ is complete in the usual metric. To check the contraction property, subtract the values of the map at two points. The constant terms cancel, so the distance between the images is one third of the distance between the two points. This factor is strictly less than one, so the **Banach fixed-point theorem** gives a unique fixed point. To find it, equate the image to the argument and collect the two terms containing the unknown on one side. The remaining coefficient is two thirds. Dividing by it gives three halves. Substitution in the original expression verifies that this value is mapped to itself. In the $(x,y)$ plane this is the intersection of the graph of $T$ with the diagonal $y=x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the fixed point of $T(x)=\dfrac{x}{4}+1$ on $\mathbb R$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|T(x)-T(y)|&=\left|\frac{x-y}{4}\right|\\
&=\frac{1}{4}|x-y| .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_*&=\frac{x_*}{4}+1,\\
x_*-\frac{x_*}{4}&=1,\\
\frac{3}{4}x_*&=1,\\
x_*&=\frac{4}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{n+1}-x_*&=T(x_n)-T(x_*)\\
&=\frac{1}{4}(x_n-x_*),\\
x_n-x_*&=4^{-n}(x_0-x_*) .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The map sends the real line into itself, and the real line is complete in its usual metric. The difference between the images of two points is one quarter of the difference between them. Thus the map is a contraction with constant $\dfrac{1}{4}$, and Banach's theorem gives existence and uniqueness. The fixed-point equation is linear. Subtracting $\dfrac{x_*}{4}$ from both sides leaves a coefficient of three quarters, so the fixed point is four thirds. The same difference formula describes the iterates. At each step the signed error relative to the fixed point is multiplied by one quarter, since $T(x_*)=x_*$. Repeating the calculation gives the exact error at step $n$. The factor is positive, so the sequence stays on the side of $x_*$ on which it started. The factor $4^{-n}$ tends to zero, so the distance tends to zero.

</div>
