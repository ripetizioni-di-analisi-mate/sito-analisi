## Exercise 1

Let $T(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\cos x$ on $[0,\dfrac{1}{2}]$. Show that the iterates starting at $x_0=\dfrac{1}{2}$ converge to a unique fixed point and bound the error after the first step.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(0)&=\frac{1}{2},\\
T\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)&=\frac{1}{2}\cos\frac{1}{2},\\
T\left(\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]\right)
&=\left[\frac{1}{2}\cos\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\\
&\subseteq\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right],\\
|T'(x)|&=\frac{1}{2}\sin x\\
&\le \frac{x}{2}\\
&\le\frac{1}{4} .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1&=\frac{1}{2}\cos\frac{1}{2},\\
|x_1-x_0|&=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\cos\frac{1}{2}\right),\\
|x_1-x_*|&\le\frac{\frac14}{1-\frac14}|x_1-x_0|\\
&=\frac{1}{3}\,\frac{1-\cos\frac12}{2}\\
&=\frac{1-\cos\frac12}{6}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x;cos(x)/2" punti="0.450184,0.450184,pieno" uguale=1 xmin=-0.02 xmax=0.56 ymin=-0.02 ymax=0.56 titolo="The diagonal meets the graph of $T$ exactly once on the interval of iteration."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The interval is closed in the real line, so it is complete in the usual metric. The cosine is positive and decreasing between zero and one half. Evaluating the map at the endpoints therefore gives its range, which lies in the interval. The map is differentiable on a neighborhood of the interval. At the points under consideration, $0\le\sin x\le x$, so the derivative has absolute value at most one quarter. By the **mean value theorem**, this bound gives the contraction property for every pair of points. The hypotheses of Banach's theorem hold, giving uniqueness and convergence of the iterates. The first iterate is the value of the map at one half. Since cosine is at most one, subtracting this value from one half gives the step length. The a posteriori bound multiplies this step length by the contraction constant divided by one minus that constant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $T(x)=\dfrac{x}{2}+\dfrac{1}{x}$ on $[1,2]$, find the fixed point and compute two iterates starting at $x_0=1$. Compare the a posteriori bound with the location of the iterates.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T'(x)&=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{x^2},\\
-\frac{1}{2}\le T'(x)&\le\frac{1}{4}\qquad(x\in[1,2]),\\
T'(x)=0&\Longleftrightarrow x=\sqrt2\quad (x\in[1,2]),\\
T(\sqrt2)&=\sqrt2,\\
T(1)&=T(2)=\frac{3}{2},\\
T([1,2])&=\left[\sqrt2,\frac{3}{2}\right]\subseteq[1,2].
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(x_*)&=x_*,\\
\frac{x_*}{2}+\frac{1}{x_*}&=x_*,\\
x_*^2&=2,\\
x_*&=\sqrt2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1&=T(1)\\
&=\frac{3}{2},\\
x_2&=T\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{3}\\
&=\frac{17}{12},\\
|x_2-x_*|&\le\frac{\frac12}{1-\frac12}|x_2-x_1|\\
&=\frac{1}{12},\\
0<x_2-x_*&=\frac{\frac{1}{144}}{\frac{17}{12}+\sqrt2}
\le\frac{1}{12}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x;x/2+1/x+0*sqrt(x-1)" punti="1.414214,1.414214,pieno;1,1.5,pieno;1.5,1.416667,pieno" segmenti="1,1,1,1.5;1,1.5,1.5,1.5;1.5,1.5,1.5,1.416667" uguale=1 xmin=-0.04 xmax=2.05 ymin=-0.04 ymax=2.05 titolo="The graph of $T$ has its minimum at $\sqrt2$, and the iterates lie at or above that minimum."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivative is negative before $\sqrt2$ and positive after it. The minimum therefore occurs at $\sqrt2$, and the maximum is attained at both endpoints. The values just calculated show that the map sends the interval into itself. The interval is complete because it is closed in $\mathbb R$. The absolute value of the derivative is at most one half throughout, so the **mean value theorem** yields a contraction with constant one half. Banach's theorem applies. The unknown is positive, so multiply the fixed-point equation by it and take the only positive root. The second iterate lies in the image of $T$, so it is at least $\sqrt2$. Rationalizing the difference gives its exact value since $(\dfrac{17}{12})^2-2=\dfrac{1}{144}$. The Banach bound, in contrast, uses only the distance between the last two iterates and does not require the exact solution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On $\mathbb R^2$ with the maximum norm, let $A(x,y)=(2y,0)$. Determine whether $I-A$ is invertible and find the inverse.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|A(x,y)\|_\infty&=2|y|\\
&\le 2\|(x,y)\|_\infty,\\
\|A(0,1)\|_\infty&=2,\\
\|A\|&=2,\\
A^2(x,y)&=A(2y,0)\\
&=(0,0).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(I-A)(I+A)&=I-A^2\\
&=I,\\
(I+A)(I-A)&=I-A^2\\
&=I,\\
(I-A)^{-1}(u,v)&=(I+A)(u,v)\\
&=(u+2v,v).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The map is linear on $\mathbb R^2$, and the bound for the absolute value of $y$ gives boundedness in the maximum norm. The vector $(0,1)$ has norm one and its image has norm two, so the bound is exact. The criterion $\|A\|<1$ is sufficient for an inverse. Here one can compute the inverse directly using the square of the operator. The first application maps the input to the horizontal axis, where the operator vanishes. Thus $A^2=0$ and the sum of the powers stops at $I+A$. Multiplying by $I-A$ in either order cancels the linear terms and leaves the identity. These two checks prove that the finite sum is the inverse. It adds twice the second coordinate to the first and leaves the second coordinate unchanged.

</div>
