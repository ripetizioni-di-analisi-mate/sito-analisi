## Exercise 1

Find the domains of $\sqrt{x-4}$ and $\dfrac1{\sqrt{x-4}}$. Check their common endpoint.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-4&\ge0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\ge4,\\[6pt]
D_{\sqrt{x-4}}&=[4,+\infty),\\[6pt]
x-4&>0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x>4,\\[6pt]
D_{\frac1{\sqrt{x-4}}}&=(4,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand $x-4$ must be nonnegative in both expressions. When the root is in the denominator, its value must also be nonzero. At $x=4$ the square root is zero. That input therefore belongs to the first domain and is excluded from the second. At $x=3$ the radicand is negative, so neither expression is defined. At $x=5$ the root equals one and therefore has a reciprocal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the domain of $\sqrt{(x-1)(3-x)}$ and check both endpoints.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1<x<3 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (x-1)(3-x)>0,\\[6pt]
x<1\ \text{or}\ x>3
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (x-1)(3-x)<0,\\[6pt]
x=1\ \text{or}\ x=3
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (x-1)(3-x)=0,\\[6pt]
D&=[1,3].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Consider the zeros $1$ and $3$ of the two factors separately. Before $1$ the first factor is negative and the second positive, giving a negative product. After $3$ their signs are reversed and the product is again negative. Between $1$ and $3$ both are positive. At each endpoint one factor is zero, so the square root exists and equals zero. At the interior input $x=2$, both factors are one, and the radical equals one. For the reciprocal of the same root, both zeros of the radicand would be excluded, leaving the open interval $(1,3)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the domain of $\dfrac{\sqrt{x-2}}{\sqrt{6-x}}$ and evaluate it at $x=4$.

::grafico{f="sqrt(x-2)/sqrt(6-x)" xmin=-0.5 xmax=7.1 ymin=-0.45 ymax=3.1 punti="2,0,pieno,A;4,1,pieno,B" asintoti="x=6" legenda="y=\dfrac{\sqrt{x-2}}{\sqrt{6-x}}" titolo="The value $2$ is included and the value $6$ is excluded"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{cases}
x-2\ge0\\
6-x>0
\end{cases}
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x\ge2\\
x<6
\end{cases}\\[6pt]
D&=[2,6),\\[6pt]
\frac{\sqrt{4-2}}{\sqrt{6-4}}
&=\frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator may be zero. The denominator must be defined and nonzero. The condition on the numerator's radicand is therefore non-strict, and the condition on the denominator's radicand is strict. Both must hold at once. At $x=2$ the denominator is $2$ and the quotient is zero, as at point $A$. At $x=6$ the denominator is zero and the endpoint is excluded. Point $B$ is at $x=4$, where the two roots have the same positive value. At $x=5$ the denominator is one, so the quotient is $\sqrt3$. That point is still on the branch before the excluded endpoint.

</div>
