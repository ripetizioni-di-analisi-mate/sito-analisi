## Exercise 1

Calculate $\sqrt[3]{-216}$ and justify the sign before checking the cube.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6^3&=216,\\[6pt]
(-6)^3&=-216,\\[6pt]
\sqrt[3]{-216}&=-6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand is negative, so its cube root is negative. To find the magnitude, factor $216=8\cdot27=2^3\cdot3^3$. The product of the two positive bases is $2\cdot3=6$. Take the negative value $-6$ and check that its cube recovers $-216$. The check matches the sign of the radicand and identifies a unique value. The positive number $6$ is the root for the radicand $216$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $\sqrt[3]{-54x^3}$ for every real $x$ and check its value at $x=-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{-54x^3}
&=\sqrt[3]{(-3x)^3\cdot2}\\[6pt]
&=-3x\sqrt[3]2,\\[6pt]
-3(-2)\sqrt[3]2&=6\sqrt[3]2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring $-54x^3$ produces the cube of $-3x$. Its cube root gives $-3x$, with its sign for each value of the variable. The factor $2$ remains under the root, since it has no integer cube factor greater than one. At $x=-2$ the radicand is positive, because $x^3$ and $-54$ are both negative. The simplified form gives $6\sqrt[3]2$. Its cube is $216\cdot2=432$, the value of the original radicand. When $x>0$, the factor $-3x$ is negative and makes the root negative. At $x=0$, both forms give zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $\dfrac{\sqrt[3]{16(x-1)^3}}{\sqrt[3]{2(x-1)^3}}$ and find its domain.

::grafico{f="2" xmin=-3 xmax=4 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.8 punti="1,2,vuoto" legenda="y=2 \quad (x\ne1)" titolo="The quotient equals $2$ at every allowed point and is undefined at $x=1$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{1\},\\[6pt]
\frac{\sqrt[3]{16(x-1)^3}}{\sqrt[3]{2(x-1)^3}}
&=\frac{(x-1)\sqrt[3]{16}}{(x-1)\sqrt[3]2}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt[3]8=2\qquad(x\in D).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both odd-index roots are real for every $x$. The denominator, however, vanishes when $(x-1)^3=0$, so the domain excludes $1$. At the other inputs, the factor $x-1$ taken out of the roots is nonzero and can cancel. The quotient of the remaining roots is $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{16}{2}}=2$. For example, at $x=0$ both original roots are negative and the quotient is positive. The open circle on the graph marks the point where the original quotient is undefined.

</div>
