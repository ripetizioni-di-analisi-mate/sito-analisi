## Exercise 1

Calculate $\sqrt[3]{-125}$.

$$
(-5)^3=-125\implies\sqrt[3]{-125}=-5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The index is odd, so negative radicands are allowed. The cube of the requested number must equal the radicand. The cube root keeps the negative sign.

Since $(-5)^3=-125$, the number $-5$ satisfies the definition. The positive number $5$ has cube $125$. The value is unique because the cubing function is strictly increasing.

The result is $-5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $\sqrt[3]{-54x^3}$, with $x\in\mathbb R$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{-54x^3}
&=\sqrt[3]{(-3x)^3\cdot2}\\[6pt]
&=-3x\sqrt[3]{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $-27x^3$ is the cube of $-3x$, so it comes outside the radical, leaving $2$ inside. The cube root of the cube of a real number returns that number regardless of its sign. Cubing the result gives $(-3x)^3\cdot2=-54x^3$.

</div>
