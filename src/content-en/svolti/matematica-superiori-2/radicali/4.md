## Exercise 1

Rationalize $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt3}$.

$$
\frac{3}{\sqrt3}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}=\frac{3\sqrt3}3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply the numerator and denominator by the radical in the denominator. The factor introduced equals one, so the value of the fraction stays the same. The new denominator is rational.

The numerator becomes $3\sqrt3$, and the denominator is $\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt3=3$. To check the resulting fraction, multiply it by the original denominator $\sqrt3$. The product should be $3$.

The result is $\sqrt{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt[3]{9}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{\sqrt[3]{9}}
&=\frac{5\sqrt[3]{3}}{\sqrt[3]{9}\sqrt[3]{3}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5\sqrt[3]{3}}{\sqrt[3]{27}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{5\sqrt[3]{3}}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is the cube root of $9=3^2$. One more factor of $3$ under the radical makes the radicand a perfect cube, so multiply the numerator and denominator by $\sqrt[3]{3}$. The new denominator is the cube root of $27=3^3$. Multiplying the result by $\sqrt[3]{9}$ gives the original numerator $5$.

</div>
