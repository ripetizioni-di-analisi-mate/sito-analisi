## Exercise 1

Rationalize $\dfrac4{\sqrt3}$ and verify the equality by multiplication.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac4{\sqrt3}
&=\frac{4\sqrt3}{\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt3}
=\frac{4\sqrt3}{3},\\[6pt]
\frac{4\sqrt3}{3}\cdot\sqrt3&=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator contains one copy of $\sqrt3$. A second copy produces $3$, since the root is positive and its square is $3$. The numerator is also multiplied by that same quantity so the ratio stays equal. The result is positive, as in the original fraction. To check it, multiply the answer by the original denominator. This recovers the numerator $4$. Since $\sqrt3$ is nonzero, recovering that numerator confirms the equality.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rationalize $\dfrac7{\sqrt[4]8}$ by choosing the factor that completes the smallest fourth power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
8&=2^3,\qquad 2^3\cdot2=2^4,\\[6pt]
\frac7{\sqrt[4]8}
&=\frac{7\sqrt[4]2}{\sqrt[4]{8\cdot2}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{7\sqrt[4]2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand contains three factors of $2$, while a fourth power requires four. Add one factor of $2$ under a root with the same index. The factor $\sqrt[4]2$ is nonzero, so multiplying the numerator and denominator by it preserves the quotient. The new denominator is $\sqrt[4]{16}=2$. Multiplying by another copy of $\sqrt[4]8$ would instead give exponent six inside the radicand. Taking out a fourth power would still leave a factor under the root. As a check, multiplying the resulting fraction by $\sqrt[4]8$ gives $\dfrac72\sqrt[4]{16}=7$, the original numerator.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize $\dfrac2{\sqrt[3]{x^2}}$ on its domain. Compare the values at $x=-8$ and $x=8$.

::grafico{f="2/cbrt(x*x)" xmin=-9 xmax=9 ymin=-0.3 ymax=4 asintoti="x=0" punti="-8,0.5;8,0.5" legenda="y=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt[3]{x^2}}=\dfrac{2\sqrt[3]{x}}{x}" titolo="The values at $-8$ and $8$ agree, and zero is excluded"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac2{\sqrt[3]{x^2}}
&=\frac{2\sqrt[3]x}{\sqrt[3]{x^3}}
=\frac{2\sqrt[3]x}{x}\qquad(x\ne0),\\[6pt]
\frac{2\sqrt[3]{-8}}{-8}
&=\frac12
=\frac{2\sqrt[3]8}{8}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original denominator vanishes only at zero. Completing the cube requires one more factor of $x$ under the root. The factor $\sqrt[3]x$ is nonzero throughout the domain, so it can be used to rationalize the denominator. The equality $\sqrt[3]{x^3}=x$ holds for negative $x$ too. In the new quotient, the numerator and denominator have the same sign, so the value stays positive. At $x=-8$ it is $-4$ divided by $-8$. At $x=8$ it is $4$ divided by $8$. The graph shows the two points at the same height.

</div>
