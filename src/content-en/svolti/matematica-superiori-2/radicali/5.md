## Exercise 1

Rationalize $\dfrac1{\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{4}}$.

$$
\frac{\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{4}}{(5)-4}=\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is a difference of radicals. Multiply by its conjugate and use the product of a sum and a difference. The radicands differ by one, so the denominator simplifies.

The conjugate is $\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{4}$. The cross terms in the product have opposite signs, leaving the difference $5-4=1$. The conjugate itself is therefore the reciprocal of the original denominator.

The result is $2 + \sqrt{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt3-\sqrt{11}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{\sqrt3-\sqrt{11}}
&=\frac{2(\sqrt3+\sqrt{11})}{3-11}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{\sqrt3+\sqrt{11}}4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is a difference, so its conjugate is the sum $\sqrt3+\sqrt{11}$. Their product is $3-11=-8$. The numerator factor $2$ cancels with a factor of $8$, and the result stays negative. The original fraction is also negative, since $\sqrt3<\sqrt{11}$ and the numerator is positive.

</div>
