## Exercise 1

Rationalize $\dfrac1{2+\sqrt3}$ and verify that the result is positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2+\sqrt3)(2-\sqrt3)&=4-3=1,\\[6pt]
\frac1{2+\sqrt3}&=\frac{2-\sqrt3}{1}=2-\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The conjugate of $2+\sqrt3$ is $2-\sqrt3$. The mixed terms are opposites, so the product is the difference between the squares $4$ and $3$. The product is already one, making the conjugate itself the required reciprocal. The root $\sqrt3$ is less than $2$ because $3<4$, so the answer is positive. This agrees with the sign of the original denominator, which adds two positive quantities. Also, $\sqrt3>1$ because $3>1^2$. Thus the result is less than one, as expected for an original denominator greater than one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rationalize $\dfrac2{\sqrt3-\sqrt{11}}$ and check the sign of the quotient.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac2{\sqrt3-\sqrt{11}}
&=\frac{2(\sqrt3+\sqrt{11})}{3-11}\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{\sqrt3+\sqrt{11}}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The conjugate $\sqrt3+\sqrt{11}$ is strictly positive, so it is an allowed factor for both the numerator and denominator. The product of the two expressions in the denominator is $3-11=-8$. Canceling the common factor of $2$ leaves a minus sign in front of a fraction with a positive denominator. The negative sign was already predictable from $\sqrt3<\sqrt{11}$ and the original numerator $2$. Using $11-3$ in place of $3-11$ would change the sign of the answer. Multiplying the final fraction by the original denominator gives $-\dfrac{3-11}{4}=2$. This recovers the numerator with its original sign.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rationalize $\dfrac1{\sqrt{x}-3}$ on its domain and compare the values at $x=4$ and $x=16$.

::grafico{f="1/(sqrt(x)-3)" xmin=-1.4 xmax=18 ymin=-3 ymax=3 asintoti="x=9" punti="4,-1;16,1" legenda="y=\dfrac1{\sqrt{x}-3}=\dfrac{\sqrt{x}+3}{x-9}" titolo="The rewritten expression still excludes $9$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=[0,+\infty)\setminus\{9\},\\[6pt]
\frac1{\sqrt x-3}
&=\frac{\sqrt x+3}{x-9}\qquad(x\in D),\\[6pt]
\frac{\sqrt4+3}{4-9}&=-1,\\[6pt]
\frac{\sqrt{16}+3}{16-9}&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root requires $x\ge0$. The denominator is zero when $\sqrt x=3$, which occurs at $x=9$. The factor $\sqrt x+3$ is positive throughout the domain, so multiplication by the conjugate introduces no further exclusions. The new numerator is positive, and the denominator $x-9$ has opposite signs on the two intervals. At $x=4$ the original denominator is $2-3=-1$, while at $x=16$ it is $4-3=1$. The marked points on opposite branches of the graph have those values. The endpoint $x=0$ is also allowed. The original fraction gives $-\dfrac13$, and the rationalized form divides $3$ by $-9$.

</div>
