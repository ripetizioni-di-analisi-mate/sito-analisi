## Exercise 1

Calculate and compare $\sqrt{(-5)^2}$ and $\sqrt[3]{(-5)^3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(-5)^2}&=\sqrt{25}=5,\\[6pt]
\sqrt[3]{(-5)^3}&=\sqrt[3]{-125}=-5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An even power turns the negative base into a positive number. The even-index root gives a nonnegative quantity, so the square root of $25$ is $5$. The number $-5$ also has square $25$ and belongs to the solution set of the equation $u^2=25$. The cube preserves the sign of its base. Thus the cube root of $-125$ is negative and recovers the original base. The indices explain why only one pair of operations preserves the sign.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write $\sqrt{(x-3)^2}$ without a radical or an absolute value. Check $x=1$ and $x=5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(x-3)^2}
&=|x-3|\\[6pt]
&=\begin{cases}
3-x & x<3,\\
x-3 & x\ge3.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square inside the root allows every real input. Taking the root gives the absolute value of the base $x-3$. When $x<3$ the base is negative and its opposite $3-x$ is positive. When $x\ge3$ the base is already nonnegative, so the result is $x-3$. At $3$ both formulas would give zero. At $1$ and $5$ the bases are opposites, $-2$ and $2$. The roots agree because both inputs are two units away from $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $\sqrt{(x+1)^2}+\sqrt{(x-3)^2}$ on the different intervals and explain the horizontal part of the graph.

::grafico{f="abs(x+1)+abs(x-3)" xmin=-3.5 xmax=5.5 ymin=-0.5 ymax=10.4 punti="-1,4,pieno,A;3,4,pieno,B;1,4" legenda="y=|x+1|+|x-3|" titolo="Between $-1$ and $3$, the sum of the two distances is $4$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(x+1)^2}+\sqrt{(x-3)^2}
&=|x+1|+|x-3|\\[6pt]
&=\begin{cases}
2-2x & x<-1,\\
4 & -1\le x\le3,\\
2x-2 & x>3.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula changes when one of the bases is zero, at $-1$ and $3$. Between those values, the first absolute value gives $x+1$ and the second gives $3-x$, so the terms in $x$ cancel. This explains the constant height of $4$. To the left of $-1$ both bases are negative, so take their opposites. To the right of $3$ both are positive and can be added directly. At the endpoints the sum stays equal to $4$, as points $A$ and $B$ show.

</div>
