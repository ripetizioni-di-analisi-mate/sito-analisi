## Exercise 1

Calculate $\sqrt{(-5)^2}$.

$$
\sqrt{(-5)^2}=|-5|=5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The principal square root is always nonnegative, so the square root of a square is the absolute value of its base. The original number is negative, so its absolute value has the opposite sign.

Squaring $-5$ gives $25$. The numbers $5$ and $-5$ have the same square, but the square root symbol denotes the nonnegative value. The result is therefore $5$.

The result is $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write $\sqrt{(x-3)^2}$ without a radical for $x<3$. Also evaluate it for $x=1$.

$$
\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=|x-3|=3-x\qquad (x<3).
$$

$$
3-1=2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For every $x$, the square root of $(x-3)^2$ is the distance from $x$ to $3$ on the number line. When $x<3$, the difference $x-3$ is negative, so its absolute value is its opposite $3-x$. At $x=1$, the square is $(-2)^2=4$ and the principal square root is $2$.

</div>
