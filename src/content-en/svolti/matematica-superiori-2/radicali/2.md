## Exercise 1

Write $\sqrt{50}-\sqrt8$ as a multiple of $\sqrt2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{50}-\sqrt8
&=\sqrt{25\cdot2}-\sqrt{4\cdot2}\\[6pt]
&=5\sqrt2-2\sqrt2\\[6pt]
&=3\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original roots have different radicands. Factoring $50$ and $8$ leaves the same factor $2$ under each root after taking out $5$ and $2$. At that point, $\sqrt2$ is a common factor and the coefficients can be subtracted. The result is positive, in agreement with $\sqrt{50}>\sqrt8$. Putting the difference under one root would give $\sqrt{42}$, whose square is $42$. The correct result has square $18$. The calculation also works in reverse. Adding $\sqrt8=2\sqrt2$ to the result recovers $5\sqrt2=\sqrt{50}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $(\sqrt{18}+\sqrt8)-(\sqrt2+2\sqrt3)$ and determine the sign of the result.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sqrt{18}+\sqrt8)-(\sqrt2+2\sqrt3)
&=3\sqrt2+2\sqrt2-\sqrt2-2\sqrt3\\[6pt]
&=4\sqrt2-2\sqrt3,\\[6pt]
(4\sqrt2)^2&=32>12=(2\sqrt3)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Taking out square factors turns $\sqrt{18}$ and $\sqrt8$ into multiples of $\sqrt2$. Subtracting $\sqrt2+2\sqrt3$ means subtracting each of its terms. The coefficient of $\sqrt2$ is therefore $3+2-1=4$, and the coefficient of $\sqrt3$ is $-2$. The simplified roots have different radicands, so they remain separate. To find the sign, compare the two positive quantities $4\sqrt2$ and $2\sqrt3$. The first square is greater, so the first term is greater and the difference is positive. If the minus sign applied only to $\sqrt2$, the contribution in $\sqrt3$ would be positive and the value would change.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $\sqrt{27}-2\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{75}$ and interpret the calculation on a line measured in units of $\sqrt3$.

::grafico{xmin=-1.6 xmax=4.9 ymin=-0.6 ymax=1.8 linee="-1.25,0 4.4,0" segmenti="0,0,0,1.4;3,0,3,0.9;-1,0,-1,0.35;4,0,4,0.35" vettori="0,1.4,3,1.4,3;3,0.9,-1,0.9,-4;-1,0.35,4,0.35,5" punti="0,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;-1,0,pieno;4,0,pieno" etichette="-1,-0.2,−1;0,-0.2,0;3,-0.2,3;4,-0.2,4" assi=0 titolo="Each arrow counts multiples of $\sqrt3$ and the final point is at $4\sqrt3$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{27}-2\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{75}
&=3\sqrt3-2(2\sqrt3)+5\sqrt3\\[6pt]
&=(3-4+5)\sqrt3\\[6pt]
&=4\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Taking out the square factors leaves the same radical in all three terms. The coefficient $-2$ multiplies the factor $2$ from $\sqrt{12}$ as well, so that term contributes $-4$ to the coefficient. The diagram starts at zero, moves forward three units and then back four, reaching $-1$. The third move adds five and ends at $4$. Since each unit on the line measures $\sqrt3$, the algebraic result is $4\sqrt3$. The reversal in direction on the second move represents the subtraction.

</div>
