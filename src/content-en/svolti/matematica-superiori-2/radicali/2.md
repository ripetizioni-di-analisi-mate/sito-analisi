## Exercise 1

Collect like terms in $6\sqrt2-\sqrt8$.

$$
\sqrt8=2\sqrt2
$$

$$
6\sqrt2-2\sqrt2=4\sqrt2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First simplify the square root of eight. The resulting radicals have the same index and radicand, so subtract their coefficients. The factor of the square root of two stays the same.

Since $8=4\cdot2$, the second term equals $2\sqrt2$. After substituting, factor out $\sqrt2$. The remaining coefficient is $6-2=4$.

The result is $4 \sqrt{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Collect like terms in $\sqrt{27}-2\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{75}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{27}-2\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{75}
&=3\sqrt3-4\sqrt3+5\sqrt3\\[6pt]
&=(3-4+5)\sqrt3\\[6pt]
&=4\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three radicands are $9\cdot3$, $4\cdot3$, and $25\cdot3$. Taking out the perfect squares leaves $3$ as the radicand in all three terms. The coefficient $-2$ in the second term also multiplies the factor $2$ taken out of $\sqrt{12}$, so this term is $-4\sqrt3$. Adding the coefficients gives $4\sqrt3$.

</div>
