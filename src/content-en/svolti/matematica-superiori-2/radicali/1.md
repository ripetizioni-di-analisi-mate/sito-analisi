## Exercise 1

Simplify $\sqrt{108}$.

$$
\sqrt{108}=\sqrt{36\cdot3}=6\sqrt3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand contains a perfect-square factor. The square root of the product is the product of the square roots because both factors are nonnegative. Take the square factor out and leave the remaining factor under the radical sign.

The factor taken outside is $6$ because $108=6^2\cdot3$. The remaining factor $3$ is prime. The simplification is complete. To check it, square $6\sqrt3$. The product $36\cdot3$ gives the original radicand $108$.

The result is $6 \sqrt{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $\sqrt{180x^2}$ for $x\in\mathbb R$, and evaluate it for $x=-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{180x^2}
&=\sqrt{(6x)^2\cdot5}\\[6pt]
&=6|x|\sqrt5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
6|-2|\sqrt5=12\sqrt5.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $180=36\cdot5$, the expression inside the radical is the product of the square $(6x)^2$ and the positive number $5$. The square root of $(6x)^2$ is $|6x|=6|x|$. For $x=-2$, the absolute value is $2$, so the result is $12\sqrt5$. Squaring it gives $720$, the value of the radicand $180x^2$ at $x=-2$.

</div>
