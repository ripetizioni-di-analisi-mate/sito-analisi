## Exercise 1

Simplify $\sqrt{108}$ and interpret the factor taken out of the root as a change in length scale.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,6 0,6" linee="2,0 2,6;4,0 4,6;0,2 6,2;0,4 6,4" riferimento=0 etichette="3,-0.45,6√3;-0.73,1,2√3;1,1,12;3,1,12;5,1,12;1,3,12;3,3,12;5,3,12;1,5,12;3,5,12;5,5,12" titolo="The square of area $108$ contains nine squares of area $12$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
108&=36\cdot3=6^2\cdot3,\\[6pt]
\sqrt{108}&=\sqrt{6^2\cdot3}=6\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $36$ is a perfect square. The factor $6$ can be taken out of the root because $6\sqrt3$ is positive and its square is $36\cdot3$. In the diagram, the square of area $108$ is made up of nine squares of area $12$, arranged in three rows and three columns. Each small square has side length $\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3$. The larger side consists of three of those lengths, so it measures $3\cdot2\sqrt3=6\sqrt3$. Splitting the area into squares checks the expression found by factoring the radicand.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Take a factor out of $\sqrt[4]{648}$ and check the result by raising it to the fourth power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
648&=81\cdot8=3^4\cdot8,\\[6pt]
\sqrt[4]{648}&=3\sqrt[4]8,\\[6pt]
(3\sqrt[4]8)^4&=3^4\cdot8=648.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Index four requires four copies of a factor under the root to take one copy out. In the factorization $648=2^3\cdot3^4$, the factor $3$ occurs four times, so one $3$ comes out. The remaining radicand is $8=2^3$. Three copies of $2$ are insufficient for another extraction. Raising the answer to the fourth power checks its magnitude, and the positivity of $3\sqrt[4]8$ checks its sign. Together those conditions identify the principal fourth root. The index of $\sqrt[4]{2^3}$ cannot be reduced either, because $3$ and $4$ share no divisor greater than one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $\sqrt{180x^2}$ for real $x$ and find its value at $x=-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{180x^2}
&=\sqrt{(6x)^2\cdot 5}\\[6pt]
&=|6x|\sqrt5=6|x|\sqrt5,\\[6pt]
6|-2|\sqrt5&=12\sqrt5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand is nonnegative for every $x$, because $180>0$ and $x^2\ge0$. Its square factor is $(6x)^2$, so $|6x|$ comes out of the root. The absolute value accounts for either sign of the variable. At $x=-2$, the simplified form gives $12\sqrt5$, which is nonnegative. Squaring gives $144\cdot5=720$, also the value of $180(-2)^2$. Substituting $x=2$ gives the same result because the radicand depends on $x^2$. Doubling $|x|$ doubles the result, while multiplying the radicand by four.

</div>
