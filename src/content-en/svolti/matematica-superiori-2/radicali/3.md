## Exercise 1

Calculate $\sqrt{12}\cdot\sqrt{48}$ in two ways, first combining the radicands and then simplifying each factor separately.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{12}\cdot\sqrt{48}
&=\sqrt{12\cdot48}=\sqrt{576}=24,\\[6pt]
(2\sqrt3)(4\sqrt3)&=8(\sqrt3)^2=24.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicands are positive, so the product of their roots equals the root of their product. The answer is $24$ because $24^2=576$ and $24$ is positive. In the other method, taking out the roots of $4$ and $16$ gives $2$ and $4$, respectively. Two factors of $\sqrt3$ remain. Their product is $3$, so this calculation also gives $8\cdot3=24$. The second method shows which two factors give the rational contribution $3$. The product is an integer even though neither original radicand is a perfect square.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $\sqrt[3]{-16}\cdot\sqrt[3]4$ and explain the sign of the result.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{-16}\cdot\sqrt[3]4
&=\sqrt[3]{(-16)\cdot4}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt[3]{-64}=-4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both roots have an odd index, so they are defined even for a negative radicand. The first factor is negative and the second is positive, making the product negative. Combining the radicands gives $-64$. Since $(-4)^3=-64$, the root is $-4$. The same result follows by writing $-16=(-2)^3\cdot2$ and noting that $\sqrt[3]2\sqrt[3]4=\sqrt[3]8=2$. The factor $-2$ keeps its sign when taken out of the cube root. To reverse the calculation, divide $-4$ by $\sqrt[3]4$. The quotient is $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{-64}{4}}$, which recovers the original factor $\sqrt[3]{-16}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the domains of $F(x)=\sqrt{x-1}\sqrt{x+2}$ and $G(x)=\sqrt{(x-1)(x+2)}$. State where the formulas coincide.

::grafico{f="sqrt((x-1)*(x+2));sqrt(x-1)*sqrt(x+2)" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-0.8 ymax=5.9 punti="-2,0,pieno;1,0,pieno;2,2" legenda="y=\sqrt{(x-1)(x+2)};y=\sqrt{x-1}\sqrt{x+2}" titolo="To the left of $-2$, only the square root of the product is defined"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_F&=[1,+\infty),\\[6pt]
D_G&=(-\infty,-2]\cup[1,+\infty),\\[6pt]
F(x)&=G(x)\qquad(x\ge1).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first expression requires two nonnegative radicands. The condition $x\ge1$ also gives $x\ge-2$, so it suffices. The second expression requires checking the sign of the product. Both factors are negative for $x<-2$ and nonnegative for $x\ge1$. At $x=-2$ the product is zero, so that point is allowed for $G$. The formula $F$ is still undefined there because $x-1=-3$. The graph shows the curves coinciding only on the branch they share to the right. Between $-2$ and $1$, the product is negative. For example, at $x=0$ the two factors give $(-1)\cdot2=-2$.

</div>
