## Exercise 1

Calculate $\sqrt{9}\cdot\sqrt{36}$.

$$
\sqrt{9}\sqrt{36}=\sqrt{324}=18
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicands are positive and the indices are equal, so the roots can be combined under one radical sign. The product of the radicands is a perfect square. The principal square root is the nonnegative value.

The radicand of the product is $9\cdot36=324=18^2$. Since $18$ is nonnegative, it is the principal square root. Simplifying each radical first gives the same result.

The result is $18$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\sqrt[3]{-16}\cdot\sqrt[3]{4}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{-16}\cdot\sqrt[3]{4}
&=\sqrt[3]{(-16)\cdot 4}\\[6pt]
&=\sqrt[3]{-64}\\[6pt]
&=-4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The index $3$ is odd, so the roots are real even when the radicands are negative. Cubing the product of the radicals gives $(-16)\cdot4=-64$. The cubing function is strictly increasing on the reals, so only one number has that cube. Since $(-4)^3=-64$, the product is $-4$.

</div>
