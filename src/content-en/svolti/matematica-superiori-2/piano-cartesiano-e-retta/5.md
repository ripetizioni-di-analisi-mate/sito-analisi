## Exercise 1

Find the line parallel to $y=4x-3$ that passes through $P(1;2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=4,\\
y-2&=4(x-1),\\
y&=4x-2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4*x-3;4*x-2" punti="1,2,pieno,P" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-4 ymax=7 titolo="The parallel line passes through P"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Parallelism fixes the slope at $4$. The point $P$ determines the position of the translated line. The resulting intercept is $-2$, while the given line has intercept $-3$. The lines have the same direction and different intercepts, so they are distinct parallel lines.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the relative position of $3x-2y+4=0$ and $-6x+4y-8=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(3x-2y+4)&=-6x+4y-8,\\
3x-2y+4=0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -6x+4y-8=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side of the second equation is the left-hand side of the first multiplied by $-2$. Multiplication by a nonzero constant preserves all solutions. Every point on the first line therefore satisfies the second equation, and vice versa. The two equations describe **the same line**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $k$ so that $y=(k-1)x+3$ and $2x+y-1=0$ are distinct parallel lines.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x+y-1=0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=-2x+1,\\
k-1&=-2,\\
k&=-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolating $y$ in the second equation gives slope $-2$. The first line must have the same slope, so set $k-1=-2$. The intercepts, $3$ and $1$, already differ. Hence $k=-1$ gives two distinct parallel lines.

</div>
