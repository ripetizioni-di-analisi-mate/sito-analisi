## Exercise 1

Find the line parallel to $y=4x-3$ that passes through $P(1;2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=4,\\
y-2&=4(x-1),\\
y&=4x-2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4*x-3;4*x-2" xmin=-0.85 xmax=3.45 ymin=-4.3 ymax=7.8 legenda="y=4x-3;y=4x-2" punti="1,2,pieno,P;1,1,pieno" segmenti="1,1,1,2" titolo="The lines have the same direction and differ by one unit in height at $x=1$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Parallelism requires the same slope $4$, so only the intercept needs to be determined. Passing through $P(1;2)$ means that height $2$ must equal $4+q$, giving $q=-2$. The new line has a height one unit greater than the given line at every horizontal coordinate. For example, at $x=1$ their heights are $2$ and $1$. They therefore have the same direction and no common point. Their intercepts $-2$ and $-3$ distinguish them. Setting $4x-2=4x-3$ gives a contradiction regardless of the horizontal coordinate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the relative position of $3x-2y+4=0$ and $-6x+4y-8=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(3x-2y+4)&=-6x+4y-8,\\
3x-2y+4=0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -6x+4y-8=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every coefficient of the second equation, including the constant term, comes from multiplying the corresponding part of the first by $-2$. This multiplication is reversible, so every point satisfying one relation satisfies the other. Solving for $y$ checks it, since both give $\dfrac32x+2$. Both the slope and the intercept agree. The point $(0;2)$ satisfies both equations, and the identical slope-intercept form guarantees that all other points are shared too. The rescaling changes the written coefficients while preserving every point that makes the left side zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $k$ so that $y=(k-1)x+3$ and $2x+y-1=0$ are distinct parallel lines.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x+y-1=0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=-2x+1,\\
k-1&=-2,\\
k&=-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Solving the second equation for $y$ gives slope $-2$. The first has slope $k-1$, so the condition for a shared direction is $k-1=-2$. This gives $k=-1$. At that value the first line meets the vertical axis at $3$ and the second at $1$. Those are different points, so the lines are distinct and parallel. Their heights differ by $2$ for every horizontal coordinate, so the equations never give the same height. The difference in heights at the vertical axis is preserved along the entire graph.

</div>
