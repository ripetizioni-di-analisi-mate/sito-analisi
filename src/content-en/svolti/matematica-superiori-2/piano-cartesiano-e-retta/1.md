## Exercise 1

Find the distance between $A(3;1)$ and $B(6;5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=6-3=3,\\
\Delta y&=5-1=4,\\
AB&=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=\sqrt{25}=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{linee="3,1 6,5;3,1 6,1 6,5" punti="3,1,pieno,A;6,5,pieno,B" etichette="4.5,0.7,3;6.3,3,4" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=0 ymax=6 uguale=1 titolo="The legs have lengths 3 and 4"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $(6;1)$ shares its $y$-coordinate with $A$ and its $x$-coordinate with $B$, so it forms a right triangle with $A$ and $B$. The horizontal leg has length $3$ and the vertical leg has length $4$. The segment $AB$ is the hypotenuse, so the Pythagorean theorem gives its squared length. The positive square root gives the length $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the distance between $A(-2;5)$ and $B(-2;-3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=-2-(-2)=0,\\
\Delta y&=-3-5=-8,\\
AB&=\sqrt{0^2+(-8)^2}=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The endpoints have the same $x$-coordinate, so the segment is vertical. From $A$ to $B$ the $y$-coordinate decreases by $8$. The minus sign indicates the direction of the displacement. The distance is the absolute value of the change, so it is $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the points $B(x;2)$ at distance $5$ from $A(1;-2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2+(2-(-2))^2&=25,\\
(x-1)^2&=25-16=9,\\
x&=1\pm 3,\\
B_1&=(-2;2),\qquad B_2=(4;2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $y$-coordinate of $B$ is fixed, and the vertical displacement has length $4$. The squared distance is $25$, so the square of the horizontal displacement is $9$. One point lies three units to the left of the $x$-coordinate of $A$ and the other three units to its right. In both cases the distance is $\sqrt{9+16}=5$.

</div>
