## Exercise 1

Find the distance between $A(3;1)$ and $B(6;5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=6-3=3,\\
\Delta y&=5-1=4,\\
AB&=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=\sqrt{25}=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=1.85 xmax=7.15 ymin=-0.65 ymax=6.24 linee="3,1 6,5;3,1 6,1 6,5;5.73,1 5.73,1.27 6,1.27" punti="3,1,pieno,A;6,5,pieno,B" etichette="4.5,0.67,3;6.34,2.92,4" titolo="The two legs have lengths three and four. Their hypotenuse is $AB$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

From $A$ to $B$ the horizontal coordinate increases by $3$ and the vertical coordinate increases by $4$. The point $(6;1)$ completes the right triangle with two sides parallel to the axes. The Pythagorean theorem gives hypotenuse $5$, which is the requested distance. Reversing direction changes the displacements to $-3$ and $-4$, while their squares remain $9$ and $16$. The distance is longer than either leg and shorter than the two-leg path of length $7$. The auxiliary point combines the horizontal coordinate of $B$ with the height of $A$, so the legs follow the axes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the distance between $A(-2;5)$ and $B(-2;-3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=-2-(-2)=0,\\
\Delta y&=-3-5=-8,\\
AB&=\sqrt{0^2+(-8)^2}=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The points have the same horizontal coordinate, so they lie on the same vertical line. The horizontal change is zero. Moving from $A$ to $B$ goes down from height $5$ to height $-3$, a change of eight units. The minus sign describes the direction and disappears when computing length. Another check splits the segment at height zero. The two vertical pieces have lengths $5$ and $3$, which again add to $8$. The same vertical line meets the horizontal axis at $(-2;0)$, where the segment is split.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the points $B(x;2)$ at distance $5$ from $A(1;-2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2+(2-(-2))^2&=25,\\
(x-1)^2&=25-16=9,\\
x&=1\pm 3,\\
B_1&=(-2;2),\qquad B_2=(4;2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the required points have height $2$, so the vertical difference from $A$ is $4$. The squared distance is $25$, of which the vertical component accounts for $16$. That leaves $9$ for the squared horizontal displacement. The displacement is $3$ units right or $3$ units left, giving horizontal coordinates $4$ and $-2$. The points are symmetric about $x=1$. The Pythagorean theorem gives $\sqrt{9+16}=5$ in both cases. The horizontal coordinate of $A$ also averages the two computed coordinates because the points are symmetric.

</div>
