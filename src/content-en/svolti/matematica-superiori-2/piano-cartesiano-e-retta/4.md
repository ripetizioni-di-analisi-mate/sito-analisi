## Exercise 1

The line $y=3x+q$ passes through $P(2;13)$. Find $q$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
13&=3\cdot 2+q,\\
q&=13-6=7,\\
y&=3x+7.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $P$ to lie on the line, $y$ must be $13$ when $x=2$. The term $3x$ contributes $6$, so the intercept adds the remaining seven units. Setting $x=0$ in the new equation gives the point $(0;7)$ on the $y$-axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the line with slope $-\dfrac32$ through $P(-2;4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y-4&=-\frac32(x+2),\\
y&=-\frac32x-3+4,\\
y&=-\frac32x+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Point-slope form uses changes measured from $P$. The change in $x$ is $x-(-2)=x+2$. The $y$-coordinate decreases by three halves of a unit for every unit to the right. Expanding the product gives the intercept. When $x=-2$, the right-hand side is $3+1=4$, as required by the given point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write an implicit equation for the line through $P(-3;2)$ and $Q(-3;7)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_P&=x_Q=-3,\\
x&=-3,\\
x+3&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two points have different $y$-coordinates and the same $x$-coordinate. The direction is vertical, so the $x$-coordinate is $-3$ throughout the line. In implicit form $ax+by+c=0$, take $a=1$, $b=0$, and $c=3$. The coefficient of $y$ is zero because every $y$-coordinate is allowed.

</div>
