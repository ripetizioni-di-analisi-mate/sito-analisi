## Exercise 1

The line $y=3x+q$ passes through $P(2;13)$. Find $q$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
13&=3\cdot 2+q,\\
q&=13-6=7,\\
y&=3x+7.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $P$ lies on the line, so its coordinates replace $x$ and $y$ in the relation. At $x=2$ the slope contributes $3\cdot2=6$ to the height. Reaching $13$ requires another $7$, so $q=7$. This is the height at $x=0$. Moving two units left from $P$ produces a drop of $6$ units and gives the same intercept. It is the only starting height that makes the substitution true. Starting from $(0;7)$, two one-unit horizontal steps add $3+3=6$ to reconstruct the given height.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the line with slope $-\dfrac32$ through $P(-2;4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y-4&=-\frac32(x+2),\\
y&=-\frac32x-3+4,\\
y&=-\frac32x+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-1.5*x+1" uguale=1 xmin=-3.7 xmax=2.97 ymin=-3.6 ymax=6.2 punti="-2,4,pieno,P;0,1,pieno" segmenti="-2,4,0,4;0,4,0,1" titolo="From $P$ to the intercept the horizontal coordinate increases by two and the height falls by three."}

<div class="spiegazione">

In point-slope form the term $x-(-2)$ measures the horizontal displacement from $P$. The height starts at $4$ and changes by $-\dfrac32$ per unit right. At $x=0$ the displacement is $2$, so the height becomes $4-3=1$. This is the intercept. The graph passes through $P$ and $(0;1)$ and slopes downwards to the right. Substituting $x=-2$ in slope-intercept form gives $y=4$ again. The two marked points are distinct and determine a unique line with that direction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write an implicit equation for the line through $P(-3;2)$ and $Q(-3;7)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_P&=x_Q=-3,\\
x&=-3,\\
x+3&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The points share horizontal coordinate $-3$ and have different heights, so their line is vertical at $-3$. Moving everything to the left gives $x+3=0$. It imposes no constraint on the height, so both $P$ and $Q$ satisfy it. This general-form equation has coefficient zero for $y$. A form $y=mx+q$ would assign a single height to each horizontal coordinate, which is incompatible with the two given points. The difference of the heights is $5$, so the general two-point formula also gives $-5(x+3)=0$.

</div>
