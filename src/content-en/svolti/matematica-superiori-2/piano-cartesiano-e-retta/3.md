## Exercise 1

Find the slope of the line through $A(3;1)$ and $B(5;7)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=5-3=2,\\
\Delta y&=7-1=6,\\
m&=\frac{6}{2}=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From $A$ to $B$, the $x$-coordinate increases by $2$ and the $y$-coordinate by $6$. The slope expresses the vertical change for one horizontal unit. Here each unit to the right corresponds to three units upward. Reversing the order of the points changes the sign of both differences, so their ratio remains $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare the directions of the line through $A(1;2)$ and $B(1;-3)$ and the line through $C(-2;4)$ and $D(3;4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_A=x_B=1 &\quad\Rightarrow\quad r_{AB}:\ x=1,\\
y_C=y_D=4 &\quad\Rightarrow\quad r_{CD}:\ y=4,\\
m_{CD}&=\frac{4-4}{3-(-2)}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line through $A$ and $B$ has constant $x$-coordinate, so it is vertical and its slope is undefined. The line through $C$ and $D$ has constant $y$-coordinate, so it is horizontal and its slope is zero. Their directions are those of the two coordinate axes, so the lines are perpendicular.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $t$ so that the line through $A(-2;3)$ and $B(t;-1)$ has slope $-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{-1-3}{t-(-2)}&=-2,\qquad t\ne -2,\\
-4&=-2(t+2),\\
t&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical displacement is $-4$. The slope must be $-2$, so the horizontal displacement is $2$. This takes the $x$-coordinate from $-2$ to $0$. The two $x$-coordinates differ, as required for the ratio of the changes to be defined.

</div>
