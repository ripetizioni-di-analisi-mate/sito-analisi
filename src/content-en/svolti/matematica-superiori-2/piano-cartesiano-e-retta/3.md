## Exercise 1

Find the slope of the line through $A(3;1)$ and $B(5;7)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=5-3=2,\\
\Delta y&=7-1=6,\\
m&=\frac{6}{2}=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving from $A$ to $B$ goes two units right and six up. Dividing the vertical change by the horizontal change gives three units up per unit right. The slope is therefore positive. Starting from $B$ makes both differences negative, and their ratio is still $3$. The value describes displacement along the line regardless of which vertical coordinate is chosen as the starting point. Using this slope and $A$ gives $y=3x-8$, which gives exactly $7$ at $x=5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare the directions of the line through $A(1;2)$ and $B(1;-3)$ and the line through $C(-2;4)$ and $D(3;4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_A=x_B=1 &\quad\Rightarrow\quad r_{AB}:\ x=1,\\
y_C=y_D=4 &\quad\Rightarrow\quad r_{CD}:\ y=4,\\
m_{CD}&=\frac{4-4}{3-(-2)}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The points $A$ and $B$ have horizontal coordinate $1$ and distinct heights, so their line is vertical. The ratio of changes would have denominator zero, making its slope undefined. For $C$ and $D$ the height stays $4$ while the horizontal coordinate changes. Their line is horizontal, and the ratio has numerator zero, giving slope zero. The two lines are perpendicular and meet at $(1;4)$, where both conditions hold. On the graph the vertical pair has the same projection onto the horizontal axis. The horizontal pair has the same projection onto the vertical axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $t$ so that the line through $A(-2;3)$ and $B(t;-1)$ has slope $-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{-1-3}{t-(-2)}&=-2,\qquad t\ne -2,\\
-4&=-2(t+2),\\
t&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical change is fixed at $-4$. To give slope $-2$, the horizontal displacement must be positive and equal to $2$. Starting at horizontal coordinate $-2$ and moving two units right gives $t=0$. The algebra excludes $t=-2$ from the outset because that would make the line vertical. Substituting $B(0;-1)$ gives changes $-4$ and $2$, whose ratio is exactly the required slope. The point $B(0;-1)$ also gives intercept $-1$, allowing a check with $y=-2x-1$.

</div>
