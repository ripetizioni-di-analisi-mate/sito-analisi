## Exercise 1

Find the intersection of $y=x+3$ and $y=-x+9$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+3&=-x+9,\\
2x&=6,\\
x&=3,\qquad y=3+3=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x+3;-x+9" xmin=-0.85 xmax=7.4 ymin=0 ymax=10.6 legenda="y=x+3;y=-x+9" punti="3,6,pieno,P" segmenti="3,0,3,6;0,6,3,6" titolo="Projecting $P$ onto the axes gives horizontal coordinate $3$ and height $6$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

At a common point both lines must give the same height for the same horizontal coordinate. Set $x+3$ equal to $-x+9$. This gives $x=3$, and substitution gives $y=6$. Check both expressions, since $3+3=6$ and $-3+9=6$. The graph shows the rising and falling lines meeting there. Their slopes differ, so the equation for equal heights has exactly one solution. Once the common horizontal coordinate is fixed, both functions assign just one height.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the intersection of the lines $x=-2$ and $3x+2y=4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(-2)+2y&=4,\\
2y&=10,\\
y&=5,\qquad P=(-2;5).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical line directly sets $x=-2$, the first coordinate of the intersection. Substitution into the second equation gives $-6+2y=4$, so $y=5$. The coefficient $2$ of $y$ is nonzero, making that height unique. As a check, $(-2;5)$ has the required horizontal coordinate and gives $3(-2)+2\cdot5=4$. Another method solves the second equation for $y$ first and reads its value at the fixed horizontal coordinate. Slope-intercept form is $y=2-\dfrac32x$, giving $5$ at horizontal coordinate $-2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the values of $k$ for which $y=kx+2$ and $y=3x-4$ share a point. Find its horizontal coordinate when it exists and determine when that coordinate is positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
kx+2&=3x-4,\\[6pt]
(3-k)x&=6,\\[6pt]
x&=\frac{6}{3-k}\qquad (k\ne3),\\[6pt]
x>0&\iff k<3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The intercepts are $2$ and $-4$. If $k=3$, the slopes agree and the different intercepts give distinct parallel lines. For $k\ne3$ the lines have exactly one common point, whose horizontal coordinate comes from setting their heights equal. The numerator of $\dfrac6{3-k}$ is positive. Requiring a positive horizontal coordinate therefore gives $3-k>0$, or $k<3$. For example, $k=1$ gives $x=3$, with height $5$ in both equations. At $k=4$, the denominator is negative and the point has horizontal coordinate $-6$.

</div>
