## Exercise 1

Find the midpoint of $A(-3;2)$ and $B(5;6)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_M&=\frac{-3+5}{2}=1,\\
y_M&=\frac{2+6}{2}=4,\\
M&=(1;4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Reach the midpoint by travelling half the displacement from $A$ to $B$. The changes are $8$ horizontally and $4$ vertically. Add half of each to the coordinates of $A$ to reach $(1;4)$. This is the same calculation as taking the two averages. As a check, both from $A$ to $M$ and from $M$ to $B$, the displacement is $4$ units right and $2$ units up. Both changes are positive, so the midpoint is to the right of and above $A$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The midpoint of $AB$ is $M(2;-1)$, and $A(-3;4)$. Find $B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{-3+x_B}{2}&=2, & x_B&=4+3=7,\\
\frac{4+y_B}{2}&=-1, & y_B&=-2-4=-6,\\
B&=(7;-6).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{uguale=1 xmin=-4.25 xmax=8.25 ymin=-7.1 ymax=5.25 linee="-3,4 7,-6" punti="-3,4,pieno,A;2,-1,pieno,M;7,-6,pieno,B" vettori="-3,4,2,4;2,4,2,-1;2,-1,7,-1;7,-1,7,-6" titolo="The horizontal and vertical changes from $A$ to $M$ match those from $M$ to $B$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two averaging equations determine the horizontal and vertical coordinates of $B$ separately. Doubling $x_M$ gives the sum of the horizontal coordinates, from which subtract $x_A=-3$. Doubling $y_M$ gives the sum of the vertical coordinates, from which subtract $y_A=4$. On the graph the displacement from $A$ to $M$ is $(5;-5)$. Repeating it from $M$ reaches $B(7;-6)$. Averaging the endpoints gives $2$ and $-1$ again. The lengths of $AM$ and $MB$ both equal $5\sqrt2$, since the two components have the same magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $k$ so that the midpoint of $A(k;-3)$ and $B(5;7)$ lies on the line $y=2x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=\left(\frac{k+5}{2};2\right),\\
2&=2\cdot\frac{k+5}{2}=k+5,\\
k&=-3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical coordinate of the midpoint is fixed because it depends only on $-3$ and $7$. Their average is $2$. On $y=2x$, a point with height $2$ must have horizontal coordinate $1$. Set the average of $k$ and $5$ equal to $1$, giving $k=-3$. Then the endpoints are $(-3;-3)$ and $(5;7)$. Their midpoint is $(1;2)$ and satisfies the line equation. The parameter moves $M$ along the horizontal line $y=2$, which crosses the given line just once.

</div>
