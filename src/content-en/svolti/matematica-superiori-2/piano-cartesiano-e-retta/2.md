## Exercise 1

Find the midpoint of $A(-3;2)$ and $B(5;6)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_M&=\frac{-3+5}{2}=1,\\
y_M&=\frac{2+6}{2}=4,\\
M&=(1;4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $x$-coordinate goes from $-3$ to $5$. The midpoint is four units to the right of $A$, so its $x$-coordinate is $1$. The $y$-coordinate goes from $2$ to $6$, so the midpoint is two units above $A$. Both displacements, from $A$ to $M$ and from $M$ to $B$, are $(4;2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The midpoint of $AB$ is $M(2;-1)$, and $A(-3;4)$. Find $B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{-3+x_B}{2}&=2, & x_B&=4+3=7,\\
\frac{4+y_B}{2}&=-1, & y_B&=-2-4=-6,\\
B&=(7;-6).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $M$ is the midpoint, the displacement from $M$ to $B$ is the same as the displacement from $A$ to $M$. The first move from $A$ to $M$ increases the $x$-coordinate by $5$ and decreases the $y$-coordinate by $5$. Repeating the same move from $M$ gives $B(7;-6)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $k$ so that the midpoint of $A(k;-3)$ and $B(5;7)$ lies on the line $y=2x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=\left(\frac{k+5}{2};2\right),\\
2&=2\cdot\frac{k+5}{2}=k+5,\\
k&=-3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The midpoint has $y$-coordinate $2$ for every $k$, since the endpoints have fixed $y$-coordinates. The line $y=2x$ contains exactly one point with $y$-coordinate $2$, at $x=1$. Setting the average of the $x$-coordinates equal to $1$ gives $k=-3$. The resulting midpoint is $(1;2)$.

</div>
