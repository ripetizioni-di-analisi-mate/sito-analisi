## Exercise 1

Find the slope of a line perpendicular to $y=3x+2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_1m_2&=-1,\\
3m_2&=-1,\\
m_2&=-\frac13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given line rises three units for every unit to the right. A quarter-turn sends the displacement $(1;3)$ to $(3;-1)$ or its opposite. The new ratio of vertical to horizontal change is $-\dfrac13$. Multiplying by the given slope yields $-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the line perpendicular to $x=-2$ that passes through $P(1;4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=-2,\\
y&=y_P=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given line is vertical because its equation fixes the $x$-coordinate. A perpendicular line is horizontal, so its $y$-coordinate is constant. Since it must pass through $P$, the constant is $4$. The lines meet at $(-2;4)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the line perpendicular to $2x-3y=6$ that passes through $P(3;1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x-3y=6 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=\frac23x-2,\\
m_2&=-\frac32,\\
y-1&=-\frac32(x-3),\\
3x+2y-11&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Slope-intercept form shows that the given line has slope $\dfrac23$. The required slope is $-\dfrac32$ because their product is $-1$. Substituting $P$ into point-slope form determines the line. The final implicit equation is satisfied by $(3;1)$, since $9+2-11=0$.

</div>
