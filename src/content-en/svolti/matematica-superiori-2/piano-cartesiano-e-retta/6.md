## Exercise 1

Find the slope of a line perpendicular to $y=3x+2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_1m_2&=-1,\\
3m_2&=-1,\\
m_2&=-\frac13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the first line, moving one unit to the right produces a rise of three units. Rotating the pair $(1;3)$ by a quarter-turn gives $(-3;1)$. The new direction therefore has slope $-\dfrac13$, since three units to the left correspond to one unit up. The product of the slopes is $3\cdot(-\dfrac13)=-1$. This determines the common direction of all perpendiculars, while the intercept can vary. For example, $y=-\dfrac13x$ has that direction and passes through the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the line perpendicular to $x=-2$ that passes through $P(1;4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=-2,\\
y&=y_P=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line $x=-2$ is vertical, so its perpendicular must be horizontal. A horizontal line has constant height, and passing through $P$ requires that height to be $4$. This gives $y=4$. The intersection is $(-2;4)$, with the horizontal coordinate set by the vertical line and the height set by the horizontal line. The vertical line has undefined slope, so this case uses the orientations of the lines relative to the axes. The line can be checked at the given point by using its vertical coordinate directly.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the line perpendicular to $2x-3y=6$ that passes through $P(3;1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x-3y=6 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=\frac23x-2,\\
m_2&=-\frac32,\\
y-1&=-\frac32(x-3),\\
3x+2y-11&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x/3-2;-1.5*x+5.5" uguale=1 xmin=-0.74 xmax=6.45 ymin=-3.25 ymax=5.8 legenda="2x-3y=6;3x+2y=11" punti="3,1,pieno,P" titolo="The lines have perpendicular directions. The falling line passes through $P$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The given line has slope $\dfrac23$, since isolating $y$ divides the term $2x$ by $3$. The perpendicular has slope $-\dfrac32$ and is fixed by passing through $P$. The form $y-1=-\dfrac32(x-3)$ gives the requested general-form equation. Substituting $P(3;1)$ gives $9+2-11=0$. On the graph the first line rises by two for every three units right, and the second falls by three for every two. The slopes therefore have the required product. The point $P$ does not lie on the given line, since $2\cdot3-3\cdot1=3$ differs from $6$.

</div>
