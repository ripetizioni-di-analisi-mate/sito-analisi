## Exercise 1

Find the distance from $O(0;0)$ to the line $3x+4y-3=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(O,r)&=\frac{|3\cdot0+4\cdot0-3|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3}{5}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the origin the terms containing $x$ and $y$ are zero. The absolute value of the constant term is $3$. The denominator is the length $5$ of the vector $(3;4)$, which is perpendicular to the line. The quotient gives the length of the segment from $O$ to the line along that direction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the distance from $P(-2;5)$ to the vertical line $x=1$ and find the closest point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=(1;5),\\
PH&=|1-(-2)|=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A segment perpendicular to a vertical line is horizontal. The foot $H$ therefore has the same $y$-coordinate as $P$ and the $x$-coordinate $1$ fixed by the line. The distance is the absolute difference of the $x$-coordinates. A point on the line with a $y$-coordinate other than $5$ would require a vertical displacement as well and would be farther away.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the values of $k$ for which $P(1;2)$ is at distance $2$ from the line $3x+4y+k=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{|3\cdot1+4\cdot2+k|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}&=2,\\
|11+k|&=10,\\
11+k&=10\quad\text{or}\quad 11+k=-10,\\
k&=-1\quad\text{or}\quad k=-21.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $k$ varies, all the lines keep the same direction because the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ are fixed. To obtain a distance of $2$, the left-hand side evaluated at $P$ must be $10$ or $-10$. The two values of $k$ give one line on each side of $P$.

</div>
