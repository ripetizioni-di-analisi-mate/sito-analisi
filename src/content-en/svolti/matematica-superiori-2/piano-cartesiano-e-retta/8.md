## Exercise 1

Find the distance from $O(0;0)$ to the line $3x+4y-3=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(O,r)&=\frac{|3\cdot0+4\cdot0-3|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{3}{5}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-0.75*x+0.75" uguale=1 xmin=-1.05 xmax=2.15 ymin=-1.08 ymax=1.9 linee="0,0 0.36,0.48" punti="0,0,pieno,O;0.36,0.48,pieno,H" titolo="The point $H$ lies on the line, and segment $OH$ is perpendicular to it."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting the zero coordinates of the origin reduces the numerator to $|-3|=3$. The denominator is $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$. To locate the foot as well, find a point in direction $(3;4)$ that satisfies the line. A point $(3t;4t)$ gives $25t=3$, so $H(\dfrac9{25};\dfrac{12}{25})$. The segment $OH$ has length $5\cdot\dfrac3{25}=\dfrac35$, confirming the result. Its direction is perpendicular to the given line. The foot also satisfies the given line equation, because $3\cdot\dfrac9{25}+4\cdot\dfrac{12}{25}=3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the distance from $P(-2;5)$ to the vertical line $x=1$ and find the closest point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=(1;5),\\
PH&=|1-(-2)|=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given line is vertical, so the perpendicular through $P$ is horizontal and keeps the height $5$. Its foot has horizontal coordinate $1$ from the vertical line and height $5$ from $P$. The horizontal segment is three units long. Another check takes any point $(1;t)$ on the vertical line. Its squared distance from $P$ is $9+(t-5)^2$, at least $9$ and equal to $9$ only at $t=5$. The line sets the foot’s horizontal coordinate, and minimizing the distance fixes its height.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the values of $k$ for which $P(1;2)$ is at distance $2$ from the line $3x+4y+k=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{|3\cdot1+4\cdot2+k|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}&=2,\\
|11+k|&=10,\\
11+k&=10\quad\text{or}\quad 11+k=-10,\\
k&=-1\quad\text{or}\quad k=-21.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-0.75*x+0.25;-0.75*x+5.25" uguale=1 xmin=-2.1 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1.43 ymax=6.22 linee="-0.2,0.4 2.2,3.6" punti="1,2,pieno,P;-0.2,0.4,pieno;2.2,3.6,pieno" legenda="k=-1;k=-21" titolo="The two perpendicular feet are equally far from $P$ and lie on opposite sides."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting $P$ gives numerator $|11+k|$ and denominator $5$. For distance $2$ the numerator must equal $10$. The absolute value allows $11+k=10$ or $11+k=-10$, giving $k=-1$ or $k=-21$. The two lines are parallel because their coefficients of $x$ and $y$ stay the same. The terms $11+k$ have opposite signs, so the two lines are on opposite sides of $P$. The graph shows the two perpendicular displacements in opposite directions, each two units long.

</div>
