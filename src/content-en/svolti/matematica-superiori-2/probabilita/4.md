## Exercise 1

Two fair dice are rolled independently. Find the probability that the sum is $5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\{(1;4),(2;3),(3;2),(4;1)\},\\
P(E)&=\frac4{36}=\frac19.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A result on the first die determines the required result on the second die. Every value from $1$ to $4$ on the first die gives a valid second result. Starting with $5$ or $6$ would require a nonpositive value. The four pairs account for four of the thirty-six equally likely outcomes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two fair dice are rolled independently. Find the probability that the sum is strictly greater than $9$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{10}&=3,\quad n_{11}=2,\quad n_{12}=1,\\
P(E)&=\frac{3+2+1}{36}=\frac16.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The only allowed sums are $10$, $11$, and $12$. The pairs for $10$ are $(4;6)$, $(5;5)$, and $(6;4)$. For $11$ the pairs are $(5;6)$ and $(6;5)$, and $12$ comes only from $(6;6)$. These cases are disjoint, so add the counts.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two fair dice are rolled independently. Given that the first die shows $4$, find the probability that the sum is at least $9$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_4&=\{(4;1),(4;2),\ldots,(4;6)\},\\
E&=\{(4;5),(4;6)\},\\
P(E\mid D_1=4)&=\frac26=\frac13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $D_1$ be the result of the first die. The condition $D_1=4$ reduces the possible outcomes to six pairs. The second die remains uniform because it is independent of the first. Starting from $4$, a $5$ or $6$ is needed to reach at least $9$. Compare the two favorable outcomes with the six pairs compatible with the given information.

</div>
