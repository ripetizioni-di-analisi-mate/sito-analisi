## Exercise 1

Roll two independent fair dice. Compare the probability of a sum of $6$ with the probability of a sum of $9$. Also find the difference between the two probabilities.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_6&=\{(1;5),(2;4),(3;3),(4;2),(5;1)\},\\[6pt]
E_9&=\{(3;6),(4;5),(5;4),(6;3)\},\\[6pt]
P(E_6)&=\frac5{36},\qquad P(E_9)=\frac4{36},\\[6pt]
P(E_6)-P(E_9)&=\frac1{36}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=7 linee="1,5 5,1;3,6 6,3" punti="1,5;2,4;3,3;4,2;5,1;3,6;4,5;5,4;6,3" titolo="Each point is an ordered pair giving one of the two fixed sums"}

<div class="spiegazione">

With the total fixed, the count starts from the smallest first coordinate that allows a second coordinate from one to six. Increasing the first by one decreases the second by one, which follows a diagonal in the diagram. The diagonal for six crosses five grid points and the one for nine crosses four. The pair $(3;3)$ is counted only once because reversing the order leaves the same pair.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Roll two independent fair dice. You know their total is $8$. What is the probability of reading different scores?

$$
\begin{aligned}
B&=\{(2;6),(3;5),(4;4),(5;3),(6;2)\},\\[6pt]
|A\cap B|&=5-1=4,\\[6pt]
P(A\mid B)&=\frac{\frac4{36}}{\frac5{36}}=\frac45 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given total fixes the set $B$ and removes all other pairs from the calculation. The five remaining pairs had the same initial weight and stay equally likely after the restriction. Only $(4;4)$ has equal scores. Four of the five pairs therefore satisfy $A$. The probability quotient explains why the equal denominators cancel and gives the same count.

</div>

## Exercise 3

With two independent fair dice you win if the total is at least $9$ or if the dice show the same score. Find the probability of winning.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|A|&=4+3+2+1=10\qquad(9,10,11,12),\\[6pt]
|B|&=6,\qquad A\cap B=\{(5;5),(6;6)\},\\[6pt]
P(A\cup B)&=\frac{10+6-2}{36}=\frac7{18}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $A$ describe the total and $B$ the matching scores. The allowed totals have four, three, two, and one ordered pairs, respectively. There are six doubles, one for each face. Double four has total eight, so it falls outside $A$. Only double five and double six also lie in $A$. Those two cases are subtracted once because every winning pair represents one outcome.

</div>
