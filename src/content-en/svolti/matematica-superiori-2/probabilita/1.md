## Exercise 1

A bag contains $12$ tiles that feel identical. Two show a star, four a square, and six a circle. Draw a tile uniformly. Let $S$ be the event “star” and find its probability. Also find how many new star tiles to add to make its probability $\dfrac13$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(S)&=\frac{2}{12}=\frac16,\\[6pt]
\frac{2+k}{12+k}&=\frac13
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3(2+k)=12+k,\\[6pt]
2k&=6\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=3,\\[6pt]
\frac{2+3}{12+3}&=\frac{5}{15}=\frac13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph counts how many tiles are in each category. The probability uses the total number of tiles because each tile has the same weight. Every added star increases both the number of favorable outcomes and the number of possible outcomes. That is why the equation uses $2+k$ above the bar and $12+k$ below. The only solution is a nonnegative integer, so the addition is possible. The new bag has five stars out of fifteen tiles.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Draw a ticket uniformly from those numbered $1$ to $18$. List the event $E$ “a multiple of $3$ that is at least $10$,” find its probability, and calculate $P(E^c)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Omega&=\{1,2,\ldots,18\},\\[6pt]
E&=\{12,15,18\},\\[6pt]
P(E)&=\frac{3}{18}=\frac16,\\[6pt]
P(E^c)&=\frac{15}{18}=\frac56 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number must pass two checks. Start with the first multiple of three that is at least ten, which is twelve. Add three up to the last allowed number, eighteen. The multiples three, six, and nine fail the size condition, while ten and eleven fail the divisibility condition. This leaves three favorable tickets. The remaining fifteen, with the same individual weights, make up the complement.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A spinner with a uniformly random stopping angle has sectors $A,B,C$ measuring $72^\circ,108^\circ,180^\circ$. A $60^\circ$ portion of $C$ is repainted and called $D$. Find $P(C)$ before and after the change, and $P(C\cup D)$ afterward.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{old}}(C)&=\frac{180}{360}=\frac12,\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{new}}(C)&=\frac{180-60}{360}=\frac13,\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{new}}(C\cup D)&=\frac{120+60}{360}=\frac12 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The choice is uniform in angle, so each sector’s probability is its fraction of the full turn. Repainting takes sixty degrees away from $C$ and gives them to a new category. Any point previously in $C$ either remains in $C$ or becomes part of $D$. The two new sectors share at most a boundary, which has probability zero, and together reconstruct the old sector.

</div>
