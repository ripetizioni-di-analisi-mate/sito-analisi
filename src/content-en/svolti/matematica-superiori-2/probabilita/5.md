## Exercise 1

An integer from $1$ to $9$ is selected uniformly. Find the probability that it is a multiple of $2$ or $3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A\cap B&=\{6\},\\
|A\cup B|&=4+3-1=6,\\
P(A\cup B)&=\frac69=\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{zone="a,ab,b" elementi="a:2,4,8;ab:6;b:3,9;u:1,5,7" nomi="A,B" universo=1 titolo="The number 6 is in the intersection"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the diagram, $A$ contains the multiples of $2$, namely $2$, $4$, $6$, and $8$. Set $B$ contains the multiples of $3$, namely $3$, $6$, and $9$. Their only common value is $6$. Subtract one from the sum of the two counts so that $6$ contributes once. The union contains six integers.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a group of $30$ people, $15$ swim, $12$ run, and $5$ do both. What is the probability that a uniformly selected person does exactly one of the two sports?

$$
\begin{aligned}
|A\setminus B|&=15-5=10,\\
|B\setminus A|&=12-5=7,\\
P(E)&=\frac{10+7}{30}=\frac{17}{30}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Remove the five runners from the swimmers and the five swimmers from the runners. Ten people remain in the first group and seven in the second. The resulting groups have no common members, so add their counts.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Of $40$ students, $23$ attend course A, $17$ attend course B, and $8$ attend neither. How many attend both?

$$
\begin{aligned}
|A\cup B|&=40-8=32,\\
|A\cap B|&=23+17-32=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The thirty-two students in the union attend at least one course. The sum $23+17$ counts students in both courses twice. Subtracting the union count from that sum therefore gives the number enrolled in both courses.

</div>
