## Exercise 1

Of $40$ students, $22$ use course $A$, $18$ use course $B$, and $7$ use both. Choose a student uniformly. Find the probability of using at least one course, exactly one course, and neither.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|A\cup B|&=22+18-7=33,\\[6pt]
|(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)|&=(22-7)+(18-7)=26,\\[6pt]
|(A\cup B)^c|&=40-33=7,\\[6pt]
P(A\cup B)&=\frac{33}{40},\quad
P((A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A))=\frac{13}{20},\quad
P((A\cup B)^c)=\frac7{40}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{zone="a,b" elementi="a:15;ab:7;b:11;u:7" universo=1 titolo="The shaded regions represent exactly one of the courses"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagram splits the group into four nonoverlapping regions. The union includes the two side regions and the centre. Exactly one includes only the sides. Neither lies outside both circles. The three requested probabilities do not form a partition because exactly one already lies within at least one. To check the counts, add the four distinct regions instead, giving $15+7+11+7=40$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two events have $P(A)=\dfrac34$ and $P(B)=\dfrac23$. Find the smallest possible intersection probability and build an equally likely example that attains it.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A\cap B)&=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cup B)\\[6pt]
&\ge\frac34+\frac23-1=\frac5{12},\\[6pt]
|A|&=4+5=9,\quad |B|=5+3=8,\quad |\Omega|=4+5+3=12.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{zone="ab" elementi="a:4;ab:5;b:3;u:0" universo=1 titolo="The construction leaves the outside region empty"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The union can have probability at most one, so the smallest overlap occurs when the two regions cover the entire sample space. Twelve equally likely outcomes are used, with four assigned to $A$ only, five to both, and three to $B$ only. The weight of $A$ is nine twelfths and that of $B$ is eight twelfths. A smaller overlap would make the three region counts exceed twelve. The construction shows that the minimum is attained.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a model, $P(A\cup B)=0.70$, $P(A\setminus B)=0.25$, and $P(B\setminus A)=0.15$. Recover the intersection and both marginal probabilities. Are the events independent?

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A\cap B)&=0.70-0.25-0.15=0.30,\\[6pt]
P(A)&=0.55,\qquad P(B)=0.45,\\[6pt]
P(A)P(B)&=0.2475\ne0.30.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The probabilities of the regions that belong to just one event are subtracted from the union. The remainder is the shared region. Then the overlap is added to each side region separately to obtain the marginals. Independence would require the overlap to equal their product. The two values differ. As a check, the overlap and side regions reconstruct the union, leaving $0.30$ outside both events.

</div>
