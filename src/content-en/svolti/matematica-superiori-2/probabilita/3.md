## Exercise 1

A card numbered from $1$ to $9$ is selected uniformly. Find the probability of drawing an even number.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\{2,4,6,8\},\\
P(E)&=\frac49.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cards are equally likely. The four even numbers from $1$ to $9$ are $2$, $4$, $6$, and $8$. The last number, $9$, is odd, so there are five odd numbers. The probabilities are $\dfrac49$ and $\dfrac59$, which add to one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An integer is selected uniformly from $11$ to $23$ inclusive. Find the probability of a multiple of $4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\{12,16,20\},\\
|\Omega|&=23-11+1=13,\\
P(E)&=\frac3{13}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first eligible multiple is $12$, and each subsequent multiple is $4$ larger. The next value after $20$ is $24$, which lies outside the range. Count the possible integers by subtracting the endpoints and adding one, since both $11$ and $23$ count.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An integer from $1$ to $36$ is selected uniformly. Find the probability that it is divisible by $3$ and odd.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=\{3,9,15,21,27,33\},\\
P(E)&=\frac6{36}=\frac16.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are twelve multiples of $3$. Consecutive multiples have opposite parity because their difference is odd. Exactly six of the twelve multiples are therefore odd. The denominator is $36$ because the initial draw uses the entire stated range of integers.

</div>
