## Exercise 1

An urn contains $2$ red balls and $7$ blue balls. Find the probability of drawing a blue ball uniformly at random.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R)&=\frac29,\\
P(\overline R)&=1-\frac29=\frac79.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In one draw, the complement of red is blue because the urn contains only those two colors. The two probabilities add to one. Direct counting confirms the result because seven of the nine outcomes are favorable.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A fair coin is tossed twice independently. Find the probability of at least one head.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\overline E)&=\frac12\cdot\frac12=\frac14,\\
P(E)&=1-\frac14=\frac34.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At least one head includes the outcomes with one head and with two heads. Its complement consists only of the outcome with two tails. Independence allows the two tail probabilities to be multiplied. Three of the four outcomes remain, with heads then tails and tails then heads counted separately.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A light bulb fails with probability $\dfrac15$. Suppose three bulbs fail independently. Find the probability that at least one fails.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\overline E)&=\left(\frac45\right)^3=\frac{64}{125},\\
P(E)&=1-\frac{64}{125}=\frac{61}{125}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The complement of at least one failure requires all three bulbs to work. A single bulb works with probability $\dfrac45$. Independence allows that value to be multiplied three times. Subtracting the product from one accounts for the cases of one, two, and three failures together.

</div>
