## Exercise 1

A bag contains seven correct tiles and one with an error. Draw a tile uniformly three times, returning it to the bag after each draw. Find the probability of seeing at least one tile with an error.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(E^c)&=\frac78\cdot\frac78\cdot\frac78=\frac{343}{512},\\[6pt]
P(E)&=1-\frac{343}{512}=\frac{169}{512}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.55 xmax=7.7 ymin=-0.8 ymax=2.2 linee="0,0 2,0 4,0 6,0" punti="0,0;2,0;4,0;6,0" etichette="0.77,0.56,7/8;2.77,0.56,7/8;4.77,0.56,7/8;5.7,-0.48,Eᶜ" titolo="The complement follows the path of three correct tiles"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $E$ be the requested event. Its complement occurs when every tile is correct. Replacement restores seven correct tiles out of eight before each draw, so the single path of three correct draws is a product of three equal factors. Subtracting it from one includes all cases with one, two, or three errors. Adding the error probabilities for individual draws would count some paths more than once.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A coin gives heads with probability $\dfrac13$. Toss it three times independently. Use a complement to find the probability of at most one head.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(N=2)&=3\cdot\left(\frac13\right)^2\cdot\frac23=\frac6{27},\\[6pt]
P(N=3)&=\left(\frac13\right)^3=\frac1{27},\\[6pt]
P(N\le1)&=1-\frac7{27}=\frac{20}{27}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

“At most one” allows zero heads and one head. The complement allows two or three. Two heads require one tail, which can occupy any of the three positions. Every order has the same product because it contains exactly the same factors. Three heads form a single path. The sum of the probabilities of those four paths is the part to subtract from the complete sample space.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A bag contains five red balls and three blue ones. Draw twice uniformly. Compare the probability of at least one blue with and without replacement. Which is greater?

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{with}}(E)&=1-\frac58\cdot\frac58=\frac{39}{64},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{without}}(E)&=1-\frac58\cdot\frac47=\frac9{14},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{without}}(E)-P_{\mathrm{with}}(E)&=\frac{15}{448}>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In both models the complement is the same event, two reds. The first factor agrees. Without replacement, the first red leaves four reds out of seven, a smaller proportion than five eighths. So the probability of two reds decreases and the probability of at least one blue increases. The positive difference confirms the comparison, and its value shows that the increase is about three percentage points.

</div>
