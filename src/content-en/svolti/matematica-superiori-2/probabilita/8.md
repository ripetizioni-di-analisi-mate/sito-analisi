## Exercise 1

An event occurs $7$ times in $20$ trials. Find its relative frequency and percentage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac7{20}=0.35,\\
100f&=35.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator counts the trials on which the event occurred, and the denominator counts all trials. Multiplying $0.35$ by one hundred gives $35\%$. This figure describes the twenty observed trials. The theoretical probability requires a model of the experiment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An event has relative frequency $0.35$ in $80$ trials. How many trials gave the event?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{n_E}{80}&=0.35,\\
n_E&=80\cdot 0.35=28.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The frequency $0.35$ represents thirty-five hundredths of the trials. Multiplying by the total of $80$ gives the integer count $28$. The other $52$ trials are the complement and have frequency $\dfrac{52}{80}=0.65$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A coin lands heads $8$ times in $10$ tosses and $18$ times in another $30$ tosses. Find the overall relative frequency of heads.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_E&=8+18=26,\\
N&=10+30=40,\\
f_E&=\frac{26}{40}=\frac{13}{20}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second group contains three times as many tosses as the first. To calculate the overall frequency, add the observed heads and the tosses and then divide. Equivalently, average the group frequencies with three times as much weight on the second group. The result $0.65$ is closer to $0.60$ than to $0.80$.

</div>
