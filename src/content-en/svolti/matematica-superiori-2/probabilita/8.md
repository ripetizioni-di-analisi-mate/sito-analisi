## Exercise 1

Two nonoverlapping groups perform the same experiment. The first records $18$ successes in $30$ trials, and the second $21$ in $45$. Find both observed frequencies and the overall frequency. Explain which group carries more weight.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{18}{30}=\frac35,\qquad
f_2=\frac{21}{45}=\frac7{15},\\[6pt]
f&=\frac{18+21}{30+45}=\frac{39}{75}=\frac{13}{25},\\[6pt]
\frac{30}{75}\cdot\frac35+\frac{45}{75}\cdot\frac7{15}
&=\frac{13}{25}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each group frequency measures successes relative to that group’s own trial count. For the combined frequency, each trial must count once. The second group contains forty-five trials, so it carries three fifths of the total weight, against two fifths for the first. The overall frequency lies between the two group frequencies and is closer to the larger group’s value. The weighted calculation gives the same result as adding the raw counts.

</div>

## Exercise 2

After $15$ trials you have seen $6$ successes, after $40$ you have seen $20$, and after $70$ you have seen $32$. Find the frequencies in blocks $16$–$40$ and $41$–$70$ alone. Compare the latter block with the final cumulative frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{16\text{–}40}&=\frac{20-6}{40-15}=\frac{14}{25},\\[6pt]
f_{41\text{–}70}&=\frac{32-20}{70-40}=\frac{12}{30}=\frac25,\\[6pt]
f_{\mathrm{cum}}&=\frac{32}{70}=\frac{16}{35}>\frac25 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph gives cumulative counts, so the count after $40$ trials already includes the first $6$ successes. To examine a block, the counts at its endpoints are subtracted on both the trial and success axes. The final cumulative frequency uses all the trials. The last block’s frequency is smaller than the cumulative one because the first $40$ trials had already accumulated $20$ successes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A set of $50$ completed trials has $28$ successes. Add $15$ more trials. What is the minimum number of new successes needed to make the overall frequency at least $60\%$? Show that the threshold can be met.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{28+k}{65}&\ge\frac35,\\[6pt]
28+k&\ge39,\qquad k\ge11,\\[6pt]
\frac{28+11}{65}&=\frac{39}{65}=\frac35.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The new denominator is fixed by the total number of trials, regardless of their outcomes. The numerator increases by a whole number $k$ between zero and fifteen. The minimum total of successes is thirty-nine. There are already twenty-eight, so eleven more are needed. The threshold is feasible because eleven is at most fifteen. Ten new successes would leave the total at thirty-eight and fall short.

</div>
