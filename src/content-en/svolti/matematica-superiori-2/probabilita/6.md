## Exercise 1

An urn contains $3$ red balls and $5$ blue balls. Find the probability of two red balls in two uniform draws with replacement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(RR)&=\frac38\cdot\frac38=\frac9{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After the first draw the ball is returned to the urn. The second draw is from eight balls, three of which are red, whatever the first outcome. Both draws must be red, so multiply the two probabilities.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An urn contains $2$ red balls and $3$ blue balls. Two uniform draws are made with replacement. Find the probability of drawing different colors.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(RB)&=\frac25\cdot\frac35=\frac6{25},\\
P(BR)&=\frac35\cdot\frac25=\frac6{25},\\
P(E)&=\frac{12}{25}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A red and a blue ball can occur in two orders. Replacement keeps each color probability the same from one draw to the next. The product along either path is $\dfrac6{25}$. The paths are mutually exclusive because their first draws differ in color, so their probabilities add.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An urn contains $1$ green ball and $3$ blue balls. Three uniform draws are made with replacement. Find the probability of exactly one green ball.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(VBB)&=\frac14\cdot\frac34\cdot\frac34=\frac9{64},\\
P(E)&=3\cdot\frac9{64}=\frac{27}{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write $V$ for green and $B$ for blue. The green ball may occur in any one of the three positions. Each position gives a product of one probability $\dfrac14$ and two probabilities $\dfrac34$. The three orders therefore have the same probability. Since each qualifying outcome has green in just one position, add the three contributions.

</div>
