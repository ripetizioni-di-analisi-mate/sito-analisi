## Exercise 1

An urn contains $4$ red balls and $3$ blue balls. Find the probability of two red balls in two uniform draws without replacement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(RR)&=\frac47\cdot\frac36=\frac27.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before the first draw there are four red balls among seven. After a red ball is drawn, three red balls remain among six balls. The second fraction uses that updated composition. Multiplying the two ratios gives the probability of the path with red on both draws.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An urn contains $3$ red balls and $2$ blue balls. Two uniform draws are made without replacement. Find the probability of getting two different colors.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(RB)&=\frac35\cdot\frac24=\frac3{10},\\
P(BR)&=\frac25\cdot\frac34=\frac3{10},\\
P(E)&=\frac35.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If the first ball is red, two of the remaining four balls are blue. If the first is blue, three of the remaining four are red. Each path has probability $\dfrac3{10}$. Both orders are allowed, so the total probability is $\dfrac35$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An urn contains $3$ red balls, $2$ blue balls, and $1$ green ball. Two uniform draws are made without replacement. Given that the first ball is not green, find the probability that the second is green.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(V_2\mid R_1)&=\frac15,\\
P(V_2\mid B_1)&=\frac15,\\
P(V_2\mid\overline{V_1})&=\frac15.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write $R$, $B$, and $V$ for red, blue, and green. The first ball can be red or blue. In either case five balls remain, and the green ball is still in the urn. The probability of green on the second draw is therefore the same in both cases compatible with the information, namely $\dfrac15$.

</div>
