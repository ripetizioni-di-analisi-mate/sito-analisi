## Exercise 1

In the system $\begin{cases}x+y+z=12\\x+y=7\\x-y=-1\end{cases}$, find $z$.

$$
z=12-(7)=5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second equation already gives the sum of $x$ and $y$. Subtracting it from the first isolates $z$. The last two equations have a nonzero determinant, so they are consistent and the value obtained is part of the solution of the system.

To check that $z$ is part of a solution triple, use the last equation as well. Adding $x+y=7$ and $x-y=-1$ gives $2x=6$, so $x=3$ and $y=4$. The sum of the three coordinates gives $7+5=12$.

The result is $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the system $\begin{cases}x+y+z=8\\2x+y+z=10\\x-y+z=2\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x+y+z)-(x+y+z)&=10-8\\[6pt]
x&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
y+z=6\\
-y+z=0
\end{cases}
\implies 2z=6.
$$

$$
z=3,\qquad y=6-3=3.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first and second equations have the same coefficients for $y$ and $z$, so taking their difference isolates $x$. With $x=2$, the first and third equations become two equations in $y,z$ with opposite coefficients of $y$. Their sum determines $z$, and the first equation determines $y$. The triple $(2;3;3)$ satisfies all three equations because $2+3+3=8$, $2\cdot2+3+3=10$, and $2-3+3=2$.

</div>
