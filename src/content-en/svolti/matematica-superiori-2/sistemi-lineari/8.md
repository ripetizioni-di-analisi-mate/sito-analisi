## Exercise 1

In the system $\begin{cases}x+y+z=12\\x+y=7\\x-y=-1\end{cases}$, find $z$.

$$
z=12-(7)=5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second equation already gives the sum of $x$ and $y$. Subtracting it from the first isolates $z$. The last two equations have a nonzero determinant, so they are consistent and the value obtained is part of the solution of the system.

To check that $z$ is part of a solution triple, use the last equation as well. Adding $x+y=7$ and $x-y=-1$ gives $2x=6$, so $x=3$ and $y=4$. The sum of the three coordinates gives $7+5=12$.

The result is $5$.

The third row also recovers the other two coordinates. With $x+y=7$ and $x-y=-1$ we get $x=3$ and $y=4$. The triple $(3;4;5)$ satisfies all the conditions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Describe all the solution triples of $\begin{cases}x+y=5\\y+z=4\\x+2y+z=9\end{cases}$. Identify a free variable and give two examples.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+y)+(y+z)&=5+4=9,\\[6pt]
y&=t,\qquad x=5-t,\qquad z=4-t,\\[6pt]
S&=\{(5-t;t;4-t):t\in\mathbb R\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4-x" legenda="z=4-y" nomi="y,z" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=7 punti="0,4;2,2" titolo="In the yz-plane the projections of the triples follow z = 4 − y"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Adding the first two rows reproduces the third, so the third adds no restriction. Choose $y=t$ freely. Each choice determines $x$ in the first row and $z$ in the second. For $t=0$ the triple is $(5;0;4)$ and for $t=2$ it is $(3;2;2)$. The $y,z$ plane shows the relation $z=4-y$. The other relation gives $x$ from the same horizontal coordinate, so the description covers every solution triple.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the system $\begin{cases}x+y+z=8\\2x+y+z=10\\x-y+z=2\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x+y+z)-(x+y+z)&=10-8\\[6pt]
x&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
y+z=6\\
-y+z=0
\end{cases}
\implies 2z=6.
$$

$$
z=3,\qquad y=6-3=3.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first and second equations have the same coefficients for $y$ and $z$, so taking their difference isolates $x$. With $x=2$, the first and third equations become two equations in $y,z$ with opposite coefficients of $y$. Their sum determines $z$, and the first equation determines $y$. The triple $(2;3;3)$ satisfies all three equations because $2+3+3=8$, $2\cdot2+3+3=10$, and $2-3+3=2$.

Checking the three original rows gives $2+3+3=8$, $4+3+3=10$, and $2-3+3=2$. Checking only the sum would not distinguish triples with the same totals.

</div>
