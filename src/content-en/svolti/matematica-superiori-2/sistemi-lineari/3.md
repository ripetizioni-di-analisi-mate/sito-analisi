## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}\dfrac x2+\dfrac y3=7\\[3pt]\dfrac x2-\dfrac y3=-1\end{cases}$.

$$
x=7+(-1)=6
$$

$$
\frac{2y}3=7-(-1)=8
$$

$$
y=12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fractions have nonzero numerical denominators, so there are no values to exclude. The sum eliminates $y$, while the difference eliminates $x$. The two operations give the unknowns directly.

In the sum, $\dfrac x2+\dfrac x2=x$. The difference contains $\dfrac y3+\dfrac y3=\dfrac{2y}3$ because a negative term from the second equation is subtracted from the first. For the pair obtained, the two terms have values $3$ and $4$. This recovers $3+4=7$ and $3-4=-1$.

The result is $\left(6;\,12\right)$.

The fractions have the same absolute coefficient on $y$, so the rows can be added directly. At $(6;12)$ the two terms have magnitudes $3$ and $4$, and the second row uses their difference.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Clear the denominators row by row and solve $\begin{cases}\dfrac{x+1}{4}+\dfrac y2=3\\[3pt]\dfrac{x-2}{3}-\dfrac y3=1\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+1+2y&=12\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x+2y=11,\\[6pt]
x-2-y&=3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x-y=5,\\[6pt]
3y&=6\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=2,\qquad x=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(11-x)/2;x-5" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=-2 ymax=7 punti="7,2,pieno,P" titolo="Clearing denominators preserves the two lines and their intersection"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Clear the first row by multiplying every term by four. A factor of three is enough for the second row. Its term $-2$ belongs to the numerator and remains after multiplication. Subtracting the second new equation from the first leaves $3y=6$. The pair $(7;2)$ gives $\dfrac84+\dfrac22=3$ in the first original row and $\dfrac53-\dfrac23=1$ in the second.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}\dfrac{x-1}{3}+\dfrac{y}{2}=3\\[3pt]\dfrac{x}{3}-\dfrac{y+1}{2}=-\dfrac76\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
2(x-1)+3y=18\\
2x-3(y+1)=-7
\end{cases}
\iff
\begin{cases}
2x+3y=20\\
2x-3y=-4.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x&=16\\[6pt]
x&=4\\[6pt]
3y&=20-8=12.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The least common multiple of the denominators is $6$. Multiplying by $6$ gives $2(x-1)$ from $\dfrac{x-1}{3}$ and $-3(y+1)$ from $-\dfrac{y+1}{2}$. The second expression therefore contributes $-3y-3$. After collecting terms, adding the equations eliminates $y$ and gives $x=4$. The first simplified equation then gives $y=4$. The original equations give $\dfrac{4-1}{3}+\dfrac42=3$ and $\dfrac43-\dfrac{4+1}{2}=-\dfrac76$, so the solution is $(4;4)$.

Before eliminating $y$, distribute the multipliers to the constants inside the brackets too. In the second row, $-3(y+1)$ contributes $-3$, which is added back on the right as $+3$.

</div>
