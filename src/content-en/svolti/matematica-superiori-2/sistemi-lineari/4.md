## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}2x+3y=19\\x+2y=11\end{cases}$ using Cramer's rule.

$$
D=2\cdot2-3\cdot1=1
$$

$$
D_x=19\cdot2-3\cdot11=5
$$

$$
D_y=2\cdot11-19=3
$$

$$
x=\frac{D_x}D=5,\qquad y=\frac{D_y}D=3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is nonzero, so the system has a unique solution. For the determinant associated with $x$, replace the first column with the constant terms. For the determinant associated with $y$, replace the second column. Divide each determinant by $D$.

Here $D=1$, so the values of $D_x$ and $D_y$ are the two coordinates. The check in the first equation is $2\cdot5+3\cdot3=19$. In the second equation it is $5+2\cdot3=11$. Both calculations give the original constant terms.

The result is $\left(5;\,3\right)$.

Cramer’s common denominator is one here, so the coordinates equal the numerator determinants. The checks on the left sides give $10+9=19$ and $5+6=11$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Use Cramer’s rule to solve $\begin{cases}2x-3y=1\\4x+y=9\end{cases}$. Explain the effect of swapping the rows.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=2\cdot1-(-3)\cdot4=14,\\[6pt]
D_x&=1\cdot1-(-3)\cdot9=28,\\[6pt]
D_y&=2\cdot9-1\cdot4=14,\\[6pt]
x&=\frac{28}{14}=2,\qquad
y=\frac{14}{14}=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(2*x-1)/3;9-4*x" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=7 punti="2,1,pieno,P" titolo="The pair from Cramer’s rule lies on both lines"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The negative coefficient of $y$ enters a product that is subtracted. That gives determinant fourteen. Each variable’s determinant retains the other column and replaces that variable’s column with the constants. Swapping the rows gives $-14$, $-28$, and $-14$ in the order $D,D_x,D_y$. The ratios are unchanged. The pair $(2;1)$ gives $4-3=1$ and $8+1=9$, matching the intersection of the lines.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}3x+2y=7\\2x+3y=3\end{cases}$ using Cramer's rule.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=3\cdot3-2\cdot2=5\\[6pt]
D_x&=7\cdot3-2\cdot3=15\\[6pt]
D_y&=3\cdot3-7\cdot2=-5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
x=\frac{15}{5}=3,\qquad y=\frac{-5}{5}=-1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is $5$, so the system has a unique solution. To compute $D_y$, keep the column for $x$ and replace the column for $y$ with the constant terms $7$ and $3$ in the original equation order. A negative determinant here produces a negative y-coordinate, as confirmed by $3\cdot3+2(-1)=7$ and $2\cdot3+3(-1)=3$. The pair is $(3;-1)$.

The second equation confirms the negative vertical coordinate because $2\cdot3+3(-1)=3$. Keeping coefficients and constants in separate columns helps you replace the right column in each determinant.

</div>
