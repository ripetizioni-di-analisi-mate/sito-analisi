## Exercise 1

How many solutions does $\begin{cases}x+2y=5\\2x+4y=11\end{cases}$ have?

$$
2x+4y=10
$$

$$
10=11\quad\text{is impossible}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the first equation by two makes its left side match that of the second equation. The constant terms differ, so no pair can satisfy both. The system has no solution.

In the plane, the first line has ordinate $\dfrac{5-x}2$ and the second has ordinate $\dfrac{11-2x}4$. Their slopes are equal and their vertical intercepts are $\dfrac{5}2$ and $\dfrac{11}4$. The two lines are therefore parallel and distinct.

The result is $\text{No solution}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

How many solutions does $\begin{cases}x+2y=6\\2x+4y=13\end{cases}$ have?

$$
2x+4y=12
$$

$$
12=13\quad\text{is impossible}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the first equation by two makes its left side match that of the second equation. The constant terms differ, so no pair can satisfy both. The system has no solution.

In the plane, the first line has ordinate $\dfrac{6-x}2$ and the second has ordinate $\dfrac{13-2x}4$. Their slopes are equal and their vertical intercepts are $\dfrac{6}2$ and $\dfrac{13}4$. The two lines are therefore parallel and distinct.

The result is $\text{No solution}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the number of solutions of $\begin{cases}2x-3y=4\\-4x+6y=-9\end{cases}$.

$$
2(2x-3y)+(-4x+6y)=2\cdot4-9
$$

$$
0=-1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of the second equation are those of the first multiplied by $-2$. If the constant term were also $-2\cdot4=-8$, the equations would describe the same line. The constant term is $-9$. Adding twice the first equation to the second leaves $0=-1$, so the solution set is empty.

</div>
