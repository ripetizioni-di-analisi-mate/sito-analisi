## Exercise 1

How many solutions does $\begin{cases}x+2y=5\\2x+4y=11\end{cases}$ have?

$$
2x+4y=10
$$

$$
10=11\quad\text{is impossible}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the first equation by two makes its left side match that of the second equation. The constant terms differ, so no pair can satisfy both. The system has no solution.

In the plane, the first line has ordinate $\dfrac{5-x}2$ and the second has ordinate $\dfrac{11-2x}4$. Their slopes are equal and their vertical intercepts are $\dfrac{5}2$ and $\dfrac{11}4$. The two lines are therefore parallel and distinct.

The result is $\text{No solution}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify $\begin{cases}x-3y=2\\-2x+6y=-4\end{cases}$ and, if possible, describe its solutions with a parameter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(x-3y)&=-2\cdot2=-4,\\[6pt]
y&=t,\qquad x=2+3t,\\[6pt]
S&=\{(2+3t;t):t\in\mathbb R\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2)/3" xmin=-3 xmax=8 ymin=-3 ymax=4 punti="2,0;5,1" titolo="Both equations describe the same line"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Every term of the second row is the first row multiplied by negative two, including the constant. Elimination therefore gives $0=0$. The line from the first equation remains. Choosing a real $y$ on it determines $x$. For instance $t=0$ gives $(2;0)$ and $t=1$ gives $(5;1)$. Every pair in the parametrisation is needed because each parameter value satisfies both rows.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $\begin{cases}0x+0y=0\\0x+0y=k\end{cases}$, give the solution set when $k=0$ and when $k\ne0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k=0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S=\mathbb R^2,\\[6pt]
k\ne0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad S=\varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation always holds. The second imposes no relation on either coordinate and only requires the number $k$ to equal zero. If it is zero every pair is allowed, for example $(0;0)$ and $(7;-3)$. If it is nonzero, every pair fails the second equation. No equation with nonzero coefficients remains after classification, so there is no case in which the solution set is a single line.

</div>
