## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}3x+2y=18\\x-2y=-2\end{cases}$ by elimination.

$$
4x=18+(-2)=16
$$

$$
x=4,\qquad 2y=18-3\cdot4=6
$$

$$
y=3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $y$ are opposites, so adding the equations eliminates $y$. After dividing by the coefficient of $x$, use the first equation to find $y$. Elimination preserves the solutions of the system.

Adding gives $4x=16$, which fixes the value of $x$. To recover $y$, keep either original equation. The second equation gives the check $x-2y=4-2\cdot3=-2$, and the first gives $3\cdot4+2\cdot3=18$.

The result is $\left(4;\,3\right)$.

Substituting into $x-2y=-2$ gives $4-6=-2$. Reversing the coordinates would make the second left side $3-8=-5$. Coordinate order matters even after correct elimination.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\begin{cases}3x+4y=6\\5x+4y=14\end{cases}$ by elimination without multiplying the equations first. Explain how to recover the row you replace.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5x+4y)-(3x+4y)&=14-6,\\[6pt]
2x&=8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=4,\\[6pt]
3\cdot4+4y&=6\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\frac32.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(6-3*x)/4;(14-5*x)/4" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-5 ymax=6 punti="4,-1.5,pieno,P" titolo="Subtracting the rows determines the horizontal coordinate of the intersection"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The rows have equal coefficients on $y$. Subtracting the first from the second cancels the two equal terms, while adding would double them. The calculation fixes $x=4$, and the retained first row gives $4y=-6$. To reconstruct the old second row, add $2x=8$ to the first. The checks in the original system give $12-6=6$ and $20-6=14$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}3x+2y=5\\5x-3y=2\end{cases}$ by elimination.

$$
\begin{cases}
9x+6y=15\\
10x-6y=4
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
19x&=19\\[6pt]
x&=1\\[6pt]
2y&=5-3=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To eliminate $y$, choose $6$ and $-6$ as the new coefficients. Multiply the entire first equation by $3$ and the entire second equation by $2$, including the constant terms. Adding gives $19x=19$, so $x=1$. The original first equation gives $y=1$. The pair $(1;1)$ satisfies both conditions because $3+2=5$ and $5-3=2$.

The products of the coefficients on $y$ give magnitude six, the smallest that permits cancellation. The solution $(1;1)$ checks both constants, $3+2=5$ and $5-3=2$.

</div>
