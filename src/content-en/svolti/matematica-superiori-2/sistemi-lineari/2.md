## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}3x+2y=18\\x-2y=-2\end{cases}$ by elimination.

$$
4x=18+(-2)=16
$$

$$
x=4,\qquad 2y=18-3\cdot4=6
$$

$$
y=3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $y$ are opposites, so adding the equations eliminates $y$. After dividing by the coefficient of $x$, use the first equation to find $y$. Elimination preserves the solutions of the system.

Adding gives $4x=16$, which fixes the value of $x$. To recover $y$, keep either original equation. The second equation gives the check $x-2y=4-2\cdot3=-2$, and the first gives $3\cdot4+2\cdot3=18$.

The result is $\left(4;\,3\right)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\begin{cases}3x+2y=23\\x-2y=-3\end{cases}$ by elimination.

$$
4x=23+(-3)=20
$$

$$
x=5,\qquad 2y=23-3\cdot5=8
$$

$$
y=4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $y$ are opposites, so adding the equations eliminates $y$. After dividing by the coefficient of $x$, use the first equation to find $y$. Elimination preserves the solutions of the system.

Adding gives $4x=20$, which fixes the value of $x$. To recover $y$, keep either original equation. The second equation gives the check $x-2y=5-2\cdot4=-3$, and the first gives $3\cdot5+2\cdot4=23$.

The result is $\left(5;\,4\right)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}3x+2y=5\\5x-3y=2\end{cases}$ by elimination.

$$
\begin{cases}
9x+6y=15\\
10x-6y=4
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
19x&=19\\[6pt]
x&=1\\[6pt]
2y&=5-3=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To eliminate $y$, choose $6$ and $-6$ as the new coefficients. Multiply the entire first equation by $3$ and the entire second equation by $2$, including the constant terms. Adding gives $19x=19$, so $x=1$. The original first equation gives $y=1$. The pair $(1;1)$ satisfies both conditions because $3+2=5$ and $5-3=2$.

</div>
