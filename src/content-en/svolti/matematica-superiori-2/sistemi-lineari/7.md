## Exercise 1

There are 17 students in a group. There are 5 more girls than boys. How many boys are there?

$$
\begin{cases}r+b=17\\r-b=5\end{cases}
$$

$$
2b=17-5=12
$$

$$
b=6
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $r$ be the number of girls and $b$ the number of boys. The sum gives the total and the difference expresses the comparison. Subtracting the equations gives twice the number of boys.

In the first equation, $r$ and $b$ have the same sign. In the second equation, the term is $-b$. Subtracting the second equation from the first gives $b-(-b)=2b$. The number of girls is therefore $6+5=11$, and adding the two groups gives the original total $17$. Both numbers are nonnegative integers.

The result is $6$ boys.

Six boys leave eleven girls. The difference is five and the sum is seventeen. The values are nonnegative integers, as a count of people requires.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A till records $18$ tickets sold at either $9$ or $5$ euros, for $123$ euros in total. Is this possible? State the final constraint on the variables.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+y&=18,\qquad 9x+5y=123,\\[6pt]
9x+5(18-x)&=123,\\[6pt]
4x&=33\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x=\frac{33}{4},\quad y=\frac{39}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="18-x;(123-9*x)/5" xmin=0 xmax=18 ymin=0 ymax=23 punti="8.25,9.75,vuoto" titolo="The intersection does not have whole-number coordinates"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Use $x$ for the nine-euro tickets and $y$ for the five-euro ones. Pricing all tickets at the lower rate starts at ninety euros. Each nine-euro ticket raises the total by four euros. The difference of thirty-three euros is not a multiple of four. The equations have one real pair, visible in the graph, but ticket counts must be nonnegative integers. The recorded revenue is incompatible with the prices.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A group buys $19$ tickets. Each full-price ticket costs $7$ euros and each discounted ticket costs $4$ euros. The total is $100$ euros. How many tickets of each type did the group buy?

$$
\begin{cases}
i+r=19\\
7i+4r=100
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
7i+4(19-i)&=100\\[6pt]
3i&=24\\[6pt]
i&=8,\quad r=19-8=11.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknowns $i$ and $r$ are the numbers of full-price and discounted tickets. The first equation counts the tickets. The second multiplies each number of tickets by the corresponding price. After substituting $r=19-i$, each full-price ticket adds $7-4=3$ euros to the cost of a discounted ticket. The $8$ full-price and $11$ discounted tickets give $8+11=19$ tickets and cost $7\cdot8+4\cdot11=100$ euros.

Subtracting the cost of nineteen reduced-price tickets isolates the contribution of the full-price ones. Each adds three euros, so the extra twenty-four euros correspond to eight full-price tickets.

</div>
