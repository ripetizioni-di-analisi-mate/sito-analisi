## Exercise 1

There are 17 students in a group. There are 5 more girls than boys. How many boys are there?

$$
\begin{cases}r+b=17\\r-b=5\end{cases}
$$

$$
2b=17-5=12
$$

$$
b=6
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $r$ be the number of girls and $b$ the number of boys. The sum gives the total and the difference expresses the comparison. Subtracting the equations gives twice the number of boys.

In the first equation, $r$ and $b$ have the same sign. In the second equation, the term is $-b$. Subtracting the second equation from the first gives $b-(-b)=2b$. The number of girls is therefore $6+5=11$, and adding the two groups gives the original total $17$. Both numbers are nonnegative integers.

The result is $6$ boys.

</div>

## Exercise 2

$$
\begin{cases}r+b=19\\r-b=5\end{cases}

$$
2b=19-5=14
$$

$$
b=7
$$

The number of girls is therefore $7+5=12$, and adding the two groups gives the original total $19$. Both numbers are nonnegative integers.

## Exercise 3

A group buys $19$ tickets. Each full-price ticket costs $7$ euros and each discounted ticket costs $4$ euros. The total is $100$ euros. How many tickets of each type did the group buy?

$$
\begin{cases}
i+r=19\\
7i+4r=100
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
7i+4(19-i)&=100\\[6pt]
3i&=24\\[6pt]
i&=8,\quad r=19-8=11.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknowns $i$ and $r$ are the numbers of full-price and discounted tickets. The first equation counts the tickets. The second multiplies each number of tickets by the corresponding price. After substituting $r=19-i$, each full-price ticket adds $7-4=3$ euros to the cost of a discounted ticket. The $8$ full-price and $11$ discounted tickets give $8+11=19$ tickets and cost $7\cdot8+4\cdot11=100$ euros.

</div>
