## Exercise 1

Suppose $ABC\sim QRP$, with $AB=3$, $AC=4$, $BC=5$, and $QR=7.5$. Find $QP$ and $RP$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    k&=\frac{QR}{AB}=\frac52,\\
    QP&=4\cdot\frac52=10,\qquad RP=5\cdot\frac52=\frac{25}{2}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The corresponding vertices are $A,Q$, $B,R$, and $C,P$. The vertex order in the similarity statement decides the correspondence, even when the second triangle’s letters are not alphabetical. Leg $AC$ matches $QP$, and hypotenuse $BC$ matches $RP$. In the diagram the right-angle vertices pick out the same pair $A,Q$. Checking all three ratios gives $QR/AB=QP/AC=RP/BC=5/2$. Use the same direction in each quotient, from the first triangle to the second.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $AB=6$, $BC=8$, $DE=9$, $EF=12$, and $\angle ABC=\angle DEF=90^\circ$, prove that the triangles are similar and find $DF$ if $AC=10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{DE}{AB}&=\frac96=\frac32=\frac{12}{8}=\frac{EF}{BC},\\
    \triangle ABC&\sim\triangle DEF,\\
    DF&=\frac32\cdot10=15.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angles at $B$ and $E$ are included between their respective sides with equal ratios. Their position is what makes the SAS similarity criterion apply. Enlarging the first triangle by $3/2$ gives the two legs of the second and the same angle between them. The rescaled triangles are congruent by SAS. The hypotenuse therefore scales by the same factor. As a check, $9^2+12^2=225=15^2$, consistent with the second triangle’s right angle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The first triangle has sides $4,5,6$, and the corresponding sides of the second are $8,10,13$. Decide whether they are similar and identify which length to change to obtain a scale factor of $2$.

$$
\frac84=\frac{10}{5}=2,\qquad \frac{13}{6}\ne2,\qquad 2\cdot6=12.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two pairs give a single candidate scale factor. The included angle is not given, so proportionality in those two pairs is insufficient. The required third side is $12$, while the given one is $13$. Replacing $13$ with $12$ makes all three ratios equal and the SSS criterion applies. The triangle inequality also checks: in the modified triangle, $8+10$ exceeds the largest side $12$.

</div>
