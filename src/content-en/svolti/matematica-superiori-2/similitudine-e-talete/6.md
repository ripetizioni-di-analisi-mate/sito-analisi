## Exercise 1

Three parallel lines cross two transversals. The consecutive segments on the first transversal measure $4$ and $8$ cm. The segment corresponding to the first measures $12$ cm on the second transversal. Find the other segment.

$$
\frac{4}{8}=\frac{12}x
$$

$$
x=\frac{8\cdot12}{4}=24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The intercept theorem guarantees that corresponding segments on the two transversals are proportional. Write each ratio in the same order. Equating the cross products isolates the unknown segment.

On the second transversal, $12$ corresponds to $4$. Apply the ratio of those lengths to segment $8$ on the first transversal.

The result is $24$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three parallel lines cross two transversals. The consecutive segments on the first transversal measure $5$ and $10$ cm. The segment corresponding to the first measures $15$ cm on the second transversal. Find the other segment.

$$
\frac{5}{10}=\frac{15}x
$$

$$
x=\frac{10\cdot15}{5}=30
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The intercept theorem guarantees that corresponding segments on the two transversals are proportional. Write each ratio in the same order. Equating the cross products isolates the unknown segment.

The unknown segment lies between the same parallel lines as segment $10$. This correspondence, together with the pairing of $5$ and $15$, determines the ratios to equate.

The result is $30$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three parallel lines cross two transversals. The two consecutive segments on the first measure $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The full segment on the second measures $25\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the segment corresponding to the one of length $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{25}&=\frac{6}{4+6} \\[5pt]
x&=25\cdot\frac6{10}=15\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The full segments lie between the outer parallel lines. The segment of length $6$ is six tenths of the first full segment, so the matching segment is six tenths of the second full segment. The remaining part measures $10$ centimeters.

</div>
