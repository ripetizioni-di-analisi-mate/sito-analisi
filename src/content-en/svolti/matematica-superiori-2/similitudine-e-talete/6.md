## Exercise 1

Three parallel lines cut a perpendicular transversal into segments of lengths $4$ and $6$. On an oblique transversal, the first corresponding segment has length $5$. Find the second.

$$
\frac46=\frac5x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4x=30\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\frac{15}{2}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On each transversal the first segment is bounded by the first two parallels and the second segment by the last two. The strip order identifies $\dfrac{4}{6}$ on the first transversal and $\dfrac{5}{x}$ on the second. In the figure the first oblique crossing also moves $3$ units horizontally and therefore has length $5$. The factor from a perpendicular crossing to its corresponding oblique crossing is $\dfrac{5}{4}$ in every strip. Multiplying $6$ by $\dfrac{5}{4}$ gives $\dfrac{15}{2}$ again.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three parallel lines meet two transversals at ordered points $A,B,C$ and $A\prime,B\prime,C\prime$. Given $AB=3$, $AC=12$, and $A\prime C\prime=20$, find $A\prime B\prime$ and $B\prime C\prime$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{AB}{AC}&=\frac3{12}=\frac14,\\
    A\prime B\prime&=\frac14\cdot20=5,\qquad B\prime C\prime=20-5=15.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Segment $AB$ occupies one quarter of the whole length $AC$. The parallel lines give the same fraction for $A\prime B\prime$ within $A\prime C\prime$. Subtract the part just found from the whole to recover the last segment. As a check, compare the two parts directly. On the first transversal, $BC=12-3=9$, so $\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{1}{3}$. On the second, $\dfrac{5}{15}=\dfrac{1}{3}$. The fractions $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$ are both correct because they compare different lengths.

</div>

## Exercise 3

On one transversal three parallels cut segments in the ratio $2:3$. On the other, the corresponding segments are $3x-2$ and $2x+7$. Find $x$ and the two lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{3x-2}{2x+7}&=\frac23,\\
    3(3x-2)&=2(2x+7)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=4,\\
    (3x-2,2x+7)&=(10,15).
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two expressions are written in corresponding segment order. By the intercept theorem the first is to the second as $2$ is to $3$. Cross-multiply and solve the linear equation. The solution $x=4$ makes both lengths positive and gives $\dfrac{10}{15}=\dfrac{2}{3}$. This check matters because an algebraic equality could otherwise give a zero denominator or lengths incompatible with the geometry. Here the longer segment still lies in the second strip.

</div>
