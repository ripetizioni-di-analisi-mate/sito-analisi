## Exercise 1

A figure has area $5$ cm². A similar figure has a length scale factor of $4$ relative to the first. Find the new area.

$$
A_2=k^2A_1=4^2\cdot5=80
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base and altitude are both multiplied by the length scale factor. Their product is therefore multiplied by the square of that factor. The area follows the same rule.

The factor $4$ applies to lengths. Since area has two dimensions, the factor applies to the base and the altitude, and their product then multiplies $5$.

The result is $80$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

A figure has area $5$ cm². A similar figure has a length scale factor of $5$ relative to the first. Find the new area.

$$
A_2=k^2A_1=5^2\cdot5=125
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base and altitude are both multiplied by the length scale factor. Their product is therefore multiplied by the square of that factor. The area follows the same rule.

The factor is greater than one, which provides a check on the direction of the change. The result must therefore exceed the original area $5$.

The result is $125$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

Every dimension of a figure of area $48\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ is reduced to $75\%$ of its original value. Find the resulting area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac34 \\[5pt]
\mathcal A_2&=48\left(\frac34\right)^2=27\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $75\%$ describes the change in lengths. The area uses its square, $\dfrac9{16}$, so the resulting area is slightly more than half the original.

</div>
