## Exercise 1

Two similar figures have areas of $54$ and $96\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. One side of the first is $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. Find the corresponding side of the second.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    k^2&=\frac{96}{54}=\frac{16}{9},\qquad k=\frac43,\\
    l_2&=\frac43\cdot9=12\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given ratio is a ratio of areas, so it is the square of the length scale factor. The positive root is $4/3$, since a ratio of geometric lengths is positive. Multiply $9$ by that factor to get the corresponding side. As a check, the new side is longer and squaring $12/9$ gives $16/9$. Using $16/9$ directly on the side would give $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ and would then scale the area by too large a factor.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a triangle with a right angle at $A$, altitude $AH$ splits the hypotenuse into $BH=9$ and $CH=16$. Use the areas of the two smaller triangles to find $AB/AC$. Then find $AB$ and $AC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{A_{ABH}}{A_{ACH}}&=\frac{BH\cdot AH/2}{CH\cdot AH/2}=\frac9{16},\\
    \frac{AB}{AC}&=\sqrt{\frac9{16}}=\frac34,\\
    AB&=\sqrt{9\cdot25}=15,\qquad AC=\sqrt{16\cdot25}=20.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two smaller triangles share altitude $AH$ when $BH$ and $CH$ serve as bases. Their areas are therefore in the same ratio as those bases. They are similar by angle-angle: both are right triangles and each is similar to the larger triangle. Their hypotenuses are $AB$ and $AC$, whose length ratio is the square root of the area ratio. For actual lengths, use $BC=25$ and the identities expressing each squared leg as the hypotenuse times its projection.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A line parallel to one side splits a triangle into a smaller triangle with length factor $1/2$ relative to the original and a trapezoid of area $60$. Find both triangle areas.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A_{\mathrm{small}}&=\left(\frac12\right)^2A_{\mathrm{large}}=\frac14 A_{\mathrm{large}},\\
    60&=\frac34 A_{\mathrm{large}},\\
    A_{\mathrm{large}}&=80,\qquad A_{\mathrm{small}}=20.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Similarity connects the two triangles. The smaller occupies one quarter of the larger area because the length scale factor must be squared. The trapezoid takes up the remaining three quarters. If three quarters are $60$, one quarter is $20$ and the whole is $80$. The length factor does not apply directly to the trapezoid, which is not the other similar figure. Adding $60$ and $20$ checks that the pieces recover the original area.

</div>
