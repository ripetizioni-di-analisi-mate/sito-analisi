## Exercise 1

The ratio of the area of the second figure to that of the first is $16$. What is the ratio of their corresponding lengths?

$$
k^2=16
$$

$$
k=\sqrt{16}=4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For similar figures, the area ratio is the square of the length ratio. To find the length ratio, take the square root. Use the positive value because the ratio compares lengths.

Because $16$ is the area ratio, it combines the changes in two dimensions. Taking the square root gives the factor for a single dimension.

The result is $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The ratio of the area of the second figure to that of the first is $25$. What is the ratio of their corresponding lengths?

$$
k^2=25
$$

$$
k=\sqrt{25}=5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For similar figures, the area ratio is the square of the length ratio. To find the length ratio, take the square root. Use the positive value because the ratio compares lengths.

The quotient compares the second area with the first, so the square root keeps the same direction. Squaring the resulting factor recovers the original ratio.

The result is $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two similar figures have areas of $98\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ and $32\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. A side of the first measures $21\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the corresponding side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\sqrt{\frac{32}{98}}=\sqrt{\frac{16}{49}}=\frac47 \\[5pt]
l_2&=\frac47\cdot21=12\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second area is smaller than the first. The ratio is therefore less than one, and so is the length scale factor. The square root is straightforward after reducing the fraction $\dfrac{32}{98}$.

</div>
