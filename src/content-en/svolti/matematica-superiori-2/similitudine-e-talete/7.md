## Exercise 1

In triangle $ABC$, segment $DE$ is parallel to $BC$, with $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$. Given $AD=6$, $DB=9$, $AC=20$, and $BC=25$, find $AE$ and $DE$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    AB&=6+9=15,\qquad k=\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac25,\\
    AE&=\frac25\cdot20=8,\qquad DE=\frac25\cdot25=10.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare the two triangles, the full side corresponding to $AD$ is $AB=AD+DB$. Its remaining part $DB$ is not a side of the whole triangle. Parallelism gives two pairs of equal angles and hence similarity. Both requested sides use factor $\dfrac{2}{5}$. On the other ray, check that $EC=20-8=12$ and $\dfrac{AE}{EC}=\dfrac{8}{12}=\dfrac{6}{9}=\dfrac{AD}{DB}$, the ratio between the two parts.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In triangle $ABC$, $D$ lies inside $AB$ and $E$ inside $AC$. Given $AD=6$, $DB=4$, $AE=9$, and $EC=6$, prove that $DE\parallel BC$.

$$
\frac{AD}{AB}=\frac6{6+4}=\frac35,\qquad
    \frac{AE}{AC}=\frac9{9+6}=\frac35.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Parallelism is what needs to be proved. Recover the two whole sides and check that the parts nearest $A$ occupy the same fraction. A line through $D$ parallel to the base must meet $AC$ at a point three fifths of the way from $A$. The given measures identify that point as $E$. You can also compare $\dfrac{AD}{DB}=\dfrac{6}{4}$ with $\dfrac{AE}{EC}=\dfrac{9}{6}$: both are $\dfrac{3}{2}$, expressing the same division into parts.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In triangle $ABC$, $AB=10$ and $AC=15$. Interior points $D\in AB$ and $E\in AC$ satisfy $AD=4$ and $AE=7$. Is $DE$ parallel to $BC$? How far from $A$ would $E$ need to be for parallelism?

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \frac4{10}&=\frac25\ne\frac7{15},\\
    AE_0&=\frac25\cdot15=6.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If the lines were parallel, the theorem would give the same fraction of each side starting from $A$. The cross products $4\cdot15=60$ and $7\cdot10=70$ show that the fractions differ. On $AC$ choose point $E_0$ at distance $6$, one unit closer to $A$ than $E$. For that point the converse establishes parallelism. The diagram shows why a small change in the positions changes the direction of the segment.

</div>
