## Exercise 1

Two similar triangles have perimeters $32$ and $48\,\mathrm{cm}$. A median of the first is $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. Find the corresponding median in the second.

$$
k=\frac{48}{32}=\frac32,\qquad m_2=\frac32\cdot8=12\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Perimeter is the sum of three lengths, each multiplied by the same factor. Thus the perimeter ratio, though based on total lengths, gives the factor for every corresponding segment. A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. Similarity preserves midpoints and therefore maps the median to its corresponding median. The result fits an enlargement: $12$ exceeds $8$ by the same factor $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A triangle is similar to the triangle with sides $3,4,5$ and has perimeter $54\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its three side lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    k&=\frac{54}{3+4+5}=\frac92,\\
    (a;b;c)&=\left(\frac{27}{2};18;\frac{45}{2}\right)\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The reference triangle has perimeter $12$. The requested triangle is an enlargement, and the perimeter ratio gives the factor $\dfrac{9}{2}$. Multiply each corresponding length by this factor. Another view is to divide the total $54$ into twelve equal parts: the shortest side uses three, the middle side four, and the longest five. The resulting lengths sum to $54$ and preserve the ratio $3:4:5$ in increasing order.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two rectangular frames measure $4\times8$ and $5\times7$. Find their perimeters and decide whether equal perimeters guarantee similarity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    P_1&=2(4+8)=24,\qquad P_2=2(5+7)=24,\\
    \frac54&\ne\frac78.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sums of the consecutive sides match, so the perimeters are equal. If the figures were similar, the perimeter ratio of one would force a ratio of one for all corresponding sides. The shorter side goes from $4$ to $5$, while the longer goes from $8$ to $7$. One grows and the other shrinks, so there is no common factor. The diagram gives a counterexample to the converse. Converting perimeter ratios into side ratios requires similarity as a given.

</div>
