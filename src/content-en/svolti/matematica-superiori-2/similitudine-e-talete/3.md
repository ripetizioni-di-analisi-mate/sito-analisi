## Exercise 1

A polygon has perimeter $12$ cm. A similar figure has scale factor $4$ relative to the original. Find the new perimeter.

$$
P_2=kP_1=4\cdot12=48
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each side is multiplied by the scale factor. Consequently, their sum, the perimeter, is multiplied by the same factor. Perimeter scales linearly.

The perimeter is a length, so it has the same unit as the sides. The factor $4$ multiplies the perimeter $12$ once.

The result is $48$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A polygon has perimeter $12$ cm. A similar figure has scale factor $5$ relative to the original. Find the new perimeter.

$$
P_2=kP_1=5\cdot12=60
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each side is multiplied by the scale factor. Consequently, their sum, the perimeter, is multiplied by the same factor. Perimeter scales linearly.

In the sum of the transformed lengths, factor out $5$. This is why knowing the initial total $12$ is enough.

The result is $60$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two similar pentagons have perimeters of $54\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $36\,\mathrm{cm}$. A side in the first figure measures $15\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the corresponding side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac{36}{54}=\frac23 \\[5pt]
l_2&=\frac23\cdot15=10\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second figure has the smaller perimeter. Its ratio to the first perimeter is the reduction factor for lengths. Multiplying it by $15$ gives the side of the second figure, which is shorter than $15$.

</div>
