## Exercise 1

$ABC\sim DEF$. In the diagram, $AC=4$, $DF=10$, and $AB=6$. Find $DE$ and the scale factor from $DEF$ back to $ABC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    k_{ABC\to DEF}&=\frac{DF}{AC}=\frac{10}{4}=\frac52,\\
    DE&=\frac52\cdot6=15,\\
    k_{DEF\to ABC}&=\frac25.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ordered vertices match $A$ with $D$, $B$ with $E$, and $C$ with $F$. The vertical sides therefore form a corresponding pair, and their ratio also gives the factor for the bases. Divide the destination length by the starting length. To return to the smaller triangle, take the reciprocal. Both pairs check: $15\cdot\dfrac{2}{5}=6$ and $10\cdot\dfrac{2}{5}=4$. The diagram preserves the angles and shows that unequal lengths are compatible with similarity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two rectangles have side lengths $6,9$ and $8,13$. Decide whether they are similar given that all their angles are right.

$$
\frac86=\frac43,\qquad \frac{13}{9}\ne\frac43.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equal angles satisfy one part of the definition. For similar rectangles, the shorter side must match the shorter side because a positive factor preserves the ordering of lengths. The shorter sides give a factor of $\dfrac{4}{3}$. This would take the longer side of length $9$ to $12$, while the other rectangle’s longer side is $13$. The mismatch rules out similarity. Rotating one rectangle does not remove it, since its shorter and longer sides remain the same.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A figure is first reduced by factor $\dfrac{3}{4}$, then enlarged by factor $\dfrac{5}{3}$. What is the overall scale factor, and what does an initial side of length $24$ become?

$$
\begin{aligned}
    k&=\frac53\cdot\frac34=\frac54,\\
    24&\longrightarrow18\longrightarrow30.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second factor applies to the already reduced length, so multiply it by the first. The order of the steps matters for the intermediate length: $24$ goes down to $18$ and then up to $30$. The final ratio $\dfrac{30}{24}=\dfrac{5}{4}$ confirms the product. The overall factor is greater than one, so the result is larger than the original figure even though the first step was a reduction.

</div>
