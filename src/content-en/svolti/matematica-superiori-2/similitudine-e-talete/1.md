## Exercise 1

A side is $5$ cm long in the first figure and $15$ cm long in the similar figure. What is the scale factor from the first to the second?

$$
k=\frac{15}{5}=3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale factor is found by dividing a length in the second figure by the corresponding length in the first. Keep the order specified in the question. The second figure is an enlargement by a factor of three.

The factor is unitless because the centimeters cancel in the quotient. Multiplying the original length $5$ by the factor gives $15$ again.

The result is $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A side is $6$ cm long in the first figure and $18$ cm long in the similar figure. What is the scale factor from the first to the second?

$$
k=\frac{18}{6}=3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale factor is found by dividing a length in the second figure by the corresponding length in the first. Keep the order specified in the question. The second figure is an enlargement by a factor of three.

The direction of the comparison determines the numerator. Reversing the transformation would give $\dfrac13$, reducing $18$ to $6$.

The result is $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A figure is reduced to $\dfrac34$ of its size, and the result is then scaled by $\dfrac23$. Find the overall scale factor relative to the original.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac34\cdot\frac23 \\[5pt]
&=\frac12
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second step uses a length that has already changed. If the original is $l$, after the first step it is $\dfrac34l$. Multiplying again by $\dfrac23$ gives $\dfrac12l$.

</div>
