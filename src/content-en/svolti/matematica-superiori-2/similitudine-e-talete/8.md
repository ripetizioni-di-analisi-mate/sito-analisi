## Exercise 1

A map has scale $1:3000$. How many meters in reality correspond to $3$ cm on the map?

$$
d=9000\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

$$
d=\frac{9000}{100}\,\mathrm{m}=90\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A scale expresses the ratio of lengths in the same unit. Multiply the centimeters on the map by the denominator of the scale. Then divide the resulting centimeters by one hundred to convert to meters.

The factor specified by $1:3000$ relates two measurements both expressed in centimeters. Convert the result to the requested unit only after multiplying.

The result is $90$ m.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A map has scale $1:4000$. How many meters in reality correspond to $3$ cm on the map?

$$
d=12000\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

$$
d=\frac{12000}{100}\,\mathrm{m}=120\,\mathrm{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A scale expresses the ratio of lengths in the same unit. Multiply the centimeters on the map by the denominator of the scale. Then divide the resulting centimeters by one hundred to convert to meters.

The length $3$ is measured on the map. The denominator of the scale tells how many real centimeters correspond to one centimeter on the map.

The result is $120$ m.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A track $120\,\mathrm m$ long is shown at scale $1:1500$. Find its length on the map in centimeters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
120\,\mathrm m&=12000\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
l_{\mathrm c}&=\frac{12000}{1500}=8\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In this problem the starting measurement is the real length. Apply the scale by dividing the real length by $1500$. First express the length in centimeters, so the result is already in the requested unit.

</div>
