## Exercise 1

A rectangular room measures $3.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $2.4\,\mathrm{cm}$ on a plan at scale $1:250$. Find its real dimensions and area in metres and square metres.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    1\,\mathrm{cm}&\longleftrightarrow 250\,\mathrm{cm}=\frac52\,\mathrm m,\\
    (L,l)&=\left(\frac{16}{5}\cdot\frac52,\frac{12}{5}\cdot\frac52\right)\mathrm m=(8,6)\,\mathrm m,\\
    A&=8\cdot6=48\,\mathrm{m}^2.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One centimetre on the plan represents $250$ real centimetres. Converting that length to metres first gives a multiplier of $2.5$ for each measurement in centimetres on the plan. The product of the real lengths is in square metres. As a check, the area on the plan is $7.68\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. One square centimetre stands for a real square of side $2.5\,\mathrm m$ and area $6.25\,\mathrm{m}^2$. Multiplying gives $48\,\mathrm{m}^2$ again.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At the same time, a vertical pole of height $1.8\,\mathrm m$ casts a shadow $2.4\,\mathrm m$ long, and a vertical tree casts a shadow $8\,\mathrm m$ long on the same level ground. Find the tree’s height and justify similarity.

$$
\frac{h}{8}=\frac{\frac{9}{5}}{\frac{12}{5}}=\frac34\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad h=6\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two verticals make right angles with the level ground. The sun’s rays at the same instant are parallel, so they make the same acute angle with the ground. The two shadow triangles are similar by angle-angle. A height corresponds to a height and a shadow to a shadow. Height divided by shadow length is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ in both triangles. If the surfaces sloped differently, one would have to verify the angle equality before setting up the proportion.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A road of real length $450\,\mathrm m$ measures $7.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ on a map. Find the scale and the real length of another segment measuring $3.2\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    450\,\mathrm m&=45000\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    N&=\frac{45000}{\frac{15}{2}}=6000,\qquad \text{scale}\ 1:6000,\\
    \frac{16}{5}\cdot6000\,\mathrm{cm}&=19200\,\mathrm{cm}=192\,\mathrm m.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To read the scale, put both lengths in the same unit. The real-to-map factor is $6000$, while the notation $1:6000$ gives the order map to reality. Multiply the second map length in centimetres by $6000$, then divide by $100$ to express it in metres. You can also check using the ratio of the two map lengths. Applying $\dfrac{3.2}{7.5}$ to the real length of $450$ metres gives $192$ metres.

</div>
