## Exercise 1

Chord $AB$ separates a minor arc of $144^\circ$ from the major arc. $C$ is on the major arc and $D$ on the minor arc. Find $\widehat{ACB}$ and $\widehat{ADB}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{ACB}&=\dfrac{144^\circ}{2}=72^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{ADB}&=\dfrac{360^\circ-144^\circ}{2}=108^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each angle choose the arc between $A$ and $B$ that does not contain its vertex. From $C$ this intercepts the minor arc. From $D$ it intercepts the major arc, whose measure is $216^\circ$. Halving the two arcs gives an acute and an obtuse angle, respectively. The measures add to a straight angle because the two intercepted arcs cover the whole circle. Moving $C$ along the major arc leaves the angle at $C$ unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 2

$AB$ is a diameter. Point $C$ is on the circle, and $\widehat{CAB}=31^\circ$. Find $\widehat{ACB}$ and $\widehat{ABC}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{ACB}&=\dfrac{180^\circ}{2}=90^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{ABC}&=180^\circ-90^\circ-31^\circ=59^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Vertex $C$ lies on the circle, so halve the central angle for the arc $AB$ that avoids $C$. Since the endpoints are diametrically opposite, that arc measures $180^\circ$. The two remaining triangle angles are complementary, and $31^\circ$ determines the other. Check using the arcs in the semicircle containing $C$. The measures of arcs $BC$ and $CA$ in that half are $62^\circ$ and $118^\circ$, which add to $180^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ is inscribed in a circle. Given $\widehat A=106^\circ$ and $\widehat B=77^\circ$, find the angles at $C$ and $D$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat C&=180^\circ-106^\circ=74^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat D&=180^\circ-77^\circ=103^\circ .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each given angle, use the vertex order to identify the opposite angle. The pair at $A$ and $C$ intercepts the two arcs of chord $BD$, while the pair at $B$ and $D$ intercepts the arcs of chord $AC$. Each pair therefore adds to a straight angle. All four angles add to $360^\circ$, as they must in a convex quadrilateral. The two computed measures are positive and one is obtuse, consistent with their intercepted arcs.

</div>
