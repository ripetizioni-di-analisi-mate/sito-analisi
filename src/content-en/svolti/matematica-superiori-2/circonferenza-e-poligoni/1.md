## Exercise 1

A central angle measures $50^\circ$. What is the measure of an inscribed angle that intercepts the same arc?

$$
\alpha=\frac{50^\circ}2=25^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The central angle and the inscribed angle intercept the same arc. The inscribed angle is half the central angle. They must intercept the same arc because the other arc would give a different angle.

The arc is between the points determined by the central angle $50^\circ$. The vertex of the inscribed angle lies on the rest of the circle.

The result is $25$ degrees.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A central angle measures $60^\circ$. What is the measure of an inscribed angle that intercepts the same arc?

$$
\alpha=\frac{60^\circ}2=30^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The central angle and the inscribed angle intercept the same arc. The inscribed angle is half the central angle. They must intercept the same arc because the other arc would give a different angle.

Angle $60^\circ$ has its vertex at the center. For the same arc, moving the vertex to the circle halves the angle.

The result is $30$ degrees.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A chord divides a circle into arcs measuring $80^\circ$ and $280^\circ$. Find the inscribed angle whose vertex lies on the minor arc.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta&=\frac{280^\circ}{2}=140^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertex is on the $80^\circ$ arc, so the sides of the angle intercept the other arc. Therefore halve $280^\circ$. The obtuse result is consistent with an arc exceeding $180^\circ$.

</div>
