## Exercise 1

A $180^\circ$ sector has area $\dfrac{49\pi}{2}$ cm$^2$. Find the radius.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac12\pi r^2&=\dfrac{49\pi}{2},\\[6pt]
r^2&=49,\\[6pt]
r&=7\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sector is half of the disk, so the total area would be $49\pi$ cm$^2$. Divide by $\pi$ to get the square of the radius. The equation has two algebraic roots, but a positive length requires $7$ cm. Check by substituting this length into the area formula and then taking half. The fact that the sector is one half concerns its area. Its straight boundary is the diameter, $14$ cm long.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sector has radius $9$ cm and central angle $120^\circ$. Find its area, arc length, and perimeter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\mathrm{s}}&=\dfrac13\,\pi\cdot9^2=27\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[6pt]
\ell&=\dfrac13\,2\pi\cdot9=6\pi\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{s}}&=(6\pi+18)\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction of a turn is one third. Applying it to the disk area gives a measure in square centimetres. Applying it to the circumference gives the arc's length. To trace the entire boundary of the sector, add the two radii, with total length $18$ cm. The formula $A_{\mathrm{s}}=\dfrac{r\ell}{2}$ offers a check from the length data. With the values found, half the product of radius and arc length is exactly $27\pi$ cm$^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In an annulus with radii of $4$ cm and $10$ cm, take the portion between rays forming $90^\circ$. Find the area and perimeter of that portion.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\dfrac14\pi(10^2-4^2)=21\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[6pt]
\ell_{10}&=5\pi\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad \ell_4=2\pi\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
P&=(7\pi+12)\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area is one quarter of the larger disk with one quarter of the inner disk removed. On the boundary, both the outer arc and the inner arc belong to the path. Add their lengths, $5\pi$ cm and $2\pi$ cm. Two straight pieces remain between the circles, each of length $10-4=6$ cm. The inner area contribution is subtracted. The inner arc counts toward the perimeter because it forms one curved side of the region.

</div>
