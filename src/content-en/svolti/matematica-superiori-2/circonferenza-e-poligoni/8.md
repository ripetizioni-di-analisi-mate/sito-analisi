## Exercise 1

Find the area of a circle with radius $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
A=\pi r^2=\pi\cdot5^2=25\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a circle is pi times the square of the radius. Square the radius before multiplying. The resulting measure is an area.

The square of $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ has units of area. The factor $\pi$ is dimensionless and converts the square of the radius into the area of the circle.

The result is $25 \pi$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the area of a circle with radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
A=\pi r^2=\pi\cdot6^2=36\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of a circle is pi times the square of the radius. Square the radius before multiplying. The resulting measure is an area.

Use the given radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Substituting the diameter into the radius formula would quadruple the area.

The result is $36 \pi$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

Between concentric circles with radii $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $9\,\mathrm{cm}$, find the area of the portion of the annulus within a $90^\circ$ central angle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A&=\frac\pi4(9^2-5^2)=14\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both sectors have the same central angle, so each occupies a quarter of its disk. Subtract the inner sector from the outer sector. The common factor is $\dfrac\pi4$, and the difference of the squares is $81-25=56$.

</div>
