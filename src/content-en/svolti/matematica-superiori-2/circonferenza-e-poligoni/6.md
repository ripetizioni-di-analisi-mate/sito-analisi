## Exercise 1

An arc subtends a central angle of $90^\circ$ in a circle with radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. How long is the arc?

$$
\ell=\frac{90}{360}\cdot2\pi\cdot6=3\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Ninety degrees is a quarter of a full turn. The arc length is therefore a quarter of the circumference. This proportion relates lengths and central angles.

Angle $90^\circ$ determines the fraction of the full turn. Applying it to the circumference with radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ gives a length.

The result is $3 \pi$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An arc subtends a central angle of $90^\circ$ in a circle with radius $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. How long is the arc?

$$
\ell=\frac{90}{360}\cdot2\pi\cdot8=4\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Ninety degrees is a quarter of a full turn. The arc length is therefore a quarter of the circumference. This proportion relates lengths and central angles.

Use the full circumference as the reference. Four copies of the arc, all of the same length, make up the full circumference.

The result is $4 \pi$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A circle has radius $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the length of an arc with a central angle of $75^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=2\pi\cdot12=24\pi \\[5pt]
\ell&=\frac{75}{360}\cdot24\pi=5\pi\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A full turn measures $360^\circ$. The ratio $\dfrac{75}{360}=\dfrac5{24}$ gives the fraction of the circumference taken up by the arc. Multiplying it by the full circumference $24\pi$ gives $5\pi$.

</div>
