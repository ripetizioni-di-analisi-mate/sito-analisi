## Exercise 1

In a circle of radius $15$ cm, an inscribed angle of $36^\circ$ intercepts arc $AB$. What is the length of the intercepted arc?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=2\cdot36^\circ=72^\circ,\\[6pt]
\ell&=\dfrac{72^\circ}{360^\circ}\,2\pi\cdot15
=\dfrac15\cdot30\pi=6\pi\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction of a turn uses the central angle. The given angle has its vertex on the circle, so the arc measure is twice that angle. The $72^\circ$ angle is one fifth of a full turn, and the arc occupies the same fraction of the total circumference. The result $6\pi$ cm is shorter than a semicircle, which would measure $15\pi$ cm. Using $36^\circ$ directly would halve the requested arc and describe a different portion of the circle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An arc of $135^\circ$ is taken on a circle of radius $8$ cm. What central angle gives the same arc length on a circle of radius $12$ cm?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_1&=\dfrac{135^\circ}{360^\circ}\,16\pi
=\dfrac38\cdot16\pi=6\pi\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\dfrac{\ell_1}{C_2}&=\dfrac{6\pi}{24\pi}=\dfrac14,\\[6pt]
\alpha_2&=90^\circ .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First find an exact length from the given angle and radius. The same length must occur on the larger circle. It takes one quarter of that circle's circumference, whereas in the first case it took three eighths. The central angle therefore decreases as the radius increases and the arc length stays fixed. The comparison of $90^\circ$ and $135^\circ$ follows that pattern. In both divisions the lengths are measured in centimetres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Chord $AB$ on a circle of radius $12$ cm bounds a minor arc of $150^\circ$. Find the lengths of the minor and major arcs.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{\min}&=\dfrac{150^\circ}{360^\circ}\,24\pi
=10\pi\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\ell_{\max}&=24\pi-10\pi=14\pi\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The minor arc takes five twelfths of the full turn and hence five twelfths of the circumference. The other arc completes the circle, so the lengths must add to $24\pi$ cm. Check by finding the measure of the major arc, $210^\circ$, which is seven twelfths of a full turn. The minor arc must be shorter than a semicircle and the major arc longer. The length $12\pi$ cm lies between the two results.

</div>
