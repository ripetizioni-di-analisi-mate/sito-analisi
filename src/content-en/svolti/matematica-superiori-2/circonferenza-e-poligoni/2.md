## Exercise 1

Inscribed angle $\widehat{ACB}$ measures $118^\circ$. What is the central angle associated with the intercepted arc? What is the smaller angle between $OA$ and $OB$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{\mathrm{arc}}&=2\cdot118^\circ=236^\circ,\\[6pt]
\alpha_{\min}&=360^\circ-236^\circ=124^\circ .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Doubling finds the measure of the arc that does not contain $C$. A value above $180^\circ$ is possible because that arc is larger than a semicircle, giving a reflex central angle. The two radius rays also enclose a smaller sector. Complete the $360^\circ$ turn to find its angle, $124^\circ$. If the vertex moved to a point on the major arc, the resulting inscribed angle would intercept the arc of $124^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an inscribed regular decagon, minor arc $AB$ contains three consecutive intervals between vertices. Vertex $C$ is on the major arc. Find $\widehat{AOB}$ for the minor arc and $\widehat{ACB}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dfrac{360^\circ}{10}&=36^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{AOB}&=3\cdot36^\circ=108^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{ACB}&=\dfrac{108^\circ}{2}=54^\circ .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The turn around the centre splits into ten equal parts, one for each consecutive pair of vertices. The given arc crosses three of those parts, so its central angle is $108^\circ$. Since $C$ lies on the other arc, the angle at $C$ intercepts the three-part arc. Halve the entire arc measure after obtaining it from the equal sectors. A different vertex on the same arc as $C$ would still give $54^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

$A,B,C$ occur in order around a circle. The arc from $A$ to $B$ avoiding $C$ measures $80^\circ$, and the arc from $B$ to $C$ avoiding $A$ measures $110^\circ$. Find the remaining arc and $\widehat{ABC}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{\mathrm{rem}}&=360^\circ-80^\circ-110^\circ=170^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{ABC}&=\dfrac{170^\circ}{2}=85^\circ .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three arcs cover the full turn. Add the two known ones and subtract from $360^\circ$ to get the arc from $C$ to $A$ that does not contain $B$. This is the arc subtended by the angle at $B$. As a check, halve both given arcs too. The $80^\circ$ arc gives the $40^\circ$ angle at $C$, and the $110^\circ$ arc gives a $55^\circ$ angle at $A$. All the interior angles complete a straight angle.

</div>
