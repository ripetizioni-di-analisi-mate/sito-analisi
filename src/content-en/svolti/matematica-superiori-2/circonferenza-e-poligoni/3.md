## Exercise 1

A tangent is drawn from $P$ to a circle with center $O$ and radius $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. If $OP=15\,\mathrm{cm}$, how long is tangent segment $PT$?

$$
OT\perp PT
$$

$$
PT=\sqrt{OP^2-OT^2}=\sqrt{225-81}=12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radius to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent. The center, the external point, and the point of contact therefore form a right triangle. The distance from the center is the hypotenuse, so the Pythagorean theorem applies.

Because segment $PT$ is perpendicular to the radius, the equation relates the squares of three sides of the same triangle. The hypotenuse square comes first in the subtraction.

The result is $12$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tangent is drawn from $P$ to a circle with center $O$ and radius $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. If $OP=20\,\mathrm{cm}$, how long is tangent segment $PT$?

$$
OT\perp PT
$$

$$
PT=\sqrt{OP^2-OT^2}=\sqrt{400-144}=16
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radius to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent. The center, the external point, and the point of contact therefore form a right triangle. The distance from the center is the hypotenuse, so the Pythagorean theorem applies.

The final step gives a length, so take the positive square root. Segment $PT$ must be shorter than the hypotenuse.

The result is $16$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A tangent from a point $P$ outside a circle with center $O$ touches the circle at $T$. If $PT=12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $OT=5\,\mathrm{cm}$, find $OP$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
OP^2&=5^2+12^2=169 \\[5pt]
OP&=13\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Leg $OT$ is the radius to the point of contact, and $PT$ is the tangent segment. They form the right angle, so add the squares of the two lengths. The square root of the sum gives the distance from the external point to the center.

</div>
