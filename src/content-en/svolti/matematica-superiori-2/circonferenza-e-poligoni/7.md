## Exercise 1

Each interior angle of a regular polygon measures $156^\circ$. How many sides does the polygon have?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{\mathrm{est}}&=180^\circ-156^\circ=24^\circ,\\[6pt]
n&=\dfrac{360^\circ}{24^\circ}=15 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At each vertex the exterior angle forming a linear pair with the interior angle completes it to a straight angle. Tracing the boundary of a convex polygon makes one full turn through the turns at the vertices. Since the polygon is regular, each turn is $24^\circ$. Their number matches the number of vertices and sides. Check by recalculating the interior angle as $180^\circ-\dfrac{360^\circ}{15}=156^\circ$. The count uses the exterior angles, each associated with one vertex.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A regular hexagon has a circumcircle of radius $6$ cm. Find its side length, apothem, and area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
a&=\sqrt{6^2-3^2}=3\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
A&=\dfrac{6s\,a}{2}=54\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each triangle from the centre to two consecutive vertices has a $60^\circ$ central angle and is isosceles. Both base angles are also $60^\circ$, so a side of the hexagon is as long as the radius. The apothem reaches the midpoint $H$ of that side. In the right triangle, the radius is the hypotenuse and half the side is a leg. The area adds the six triangles of base $6$ cm and height $3\sqrt3$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a regular polygon, two radii to vertices bound exactly two consecutive sectors. The combined sector measures $60^\circ$. Find the side count and the interior angle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\dfrac{60^\circ}{2}=30^\circ,\\[6pt]
n&=\dfrac{360^\circ}{30^\circ}=12,\\[6pt]
\beta&=180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The division that counts sides uses the sector between consecutive vertices. The given measure includes two equal sectors, so each measures $30^\circ$. Twelve sectors of that size fill the full turn. The interior angle is supplementary to the central angle of a single sector, because it combines base angles of the two central triangles meeting at the vertex. Using the whole combined angle in the division would count pairs of sectors instead of sides.

</div>
