## Exercise 1

Find the circumference of a circle with radius $4\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
C=2\pi r=2\pi\cdot4=8\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circumference is twice pi times the radius. Substitute the given radius and keep pi for an exact result. The resulting measure is a length.

Radius $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ is half the diameter. Doubling it gives the diameter, which is multiplied by $\pi$.

The result is $8 \pi$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the circumference of a circle with radius $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
C=2\pi r=2\pi\cdot5=10\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circumference is twice pi times the radius. Substitute the given radius and keep pi for an exact result. The resulting measure is a length.

The given length is the distance from the center. Multiplying by $2\pi$ scales the length while keeping the unit.

The result is $10 \pi$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel of diameter $40\,\mathrm{cm}$ turns five times without slipping. Find the distance traveled in meters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=40\pi\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
d&=5C=200\pi\,\mathrm{cm}=2\pi\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In one turn without slipping, the wheel advances by its circumference. The given length is the diameter, so one turn covers $40\pi$ centimeters. Five turns cover $200\pi$ centimeters, or $2\pi$ meters.

</div>
