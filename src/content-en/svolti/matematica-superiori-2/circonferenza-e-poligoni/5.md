## Exercise 1

A circle has circumference $28\pi$ cm. Find the radius and diameter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\pi r&=28\pi,\\[6pt]
r&=\dfrac{28\pi}{2\pi}=14\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
2r&=28\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circumference is the radius multiplied by $2\pi$. To recover the radius, divide by that whole factor, so $\pi$ cancels and the result stays in centimetres. The diameter is twice the radius. Alternatively, use $C=\pi d$ to find $d$ with a single division by $\pi$. Substituting the result into the circumference formula recovers $28\pi$ cm. Dividing by $2$ alone would leave a factor of $\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two concentric circles have radii of $3$ cm and $7$ cm. How much do their circumferences differ, and what is their sum?

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_7-C_3&=2\pi(7-3)=8\pi\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
C_7+C_3&=2\pi(7+3)=20\pi\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each boundary contributes its own radius multiplied by the same factor $2\pi$. Factor that out before adding or subtracting. The difference between the lengths depends only on the difference of the radii, here $4$ cm. The sum measures the whole boundary of an annulus, which has an outer and an inner part. Check by finding the separate lengths, $6\pi$ cm and $14\pi$ cm, and verifying that their difference and sum agree.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A wheel of diameter $70$ cm rolls without slipping for $12$ complete turns. What distance does it travel, in metres?

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=\pi\cdot70=70\pi\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
12C&=840\pi\,\mathrm{cm}=\dfrac{42\pi}{5}\,\mathrm m .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point of the rim touching the ground changes as the wheel rolls. In one full turn, the entire rim passes the contact once, and the wheel moves forward by one circumference. The no-slip assumption connects these two motions. Since the diameter is given, multiply it by $\pi$ directly and then multiply by the number of turns. The final conversion divides by $100$, because the starting value is in centimetres.

</div>
