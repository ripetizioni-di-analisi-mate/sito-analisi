## Exercise 1

Chord $AB$ is at distance $5$ cm from the centre of a circle of radius $13$ cm. Find its length and compare it with the diameter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AH&=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=12\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
AB&=2AH=24\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
2r&=26\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perpendicular from the centre to the chord meets $AB$ at its midpoint $H$. The radius to $A$ is the triangle's hypotenuse, and the given distance is a leg. The Pythagorean theorem gives $12$ cm, which covers only the part from $A$ to $H$. Double it because the part from $H$ to $B$ has that length too. The diameter is $2$ cm longer. If the distance from the centre dropped to zero, the half-chord would equal the radius.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the same circle of radius $10$ cm, two chords measure $16$ cm and $12$ cm. How far is each from the centre?

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{16}&=\sqrt{10^2-8^2}=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
d_{12}&=\sqrt{10^2-6^2}=8\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Join the centre to an endpoint of each chord and drop a perpendicular from the centre to the line through the chord. The two right triangles have the same hypotenuse and a leg equal to half their respective chords. For the longer chord, subtract $8^2$ from $10^2$ in place of $6^2$, so the resulting distance is smaller. The arrangement in the figure shows the comparison. The chords are parallel there to make the distances visible, but the calculation holds for any orientation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Chord $AB$ measures $18$ cm and is at distance $12$ cm from the centre. Find the radius and diameter of the circle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AH&=\dfrac{18}{2}=9\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
r&=\sqrt{9^2+12^2}=\sqrt{225}=15\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
2r&=30\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right triangle uses the centre, the foot of the perpendicular, and one chord endpoint. Its legs therefore measure $12$ cm and half the chord, $9$ cm. Here the unknown is the hypotenuse, so add the squares of the legs. Find the diameter only after finding the radius. The result $15$ cm exceeds the distance $12$ cm, as required for a line that intersects the circle at two distinct points.

</div>
