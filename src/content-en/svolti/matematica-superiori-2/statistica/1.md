## Exercise 1

For the values $2,4,6,12$, a mean of $7$ is proposed. Use deviations to test it, then find the correct mean.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2-7)+(4-7)+(6-7)+(12-7)&=-4,\\[5pt]
\bar x&=\frac{2+4+6+12}{4}=6,\\[5pt]
-4-2+0+6&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.8 xmax=13.4 ymin=-0.45 ymax=2.65 nomi="x," punti="2,1;4,1;6,1;12,1" segmenti="6,0,6,2.2" etichette="5.75,2.42,6" titolo="The signed deviations balance around the line at six"}

<div class="spiegazione">

If the reference is the mean, the **sum of deviations** must be zero. Using $7$ gives $-4$ because the reference is too high. Lowering it by one increases each of the four deviations by one, bringing their sum to zero. Dividing the data sum by four gives the same result. In the plot, the distance from $12$ to the new centre offsets the distances for $2$ and $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Six durations have a mean of $13$ minutes. One duration recorded as $18$ minutes was actually $9$ minutes. Calculate the corrected mean and its change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=6\cdot13=78,\\[5pt]
S^\prime&=78-18+9=69,\\[5pt]
\bar x^\prime&=\frac{69}{6}=11.5,\qquad \bar x^\prime-\bar x=-1.5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The incorrect duration is **replaced**, so subtract the old value and add the new one while keeping six observations. The total falls by nine minutes. Dividing the corrected total by six gives $11.5$ minutes. The change can also be calculated on its own. Spread across six observations, the change in the sum is $-\dfrac{9}{6}=-1.5$. Simply removing the duration would change the denominator as well.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two disjoint groups have $6$ and $14$ people, with means $10$ and $15$. After a check, every value in the first group is increased by $5$. Find the combined means before and after.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{6\cdot10+14\cdot15}{20}=\frac{270}{20}=13.5,\\[5pt]
\bar x^\prime&=\frac{270+6\cdot5}{20}=15.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To combine the groups, recover their sums by multiplying each mean by its group size. The correction adds thirty units because it applies only to the six people in the first group. Thus the sum rises by thirty while the count stays at twenty. As a check, after the correction both groups have mean $15$. The combined mean must also be $15$, regardless of their different sizes.

</div>
