## Exercise 1

Find the mean of $3$, $5$, $7$, and $9$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{3+5+7+9}{4}=\frac{24}{4}=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each of the four observations has equal weight. The pairs $3$ and $9$, then $5$ and $7$, each sum to $12$. Both pairs therefore have mean $6$. The overall mean is also $6$, halfway between the minimum and the maximum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The mean of four data values is $7$. Three values are $2$, $6$, and $9$. Find the fourth value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2+6+9+x&=4\cdot7=28,\\
x&=28-17=11.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The known mean gives the required sum of the four values. Since the three known values add to $17$, the missing observation must supply the other $11$ units. Recalculating the mean with $11$ gives $\dfrac{28}{4}=7$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Five journey times are $12$, $13$, $14$, $15$, and $46$ minutes. Compare the mean of all five times with the mean of the first four.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x_4&=\frac{12+13+14+15}{4}=13.5,\\
\bar x_5&=\frac{54+46}{5}=20,\\
\bar x_5-\bar x_4&=6.5\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fifth journey takes much longer than the others and adds $46$ to the sum. The number of observations also increases, from four to five. Since $46$ exceeds the initial mean $13.5$, including it raises the mean. Only the fifth journey exceeds the new mean.

</div>
