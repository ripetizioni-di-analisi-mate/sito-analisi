## Exercise 1

Disjoint groups $A$ and $B$ have two values each and medians $2$ and $4$. Is the median of all four determined? Give two examples.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A=(0,4),\ B=(0,8)&\ \Longrightarrow\ (0,0,4,8),\quad \operatorname{Med}=2,\\[5pt]
A=(2,2),\ B=(4,4)&\ \Longrightarrow\ (2,2,4,4),\quad \operatorname{Med}=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In both examples the original group medians are $2$ and $4$. Combining the data requires sorting them together, since the middle positions can come from different groups. In the first list the middle values are $0$ and $4$, and in the second they are $2$ and $4$. Averaging the group medians gives $3$ in both cases, yet the actual median is different in the first. The two examples show that the group medians alone are insufficient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The sorted observations are $1,3,u,v,9,12$. The median is $6$ and the two middle values differ by $4$. Find $u$ and $v$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{u+v}{2}&=6,\qquad v-u=4,\\[5pt]
u&=6-2=4,\qquad v=6+2=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The median is the midpoint between the third and fourth observations. Since $v$ comes after $u$, the distance from $6$ to the higher value is half the total gap, or $2$. The lower value is obtained by subtracting the same amount. The original ordering also needs to be checked. The values $4$ and $8$ both lie between $3$ and $9$, have distance $4$, and average $6$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The values $1$, $2$, $6$, and $9$ occur $3$, $2$, $2$, and $3$ times. Find $Q_1$, the median, and $Q_3$, using nearest ranks for the outer quartiles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=3,\qquad r_3=8,\\[5pt]
Q_1&=1,\qquad Q_3=9,\\[5pt]
Q_2&=\frac{x_{(5)}+x_{(6)}}2=\frac{2+6}{2}=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

| Value | Frequency | Cumulative count |
|---|---:|---:|
| $1$ | $3$ | $3$ |
| $2$ | $2$ | $5$ |
| $6$ | $2$ | $7$ |
| $9$ | $3$ | $10$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

The cumulative count reaches $3$ at $1$, so the first quartile is $1$. Rank $8$ lies just after cumulative count $7$, at the start of the group of $9$s. The median instead uses the fifth and sixth positions. The fifth is the last $2$, and the sixth is the first $6$. Their mean is $4$, a value absent from the data. The positions count every observation, including repeats.

</div>
