## Exercise 1

Find the median of $3$, $4$, $7$, and $10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_e&=\frac{x_{(2)}+x_{(3)}}{2}=\frac{4+7}{2}=\frac{11}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The list is already sorted. With four data values, the central positions are the second and third. The median is halfway between $4$ and $7$, so it is $\dfrac{11}{2}$. It lies between the two observations even though none of the observations equals it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Among ten measurements, $1$ occurs four times, $3$ three times, and $8$ three times. Find the median.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4<5<6\le7&=4+3,\\
x_{(5)}=x_{(6)}&=3,\\
M_e&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The central positions are the fifth and sixth. The value $3$ occupies positions five through seven because the four occurrences of $1$ precede it. Both central observations are therefore $3$, and so is their average.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The sorted list $1$, $3$, $x$, $9$, with $3\le x\le9$, has median $5$. Find $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3+x}{2}&=5,\\
x&=10-3=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The order constraint makes $3$ and $x$ the two central values. Their sum must be $10$ to give median $5$. The result $x=7$ lies between $3$ and $9$. It therefore satisfies both the required order and the given median.

</div>
