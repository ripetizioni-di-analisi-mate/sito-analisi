## Exercise 1

Find the descriptive standard deviation of $3$ and $7$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=5,\\
\sigma^2&=\frac{(3-5)^2+(7-5)^2}{2}=4,\\
\sigma&=\sqrt4=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean is the midpoint of the two values. Each is two units from the mean and has squared deviation $4$. The descriptive variance is therefore $4$. The square root expresses the dispersion in the same units as the data.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A data set has standard deviation $4$. Apply $y=10-2x$ to each value. Find the new standard deviation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar y&=10-2\bar x,\\
y_i-\bar y&=-2(x_i-\bar x),\\
\sigma_y&=|-2|\sigma_x=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant $10$ cancels when the new mean is subtracted. The factor $-2$ reverses the signs of the deviations and doubles their magnitudes. Their squares are multiplied by $4$. After taking the square root, the standard deviation is twice the original.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two measurements, $2\,\mathrm m$ and $4\,\mathrm m$, are converted to centimeters. Find the standard deviation of the converted data.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=300\,\mathrm{cm},\\
\sigma^2&=\frac{100^2+100^2}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\
\sigma&=100\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After conversion the values are $200\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $400\,\mathrm{cm}$. Their deviations from the mean are $-100\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $100\,\mathrm{cm}$. The standard deviation is $100\,\mathrm{cm}$, or $1\,\mathrm m$. The numerical value changes with the units while the dispersion of the measurements stays the same.

</div>
