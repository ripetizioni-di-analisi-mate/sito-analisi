## Exercise 1

Durations have mean $8$ minutes and standard deviation $2$ minutes. Convert to seconds and subtract $30$ seconds from each to correct the time origin. Find the corrected mean, variance, and standard deviation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar y&=60\cdot8-30=450\,\mathrm s,\\[5pt]
\sigma_y&=60\cdot2=120\,\mathrm s,\\[5pt]
\sigma_y^2&=120^2\,\mathrm s^2=14400\,\mathrm s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The conversion multiplies the durations by sixty. Subtracting thirty seconds shifts the centre of the series by the same amount and cancels in the deviations. The new standard deviation therefore depends only on the factor sixty. For variance, square both the value and its unit. To check another way, start with four square minutes and apply the factor $60^2$. This gives the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A series has mean $3$ and standard deviation $4$. Each value is replaced by $y=7-2x$. Find the new mean and standard deviation and explain the sign of the factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar y&=7-2\cdot3=1,\\[5pt]
y-\bar y&=-2(x-\bar x),\\[5pt]
\sigma_y&=|-2|\cdot4=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean follows the same rule as every value. Deviations change sign and double in absolute value because the coefficient is negative. Each squared deviation becomes four times its original value. Taking the square root in the standard deviation gives a factor of two. The negative sign reverses the order of observations, but distances from the new mean remain nonnegative.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Series $A$ is $0,0,2,2$. Series $B$ contains $-1$, six values of $1$, and $3$. Compare means, standard deviations, and ranges.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x_A&=\bar x_B=1,\\[5pt]
\sigma_A&=\sqrt{\frac{4}{4}}=1,\qquad \sigma_B=\sqrt{\frac{8}{8}}=1,\\[5pt]
R_A&=2,\qquad R_B=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every deviation in $A$ is one in absolute value. In $B$ only two deviations are nonzero, both of absolute value two. The sum of squared deviations in $B$ is twice as large, but so is the number of observations. The standard deviations therefore agree. The range of $B$ is wider because two values lie farther from the centre. The summaries alone do not reconstruct the distribution.

</div>
