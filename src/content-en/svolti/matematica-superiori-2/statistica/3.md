## Exercise 1

Find the median of $10$, $3$, $6$, $4$, and $12$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{(1)},\ldots,x_{(5)})&=(3,4,6,10,12),\\
M_e&=x_{(3)}=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First put the five observations in increasing order. The third has two observations on either side. In this example the value written third originally is also $6$, but its position in the sorted list determines its role as the median.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The value $1$ occurs twice, $2$ four times, and $4$ three times. Find the median.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2+4+3=9,\\
\frac{N+1}{2}&=5,\\
2<5&\le2+4=6,\\
M_e&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two occurrences of $1$ occupy the first two positions in the sorted list. The four occurrences of $2$ occupy positions three through six. The fifth observation therefore falls in that group. Count every occurrence even when several positions have the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In the sorted list $2$, $4$, $7$, $9$, and $21$, replace $21$ with $100$. Find the new median.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2,4,7,9,21)&\longrightarrow(2,4,7,9,100),\\
M_e&=x_{(3)}=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both $21$ and $100$ exceed $9$, so the changed observation stays in the last position. The other positions are unchanged and the third value is still $7$. The median stays the same. The mean increases by $\dfrac{100-21}{5}=15.8$.

</div>
