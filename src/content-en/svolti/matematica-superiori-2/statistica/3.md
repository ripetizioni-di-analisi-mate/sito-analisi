## Exercise 1

Nine measurements take values $1$, $3$, $5$, $8$ with counts $2$, $1$, $4$, $2$. Find the median and quartiles, using nearest ranks for $Q_1$ and $Q_3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=3,\qquad r_2=5,\qquad r_3=7,\\[5pt]
Q_1&=3,\qquad Q_2=5,\qquad Q_3=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

| Value | Frequency | Cumulative count |
|---|---:|---:|
| $1$ | $2$ | $2$ |
| $3$ | $1$ | $3$ |
| $5$ | $4$ | $7$ |
| $8$ | $2$ | $9$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

The products for the quartiles are $\dfrac{9}{4}$ and $\dfrac{27}{4}$, rounded up to $3$ and $7$ respectively. The median uses rank five. The first three observations end with value $3$. The value $5$ takes positions four through seven, so it is both the median and third quartile. These two summaries can coincide because one value occupies several ranks.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The values are $7,8,9,10,12,16,19$. The maximum is corrected from $19$ to $8$. Compare the medians before and after.

$$
\begin{aligned}
7,8,9,\boxed{10},12,16,19&\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{Med}=10,\\[5pt]
7,8,8,\boxed{9},10,12,16&\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{Med}^\prime=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Sort the list again after the correction. The new $8$ takes a position before the old centre. There are still seven observations, so the fourth value is needed, but that position now contains $9$. The median stays the same if the maximum is moved but that observation remains beyond the centre. Here it crosses the central positions, so the **median falls**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The seven values $2,4,6,9,12,15,k$ may repeat. For which values of $k$ is the median $6$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&\le 6 \ \Longrightarrow\ N_{\le6}=4,\quad N_{<6}\le3,\\[5pt]
k&>6 \ \Longrightarrow\ N_{\le6}=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With seven observations, look at the fourth position. Three of the six known values are at most $6$. If $k$ exceeds $6$, counting up to $6$ never reaches fourth place. If $k\le6$, four observations are at most $6$ and at most three are strictly smaller. So the fourth position is $6$. Equality is allowed because two copies occupy two positions.

</div>
