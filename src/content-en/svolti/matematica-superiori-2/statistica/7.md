## Exercise 1

Find the descriptive variance of $3$, $5$, and $7$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{3+5+7}{3}=5,\\
\sigma^2&=\frac{(-2)^2+0^2+2^2}{3}=\frac83.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The deviations $-2$ and $2$ describe observations at the same distance from the mean. Squaring gives each the same positive contribution, $4$. The central value contributes zero. Divide the sum of the three squares by $3$ because the aim is to describe the dispersion of these three observed values.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find and compare the descriptive variances for $A=(0,3,6)$ and $B=(2,3,4)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x_A=\bar x_B&=3,\\
\sigma_A^2&=\frac{9+0+9}{3}=6,\\
\sigma_B^2&=\frac{1+0+1}{3}=\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both groups have three values and the same mean, $3$. In A, two observations are at distance $3$ from the mean. In B, two observations are at distance $1$. A has greater variance because its squared deviations are larger, although the two groups have the same center.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The value $2$ occurs three times and $6$ occurs once. Find the descriptive variance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{3\cdot2+6}{4}=3,\\
\sigma^2&=\frac{3(2-3)^2+(6-3)^2}{4}=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean counts the value $2$ three times. The same weight applies to its squared deviation, which is $1$ on three observations. The value $6$ contributes $9$ once. The squared deviations sum to $12$, to be divided by the four observations.

</div>
