## Exercise 1

The value $3$ occurs twice and $8$ occurs three times. Find the mean.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=2+3=5,\\
\bar x&=\frac{2\cdot3+3\cdot8}{5}=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The five values are $3$, $3$, $8$, $8$, and $8$. Since eight occurs three times, it contributes more to the sum than three does. The denominator is the total number of observations, $5$. The mean is closer to $8$, the more frequent value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four students have mean $7$. Six other students have mean $9$. Find the combined mean for the ten students.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1&=4\cdot7=28,\\
S_2&=6\cdot9=54,\\
\bar x&=\frac{28+54}{10}=8.2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying a group mean by its size gives the group total. The two groups contribute totals of $28$ and $54$. Divide by ten because each student has equal weight in the combined mean. The group of six therefore has greater weight than the group of four.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The value $2$ occurs three times and $7$ occurs $n$ times. Determine whether any count $n$ gives mean $5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6+7n}{3+n}&=5,\\
6+7n&=15+5n,\\
n&=\frac92.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation requires $n=\dfrac92$, but an absolute frequency must be an integer. Therefore no count produces exactly the stated mean. For example, four occurrences of $7$ give mean $\dfrac{34}{7}$ and five give mean $\dfrac{41}{8}$.

</div>
