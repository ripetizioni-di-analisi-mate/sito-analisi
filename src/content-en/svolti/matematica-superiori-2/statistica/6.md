## Exercise 1

In a group of $20$ students, $7$ choose an activity. Find its percentage frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac7{20}=0.35,\\
100f&=35.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio uses all twenty students as its reference. Expressing the fraction with denominator $100$ gives $\dfrac{35}{100}$. The percentage frequency is therefore $35\%$. The other thirteen students account for the remaining $65\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a school of $80$ students, $15\%$ travel by bicycle. How many students is that?

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm b}&=80\cdot\frac{15}{100}=12.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The percentage means fifteen students per hundred, so it expresses a fraction of the school total. For a total of eighty, multiply $80$ by $\dfrac{15}{100}$ to get the count. Dividing back, $\dfrac{12}{80}$ gives $0.15$ again.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Group A has $6$ sign-ups out of $10$. Group B has $9$ sign-ups out of $30$. Find the overall percentage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{6+9}{10+30}=\frac{15}{40}=0.375,\\
100f&=37.5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two groups differ in size. The overall percentage covers all forty participants, so add the sign-up counts and the totals before dividing. The result $37.5\%$ is closer to group B’s $30\%$, since B has three times as many people as A.

</div>
