## Exercise 1

Two disjoint groups have $20$ and $30$ members. In the first, $50\%$ finish a test. The combined proportion is $48\%$. Find the second group’s count and proportion of finishers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_1&=20\cdot\frac12=10,\qquad n_{\rm tot}=50\cdot\frac{48}{100}=24,\\[5pt]
n_2&=24-10=14,\qquad f_2=\frac{14}{30}=\frac7{15}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two percentages use different totals. The first group’s $50\%$ represents ten people, and the combined $48\%$ represents twenty-four. These counts can be subtracted because no one in the first group is in the second. There are fourteen out of thirty, about $46.67\%$. The second group’s proportion makes sense below $48\%$, because the first is above it and the overall proportion lies between the two.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Thirty-six single-choice votes are split among $A$, $B$, and $C$. Twelve go to $A$ and six to $B$. Find the sector angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_C&=36-12-6=18,\\[5pt]
\alpha_A&=360^\circ\cdot\frac{12}{36}=120^\circ,\\[5pt]
\alpha_B&=360^\circ\cdot\frac6{36}=60^\circ,\qquad
\alpha_C=180^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

Each vote counts in one sector and the categories cover every vote. The sector for $C$ contains the remaining votes, which are half of thirty-six, and fills half the circle. ## Exercise 3

Two hundred people answer a survey. One category is reported as $37\%$ after rounding to the nearest whole percent, with half values rounded up. What counts are possible?

$$
\begin{aligned}
36.5&\le \frac{100n}{200}<37.5,\\[5pt]
73&\le n<75,\qquad n\in\{73;74\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An exact percentage of $36.5\%$ rounds to $37\%$. The value $37.5\%$ already rounds to $38\%$. With two hundred respondents, each person accounts for half a percentage point. The interval therefore allows only the whole counts from seventy-three inclusive to seventy-five exclusive. The count $74$ gives exactly $37\%$, while $73$ gives the same published value only after rounding.

</div>
