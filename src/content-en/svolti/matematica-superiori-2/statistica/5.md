## Exercise 1

Find the mode of $3$, $5$, $5$, $5$, $8$, and $8$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_3&=1,\quad n_5=3,\quad n_8=2,\\
M_o&=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{poligoni="2.5,0 3.5,0 3.5,1 2.5,1;4.5,0 5.5,0 5.5,3 4.5,3;7.5,0 8.5,0 8.5,2 7.5,2" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=4 nomi="x,n" titolo="The tallest bar corresponds to the value 5"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute frequencies are one for $3$, three for $5$, and two for $8$. The largest frequency is three and belongs to the value $5$. The mode is therefore the value $5$, which occurs three times.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The frequencies for bus, bicycle, and walking are $8$, $8$, and $3$. Find the modes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max(8,8,3)&=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Bus and bicycle have the same frequency, and it exceeds the frequency for walking. Both categories are therefore modes. The maximum frequency is eight, so the result describes a tie between the most frequent categories. This comparison also works for categories without a natural order.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Add another $4$ and another $2$ to $1$, $1$, $2$, $2$, and $4$. Find the mode of the updated data set.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(n_1,n_2,n_4)&=(2,2,1),\\
(n_1',n_2',n_4')&=(2,3,2),\\
M_o&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value $1$ keeps two occurrences. The count for $4$ rises from one to two and the count for $2$ from two to three. The initial tie between $1$ and $2$ is broken. With the highest updated frequency, $2$ is the unique mode.

</div>
