## Exercise 1

In a survey on reading preferences, categories $A$, $B$, and $C$ receive $6$, $6$, and $4$ votes. Find the modes and decide whether averaging the codes $1$, $2$, and $3$ is meaningful.

$$
n_{\max}=6,\qquad f_{\max}=\frac{6}{16}=\frac{3}{8}.
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.2 ymin=-0.45 ymax=7.45 nomi="c,n" poligoni="0.72,0 1.28,0 1.28,6 0.72,6;1.72,0 2.28,0 2.28,6 1.72,6;2.72,0 3.28,0 3.28,4 2.72,4" etichette="0.89,6.44,6;1.89,6.44,6;2.89,4.44,4" titolo="The codes 1, 2, and 3 identify A, B, and C without measuring the categories"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two bars reach the maximum height, so **$A$ and $B$** are both modes. The count $6$ gives the number of votes for each. The categories are labels. Swapping the codes for $A$ and $C$ would change the numerical mean because their counts differ, while leaving the preferences unchanged. Counts and the names of the modes describe the responses independently of how the categories are numbered.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a histogram of waiting times, the classes $[0,1)$, $[1,5)$, and $[5,7)$ have heights of $3$, $2$, and $2$ waits per minute. Find the class with the most waits and the class with the greatest density.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_1&=1\cdot3=3,\qquad n_2=4\cdot2=8,\\[5pt]
n_3&=2\cdot2=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.7 xmax=8.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=4.3 nomi="t,d" poligoni="0,0 1,0 1,3 0,3;1,0 5,0 5,2 1,2;5,0 7,0 7,2 5,2" linee="0,3 1,3 1,0;1,2 5,2 5,0;5,2 7,2 7,0" etichette="0.34,1.2,3;2.7,0.82,8;5.76,0.82,4" titolo="The numbers inside the rectangles are counts. Heights on the axis are densities."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The heights represent **densities**. The first class is tallest because it contains more waits per minute of width. To count the waits, multiply each height by its width. The middle rectangle is wider and has the greatest area at eight. The last two classes share a height, but the middle width is twice the last one, so it contains twice as many waits.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The values $4$, $5$, and $8$ have counts of $3$, $2$, and $4$. One observation of $8$ is corrected to $4$. Find the mode and median before and after.

$$
\operatorname{Mode}:\ 8\longmapsto4,\qquad \operatorname{Med}:\ 5\longmapsto5.
$$

| Value | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| $4$ | $3$ | $4$ |
| $5$ | $2$ | $2$ |
| $8$ | $4$ | $3$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

The correction moves one observation from $8$ to $4$. The highest count is still four but is now attained by $4$. For the median, both lists use the fifth position. Before correction the cumulative counts through $4$ and $5$ are three and five. After correction they are four and six. In either case rank five is in the block of $5$s.

</div>
