## Exercise 1

Solve $\sqrt{x+20}=x$ and identify the extraneous candidate.

::grafico{f="sqrt(x+20);x" legenda="y=\sqrt{x+20};y=x" xmin=-6.6 xmax=7.8 ymin=-5.45 ymax=8.4 punti="5,5,pieno" titolo="The negative candidate is not an intersection"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x&\ge0\\[6pt]
         x+20=x^2&\iff(x-5)(x+4)=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\{5\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radical exists for $x\ge-20$, but equality also requires $x\ge0$. Squaring gives two values, $5$ and $-4$. The second satisfies only the radicand condition and must still pass the sign check. There the radical equals $\sqrt{16}=4$, and the other side is $-4$. At $5$ we get $\sqrt{25}=5$, so the positive candidate is allowed. The graph shows the original curves with just one intersection, at $(5;5)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\sqrt{x^2}=-x$. Why does the identity obtained by squaring need interpretation?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         \sqrt{x^2}&=|x|\\[6pt]
         |x|=-x&\iff x\le0\\[6pt]
         S&=(-\infty,0].
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radical is defined for every real number, but equality requires $-x$ to be nonnegative. For $x\le0$, we have $|x|=-x$, so every such value works. For $x>0$, the radical is positive and the right side is negative. The squared equation $x^2=x^2$ has lost this information. The boundary $x=0$ is included because both sides really do equal zero there. For instance, $x=-3$ works and $x=3$ fails.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\sqrt{x+4}=2-x$. Keep both restrictions before squaring.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x+4\ge0,\quad 2-x\ge0&\iff -4\le x\le2\\[6pt]
         x+4=(2-x)^2&\iff x(x-5)=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\{0\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand gives the lower bound $-4$, and the sign of the right side gives the upper bound $2$. Equate the squares on their common interval. The binomial on the right gives $4-4x+x^2$, so the candidates are $0$ and $5$. The latter is disallowed because $2-x=-3$ there. At $x=0$, both sides equal $2$. The check uses both the domain of the root and its nonnegative sign.

</div>
