## Exercise 1

Solve $\sqrt{x+20}=x$.

$$
x\ge0
$$

$$
x+20=x^2
$$

$$
(x-5)(x+4)=0
$$

$$
x=5\quad\text{is allowed}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left side is nonnegative, so $x$ must also be nonnegative. Squaring gives two candidates, but the negative one violates this condition. The positive root belongs to the domain and satisfies the original equation.

The roots of the quadratic are $5$ and $-4$. The second would make the right side of the original equation negative. Substituting the first gives $\sqrt{5+20}=\sqrt{25}=5$.

The result is $\{5\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\sqrt{x+4}=2-x$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x+4\ge0\\
2-x\ge0
\end{cases}
\qquad\iff\qquad -4\le x\le2.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+4&=(2-x)^2\\[6pt]
x^2-5x&=0\\[6pt]
x(x-5)&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before squaring, $x+4$ must be nonnegative and $2-x$ must have the sign of a square root. Squaring gives $0$ and $5$. The second value lies outside the allowed interval and would give $2-5=-3$ on the right. For $x=0$, both sides equal $2$, so the only solution is $0$.

</div>
