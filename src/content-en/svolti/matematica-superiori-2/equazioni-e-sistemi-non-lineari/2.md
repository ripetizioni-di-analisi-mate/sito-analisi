## Exercise 1

Solve $x^{3} - 16 x=0$.

$$
x(x^2-16)=0
$$

$$
x(x-4)(x+4)=0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor out $x$ and factor the difference of squares. The product is zero when at least one of the three factors is zero. There are three distinct real roots.

Since $16=4^2$, the factor $x^2-16$ can be written as $(x-4)(x+4)$. The two linear factors give opposite roots. The factor taken out at the beginning also gives $x=0$.

The result is $\{-4;\,0;\,4\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $x^3-x^2-6x=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^3-x^2-6x
&=x(x^2-x-6)\\[6pt]
&=x(x-3)(x+2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring out $x$ leaves a quadratic trinomial. The numbers $-3$ and $2$ have sum $-1$ and product $-6$, so the trinomial is $(x-3)(x+2)$. The product of the three factors is zero when at least one of them is zero. The solutions are $-2$, $0$, and $3$.

</div>
