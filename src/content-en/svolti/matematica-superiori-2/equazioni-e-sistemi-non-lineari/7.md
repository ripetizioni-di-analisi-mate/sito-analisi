## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}x+y=9\\x^2+y^2=41\end{cases}$.

$$
y=9-x
$$

$$
x^2+(9-x)^2=41
$$

$$
2(x-4)(x-5)=0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting the linear relation into the quadratic equation gives an equation of degree two. Each value of $x$ determines exactly one value of $y$. Check the pairs in the original equations.

Expanding $(x+y)^2$ also gives a term $2xy$. Subtracting $41$ from $81$ leaves $40$, twice the product. The coordinates therefore have product $20$ and give the required sum of squares.

The result is $\{\left(4;\,5\right);\, \left(5;\,4\right)\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\begin{cases}x+y=11\\x^2+y^2=61\end{cases}$.

$$
y=11-x
$$

$$
x^2+(11-x)^2=61
$$

$$
2(x-5)(x-6)=0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting the linear relation into the quadratic equation gives an equation of degree two. Each value of $x$ determines exactly one value of $y$. Check the pairs in the original equations.

Expanding $(x+y)^2$ also gives a term $2xy$. Subtracting $61$ from $121$ leaves $60$, twice the product. The coordinates therefore have product $30$ and give the required sum of squares.

The result is $\{\left(5;\,6\right);\, \left(6;\,5\right)\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}x+y=6\\x^2+y^2=18\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+(6-x)^2&=18\\[6pt]
2x^2-12x+18&=0\\[6pt]
2(x-3)^2&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
x=3,\qquad y=6-3=3.
$$

::grafico{f="6-x" curve="sqrt(18)*cos(t),sqrt(18)*sin(t),0,6.283185307" legenda="y=6-x;x^2+y^2=18" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="3,3,pieno,P" titolo="The line is tangent to the circle at $(3;3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting $y=6-x$ into the second equation gives a trinomial equal to twice $(x-3)^2$. The square vanishes only for $x=3$, which also fixes $y=3$. The pair satisfies $3+3=6$ and $3^2+3^2=18$. The double root corresponds to a single point shared by the curves.

</div>
