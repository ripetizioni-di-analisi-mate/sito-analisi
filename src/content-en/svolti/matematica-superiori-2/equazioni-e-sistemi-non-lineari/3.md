## Exercise 1

Solve $\sqrt{x+4}=5$.

$$
x\ge-4
$$

$$
x+4=25
$$

$$
x=21
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square root equals a positive number. On the domain, squaring both sides does not introduce a sign conflict. The value obtained also satisfies the original equation.

The right side $5$ is positive, so squaring does not change the solution set on the domain. Substituting $x=21$ gives the radicand $21+4=25$. The principal square root is $5$.

The result is $\{21\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\sqrt{x+9}=\sqrt x+2$.

$$
x\ge0
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+9&=x+4\sqrt x+4\\[6pt]
4\sqrt x&=5\\[6pt]
x&=\frac{25}{16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right side is positive on the domain $x\ge0$, so squaring first preserves equivalence. Expanding $(\sqrt x+2)^2$ gives the cross term $4\sqrt x$, which remains after canceling $x$ on both sides. The equation $\sqrt x=\dfrac54$ gives $x=\dfrac{25}{16}$. Substituting gives $\dfrac{13}{4}$ for $\sqrt{x+9}$ and $\dfrac54+2=\dfrac{13}{4}$ for the right side.

</div>
