## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}x+y=9\\xy=20\end{cases}$. How many solutions are there as ordered pairs?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         t^2-9t+20&=(t-4)(t-5)=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\{(4;5),(5;4)\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given sum determines the linear coefficient $-9$, and the product fixes the constant $20$. The values $4$ and $5$ make the auxiliary polynomial zero. Both are needed in the same system: assigning one to $x$ fixes the other by the sum. The ordered pairs differ because the values differ. The checks $4+5=9$ and $4\cdot5=20$ hold in either order. Asking for two numbers without any order would give just one pair of values.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Can two real numbers satisfy $x+y=6$ and $xy=10$? Show this without solving for the unknowns individually.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         (x-y)^2&=(x+y)^2-4xy\\[6pt]
         &=36-40=-4.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If both equations held, the squared difference would be negative, which is impossible over the reals. The greatest product of two real numbers with sum $6$ is $9$: it occurs when their difference is zero, with $x=y=3$. The auxiliary equation $t^2-6t+10=0$ gives the same result because its discriminant is $-4$. The two conditions are incompatible even though each equation on its own has solutions.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}x+y=6\\xy=9\end{cases}$ and link the double root to the point of intersection.

::grafico{f="6-x;9/x" asintoti="x=0" legenda="y=6-x;y=\dfrac9x" xmin=-0.25 xmax=6.8 ymin=-0.35 ymax=8.4 punti="3,3,pieno" titolo="The line meets the positive branch at just one point"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         t^2-6t+9&=(t-3)^2=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\{(3;3)\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root $3$ occurs twice in the polynomial, so it assigns the same value to both unknowns. Swapping the coordinates gives no second pair. The checks are $3+3=6$ and $3\cdot3=9$. The graph shows the line $y=6-x$ and the positive branch of $y=\dfrac9x$. The other part of the curve, where both coordinates are negative, cannot have sum $6$. Thus $(3;3)$ is the only intersection of the entire system.

</div>
