## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x^3-16x}{x-4}=0$ and distinguish the numerator zeroes from the zeroes of the fraction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{4\}\\[6pt]
         x^3-16x&=x(x-4)(x+4)\\[6pt]
         S&=\{-4,0\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is defined only if $x\ne4$. The numerator has three zeroes, $-4$, $0$, and $4$. At $4$, both numerator and denominator vanish and the fraction is undefined. At the other two points the denominators are $-8$ and $-4$, so the quotient is zero. Cancelling $x-4$ gives the same set, since it amounts to using $x(x+4)$ on the original domain. The new expression alone would also be defined at the excluded value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{x^3-9x}{x-3}=6x$. Why does one candidate fail even though it satisfies the simplified equation?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x&\ne3,\\[6pt]
         x(x+3)&=6x\\[6pt]
         x(x-3)&=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\{0\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring the numerator as $x(x-3)(x+3)$ allows cancellation only where the common factor is nonzero. The simplified equation gives candidates $0$ and $3$. The latter makes both $x(x+3)$ and $6x$ equal $18$, but the original fraction already excludes it. Check $x=0$ instead: the numerator is zero, the denominator is $-3$, and $6x$ is also zero. Solving the simplified equation works only together with the domain condition.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x^3-4x}{x^2-4}=0$. Identify which points of the simplified line are missing from the original graph.

::grafico{f="x" xmin=-3.4 xmax=3.5 ymin=-3.4 ymax=3.5 uguale=1 punti="-2,-2,vuoto;0,0,pieno;2,2,vuoto" titolo="The two open circles mark the original exclusions"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,2\}\\[6pt]
         \frac{x(x^2-4)}{x^2-4}&=x\qquad(x\in D),\\[6pt]
         S&=\{0\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is $(x-2)(x+2)$, so both horizontal coordinates where it vanishes are excluded. Cancelling it shows that the graph agrees with $y=x$ only on its domain. The missing points are $(-2,-2)$ and $(2,2)$. The origin is present because the denominator equals $-4$ and the numerator is zero. It is therefore the fraction’s only real zero. Cancelling two factors still leaves the domain determined by the original form.

</div>
