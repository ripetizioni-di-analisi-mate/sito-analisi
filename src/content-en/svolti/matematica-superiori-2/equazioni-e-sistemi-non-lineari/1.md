## Exercise 1

Solve $x^4-13x^2+36=0$ and recover the four horizontal coordinates of the zeroes.

::grafico{f="x^4-13*x^2+36" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-8.5 ymax=40.7 punti="-3,0,pieno;-2,0,pieno;2,0,pieno;3,0,pieno" titolo="Two positive values of t give four zeroes"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         t&=x^2\ge0\\[6pt]
         t^2-13t+36&=(t-4)(t-9)=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\{-3,-2,2,3\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $x^4$ is the square of $x^2$, so substitution gives a quadratic. The sum of $4$ and $9$ is $13$, and their product is $36$, so those are the two values of $t$. Both are positive, and each gives two opposite coordinates. The graph is symmetric about the vertical axis because changing the sign of $x$ preserves the even powers. An exact check is $x^4-13x^2+36=(x^2-4)(x^2-9)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $2x^4-18x^2=0$ and explain why it has three distinct roots.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         2t^2-18t&=2t(t-9)=0\\[6pt]
         t&\in\{0,9\}\\[6pt]
         S&=\{-3,0,3\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $t=x^2$, factor out $2t$. One value found is zero, which corresponds only to $x=0$. The other is nine, giving the two coordinates $-3$ and $3$. To check the count in $x$, factor the original polynomial into $2x^2(x-3)(x+3)$. The factor $x^2$ carries two copies of the same zero and each linear factor contributes another. There are three distinct roots even though the polynomial has degree four.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $x^4+x^2-6=0$ over the reals. Which auxiliary value must be rejected?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         t^2+t-6&=(t+3)(t-2)=0\\[6pt]
         t=-3&\notin[0,+\infty)\\[6pt]
         t=2&\implies x=\pm\sqrt2.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation in $t$ has one negative and one positive root. To return to $x$, however, $t$ must be a real square. The value $-3$ solves only the auxiliary equation and gives no solution of the original. With $t=2$, both signs of the square root remain. For both, $x^2=2$ and $x^4=4$, so substitution gives $4+2-6=0$. This check uses only the square and works the same way for the positive and negative roots.

</div>
