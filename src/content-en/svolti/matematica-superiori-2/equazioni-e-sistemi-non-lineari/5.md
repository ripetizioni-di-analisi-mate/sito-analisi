## Exercise 1

Solve $|x-4|=5$.

$$
x-4=5\quad\text{or}\quad x-4=-5
$$

$$
x=9\quad\text{or}\quad x=-1
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x-4);5" punti="-1,5,pieno,A;9,5,pieno,B" xmin=-3 xmax=11 ymin=-1 ymax=9 titolo="The solutions of the first exercise are $-1$ and $9$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value measures distance from the given number. A positive distance gives one point on each side of that number. This leads to two linear equations.

The distance is measured from $4$ and must equal $5$. Subtracting this distance gives $4-5=-1$, and adding it gives $4+5=9$. The two points lie on opposite sides of the starting value.

The result is $\{-1;\,9\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $|2x-1|=x+2$.

$$
x\ge-2
$$

Where $x\ge\dfrac12$, the absolute value is $|2x-1|=2x-1$.

$$
2x-1=x+2\implies x=3.
$$

Where $-2\le x<\dfrac12$, the absolute value is $|2x-1|=1-2x$.

$$
1-2x=x+2\implies x=-\frac13.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right side must be nonnegative. On the resulting domain, $x=\dfrac12$ is where $2x-1$ changes sign. The solution $3$ belongs to the first branch and makes both sides equal to $5$. The solution $-\dfrac13$ belongs to the second branch and makes both sides equal to $\dfrac53$.

</div>
