## Exercise 1

Solve $|x-4|=5$ and explain how to read the solutions on the number line.

::grafico{xmin=-3.15 xmax=11.3 ymin=-0.9 ymax=1.13 uguale=1 assi=0 vettori="-2.7,0,10.7,0;4,0.55,-1,0.55;4,0.55,9,0.55" punti="-1,0,pieno;4,0,pieno;9,0,pieno" segmenti="-1,0,-1,0.55;4,0,4,0.55;9,0,9,0.55" etichette="-1.16,-0.47,−1;3.91,-0.47,4;8.89,-0.47,9;1.36,0.8,5;6.38,0.8,5;10.81,0.1,x" titolo="Both solutions are five units from 4"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x-4=-5&\implies x=-1\\[6pt]
         x-4=5&\implies x=9.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value measures distance from $4$. Moving five units left gives $-1$, and moving five units right gives $9$. The two cases correspond to arguments $-5$ and $5$ inside the absolute value. The checks are $|-1-4|=5$ and $|9-4|=5$. The coordinates are not opposites about the origin because their centre is $4$. Their midpoint $\dfrac{-1+9}{2}=4$ checks symmetry about the correct centre.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $|x+1|=|2x-5|$ by equating their squares.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         (x+1)^2-(2x-5)^2&=0\\[6pt]
         (6-x)(3x-4)&=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\left\{\frac43,6\right\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both original sides are nonnegative, so comparing them is equivalent to comparing their squares. The difference of squares vanishes when the arguments agree or are opposites. At $x=6$, both arguments are $7$. At $x=\dfrac43$, they are $\dfrac73$ and $-\dfrac73$. In both cases the absolute values agree. There is no additional sign restriction on the arguments, because nonnegativity applies to the absolute values as a whole.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $|3x-2|=x+2$ and match each solution to its branch.

::grafico{f="abs(3*x-2);x+2" legenda="y=|3x-2|;y=x+2" xmin=-1.85 xmax=3.2 ymin=-0.65 ymax=7.4 punti="0,2,pieno;2,4,pieno;0.666666667,0,pieno" titolo="The intersections fall on different branches"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x&\ge-2,\\[6pt]
         2-3x=x+2&\implies x=0 \qquad(-2\le x<\tfrac23),\\[6pt]
         3x-2=x+2&\implies x=2 \qquad(x\ge\tfrac23).
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right side is nonnegative only for $x\ge-2$. The argument of the absolute value changes sign at $\dfrac23$. To its left, use $2-3x$, and the resulting value $0$ lies on that branch. To its right, including the boundary, use $3x-2$, and the resulting value $2$ lies on the other branch. The common values in the checks are $2$ and $4$. At $x=\dfrac23$ the absolute value is zero while the right side is positive.

</div>
