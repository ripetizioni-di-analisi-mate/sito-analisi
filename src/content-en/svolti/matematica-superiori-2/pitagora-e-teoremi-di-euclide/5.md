## Exercise 1

In triangle $ABC$ right at $A$, hypotenuse $BC$ is $26$ cm and $BH=8$ cm, with $H$ the foot of the altitude. Find both legs and their ratio $AB:AC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
HC&=26-8=18\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
AB&=\sqrt{26\cdot8}=4\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
AC&=\sqrt{26\cdot18}=6\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
AB:AC&=2:3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Segment $BH$ starts at $B$, so it is the projection of $AB$. Subtract it from $BC$ to find the other segment, which matches $AC$. The same hypotenuse goes into both products in the first theorem. Common factors cancel in the leg ratio. Alternatively find the ratio directly as the square root of $\dfrac{8}{18}$. The longer leg is $AC$, consistent with the longer segment $HC$.

The projections add to the full base. The sum of the squares of the legs gives the square of that same base.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a right triangle, leg $AB$ measures $12$ cm and its projection $BH$ measures $\dfrac{144}{13}$ cm. Find $BC$ and $HC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC&=\dfrac{AB^2}{BH}
=144\cdot\dfrac{13}{144}=13\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
HC&=13-\dfrac{144}{13}=\dfrac{25}{13}\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hypotenuse times the projection equals the square of the given leg. The second factor is known, so divide the square by the projection. Dividing by the fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal. The result $13$ cm is greater than the given leg, and subtracting leaves a positive measure for $HC$. As an extra check, $AC^2=BC\cdot HC=25$ gives a leg of $5$ cm.

Check that the given projection is shorter than the leg, since their ratio is less than one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

$ABC$ is right at $A$, and $H$ is the foot of the altitude. Given $AB=8$ cm and $BH=4$ cm, find $AC$ and identify the acute angle measuring $30^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC&=\dfrac{8^2}{4}=16\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
HC&=16-4=12\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
AC&=\sqrt{16\cdot12}=8\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\widehat{ACB}&=30^\circ .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given $BH$ is the projection of the known leg, so the first theorem gives $BC$ by division. The other projection is the remaining part of the hypotenuse, and it gives $AC$. The lengths match half of an equilateral triangle of side $16$ cm. Indeed, leg $AB$ is half the hypotenuse and the other leg is the equilateral triangle's altitude. The angle opposite $AB$ is therefore $30^\circ$, at $C$.

Comparing the projections identifies the smaller leg before computing the roots from the first theorem.

</div>
