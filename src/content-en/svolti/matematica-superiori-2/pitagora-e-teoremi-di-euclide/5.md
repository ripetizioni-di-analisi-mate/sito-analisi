## Exercise 1

The hypotenuse of a right triangle measures $25\,\mathrm{cm}$. The projection of leg $AB$ onto the hypotenuse measures $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find $AB$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^2&=cp=25\cdot9=225 \\[5pt]
AB&=\sqrt{225}=15\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two given quantities are the full hypotenuse and the projection of the requested leg. Thus **Euclid’s first theorem** applies directly. The length $9$ belongs to the segment on the hypotenuse. The theorem multiplies it by $25$ to give the square of $AB$. Since $p<c$, it follows that $cp<c^2$. The square root of the product therefore satisfies $AB<c$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a right triangle the hypotenuse is $13\,\mathrm{cm}$ long and leg $AB$ is $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. Find both projections onto the hypotenuse.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p&=\frac{AB^2}{c}=\frac{25}{13}\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
q&=c-p=13-\frac{25}{13} \\[5pt]
&=\frac{144}{13}\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In Euclid’s first theorem, the projection can be isolated by dividing the square of the leg by the hypotenuse. Here $AB$ is known, so the first calculation gives $p$, the segment corresponding to $AB$. The foot of the altitude divides the hypotenuse into the two projections. Subtracting the known projection from $13$ gives the projection of the other leg.

</div>
