## Exercise 1

An isosceles triangle has equal sides of $17$ cm and base $16$ cm. Find its altitude and area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\dfrac{16}{2}=8\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
h&=\sqrt{17^2-8^2}=15\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\mathcal A&=\dfrac{16\cdot15}{2}=120\,\mathrm{cm}^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The altitude to the base splits the isosceles triangle into two congruent right triangles. In each the sloping side of $17$ cm is the hypotenuse, and each half of the base is $8$ cm long. Thus finding the altitude subtracts the square of $8$ from $17^2$. The area of the whole triangle instead uses the base of $16$ cm. As a check, add the two halves, each with area $\dfrac{8\cdot15}{2}=60$ cm$^2$.

To check the right triangle used, the square of half the base plus the square of the altitude must recover the square of the equal side.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isosceles triangle has base $24$ cm and area $60$ cm$^2$. Find its perimeter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=\dfrac{2\cdot60}{24}=5\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
l&=\sqrt{12^2+5^2}=13\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
p_{\mathrm{tri}}&=24+2\cdot13=50\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area gives the altitude by doubling the area and dividing by the base. In the isosceles triangle this altitude is also a median, so take a right triangle with legs of $12$ cm and $5$ cm. The sloping side is its hypotenuse and comes from adding the squares. The perimeter contains both copies of this side and the whole base. The two sides of $13$ cm can meet because their combined length exceeds the base.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An isosceles triangle has equal sides of $10$ cm and area $48$ cm$^2$. Find every possible base length.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^2+h^2&=100,\qquad sh=48,\\[6pt]
(s+h)^2&=100+2\cdot48=196,\\[6pt]
s+h&=14,\qquad h=14-s,\\[6pt]
s(14-s)&=48\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(s-6)(s-8)=0,\\[6pt]
2s&\in\{12\,\mathrm{cm},\,16\,\mathrm{cm}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write $s$ for half the base and $h$ for the altitude. The product $sh$ is the area of the entire isosceles triangle. In the resulting right triangle the squares instead add to $10^2$. Expanding the square of the sum gives $s+h=14$. The two positive numbers with that sum and product $48$ are $6$ and $8$. Either can be the half-base. The two orders swap the half-base and altitude and both satisfy the given area.

The product and sum conditions give a quadratic with two distinct positive solutions.

</div>
