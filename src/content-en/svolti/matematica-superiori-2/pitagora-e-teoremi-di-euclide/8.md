## Exercise 1

An isosceles triangle has equal sides of length $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a base of length $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^2&=15^2-\left(\frac{18}2\right)^2 \\[5pt]
h&=12 \\[5pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{18\cdot12}2=108\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The altitude to the base splits the isosceles triangle into two congruent right triangles. In either one, the known lengths are the hypotenuse $15$ and a leg equal to half the base. To obtain the square of the altitude, subtract the square of $9$ from the square of $15$. After taking the square root, return to the full triangle. The area uses all $18$ centimeters of the base.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isosceles triangle has equal sides of length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a base of length $16\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^2&=10^2-\left(\frac{16}2\right)^2 \\[5pt]
h&=6 \\[5pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{16\cdot6}2=48\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The altitude to the base splits the isosceles triangle into two congruent right triangles. In either one, the known lengths are the hypotenuse $10$ and a leg equal to half the base. To obtain the square of the altitude, subtract the square of $8$ from the square of $10$. After taking the square root, return to the full triangle. The area uses all $16$ centimeters of the base.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An isosceles triangle has perimeter $32\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a base of $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area.

::grafico{uguale=1 assi=0 poligoni="0,0 12,0 6,8" linee="6,8 6,0;6,0.45 6.45,0.45 6.45,0" etichette="6,-0.55,12 cm;6.55,3.5,h" xmin=-1 xmax=13 ymin=-1.5 ymax=9 titolo="The altitude of the isosceles triangle bisects the base"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
l&=\frac{32-12}2=10 \\[5pt]
h&=\sqrt{10^2-6^2}=8 \\[5pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{12\cdot8}2=48\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the base from the perimeter leaves the two equal sides. Each measures $10$ and is the hypotenuse of one of the right triangles formed by drawing the altitude. The leg on the base measures $6$, so the Pythagorean theorem gives $h=8$. The requested area is for the whole triangle, with base $12$.

</div>
