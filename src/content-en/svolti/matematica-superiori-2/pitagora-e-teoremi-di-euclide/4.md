## Exercise 1

Find the altitude of an equilateral triangle of side length $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^2&=6^2-\left(\frac{6}2\right)^2 \\[5pt]
&=36-9=27 \\[5pt]
h&=\sqrt{9\cdot3}=3\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In an equilateral triangle, the altitude splits the base into two equal segments. The resulting right triangle therefore has hypotenuse $6$ and one leg of length $3$. The leg opposite the $60^\circ$ angle is the altitude. Isolating its square gives $27$. Since $27$ is the product of $9$ and $3$, the altitude has factor $3$ outside the radical.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The altitude of an equilateral triangle measures $6\sqrt3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the perimeter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6\sqrt3&=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}l \\[5pt]
l&=\frac{2\cdot6\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}=12 \\[5pt]
P&=3l=36\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The altitude is proportional to the side length with factor $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$. Since the altitude is known and the side length is needed, divide by that nonzero factor. The factor $\sqrt3$ cancels, giving $l=12$. All three sides have the same length, so the perimeter is $36$ centimeters.

</div>
