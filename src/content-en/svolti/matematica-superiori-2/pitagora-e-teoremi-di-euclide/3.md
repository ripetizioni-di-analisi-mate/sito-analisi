## Exercise 1

Find the diagonal of a rectangle with sides $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.

::grafico{uguale=1 assi=0 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,3 0,3" linee="0,0 6,3" etichette="3.0,-0.3,6;-0.4,1.5,3" xmin=-1 xmax=6.75 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.75 titolo="The interior segment is the diagonal of the rectangle"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^2&=3^2+6^2=45 \\[5pt]
d&=\sqrt{45}=\sqrt{9\cdot5} \\[5pt]
&=3 \sqrt{5}\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonal joins two opposite vertices. One of the other vertices is at the right angle, so the sides of lengths $3$ and $6$ are the legs of a triangle with hypotenuse $d$. The Pythagorean theorem gives $d^2=45$. To simplify the root, write $45$ as the product of $9$, a perfect square, and $5$. The factor $3$ comes out of the root. The unit is centimeters again because a squared length was square-rooted.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A device has a rectangular screen. Its longer side is twice the shorter side, and its diagonal measures $10\sqrt5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the side lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(10\sqrt5)^2&=s^2+(2s)^2 \\[5pt]
500&=5s^2 \\[5pt]
s&=10\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
2s&=20\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $s$ be the shorter side. The ratio lets us write the other side as $2s$, so the Pythagorean theorem gives an equation in one unknown. The diagonal has square $500$, and the two legs contribute $s^2$ and $4s^2$, respectively. The positive solution is $s=10$.

</div>
