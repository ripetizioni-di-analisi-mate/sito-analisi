## Exercise 1

Triangle $ABC$ is right at $A$ with $AB=35$ cm and $AC=12$ cm. Find $BC$ and check that it is the greatest length.

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC^2&=35^2+12^2=1225+144=1369,\\[6pt]
BC&=37\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two known sides form the right angle, so they are both legs. The side opposite that angle is the hypotenuse $BC$. Add the squares and take the positive square root. The result $37$ cm is longer than both $35$ cm and $12$ cm, as a hypotenuse must be, and shorter than the sum of the legs. The equality of squares is more precise than these bounds and justifies the length.

Check the area of the square on the hypotenuse by expanding $(40-3)^2$ with its mixed term.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On a flat surface, two consecutive perpendicular sections measure $0.6$ m and $80$ cm. What is the direct distance in metres between the endpoints of the route?

$$
\begin{aligned}
80\,\mathrm{cm}&=\dfrac45\,\mathrm m,\\[6pt]
d^2&=\left(\dfrac35\right)^2+\left(\dfrac45\right)^2
=\dfrac{9+16}{25}=1,\\[6pt]
d&=1\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Express the sections in the same unit before squaring. The first equals three fifths of a metre and the second four fifths. The right angle is at the meeting point of the consecutive sections. The direct segment does not pass through that vertex and is therefore the hypotenuse. The whole route is $1.4$ m long and the direct segment is shorter. The result is longer than either individual section, consistent with the figure.

Squaring before converting would mix square metres and square centimetres in the same calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two sides of a triangle measure $7$ cm and $24$ cm. Find the third side if the two are perpendicular. What values would be possible without that hypothesis?

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\sqrt{7^2+24^2}=25\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
|24-7|&<d<24+7
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad17<d<31 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Perpendicularity says that the two known lengths are the legs. The square root of the sum of their squares then gives the third side. If only the two lengths are known, the third can take any length in the open interval from the triangle inequality. The value $25$ cm is one of these. The included angle is what turns an interval of possibilities into a single length.

For example, $20$ cm lies in the interval and gives a triangle in which the given sides are not perpendicular.

</div>
