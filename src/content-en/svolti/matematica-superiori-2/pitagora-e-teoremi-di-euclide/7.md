## Exercise 1

In a right triangle, the projections of the legs onto the hypotenuse measure $27\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $48\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=27+48=75 \\[5pt]
h&=\sqrt{27\cdot48}=36 \\[5pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{75\cdot 36}2=1350\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The choice of base determines which altitude to use. The given quantities are the two parts of the hypotenuse, so take the full hypotenuse of length $75$ as the base. To find the perpendicular altitude to that base, apply Euclid’s second theorem. The square root of the product of the two parts gives $h=36$. Halving the product of the hypotenuse and that altitude gives the area.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A right triangle has hypotenuse $25\,\mathrm{cm}$. One projection is $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. Find the area in two ways.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=25-9=16 \\[5pt]
h&=\sqrt{9\cdot16}=12 \\[5pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{25\cdot12}2=150\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB&=\sqrt{25\cdot9}=15 \\[5pt]
AC&=\sqrt{25\cdot16}=20 \\[5pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{15\cdot20}2=150\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Using the hypotenuse as base requires its corresponding altitude. The projections are $9$ and $16$, so Euclid’s second theorem gives $h=12$. With leg $AB$ as the base, the altitude is $AC$ because the two legs are perpendicular. Euclid’s first theorem gives their lengths. The two half-products agree because they describe the same triangle.

</div>
