## Exercise 1

The leg projections of a right triangle are $4$ cm and $9$ cm. Find the area in two ways.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=4+9=13\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad h=\sqrt{4\cdot9}=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\mathcal A&=\dfrac{13\cdot6}{2}=39\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[6pt]
a&=2\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad b=3\sqrt{13}\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\dfrac{ab}{2}&=\dfrac{6\cdot13}{2}=39\,\mathrm{cm}^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Taking the hypotenuse as the base, add the projections and find the altitude from their product. The other route starts with the legs. The first theorem gives two roots with the shared factor $\sqrt{13}$, making their product an integer. The expressions must agree because they measure the same area. The result has square units whether we multiply the legs or multiply the hypotenuse by its altitude.

The hypotenuse method avoids computing both legs, while the other method provides those two additional lengths.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A drawing is supposed to represent a right triangle with area $48$ cm$^2$ and hypotenuse $12$ cm. Can it exist?

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=\dfrac{2\cdot48}{12}=8\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
h_{\max}&=\dfrac{12}{2}=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
8&>6 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area equation produces a positive altitude, but that is insufficient. With the hypotenuse fixed, the altitude squared is the product of two positive numbers adding to that hypotenuse. The product is largest for equal projections. The altitude can therefore be at most $6$ cm. The value inferred from the area is above that bound, making the data incompatible. The maximum area, $36$ cm$^2$, is below the given area as well.

Equivalently, the projection product would have to be $64$, although its maximum for projections adding to $12$ is $36$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

$ABC$ is right at $A$ with hypotenuse $29$ cm and area $145$ cm$^2$. With $H$ the foot of the altitude to $BC$ and $AB<AC$, find $BH$ and $HC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=\dfrac{2\cdot145}{29}=10\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
p+q&=29,\qquad pq=100,\\[6pt]
p(29-p)&=100
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(p-4)(p-25)=0,\\[6pt]
BH&=4\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad HC=25\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First use the area with the hypotenuse as the base to find the altitude. The projections must then have sum $29$ and product $100$. Identify $4$ and $25$ as factors with the required sum, or solve the equation. The leg order identifies the arrangement along the base: $AB$ is shorter, so its projection $BH$ is shorter too. Both projection relations check the resulting configuration.

Reversing the leg inequality would only swap the projections. The area and hypotenuse length would stay the same.

</div>
