## Exercise 1

The altitude to the hypotenuse splits it into segments of lengths $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $27\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the altitude of the right triangle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^2&=pq=12\cdot27=324 \\[5pt]
h&=\sqrt{324}=18\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both given segments lie on the hypotenuse. By **Euclid’s second theorem**, their product is the square of the requested altitude. Since $h$ is a length, take the positive square root of $324$. The altitude $18$ lies between the two projection lengths because $12^2<324<27^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The altitude to the hypotenuse is $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. One projection is four times the other. Find the projection lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=4p \\[5pt]
12^2&=p(4p)=4p^2 \\[5pt]
p&=6\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
q&=24\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $p$ be the shorter projection. The stated ratio gives $q=4p$, so Euclid’s second theorem relates $p^2$ directly to the square of the altitude. The shorter projection is positive, so it is $6$. The lengths $6$ and $24$ have ratio $4$ and product $144$, as required.

</div>
