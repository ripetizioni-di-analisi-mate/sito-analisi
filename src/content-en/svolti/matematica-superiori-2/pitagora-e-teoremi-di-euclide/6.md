## Exercise 1

Triangle $ABC$ is right at $A$. The foot of the altitude to hypotenuse $BC$ is $H$. Given $BH=3$ cm and $HC=12$ cm, find $AH$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AH^2&=BH\cdot HC=3\cdot12=36,\\[6pt]
AH&=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\dfrac{BH}{AH}&=\dfrac36=\dfrac6{12}=\dfrac{AH}{HC}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two pieces of the hypotenuse are the projections of the legs. The second theorem uses their product, a quantity in square centimetres. Its positive square root gives the altitude. The value $6$ cm is also the positive middle term of the proportion between $3$ and $12$. Check by dividing the shorter projection by the altitude and the altitude by the longer projection. The ratios agree, as required by the correspondence between the smaller triangles.

The sum instead gives the full hypotenuse, which measures $15$ cm in this figure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The altitude to the hypotenuse measures $10$ cm, and one projection measures $5$ cm. Find the other projection and the hypotenuse.

$$
\begin{aligned}
10^2&=5q,\\[6pt]
q&=\dfrac{100}{5}=20\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
c&=5+20=25\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the altitude is known, so solve the second theorem for one of the factors. Divide the squared altitude by the known projection, going from square units back to linear units. Recover the hypotenuse afterwards by adding its two consecutive segments. A direct check is $\sqrt{5\cdot20}=10$ cm. The altitude is also below half the hypotenuse, as every configuration with those data requires.

The recovered projection exceeds the altitude, as expected since the other projection is shorter than the altitude.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In $ABC$ right at $A$, $H$ is the foot of the altitude to $BC$. Given $BC=50$ cm and $AH=24$ cm. What lengths can $BH$ and $HC$ have? Match them given also that $AB>AC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p+q&=50,\qquad pq=24^2=576,\\[6pt]
p(50-p)&=576,\\[6pt]
(p-25)^2&=625-576=49,\\[6pt]
p&=25\pm7 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The projections must satisfy both the sum known from the hypotenuse and the product known from the altitude. Substitute the sum and complete the square. The two solutions are $18$ cm and $32$ cm, both positive. With no information about the longer leg, the two segments could be swapped. The first theorem matches the longer projection to the longer leg. Thus $BH=32$ cm and $HC=18$ cm.

The two lengths also satisfy the product condition directly. The converse can then be used to construct a right triangle.

</div>
