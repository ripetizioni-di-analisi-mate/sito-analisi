## Exercise 1

A right triangle has hypotenuse $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a leg of length $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the other leg.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^2&=c^2-a^2 \\[5pt]
&=15^2-9^2=225-81=144 \\[5pt]
b&=\sqrt{144}=12\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the identity $c^2=a^2+b^2$, the square of the hypotenuse is already known. To isolate $b^2$, subtract $81$ from $225$. The difference is positive because the hypotenuse is longer than the given leg. Take the positive square root of $144$. As a check, adding $144$ to the square of the known leg gives $225$, the square of the hypotenuse.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A right triangle has hypotenuse $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a leg of length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the other leg.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^2&=c^2-a^2 \\[5pt]
&=14^2-10^2=196-100=96 \\[5pt]
b&=\sqrt{96}=4 \sqrt{6}\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the identity $c^2=a^2+b^2$, the square of the hypotenuse is already known. To isolate $b^2$, subtract $100$ from $196$. The difference is positive because the hypotenuse is longer than the given leg. Take the positive square root of $96$. As a check, adding $96$ to the square of the known leg gives $196$, the square of the hypotenuse.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $13\,\mathrm m$ ladder reaches a point $12\,\mathrm m$ above the ground on a vertical wall. Find the distance from the foot of the ladder to the wall.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^2&=13^2-12^2 \\[5pt]
&=(13-12)(13+12)=25 \\[5pt]
d&=5\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wall and ground are perpendicular. The ladder crosses the triangle and lies opposite that angle, so it is the hypotenuse. The gap is the horizontal leg. Its square is the difference of the two squares, which here can be found by multiplying the difference and the sum of $13$ and $12$.

</div>
