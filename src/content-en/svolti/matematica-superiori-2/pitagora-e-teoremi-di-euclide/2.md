## Exercise 1

A right triangle has hypotenuse $25$ cm and one leg of length $15$ cm. Find the other leg.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^2&=25^2-15^2\\[6pt]
&=(25-15)(25+15)=400,\\[6pt]
b&=20\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hypotenuse is already known. To find the square of the missing leg, remove the other leg's square from the hypotenuse square. Factoring the difference of squares makes this a mental calculation, multiplying $10$ by $40$. The operation concerns the areas of the squares built on the sides. Taking the square root then gives the leg length of $20$ cm. Check that $15^2+20^2=25^2$.

The simple difference between the hypotenuse and the known leg is smaller than the missing length, as the triangle inequality also requires.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The hypotenuse of a right triangle measures $\sqrt{65}$ cm and one leg measures $4$ cm. Find the other leg and check the order of the three lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^2&=(\sqrt{65})^2-4^2=65-16=49,\\[6pt]
b&=7\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
4&<7<\sqrt{65}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of the hypotenuse is exactly $65$, so no decimal approximation is needed. The known leg has square $16$. The difference is $49$, the square of $7$. Positive lengths can be put in order by comparing their squares. In particular $49<65$ shows that the new leg is shorter than the hypotenuse, while $16<49$ shows that it is longer than the given leg.

If $\sqrt{65}$ were a leg instead, adding the squares would give a hypotenuse of $\sqrt{65+16}=9$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two sides of a right triangle measure $8$ cm and $10$ cm. Find all possible lengths of the third side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{hyp}}&=\sqrt{8^2+10^2}=2\sqrt{41}\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
d_{\mathrm{leg}}&=\sqrt{10^2-8^2}=6\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If both lengths are legs, the required side is the hypotenuse and the squares are added. If one known length is the hypotenuse, it must be the greater of the two. In that case subtract the square of $8$ from the square of $10$. A third configuration with hypotenuse $8$ cm is impossible because the $10$ cm leg would be longer. The two possible values come from assigning the sides different roles.

</div>
