## Exercise 1

A parallelogram has base $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and corresponding height $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=bh=8\cdot5=40
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the perpendicular height to the base. A parallelogram has the same area as a rectangle with the same base and height. The slanted side does not appear in the area formula.

Height $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ measures the separation between the lines through the parallel sides. Sliding the upper base along its line preserves that distance and the area.

The result is $40$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

A parallelogram has base $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ and corresponding height $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=bh=9\cdot6=54
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the perpendicular height to the base. A parallelogram has the same area as a rectangle with the same base and height. The slanted side does not appear in the area formula.

Rearranging the figure into a rectangle with the same base and height gives a different region containing the same number of square centimeters.

The result is $54$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

A parallelogram has base $12\,\mathrm{cm}$, slanted side $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, and height to the base $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area and perimeter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A&=12\cdot4=48\,\mathrm{cm}^2 \\[5pt]
P&=2(12+5)=34\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The height enters the area because it is the perpendicular distance between the bases. The perimeter follows the boundary. The two five-centimeter slanted sides are therefore needed for the perimeter.

</div>
