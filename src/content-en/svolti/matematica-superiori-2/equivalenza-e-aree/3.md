## Exercise 1

A parallelogram has base $7$, height $4$, and slanted side $5$. Rearrange it as a rectangle with base $7$ and compare the perimeters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&=7\cdot4=28,\\
    P_{\mathrm{par}}&=2(7+5)=24,\qquad P_{\mathrm{rect}}=2(7+4)=22.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Cut off the triangle with base $3$ and height $4$ to the left of the altitude’s foot. Move it right by one whole base to fill the empty part and form a $7\times4$ rectangle. Translation preserves the piece’s area, so the total remains $28$. The boundary changes, however. The two slanted sides of length $5$ give way to perpendicular sides of length $4$. The figures have equal areas, while their perimeters differ by two units.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A parallelogram has area $60$. Its consecutive sides measure $12$ and $10$. Find the height corresponding to each side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    h_{12}&=\frac{60}{12}=5,\\
    h_{10}&=\frac{60}{10}=6.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Using the side of length $12$ as base makes the height the perpendicular distance between the two lines containing sides of that length. Choosing the side of length $10$ changes the pair of parallel lines. The area is the same, so the products must match and the shorter base has the greater height. Check that $12\cdot5=10\cdot6=60$. Neither height should be confused with a consecutive side, since consecutive sides are generally oblique.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A parallelogram has base $6$ and height $3$. Its upper base is shifted horizontally by $10$. Find its area by subtracting two triangles from a rectangle.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A_{\mathrm{rect}}&=(6+10)\cdot3=48,\\
    A_{\mathrm{par}}&=48-2\cdot\frac{10\cdot3}{2}=18.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The surrounding rectangle is $16$ wide because it spans the entire lower base and the shift of the upper side. Outside the parallelogram, the left and right regions are right triangles with legs $10$ and $3$. Subtract each area once. Even if the foot of the height lies outside the base segment, the parallel edges are still $3$ apart. The result matches $6\cdot3=18$ and shows why a change in slant alone leaves the area unchanged.

</div>
