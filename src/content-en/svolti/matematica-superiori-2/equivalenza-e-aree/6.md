## Exercise 1

A regular hexagon has side length $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and apothem $3 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
P=6\cdot6=36
$$

$$
A=\frac{P a}2=\frac{36\cdot(3 \sqrt{3})}2=54 \sqrt{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polygon splits into six triangles with altitude equal to the apothem. The sum of the bases is the perimeter. The total area is therefore half the product of the perimeter and the apothem.

The perimeter is six times side $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Adding six triangle areas with base $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and altitude $3 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$ is equivalent to using the perimeter as the sum of their bases.

The result is $54 \sqrt{3}$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

A regular hexagon has side length $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and apothem $4 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
P=6\cdot8=48
$$

$$
A=\frac{P a}2=\frac{48\cdot(4 \sqrt{3})}2=96 \sqrt{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polygon splits into six triangles with altitude equal to the apothem. The sum of the bases is the perimeter. The total area is therefore half the product of the perimeter and the apothem.

Apothem $4 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm}$ reaches the midpoint of a side. It is the altitude of each of the six triangles.

The result is $96 \sqrt{3}$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

A regular polygon is divided into eight congruent triangles by joining the vertices to the center. Its area is $16(2+2\sqrt2)\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, and its perimeter is $32\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the apothem from the area and perimeter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{2\mathcal A}{P} \\[5pt]
&=\frac{2\cdot16(2+2\sqrt2)}{32}=(2+2\sqrt2)\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The apothem is the same altitude in all eight triangles. Adding their areas gives $\mathcal A=\dfrac{Pa}{2}$. To isolate $a$, double the area and divide by the perimeter.

</div>
