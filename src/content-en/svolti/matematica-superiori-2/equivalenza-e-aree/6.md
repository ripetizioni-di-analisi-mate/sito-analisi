## Exercise 1

A regular hexagon has side length $6$. Use a triangle with a vertex at the centre to find its apothem and area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    a&=\sqrt{6^2-3^2}=3\sqrt3,\\
    A&=\frac{(6\cdot6)(3\sqrt3)}2=54\sqrt3.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The six sectors of $60^\circ$ split the hexagon into equilateral triangles. The two sides from the centre are equal radii and the angle between them is $60^\circ$. The altitude from that vertex meets the side at its midpoint, forming a right triangle with hypotenuse $6$ and one leg $3$. The other leg is the apothem. Each of the six areas uses the same altitude, so adding them equals multiplying the apothem by half the perimeter. The radical keeps the result exact.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A square has perimeter $48\,\mathrm{cm}$ and area $144\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. Find its apothem from the area formula and from the side length.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    a&=\frac{2A}{P}=\frac{288}{48}=6\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    l&=48/4=12\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad a=l/2=6\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Solving the regular polygon formula for the apothem gives twice the area divided by the perimeter. The geometric calculation uses the fact that in a square the distance from the centre to a side is half the side length. Divide the perimeter by four to find the side, and the two methods agree. The apothem measures a distance, so it is in centimetres. Dividing an area by a length cancels one of the two unit factors.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A convex polygon is circumscribed about a circle of radius $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its perimeter is $36\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area without assuming equal sides.

$$
A=\frac{4(l_1+\cdots+l_n)}2=\frac{4\cdot36}2=72\,\mathrm{cm}^2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Joining the circle’s centre to the vertices divides the convex polygon into triangles based along its boundary. The bases need not have equal lengths. However, every side is tangent, so the perpendicular distance from the centre to each side equals the radius $4$. This common factor can be taken outside the sum of triangle areas. The base lengths sum to the given perimeter. Tangency supplies the common altitude even when the polygon is irregular.

</div>
