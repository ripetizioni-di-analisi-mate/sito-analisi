## Exercise 1

A triangle has area $42\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ and base $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. The foot of its altitude lies outside the base segment. Find the altitude.

$$
h=\frac{2A}{b}=\frac{84}{12}=7\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The altitude measures perpendicular distance from the vertex to the base line, so an exterior foot does not change the area formula. Double the area to obtain the product of base and altitude, then divide by $12$. To check, two copies of the triangle form a parallelogram of area $84\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. With base $12$, that requires height $7$. Moving the foot along the line while keeping the perpendicular distance preserves the area.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A trapezoid has bases of lengths $13$ and $7$ and area $50$. Find its height and the areas of the two triangles made by a diagonal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    h&=\frac{2\cdot50}{13+7}=5,\\
    A_1&=\frac{13\cdot5}{2}=\frac{65}{2},\qquad
    A_2=\frac{7\cdot5}{2}=\frac{35}{2}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First find the perpendicular distance between the bases by dividing $100$ by their sum, $20$. The diagonal splits the region into two triangles with that same altitude. Their areas therefore have the same ratio as the bases, $13:7$. You can also obtain them by taking thirteen twentieths and seven twentieths of the total area. The fractions $65/2$ and $35/2$ add to $50$, consistent with the split into two disjoint parts.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A triangle of base $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and altitude $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ has the same area as a parallelogram with base $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the parallelogram’s height.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&=\frac{8\cdot9}{2}=36\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\
    h&=\frac{36}{12}=3\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equal area makes one quantity common even though the shapes have different bases, heights, and boundaries. For the triangle, halve the product $8\cdot9$ to obtain $36$. For the parallelogram, area is the entire product of base and height, so divide $36$ by $12$. A direct check sets $12h=8\cdot9/2$. Keeping the factor $1/2$ in the parallelogram calculation would double the height and give the wrong area.

</div>
