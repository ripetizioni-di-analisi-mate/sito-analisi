## Exercise 1

A triangle has base $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and corresponding altitude $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=\frac{bh}2=\frac{8\cdot6}2=24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given altitude corresponds to the specified base, so use their product. The triangle covers half the area of a parallelogram with the same base and height. Divide the product by two.

The vertex opposite base $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ is at perpendicular distance $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the line through the base. The foot of the altitude can move without changing the area.

The result is $24$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

A triangle has base $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and corresponding altitude $7\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=\frac{bh}2=\frac{10\cdot7}2=35
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given altitude corresponds to the specified base, so use their product. The triangle covers half the area of a parallelogram with the same base and height. Divide the product by two.

A second copy of the triangle with the same dimensions can be joined to the first. The two copies form the region whose area is then halved.

The result is $35$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

An obtuse triangle has base $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and corresponding altitude $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The foot of the altitude lies on the extension of the base, $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the nearer endpoint. Find the area by subtraction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A&=\frac{9\cdot4}{2}-\frac{2\cdot4}{2}=14\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The larger right triangle has base $7+2=9$. Remove the right triangle with base $2$. The altitudes are equal, so seven units of base remain in the area calculation.

</div>
