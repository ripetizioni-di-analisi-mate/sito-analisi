## Exercise 1

In triangle $ABC$, $AB=7$, $AC=5$, and the foot $H$ of the altitude from $C$ is on the extension of $AB$ beyond $A$, with $AH=3$. Find the altitude and area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    CH&=\sqrt{AC^2-AH^2}=\sqrt{25-9}=4,\\
    A_{ABC}&=\frac{7\cdot4}{2}=14.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slanted side $AC$ is the hypotenuse of the exterior right triangle $AHC$. Subtract the square of $AH$ from its square to recover the leg perpendicular to the base. Point $H$ lies beyond segment $AB$, but the distance from $C$ to its line is still $CH$. The diagram also gives a check by subtraction. Triangle $HBC$ has base $10$ and area $20$. Removing area $6$ of $HAC$ leaves the requested area $14$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Triangle $ABC$ has area $39$. A point $M$ on $AB$ splits it into $AM=4$ and $MB=9$. Find the areas of $AMC$ and $MBC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A_{AMC}&=39\cdot\frac4{4+9}=12,\\
    A_{MBC}&=39\cdot\frac9{4+9}=27.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two triangles have bases on the same line. The distance from $C$ to that line is therefore the same in both calculations. Their areas are in the base ratio $4:9$. Of thirteen parts in the whole area, four belong to the first triangle and nine to the second. Check that $12+27=39$. You can also recover their common altitude, $2\cdot39/13=6$, and check each area with the triangle formula.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A triangle has side $AB=12$ and altitude $5$ from $C$. Side $AC$ measures $10$. Find the altitude from $B$ to the line $AC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&=\frac{12\cdot5}{2}=30,\\
    h_{AC}&=\frac{2\cdot30}{10}=6.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

You can choose a different side of the same triangle as base. The corresponding altitude changes, while the area remains $30$. Twice the area always equals the chosen base times its altitude, giving $12\cdot5=10\cdot h_{AC}$. The shorter base of length $10$ must be paired with a longer altitude. The result $6$ fits that comparison and gives the original area when substituted into the second formula.

</div>
