## Exercise 1

A $5\times4$ rectangle is cut from the upper-right corner of a $13\times9$ rectangle. Find the remaining area in two ways.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&=13\cdot9-5\cdot4=117-20=97,\\
    A&=8\cdot9+5\cdot5=72+25=97.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first method starts with the enclosing rectangle and subtracts the missing corner. In the second, cut vertically along the edge of the notch. The left piece keeps the full height $9$, with width $13-5=8$. The right piece has width $5$ and remaining height $9-4=5$. These two pieces have disjoint interiors and exactly fill the L-shape. The matching results provide an arithmetic check of the two methods.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sheet $2\,\mathrm m\times1.2\,\mathrm m$ has a rectangular hole entirely inside it, with sides of lengths $40$ and $30\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the remaining area and the full boundary length including the hole.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&=2\cdot\frac65-\frac25\cdot\frac3{10}=\frac{57}{25}\,\mathrm{m}^2,\\
    P&=2\left(2+\frac65\right)+2\left(\frac25+\frac3{10}\right)
    =\frac{39}{5}\,\mathrm m.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First convert the hole’s sides to $0.4$ and $0.3$ metres. The full sheet has area $2.4\,\mathrm{m}^2$, and the hole removes $0.12\,\mathrm{m}^2$, leaving $2.28\,\mathrm{m}^2$. The boundary calculation uses a different operation. The outer boundary stays $6.4$ metres long and the cut creates an inner boundary of another $1.4$ metres. Both are part of the remaining sheet’s boundary, so add them to get $7.8$ metres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rectangles $7\times5$ and $4\times6$ overlap in a $2\times3$ rectangle. Find the area covered by at least one of them.

$$
A=7\cdot5+4\cdot6-2\cdot3=35+24-6=53.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Adding the two rectangle areas counts the cells in the overlap twice. Subtracting one copy of that area leaves each cell counted once. In the diagram the overlap is the highlighted rectangle of area $6$. Check by taking the parts belonging to just one rectangle, $35-6=29$ and $24-6=18$, then adding the overlap once: $29+18+6=53$. Subtracting it twice instead would give the area covered by exactly one rectangle.

</div>
