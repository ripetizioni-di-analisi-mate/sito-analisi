## Exercise 1

A rectangle measuring $5$ cm by $3$ cm is removed from the inside of a rectangle measuring $9$ cm by $7$ cm. What area remains?

$$
A=(9)(7)-(5)(3)=48
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The removed rectangle is completely inside the figure. Subtract its area from the area of the outer rectangle. Both products are measured in square centimeters.

The removed rectangle does not overlap the pieces that remain. Subtract the product of $9$ and $7$ from the product of $5$ and $3$.

The result is $48$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle measuring $6$ cm by $4$ cm is removed from the inside of a rectangle measuring $10$ cm by $8$ cm. What area remains?

$$
A=(10)(8)-(6)(4)=56
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The removed rectangle is completely inside the figure. Subtract its area from the area of the outer rectangle. Both products are measured in square centimeters.

The area can also be found by dividing the remaining region into disjoint rectangles. Subtraction avoids having to find the dimensions of each remaining strip.

The result is $56$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular panel measuring $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ has two disjoint square holes of side length $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the area of the remaining material.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 12,0 12,2 0,2;0,5 12,5 12,8 0,8;0,2 1,2 1,5 0,5;4,2 8,2 8,5 4,5;11,2 12,2 12,5 11,5" linee="0,0 12,0 12,8 0,8 0,0;1,2 4,2 4,5 1,5 1,2;8,2 11,2 11,5 8,5 8,2" etichette="6,-0.35,12;12.5,3.7,8;2.5,3.4,3;9.5,3.4,3" xmin=-0.8 xmax=13.2 ymin=-1 ymax=9 titolo="The interior squares are the two holes"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A&=12\cdot8-2\cdot 3^2=78\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The whole rectangle covers $96$ square centimeters. The two holes are inside the rectangle and disjoint, so together they occupy twice the area of one square. Subtract $18$ square centimeters.

</div>
