## Exercise 1

Find the area of a rectangle with base $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $4\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
A=bh=6\cdot4=24
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,4 0,4" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=5 titolo="The first rectangle has base $6$, height $4$, and area $24$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of the rectangle is the product of its base and height. The lengths use the same unit, so multiply them directly. Express the result in square units.

The product counts how many unit squares fit in the figure. Each one-centimeter-wide strip has area given by the numerical value of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The strips cover the full base $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The result is $24$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the area of a rectangle with base $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $5\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
A=bh=7\cdot5=35
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of the rectangle is the product of its base and height. The lengths use the same unit, so multiply them directly. Express the result in square units.

This calculation combines two perpendicular dimensions. The perimeter would instead require adding the four lengths, with centimeters as its unit.

The result is $35$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two rectangles have equal areas. The first has sides $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The second has a side of $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the other side and compare their perimeters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A&=12\cdot5=60\,\mathrm{cm}^2 \\[5pt]
h_2&=\frac{60}{10}=6\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
P_1&=2(12+5)=34\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
P_2&=2(10+6)=32\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equality of area preserves the product of the sides. In the second rectangle, divide the known area by $10$ to find the missing side. Adding the lengths gives the perimeters. The second perimeter is $2$ centimeters shorter.

</div>
