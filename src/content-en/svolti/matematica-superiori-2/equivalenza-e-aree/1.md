## Exercise 1

A rectangular sheet measures $160\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $0.75\,\mathrm m$. Find its area in square metres and in square centimetres.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A&=\frac85\cdot\frac34=\frac65\,\mathrm{m}^2,\\
    A&=160\cdot75=12000\,\mathrm{cm}^2.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For an area in square metres, first convert the longer side: $160\,\mathrm{cm}=1.6\,\mathrm m$. For square centimetres, instead convert the shorter side to $75\,\mathrm{cm}$. Both calculations measure the same sheet with different unit squares. The numerical value in square centimetres is $100^2$ times the value in square metres, because a one-metre square contains one hundred rows and one hundred columns of one-centimetre squares. Comparing the results checks the conversions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two rectangles measure $7\times5$ and $8\times4$. Compare their areas and perimeters. What area would a square with that same perimeter have?

$$
\begin{aligned}
    2(7+5)&=2(8+4)=24,\\
    7\cdot5&=35,\qquad 8\cdot4=32,\\
    \left(\frac{24}{4}\right)^2&=36.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The consecutive sides add to $12$ in both shapes, so the boundary lengths match. Their products differ, giving different areas. A square with perimeter $24$ has four sides of length $6$ and a still larger area. View the three products as $(6+1)(6-1)$, $(6+2)(6-2)$, and $6^2$. Moving the sides farther from half their sum lowers their product by a square, even though the total side length does not change.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangle has area $54\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ and its longer side exceeds its shorter side by $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the side lengths and perimeter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    x(x+3)&=54,\qquad (x-6)(x+9)=0,\\
    x&=6,\qquad x+3=9,\qquad P=2(6+9)=30\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If the shorter side is $x$, the longer one is $x+3$. The area formula gives a quadratic equation. To factor it, find two numbers with product $-54$ and sum $3$. The numbers $-6$ and $9$ give factors $x-6$ and $x+9$. Setting the factors equal to zero gives roots $6$ and $-9$. Discard $-9$ because the lengths must be positive. The pair $6,9$ checks the product $54$ and the difference $3$. Each length occurs twice along the boundary. The perimeter is in centimetres because it is a sum of lengths.

</div>
