## Exercise 1

A rhombus has diagonals of lengths $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=\frac{Dd}2=\frac{24\cdot18}2=216
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular and bisect each other. The four resulting right triangles have a total area equal to half the product of the diagonals. Apply this formula.

Each right triangle uses half of $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ and half of $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. Multiplying the area of one triangle by four gives the area of the entire rhombus.

The result is $216$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rhombus has diagonals of lengths $32\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $24\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=\frac{Dd}2=\frac{32\cdot24}2=384
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular and bisect each other. The four resulting right triangles have a total area equal to half the product of the diagonals. Apply this formula.

The product of the diagonals gives the area of a rectangle enclosing the rhombus. The rhombus occupies exactly half that rectangle.

The result is $384$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

The diagonals of a rhombus measure $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $16\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the perimeter and area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
l&=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
P&=4l=40\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
\mathcal A&=\frac{12\cdot16}{2}=96\,\mathrm{cm}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A side of the rhombus is the hypotenuse of a triangle whose legs are half the diagonals. Multiply that side length by four for the perimeter. Use the two full diagonals for the area.

</div>
