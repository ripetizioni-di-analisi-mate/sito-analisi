## Exercise 1

A trapezoid has bases $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=\frac{(B+b)h}2=\frac{(9+5)\cdot4}2=28
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases are the parallel sides. Add their lengths and multiply by the perpendicular distance between them. Dividing by two follows from splitting the trapezoid into two triangles.

A rectangle of height $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ with the same area has a base equal to the average of $9\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. This average is obtained by adding and dividing by two.

The result is $28$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 2

A trapezoid has bases $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area.

$$
A=\frac{(B+b)h}2=\frac{(10+6)\cdot4}2=32
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases are the parallel sides. Add their lengths and multiply by the perpendicular distance between them. Dividing by two follows from splitting the trapezoid into two triangles.

Drawing a diagonal gives one triangle with base $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and one with base $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Both have altitude $4\,\mathrm{cm}$, so that factor is common to their areas.

The result is $32$ cm².

</div>

## Exercise 3

A trapezoid of height $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ has area $54\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. The larger base is $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ longer than the smaller one. Find both bases.

$$
\begin{aligned}
B+b&=\frac{2\cdot54}{6}=18 \\[5pt]
2b+4&=18 \\[5pt]
b&=7\,\mathrm{cm} \\[5pt]
B&=11\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the area to find the sum of the bases first. If the shorter base is $b$, the longer one is $b+4$. The sum $2b+4$ must equal $18$, so $b=7$. The lengths $7$ and $11$ have the required sum and difference.

</div>
