## Exercise 1

A trapezoid has bases $12$ and $6$ and height $5$. Use one diagonal to find its area as a sum of two triangles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    A_1&=\frac{12\cdot5}{2}=30,\qquad A_2=\frac{6\cdot5}{2}=15,\\
    A&=30+15=45.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonal gives two parts with no overlap. One triangle uses the lower base and has its opposite vertex on the upper base line. The other reverses those roles. Both altitudes therefore equal the distance between the bases, which is $5$. The first triangle has twice the area of the second because the bases are in the ratio $2:1$. Factoring out the height gives $((12+6)\cdot5)/2$. The diagonal splits the region but is not an altitude in the calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A trapezoid has area $60\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, height $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, and shorter base $7\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the longer base.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    B+b&=\frac{2A}{h}=\frac{120}{6}=20\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    B&=20-7=13\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Twice the area equals the sum of the bases times the height. Dividing by $6$ first gives that sum, rather than the length of one base. Subtract the known shorter base. The length $13$ exceeds $7$, as the ordering requires. Another check uses the mean of the two bases, $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, which gives $60\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ when multiplied by the height. Dividing by the height turns the square units back into units of length.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A trapezoid is claimed to have area $36$, height $4$, and shorter base $10$. Can all three statements be true?

$$
\begin{aligned}
    B+b&=\frac{2\cdot36}{4}=18,\\
    B&=18-10=8<10.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula requires the bases to sum to $18$. If one is $10$, the other is $8$, making the stated base the longer one. This contradicts the description of it as shorter. Another check starts from the fact that if both bases were at least $10$, their mean would be at least $10$. Multiplying by height $4$ would give area at least $40$, already above the stated area. A positive computed length does not by itself verify every condition.

</div>
