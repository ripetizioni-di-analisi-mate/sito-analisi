## Exercise 1

Solve $x^{2} - 10 x + 24=0$ using the quadratic formula.

$$
\Delta=(-10)^2-4\cdot24=4
$$

$$
x=\frac{10\pm\sqrt{4}}2
$$

$$
x_1=4,\qquad x_2=6
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Identify the coefficients in standard form. The discriminant is positive, so there are two distinct real roots. The two signs in the formula give the two solutions.

The linear coefficient is $-10$, so the numerator in the formula starts with $10$. Since $\sqrt\Delta=2$, the two numerators are $8$ and $12$. Dividing each by $2$ gives the roots $4$ and $6$.

The result is $\{4;\,6\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2x^2+x-4=0$ using the quadratic formula.

$$
\Delta=1^2-4\cdot2\cdot(-4)=33
$$

$$
x_1=\frac{-1-\sqrt{33}}4,\qquad x_2=\frac{-1+\sqrt{33}}4.
$$

::grafico{f="2*x^2+x-4" xmin=-2.3 xmax=2.1 ymin=-5 ymax=4 punti="-1.686140662,0,pieno,A;1.186140662,0,pieno,B" titolo="The two roots are the x-coordinates of the intersections with the $x$-axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant term is $-4$, so the contribution $-4ac$ is positive. The discriminant is $33$, and each sign in front of $\sqrt{33}$ gives a distinct real root. The denominator of the formula is $2a=4$. To check the signs, note that $\sqrt{33}>1$, so the first root is negative and the second is positive. Viète's product $-\dfrac42=-2$ also confirms that they have opposite signs.

</div>
