## Exercise 1

A rectangle has area $28\,\mathrm{cm}^2$, and its base is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ longer than its height. What is its height?

$$
h(h+3)=28
$$

$$
(h-4)(h+7)=0
$$

$$
h=4\quad(h>0)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $h$ be the height and $h+3$ the base. Their product is the area and gives a quadratic equation. The negative root is excluded because a length is positive.

Factoring $(h-4)(h+7)=0$ gives the two roots $4$ and $-7$. For the positive root, the base is $7$ centimeters and the area is $4\cdot7=28$ square centimeters.

The result is $4$ cm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangle has area $45\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ and perimeter $28\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the side lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b+h&=14\\[6pt]
bh&=45.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
t^2-14t+45=(t-5)(t-9).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The semiperimeter is the sum of the lengths $b$ and $h$, and the area is their product. By Viète's formulas, the lengths are the roots of the monic trinomial $t^2-14t+45$. Expanding $(t-5)(t-9)$ recovers the trinomial, so the sides are $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. Both are positive, their product is $45$, and their sum gives the perimeter $2(5+9)=28\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
