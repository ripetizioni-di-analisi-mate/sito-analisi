## Exercise 1

A rectangle has area $28\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ and width $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ greater than its height. Find the sides and explain why one root is rejected.

::grafico{xmin=-1.13 xmax=7.82 ymin=-0.87 ymax=5 uguale=1 assi=0 poligoni="0,0 7,0 7,4 0,4" linee="0,0 7,0 7,4 0,4 0,0" segmenti="4,0,4,4" etichette="-0.59,1.91,4;1.9,-0.48,4;5.38,-0.48,3;3.36,4.43,7" titolo="Lengths in centimetres give the required area and difference"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         h(h+3)&=28\\[6pt]
         h^2+3h-28&=(h-4)(h+7)=0\\[6pt]
         h&=4,\qquad b=7.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If $h$ is the height in centimetres, the width is $h+3$. The area equation gives candidates $4$ and $-7$. Taking $-7$ would make the width $-4$, so the product would still be positive but neither length would be possible. With $h=4$, the width is $7$ and the area is $4\cdot7=28$. The diagram checks the difference: the full width consists of a part equal to the height and another three centimetres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has perimeter $20\,\mathrm{cm}$. Can it have area $21\,\mathrm{cm}^2$? What about $26\,\mathrm{cm}^2$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         b+h&=10,\\[6pt]
         t^2-10t+21&=(t-3)(t-7),\\[6pt]
         \Delta_{26}&=100-104=-4.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two adjacent sides have sum $10$. If their product is $21$, the polynomial with those values has roots $3$ and $7$, both positive. The rectangle exists and $2(3+7)=20$ checks its perimeter. If the required product is $26$, the negative discriminant rules out every real pair. The bound also follows from $h(10-h)=25-(h-5)^2$: the greatest area with that perimeter is $25$, attained with equal sides.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangle has area $45\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ and perimeter $28\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its side lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         b+h&=14,\qquad bh=45,\\[6pt]
         t^2-14t+45&=(t-5)(t-9),\\[6pt]
         \{b,h\}&=\{5,9\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The half-perimeter gives the sum of adjacent sides and the area gives their product. The two lengths must therefore be roots of the monic polynomial with linear coefficient $-14$ and constant $45$. The values $5$ and $9$ do have sum $14$ and product $45$. Both are positive. Here the two roots together represent the sides of the same rectangle. Swapping them changes the labels for width and height while keeping all required measurements.

</div>
