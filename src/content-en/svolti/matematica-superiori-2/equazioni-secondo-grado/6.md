## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x^2-10x+24}{x-4}=0$. Identify the allowed zero and the missing point of the graph.

::grafico{f="x-6" xmin=-0.4 xmax=8.1 ymin=-6.9 ymax=3 punti="4,-2,vuoto;6,0,pieno" segmenti="4,-2,4,0" titolo="The open point remains excluded after simplification"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{4\}\\[6pt]
         x^2-10x+24&=(x-4)(x-6)\\[6pt]
         \frac{(x-4)(x-6)}{x-4}&=x-6\quad(x\in D)\\[6pt]
         S&=\{6\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator excludes $4$ before any calculation. The numerator vanishes at both $4$ and $6$. Only the second value belongs to the domain. Cancelling the common factor gives the line $y=x-6$ at the allowed horizontal coordinates. There is a hole at $(4;-2)$, because the original expression would require division by zero. At $x=6$ the numerator is zero and the denominator is $2$, so the quotient really is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare the solution sets of $\dfrac{x^2-9}{(x-3)(x+3)}=0$ and the same equation with right-hand side $1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-3,3\},\\[6pt]
         \frac{x^2-9}{(x-3)(x+3)}&=1\quad(x\in D),\\[6pt]
         S_0&=\varnothing,\qquad S_1=D.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two denominator factors vanish at $3$ and $-3$. At every other point the numerator and denominator are exactly the same nonzero number, so the fraction equals $1$. The equation with right side zero has no solution, and the one with right side one is an identity in the domain. Looking only at the numerator would give two candidates, but both make the denominator zero. Changing the required quotient changes the solutions while the domain remains the same.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x+3}{x-1}=\dfrac8{x(x-1)}$ and check that both candidates are allowed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{0,1\}\\[6pt]
         x(x+3)&=8\\[6pt]
         x&=\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{41}}2.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplication by $x(x-1)$ is permitted after excluding $0$ and $1$. The resulting equation is $x^2+3x-8=0$, with discriminant $41$. To check the domain without rounding, test the two excluded values in the equation with denominators cleared. At $x=0$ the product $x(x+3)$ is $0$, and at $x=1$ it is $4$. Neither gives $8$, so both roots found are allowed. At these two values multiplying and dividing by the common denominator are inverse operations.

</div>
