## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-4x=0$. Which root is lost by dividing by $x$?

::grafico{f="x^2-4*x" xmin=-1.4 xmax=5.3 ymin=-5.2 ymax=6.4 punti="0,0,pieno;4,0,pieno;2,-4,pieno" titolo="The graph meets the axis at x = 0 and x = 4"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x^2-4x&=x(x-4)\\[6pt]
         x(x-4)=0&\iff x\in\{0,4\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $x$ is shared by both terms, so the polynomial is a product. Check when each factor vanishes: $x=0$ gives $0\cdot(-4)=0$, and $x=4$ gives $4\cdot0=0$. Dividing by $x$ is reversible only for $x\ne0$ and would leave the second case. The graph shows both zeroes. Between them the first factor is positive and the second is negative, so the graph lies below the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One root of $3x^2+bx=0$ is $-5$. Find $b$ and the other root.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         3x^2+bx&=x(3x+b)\\[6pt]
         3(-5)+b&=0\\[6pt]
         b&=15,\qquad S=\{-5,0\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given root is nonzero, so it must make the factor $3x+b$ vanish. Substituting $-5$ gives $-15+b=0$, so $b=15$. The polynomial is $3x(x+5)$ and the factor $x$ gives the root zero. We can also check the coefficient using the sum of the roots. It equals $-5$, and Viète’s relation requires $-\dfrac b3=-5$. Both methods use the fixed leading coefficient.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2(x-1)^2-6(x-1)=0$, keeping $x-1$ intact in the first step.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         2(x-1)^2-6(x-1)&=2(x-1)(x-4),\\[6pt]
         2(x-1)(x-4)=0&\iff x\in\{1,4\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $u=x-1$, the equation in $u$ has no constant term: factoring $2u$ out of $2u^2-6u$ gives $2u(u-3)=0$. The candidates are $u=0$ and $u=3$. Converting back to $x$ gives $1$ and $4$. At the first value both original terms vanish. At the second the expression $x-1$ equals $3$ and the two terms are $18$ and $-18$. Dividing both sides by $x-1$ would have lost the solution $x=1$.

</div>
