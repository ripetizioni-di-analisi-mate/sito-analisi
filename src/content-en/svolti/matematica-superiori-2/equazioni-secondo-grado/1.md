## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-16=0$ and explain how to read both solutions from the graph.

::grafico{f="x^2;16" legenda="y=x^2;y=16" xmin=-5.1 xmax=5.1 ymin=-1.7 ymax=21 punti="-4,16,pieno;4,16,pieno" segmenti="-4,0,-4,16;4,0,4,16" titolo="The horizontal coordinates are −4 and 4"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x^2&=16\\[6pt]
         |x|&=4\\[6pt]
         S&=\{-4,4\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation requires the square to equal $16$. Its positive square root is $4$, but the equation involves $x^2$, which gives the same value for $x$ and its opposite. Both coordinates $4$ units from the origin are possible. On the graph, both points belong to the parabola $y=x^2$ and the horizontal line $y=16$. Another check writes $(x-4)(x+4)=0$: one factor vanishes at each root.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify the real solutions of $2x^2+k=0$ as $k$ varies.

$$
x^2=-\frac{k}{2},\qquad
S=\begin{cases}
\left\{-\sqrt{-k/2},\sqrt{-k/2}\right\} & k<0,\\[6pt]
\{0\} & k=0,\\[6pt]
\varnothing & k>0.
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After dividing by $2$, compare $-\dfrac k2$ with zero. If $k<0$, the number is positive and gives opposite roots. For instance, $k=-8$ gives $x^2=4$ and hence $x=\pm2$. If $k=0$, the equation is $2x^2=0$: the factor $x$ is squared, so zero is a double root. If $k>0$, the right side is negative. In that case $2x^2+k$ is always positive and cannot be zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $3x^2-18=0$ without rounding. Between which integers does each root lie?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         3x^2-18=0&\iff x^2=6\\[6pt]
         x&=\pm\sqrt6,\\[6pt]
         -3<-\sqrt6&<-2,\qquad 2<\sqrt6<3.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the whole equation by $3$ gives $x^2-6=0$. The two exact solutions are opposites. To locate the positive one, compare $6$ with $2^2=4$ and $3^2=9$. The positive root is between $2$ and $3$, and reflecting it about zero gives the interval between $-3$ and $-2$. Both signs give the same square, $6$, so $3(\pm\sqrt6)^2-18=0$. Using $2.45$ would only give an approximation.

</div>
