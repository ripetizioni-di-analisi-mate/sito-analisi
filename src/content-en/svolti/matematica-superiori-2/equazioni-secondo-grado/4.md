## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-8x+16=0$ and explain why the root has multiplicity two.

::grafico{f="(x-4)^2" xmin=-0.3 xmax=7.6 ymin=-1.3 ymax=9.2 punti="4,0,pieno;3,1,pieno;5,1,pieno" titolo="The graph touches the axis at x = 4"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x^2-8x+16&=(x-4)^2\\[6pt]
         (x-4)^2=0&\iff x=4.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three terms make the square of $x-4$. The square vanishes exactly when its base does. There is one value, $4$, but the factor $x-4$ occurs twice. This is why the solution set is $\{4\}$ and the root is double. The vertex lies on the horizontal axis. On either side the base is nonzero and the square is positive, so the graph touches the axis and stays on the same side.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For what $k$ does the parabola $y=-3x^2+6x+k$ touch the horizontal axis? Give the point of contact.

::grafico{f="-3*(x-1)^2" xmin=-1.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-9.3 ymax=1.1 punti="1,0,pieno;0,-3,pieno;2,-3,pieno" titolo="For k = −3 the maximum touches the axis at (1, 0)"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         -3x^2+6x+k&=-3(x-1)^2+3+k\\[6pt]
         3+k=0&\iff k=-3,\quad V=(1,0).
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola opens downwards. Its maximum is the value at $x=1$, namely $3+k$. To touch the axis without crossing it, the maximum must be zero, so $k=-3$. With that parameter the expression is a square times $-3$, which vanishes only at $1$. Checking with the formula gives $\Delta=36+12k$, zero for the same parameter. For smaller parameters, even the highest point lies below the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $4x^2+12x+9=0$ and check the factorisation using the formula as well.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         4x^2+12x+9&=(2x+3)^2,\\[6pt]
         \Delta&=12^2-4\cdot4\cdot9=0,\\[6pt]
         x&=\frac{-12}{2\cdot4}=-\frac32.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The squares at the ends have algebraic bases $2x$ and $3$. Twice their product is $12x$, just the middle term. Factoring gives $2x+3=0$, hence $-\dfrac32$. In the formula we must divide by $2a=8$, which gives the same root. To see the monic factor, rewrite the polynomial as $4\left(x+\dfrac32\right)^2$. The coefficient outside the square is retained.

</div>
