## Exercise 1

For which value of $k$ does the equation $x^2-8x+k=0$ have a double root?

$$
\Delta=64-4k
$$

$$
\Delta=0\iff k=16
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A double root requires a zero discriminant. Write the discriminant in terms of the parameter and solve the resulting equation. The leading coefficient is one, so the equation remains quadratic.

For $k=16$, the polynomial is $(x-4)^2$, so the only distinct root is $4$. A higher value of $k$ raises the minimum above zero, and a lower value brings it below zero. Thus only $k=16$ makes the vertex touch the axis.

The result is $16$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For which real values of $k$ does $(k-1)x^2+4x+1=0$ have exactly one real solution?

$$
k=1 \implies 4x+1=0 \implies x=-\frac14
$$

$$
k\ne1 \implies \Delta=16-4(k-1)=20-4k.
$$

$$
\Delta=0\iff k=5.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $k=1$, the quadratic term vanishes. The remaining linear equation has one solution. In the other cases, the quadratic formula gives one real value when the discriminant is zero. This happens at $k=5$, where the equation is still quadratic. The requested values are $1$ and $5$.

</div>
