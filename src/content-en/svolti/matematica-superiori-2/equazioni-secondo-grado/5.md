## Exercise 1

How many real roots does $x^2+2x+5=0$ have?

$$
\Delta=2^2-4\cdot5=-16<0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discriminant is negative. The square root of a negative number is not real, so the formula gives no real roots. The equation has no solution in the real numbers.

The result can be checked by writing $x^2+2x+5=(x+1)^2+4$. The square is nonnegative, and the added number is positive. Even at $x=-1$, where the square is zero, the polynomial is $4$.

The result is $0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

How many real roots does $2x^2-8x+11=0$ have? Complete the square.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^2-8x+11
&=2(x^2-4x+4)+3\\[6pt]
&=2(x-2)^2+3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of $x-2$ is nonnegative for every $x$. Twice the square plus $3$ is therefore at least $3$, even at $x=2$ where the square is zero. The left side is always positive, so the equation has no real roots.

</div>
