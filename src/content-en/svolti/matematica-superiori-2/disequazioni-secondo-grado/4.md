## Exercise 1

Solve $(x-3)^2>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-3)^2=0&\iff x=3,\\
x&\in\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A real square is at least zero. Here it has to be strictly greater than zero, so find the one value to exclude. The base $x-3$ is zero at $x=3$. The base is negative below $3$ and positive above $3$, but its square is positive in both cases. The number $3$ splits the solution into two half-lines. With $\ge$, that point would also be allowed and the solution would be $\mathbb R$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $(2x+1)^2<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x+1)^2&\ge0\quad\forall x\in\mathbb R,\\
S&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of a real number is always nonnegative, even when the base is negative. To check equality, solve $2x+1=0$, giving $x=-\dfrac12$. This point gives zero, which the sign $<$ excludes. All other values give strictly positive squares and are also excluded. With $\le$ in place of $<$, the value $-\dfrac12$ would instead be an isolated solution. For example, at $x=0$ the value is $1$, strictly above the minimum of zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $9-(x+1)^2>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+1)^2&<9,\\
-3<x+1&<3,\\
x&\in(-4,2).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="9-(x+1)^2" xmin=-5.1 xmax=3.1 ymin=-4 ymax=10.4 punti="-4,0,pieno;2,0,pieno;-1,9,pieno" segmenti="-1,0,-1,9" titolo="The positive part of the parabola lies between the horizontal coordinates $-4$ and $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolating the square gives $(x+1)^2<9$. The base can be positive or negative as long as its distance from zero is below $3$. Thus both bounds $-3<x+1<3$ are required. Subtracting $1$ gives $-4<x<2$. At $-4$ and $2$ the square equals $9$, so both endpoints are excluded. The vertex at $x=-1$ gives the maximum $9$ of the original expression and lies inside the solution. The interval is symmetric about $-1$, with both endpoints three units away.

</div>
