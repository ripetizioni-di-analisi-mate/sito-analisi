## Exercise 1

Solve $(x-3)^2>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-3)^2=0&\iff x=3,\\
x&\in\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of $x-3$ is always nonnegative. It is zero exactly when its base is zero, at $x=3$. To the left and right of $3$, the base has opposite signs and the square remains positive. The strict comparison excludes only $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $(2x+1)^2<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x+1)^2&\ge0\quad\forall x\in\mathbb R,\\
S&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For any real value of $x$, the expression $2x+1$ is real and its square is nonnegative. The value $x=-\dfrac12$ also fails the inequality because it gives zero and the comparison is strict. The solution set is empty.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $9-(x+1)^2>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+1)^2&<9,\\
-3<x+1&<3,\\
x&\in(-4,2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving the square to the right-hand side gives a comparison with $9$. Since $9=3^2$, the distance of $x+1$ from zero must be less than $3$. The endpoints are the two values that make the square equal to $9$ and are excluded. For example, $x=-1$ makes the square zero and satisfies the inequality.

</div>
