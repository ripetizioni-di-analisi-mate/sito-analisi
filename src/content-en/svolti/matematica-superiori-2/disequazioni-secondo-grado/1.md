## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-8x+15\le0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-8x+15&=(x-3)(x-5),\\
(x-3)(x-5)\le0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in[3,5].
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-8*x+15" area="0;x^2-8*x+15;3;5" punti="3,0,pieno,A;5,0,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=8 ymin=-2 ymax=9 titolo="The arc at nonpositive height has x-coordinates from 3 to 5"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive and the roots are $3$ and $5$. Between these values, $x-3$ is positive and $x-5$ is negative, so the product is negative. Outside, the factors have the same sign. Both roots belong to the solution set because the inequality allows equality.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $x^2\le 3x+10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-3x-10&\le0,\\
(x+2)(x-5)&\le0,\\
x&\in[-2,5].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $3x+10$ from both sides preserves the comparison and leaves a single trinomial to study. The numbers $2$ and $-5$ have sum $-3$ and product $-10$, giving the factorization. For example, $x=0$ is between the roots and satisfies the original inequality, while $x=6$ would give $36\le28$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2x^2-x-3\le0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^2-x-3&=(x+1)(2x-3),\\
x+1=0&\iff x=-1,\\
2x-3=0&\iff x=\frac32,\\
x&\in\left[-1,\frac32\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $2x-3$ is zero at $\dfrac32$. For $x$ between $-1$ and $\dfrac32$, the factor $x+1$ is positive and $2x-3$ is negative. Their product is negative and vanishes at the endpoints. The coefficient $2$ changes the root of the second factor while the sign rule stays the same.

</div>
