## Exercise 1

Solve $-x^2+8x-15\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^2+8x-15\ge0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2-8x+15\le0,\\
x^2-8x+15&=(x-3)(x-5),\\
x&\in[3,5].
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-x^2+8*x-15" area="-x^2+8*x-15;0;3;5" punti="3,0,pieno,A;5,0,pieno,B" xmin=1 xmax=7 ymin=-7 ymax=2 titolo="The parabola lies above the axis between the roots"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying both sides by $-1$ reverses the inequality. We now seek where the trinomial with positive leading coefficient is at most zero. This gives the interval from $3$ to $5$, including both endpoints. On the graph of the original trinomial, it corresponds to the arc above the axis between the roots.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-2x^2+10x-12<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x^2+10x-12&=-2(x-2)(x-3),\\
-2(x-2)(x-3)<0&\iff (x-2)(x-3)>0,\\
x&\in(-\infty,2)\cup(3,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant factor $-2$ reverses the sign of the product of the two linear factors. To make the result negative, $(x-2)(x-3)$ must be positive. The two factors have the same sign on the outer intervals. The strict inequality excludes $2$ and $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $4-2x^2\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4-2x^2&=2(2-x^2),\\
x^2&\le2,\\
x&\in[-\sqrt2,\sqrt2].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing by $2$ preserves the direction. The condition $x^2\le2$ requires the absolute value of $x$ to be at most $\sqrt2$. This is why both the positive and negative roots appear. At $x=\pm\sqrt2$, the term $2x^2$ equals $4$, giving equality.

</div>
