## Exercise 1

Solve $-x^2+8x-15\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^2+8x-15\ge0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2-8x+15\le0,\\
x^2-8x+15&=(x-3)(x-5),\\
x&\in[3,5].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying by $-1$ changes the sign of each term and reverses the comparison. We are therefore looking for where $x^2-8x+15$ is at most zero. The quadratic is positive outside its roots $3$ and $5$ and negative between them. The non-strict comparison includes both boundaries. In the original expression, $x=4$ gives $-16+32-15=1$, while $x=2$ gives $-3$. The first value meets the comparison and the second is excluded. The form $1-(x-4)^2$ gives the same interval, with distance from $4$ at most one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-2x^2+10x-12<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x^2+10x-12&=-2(x-2)(x-3),\\
-2(x-2)(x-3)<0&\iff (x-2)(x-3)>0,\\
x&\in(-\infty,2)\cup(3,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After factorizing, divide by the negative coefficient $-2$. The inequality reverses, so the product of the two factors must be positive. The roots are $2$ and $3$. Outside that interval both factors have the same sign. In the original graph those parts are below the axis, because the coefficient $-2$ reverses the sign of the product. The two crossings are excluded. At the midpoint $x=\dfrac52$, the value is $\dfrac12$, so the middle stretch fails the condition. Substituting zero gives $-12<0$, as expected for a point on the left branch.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $4-2x^2\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4-2x^2&=2(2-x^2),\\
x^2&\le2,\\
x&\in[-\sqrt2,\sqrt2].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is equivalent to $x^2\le2$. The values whose square is $2$ are $-\sqrt2$ and $\sqrt2$. Between them the distance from zero is at most $\sqrt2$, so the square is at most $2$. Another check uses the form $2(\sqrt2-x)(\sqrt2+x)$. Outside the roots one factor is negative and the other positive, so the original expression is negative. At the endpoints it is zero, allowed by $\ge$. The interval is symmetric because changing $x$ to $-x$ leaves the quadratic unchanged.

</div>
