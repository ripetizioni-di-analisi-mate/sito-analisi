## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x-3}{x-5}\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&\ne5,\\
\frac{x-3}{x-5}&>0\quad(x<3\;\lor\;x>5),\\
\frac{x-3}{x-5}&=0\quad(x=3),\\
S&=(-\infty,3]\cup(5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-3)/(x-5)" xmin=0 xmax=9.4 ymin=-6.2 ymax=7 asintoti="x=5;y=1" punti="3,0,pieno;4,-1,pieno;6,3,pieno" titolo="The graph crosses the axis at $3$ and is undefined at $5$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Exclude $5$ from the denominator before doing any calculations. At $3$ the numerator vanishes and the denominator is $-2$, so the quotient is zero. Below $3$ both signs are negative, between $3$ and $5$ they differ, and above $5$ both are positive. The quotient is therefore positive on the outer intervals. The non-strict comparison includes $3$, while $5$ stays excluded regardless of the comparison. As a check, at $x=4$ the quotient is $-1$. Multiplying by $(x-5)^2$ recovers the sign of the two-factor product on the allowed points.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{(x-1)(x+2)}{x-1}\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{1\},\\[6pt]
\frac{(x-1)(x+2)}{x-1}&=x+2 \qquad(x\in D),\\[6pt]
x+2&\ge0\iff x\ge-2,\\[6pt]
S&=[-2,1)\cup(1,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x+2" xmin=-3.4 xmax=3.45 ymin=-1.65 ymax=5.85 punti="-2,0,pieno;1,3,vuoto" segmenti="1,0,1,3" titolo="Simplification gives a line with an excluded point at $(1;3)$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The quotient exists only for $x\ne1$. Under that condition the factor $x-1$ can cancel, leaving $x+2$. The line reaches zero at $-2$, allowed by $\ge$, and is positive to its right. At $1$ the line would have value $3$, but the original fraction would have a zero denominator. The solution therefore has two pieces. At $0$ and $2$ the fraction is defined and gives $2$ and $4$, confirming both pieces. Near the excluded coordinate, the values follow the line and approach $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{(x-2)^2}\le0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&\ne2,\\
(x-2)^2&>0\quad(x\ne2),\\
x+1&\le0,\\
x&\in(-\infty,-1].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is a square, so it is positive everywhere except at $2$. On that domain, multiplying by $(x-2)^2$ preserves the comparison. This leaves $x+1\le0$, or $x\le-1$. The point $2$ is already outside the result. Include $-1$ because the numerator is zero and the denominator is $9$. If the sign were $\ge$, the bound would be $x\ge-1$, and $2$ would still have to be removed after solving. An example in the allowed interval is $x=-2$, giving a negative value with denominator $16$.

</div>
