## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x-3}{x-5}\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&\ne5,\\
\frac{x-3}{x-5}&>0\quad(x<3\;\lor\;x>5),\\
\frac{x-3}{x-5}&=0\quad(x=3),\\
S&=(-\infty,3]\cup(5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before studying the sign, exclude $5$, which makes the denominator zero. Below $3$ both factors are negative and the quotient is positive. Between $3$ and $5$ the quotient is negative, and above $5$ it is positive. Include $3$ because it makes the numerator zero while the denominator remains nonzero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{(x-1)(x+2)}{x-1}\le0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&\ne1,\\
\frac{(x-1)(x+2)}{x-1}&=x+2\quad (x\ne1),\\
x+2&\le0,\\
x&\in(-\infty,-2].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction may be simplified after excluding the zero of the denominator. The comparison then reduces to $x+2\le0$. The excluded value $1$ lies outside $(-\infty,-2]$, so the original restriction does not split the interval here. At the endpoint $-2$, the original fraction is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{(x-2)^2}\le0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&\ne2,\\
(x-2)^2&>0\quad(x\ne2),\\
x+1&\le0,\\
x&\in(-\infty,-1].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is the square of a nonzero quantity, so it is positive throughout the domain. The quotient has the sign of the numerator on both sides of $2$. Thus we seek where $x+1$ is nonpositive. The point $2$ is excluded from the domain and here lies outside the resulting interval.

</div>
