## Exercise 1

Solve $-x^2-3<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2&\ge0,\\
-x^2-3&\le-3<0,\\
S&=\mathbb R.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square is nonnegative for every $x$, so its opposite is at most zero. Subtracting $3$ makes the maximum value $-3$, attained at $x=0$. The inequality holds for every real number because even the maximum is negative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-x^2+4x-5\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^2+4x-5&=-(x-2)^2-1,\\
-(x-2)^2-1&\le-1<0,\\
S&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Completing the square writes the expression as the opposite of a square plus $-1$. Its greatest possible value is $-1$ at $x=2$. The inequality requires a nonnegative value, so no real number satisfies it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $-2x^2+2x-1<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&=2^2-4(-2)(-1)=-4,\\
a&=-2<0,\\
S&=\mathbb R.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A negative discriminant means the trinomial has no real roots. Its continuous graph therefore has a constant sign. At $x=0$, the value is $-1$, so it is negative throughout the real line. Equivalently, when $\Delta<0$, the trinomial has the sign of its leading coefficient $-2$ everywhere.

</div>
