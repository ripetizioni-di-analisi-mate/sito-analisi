## Exercise 1

Solve $-x^2-3<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2&\ge0,\\
-x^2-3&\le-3<0,\\
S&=\mathbb R.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $x^2\ge0$, the term $-x^2$ is at most zero. Subtracting $3$ gives a value at most $-3$, attained at $x=0$. The comparison asks for a strictly negative value and even the maximum is negative. Every real number is therefore a solution. The discriminant $\Delta=0^2-4(-1)(-3)=-12$ gives a separate check. It is negative and the leading coefficient is negative, so the quadratic has no roots and stays below the axis. The maximum occurs at the vertex, and the parabola opens downwards.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-x^2+4x-5\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^2+4x-5&=-(x-2)^2-1,\\
-(x-2)^2-1&\le-1<0,\\
S&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring a minus sign from the quadratic and linear terms reveals the square $(x-2)^2$. The constant term left over is $-1$. The maximum is therefore $-1$, attained at $2$. The parabola stays below the horizontal axis at every point, so the solution set is empty. If the bound were $-1$ instead of zero, the only permitted value would be $2$, which gives the maximum. Comparing the bound with the vertex thus identifies both impossibility and equality. The discriminant is $4^2-4(-1)(-5)=-4$, in agreement with the lack of horizontal intercepts.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $-2x^2+2x-1<0$. For which values of $k$ does $-2x^2+2x+k<0$ hold for every real number?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&=2^2-4(-2)(-1)=-4,\\[6pt]
a&=-2<0,\qquad S=\mathbb R,\\[6pt]
-2x^2+2x+k
&=-2\left(x-\frac12\right)^2+k+\frac12,\\[6pt]
k+\frac12<0&\iff k<-\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-2*x^2+2*x-1" xmin=-1.17 xmax=2.17 ymin=-6 ymax=0.88 punti="0.5,-0.5,pieno" segmenti="0.5,0,0.5,-0.5" titolo="For $k=-1$ the vertex is $(\dfrac12;-\dfrac12)$ and the parabola is always negative."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The discriminant of the quadratic with constant term $-1$ is negative. Since the leading coefficient is negative, the quadratic is strictly negative for every real number. For the whole family, completing the square gives a maximum of $k+\dfrac12$ at $x=\dfrac12$. The inequality holds for all real numbers exactly when the maximum is strictly negative. At $k=-\dfrac12$ the maximum is zero and the vertex would be excluded. For $k>-\dfrac12$ the vertex has a positive value. For the family, the discriminant $4+8k$ is negative exactly when $k<-\dfrac12$.

</div>
