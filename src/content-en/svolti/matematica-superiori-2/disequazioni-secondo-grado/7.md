## Exercise 1

Find the domain of $\sqrt{x^2-8x+15}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-8x+15&\ge0,\\
(x-3)(x-5)&\ge0,\\
D&=(-\infty,3]\cup[5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A square root on its own or in a numerator requires a nonnegative radicand. The quadratic factors with zeros at $3$ and $5$. Below $3$ both factors are negative, and above $5$ both are positive. The product is therefore positive on the outer intervals. At $3$ and $5$ the square root is defined and equals zero. At the middle point $4$ the radicand is $-1$, so there is no real square root. At $x=2$, a radicand of $3$ confirms that the left branch belongs to the domain.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the domain of $\dfrac1{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4-x^2&>0,\\
x^2&<4,\\
D&=(-2,2).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="1/sqrt(4-x^2)" xmin=-3.15 xmax=3.15 ymin=-0.28 ymax=2.65 asintoti="x=-2;x=2" punti="0,0.5,pieno" titolo="The graph exists between the vertical lines at $-2$ and $2$, both excluded."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The root is in the denominator, so the radicand must be positive. Solving $4-x^2>0$ gives $x^2<4$, or $-2<x<2$. At the endpoints the root would be defined and equal to zero, so division would be impossible. For example, at $x=0$ the denominator is $2$ and the fraction is $\dfrac12$. On the graph the two boundary values mark where the function is undefined and where it grows without bound when approached from inside. The domain is symmetric because substituting $-x$ for $x$ leaves the radicand unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the domain of $\dfrac{\sqrt{(x-1)(4-x)}}{x-2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)(4-x)\ge0 &\iff x\in[1,4],\\
x&\ne2,\\
D&=[1,2)\cup(2,4].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is defined whenever $(x-1)(4-x)\ge0$. Both factors are nonnegative together from $1$ to $4$, and one is negative at every outer point. The denominator then requires $x\ne2$. Removing it splits the range into two intervals. At $1$ the numerator is zero and the denominator is $-1$, so the value is allowed. At $4$ the denominator is $2$ and that endpoint is also allowed. The point $2$ is the only gap inside. As a check on the right branch, at $3$ the expression is $\sqrt2$ with denominator one.

</div>
