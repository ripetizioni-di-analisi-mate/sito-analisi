## Exercise 1

Find the domain of $\sqrt{x^2-8x+15}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-8x+15&\ge0,\\
(x-3)(x-5)&\ge0,\\
D&=(-\infty,3]\cup[5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A real square root requires a nonnegative radicand. The product of the factors is positive below $3$ and above $5$. At the endpoints, the radicand is zero and the square root is defined. Between the roots, the product is negative and the square root has no real value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the domain of $\dfrac1{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4-x^2&>0,\\
x^2&<4,\\
D&=(-2,2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand must be nonnegative and the denominator must be nonzero. Together the conditions require $4-x^2$ to be strictly positive. At $x=\pm2$ the square root exists and equals zero, so the fraction is undefined at both points.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the domain of $\dfrac{\sqrt{(x-1)(4-x)}}{x-2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)(4-x)\ge0 &\iff x\in[1,4],\\
x&\ne2,\\
D&=[1,2)\cup(2,4].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator requires $(x-1)(4-x)$ to be nonnegative. Both factors are nonnegative between $1$ and $4$, while one is negative outside. Then exclude $2$ because it makes the denominator zero. At the endpoints $1$ and $4$, the fraction is defined and equals zero.

</div>
