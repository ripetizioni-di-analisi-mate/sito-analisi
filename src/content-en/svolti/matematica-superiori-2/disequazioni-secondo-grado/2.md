## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-8x+15>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-8x+15&=(x-3)(x-5),\\
x&\in(-\infty,3)\cup(5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $x<3$, both factors are negative. For $x>5$, both are positive. The product is therefore positive on the two outer intervals. Between $3$ and $5$, the second factor is negative and the first is positive. The roots are excluded because the product must be strictly positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $x^2+4>4x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-4x+4&>0,\\
(x-2)^2&>0,\\
x&\in\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2)^2" punti="2,0,vuoto" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=9 titolo="The strict inequality excludes x = 2"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $4x$ gives a perfect square. The square is positive for every nonzero base. Its base is $x-2$, so the only excluded value is $2$. In the graph, the parabola touches the axis there and has positive height on either side.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $x^2+2x+3>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+2x+3&=(x+1)^2+2,\\
(x+1)^2+2&\ge2>0,\\
x&\in\mathbb R.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Completing the square gives the sum of a square and $2$. The minimum value of the square is zero, attained when $x=-1$. The trinomial then equals $2$ and remains positive for every other $x$. Thus the strict inequality holds for every real number.

</div>
