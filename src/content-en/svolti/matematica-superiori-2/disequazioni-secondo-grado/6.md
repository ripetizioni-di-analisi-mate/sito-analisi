## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}x^2-8x+15\le0\\x>4\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-8x+15\le0 &\iff x\in[3,5],\\
x>4 &\iff x\in(4,+\infty),\\
S&=[3,5]\cap(4,+\infty)=(4,5].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first condition allows points from $3$ to $5$. The second allows points strictly greater than $4$. Keep only the values satisfying both conditions. The point $4$ satisfies the first condition and is excluded by the second. The point $5$ belongs to both sets.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\begin{cases}x^2-4>0\\-3\le x\le2\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-4>0 &\iff x\in(-\infty,-2)\cup(2,+\infty),\\
S&=\bigl(( -\infty,-2)\cup(2,+\infty)\bigr)\cap[-3,2]\\
&=[-3,-2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of $x$ must exceed $4$, so consider the two regions outside $[-2,2]$. The restriction $x\le2$ removes the entire right-hand region. On the left, $x\ge-3$ keeps just the values from $-3$ up to but excluding $-2$. The point $2$ is excluded because it makes $x^2-4$ zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}(x-1)^2\le0\\x>1\end{cases}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2\le0&\iff x=1,\\
S&=\{1\}\cap(1,+\infty)=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A square is nonnegative, so the first condition requires the square to be zero. The only candidate is $x=1$. The second condition requires a value strictly greater than $1$. The intersection is empty even though each inequality separately has solutions.

</div>
