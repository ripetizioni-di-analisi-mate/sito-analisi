## Exercise 1

A cart starts at $x_0=2\,\mathrm{m}$ with constant velocity $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Determine its position after $4\,\mathrm{s}$ and the time needed to reach $x=20\,\mathrm{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(4)&=x_0+v\cdot4=2+3\cdot4=14\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
20&=2+3t,\\[6pt]
t&=\frac{20-2}{3}=6\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2+3*x" xmin=0 xmax=6.5 ymin=0 ymax=23 nomi="t,x" punti="4,14,pieno;6,20,pieno" legenda="x=2+3t" titolo="Time in seconds and position in meters: intercept is 2 m and slope is 3 m/s"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The cart covers equal distances in equal time intervals, so the **uniform-motion** equation applies. The term $x_0$ is the starting position relative to the chosen origin; displacement after four seconds is $12\,\mathrm{m}$, whereas position is $14\,\mathrm{m}$. To find arrival time, set the required position and solve for $t$. The graph checks both results: points $(4,14)$ and $(6,20)$ lie on the same straight line and its slope remains $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vehicle travels at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with constant acceleration $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Determine stopping time and distance, taking the initial direction of motion as positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=18-3t,\\[6pt]
t&=6\,\mathrm{s},\\[6pt]
s&=18\cdot6-\frac12\cdot3\cdot6^2\\[6pt]
 &=108-54=54\,\mathrm{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="18-3*x" xmin=0 xmax=6.5 ymin=-2 ymax=20 nomi="t,v" area="18-3*x;0;0;6" legenda="v=18-3t" titolo="Time in seconds and velocity in m/s: the triangle area is 54 m"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Stopping occurs when velocity reaches zero. The equation $v=v_0+at$ therefore first gives time; that value is then substituted into the displacement equation. The acceleration term is negative because braking reduces initial velocity. During the six seconds, the vehicle keeps moving in the positive direction, so distance traveled equals displacement. An independent check uses the area under the velocity-time graph: the triangle has base $6\,\mathrm{s}$ and height $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$, hence area $54\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A robot moves along an axis at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $3\,\mathrm{s}$, then at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $2\,\mathrm{s}$. Determine displacement, distance traveled, average velocity and average speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=4\cdot3-2\cdot2=8\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
s&=4\cdot3+2\cdot2=16\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
v_m&=\frac85=1.6\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[6pt]
\overline v_{\mathrm{rap}}&=\frac{16}{5}=3.2\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second segment has negative velocity, so it reduces the displacement gained in the first. Distance traveled instead counts both segments positively because it measures route length. Total time is $3+2=5\,\mathrm{s}$. Average velocity uses displacement and retains directional information; average speed uses distance. The arithmetic mean of the two velocities would give $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and would be incorrect because the two values are maintained for different durations.

</div>
