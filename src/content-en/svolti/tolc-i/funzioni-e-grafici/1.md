## Exercise 1

Find the domain and zeros of $f(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x-2}}{x-5}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x-2\ge0,\\
x-5\ne0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x\ge2,\\
x\ne5.
\end{cases}
$$
$$
D=[2,5)\cup(5,+\infty).
$$
$$
f(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sqrt{x-2}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **square root** requires a nonnegative radicand; the denominator instead requires excluding the value that makes it zero. The conditions must hold together, so we start from the half-line $x\ge2$ and remove only $5$. The point $2$ remains allowed because the denominator there is $-3$.

To find a zero, set the numerator equal to zero and check that the result belongs to the domain. The square root vanishes only at $x=2$, so this is the only zero. In particular, a zero of the denominator is not a zero of the function: at $5$, its value is undefined.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the domain, zeros and graph of $g(x)=\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}$.

$$
D=\mathbb R\setminus\{2\},\qquad
x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2).
$$
$$
g(x)=x+2\quad\text{for }x\ne2.
$$
$$
g(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=-2.
$$

::grafico{f="x+2" xmin=-4 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=7 punti="2,4,vuoto;-2,0" titolo="The line has an excluded point at (2, 4); its zero is -2."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator makes the original formula undefined at $x=2$, so that point is excluded before any simplification. The **difference of squares** then allows the numerator to be written as a product. Canceling the factor $x-2$, valid only for $x\ne2$, produces a line with a missing point.

The only zero is $-2$, which belongs to the domain and makes the simplified expression zero. At $x=2$, the complete line would have vertical coordinate $4$, but the given function assigns no value. The open point distinguishes the two functions even though their values agree at every other input.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the domain and zeros of $h(x)=\ln\left(\dfrac{x-4}{x+2}\right)$.

$$
\frac{x-4}{x+2}>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x<-2\ \text{or}\ x>4.
$$
$$
D=(-\infty,-2)\cup(4,+\infty).
$$
$$
h(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{x-4}{x+2}=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x-4=x+2.
$$
$$
-4=2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{no zeros}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The logarithm argument must be **strictly positive**. The points $-2$ and $4$ divide the line into three intervals. In the first, numerator and denominator are negative; in the second, they have opposite signs; in the third, they are positive. Thus the ratio is positive exactly on the two outer intervals, without endpoints.

A logarithm equals zero when its argument equals $1$, so finding zeros leads to the stated rational equation. Within the domain, the denominator is nonzero and multiplication is allowed. The resulting equality is impossible, so the function does not meet the horizontal axis. A nonempty domain does not guarantee the existence of zeros.

</div>
