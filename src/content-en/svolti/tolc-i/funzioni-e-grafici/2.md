## Exercise 1

The function $f$ is represented by the piecewise-linear graph joining $(0,1)$, $(2,5)$ and $(6,1)$. Find $f(4)$ and all solutions of $f(x)=3$.

::grafico{linee="0,1 2,5 6,1" punti="0,1;2,5;6,1" segmenti="0,3,6,3" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=6 titolo="The two segments meet the level y = 3."}

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}
2x+1,&0\le x\le2,\\
7-x,&2<x\le6.
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f(4)=7-4=3.
$$
$$
2x+1=3\Longrightarrow x=1,\qquad
7-x=3\Longrightarrow x=4.
$$
$$
\{x:f(x)=3\}=\{1,4\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each piece is linear, so its two endpoints determine its slope. On the first piece the slope is $\dfrac{5-1}{2-0}=2$ and the intercept is $1$. On the second it is $\dfrac{1-5}{6-2}=-1$, and passing through $(2,5)$ gives intercept $7$.

The input $4$ belongs to the second interval, so the formula $7-x$ applies. To find all solutions at level $3$, instead solve an equation on each piece. Both results belong to their respective intervals. The figure shows why reading only one intersection would miss a solution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

It is known that $f(4)=-2$ and that the domain of $f$ is $[-1,4]$. For $g(x)=f(x+3)-1$, find the corresponding point and the new domain.

$$
x+3=4\Longrightarrow x=1,\qquad
g(1)=f(4)-1=-3.
$$
$$
-1\le x+3\le4\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -4\le x\le1.
$$
$$
P'=(1,-3),\qquad D_g=[-4,1].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The known value of $f$ can be used when its inner input becomes $4$ again. This requires $x=1$. After evaluating $f(4)$, subtract one from the vertical coordinate to obtain $-3$. The point therefore undergoes two distinct transformations.

The same condition on the inner input determines the full domain. Each allowed $x$ must place $x+3$ inside the original interval. Subtracting $3$ from both endpoints gives $[-4,1]$. The vertical translation does not change the allowed inputs and adds no restrictions. The point found agrees with the right endpoint of the new domain.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the minimum, maximum and range of $h(x)=(x-2)^2+1$ for $x\in[3,5]$.

$$
3\le x\le5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad1\le x-2\le3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad1\le(x-2)^2\le9.
$$
$$
2\le h(x)\le10,\qquad h(3)=2,\qquad h(5)=10.
$$
$$
\min h=2,\qquad\max h=10,\qquad h([3,5])=[2,10].
$$

::grafico{curve="t,(t-2)^2+1,3,5" legenda="y=(x-2)^2+1" xmin=0 xmax=6 ymin=0 ymax=12 punti="3,2;5,10" segmenti="3,0,3,2;5,0,5,10" titolo="The branch on domain [3, 5] runs from (3, 2) to (5, 10); the vertex is excluded."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola's vertex is $(2,1)$, but the input $2$ is outside the stated domain. On $[3,5]$, the number $x-2$ is positive and increasing; squaring it preserves order. Therefore the smallest value occurs at $x=3$ and the largest at $x=5$.

The parabola takes all intermediate values without a break, so its range is the entire closed interval $[2,10]$. The minimum is the value $2$, whereas $3$ is the input where it is attained. The value $1$ would be the minimum if the domain included the vertex, but it is not attained on the assigned domain.

</div>
