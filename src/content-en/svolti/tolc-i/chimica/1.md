## Exercise 1: Neutrons in an isotope

A neutral atom has atomic number $8$ and mass number $18$. How many neutrons does it contain?

- $10$
- $8$
- $18$
- $26$
- $16$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $10$

The mass number includes protons and neutrons, whereas the atomic number counts only protons. Therefore $N=A-Z=18-8=10$. Neutrality also establishes that there are eight electrons, but these do not enter the mass number. Eighteen is the total number of nucleons, not just neutrons.

$$
N=A-Z=18-8=10,\qquad n_e=Z=8.
$$

The value identifying oxygen is eight, its proton number. Eighteen instead describes the total number of nucleons in this isotope. Reconstructing the nucleus requires subtracting the already counted protons from the total. Electrons can be determined separately using neutrality, but play a different role in the problem. The final check reconstructs mass number: eight protons plus ten neutrons give eighteen nucleons.

</div>

## Exercise 2: Counting an ion's particles

Which triple gives protons, neutrons and electrons in $^{35}_{17}\mathrm{Cl^-}$?

- $17,18,18$
- $17,18,16$
- $18,17,18$
- $35,17,18$
- $17,35,17$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $17,18,18$

Atomic number gives seventeen protons. The neutron number is $35-17=18$. Charge $-1$ means one extra electron compared with the neutral atom's seventeen, so there are eighteen electrons. Mass number and charge play different roles: the former describes the nucleus, the latter the proton–electron balance.

$$
n_p=17,\qquad n_n=35-17=18,\qquad n_e=17+1=18.
$$

The notation contains three independent pieces of information. The subscript identifies the element; the upper-left number counts nucleons; the upper-right superscript gives the charge of the whole species. A negative charge results from increasing electrons relative to protons without changing the nucleus. The result can be checked by charge balance: seventeen positive and eighteen negative charges give one negative elementary charge overall.

</div>

## Exercise 3: Average isotopic mass

An element has isotopes with approximate masses $20\,\mathrm u$ and $22\,\mathrm u$, with abundances $75\%$ and $25\%$. What is the approximate average mass?

- $20.5\,\mathrm u$
- $21\,\mathrm u$
- $21.5\,\mathrm u$
- $42\,\mathrm u$
- $22\,\mathrm u$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $20.5\,\mathrm u$

Abundances weight the contributions to the mean: $20\cdot0.75+22\cdot0.25=15+5.5=20.5\,\mathrm u$. The simple mean $21\,\mathrm u$ would assume equal abundances. The correct result lies between the masses and is closer to that of the isotope present at seventy-five percent.

$$
\begin{aligned}\overline m&=20\cdot0.75+22\cdot0.25\\[6pt]&=15+5.5=20.5\,\mathrm u.\end{aligned}
$$

The percentages state how frequently each mass occurs in the sample. Consider a representative group of four atoms: three have mass twenty and one mass twenty-two, for a total of eighty-two units. Dividing by four gives twenty point five again. This check also shows why averaging the two masses alone would give too much weight to the less abundant isotope.

</div>
