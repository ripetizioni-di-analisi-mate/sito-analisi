## Exercise 1: Formula of an ionic compound

What is the empirical formula of the neutral compound formed by $\mathrm{Al^{3+}}$ and $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$?

- $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$
- $\mathrm{Al_3O_2}$
- $\mathrm{AlO}$
- $\mathrm{AlO_3}$
- $\mathrm{Al_2O}$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$

Neutrality requires equal magnitudes of positive and negative charge. The smallest common total is six: two aluminum ions give $2\cdot3=6$ and three oxygen ions give $3(-2)=-6$. Thus the smallest ratio is $2:3$. Subscripts are not simply the charges written on the same element.

$$
2(+3)+3(-2)=0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\mathrm{Al_2O_3}.
$$

The aim is the smallest electrically neutral group of ions. One charge of plus three cannot be balanced by an integer number of charges of minus two. Two cations instead give plus six, exactly balanced by three anions. The ratio does not identify isolated molecules in the crystal, but the proportion of lattice ions. Multiplying both subscripts by the same number would preserve neutrality without giving the empirical formula.

</div>

## Exercise 2: Structural isomers

Ethanol $\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}$ and dimethyl ether $\mathrm{CH_3OCH_3}$ both have formula $\mathrm{C_2H_6O}$. Which relationship describes them?

- They are structural isomers
- They are isotopes
- They are the same rotated compound
- They are oppositely charged ions
- They differ only in neutron number

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** They are structural isomers

The formulas count the same atoms but connect them differently. In ethanol, oxygen is bonded to one carbon and one hydrogen; in dimethyl ether it is bonded to two carbons. Different connectivity defines structural isomerism and changes the functional group. Rotating the whole molecule instead preserves every connection.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}\qquad\mathrm{CH_3OCH_3}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\mathrm{C_2H_6O}.
$$

Counting atoms in both structures gives two carbons, six hydrogens and one oxygen. The next comparison concerns which atoms oxygen is bonded to: the alcohol has an O–H bond, while the ether has two O–C bonds. Rotation cannot remove this difference because rotation preserves connectivity. Recognizing the functional group explains why identical composition does not necessarily imply identical physical properties.

</div>

## Exercise 3: The reducing agent in a reaction

In $\mathrm{Zn+Cu^{2+}\to Zn^{2+}+Cu}$, which initial species acts as the reducing agent?

- $\mathrm{Zn}$
- $\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}$
- $\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}$
- $\mathrm{Cu}$
- Both products

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** $\mathrm{Zn}$

Zinc changes from oxidation number zero to $+2$, losing two electrons. Copper(II) gains those electrons and is reduced to metallic copper. The species that enables the other's reduction by donating electrons is the reducing agent: here it is the initial zinc. The reducing agent itself is oxidized.

$$
\begin{aligned}\mathrm{Zn}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}+2e^-,\\[6pt]\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}+2e^-&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cu}.\end{aligned}
$$

Separating the half-reactions identifies electron transfer without relying only on the agent's name. In the first, zinc loses electrons and is oxidized; in the second, copper(II) receives them and is reduced. The reducing agent is the species supplying electrons for the other's reduction. Adding eliminates the two electrons from both sides and restores the initial equation, with mass and charge conserved.

</div>
