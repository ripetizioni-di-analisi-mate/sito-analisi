## Exercise 1

Find the parabola with roots $-2$ and $4$ passing through $(0,-16)$. Find its vertex and intersections with the line $y=-16$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=a(x+2)(x-4),\\[6pt]
-16&=-8a\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=2,\\[6pt]
y&=2x^2-4x-16=2(x-1)^2-18.
\end{aligned}
$$

The vertex is $V=(1,-18)$. Also,
$$
2x^2-4x-16=-16
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2x(x-2)=0.
$$
The required points are $(0,-16)$ and $(2,-16)$.

::grafico{f="2*(x-1)^2-18;-16" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-20 ymax=4 punti="1,-18,pieno;0,-16,pieno;2,-16,pieno" titolo="The intersections with y = -16 are symmetric about x = 1"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The roots give the two linear factors, while the additional point determines coefficient $a$. The factored form therefore reconstructs the equation without introducing three unknowns. Completing the square then reveals the vertex: the term $2(x-1)^2$ is minimized at $x=1$, where it vanishes, leaving vertical coordinate $-18$. The horizontal line imposes a value of $y$, so intersections follow by solving an equation in $x$. The two results have mean $1$, equal to the horizontal coordinate of the symmetry axis. This check links the calculation to the figure and confirms the second point beyond the one already given.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$, find the foci and axis lengths. If a point on the ellipse is $3$ from one focus, find its distance from the other.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=5,\qquad b=3,\\[6pt]
c&=\sqrt{25-9}=4,\\[6pt]
F_1&=(-4,0),\qquad F_2=(4,0),\\[6pt]
2a&=10,\qquad 2b=6,\\[6pt]
PF_2&=10-3=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators in the equation are the squared semiaxes. The larger denominator belongs to $x^2$, so the major axis and foci are horizontal. Each focus’s distance from the center is found by subtracting the squared semiaxes; the distance between the two foci would instead be $2c=8$. The full axis lengths are twice the semiaxes, namely $10$ and $6$. Finally apply the ellipse’s focal property: at every point, the sum of distances to the two foci is $2a=10$. A given distance of $3$ therefore leaves $7$ to the other focus, without requiring the coordinates of $P$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the center, vertices, and asymptotes of the hyperbola $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{9}-\dfrac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=(2,-1),\qquad a=3,\qquad b=2,\\[6pt]
V_1&=(2-3,-1)=(-1,-1),\\[6pt]
V_2&=(2+3,-1)=(5,-1),\\[6pt]
y+1&=\pm\frac23(x-2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The translated coordinates $X=x-2$ and $Y=y+1$ put the equation in standard form centered at the origin. The positive term is $X^2$, so the branches open horizontally and the vertices are $a=3$ from the center. The asymptote slopes are $\pm\dfrac ba=\pm\dfrac23$; each line must also pass through $C$. Thus retain the translated form $y+1=\pm\dfrac23(x-2)$. Writing only $y=\pm\dfrac23x$ would produce lines parallel to the correct asymptotes but passing through a different point.

</div>
