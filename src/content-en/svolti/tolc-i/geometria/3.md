## Exercise 1

Find the line through $P=(2,-1)$ perpendicular to $y=-\dfrac12x+4$, and find where the two lines intersect.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=-\frac1{-\frac12}=2,\\[6pt]
y+1&=2(x-2),\\[6pt]
y&=2x-5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
2x-5=-\frac12x+4
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac52x=9
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H=\left(\frac{18}{5},\frac{11}{5}\right).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Determine the required line’s direction before its intercept. Perpendicularity requires the product of slopes to be $-1$, so slope $-\dfrac12$ corresponds to $2$. Then use the given point in the form $y-y_P=m(x-x_P)$. To find the intersection, equate the two expressions for $y$, giving a linear equation. Point $H$ lies on both lines and is the foot of the perpendicular from $P$. Substituting its horizontal coordinate gives $2\cdot\dfrac{18}{5}-5=\dfrac{11}{5}$ and also $-\dfrac12\cdot\dfrac{18}{5}+4=\dfrac{11}{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the center and radius of $x^2+y^2-4x+6y-12=0$ and its intersections with the line $x=5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-2)^2-4+(y+3)^2-9&=12,\\[6pt]
(x-2)^2+(y+3)^2&=25,\\[6pt]
C&=(2,-3),\qquad r=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
9+(y+3)^2=25
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y+3=\pm4.
$$

The intersection points are $A=(5,1)$ and $B=(5,-7)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

To express the equation as distance from a center, complete the squares in $x$ and $y$ separately. The terms $4$ and $9$ added inside the squares are balanced by subtracting them outside. The final right side is $25=r^2$, so the radius is $5$. The vertical line imposes horizontal coordinate $5$, which is $3$ away from the center’s horizontal coordinate; two vertical offsets of magnitude $4$ remain. Adding and subtracting that offset from vertical coordinate $-3$ gives $1$ and $-7$. The two points are symmetric about the chord midpoint $(5,-3)$ and are $8$ apart.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the locus of points $P=(x,y)$ equidistant from $A=(0,0)$ and $B=(4,2)$. Find where it meets the $x$-axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
PA^2&=PB^2,\\[6pt]
x^2+y^2&=(x-4)^2+(y-2)^2,\\[6pt]
8x+4y&=20,\\[6pt]
2x+y&=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting $y=0$ gives $H=\left(\dfrac52,0\right)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Distances are nonnegative, so equating them is equivalent to equating their squares. After expansion, the $x^2$ and $y^2$ terms cancel, leaving a line. Segment $AB$ has slope $\dfrac12$ and midpoint $(2,1)$. The resulting line has slope $-2$ and passes through $(2,1)$, so it is the segment’s perpendicular bisector. For point $H$, the distance from $A$ is $\dfrac52$; its distance from $B$ is $\sqrt{\left(\dfrac32\right)^2+2^2}=\dfrac52$. This comparison confirms the locus property at the requested point.

</div>
