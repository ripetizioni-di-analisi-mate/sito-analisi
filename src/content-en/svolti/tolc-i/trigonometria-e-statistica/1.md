## Exercise 1

Convert $225^\circ$ to radians and find the coordinates of the corresponding point on the unit circle and the value of tangent.

$$
225\cdot\frac\pi{180}=\frac{5\pi}{4}=\pi+\frac\pi4.
$$
$$
P=\left(\cos\frac{5\pi}{4},\sin\frac{5\pi}{4}\right)
=\left(-\frac{\sqrt2}{2},-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\right).
$$
$$
\tan\frac{5\pi}{4}=\frac{-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}}{-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}}=1.
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.2831853072" legenda=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 punti="-0.7071067812,-0.7071067812,pieno,P" segmenti="0,0,-0.7071067812,-0.7071067812;-0.7071067812,0,-0.7071067812,-0.7071067812;0,-0.7071067812,-0.7071067812,-0.7071067812" titolo="The angle of 225 degrees identifies a point in the third quadrant."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The conversion uses the ratio $\dfrac\pi{180}$ because a straight angle measures both $180^\circ$ and $\pi$ radians. The result exceeds $\pi$ by $\dfrac\pi4$: the terminal side is therefore in the **third quadrant**, with reference angle $45^\circ$.

Both coordinates are negative in that quadrant. The absolute values of sine and cosine are both $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$; the point lists cosine first and sine second. Tangent is defined because cosine is nonzero, and it is positive because it is the ratio of two equal negative numbers.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given $\sin\alpha=\dfrac35$ and $\dfrac\pi2<\alpha<\pi$, determine $\cos\alpha$, $\tan\alpha$ and $\sin(2\alpha)$.

$$
\cos^2\alpha=1-\frac9{25}=\frac{16}{25},\qquad
\cos\alpha=-\frac45.
$$
$$
\tan\alpha=\frac{\frac35}{-\frac45}=-\frac34.
$$
$$
\sin(2\alpha)=2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha
=2\cdot\frac35\cdot\left(-\frac45\right)=-\frac{24}{25}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fundamental identity initially determines the **square of cosine**, leaving two possible signs. The hypothesis places $\alpha$ in the second quadrant, where the horizontal coordinate is negative, so we choose $-\dfrac45$. Without this restriction, its sign could not be determined.

Tangent is obtained by dividing sine by cosine, which is allowed because the denominator is nonzero. The double-angle sine also requires the factor $2$. The negative result agrees with $\pi<2\alpha<2\pi$, where sine is negative. This sign check uses the double angle, not the quadrant of the original angle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\cos15^\circ$ exactly and verify that the result is positive and less than $1$.

$$
\cos15^\circ=\cos(45^\circ-30^\circ)
=\cos45^\circ\cos30^\circ+\sin45^\circ\sin30^\circ.
$$
$$
\cos15^\circ=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\frac{\sqrt3}{2}
+\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\frac12
=\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}.
$$
$$
\left(\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}\right)^2
=\frac{8+4\sqrt3}{16}=\frac{2+\sqrt3}{4}<1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle $15^\circ$ is expressed as the difference of two special angles. The **cosine subtraction formula** contains the sum of two products; simply subtracting the cosines does not apply any valid identity. Substituting the known values gives a sum of radicals with a common denominator.

The result is positive because both radicals are positive. To compare it with $1$, we may square both sides because both are nonnegative. Since $\sqrt3<2$, the calculated square is less than $1$. The result is therefore consistent with the cosine of a nonzero acute angle.

</div>
