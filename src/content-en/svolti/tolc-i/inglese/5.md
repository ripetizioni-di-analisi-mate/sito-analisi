## Exercise 1: Word order in an indirect question

Complete: “Could you tell me where ___?”

- the station is
- is the station
- does the station
- the station does
- the is station

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: the station is**

The clause after *where* is an indirect question and uses subject-verb order: *the station is*. The inversion *is the station* belongs to a standalone direct question. The polite introduction does not change the requested place, but changes the subordinate clause's structure.

The complete sentence is “Could you tell me where the station is?” The main question is *Could you tell me...?*, retaining auxiliary inversion. The requested information is expressed by the subordinate clause *where the station is*. Separating these units explains why the whole sentence can be interrogative without repeating inversion in the part introduced by *where*.

</div>

## Exercise 2: The form after suggest

Complete: “Sam suggested ___ the instructions again.”

- reading
- to read
- read
- to reading
- have read

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: reading**

In this construction, *suggest* is followed by an *-ing* form: *suggested reading*. A different structure with a clause is also possible, but none of the alternatives supplies it. *Suggested to read* is not the pattern used to identify the proposed activity.

The complete sentence is “Sam suggested reading the instructions again.” The subject proposes an activity, but the sentence does not say who will actually perform it. The *-ing* form names the proposed activity. Using *to read* by analogy with *decided to read* would transfer another verb's pattern to *suggest*; the two patterns must be recognized separately.

</div>

## Exercise 3: Concession before a noun

Complete: “___ the heavy rain, the match continued.”

- Despite
- Although
- Even though
- So that
- Because it

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: Despite**

*The heavy rain* is a noun phrase, so it can be introduced by *despite*. *Although* and *even though* would require a clause, for example *it was raining*. The selected construction indicates that the match continued despite the rain.

The complete sentence is “Despite the heavy rain, the match continued.” To choose the connector, first inspect the structure immediately following it: *the heavy rain* contains no finite verb. An equivalent rewrite would be “Although it was raining heavily, the match continued.” The concessive meaning remains, but changing the connector also requires changing what follows it.

</div>
