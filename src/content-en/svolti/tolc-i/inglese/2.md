## Exercise 1: A finished past time

Complete: “I ___ this film yesterday evening.”

- watched
- watch
- have watched
- am watching
- watches

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: watched**

*Yesterday evening* locates the viewing in a finished past time. *Watched* is the regular past simple of *watch*. The present perfect *have watched* does not normally combine with this finished time reference; the present forms do not describe the stated event.

The complete sentence is “I watched this film yesterday evening.” The time reference is closed and does not include the moment of speaking. Removing *yesterday evening* could allow an experience context with *I have watched this film*, but that would be a different statement. The solution must use the supplied information rather than delete the time expression determining the tense.

</div>

## Exercise 2: Past background

Complete: “At the moment the accident happened yesterday, I ___ for the bus.”

- was waiting
- am waiting
- waits
- will wait
- have been waited

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: was waiting**

Waiting is presented as ongoing at the precise moment of the past accident. The past continuous with *I* is *was waiting*. Present or future forms do not respect the reference to yesterday; *have been waited* does not construct intransitive *wait* here.

The complete sentence is “At the moment the accident happened yesterday, I was waiting for the bus.” Two past forms describe different temporal roles: *happened* identifies the event, *was waiting* describes what was ongoing. The sentence does not reveal how long the wait had already lasted or continued afterwards; it gives only the overlap at the accident's moment.

</div>

## Exercise 3: Two events in order

Read: “The train had already left when we arrived at the station.” Which of the two stated events happened first?

- The train's departure.
- Our arrival at the station.
- Both events necessarily happened together.
- The purchase of our tickets.
- The opening of the station.

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer: The train's departure.**

*Had already left* is past perfect and places departure before arrival, expressed in the past simple. The text gives no information about buying tickets or opening the station. The answer follows from the temporal relationship between the two verbs, not an assumed order of other activities.

The temporal relationship can be read without inventing clock times: first the train departs, then the group arrives, and finally the narrator tells the story. The past perfect makes priority relative to arrival explicit. In the figure, points represent this order, not scaled durations. The result does not change when rewritten as “When we arrived, the train had already left.”

</div>
