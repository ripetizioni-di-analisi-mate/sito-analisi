## Exercise 1

All A are B. No B is C. Which conclusion holds even if A is empty?

- No A is C
- There is an A that is B
- All C are A
- Some B are C
- A and B are identical

$$
A\subseteq B,\qquad B\cap C=\varnothing
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\cap C=\varnothing.
$$

::venn{tipo="incluso" nomi="B,A" zone="ab" titolo="Every possible element of A also belongs to B"}

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** No A is C.

The inclusion $A\subseteq B$ places every possible element of A inside B. Since $B\cap C=\varnothing$, none of those elements can belong to C; therefore $A\cap C=\varnothing$. This conclusion does not require A to contain any elements. Universal premises guarantee no existence and do not require the two sets to be equal.

A contradiction argument makes the connection explicit. If an element belonged to both A and C, its membership in A would also place it in B. It would then belong to B and C, contrary to the second premise. The exclusion therefore concerns every possible element. Drawing an inclusion represents a relationship between sets and does not itself assert that an element of A exists.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Some instruments are portable and uncalibrated. All instruments in compartment A are calibrated. Which conclusion follows?

- Some portable instruments are outside compartment A
- All portable instruments are outside compartment A
- Compartment A is empty
- No instrument is calibrated
- All instruments outside compartment A are portable

$$
\begin{aligned}
&x\in P,\qquad x\notin K,\\
&A\subseteq K,\\[6pt]
&x\notin A,\qquad x\in P\setminus A.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** Some portable instruments are outside compartment A.

The first premise guarantees at least one portable uncalibrated instrument. If that instrument were in compartment A, the second premise would make it calibrated, creating a contradiction. It must therefore be outside A. The other portable instruments are not described, so the conclusion remains existential.

P is the set of portable instruments and K the set of calibrated ones; A denotes instruments in the compartment. The letter x names an instrument whose existence is already guaranteed by the first sentence. Every later step concerns that same x. No independent second existence is introduced. There may also be calibrated portable instruments inside A, so “all portable instruments are outside” is unjustified.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Some mountain photographs are in color and some color photographs are printed. Which situation shows that “some mountain photographs are printed” does not follow?

- Two color photos: an unprinted mountain photo and a printed seaside photo
- A mountain photo in color and printed
- All mountain photos are printed
- No photo is in color
- No photo is printed

$$
A=\{a\},\qquad B=\{a,b\},\qquad C=\{b\}.
$$
$$
A\cap B=\{a\},\qquad B\cap C=\{b\},\qquad A\cap C=\varnothing.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Answer.** Two color photos: an unprinted mountain photo and a printed seaside photo.

In the stated case, the mountain photo satisfies the first existence claim and the seaside photo satisfies the second. No mountain photo is printed, so the conclusion is false while both premises are true. This is a valid counterexample because the witnesses of the two statements may be distinct people or objects.

In this representation, A denotes mountain photos, B color photos and C printed photos. The model uses only two elements, so every membership can be checked directly. Photo a satisfies the first premise and b the second, but no element satisfies the conclusion. One such model suffices to show that the deduction is invalid; there is no need to show that the conclusion is always false.

</div>
