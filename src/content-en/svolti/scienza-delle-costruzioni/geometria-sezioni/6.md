## Exercise 1

A right triangle has vertices $(z,y)=(0,0),(60,0),(0,40)$ in millimeters. Find the product of inertia about centroidal axes parallel to its legs.
::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="0,0 60,0 0,40" frecce="0,0,74,0,z;0,0,0,54,y" quote="0 0,60 0,60 mm,8;0 0,0 40,40 mm,-9" titolo="Axes with their origin at the right-angle vertex"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=1\,200\,\mathrm{mm^2},\qquad
z_G=20\,\mathrm{mm},\qquad
y_G=\frac{40}{3}\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
I_{yz}&=\int_0^{40}\int_0^{60(1-\frac{y}{40})}yz\,dz\,dy\\[6pt]
&=\frac{60^2}{2}\int_0^{40}y\left(1-\frac{y}{40}\right)^2dy\\[6pt]
&=240\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
I_{yz,G}=I_{yz}-Ay_Gz_G
=-80\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Measured from the right-angle vertex, the centroid coordinates equal one third of the respective leg lengths. In the original frame, all interior coordinates are positive when integrating the product $yz$. Shifting to axes through $G$ subtracts the product $Ay_Gz_G$. The parallel-axis term exceeds the product of inertia about the original axes, so the centroidal product is negative.

**Check.** Use vertical strips to integrate the product of the shifted coordinates directly. The residual is

$$
\int_0^{60}\int_0^{40(1-\frac{z}{60})}
(z-20)\left(y-\frac{40}{3}\right)dy\,dz
+80\,000=0,
$$

in $\mathrm{mm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An outer rectangle of width $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and height $80\,\mathrm{mm}$ is centered at the origin. A square hole of side $20\,\mathrm{mm}$ is centered at $(20,-10)\,\mathrm{mm}$ and has sides parallel to the rectangle. Find the product of inertia about centroidal axes parallel to the sides.
::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="-50,-40 50,-40 50,40 -50,40" fori="10,-20 30,-20 30,0 10,0" frecce="0,0,66,0,z;0,0,0,56,y" punti="20,-10,C,centro" quote="-50 -40,50 -40,100 mm,8;50 -40,50 40,80 mm,27;10 -20,30 -20,20 mm,6" titolo="Section with a hole below and to the right of center"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=8\,000-400=7\,600\,\mathrm{mm^2},\\[6pt]
z_G&=-\frac{400\cdot20}{7\,600}\,\mathrm{mm}
=-\frac{20}{19}\,\mathrm{mm},\\[6pt]
y_G&=-\frac{400\cdot(-10)}{7\,600}\,\mathrm{mm}
=\frac{10}{19}\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{yz}&=-400\cdot20\cdot(-10)\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=80\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
I_{yz,G}&=I_{yz}-Ay_G z_G
=\frac{1\,600\,000}{19}\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solid rectangle has zero product of inertia about its centroidal axes. So does the hole about its own centroidal axes. In the original frame, however, the hole has a negative product of inertia because of the location of its center. Subtracting it gives a positive net product. Transferring to the actual centroid adds a positive contribution, since $y_G$ and $z_G$ have opposite signs.

**Check.** The integral of the coordinate product over each rectangle factors into two integrals of centroidal coordinates. The residual is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left(100\cdot\frac{20}{19}\right)
 \left(80\cdot\frac{-10}{19}\right)\\[6pt]
&\quad-\left(20\cdot\frac{400}{19}\right)
 \left(20\cdot\frac{-200}{19}\right)
-\frac{1\,600\,000}{19}=0,
\end{aligned}
$$

in $\mathrm{mm^4}$.

</div>
