## Exercise 1

A centered rectangle has width $90\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $z$ and height $30\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $y$. The $z^\prime$ axis is obtained by rotating $z$ through $45^\circ$ toward $y$. Calculate $I_{z^\prime}$ and $I_{y^\prime z^\prime}$.
::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="-45,-15 45,-15 45,15 -45,15" frecce="0,0,58,0,z;0,0,0,32,y;0,0,28,28,z′;0,0,-27,27,y′" quote="-45 -15,45 -15,90 mm,9;45 -15,45 15,30 mm,25" titolo="Rectangle with axes rotated through 45°"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_z&=\frac{90\cdot30^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=202\,500\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
I_y&=\frac{30\cdot90^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=1\,822\,500\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{z^\prime}&=\frac{I_z+I_y}{2}
=1\,012\,500\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
I_{y^\prime z^\prime}&=\frac{I_z-I_y}{2}
=-810\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial product of inertia is zero because the sides are parallel to the axes of symmetry. For a rotation of $45^\circ$, the squared sine and squared cosine are both one half. The second moment about $z^\prime$ is therefore the mean of the initial values. The cross term depends on their difference and is negative.

**Check.** In the new coordinate system $y^\prime z^\prime$ equals $\dfrac{y^2-z^2}{2}$. Direct integration of the product over the rectangle gives the residual

$$
\int_{-45}^{45}\int_{-15}^{15}\frac{y^2-z^2}{2}\,dy\,dz
+810\,000=0,
$$

in $\mathrm{mm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An angle section consists of two perpendicular legs of outside length $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $10\,\mathrm{mm}$. From their common outside corner they extend along the positive $z$ and $y$ axes. Calculate the principal second moments and identify the axis with the smaller value.
::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="0,0 60,0 60,10 10,10 10,60 0,60" frecce="0,0,76,0,z;0,0,0,75,y" quote="0 0,60 0,60 mm,9;0 0,0 60,60 mm,-9;0 60,10 60,10 mm,-22;60 0,60 10,10 mm,9" titolo="Equal-leg angle with axes along the outer edges"}

$$
A=600+500=1\,100\,\mathrm{mm^2},
\qquad
z_G=y_G=\frac{205}{11}\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_z=I_y&=\frac{60\cdot10^3+10\cdot50^3}{12}\\[6pt]
&\quad+600\left(5-\frac{205}{11}\right)^2
+500\left(35-\frac{205}{11}\right)^2\\[6pt]
&=\frac{11\,702\,500}{33}\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{yz}
&=600\left(30-\frac{205}{11}\right)
      \left(5-\frac{205}{11}\right)\\[6pt]
&\quad+500\left(5-\frac{205}{11}\right)
      \left(35-\frac{205}{11}\right)\\[6pt]
&=-\frac{2\,250\,000}{11}\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{\min}&=I_z+I_{yz}
=\frac{4\,952\,500}{33}\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
I_{\max}&=I_z-I_{yz}
=\frac{1\,677\,500}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Symmetry about the angle bisector gives equal second moments about the centroidal axes parallel to the edges. The centroids of the two rectangles lie in quadrants where the coordinate product is negative. Thus $I_{yz}<0$. In the rotation formula the term $-I_{yz}\sin(2\theta)$ is smallest for $\theta=-45^\circ$. The weak axis has slope $-1$ and passes through $G$, so its equation is $y+z=\dfrac{410}{11}\,\mathrm{mm}$.

**Check.** The squared distance to the weak axis is $\dfrac{(y+z-\frac{410}{11})^2}{2}$. Integrate over the full outer square and subtract the missing square. Denoting that squared distance by $g(z,y)$, the residual is

$$
\int_0^{60}\int_0^{60}g(z,y)\,dy\,dz
-\int_{10}^{60}\int_{10}^{60}g(z,y)\,dy\,dz
-\frac{4\,952\,500}{33}=0,
$$

in $\mathrm{mm^4}$.

</div>
