## Exercise 1

Calculate $D$ for a plate with $E=30\,\mathrm{GPa}$, $\nu=0.2$, and thickness $h=120\,\mathrm{mm}$.
::struttura{poligoni="0,-0.55 6,-0.55 6,0.55 0,0.55" tratteggi="0,0 6,0" quote="6 -0.55,6 0.55,120 mm" frecce="0,0,2,0,x;0,0,0,-1,z" riferimento=0 titolo="Thickness h and coordinate z positive downward"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
h&=0.12\,\mathrm m,\\[6pt]
D&=\frac{30\cdot10^9(0.12)^3}{12(1-0.2^2)}\,\mathrm{N\,m}\\[6pt]
&=4.5\,\mathrm{MN\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Express the thickness in meters because the modulus has been converted to pascals. Include the Poisson factor in the flexural rigidity of the isotropic plate. The thickness appears cubed because the bending moment is the through-thickness integral of a linearly varying stress times its moment arm.

</div>

**Independent check.** For a strip one centimeter wide, the second moment of area is $144\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. The value per unit width is therefore $0.000144\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Using this value directly gives the following residual for the flexural rigidity, in meganewton-meters.

$$
\frac{30\,000\cdot0.000144}{0.96}-4.5=0.
$$

## Exercise 2

Two plates have the same Young’s modulus $E$. The first has $h_1=80\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\nu_1=0.2$. The second has $h_2=120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\nu_2=0.4$. Calculate the ratio $\dfrac{D_2}{D_1}$.
::struttura{poligoni="0,-0.55 6,-0.55 6,0.55 0,0.55" tratteggi="0,0 6,0" quote="6 -0.55,6 0.55,h" frecce="0,0,2,0,x;0,0,0,-1,z" riferimento=0 titolo="Thickness h and coordinate z positive downward"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{D_2}{D_1}
&=\left(\frac{h_2}{h_1}\right)^3
  \frac{1-\nu_1^2}{1-\nu_2^2}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{27}{8}\cdot\frac{0.96}{0.84}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{27}{7}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The plates have the same Young’s modulus, so it cancels in the ratio. The lengths can remain in millimeters because they appear only as a ratio. The higher Poisson ratio of the second plate reduces its denominator, increasing the rigidity ratio beyond the effect of thickness alone.

</div>

**Independent check.** Impose the same cylindrical curvature on both specimens. At a common depth $z$ the stress ratio is $\dfrac87$. Taking the moment of this stress introduces the second moment of area through the thickness. The ratio calculated this way gives the residual

$$
\frac87\left(\frac{120}{80}\right)^3-\frac{27}{7}=0.
$$
