## Exercise 1

A plate of thickness $h=6\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a uniform stress $\sigma_x=42\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The cut normal to $x$ has length $0.75\,\mathrm m$ in the $y$ direction. Calculate resultant $N_x$ and the total force across the cut.
::struttura{poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,3.4 0,3.4" frecce="0,0,2,0,x;0,0,0,1.7,y" forze="0 1.7,-0.85,0,σ_x;6 1.7,0.85,0,σ_x" riferimento=0 titolo="Plate under uniform tension in the x direction"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_x&=42\cdot6\,\mathrm{N/mm}\\[6pt]
&=252\,\mathrm{kN/m},\\[6pt]
F_x&=252\cdot0.75\,\mathrm{kN}\\[6pt]
&=189\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A stress of one megapascal is one newton per square millimeter. Integrating through the six-millimeter thickness gives the force per millimeter of cut. Its numerical value is the same in kilonewtons per meter. The stress is constant along the cut, so multiply the resultant by the cut length.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a plate of thickness $h=80\,\mathrm{mm}$, the stress is $\sigma_x=(30+400z)\,\mathrm{MPa}$, with $z$ in meters measured downward from the midplane. Calculate $N_x$ and $M_x$.
::struttura{poligoni="0,-0.55 6,-0.55 6,0.55 0,0.55" tratteggi="0,0 6,0" quote="6 -0.55,6 0.55,80 mm" frecce="0,0,2,0,x;0,0,0,-1,z" riferimento=0 titolo="Thickness h and coordinate z positive downward"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_x
&=\int_{-0.04}^{0.04}(30+400z)\,\mathrm dz\,\mathrm{MN/m}\\[6pt]
&=2.4\,\mathrm{MN/m},\\[6pt]
M_x
&=400\int_{-0.04}^{0.04}z^2\,\mathrm dz\,\mathrm{MN}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{128}{7\,500}\,\mathrm{MN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The uniform term contributes only to $N_x$. The term linear in $z$ integrates to zero by symmetry in the force resultant. It contributes to the moment because the definition of $M_x$ multiplies the stress by an additional power of $z$. A positive moment corresponds to higher stress in the bottom half.

</div>

**Independent check.** The stresses at the two faces are $14$ and $46\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The mean stresses in the two halves are $22$ and $38\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Recalculating the total force across the section gives the following residual in meganewtons per meter.

$$
22\cdot0.04+38\cdot0.04-2.4=0.
$$
