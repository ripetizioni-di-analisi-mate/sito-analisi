## Exercise 1

The principal stresses in descending order are $\sigma_1=110\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_2=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\sigma_3=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The same stress $-45\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is added to each principal component. Calculate the new von Mises equivalent stress.
::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 1,-1 1,1 -1,1" frecce="-1.03,0,-1.8,0,;1.03,0,1.8,0,σ_1;0,-1.03,0,-1.8,;0,1.03,0,1.8,σ_3" etichette="-1.9,2,σ_2 = 20 MPa" titolo="State of stress in principal coordinates"}

A hydrostatic increment adds the same amount to each principal stress.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sigma_1^*,\sigma_2^*,\sigma_3^*)&=\left(65,-25,-25\right)\,\mathrm{MPa} \\[6pt]
(\sigma_1^*-\sigma_2^*,\sigma_2^*-\sigma_3^*,\sigma_3^*-\sigma_1^*)&=\left(90,0,-90\right)\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}&=\sqrt{\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2+(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2+(\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}{2}} \\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{\frac{90^2+0^2+\left(-90\right)^2}{2}} \\[6pt]
&=90\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}^{\,*}&=90\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Changing all three principal stresses equally preserves their differences. Since von Mises stress depends only on those differences, the equivalent stress for the original state also applies to the modified state.

The check uses the traces of the stress tensor and its square. The invariant formula gives the same squared equivalent stress.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&=\frac{3\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2)-(\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\sigma})^2}{2}-\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}^2 \\[6pt]
&=\frac{3\cdot5475-15^2}{2}-8100=0\,\mathrm{MPa^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

</div>

## Exercise 2

The principal stresses in descending order are $\sigma_1=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_2=25\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\sigma_3=-25\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The same stress $80\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is added to each principal component. Calculate the new von Mises equivalent stress.
::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 1,-1 1,1 -1,1" frecce="-1.03,0,-1.8,0,;1.03,0,1.8,0,σ_1;0,-1.8,0,-1.03,;0,1.8,0,1.03,σ_3" etichette="-1.9,2,σ_2 = 25 MPa" titolo="State of stress in principal coordinates"}

A hydrostatic increment adds the same amount to each principal stress.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sigma_1^*,\sigma_2^*,\sigma_3^*)&=\left(155,105,55\right)\,\mathrm{MPa} \\[6pt]
(\sigma_1^*-\sigma_2^*,\sigma_2^*-\sigma_3^*,\sigma_3^*-\sigma_1^*)&=\left(50,50,-100\right)\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}&=\sqrt{\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2+(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2+(\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}{2}} \\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{\frac{50^2+50^2+\left(-100\right)^2}{2}} \\[6pt]
&=50 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}^{\,*}&=50 \sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Changing all three principal stresses equally preserves their differences. Since von Mises stress depends only on those differences, the equivalent stress for the original state also applies to the modified state.

The check uses the traces of the stress tensor and its square. The invariant formula gives the same squared equivalent stress.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&=\frac{3\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2)-(\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\sigma})^2}{2}-\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}^2 \\[6pt]
&=\frac{3\cdot38075-315^2}{2}-7500=0\,\mathrm{MPa^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

</div>
