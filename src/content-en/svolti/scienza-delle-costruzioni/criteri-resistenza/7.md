## Exercise 1

The principal stresses in descending order are $\sigma_1=135\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_2=45\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\sigma_3=45\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The uniaxial yield strength is $f_y=270\,\mathrm{MPa}$. All stresses are scaled by one load multiplier. Calculate the factor of safety using the von Mises criterion.
::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 1,-1 1,1 -1,1" frecce="-1.03,0,-1.8,0,;1.03,0,1.8,0,σ_1;0,-1.03,0,-1.8,;0,1.03,0,1.8,σ_3" etichette="-1.9,2,σ_2 = 45 MPa" titolo="State of stress in principal coordinates"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}&=\sqrt{\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2+(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2+(\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}{2}} \\[6pt]
&=\sqrt{\frac{90^2+0^2+\left(-90\right)^2}{2}} \\[6pt]
&=90\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_V&=\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}}{f_y}=\frac{90}{270}=\frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=\frac{1}{\eta}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{3}}=3
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A positive multiplier preserves the principal stress ordering. The utilization ratio increases in direct proportion, so multiplying by its reciprocal brings the stress state exactly to the failure surface.

Apply the multiplier to the stresses and compare the scaled equivalent stress with the uniaxial strength.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq,V}}(n\sigma)-f_y&=270-270=0\,\mathrm{MPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

</div>

## Exercise 2

The principal stresses in descending order are $\sigma_1=12\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_2=-15\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\sigma_3=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The tensile strength is $f_t=36\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and the magnitude of the compressive strength is $f_c=180\,\mathrm{MPa}$. All stresses are scaled by one load multiplier. Calculate the factor of safety using the Coulomb–Mohr criterion.
::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 1,-1 1,1 -1,1" frecce="-1.03,0,-1.8,0,;1.03,0,1.8,0,σ_1;0,-1.8,0,-1.03,;0,1.8,0,1.03,σ_3" etichette="-1.9,2,σ_2 = -15 MPa" titolo="State of stress in principal coordinates"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_{CM}&=\frac{\sigma_1}{f_t}-\frac{\sigma_3}{f_c} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{12}{36}+\frac{30}{180}=\frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&=\frac{1}{\eta}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}}=2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A positive multiplier preserves the principal stress ordering. The utilization ratio increases in direct proportion, so multiplying by its reciprocal brings the stress state exactly to the failure surface.

Substitute the scaled stresses into the failure condition. The contributions must sum to one.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta_{CM}(n\sigma)-1&=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3}-1=0
\end{aligned}
$$

</div>
