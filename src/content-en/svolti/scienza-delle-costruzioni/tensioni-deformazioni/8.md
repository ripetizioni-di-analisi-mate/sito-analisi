## Exercise 1

In a simply connected planar region, the strains are $\varepsilon_x=12y^2\cdot10^{-6}$, $\varepsilon_y=8x^2\cdot10^{-6}$, and $\gamma_{xy}=kxy\cdot10^{-6}$, where $x,y$ are the numerical coordinates in meters. Find $k$ and construct a compatible displacement field.
::struttura{poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,2 0,2" frecce="0,0,3.8,0,x;0,0,0,2.8,y" punti="1.5,1,P,pieno" riferimento=0 titolo="Point in the planar region under study"}


$$
\frac{\partial^2\varepsilon_x}{\partial y^2}
+\frac{\partial^2\varepsilon_y}{\partial x^2}
=(24+16)\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}},
\qquad
\frac{\partial^2\gamma_{xy}}{\partial x\partial y}=k\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}.
$$
$$
k=40,\qquad
u_x=12xy^2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m,\qquad
u_y=8x^2y\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The compatibility identity fixes the shear strain coefficient. Integrate the first normal strain along $x$ and the second along $y$ to obtain two candidate displacement components. The additive functions may be set to zero because the sum of the cross derivatives already has the required value.

**Check.** The displacement field gives both prescribed normal strains directly. The residual for shear strain is $(24xy+16xy-40xy)\cdot10^{-6}=0$ at every point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isotropic, linearly elastic material has $E=150\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\nu=0.25$. Find the relative volume change and strain energy density under hydrostatic pressure $50\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
::struttura{poligoni="0,0 2.8,0 2.8,1.8 0,1.8" linee="2.8,0 3.6,0.65 3.6,2.45 0.8,2.45 0,1.8;2.8,1.8 3.6,2.45" frecce="3.95,1.225,3.2,1.225,σ_x;1.8,2.85,1.8,2.125,σ_y;0.7,0.45,1.4,1.02,σ_z" titolo="Hydrostatic compression of an element"}


$$
\varepsilon_x=\varepsilon_y=\varepsilon_z
=\frac{-50(1-2\cdot0.25)}{150\,000}=-\frac1{6\,000}.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta V}{V}&=3\left(-\frac1{6\,000}\right)=-500\cdot10^{-6},\\[6pt]
w&=\frac12\left(\sigma_x\varepsilon_x+\sigma_y\varepsilon_y+\sigma_z\varepsilon_z\right)
=\frac12\cdot3\cdot\frac{50}{6\,000}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=12.5\,\mathrm{kJ/m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Hydrostatic pressure applies the same compressive stress to all faces. The normal strains are equal and negative, so the volume decreases. When loading increases linearly, work per unit volume is half the product of the final stress and strain in each component. The product is positive because stress and strain are both negative.

**Check.** The bulk modulus is $K=\dfrac{E}{3(1-2\nu)}=100\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The alternative expression for the energy gives $\dfrac{50^2}{2\cdot100\,000}-0.0125=0$ in $\mathrm{MPa}$.

</div>
