## Exercise 1

The planar displacement field is $u_x=(300x+60y)\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$ and $u_y=(-20x+90y)\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$, with coordinates expressed numerically in meters. Calculate the in-plane strains and the infinitesimal counterclockwise rotation.
::struttura{poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,2 0,2" frecce="0,0,3.8,0,x;0,0,0,2.8,y" punti="1.5,1,P,pieno" riferimento=0 titolo="Point in the planar region under study"}


$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_x&=300\cdot10^{-6},&
\varepsilon_y&=90\cdot10^{-6},\\
\gamma_{xy}&=(60-20)\cdot10^{-6}=40\cdot10^{-6},&
\omega&=\frac{-20-60}{2}\cdot10^{-6}=-40\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{rad}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivatives along each component's own direction give the two relative length changes. The sum of the cross derivatives gives the engineering shear strain. Half their difference, taking counterclockwise as positive, gives the material rotation.

**Check.** Reconstruct $\partial_xu_y$ from the symmetric and skew-symmetric parts. In microstrain, the difference from the given value is $\frac{40}{2}-40-(-20)=0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The planar field is $u_x=(80x^2+30xy)\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$ and $u_y=(-50xy+20y^2)\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm m$, with $x,y$ measured numerically in meters. Calculate $\varepsilon_x$, $\varepsilon_y$, and $\gamma_{xy}$ at $P=(1,1)\,\mathrm m$ and the relative extension of an infinitesimal segment inclined at $45^\circ$.
::struttura{poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,2 0,2" frecce="0,0,3.8,0,x;0,0,0,2.8,y" punti="1,1,P,pieno" quote="0 0,1 0,1 m;0 0,0 1,1 m,-0.65" riferimento=0 titolo="Coordinates of the point where strain is evaluated"}


$$
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_x(P)&=(160x+30y)\big|_{P}\cdot10^{-6}=190\cdot10^{-6},\\
\varepsilon_y(P)&=(-50x+40y)\big|_{P}\cdot10^{-6}=-10\cdot10^{-6},\\
\gamma_{xy}(P)&=(30x-50y)\big|_{P}\cdot10^{-6}=-20\cdot10^{-6},\\[6pt]
\varepsilon_{45}(P)&=\frac{190-10-20}{2}\cdot10^{-6}
=80\cdot10^{-6}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gradient varies with position, so differentiate the field before inserting the coordinates. In the direction at $45^\circ$ the two normal strains receive equal weights. Shear strain contributes a negative term equal to half of $\gamma_{xy}$.

**Check.** At $P$ the gradient in microstrain is $\begin{pmatrix}190&30\\-50&-10\end{pmatrix}$. For the normalized unit vector $(1,1)$, projecting the directional derivative onto that vector gives $\frac{190+30-50-10}{2}-80=0$.

</div>
