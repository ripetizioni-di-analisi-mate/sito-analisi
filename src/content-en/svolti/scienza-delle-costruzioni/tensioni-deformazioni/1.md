## Exercise 1

In plane stress, $\sigma_x=50\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=14\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy}=12\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Calculate the two components of traction along $\mathbf n=(\dfrac35,\dfrac45)$ and $\mathbf m=(-\dfrac45,\dfrac35)$.
::struttura{poligoni="0,0 2.4,0 0,1.8" frecce="1.2,0.9,1.74,1.62,n;1.2,0.9,0.48,1.44,t" titolo="Normal and tangential directions on the face"}


$$
\mathbf t=\begin{pmatrix}50&12\\12&14\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\frac35\\[2pt]\frac45\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{MPa}
=\begin{pmatrix}39.6\\18.4\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{MPa}.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_n&=39.6\cdot\frac35+18.4\cdot\frac45
=38.48\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
\tau_{nt}&=-39.6\cdot\frac45+18.4\cdot\frac35
=-20.64\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply the stress tensor by the normal because the traction vector depends on the orientation of the face. The two projections use perpendicular unit vectors, so a negative shear component points opposite to $\mathbf m$.

**Check.** Rotating the coordinate system preserves the magnitude of the traction. Indeed, $(38.48)^2+(20.64)^2-(39.6)^2-(18.4)^2=0$ in $\mathrm{MPa^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given a plane stress tensor with $\sigma_x=75\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=-25\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy}=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$, calculate $\sigma_n$ and $\tau_{nt}$ for $\mathbf n=(\dfrac45,-\dfrac35)$ and $\mathbf m=(\dfrac35,\dfrac45)$.
::struttura{poligoni="0,0 1.8,0 3.15,1.8 0,1.8" frecce="2.475,0.9,3.195,0.36,n;2.475,0.9,2.88,1.44" etichette="3.03,1.53,t" titolo="Face orientation in global coordinates"}


$$
p=\frac{75-25}{2}=25,\quad d=\frac{75+25}{2}=50,\quad
\cos2\theta=\frac7{25},\quad \sin2\theta=-\frac{24}{25}.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_n&=\left(25+50\cdot\frac7{25}-20\cdot\frac{24}{25}\right)\mathrm{MPa}
=19.8\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
\tau_{nt}&=\left(50\cdot\frac{24}{25}+20\cdot\frac7{25}\right)\mathrm{MPa}
=53.6\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sine of the angle is negative because the normal has a negative $y$ component. The double-angle formula determines the signs of the two cross terms. The resulting normal component is positive, and the shear component points along $\mathbf m$.

**Check.** Direct multiplication of the tensor by $\mathbf n$ gives $(48,31)\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Reconstructing the components gives $19.8\cdot\dfrac45+53.6\cdot\dfrac35-48=0$ and $-19.8\cdot\dfrac35+53.6\cdot\dfrac45-31=0$.

</div>
