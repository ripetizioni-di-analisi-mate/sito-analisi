## Exercise 1

A thin plate in plane stress has $\sigma_x=80\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=20\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy}=40\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Determine the in-plane principal stresses and the direction of the normal for the larger stress in $[-90^\circ,90^\circ)$.
::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 1,-1 1,1 -1,1" forze="1 0,0.62,0,σ_x;-1 0,-0.62,0;0 1,0,0.62,σ_y;0 -1,0,-0.62" frecce="1.16,-0.56,1.16,0.56,τ_xy;-1.16,0.56,-1.16,-0.56;-0.56,1.16,0.56,1.16;0.56,-1.16,-0.56,-1.16" titolo="Element with positive normal and shear stresses"}


$$
p=50\,\mathrm{MPa},\qquad d=30\,\mathrm{MPa},\qquad
R=\sqrt{30^2+40^2}\,\mathrm{MPa}=50\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_1&=100\,\mathrm{MPa},&
\sigma_2&=0,\\
\cos2\theta_1&=\frac35,&
\sin2\theta_1&=\frac45,\\
\theta_1&\simeq26.57^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The principal stresses are the intersections of the circle with the horizontal axis. Both the sine and cosine of $2\theta_1$ are positive, so the double angle lies in the first quadrant.

**Check.** With unit vector $\mathbf n=(\dfrac2{\sqrt5},\dfrac1{\sqrt5})$, the residual of the matrix–vector product is $\dfrac1{\sqrt5}(80\cdot2+40-100\cdot2,\,40\cdot2+20-100)=(0,0)$ in $\mathrm{MPa}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An element in plane stress has $\sigma_x=-70\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $\sigma_y=-30\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy}=15\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Calculate the maximum shear stress magnitude in the $xy$ plane and in three dimensions.
::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 1,-1 1,1 -1,1" forze="1 0,-0.65,0,σ_x;-1 0,0.65,0;0 1,0,-0.65,σ_y;0 -1,0,0.65" frecce="1.16,-0.56,1.16,0.56,τ_xy;-1.16,0.56,-1.16,-0.56;-0.56,1.16,0.56,1.16;0.56,-1.16,-0.56,-1.16" titolo="Element in compression along both axes"}


$$
p=-50\,\mathrm{MPa},\quad d=-20\,\mathrm{MPa},\quad
R=\sqrt{(-20)^2+15^2}\,\mathrm{MPa}=25\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_1&=-25\,\mathrm{MPa},&\sigma_2&=-75\,\mathrm{MPa},\\
|\tau|_{\max,xy}&=25\,\mathrm{MPa},&
|\tau|_{\max,\mathrm{space}}&=\frac{0-(-75)}2\,\mathrm{MPa}=37.5\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both in-plane principal stresses are compressive. The out-of-plane normal has zero principal stress and therefore enters the range between the extreme three-dimensional values. This range is wider than the in-plane range.

**Check.** Let $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf e_z$ be the principal directions corresponding to $-75\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and zero, respectively. For normal $\dfrac{\mathbf a+\mathbf e_z}{\sqrt2}$ and tangent $\dfrac{\mathbf e_z-\mathbf a}{\sqrt2}$, direct projection of traction gives $\dfrac{75}2-37.5=0$ in $\mathrm{MPa}$.

</div>
