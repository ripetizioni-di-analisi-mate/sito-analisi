## Exercise 1

A rectangular section of width $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ and depth $240\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a vertical shear force of magnitude $36\,\mathrm{kN}$. Find the magnitudes of the width-averaged shear stress at the centroid and at elevation $60\,\mathrm{mm}$.
::struttura{poligoni="-60,-120 60,-120 60,120 -60,120" frecce="-74.4,0,74.4,0,z;0,-134.4,0,134.4,y" quote="-60 -120,60 -120,120 mm,14.4;60 -120,60 120,240 mm,28.8" tratteggi="-60,60 60,60" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular section and strip 60 mm from the centroid"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_z&=\frac{120\cdot240^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}=138\,240\,000\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
S_z(0)&=120\cdot120\cdot60\,\mathrm{mm^3}=864\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3},\\[6pt]
S_z(60)&=120\cdot60\cdot90\,\mathrm{mm^3}=648\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\overline{\tau}_{xy}(0)|&=\frac{36\,000\cdot864\,000}{138\,240\,000\cdot120}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=1.875\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
|\overline{\tau}_{xy}(60)|&=\frac{36\,000\cdot648\,000}{138\,240\,000\cdot120}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=1.40625\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the Jourawski shear formula, take the area above the strip. The centroid of the upper half lies $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the origin, and the centroid of the part above the specified elevation lies $90\,\mathrm{mm}$ from the origin. The local width remains $120\,\mathrm{mm}$.

</div>

**Independent check**

Recover the magnitude of the shear force by integrating the parabolic distribution over the cross section. With $y$ in millimeters, this gives

$$
120\cdot1.875\int_{-120}^{120}
\left(1-\frac{y^2}{120^2}\right)\mathrm dy-36\,000
=120\cdot1.875\cdot160-36\,000=0.
$$

## Exercise 2

An I-section of overall depth $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ has flanges of width $100\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $10\,\mathrm{mm}$. The web thickness is $10\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the vertical shear force has magnitude $30\,\mathrm{kN}$. Find the magnitudes of the width-averaged shear stress immediately above and below the top flange–web junction.
::struttura{poligoni="-50,-50 50,-50 50,-40 5,-40 5,40 50,40 50,50 -50,50 -50,40 -5,40 -5,-40 -50,-40" frecce="-59,0,59,0,z;0,-58,0,56.5,y" quote="-50 50,50 50,100 mm,-18;50 -50,50 50,100 mm,25" tratteggi="-50,40 50,40" riferimento=0 titolo="Top flange and web at the junction"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_z&=\frac{100\cdot100^3-90\cdot80^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=\frac{13\,480\,000}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
S_z(40)&=100\cdot10\cdot45\,\mathrm{mm^3}
=45\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3},\\[6pt]
|q_V|&=\frac{30\,000\cdot45\,000}{\frac{13\,480\,000}{3}}\,\mathrm{N/mm}
=\frac{101\,250}{337}\,\mathrm{N/mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
|\overline{\tau}_{\mathrm{flange}}|
=\frac{2\,025}{674}\,\mathrm{MPa}\simeq3.00\,\mathrm{MPa},
\qquad
|\overline{\tau}_{\mathrm{web}}|
=\frac{10\,125}{337}\,\mathrm{MPa}\simeq30.04\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the junction, the first moment of area is that of the top flange alone. It approaches the same value from above and below. The average shear stresses differ because the available width immediately below the junction is one tenth of the width immediately above it.

</div>

**Independent check**

Check the resultant shear force for the full shear stress field from the Jourawski formula. The factor $b(y)$ cancels inside the integral. On reversing the order of integration, the interval of elevations $y$ below each elevation $\xi$ has length $\xi+50$. This gives

$$
\int_{-50}^{50} S_z(y)\,\mathrm dy
=\int_A \xi(\xi+50)\,\mathrm dA
=I_z+50\int_A\xi\,\mathrm dA=I_z.
$$

The difference between the recovered and applied shear force is therefore $\dfrac{30\,000}{I_z}I_z-30\,000=0$.
