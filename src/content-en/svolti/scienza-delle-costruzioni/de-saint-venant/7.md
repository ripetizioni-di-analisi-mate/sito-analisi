## Exercise 1

The legs of an angle section have midline lengths $80\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and uniform thickness $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. The torque magnitude is $5.2\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ and $G=80\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Assuming free warping, find the torsion constant and the maximum shear stress magnitude away from the junction.
::struttura{poligoni="-1,-1 80,-1 80,1 1,1 1,50 -1,50" tratteggi="0,50 0,0 80,0" frecce="-4,0,86,0,z;0,-6,0,56,y" quote="0 0,80 0,80 mm,10;0 0,0 50,50 mm,14" riferimento=0 titolo="Angle section of uniform thickness"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
J_t&\simeq\frac{80\cdot2^3+50\cdot2^3}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=\frac{1\,040}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
|\tau|_{\max}
&\simeq\frac{5\,200\cdot2\cdot3}{1\,040}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=30\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The section is open, so the torsional rigidities of the two strips add. Both walls have the same thickness, so the model gives the same maximum shear stress magnitude in each strip. The shear stresses on opposite faces of a strip have opposite signs.

</div>

**Independent check**

Check the strain energy per unit length. With $n$ measured in millimeters from the wall midline, the stress magnitude is $30|n|$ in $\mathrm{MPa}$. The twist per unit length is $\vartheta'=\dfrac{30}{80\,000\cdot2}\,\mathrm{rad/mm}$. The difference between the integrated strain energy and the elastic work done by the torque is

$$
130\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(30n)^2}{2\cdot80\,000}\,\mathrm dn
-\frac{5\,200}{2}\frac{30}{80\,000\cdot2}
=0.4875-0.4875=0.
$$

## Exercise 2

Model an open angle section as two strips. The horizontal strip has length $60\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $2\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the vertical strip has length $45\,\mathrm{mm}$ and thickness $4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Given $|M_t|=11.2\,\mathrm{N\,m}$ and free warping, find the maximum shear stress magnitude in each strip away from the junction.
::struttura{poligoni="-2,-1 60,-1 60,1 2,1 2,45 -2,45" tratteggi="0,45 0,0 60,0" frecce="-8,0,69,0,z;0,-9,0,53,y" quote="0 0,60 0,60 mm,9;0 0,0 45,45 mm,11" riferimento=0 titolo="Strips of thickness 2 mm and 4 mm"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
J_{\mathrm{oriz}}&\simeq\frac{60\cdot2^3}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=160\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
J_{\mathrm{vert}}&\simeq\frac{45\cdot4^3}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=960\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
|\tau_{\mathrm{oriz}}|_{\max}
&\simeq\frac{11\,200\cdot2}{1\,120}\,\mathrm{MPa}=20\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
|\tau_{\mathrm{vert}}|_{\max}
&\simeq\frac{11\,200\cdot4}{1\,120}\,\mathrm{MPa}=40\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both strips belong to the same section and have the same twist per unit length. For a given twist, shear stress is proportional to distance from the wall midline. The maximum stress in the thicker wall is therefore twice that in the thinner wall.

</div>
