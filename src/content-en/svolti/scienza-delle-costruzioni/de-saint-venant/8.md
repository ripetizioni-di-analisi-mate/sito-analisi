## Exercise 1

A solid shaft of diameter $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a tensile force of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ and a torque of magnitude $240\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Find the von Mises equivalent stress at the surface away from the ends.
::struttura{cerchi="0,0,20" frecce="-26,0,26,0,z;0,-21,0,26,y" quote="-20 -20,20 -20,Ø 40 mm,4" riferimento=0 titolo="Solid shaft section under axial tension and torsion"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_x&=\frac{20\,000}{400\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}=\frac{50}{\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
J&=80\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
|\tau|&=\frac{240\,000\cdot20}{80\,000\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=\frac{60}{\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\sqrt{\sigma_x^2+3\tau^2}
=\frac{10\sqrt{133}}{\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}\simeq36.71\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axial force produces a uniform normal stress, while the torsional shear stress attains its maximum magnitude at the surface. At any point on the circumference, choose a local axis along the tangent. Only one shear component is then present, and the equivalent stress follows by combining it with the axial stress at the same point.

</div>

**Independent check**

Check the equivalent stress using the two principal stresses of the in-plane block. Their sum is $\sigma_x$ and their product is $-\tau^2$. In principal axes the square of the equivalent stress is

$$
\sigma_1^2-\sigma_1\sigma_2+\sigma_2^2
=(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)^2-3\sigma_1\sigma_2
=\frac{13\,300}{\pi^2}\,\mathrm{MPa^2}.
$$

Subtracting the square of the computed result gives zero.

## Exercise 2

A rectangular section of width $80\,\mathrm{mm}$ and depth $120\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a bending moment $M_z=3.84\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ and a vertical shear force of magnitude $128\,\mathrm{kN}$. Use the width-averaged shear stress from the Jourawski formula to find the maximum von Mises equivalent stress over the depth.
::struttura{poligoni="-40,-60 40,-60 40,60 -40,60" frecce="-49.6,0,49.6,0,z;0,-69.6,0,69.6,y" quote="-40 -60,40 -60,80 mm,9.6;40 -60,40 60,120 mm,19.2" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular section under bending and shear"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_z&=\frac{80\cdot120^2}{6}\,\mathrm{mm^3}=192\,000\,\mathrm{mm^3},\\[6pt]
|\sigma_x|_{\mathrm{edge}}&=\frac{3\,840\,000}{192\,000}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=20\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
|\overline{\tau}_{xy}(0)|&=\frac{3\cdot128\,000}{2\cdot80\cdot120}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=20\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
u=\left(\frac{y}{60\,\mathrm{mm}}\right)^2,\quad
0\le u\le1,\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}^2
=\left[400u+1\,200(1-u)^2\right]\,\mathrm{MPa^2}.
$$

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}(u=0)=20\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa},\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{eq}}(u=1)=20\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of the equivalent stress is a strictly convex polynomial in $u$ because the coefficient of $u^2$ is positive. Its maximum over a closed interval is therefore at an endpoint. The value at mid-depth exceeds that at the extreme fibers. The maximum is $20\sqrt3\,\mathrm{MPa}$ at elevation $y=0$.

</div>

**Independent check**

Recover the shear force by integrating the width-averaged shear stress distribution. With $y$ in millimeters, the difference between the recovered and applied shear forces is

$$
80\int_{-60}^{60}20\left(1-\frac{y^2}{60^2}\right)\mathrm dy-128\,000
=80\cdot20\cdot80-128\,000=0.
$$
