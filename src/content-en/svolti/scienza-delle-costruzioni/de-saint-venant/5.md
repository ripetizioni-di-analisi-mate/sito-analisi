## Exercise 1

A solid shaft of diameter $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ transmits a torque $M_t=200\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Find the maximum shear stress magnitude and the angle of twist over a length $500\,\mathrm{mm}$, given $G=80\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
::struttura{cerchi="0,0,25" frecce="-32.5,0,32.5,0,z;0,-26.25,0,32.5,y" quote="-25 -25,25 -25,Ø 50 mm,5" riferimento=0 titolo="Cross section of the 50 mm diameter solid shaft"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
J&=\frac{\pi\,25^4}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=\frac{390\,625\pi}{2}\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
|\tau|_{\max}&=\frac{200\,000\cdot25\cdot2}{390\,625\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=\frac{25.6}{\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
\varphi&=\frac{200\,000\cdot500\cdot2}{80\,000\cdot390\,625\pi}\,\mathrm{rad}
=\frac{0.0064}{\pi}\,\mathrm{rad}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The shear stress increases from the center to the surface. The angle of twist depends on the torsional rigidity of the entire cross section and the length of the shaft. Convert the torque to newton-millimeters in both calculations.

</div>

**Independent check**

Recover the transmitted torque by integrating the circumferential shear stress multiplied by its moment arm. The slope of the radial stress distribution is $\dfrac{25.6}{25\pi}$ in $\mathrm{MPa/mm}$. The residual in $\mathrm{N\,mm}$ is

$$
\frac{25.6}{25\pi}\int_0^{25}2\pi\rho^3\,\mathrm d\rho-200\,000
=200\,000-200\,000=0.
$$

## Exercise 2

A hollow shaft has outer radius $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ and inner radius $30\,\mathrm{mm}$. It transmits a torque of magnitude $700\,\mathrm{N\,m}$. Find the shear stress magnitude at each surface.
::struttura{cerchi="0,0,40;0,0,30,foro" frecce="-52,0,52,0,z;0,-42,0,52,y" quote="-40 -40,40 -40,Ø 80 mm,8" riferimento=0 titolo="Hollow shaft with an inner radius of 30 mm"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
J&=\frac{\pi}{2}(40^4-30^4)\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=875\,000\pi\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
|\tau(30)|&=\frac{700\,000\cdot30}{875\,000\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=\frac{24}{\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
|\tau(40)|&=\frac{700\,000\cdot40}{875\,000\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=\frac{32}{\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bore reduces the polar second moment of area. Shear stress still varies linearly with radius in the wall, so its magnitude is already nonzero at the inner surface. The free cylindrical surface carries zero traction because the calculated shear acts circumferentially on the transverse face.

</div>

**Independent check**

The resultant shear force on a cross section in pure torsion must vanish. Around each circle, the vertical component is proportional to $\cos\theta$. The check gives

$$
\int_{30}^{40}\frac{0.8}{\pi}\rho^2\,\mathrm d\rho
\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,\mathrm d\theta=0.
$$
