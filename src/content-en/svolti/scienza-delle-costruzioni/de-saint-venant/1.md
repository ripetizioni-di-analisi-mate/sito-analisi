## Exercise 1

A tie of length $600\,\mathrm{mm}$ has a rectangular cross section of width $30\,\mathrm{mm}$ and depth $50\,\mathrm{mm}$. A concentric tensile force of $45\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts on it. Given $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$, find the normal stress and the elongation of the member.
::struttura{poligoni="-15,-25 15,-25 15,25 -15,25" frecce="-18.6,0,18.6,0,z;0,-28.6,0,28.6,y" quote="-15 -25,15 -25,30 mm,3.6;15 -25,15 25,50 mm,7.2" riferimento=0 titolo="Cross section of the concentrically loaded tie"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=30\cdot50\,\mathrm{mm^2}=1\,500\,\mathrm{mm^2},\\[6pt]
\sigma_x&=\frac{45\,000}{1\,500}\,\mathrm{MPa}=30\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
\varepsilon_x&=\frac{30}{200\,000}=1.5\cdot10^{-4},\\[6pt]
\Delta L&=\varepsilon_xL=0.090\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force passes through the centroid, so the nominal stress is uniform. The elastic strain is the elongation per unit length. Multiplying it by the length of the member gives the total elongation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tube of outer radius $25\,\mathrm{mm}$ and inner radius $15\,\mathrm{mm}$ carries a concentric compressive force of magnitude $24\pi\,\mathrm{kN}$. Given $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\nu=0.30$, find the change in the outside diameter away from the ends if the tube is free to expand laterally.
::struttura{cerchi="0,0,25;0,0,15,foro" frecce="-32.5,0,32.5,0,z;0,-26.25,0,32.5,y" quote="-25 -25,25 -25,Ø 50 mm,5" riferimento=0 titolo="Tube cross section with a 30 mm diameter bore"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &=\pi(25^2-15^2)\,\mathrm{mm^2}=400\pi\,\mathrm{mm^2},\\[6pt]
\sigma_x&=-\frac{24\,000\pi}{400\pi}\,\mathrm{MPa}=-60\,\mathrm{MPa},\\[6pt]
\varepsilon_x&=-\frac{60}{200\,000}=-3\cdot10^{-4},\\[6pt]
\varepsilon_z&=-\nu\varepsilon_x=9\cdot10^{-5},\\[6pt]
\Delta D&=50\cdot9\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mm}=0.0045\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load-bearing area is the area of the annulus. The outer surface is free, so the Poisson effect causes the tube to expand laterally. In isotropic elasticity the lateral strain is the same in every direction in the cross section. The increase in outside diameter can therefore be found along any diameter.

</div>
