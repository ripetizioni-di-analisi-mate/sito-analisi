## Exercise 1

The wall midline of a closed section forms a rectangle of width $70\,\mathrm{mm}$ and height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$. The walls have uniform thickness $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. For a torque of magnitude $280\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, find the shear flow and the magnitude of the mean wall shear stress.
::struttura{poligoni="-36,-26 36,-26 36,26 -36,26" fori="-34,-24 34,-24 34,24 -34,24" tratteggi="-35,-25 35,-25 35,25 -35,25 -35,-25" frecce="-43.4,0,43.4,0,z;0,-31,0,31,y" quote="-35 -25,35 -25,70 mm,7.5;35 -25,35 25,50 mm,19.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Uniform-thickness cell with a dashed wall midline"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_m&=70\cdot50\,\mathrm{mm^2}=3\,500\,\mathrm{mm^2},\\[6pt]
|q_t|&=\frac{280\,000}{2\cdot3\,500}\,\mathrm{N/mm}=40\,\mathrm{N/mm},\\[6pt]
|\overline{\tau}_t|&=\frac{40}{2}\,\mathrm{MPa}=20\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The section encloses a single closed cell, so the Bredt shear flow is constant. The wall thickness is uniform, so the magnitude of the mean shear stress is also constant around the cell.

</div>

**Independent check**

Sum the moments of the four equivalent wall forces about the center. The horizontal walls have moment arm $25\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the vertical walls have moment arm $35\,\mathrm{mm}$. The residual in $\mathrm{N\,mm}$ is

$$
2(40\cdot70)\cdot25+2(40\cdot50)\cdot35-280\,000=0.
$$

## Exercise 2

A closed cell has a rectangular wall midline of width $90\,\mathrm{mm}$ and height $50\,\mathrm{mm}$. The horizontal walls have thickness $3\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the vertical walls thickness $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Given $M_t=810\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, $G=80\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$, and $L=600\,\mathrm{mm}$, find the relative rotation of the ends.
::struttura{poligoni="-46,-26.5 46,-26.5 46,26.5 -46,26.5" fori="-44,-23.5 44,-23.5 44,23.5 -44,23.5" tratteggi="-45,-25 45,-25 45,25 -45,25 -45,-25" frecce="-55.8,0,55.8,0,z;0,-31,0,31,y" quote="-45 -25,45 -25,90 mm,7.5;45 -25,45 25,50 mm,25.2" riferimento=0 titolo="Cell with walls of different thicknesses"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_m&=4\,500\,\mathrm{mm^2},\\[6pt]
\oint\frac{\mathrm ds}{t(s)}
&=\frac{2\cdot90}{3}+\frac{2\cdot50}{2}=110,\\[6pt]
J_t&=\frac{4\cdot4\,500^2}{110}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=\frac{81\,000\,000}{110}\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
\varphi&=\frac{810\,000\cdot600\cdot110}{80\,000\cdot81\,000\,000}\,\mathrm{rad}
=0.00825\,\mathrm{rad}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the area enclosed by the wall midline. In the sum around the perimeter, each pair of sides enters with its own thickness. The thinner walls contribute more to the torsional flexibility because thickness appears in the denominator of the integrand.

</div>

**Independent check**

Compatibility around the closed perimeter requires the line integral of shear strain to equal $2A_m\vartheta'$. The shear flow $q_t=90\,\mathrm{N/mm}$ gives shear strains $\dfrac{90}{80\,000\cdot3}$ and $\dfrac{90}{80\,000\cdot2}$ in the two pairs of walls. The residual in millimeters is

$$
180\frac{90}{80\,000\cdot3}
+100\frac{90}{80\,000\cdot2}
-9\,000\frac{0.00825}{600}=0.
$$
