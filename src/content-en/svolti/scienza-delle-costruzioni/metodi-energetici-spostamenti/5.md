## Exercise 1

A beam of length $4\,\mathrm m$ is fixed at $A$ and propped at $B$. The prop does not settle. The beam has $EI=8\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. A downward force of $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at midspan. Calculate the vertical reaction at $B$.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m" forze="2 0,0,-0.9,P" titolo="Point load at the center of the beam"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(x)&=X(4-x)-20(2-x)\quad (0\le x\le2),\\[6pt]
M(x)&=X(4-x)\quad (2<x\le4).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=EI\,\frac{\partial U}{\partial X}\\[6pt]
&=X\int_0^4(4-x)^2\,dx
-20\int_0^2(2-x)(4-x)\,dx\\[6pt]
&=X\frac{64}{3}-\frac{400}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
X=6.25\,\mathrm{kN}\quad\text{upward}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Remove the prop and replace it with the upward force $X$. The midspan load contributes to the bending moment only to the left of its point of application. Since the prop prevents vertical displacement, the derivative of the strain energy with respect to $X$ is zero. The positive sign confirms the assumed direction.

**Check.** Under the actual force alone, the elastic curve gives a downward tip deflection of $\dfrac{20\cdot2^2(3\cdot4-2)}{6\cdot8\,000}\,\mathrm m$. The force $X$ acting alone produces an upward deflection of $\dfrac{6.25\cdot4^3}{3\cdot8\,000}\,\mathrm m$. Their difference is zero, so the prescribed displacement at $B$ is satisfied.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A beam of length $4\,\mathrm m$ is fixed at $A$ and propped vertically at $B$. Its flexural rigidity is constant, and the uniform downward load has intensity $10\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Find the prop reaction and the support reactions at $A$.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m" carichi="A,B,0.55,0.55,q" titolo="Propped beam under a uniform load"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(x)&=X(L-x)-\frac{q(L-x)^2}{2},\\[6pt]
0&=EI\,\frac{\partial U}{\partial X}
=\frac{XL^3}{3}-\frac{qL^4}{8},\\[6pt]
X&=\frac{3qL}{8}=15\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_A&=qL-X=25\,\mathrm{kN},\\[6pt]
M_A&=\frac{qL^2}{2}-XL=20\,\mathrm{kN\,m},\\[6pt]
H_A&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the primary structure, the uniform load and the tip reaction produce curvatures of opposite signs. Stationarity requires the corresponding displacements at $B$ to cancel. Once the upward prop reaction is known, vertical force equilibrium gives an upward reaction at $A$ as well. Moment equilibrium about $A$ gives a counterclockwise reaction moment.

**Check.** Take moments about end $B$. The load resultant acts a distance $2\,\mathrm m$ to the left of $B$. The sum of the moments about this second point is $20-25\cdot4+40\cdot2=0$, in $\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

</div>
