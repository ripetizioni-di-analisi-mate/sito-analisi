## Exercise 1

A tie consists of two segments in series. The first segment has length $1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $EA_1=18\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$. The second segment has length $2\,\mathrm{m}$ and $EA_2=36\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$. A tensile force of $18\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at the free end. Calculate the total strain energy.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,1,0;C,3,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="C,0.6,0,P" quote="A,B,1 m;B,C,2 m" titolo="Tie with two axial rigidities"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_1&=\frac{18^2\cdot1}{2\cdot18\,000}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
=0.009\,\mathrm{kN\,m},\\[6pt]
U_2&=\frac{18^2\cdot2}{2\cdot36\,000}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
=0.009\,\mathrm{kN\,m},\\[6pt]
U&=U_1+U_2=0.018\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

No load acts at the junction, so both segments carry the same axial force. The two length-to-axial-rigidity ratios are equal, so the two strain energies are equal. Add them because they belong to separate segments.

**Check.** The elongations obtained from the elastic constitutive law add to $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Comparison with the external work during the gradual application of the load gives $\dfrac12\cdot18\cdot0.002-0.018=0$, in $\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A simply supported beam of span $6\,\mathrm{m}$ has $EI=5\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. A downward point load of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at midspan. Calculate the strain energy, neglecting shear deformation.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,3,0;B,6,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-1.05,P" quote="A,C,3 m;C,B,3 m" titolo="Simply supported beam loaded at midspan"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_A=R_B&=5\,\mathrm{kN},\\[6pt]
M(x)&=5x\qquad (0\le x\le3),\\[6pt]
U&=2\int_0^3\frac{25x^2}{2\cdot5\,000}\,dx\,\mathrm{kN\,m}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{25\cdot3^3}{3\cdot5\,000}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
=0.045\,\mathrm{kN\,m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By symmetry, each support carries half the load. The bending moment increases linearly along the left half of the beam. The right half has the mirror image of the same diagram, so evaluate the strain energy integral over half the span and double it.

**Check.** For a simply supported beam with a central point load, the elastic curve gives $v_C=\dfrac{10\cdot6^3}{48\cdot5\,000}\,\mathrm m=0.009\,\mathrm m$. The residual between external work and the strain energy integral is $\dfrac12\cdot10\cdot0.009-0.045=0$, in $\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

</div>
