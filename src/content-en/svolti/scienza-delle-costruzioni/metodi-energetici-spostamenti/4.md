## Exercise 1

A cantilever of length $3\,\mathrm m$ has $EI=4\,500\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. A uniform downward load of intensity $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts over the full span. Calculate the tip deflection.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" quote="A,B,3 m" carichi="A,B,0.42,0.42,q" titolo="Uniform load on the cantilever"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(x,F)&=-\frac{q(L-x)^2}{2}-F(L-x),\\[6pt]
\frac{\partial M}{\partial F}&=-(L-x),\\[6pt]
v_B&=\left.\int_0^L\frac{M(x,F)}{EI}
\frac{\partial M}{\partial F}\,dx\right|_{F=0}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{qL^4}{8EI}=0.0135\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the tip, add a downward auxiliary force conjugate to the desired deflection. After differentiating the strain energy, set the auxiliary force to zero. Only the contribution of the distributed load remains. The positive derivative indicates a displacement of $13.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ downward.

**Check.** Integrating $EIv^{\prime\prime}=\dfrac{q(L-x)^2}{2}$ from the fixed end with zero initial rotation and deflection gives a residual of $\dfrac{6}{2\cdot4\,500}\int_0^3(3-x)^3\,dx-0.0135=0$, in $\mathrm m$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cantilever of span $5\,\mathrm m$ has $EI=4\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. A downward force of $10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts a distance $2\,\mathrm m$ from the fixed end. Calculate the deflection at the free tip.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,5,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="C,0,-0.9,P" quote="A,C,2 m;C,B,3 m" titolo="Point load within the cantilever span"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_P(x)&=-P(a-x)\qquad (0\le x\le a),\\[6pt]
M_P(x)&=0\qquad (a<x\le L),\\[6pt]
\frac{\partial M}{\partial F}&=-(L-x).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_B&=\frac{P}{EI}\int_0^a(a-x)(L-x)\,dx\\[6pt]
&=\frac{Pa^2(3L-a)}{6EI}
=\frac{13}{600}\,\mathrm m
\simeq21.67\,\mathrm{mm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The auxiliary tip force produces a moment throughout the cantilever. The actual force leaves the segment between its point of application and the free end unloaded. The product of the two moment diagrams is therefore zero over that segment. The tip deflection still includes the displacement of the unloaded segment caused by rotation of the curved segment.

**Check.** The elastic curve gives a deflection $\dfrac{Pa^3}{3EI}$ and downward slope $\dfrac{Pa^2}{2EI}$ at $C$. Segment $CB$ remains straight, and the residual at the tip is $\dfrac{10\cdot2^3}{3\cdot4\,000}+3\,\dfrac{10\cdot2^2}{2\cdot4\,000}-\dfrac{13}{600}=0$, in $\mathrm m$.

</div>
