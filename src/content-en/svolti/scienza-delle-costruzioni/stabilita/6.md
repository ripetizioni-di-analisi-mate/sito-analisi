## Exercise 1

The column shown is pinned at both ends in the plane of bending. Its length is $4\,\mathrm m$. The flexural rigidities of halves $AC$ and $CB$ are $4000$ and $8000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$, respectively. Using $\varphi(x)=\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$, calculate an upper bound for the first buckling load.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,2,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-0.88,0,P" quote="A,C,2 m,0.75;C,B,2 m,0.75" etichette="1,0.32,EI_1;3,0.32,EI_2" titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

Let $k=\dfrac{\pi}{L}$. Then $\varphi''=-k^2\sin(kx)$ and $\varphi'=k\cos(kx)$. By symmetry, the squared sine has equal integrals over the two halves.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{\dfrac L2}\sin^2(kx)\,dx&=\dfrac L4,\\[6pt]
\int_0^L\cos^2(kx)\,dx&=\dfrac L2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm R}
&=\dfrac{k^4(EI_1+EI_2)\dfrac L4}{k^2\dfrac L2}\\[6pt]
&=\dfrac{\pi^2(4000+8000)}{2\cdot16}\,\mathrm{kN}
=375\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stiffness jump changes the coefficient in the bending energy, so integrate separately over the two halves. The squared curvature has equal integrals over the two halves because the stiffness change occurs at the midpoint of the sinusoid. The result uses the arithmetic mean of the two flexural rigidities, weighting them equally.

**Check.** For the same mode shape, uniform rigidities $4000$ and $8000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$ give $250\pi^2$ and $500\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$, respectively. The difference between the variable-rigidity estimate and the mean of these two results is $(375-\dfrac{250+500}{2})\pi^2=0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The column shown is pinned at both ends in the plane of bending. Its length is $4\,\mathrm m$. The flexural rigidity is $EI=480\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. Evaluate the Rayleigh estimate using $\varphi=x(L-x)$ and compare it with Euler’s exact solution.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-0.88,0,P" quote="A,B,4 m,0.75"  titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

The parabola vanishes at $0$ and $L$. Its derivatives are $\varphi'=L-2x$ and $\varphi''=-2$. This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^L EI(\varphi'')^2\,dx&=4EI L,\\
\int_0^L(\varphi')^2\,dx&=\dfrac{L^3}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
P_{\mathrm R}=\dfrac{12 EI}{L^2}=360\,\mathrm{kN},
\qquad P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2EI}{L^2}=30\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The parabola satisfies the prescribed displacements, so it is an admissible trial shape. Its curvature is nonzero at the ends, so it differs from the exact mode shape of a pin-ended member. The estimated load exceeds the exact load, as the energy principle predicts.

**Check.** The difference is $30(12-\pi^2)\,\mathrm{kN}>0$. Evaluating the quotient instead with $\sin(\pi x/L)$ gives zero difference between the energy quotient and $30\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$.

</div>
