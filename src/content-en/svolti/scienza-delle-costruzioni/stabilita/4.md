## Exercise 1

The member shown has length $2.4\,\mathrm m$ and a rectangular cross section of width $40\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $z$ and depth $80\,\mathrm{mm}$ along $y$. Young’s modulus is $E=210\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. The ends are pinned in both planes. Calculate the lowest elastic buckling load and the corresponding average stress.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-0.65,0,P" quote="A,B,2.4 m" titolo="End conditions are the same in both principal planes"}

The second moments of area and the cross-sectional area are
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_y&=\dfrac{80\cdot40^3}{12}\,\mathrm{mm^4}
=\dfrac{1\,280\,000}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^4},\\[6pt]
I_z&=4 I_y,\qquad A=40\cdot80\,\mathrm{mm^2}=3200\,\mathrm{mm^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since the effective lengths are equal, bending about $y$ governs.
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{cr}}
&=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot210\,000\cdot I_y}{2400^2}\simeq153.53\,\mathrm{kN},\\[6pt]
\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}}
&=\dfrac{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}{A}\simeq47.98\,\mathrm{MPa}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second moment of area about axis $y$ depends on the cube of the width. Because the width is half the depth, $I_y$ is smaller than $I_z$. The governing load therefore produces displacement in the $z$ direction. With $E$ in $\mathrm{N/mm^2}$ and lengths in millimeters, the resulting force is in newtons.

**Check.** The radius of gyration satisfies $i_y^2=\dfrac{I_y}{A}=\dfrac{400}{3}\,\mathrm{mm^2}$. Consequently, $\lambda_y^2=43\,200$ and $\dfrac{\pi^2 E}{43\,200}\simeq47.98\,\mathrm{MPa}$, consistent with dividing the load by the area.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The member shown has length $4\,\mathrm m$ and $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Take $I_y=2\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$ and $I_z=6\cdot10^6\,\mathrm{mm^4}$. For bending about $y$, both end rotations are restrained. For bending about $z$, both ends are pinned. The ends are restrained against transverse displacement in both planes. Determine which plane governs buckling.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-0.88,0,P" quote="A,B,4 m,0.75"  titolo="Model in the plane where the end rotations are free"}

For bending about $y$, use $L_{\mathrm{eff},y}=2000\,\mathrm{mm}$. For bending about $z$, use $L_{\mathrm{eff},z}=4000\,\mathrm{mm}$. The corresponding loads are
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{cr},y}
&=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot200\,000\cdot2\cdot10^6}{2000^2}\,\mathrm N
=100\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN},\\[6pt]
P_{\mathrm{cr},z}
&=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot200\,000\cdot6\cdot10^6}{4000^2}\,\mathrm N
=75\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Bending about $z$ governs, with displacement in the $y$ direction.

<div class="spiegazione">

The second moment of area about $z$ is three times as large, but the effective length is twice that in the other plane. Squaring the effective length therefore introduces a factor of four. The critical load for bending about $z$ is consequently lower despite the larger second moment of area.

**Check.** Taking the ratio of the two second moments of area divided by their respective effective lengths squared gives $\dfrac{I_y}{I_z}\left(\dfrac{K_z}{K_y}\right)^2=\dfrac13\cdot4=\dfrac43$, the same ratio as for the calculated loads.

</div>
