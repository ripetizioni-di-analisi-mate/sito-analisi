## Exercise 1

In the plane of bending, the member shown is fixed at $A$ and free at $B$. Its length is $4\,\mathrm m$. The flexural rigidity is $EI=640\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. The tip load remains parallel to the undeformed axis. Calculate the first buckling load.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="B,-0.88,0,P" quote="A,B,4 m,0.75"  titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

The effective length of the cantilever is
$$
L_{\mathrm{eff}}=2L=8\,\mathrm m.
$$
This gives
$$
P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2 EI}{L_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}
=\dfrac{640\pi^2}{64}\,\mathrm{kN}
=10\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both displacement and rotation are free at the tip. The first mode therefore has its maximum displacement at the tip. An effective length of twice the actual length means that a pin-ended member of that length and the same flexural rigidity would have the same buckling load.

**Check.** Given $k=\dfrac{\pi}{8}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, take $v=C(1-\cos(kx))$. This gives $v''(4)=Ck^2\cos(\pi/2)=0$. Moreover, $v'''=-k^2v'$, so $EI v'''+P_{\mathrm{cr}}v'=(P_{\mathrm{cr}}-EI k^2)v'=0$ also holds at the tip.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the plane of bending, the member shown is fixed at $A$ and guided at $B$. Its length is $6\,\mathrm m$. The flexural rigidity is $EI=900\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. Both transverse displacement and rotation are restrained at $B$. Axial translation remains free. Calculate the first buckling load.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,doppio-pendolo,270" forze="B,-1.32,0,P" quote="A,B,6 m,0.75"  titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

Transverse displacement and rotation vanish at both ends. Therefore use
$$
L_{\mathrm{eff}}=\dfrac{L}{2}=3\,\mathrm m.
$$
This gives
$$
P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot900}{3^2}\,\mathrm{kN}
=100\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide transmits a transverse force and a couple while permitting axial translation. The flexural boundary conditions are those of a member fixed at both ends. In the first mode, the middle portion bends in the opposite direction to the portions near the ends.

**Check.** Use $v=C(1-\cos(kx))$ with $k=\dfrac{\pi}{3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. At $B$, this gives $v(6)=C(1-\cos 2\pi)=0$ and $v'(6)=Ck\sin 2\pi=0$. The governing equation is satisfied because $v^{(4)}+k^2v''=0$.

</div>
