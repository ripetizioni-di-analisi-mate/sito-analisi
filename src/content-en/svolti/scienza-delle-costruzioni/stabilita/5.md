## Exercise 1

The column shown is pinned at both ends in the plane of bending. Its length is $3\,\mathrm m$. The given properties are $E=210\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$, $i=25\,\mathrm{mm}$, and an elastic limit $200\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Calculate the slenderness ratio and determine whether the average Euler buckling stress lies within this limit.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-0.66,0,P" quote="A,B,3 m,0.75"  titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

The effective length equals the actual member length. Calculate
$$
\lambda=\dfrac{3000}{25}=120,
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}}
=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot210\,000}{120^2}\,\mathrm{MPa}
=\dfrac{175\pi^2}{12}\,\mathrm{MPa}
\simeq143.93\,\mathrm{MPa}.
$$
The corresponding average stress is within the specified limit.

<div class="spiegazione">

The slenderness ratio is dimensionless, so both lengths must use the same units. Compare the limit with the average stress at which the model predicts buckling. At this stress, the result is consistent with the assumed elastic behavior.

**Check.** Keeping the area $A$ symbolic, substitute $I=A(25\,\mathrm{mm})^2$ into the load formula. The residual $\dfrac{P_{\mathrm{cr}}}{A}-\dfrac{\pi^2 E}{120^2}$ vanishes because $\dfrac{25^2}{3000^2}=\dfrac{1}{120^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The cantilever shown is fixed at $A$ and free at $B$. The tip load remains parallel to the undeformed axis. The member has $i=30\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $E=200\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$. Find the actual length for which the average Euler buckling stress is $200\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" forze="B,-1.2,0,P" quote="A,B,L" titolo="Length L is the unknown geometric dimension of the cantilever"}

For a cantilever, $L_{\mathrm{eff}}=2L$. From the specified stress,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda^2&=\dfrac{\pi^2\cdot200\,000}{200}=1000\pi^2,\\[6pt]
L&=\dfrac{i\lambda}{2}
=15\pi\sqrt{1000}\,\mathrm{mm}\\[4pt]
&=150\pi\sqrt{10}\,\mathrm{mm}\simeq1.490\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required quantity is the actual length of the member. The effective length is the radius of gyration times the slenderness ratio. Divide it by two to obtain the actual length. Take the positive value because length is positive.

**Check.** Keeping area $A$ symbolic, calculate $P_{\mathrm{cr}}=\dfrac{\pi^2 E A\cdot30^2}{4(150\pi\sqrt{10})^2}=200 A$ with forces in newtons and area in square millimeters. The result recovers the specified stress.

</div>
