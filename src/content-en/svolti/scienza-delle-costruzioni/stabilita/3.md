## Exercise 1

The column shown is pinned at both ends in the plane of bending. Its length is $8\,\mathrm m$. The flexural rigidity is $EI=512\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. Calculate the load for the third mode and the positions of its interior nodes. Measure $x$ from $A$.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,8,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-1.76,0,P" quote="A,B,8 m,0.75"  titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

For the third mode, $k_3=\dfrac{3\pi}{L}$. This gives
$$
P_3=EI k_3^2
=\dfrac{9\cdot512\pi^2}{64}\,\mathrm{kN}
=72\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$
The interior zeros satisfy $k_3 x=j\pi$, with $j=1,2$. Therefore
$$
x_1=\dfrac83\,\mathrm m,\qquad x_2=\dfrac{16}{3}\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The third mode contains three half-waves of equal length. The junction between successive half-waves is a node, a section of zero transverse displacement. Its wave number is three times the fundamental wave number, so its load is nine times the fundamental load.

**Check.** The two coordinates divide the member into three segments of length $\dfrac83\,\mathrm m$. Substitution into the mode shape gives $\sin\pi=0$ and $\sin2\pi=0$, respectively.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The pin-ended member shown has length $6\,\mathrm m$ and flexural rigidity $EI=180\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. An ideal brace at midlength allows rotation and prevents transverse displacement. The member is continuous through the brace. Calculate the first buckling load with the brace in place.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,3,0;B,6,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello;B,carrello" forze="B,-1.2,0,P" quote="A,C,3 m;C,B,3 m" titolo="The central brace restrains transverse displacement while the member remains continuous"}

The first mode of the unbraced member has its maximum displacement at midlength. The brace excludes that mode. The second mode $v=C\sin\!\left(\dfrac{2\pi x}{L}\right)$, however, satisfies $v(\dfrac L2)=0$. Therefore
$$
P_{\mathrm{cr}}=4\dfrac{\pi^2 EI}{L^2}
=4\dfrac{180\pi^2}{36}\,\mathrm{kN}
=20\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$
To establish that this is the lowest load, consider the two halves separately. Admissible shapes vanish at the ends of each half, so the energy quotient for each segment is at least $\dfrac{\pi^2EI}{(\dfrac L2)^2}$. The same inequality holds when the contributions of the two halves are combined. The second mode attains that value.

<div class="spiegazione">

The brace adds a geometric condition without interrupting the member at midlength. The two halves of this mode meet with the same slope. Zero moment at midlength is a property of this mode. The brace prescribes only the displacement.

**Check.** With $k=\dfrac{\pi}{3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, the value at $C$ is $v'(3)=Ck\cos\pi=-Ck$ on both sides. In addition, $EI v''(3)=-EI k^2 C\sin\pi=0$.

</div>
