## Exercise 1

The column shown is pinned at both ends in the plane of bending. Its length is $5\,\mathrm m$. The flexural rigidity is $EI=500\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. Calculate the first buckling load and identify the section of maximum deflection in the corresponding mode.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-1.1,0,P" quote="A,B,5 m,0.75"  titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

The exact shape function for the first mode is $v=C\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)$, with $C>0$. The wave number is $k=\dfrac{\pi}{L}$. Substituting $L=5\,\mathrm m$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{cr}}
&=EI\left(\dfrac{\pi}{L}\right)^2\\[5pt]
&=\dfrac{500\pi^2}{25}\,\mathrm{kN}\\[5pt]
&=20\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Deflection is greatest halfway along the half-wave, at $x=2.5\,\mathrm m$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The pins prevent end displacement but allow both ends to rotate freely. The first sinusoidal mode that vanishes at both ends consists of a single half-wave. For a sinusoidal mode, curvature is proportional to deflection. Equilibrium therefore requires an axial force proportional to the square of the wave number.

**Check.** At midlength, $v=C$ and $v''=-\dfrac{\pi^2}{25}C$ in the units used here. The residual is $EI v''+P_{\mathrm{cr}}v=-20\pi^2 C+20\pi^2 C=0$. The bending moment also vanishes at the supports because $v''$ is proportional to $v$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The column shown is pinned at both ends in the plane of bending. Its length is $6\,\mathrm m$. The specified lowest elastic buckling load is $30\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}$. Calculate the flexural rigidity $EI$ and find the critical load when the same member is shortened to $3\,\mathrm m$.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="B,-1.32,0,P" quote="A,B,6 m,0.75"  titolo="Compression member and its flexural end restraints"}

Solve Euler’s relation for $EI$.
$$
EI=\dfrac{P_{\mathrm{cr}}L^2}{\pi^2}
=\dfrac{30\pi^2\cdot36}{\pi^2}\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}
=1080\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}.
$$
The end restraints and flexural rigidity are unchanged, so the critical load ratio is the square of the inverse length ratio.
$$
\dfrac{P_{\mathrm{cr},3}}{P_{\mathrm{cr},6}}=
\left(\dfrac63\right)^2=4,
\qquad P_{\mathrm{cr},3}=120\pi^2\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Flexural rigidity has units of force times length squared. Halving the span fits the half-wave of the same mode into half the distance. The wave number doubles and the critical load quadruples.

**Check.** For the segment of length $3\,\mathrm m$, the root of the modal equation is $k=\sqrt{\dfrac{120\pi^2}{1080}}=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ in $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. The condition at the second support gives $\sin(3k)=\sin\pi=0$.

</div>
