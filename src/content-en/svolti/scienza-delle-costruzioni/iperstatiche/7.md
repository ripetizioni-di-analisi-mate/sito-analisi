## Exercise 1

A bar of length $2\,\mathrm m$ and cross-sectional area $800\,\mathrm{mm^2}$ is restrained axially at both ends. Its Young’s modulus is $210\,000\,\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\alpha=12\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Find the axial force for a uniform temperature increase of $35\,\mathrm K$ while the bar remains straight.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,incastro,0" quote="A,B,2 m" etichette="1,0.32,ΔT" titolo="Uniform heating with both ends restrained"}

The total longitudinal strain is zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{N}{EA}+\alpha\Delta T&=0,\\[5pt]
N&=-210\,000\cdot800\cdot12\cdot10^{-6}\cdot35\,\mathrm N\\[5pt]
&=-70.56\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bar would expand freely if heated without restraint. An elastic strain of the opposite sign is required to maintain the distance between the supports. The resulting axial force is therefore compressive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bar restrained axially at both ends consists of a segment $AB$ of length $2\,\mathrm m$ with $EA_1=80\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$ and a segment $BC$ of length $3\,\mathrm m$ with $EA_2=60\,000\,\mathrm{kN}$. The free thermal strains are $4\cdot10^{-4}$ and $2\cdot10^{-4}$. Find the axial force and the displacement of joint $B$.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0;C,5,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,incastro,180;C,incastro,0" quote="A,B,2 m;B,C,3 m" etichette="1,0.45,segment 1;3.5,0.45,segment 2" titolo="Two segments heated by different amounts"}

The axial force is constant along the bar because no external axial force acts at the joint. Compatibility applies to the total length.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N\left(\frac{2}{80\,000}+\frac{3}{60\,000}\right)
+2(4\cdot10^{-4})+3(2\cdot10^{-4})&=0,\\[5pt]
N&=-\frac{56}{3}\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
u_B=\left[\frac{-\frac{56}{3}\cdot2}{80\,000}+2(4\cdot10^{-4})\right]\mathrm m
=\frac13\,\mathrm{mm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each segment’s thermal expansion depends on its length and its free thermal strain. The common axial force produces different elastic shortenings because the segments have different axial rigidities. The first segment elongates by one-third of a millimeter, so $B$ moves to the right even though the ends remain stationary.

</div>

**Independent check**

Calculate the net shortening of the second segment. Adding this signed change in meters to that of the first segment restores the prescribed distance between the ends.

$$
\frac1{3\,000}+\frac{-\frac{56}{3}\cdot3}{60\,000}+3(2\cdot10^{-4})=0.
$$
