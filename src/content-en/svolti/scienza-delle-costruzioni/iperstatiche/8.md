## Exercise 1

A propped beam of span $4\,\mathrm m$ has $EI=12\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$, depth $h=0.20\,\mathrm m$, and $\alpha=10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. A linear temperature variation through the depth makes the top fiber warmer than the bottom fiber by $24\,\mathrm K$. Calculate the reaction of the stationary prop.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m" etichette="2,0.47,ΔT_sup;2,-0.36,ΔT_inf" titolo="Stationary prop and temperature gradient through the depth"}

Taking $v$ as positive downward gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_T&=\frac{10^{-5}\cdot24}{0.20}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}
=0.0012\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},\\[5pt]
v_T(L)&=\frac{\kappa_T L^2}{2}=0.0096\,\mathrm m,\\[5pt]
R_B&=\frac{3EI}{L^3}v_T(L)=5.4\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The top fiber expands more than the bottom fiber, so the free curvature produces a positive downward deflection. The prop reaction must cancel that deflection and therefore acts upward. The temperature gradient contributes an imposed strain even in the absence of an external transverse load.

</div>

**Independent check**

Use the total bending moment $M=5.4(4-x)$ and integrate the full curvature $v''=\kappa_T-\dfrac{M}{EI}$ with the two conditions at the fixed end. The resulting tip deflection is

$$
\int_0^4(4-x)\left(0.0012-\frac{5.4(4-x)}{12\,000}\right)\mathrm dx
=0.0096-0.0096=0.
$$

## Exercise 2

A propped cantilever of span $4\,\mathrm m$ has $EI=12\,000\,\mathrm{kN\,m^2}$. The beam carries a uniform downward load $6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ and experiences a temperature gradient producing free curvature $\kappa_T=-10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$, with deflection positive downward. The tip support settles by $2\,\mathrm{mm}$. Calculate the vertical support reaction.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.65,0.65,q" quote="A,B,4 m" frecce="4.55,0.12,4.55,-0.28,s" titolo="Uniform load, thermal curvature, and settlement at B"}

With the prop removed, the load and the temperature gradient produce tip deflections of

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_q&=\frac{6\cdot4^4}{8\cdot12\,000}\,\mathrm m=0.016\,\mathrm m,\\[5pt]
v_T&=\frac{-10^{-3}\cdot4^2}{2}\,\mathrm m=-0.008\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.016-0.008-\frac{4^3}{3\cdot12\,000}R_B&=0.002,\\[5pt]
R_B&=3.375\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load deflects the tip downward, and the temperature gradient raises it partway back. The prop reaction must account for the difference between the free deflection and the prescribed settlement. The free deflection exceeds the settlement, so the reaction acts upward.

</div>

**Independent check**

Equilibrium at $A$ gives $V_A=20.625\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $M_A=34.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Check the moment balance about $B$. The temperature gradient changes the strains without adding an external force. The residual, in $\mathrm{kN\,m}$, is

$$
34.5-20.625\cdot4+24\cdot2=0.
$$
