## Exercise 1

A prismatic beam of length $6\,\mathrm m$ is fixed at $A$ and supported at $B$ and $C$, at distances $3\,\mathrm m$ and $6\,\mathrm m$ from $A$. The uniform load acts downward with intensity $7\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Find the two support reactions.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello;C,carrello" carichi="A,C,0.7,0.7,q" quote="A,B,3 m;B,C,3 m" titolo="Cantilever with supports at B and C"}

Remove both supports and apply upward unit loads. The first moment diagram is $m_B=3-x$ on $AB$ and zero on $BC$. The second is $m_C=6-x$ over the full span. The actual bending moment is $M_0=-\dfrac72(6-x)^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
EI f_{BB}&=\int_0^3(3-x)^2\,\mathrm dx=9\,\mathrm{m^3},\\[5pt]
EI f_{BC}&=\int_0^3(3-x)(6-x)\,\mathrm dx=\frac{45}{2}\,\mathrm{m^3},\\[5pt]
EI f_{CC}&=\int_0^6(6-x)^2\,\mathrm dx=72\,\mathrm{m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-EI\delta_{0B}&=\frac72\int_0^3(6-x)^2(3-x)\,\mathrm dx
=\frac{3\,213}{8}\,\mathrm{kN\,m^3},\\[5pt]
-EI\delta_{0C}&=\frac72\int_0^6(6-x)^3\,\mathrm dx
=1\,134\,\mathrm{kN\,m^3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
9R_B+\dfrac{45}{2}R_C=\dfrac{3\,213}{8},\\[3pt]
\dfrac{45}{2}R_B+72R_C=1\,134.
\end{cases}
\qquad
R_B=24\,\mathrm{kN},\quad R_C=8.25\,\mathrm{kN}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each of the two reactions produces displacement at both supports. Only $AB$ contributes to the reciprocal flexibility because the $B$ diagram is zero on the remaining segment. Equilibrium gives $V_A=9.75\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $M_A=4.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

</div>

**Independent check**

Sum moments about $C$. The load resultant is $42\,\mathrm{kN}$ and its moment arm is $3\,\mathrm m$. The residual, in $\mathrm{kN\,m}$, is

$$
4.5-9.75\cdot6-24\cdot3+42\cdot3=0.
$$

## Exercise 2

A prismatic beam of span $4\,\mathrm m$ is fixed at both ends and carries a uniform downward load $9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Find the internal bending moments at the ends and the vertical reactions.
::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,incastro,0" carichi="A,B,0.65,0.65,q" quote="A,B,4 m" titolo="Beam fixed at both ends"}

Release both end rotations to obtain a simply supported beam. By symmetry, the internal bending moments at both ends are $M_e$. The total bending moment is $M=M_e+\dfrac q2x(L-x)$. The difference between the final end rotations is zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= \int_0^L M\,\mathrm dx
=M_e L+\frac{qL^3}{12},\\[5pt]
M_e&=-\frac{qL^2}{12}=-12\,\mathrm{kN\,m},\\[5pt]
V_A&=V_B=\frac{qL}{2}=18\,\mathrm{kN}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The external end couples act in opposite directions, so they contribute a constant internal bending moment. By symmetry, the end rotations have opposite signs. If their difference is zero, both rotations must vanish. Negative internal moments indicate tension in the top fibers.

</div>
