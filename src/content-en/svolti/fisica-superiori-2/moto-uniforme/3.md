## Exercise 1

An object moves from $20\,\mathrm{m}$ to $8\,\mathrm{m}$ in $4\,\mathrm{s}$. What is its signed average velocity?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x &= 8-20 = -12\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
v_m &= \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{-12}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s} \simeq -3\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=5 ymin=4 ymax=24 punti="0,20,pieno,A;4,8,pieno,B" segmenti="0,20,4,8;0,8,4,8" nomi="t (s),x (m)" titolo="The dashed segment from A to B joins the two readings and has slope −3 m/s."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The endpoint positions and the duration are enough to find the average velocity. Subtract the initial position from the final position to get $-12\,\mathrm{m}$, then divide by $4\,\mathrm{s}$. The quotient is negative since the final coordinate is lower than the initial coordinate. Average speed would additionally require the distance along the entire route.

The segment in the graph joins the two recorded positions. Its slope is negative because the coordinate falls by twelve metres in four seconds. If the body reversed between readings, the slope between the endpoints would give the same average even though the actual position graph would have a different shape.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A trolley is recorded at $x=17\,\mathrm m$ at $t=6\,\mathrm s$. A second station uses an axis pointing the same way as the $x$ axis, with its origin at $x=10\,\mathrm m$. Its coordinate at $t=14\,\mathrm s$ is $\xi=-5\,\mathrm m$. What is the signed average velocity in the first reference frame?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_f &=10\,\mathrm m+\xi=5\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
\Delta x &= (5-17)\,\mathrm m=-12\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
v_m &=\frac{-12\,\mathrm m}{(14-6)\,\mathrm s}=-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before subtracting, express both coordinates in the same reference frame. The second station's origin is ten metres from the first. Since the axes point the same way, the second station's negative reading puts the trolley five metres before its origin, at $x=5\,\mathrm m$. The displacement is $-12\,\mathrm m$ over eight seconds. The average is therefore negative. To check, in the second frame the initial coordinate would be $\xi_i=17-10=7\,\mathrm m$ and the difference $-5-7=-12\,\mathrm m$. Both frames give the same average velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cart is at $x=20\,\mathrm{m}$ at $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$, turns around at $x=26\,\mathrm{m}$ and at $11\,\mathrm{s}$ reaches $x=8\,\mathrm{m}$. Assume no other turn. What are its average velocity and average speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}v_m &= \frac{8-20}{11-3}\,\mathrm{m/s}=-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]u_m &= \frac{(26-20)+(26-8)}{11-3}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interval from 3 s to 11 s lasts 8 s. The final coordinate is 12 m lower than the initial one. Dividing this signed displacement by elapsed time gives the negative average velocity. For the average speed add the 6 m outward leg and the 18 m return leg, assuming there is only one reversal. The total distance is 24 m. Its ratio to duration is positive.

The averages differ because they describe different outcomes. Over eight seconds the net displacement of $-12\,\mathrm m$ is equivalent to a constant velocity of $-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Covering twenty-four metres in that time instead requires a constant speed of $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The first preserves the change in position, and the second preserves the length of the route.

</div>
