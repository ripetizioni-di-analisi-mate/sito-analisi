## Exercise 1

A cart has initial position $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and constant velocity $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What coordinate does it occupy after $2\,\mathrm{s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t &= 2-0 = 2\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta x &= v\Delta t = (3)\cdot 2 = 6\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
x_f &= x_0+\Delta x = 3+(6) = 9\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the uniform-motion equation because the velocity is constant. The interval lasts $2\,\mathrm{s}$. The product of the velocity and this duration gives displacement $6\,\mathrm{m}$. The coordinate increases by $3\,\mathrm{m}$ each second. It starts at $3\,\mathrm{m}$, so the final coordinate $9\,\mathrm{m}$ includes both the known position and the change.

To check the final coordinate, work back to the initial point. The change over two seconds is six metres, so $9-6=3\,\mathrm m$. This distinguishes the final position from the six metres travelled during the observation. Position is measured relative to the origin, while these six metres are measured from the body's starting point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart has initial position $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and constant velocity $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What coordinate does it occupy after $3\,\mathrm{s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t &= 3-0 = 3\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta x &= v\Delta t = (-4)\cdot 3 = -12\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
x_f &= x_0+\Delta x = 6+(-12) = -6\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the uniform-motion equation because the velocity is constant. The interval lasts $3\,\mathrm{s}$. The product of the velocity and this duration gives displacement $-12\,\mathrm{m}$. The negative slope means that in this interval the cart moves towards decreasing coordinates. The final value is $-6\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance travelled is $12\,\mathrm{m}$ because the direction stays the same.

The trolley crosses the origin before the final reading. Moving from six metres to zero at four metres per second takes $\dfrac{6}{4}\,\mathrm s=1.5\,\mathrm s$. In the remaining second and a half it travels another six metres in the negative direction. The two legs confirm that the final coordinate is the opposite of the starting coordinate.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sensor records a cart at $9\,\mathrm{m}$ at time $3\,\mathrm{s}$. Its constant velocity is $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Where is it at $7\,\mathrm{s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t &= 7-3 = 4\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta x &= v\Delta t = (5)\cdot 4 = 20\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
x_f &= x_0+\Delta x = 9+(20) = 29\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the uniform-motion equation because the velocity is constant. The interval lasts $4\,\mathrm{s}$. The product of the velocity and this duration gives displacement $20\,\mathrm{m}$. The coordinate increases by $5\,\mathrm{m}$ each second. It starts at $9\,\mathrm{m}$, so the final coordinate $29\,\mathrm{m}$ includes both the known position and the change.

On the graph, I and P have time coordinates $3$ and $7\,\mathrm s$. The ratio of their position change to their time interval is $\dfrac{29-9}{7-3}\,\mathrm{m/s}=5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Using seven seconds as the duration would include time before the first measurement and give a position of $44\,\mathrm m$.

</div>
