## Exercise 1

A vehicle maintains a speed of $72\,\mathrm{km/h}$. Express this value in $\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= 72\,\frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{h}} = \frac{72\cdot1000}{3600}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
&= 20\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert the kilometre in the numerator to 1000 metres and the hour in the denominator to 3600 seconds. The ratio of the conversion factors is $\dfrac{1000}{3600}$, so the numerical value decreases. Travelling for one hour at the converted speed would reproduce the distance in kilometres given by the initial speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A vehicle maintains a speed of $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Express this value in $\mathrm{km/h}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= 12\,\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}=\frac{12}{1000}\cdot3600\,\mathrm{km/h} \\[6pt]
&= 43.2\,\mathrm{km/h}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert both the distance and the time. There are 3600 seconds in an hour, so the distance in metres is 3600 times the distance covered in one second. Dividing by 1000 expresses the same distance in kilometres. The result in kilometres per hour is the distance in kilometres that would be travelled in one hour.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two treadmills report $14.4\,\mathrm{km/h}$ and $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ respectively. What is the difference in speed, second minus first, in SI units?

$$
\begin{aligned}u_1 &= \frac{14.4\cdot1000}{3600}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]u_2-u_1 &= (5-4)\,\mathrm{m/s}=1\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare speeds, express them in the same unit. The first is 4 m/s because each kilometre gives 1000 metres and the denominator is 3600 seconds per hour. The second is already in metres per second. The result means the second treadmill covers an additional metre every second.

</div>
