## Exercise 1

A runner covers $120\,\mathrm{m}$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and another $120\,\mathrm{m}$ at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the average speed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_1 &= \frac{120}{4}\,\mathrm{s} = 30\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
t_2 &= \frac{120}{6}\,\mathrm{s} = 20\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
u &= \frac{2\cdot 120}{\frac{120}{4}+\frac{120}{6}}\,\mathrm{m/s}= 4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first leg takes $30\,\mathrm{s}$ and the second $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Average speed is the ratio of $240\,\mathrm{m}$ to $50\,\mathrm{s}$. Because the durations differ, the slower leg carries more weight and brings the result closer to the lower speed.

The value $4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is closer to four than to six. The first leg takes $\dfrac{30}{50}$ of the time and the second $\dfrac{20}{50}$. Weighting them gives the same average, $\dfrac{30}{50}\cdot4+\dfrac{20}{50}\cdot6$. Equal lengths do not give equal time weights.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two athletes cover the same $48\,\mathrm m$ route, divided into two $24\,\mathrm m$ stages. A holds speeds of $4$ and $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. B holds speeds of $6$ and $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Neither stops. Who has the greater average speed, and what is it?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_A&=\left(\frac{24}{4}+\frac{24}{12}\right)\,\mathrm s=8\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
t_B&=\left(\frac{24}{6}+\frac{24}{8}\right)\,\mathrm s=7\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
u_A&=\frac{48}{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}=6\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[4pt]
u_B&=\frac{48}{7}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq6.86\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=0 ymax=51 linee="0,0 6,24 8,48;0,0 4,24 7,48" segmenti="0,48,8,48;6,0,6,24;7,0,7,48;8,0,8,48" punti="6,24,pieno;4,24,pieno;7,48,pieno;8,48,pieno" etichette="6.65,28,A;5.25,37,B" nomi="t (s),d (m)" titolo="B reaches the distance of 48 m one second before A."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The athletes cover the same distance. To find their times, divide the length of each stage by its speed. A spends six seconds on the first half, three times the duration of the second. B's durations are four and three seconds. B's total time is shorter, so B has the greater average speed. The arithmetic mean of the two speeds would give $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for A and $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for B. That comparison weights both stages equally even though the time spent at the lower speed differs from the time spent at the higher speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A relay runner covers two legs of equal length. The first speed is $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the average speed over the entire route is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What constant speed does the runner keep on the second leg?

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{u_2} &= \frac{2}{u_m}-\frac{1}{u_1}\\[6pt]&=\left(\frac{2}{6}-\frac{1}{8}\right)\mathrm{s/m}=\frac{5}{24}\,\mathrm{s/m}\\[6pt]u_2 &= \frac{24}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let d be the common length. At the stated average speed, total time is 2d divided by 6 m/s. The first leg takes d divided by 8 m/s. Subtract to find the remaining time. Divide d by the remaining time; d cancels, so the same speed follows for any positive length. It is below 6 m/s, as expected to balance the faster first leg.

A numerical example checks the answer without symbolic cancellation. With two $24\,\mathrm m$ legs, the required total time is $8\,\mathrm s$. The first leg takes $3\,\mathrm s$, so five remain. Covering the second leg in five seconds requires $\dfrac{24}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, matching the calculated result.

</div>
