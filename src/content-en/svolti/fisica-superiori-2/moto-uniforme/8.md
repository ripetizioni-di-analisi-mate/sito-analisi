## Exercise 1

A runner covers $120\,\mathrm{m}$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and another $120\,\mathrm{m}$ at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the average speed?

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=50.0 ymin=0 ymax=244 linee="0,0 30.000000,120 50.000000,240" nomi="t (s),d (m)" titolo="The two increases in distance are equal, while the durations differ."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_1 &= \frac{120}{4}\,\mathrm{s} \simeq 30\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
t_2 &= \frac{120}{6}\,\mathrm{s} \simeq 20\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
v_m &= \frac{2\cdot 120}{\frac{120}{4}+\frac{120}{6}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq 4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first leg takes $30\,\mathrm{s}$ and the second $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Average speed is the ratio of $240\,\mathrm{m}$ to $50\,\mathrm{s}$. Because the durations differ, the slower leg carries more weight and brings the result closer to the lower speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A runner covers $180\,\mathrm{m}$ at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and another $180\,\mathrm{m}$ at $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the average speed?

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=56.0 ymin=0 ymax=364 linee="0,0 36.000000,180 56.000000,360" nomi="t (s),d (m)" titolo="The two increases in distance are equal, while the durations differ."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_1 &= \frac{180}{5}\,\mathrm{s} \simeq 36\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
t_2 &= \frac{180}{9}\,\mathrm{s} \simeq 20\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
v_m &= \frac{2\cdot 180}{\frac{180}{5}+\frac{180}{9}}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq 6.4286\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first leg takes $36\,\mathrm{s}$ and the second $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Average speed is the ratio of $360\,\mathrm{m}$ to $56\,\mathrm{s}$. Because the durations differ, the slower leg carries more weight and brings the result closer to the lower speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A relay runner covers two legs of equal length. The first speed is $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the average speed over the entire route is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What constant speed does the runner keep on the second leg?

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac{1}{u_2} &= \frac{2}{u_m}-\frac{1}{u_1}\\[6pt]&=\left(\frac{2}{6}-\frac{1}{8}\right)\mathrm{s/m}=\frac{5}{24}\,\mathrm{s/m}\\[6pt]u_2 &= \frac{24}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s}=4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let d be the common length. At the stated average speed, total time is 2d divided by 6 m/s. The first leg takes d divided by 8 m/s. Subtract to find the remaining time. Divide d by the remaining time; d cancels, so the same speed follows for any positive length. It is below 6 m/s, as expected to balance the faster first leg.

</div>
