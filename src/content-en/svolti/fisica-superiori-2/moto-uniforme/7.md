## Exercise 1

A sledge moves at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $6\,\mathrm{s}$ and then at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $4\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the total displacement?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x_1 &= 3\cdot 6 = 18\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta x_2 &= -2\cdot 4 = -8\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta x &= (18)+(-8) = 10\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=-4 ymax=5 linee="0,3 6,3;6,-2 10,-2" segmenti="6,0,6,3;6,0,6,-2;10,0,10,-2" etichette="2.7,1.53,+18 m;7.55,-1.28,−8 m" nomi="t (s),v (m/s)" titolo="The rectangle above the axis contributes 18 m, and the one below it −8 m."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The displacement on the first leg is $18\,\mathrm{m}$ and on the second it is $-8\,\mathrm{m}$. Their signed sum is $10\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance is the sum of the magnitudes, giving $26\,\mathrm{m}$.

On the velocity–time graph the rectangles measure the two contributions. The first six seconds are above the axis and give $18\,\mathrm m$, while the later rectangle lies below and gives $-8\,\mathrm m$. The sled ends on the positive side of its starting point, although it moves in the negative direction at the end.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sledge moves at $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $4\,\mathrm{s}$ and then at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $9\,\mathrm{s}$. How far does it travel in total?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x_1 &= -3\cdot 4 = -12\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
\Delta x_2 &= 2\cdot 9 = 18\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
d &= \lvert -12\rvert + \lvert 18\rvert = 30\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The displacement on the first leg is $-12\,\mathrm{m}$ and on the second it is $18\,\mathrm{m}$. Their signed sum is $6\,\mathrm{m}$. The distance is the sum of the magnitudes, giving $30\,\mathrm{m}$.

To picture the route, take the starting point as the origin. The first leg ends at $-12\,\mathrm m$, and the second takes the sled to $6\,\mathrm m$. The second leg crosses the origin after twelve of its eighteen metres. All eighteen count towards distance, including the metres that take the sled back near its starting point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sled moves at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $3\,\mathrm s$, stops, then moves at $-5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ for $6\,\mathrm s$. Its average velocity for the whole interval is $-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long did it stop?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=[4\cdot3+(-5)\cdot6]\,\mathrm m=-18\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
\Delta t&=\frac{-18\,\mathrm m}{-1.5\,\mathrm{m/s}}=12\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
t_p&=(12-3-6)\,\mathrm s=3\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During the stop the position does not change. Total displacement thus comes from the two moving stages and is negative because the second contribution has the larger magnitude. The given average includes the stop, so displacement and average velocity determine the total duration. Three seconds remain after subtracting the nine seconds spent in motion. Using the distance of forty-two metres in the numerator would not give the requested duration because distance enters the average speed. Over twelve seconds that speed would be $\dfrac{42}{12}\,\mathrm{m/s}=3.5\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>
