## Exercise 1

A cyclist travels $180\,\mathrm{m}$ in a straight line at constant speed $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t &= \frac{d}{u} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{180}{6}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 30\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both quantities are already in SI units. At $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the cyclist covers $6\,\mathrm{m}$ each second, so divide the distance by the speed to get $30\,\mathrm{s}$. Multiplying the exact time by $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives the original distance $180\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $40\,\mathrm{m}$ train crosses an $86\,\mathrm{m}$ bridge at $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long elapses from the front entering to the rear fully leaving?

$$
t=\frac{86+40}{9}\,\mathrm{s}=14\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At first the front is at the entrance and the rear is 40 m behind. When the front reaches the exit, the rear is still on the bridge. The crossing ends after the train advances another 40 m. Divide the sum of the lengths by 9 m/s. The result exceeds the time for the front alone, since the train has non-zero length.

</div>
