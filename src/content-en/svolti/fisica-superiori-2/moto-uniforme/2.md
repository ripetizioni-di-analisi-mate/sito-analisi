## Exercise 1

A cyclist travels $180\,\mathrm{m}$ in a straight line at constant speed $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta t &= \frac{d}{u} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{180}{6}\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 30\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both quantities are already in SI units. At $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ the cyclist covers $6\,\mathrm{m}$ each second, so divide the distance by the speed to get $30\,\mathrm{s}$. Multiplying the exact time by $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives the original distance $180\,\mathrm{m}$.

A check halfway through makes the ratio concrete. In fifteen seconds the cyclist covers $6\cdot15=90\,\mathrm m$, exactly half the required distance. Since the speed is unchanged, the remaining ninety metres take another fifteen seconds. The result is an elapsed interval and is independent of the initial clock reading.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A probe passes $x=-14\,\mathrm m$ when the clock reads $6\,\mathrm s$. It continues in the positive direction at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take to reach $x=46\,\mathrm m$, and what does the clock read then?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x &= [46-(-14)]\,\mathrm m=60\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
\Delta t &= \frac{60\,\mathrm m}{5\,\mathrm{m/s}}=12\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
t_f &=6\,\mathrm s+12\,\mathrm s=18\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=5 xmax=20 ymin=-24 ymax=56 f="5*x-44" punti="6,-14,pieno,A;18,46,pieno,B" segmenti="6,-14,18,-14;18,-14,18,46" nomi="t (s),x (m)" titolo="The span of 12 s is the difference between the two clock readings, 6 s and 18 s."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first measurement occurs while the clock is already running. The distance to cover equals the positive displacement because the probe keeps moving in the same direction. Subtract the initial coordinate from the final one. Subtracting a negative coordinate adds the fourteen metres before the origin to the forty-six metres beyond it. The probe takes twelve seconds to travel the sixty metres. The final clock reading is eighteen seconds because twelve elapsed seconds are added to the six already shown. Checking the position equation at $t=18\,\mathrm s$ gives $-14+5(18-6)=46$, in metres.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $40\,\mathrm{m}$ train crosses an $86\,\mathrm{m}$ bridge at $9\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long elapses from the front entering to the rear fully leaving?

$$
t=\frac{86+40}{9}\,\mathrm{s}=14\,\mathrm{s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At first the front is at the entrance and the rear is 40 m behind. When the front reaches the exit, the rear is still on the bridge. The crossing ends after the train advances another 40 m. Divide the sum of the lengths by 9 m/s. The result exceeds the time for the front alone, since the train has non-zero length.

A direct check follows the rear of the train. With the entrance as the origin, its initial position is $-40\,\mathrm m$. At the end it must be at $86\,\mathrm m$, the exit. Its displacement is therefore $86-(-40)=126\,\mathrm m$, the same total distance the front covers by that instant.

</div>
