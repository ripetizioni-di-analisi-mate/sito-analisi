## Exercise 1

A block of mass $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides on a horizontal surface with kinetic friction coefficient $0.2$. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the magnitude of friction.

::grafico{xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.3 ymin=-3.05 ymax=3.05 linee="-3,-0.56 3,-0.56" vettori="0,0,0.0000,-2.5000,mg;0,0,0.0000,2.5000,N;0,0,-0.5000,0.0000,f" uguale=1 assi=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="If the block slides rightward, friction points leftward."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
N &= mg-F_y=50-0=50\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
f_d &= \mu_d N=0.2\cdot 50\,\mathrm{N}=10\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The block is sliding, so use the kinetic coefficient. First find the normal force by adding the vertical forces, where acceleration is zero. Weight is $50\,\mathrm{N}$ and the upwards pull in addition to the normal force is $0\,\mathrm{N}$, so the contact supplies $50\,\mathrm{N}$. The coefficient is dimensionless, so the product is in newtons. Friction points against sliding.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ block slides right along a horizontal surface with $\mu_d=0.35$ and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. One string pulls upwards with $20\,\mathrm N$ and another pulls rightwards with $30\,\mathrm N$. Contact is maintained. Find the acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=(80-20)\,\mathrm N=60\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
f_d&=0.35\cdot60\,\mathrm N=21\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
a_x&=\frac{30-21}{8}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=1.125\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The upward pull enters the balance that determines the normal force. The block has no upward acceleration, so the surface only needs to support $60\,\mathrm N$. Because the block slides to the right, the tangential contact force is to the left. This leaves $9\,\mathrm N$ rightwards, so speed rises. The upward string affects the horizontal acceleration through friction even though its own horizontal component is zero. Without it, the normal force would rise to $80\,\mathrm N$ and friction to $28\,\mathrm N$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ block slides leftwards on a horizontal plane with $\mu_d=0.4$. It is pushed vertically down into the surface with $32\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the acceleration taking right as positive? Friction is the only horizontal force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=(50+32)\,\mathrm N=82\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
f_d&=0.4\cdot82\,\mathrm N=32.8\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
a_x&=\frac{32.8}{5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=6.56\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The added push points the same way as weight, so both increase the load that the plane must support. The normal force is $82\,\mathrm N$. Kinetic friction points right because the sliding relative to the plane is to the left. The acceleration component is therefore positive, which means slowing down here because velocity is negative. If the block stops with no horizontal force tending to make it slide, kinetic friction ceases, so the formula used applies only until stopping.

</div>
