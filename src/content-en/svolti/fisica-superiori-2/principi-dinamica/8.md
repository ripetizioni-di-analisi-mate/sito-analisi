## Exercise 1

A block initially at rest is pushed with $3\,\mathrm{N}$ to the right. Maximum static friction is $8\,\mathrm{N}$ and the block stays at rest. What is the actual friction magnitude?

::grafico{xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.3 ymin=-3.05 ymax=3.05 linee="-3,-0.56 3,-0.56" vettori="0,0,2.5000,0.0000,F₁;0,0,-2.5000,0.0000,f" uguale=1 assi=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="The horizontal net force is zero."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{Rx}&=3-0-f_s=0 \\[6pt]
f_s&=3\,\mathrm{N}<8\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At rest the horizontal net force is zero. The known pushes add with sign to $3\,\mathrm{N}$ to the right. The contact must supply exactly that magnitude to the left. Since $3\,\mathrm{N}$ is below the limit $8\,\mathrm{N}$, rest is consistent. The limit specifies the contact's capacity. Force balance selects the actual value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block at rest on a horizontal plane has a normal force of $40\,\mathrm N$. It experiences a $14\,\mathrm N$ push to the right and a $6\,\mathrm N$ push to the left. What minimum static coefficient allows it to remain at rest?

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{s,\mathrm{rich}}&=(14-6)\,\mathrm N=8\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
\mu_{s,\min}&=\frac{8}{40}=0.20.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Without friction, the two pushes would give $8\,\mathrm N$ to the right. Rest requires an equal force to the left. The surface can supply it only if the limit $\mu_sN$ is at least $8\,\mathrm N$. The coefficient threshold is therefore $0.20$. With $\mu_s=0.25$, the threshold would rise to $10\,\mathrm N$, while actual friction would stay at $8\,\mathrm N$ so long as the block stays at rest. The extra margin allows a larger push before sliding.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A spring pulls a stationary block leftwards with $16\,\mathrm N$. A string applies an adjustable pull $F$ to the right. Maximum static friction is $14\,\mathrm N$. For what values of $F$ can the block remain at rest?

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_x&=16\,\mathrm N-F\\[6pt]
|16\,\mathrm N-F|&\leq14\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
2\,\mathrm N&\leq F\leq30\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Friction must cancel the sum of the forces already present. With right positive, its component is $16\,\mathrm N-F$. The limit applies to the magnitude, giving two inequalities. At the minimum $F=2\,\mathrm N$, friction helps the string pull to the right. At the maximum $F=30\,\mathrm N$, it helps the spring pull left. When $F=16\,\mathrm N$, the two pulls balance even with zero friction. Checking both bounds verifies the limiting magnitude and the change in direction.

</div>
