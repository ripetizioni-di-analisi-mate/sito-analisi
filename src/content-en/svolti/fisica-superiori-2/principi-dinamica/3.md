## Exercise 1

An object of mass $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ is where the acceleration due to gravity is $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is the magnitude of its weight?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 poligoni="-0.45,-0.35 0.45,-0.35 0.45,0.35 -0.45,0.35" vettori="0,0,0,-1.5,P" xmin=-1.3 xmax=1.3 ymin=-1.8 ymax=0.8 titolo="Arrow P shows the 40 N weight in the stated field."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= mg=4\cdot 10\,\mathrm{N} = 40\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight is the gravitational force on the object. If it were the only force, the object would accelerate with magnitude $10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Apply the second law to that situation to express weight as mass times gravitational acceleration. The value $40\,\mathrm{N}$ is in newtons, while the mass remains $4\,\mathrm{kg}$. The direction is vertically down. Changing location could change weight while mass stays the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sample weighs $19.62\,\mathrm N$ in a laboratory where $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What mass and weight does it have after it is taken to a location where $g=1.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &=\frac{19.62}{9.81}\,\mathrm{kg}=2\,\mathrm{kg}\\[6pt]
P_2&=(2)(1.6)\,\mathrm N=3.2\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first reading is the magnitude of a force. Dividing by the original gravitational acceleration gives the mass being transferred, which stays at $2\,\mathrm{kg}$. Multiply this mass by the new gravitational field to find the new weight. The mass is the same, so the ratio of weights equals the ratio of the two values of $g$. The new field is less than one fifth of the original, consistent with a new weight of $3.2\,\mathrm N$ compared with the initial reading.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $7.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ sample rests on a scale in a lift accelerating downwards at $1.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the weight and the normal force exerted by the scale.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=(7.5)(10)\,\mathrm N=75\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
N-P&=(7.5)(-1.6)\,\mathrm N=-12\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
N&=(75-12)\,\mathrm N=63\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight is determined by the local field and points downwards. With upwards positive, the specified acceleration has a negative component. The scale supports only part of the weight so that a downward net force of $12\,\mathrm N$ remains. The result $N=63\,\mathrm N$ is positive, so contact with the scale is admissible. At constant velocity the same sample would have the same weight, while the scale would exert $75\,\mathrm N$ to give zero net force. The reading depends on the lift's acceleration.

</div>
