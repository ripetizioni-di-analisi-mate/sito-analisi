## Exercise 1

An ideal spring with spring constant $120\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is displaced $0.05\,\mathrm{m}$ from its natural length in the linear range. Find the magnitude of the spring force.

::grafico{xmin=-6.1 xmax=6.1 ymin=-7.92 ymax=7.92 f="-1.2*x" punti="5,-6,pieno,P" nomi="x (cm),F (N)" titolo="Restoring force in the linear range."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_e &= -kx=-120(0.05)\,\mathrm{N}=-6\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
\lvert F_e\rvert &= 6\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Hooke’s law holds in the linear range. The minus sign shows restoring action: force points opposite displacement from natural length. Spring constant times the displacement magnitude gives $6\,\mathrm{N}$. Length units cancel, leaving newtons. On the graph the origin is the natural-length state and P is the stated displacement.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A horizontal spring with $k=160\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and natural length $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ is compressed to $14\,\mathrm{cm}$. A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ block attached to its right end is released on a frictionless surface. With right positive, find the spring force and acceleration at release.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=(0.14-0.18)\,\mathrm m=-0.04\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
F_e&=-160(-0.04)\,\mathrm N=6.4\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
a_x&=\frac{6.4}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=3.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference between compressed and natural lengths is negative. Since the block is at the right end, a positive force pushes it away from the support and extends the spring towards its natural length. After release, the spring supplies the only horizontal force, so divide by the block's mass. The acceleration found is the initial one. As the block moves, compression decreases and the restoring-force magnitude changes. The horizontal net force is zero at the natural length.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An ideal spring requires a pull of $6.9\,\mathrm N$ at $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ extension and $11.5\,\mathrm N$ at $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its stiffness and the spring-force component at a compression of $7\,\mathrm{cm}$, still within the linear range. Extension is positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac{11.5-6.9}{0.05-0.03}\,\mathrm{N/m}=230\,\mathrm{N/m}\\[6pt]
F_e&=-230(-0.07)\,\mathrm N=16.1\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Stiffness is the slope of the line relating pull magnitude to extension. The readings give an additional $4.6\,\mathrm N$ over $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, or $2.3\,\mathrm{N/cm}$. Substituting the smaller extension recovers $6.9\,\mathrm N$, as expected. In the final configuration deformation is negative because the spring is compressed. The minus sign in the law gives a positive component. The problem places this compression in the linear range, so the same stiffness applies.

</div>
