## Exercise 1

A cart of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ experiences a horizontal force of $9\,\mathrm{N}$ to the right and $5\,\mathrm{N}$ to the left. Find the acceleration with right positive.

::grafico{xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.3 ymin=-3.05 ymax=3.05 linee="-3,-0.56 3,-0.56" vettori="0,0,2.5000,0.0000,F₁;0,0,-1.3889,0.0000,F₂" uguale=1 assi=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Horizontal force components on the cart."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{Rx} &= 9-5 = 4\,\mathrm{N} \\[6pt]
a_x &= \frac{4}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the ground frame, take the horizontal force components on the cart. The rightward push enters with a positive sign, and the leftward one with a negative sign. Their difference is $4\,\mathrm{N}$. Newton’s second law divides this net force by the mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$. Acceleration is $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and points with the stronger force. The initial velocity does not enter this calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart experiences a $19\,\mathrm N$ push to the left. The other push points right. To obtain $a_x=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ with right positive, what magnitude must the other push have?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_x-19\,\mathrm N &= (4)(-2)\,\mathrm N=-8\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
F_x &= (19-8)\,\mathrm N=11\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The specified acceleration sets the horizontal net force at $8\,\mathrm N$ to the left. The unknown push is to the right, so its component is positive. If it equalled the $19\,\mathrm N$ push, the net force would vanish. The leftward push must instead exceed it by $8\,\mathrm N$. Substituting in the balance gives $(11-19)\,\mathrm N=-8\,\mathrm N$. Dividing by the mass reproduces the specified acceleration and its negative sign.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart moves right at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. It experiences opposing pushes of $17\,\mathrm N$ to the right and $6\,\mathrm N$ to the left, and a constant resistance of $3\,\mathrm N$. Find its velocity after $0.5\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{Rx}&=(17-6-3)\,\mathrm N=8\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
a_x&=\frac{8}{2.5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=3.2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
v_x&=(2+3.2\cdot0.5)\,\mathrm{m/s}=3.6\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The resistance acts leftwards because the cart slides to the right. It therefore enters the sum with the same sign as the $6\,\mathrm N$ push. The net force is positive and the cart speeds up to the right, confirming the direction of resistance throughout the interval. Acceleration stays constant because all the forces are constant. Adding the $1.6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ increase to the initial $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ gives the final velocity. Using only the increase would amount to assuming the cart starts from rest.

</div>
