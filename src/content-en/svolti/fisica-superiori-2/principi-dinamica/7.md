## Exercise 1

An object of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ follows a circle of radius $2\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant speed $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of the radial net force?

::grafico{curve="2.0*cos(t),2.0*sin(t),0,6.2831853" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.9 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.6 punti="0,0,pieno;2.0,0,pieno,P" vettori="2.0,0,0.56,0,Fᵣ" uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="At P the radial net force points towards O."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_c &= \frac{4^2}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}= 8\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \\[6pt]
F_R &= ma_c = 16\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Speed is constant even while the vector changes direction. Radial acceleration is speed squared divided by radius, giving $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The second law multiplies this by mass, yielding $16\,\mathrm{N}$ towards the centre. This is the required total component and may come from different interactions depending on the problem. Other components cancel in uniform circular motion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ body moves at a constant speed of $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The radial net force is $38.4\,\mathrm N$. Find the radius of its circular path.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_r&=\frac{38.4}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=12.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
r&=\frac{8^2}{12.8}\,\mathrm m=5\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First divide the inward net force by the mass to obtain radial acceleration. To reconstruct the radius, divide speed squared by that acceleration. The ratio has units of length. Substituting $r=5\,\mathrm m$ in the formula for the net force recovers $(3)\dfrac{8^2}{5}\,\mathrm N=38.4\,\mathrm N$. A larger radius at the same speed would require a smaller net force because velocity would change direction more slowly. This determines the radius regardless of which way around the circle the body travels.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ particle travels around a horizontal circle of radius $5\,\mathrm m$ at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its only horizontal forces are an inward pull and a $6\,\mathrm N$ outward force. Find the inward pull.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{R,r}&=4\frac{5^2}{5}\,\mathrm N=20\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
T-6\,\mathrm N&=20\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
T&=26\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required radial net force is $20\,\mathrm N$ inwards. Take that direction as positive. The $6\,\mathrm N$ force has a negative radial component and is subtracted from the pull. A pull of only $20\,\mathrm N$ would leave a $14\,\mathrm N$ net force, too small for the stated speed and radius. The necessary pull is $26\,\mathrm N$, giving $26-6=20$ in that same balance. The centripetal force here is the net effect of the two interactions.

</div>
