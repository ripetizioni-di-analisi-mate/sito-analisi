## Exercise 1

An object of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ follows a circle of radius $2\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant speed $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of the radial net force?

::grafico{curve="2.0*cos(t),2.0*sin(t),0,6.2831853" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.9 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.6 punti="0,0,pieno,O;2.0,0,pieno,P" vettori="2.0,0,0.56,0,Fᵣ" uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="At P the radial net force points towards O."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_c &= \frac{4^2}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq 8\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \\[6pt]
F_R &= ma_c \simeq 16\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Speed is constant even while the vector changes direction. Radial acceleration is speed squared divided by radius, giving $8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The second law multiplies this by mass, yielding $16\,\mathrm{N}$ towards the centre. This is the required total component and may come from different interactions depending on the problem. Other components cancel in uniform circular motion.

</div>
