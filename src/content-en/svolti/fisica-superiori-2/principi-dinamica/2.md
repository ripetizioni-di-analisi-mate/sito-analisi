## Exercise 1

An object of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ has an acceleration magnitude of $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the magnitude of its net force.

::grafico{xmin=-1 xmax=5.0 ymin=-1 ymax=11.2 f="2*x" punti="4,8,pieno,P" nomi="a (m/s²),F (N)" titolo="For fixed mass the slope equals the mass."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_R &= ma=2\cdot(4)\,\mathrm{N} = 8\,\mathrm{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given acceleration is caused by all forces together. Newton’s second law gives net force by multiplying $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ by $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The product of the units is the newton. The sum of forces points in the same direction as acceleration. The relation determines net force and leaves the separate contributions undetermined.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ body has planar acceleration components $a_x=-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $a_y=4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the components and magnitude of the net force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{Rx}&=(6)(-3)\,\mathrm N=-18\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
F_{Ry}&=(6)(4)\,\mathrm N=24\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
|\vec F_R|&=\sqrt{(-18)^2+24^2}\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the second law separately along the two perpendicular axes. Multiplication by the same positive mass leaves the first component negative and the second positive. The magnitude is the hypotenuse of the component triangle. As a check, first find the acceleration magnitude, $\sqrt{(-3)^2+4^2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Multiplying by $6\,\mathrm{kg}$ again gives $30\,\mathrm N$. The signs reconstruct the orientation of the net force; both component squares enter its magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The horizontal velocity of a $1.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart changes from $+3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-1\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $2\,\mathrm s$ at constant acceleration. What horizontal component does the net force have?

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_x&=\frac{-1-3}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
F_{Rx}&=(1.5)(-2)\,\mathrm N=-3\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The final velocity minus the initial velocity is $-4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. With constant acceleration, dividing by the elapsed time gives the instantaneous acceleration throughout the interval. The net force is negative because mass is positive. During the first $1.5\,\mathrm s$ the cart slows to rest, and during the remaining time it gains speed in the negative direction. The net force points the same way in both phases. The reversal in velocity is therefore consistent with a constant net force.

</div>
