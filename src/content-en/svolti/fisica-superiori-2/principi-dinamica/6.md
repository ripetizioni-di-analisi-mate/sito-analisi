## Exercise 1

An object slides on a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the acceleration along the plane?

::grafico{xmin=-3.4 xmax=3.4 ymin=-3.25 ymax=3.25 punti="0,0,pieno,B" linee="-2.598076,-1.500000 2.598076,1.500000" vettori="0,0,0,-2,mg;0,0,-0.866025,1.500000,N;0,0,-0.866025,-0.500000,F∥" uguale=1 assi=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="N is perpendicular to the slope and F∥ is weight projected along it."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= g\sin\alpha=10\sin30^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq 5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose a downhill axis along the plane. The normal force is perpendicular to the plane and contributes nothing along that axis. Weight projects along the axis as $mg\sin\alpha$, because the parallel leg of the component triangle is opposite the slope angle. Divide by $m$ in the second law, leaving $g\sin\alpha$. The result $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is below $g$, since the angle is less than a right angle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A block moves uphill on a smooth $30^\circ$ plane with initial velocity $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ uphill. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, when does it first stop and how far has it travelled along the plane?

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_x&=-10\sin30^\circ\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
t&=\frac{6}{5}\,\mathrm s=1.2\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
\Delta s&=\frac{6+0}{2}\cdot1.2\,\mathrm m=3.6\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Taking uphill as positive, the initial velocity is positive and the parallel weight component is negative. The normal force has zero parallel component. Acceleration is constant, reducing the velocity component by $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second. Cancelling the initial $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ takes $1.2\,\mathrm s$. Over that interval the average velocity is the mean of the endpoint values because velocity changes linearly. The block therefore travels $3.6\,\mathrm m$ along the plane and can then slide down again.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ block is on a smooth $30^\circ$ plane. A string exerts a vertical upward pull of $10\,\mathrm N$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find the normal force and acceleration along the plane.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P-T&=(40-10)\,\mathrm N=30\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
N&=30\cos30^\circ\,\mathrm N=15\sqrt3\,\mathrm N\\[6pt]
a&=\frac{30\sin30^\circ}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=3.75\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Weight and tension are vertical and opposite, so their sum is $30\,\mathrm N$ downwards. Projecting that sum perpendicular to the plane gives the reaction needed to stay on the surface. Its positive value confirms the support can supply it. The parallel projection points downhill and is $15\,\mathrm N$, so it determines the acceleration. The pull changes both the normal force and the total component along the plane. The gravitational field retains its stated value.

</div>
