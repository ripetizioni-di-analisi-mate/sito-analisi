## Exercise 1

A concave spherical mirror has radius of curvature $30\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the paraxial approximation, what is its focal length?

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{R}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(30\right)}{2} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 15\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2.5,0 34,0;0.606,-6 0.268,-4 0.1504,-3 0.0667,-2 0,0 0.0667,2 0.1504,3 0.268,4 0.606,6" vettori="33,3,0.1504,3;33,-3,0.1504,-3;0.1504,3,15,0;0.1504,-3,15,0" unicolore=1 punti="0,0,pieno;15,0,pieno,F;30,0,pieno,C" etichette="-2.1,-1.3,V" xmin=-4 xmax=36 ymin=-7 ymax=7 titolo="Rays parallel to the axis converge near F, about 15 cm from the vertex V. The centre C is 30 cm from the vertex."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The centre of curvature is $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex. For near-axis reflections, the axial focus is about halfway along. The radius is given as a magnitude and the concave shape gives positive focal length. Focal length is a distance from the vertex, so it is also measured in centimetres.

If the paraxial rays are drawn before reflection, their directions are parallel to the axis. After reflection they converge in front of the mirror. A plane at the calculated distance intercepts the convergence region and allows the real focus to be located.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A concave mirror must bring paraxial rays that are parallel to the axis to a focus $35\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex. What radius of curvature is needed?

$$
R\simeq2f=2\cdot35\,\mathrm{cm}=70\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The requested distance is the focus. In the paraxial construction the centre of curvature is at twice that distance. The relationship is approximate because a spherical surface reflects far-off-axis rays differently from paraxial rays.

Half the $70\,\mathrm{cm}$ radius is exactly $35\,\mathrm{cm}$. Place the screen where the rays meet in front of the vertex. Measuring from the centre rather than the vertex changes the origin of the construction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two concave mirrors have radii of curvature $24$ and $60\,\mathrm{cm}$. Where should screens be placed to focus parallel rays, and by how much do their distances differ?

$$
\begin{aligned}f_1&\simeq12\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad f_2\simeq30\,\mathrm{cm}\\ f_2-f_1&\simeq18\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each mirror the screen must be in front of the vertex, near the real focus. The focal lengths are half their respective radii. Their difference is therefore half the difference between the radii of curvature.

The second screen goes farther from the vertex because that mirror has a more distant centre of curvature. The radii differ by $36\,\mathrm{cm}$, confirming the $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ difference between the two screen positions.

</div>
