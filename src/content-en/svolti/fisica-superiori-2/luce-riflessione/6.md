## Exercise 1

A concave spherical mirror has radius of curvature $30\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the paraxial approximation, what is its focal length?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-3.461538,0 48.461538,0;0,-5.85 0,5.85" vettori="45,0,45,4.5;45,4.5,0,4.5;45,4.5,0,0;0,4.5,22.5,-2.25;0,0,22.5,-2.25;22.5,0,22.5,-2.25" punti="15,0,pieno,F;22.5,-2.25,pieno,I;45,4.5,pieno,O" xmin=-6.923077 xmax=51.923077 ymin=-6.435 ymax=6.435 titolo="Point F lies 15 cm from the reference surface. For rays close to the axis, f = R/2."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{R}{2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(30\right)}{2} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 15\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The centre of curvature is $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex. For near-axis reflections, the axial focus is about halfway along. The radius is given as a magnitude and the concave shape gives positive focal length. Focal length is a distance from the vertex, so it is also measured in centimetres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

To collect parallel paraxial rays $35\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex, choose a concave mirror. What radius of curvature is needed?

$$
R\simeq2f=2\cdot35\,\mathrm{cm}=70\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The requested distance is the focus. In the paraxial construction the centre of curvature is at twice that distance. The relationship is approximate because a spherical surface reflects far-off-axis rays differently from paraxial rays.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two concave mirrors have radii of curvature $24$ and $60\,\mathrm{cm}$. Where should screens be placed to focus parallel rays, and by how much do their distances differ?

$$
\begin{aligned}f_1&\simeq12\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad f_2\simeq30\,\mathrm{cm}\\ f_2-f_1&\simeq18\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each mirror the screen must be in front of the vertex, near the real focus. The focal lengths are half their respective radii. Their difference is therefore half the difference between the radii of curvature.

</div>
