## Exercise 1

A convex mirror has focal length $f=-6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a real object is at $p=18\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the signed image distance using the convention in the theory.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= \frac{f p}{- f + p} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(-6\right) \left(18\right)}{- \left(-6\right) + \left(18\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq -4.5\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-7.846154,0 19.846154,0;0,-9.75 0,9.75" vettori="18,0,18,3;18,3,0,3;18,3,0,0;0,3,9,7.5;0,0,9,-1.5" segmenti="0,3,-4.5,0.75;0,0,-4.5,0.75;-4.5,0,-4.5,0.75" punti="-6,0,pieno,F;-4.5,0.75,vuoto,I;18,3,pieno,O" xmin=-9.692308 xmax=21.692308 ymin=-10.725 ymax=10.725 titolo="The backward extensions identify I 4.5 cm behind the mirror."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The focal length $f=-6\,\mathrm{cm}$ is negative and the object at $p=18\,\mathrm{cm}$ is real. The two terms $1/f$ and $-1/p$ are both negative, leading to $q<0$. Rays spread in front of the mirror while their backward extensions meet behind. The magnitude of $q$ is less than that of the focal length.

To check the order of the points, compare the magnitude of the image distance with the six-centimetre focal length. The image lies between the vertex and the virtual focus, as the backward extensions in the diagram require.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ object is $p=30\,\mathrm{cm}$ in front of a convex mirror with $f=-10\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find image position, orientation and height.

$$
\begin{aligned}q&=\frac{-10\cdot30}{30+10}\,\mathrm{cm}=-7.5\,\mathrm{cm}\\ M&=-\frac{-7.5}{30}=0.25\\ h_i&=0.25\cdot8\,\mathrm{cm}=2\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Negative q puts the image behind the mirror, where the backward ray extensions intersect. Object distance is positive. Thus M is positive and the image is upright. Its magnitude of one quarter reduces height from eight to two centimetres.

The ray construction puts the image tip on the same side of the axis as the object tip. A negative height would give a different orientation. The image-distance magnitude is also below the focal length, so the image lies between the vertex and the virtual focus.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A convex mirror forms a virtual image at $q=-6\,\mathrm{cm}$ of an object at $p=15\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find focal length and radius-of-curvature magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}\frac1f&=\left(\frac1{15}-\frac1{6}\right)\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}=-\frac1{10}\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\\ f&=-10\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad R\simeq20\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The virtual image gives a negative reciprocal. Adding the positive reciprocal for the object still gives a negative value, consistent with a convex mirror. The radius magnitude is twice the magnitude of the focal length.

Substituting the resulting value in the equation for $q$ gives $\dfrac{(-10)(15)}{15+10}\,\mathrm{cm}=-6\,\mathrm{cm}$, as stated. The radius of curvature is requested as a magnitude, so it is a positive distance even though the signed focal length is negative.

</div>
