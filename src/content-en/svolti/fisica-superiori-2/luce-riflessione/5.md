## Exercise 1

An object is $0.4\,\mathrm m$ from a plane mirror. How far is the object from its image?

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= 2 d \\[6pt]
&= 2 \left(0.4\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 0.8\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-0.52,0 0.68,0;0,-0.16 0,0.24" segmenti="-0.4,0,-0.4,0.14;0,0.06,-0.4,0.14" vettori="0.4,0,0.4,0.14;0.4,0.14,0,0.06;0,0.06,0.592,-0.0584" punti="-0.4,0.14,vuoto,I;0.4,0.14,pieno,O;0.592,-0.0584,pieno,E" xmin=-0.572 xmax=0.744 ymin=-0.204 ymax=0.28 titolo="The image is 0.4 m behind the plane. Total separation is 0.8 m."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The object is $0.4\,\mathrm m$ from the plane. Its image is equally far behind because it is the symmetric point about the plane. To get the separation between the two points, add two positive distances. The drawing also shows the extension that makes the image visible even though there are no physical rays behind the mirror.

Putting the origin on the mirror and taking the object's side as positive gives perpendicular coordinates of $+0.4\,\mathrm m$ for the object and $-0.4\,\mathrm m$ for the image. Their difference matches the distance found by adding the magnitudes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object sits $0.9\,\mathrm m$ from a plane mirror and moves $0.25\,\mathrm m$ closer. How much does the separation between object and image decrease?

$$
\begin{aligned}s_i&=2\cdot0.9\,\mathrm m=1.8\,\mathrm m\\s_f&=2(0.9-0.25)\,\mathrm m=1.3\,\mathrm m\\s_i-s_f&=0.50\,\mathrm m.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The image follows the symmetric motion behind the plane. When the object comes twenty-five centimetres closer, so does the image on the other side. The total separation falls by the sum of the movements, fifty centimetres.

In the second position each point is $0.65\,\mathrm m$ from the mirror. The separation is therefore $1.30\,\mathrm m$. Subtracting this from the initial separation gives the fifty-centimetre decrease. Using only the movement of the object would count one half of the segment.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An observer and object lie on the same normal in front of a plane mirror, respectively $1.2\,\mathrm m$ and $0.4\,\mathrm m$ from the plane. How far from the observer does the image appear?

$$
s_{\mathrm{obs}}=(1.2+0.4)\,\mathrm m=1.6\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The symmetric point lies forty centimetres behind the mirror. The requested distance starts at the observer, who is further from the mirror than the object. Add the observer-to-plane distance and the image-to-plane distance.

The reflected ray reaches the observer from the surface. Its apparent origin is found by tracing that direction beyond the surface. In front of the mirror, the direct distance from observer to object would instead be $0.8\,\mathrm m$, since it refers to a different point.

</div>
