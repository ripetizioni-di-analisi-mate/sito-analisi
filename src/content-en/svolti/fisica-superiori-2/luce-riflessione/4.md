## Exercise 1

A ray makes an angle of $15^\circ$ with a plane mirror’s surface. What is its angle of reflection from the normal?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3.6,0 3.6,0;0.792059,0.212232 0.738794,0.355785 0.659770,0.486933 0.557742,0.601103 0.436266,0.694314 0.299580,0.763316 0.152448,0.805705 0.000000,0.820000;0.000000,0.820000 -0.152448,0.805705 -0.299580,0.763316 -0.436266,0.694314 -0.557742,0.601103 -0.659770,0.486933 -0.738794,0.355785 -0.792059,0.212232" segmenti="0,-0.4,0,3.5" vettori="-2.897777,0.776457,0,0;0,0,2.897777,0.776457" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.8 etichette="-2.1,3.4,incident;2.1,3.4,reflected;-1.024003,0.966894,75°;0.644003,0.966894,75°" titolo="The 15° angle with the plane corresponds to 75° from the normal."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_r &= 90 - A \\[6pt]
&= 90 - \left(15\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 75{}^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle $15^\circ$ is relative to the surface. The normal is at right angles to that surface, so the angle of incidence measured from the normal is the complement. After reflecting, the angle from the normal keeps the same value. The two arms lie on opposite sides of the normal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In reflection from a plane mirror, the two ray arms from the point of incidence form an angle of $104^\circ$ in the medium above the surface. Find the angle of each ray to the surface.

$$
\begin{aligned}\theta_i&=\theta_r=\frac{104^\circ}{2}=52^\circ\\ \varphi&=90^\circ-52^\circ=38^\circ.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The normal divides the opening between the ray arms into equal angles. The requested reference line is tangent to the mirror and perpendicular to the normal. Subtract from ninety degrees to get the angle measured from the surface.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ray is initially normally incident on a plane mirror. The mirror is then rotated by $12^\circ$ about the point of incidence, in the ray plane. How far does the reflected direction rotate?

$$
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{rif}}=2\cdot12^\circ=24^\circ.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The normal rotates by twelve degrees with the mirror. The incident beam stays fixed, so the new reflected ray lies twelve degrees on the other side of the normal. The two angular changes add relative to the original direction, giving a magnitude of twenty-four degrees.

</div>
