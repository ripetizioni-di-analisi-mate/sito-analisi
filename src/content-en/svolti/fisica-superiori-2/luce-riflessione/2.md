## Exercise 1

A pulse of light covers $600\,\mathrm m$ in a vacuum. Use $c=3\cdot10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. How long does it take?

::grafico{f="x/300" punti="600,2,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=750 ymin=0 ymax=2.8 nomi="d (m),t (μs)" titolo="The slope shows the fraction of a second needed per metre."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{d}{c} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(600\right)}{\left(3\cdot10^{8}\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The path $600\,\mathrm m$ is the full distance travelled by the ray, so no division into outward and return paths is needed. Divide by $c=3\cdot10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. On the graph use the distance on the horizontal axis and read the corresponding time in microseconds. Speed times the time recovers six hundred metres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pulse meets a reflector $450\,\mathrm m$ away in vacuum and returns to a detector at the source. Neglecting any reflector delay, find the total time with $c=3.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
t=\frac{2\cdot450}{3.0\cdot10^8}\,\mathrm s=3.0\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm s=3.0\,\mathrm{\mu s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The detector measures the entire 900-metre route. Divide the total distance by the constant speed. Convert to microseconds at the end to compare it with the one-way time, which would be half as much.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two vacuum paths link the same source and detector. The first measures $720\,\mathrm m$ and the second $1.32\,\mathrm{km}$. Find the delay of the second relative to the first with $c=3.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\Delta t=\frac{1320-720}{3.0\cdot10^8}\,\mathrm s=2.0\,\mathrm{\mu s}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the same speed, the difference of times equals the path difference divided by speed. Convert both path lengths to metres. The second is six hundred metres longer, so it reaches the detector two microseconds later.

</div>
