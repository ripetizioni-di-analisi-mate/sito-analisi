## Exercise 1

A real object is $p=18\,\mathrm{cm}$ from a concave mirror of focal length $f=6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Using the paraxial approximation, find the signed image distance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= \frac{f p}{- f + p} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(6\right) \left(18\right)}{- \left(6\right) + \left(18\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 9\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-1.384615,0 19.384615,0;0,-3.9 0,3.9" vettori="18,0,18,3;18,3,0,3;18,3,0,0;0,3,9,-1.5;0,0,9,-1.5;9,0,9,-1.5" punti="6,0,pieno,F;9,-1.5,pieno,I;18,3,pieno,O" xmin=-2.769231 xmax=20.769231 ymin=-4.29 ymax=4.29 titolo="Point I is 9 cm in front of the mirror."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Use $f=6\,\mathrm{cm}$ as a positive focal length in the paraxial model. The object at $p=18\,\mathrm{cm}$ is three focal lengths from the vertex. The difference of reciprocals $1/f-1/p$ is positive, so rays meet in front of the mirror. Nine centimetres lies between one and two focal lengths, and the image is inverted.

The result also passes the magnification check. With the image distance half the object distance, the magnitude of $M$ is one half. In the diagram, rays from the object tip cross below the axis at the calculated distance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A concave mirror has $f=12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the object is at $p=18\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find signed image distance and magnification.

$$
\begin{aligned}q&=\frac{12\cdot18}{18-12}\,\mathrm{cm}=36\,\mathrm{cm}\\M&=-\frac{36}{18}=-2.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object lies between the focus and centre of curvature. Subtracting reciprocals gives a small positive number, so its reciprocal is a large positive distance. Negative magnification indicates inversion and magnitude two means the image is twice as tall.

As a check, the centre of curvature is $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertex. The image at $36\,\mathrm{cm}$ is beyond the centre, while the object at $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ is between the centre and focus. In this regime the magnification magnitude exceeds one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A concave mirror with focal length $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ forms a real image on a screen at $q=15\,\mathrm{cm}$. How far away is the object and how does its height compare with the image?

$$
\begin{aligned}p&=\frac{10\cdot15}{15-10}\,\mathrm{cm}=30\,\mathrm{cm}\\M&=-\frac{15}{30}=-0.50.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the screen position is known, subtract the reciprocal of image distance this time and isolate that of object distance. The image forms between the focus and centre of curvature, while the object lies beyond the latter. The image is inverted and half the object height.

The solution can be checked by exchanging the two distances in the equation, where the reciprocals are added symmetrically. An object at the former screen position would produce a real image at the former object position. The new magnification would have reciprocal magnitude.

</div>
