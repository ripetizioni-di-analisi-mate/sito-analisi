## Exercise 1

A cart's velocity changes from $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm{s}$. Find its average acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta v &= 8-(2)= 6\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
a_m &= \frac{6}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the initial velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from the final velocity $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The change is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Divide it by the duration $3\,\mathrm{s}$. The quotient is in metres per second per second. Its sign indicates the direction of change. If the velocity changes linearly, the slope of the line through the two readings is also the acceleration at each instant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart's velocity changes from $-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ to $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in $3\,\mathrm{s}$. Find its average acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta v &= -2-(-8)= 6\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
a_m &= \frac{6}{3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\simeq 2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the initial velocity $-8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from the final velocity $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The change is $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Divide it by the duration $3\,\mathrm{s}$. The quotient is in metres per second per second. Its sign indicates the direction of change. If the velocity changes linearly, the slope of the line through the two readings is also the acceleration at each instant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the first $2\,\mathrm s$, a trolley maintains a velocity of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. At the end of a second straight-line stage its velocity is $-3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The average acceleration over both stages is $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the duration and average acceleration of the second stage alone.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta v &=-3-5=-8\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
T&=\frac{-8}{-2}\,\mathrm s=4\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
T_2&=(4-2)\,\mathrm s=2\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
a_{m,2}&=\frac{-8}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The overall change compares the final velocity with the starting value. The negative sign indicates a decrease in signed velocity. Dividing the change by the given average acceleration gives four seconds and includes the constant-velocity stage.

That stage leaves the velocity unchanged, so the entire variation belongs to the second stage. It lasts two seconds and has a more negative average acceleration. To check, add the changes in both stages. The first contributes zero and the second reproduces the total change. Dividing their sum by the total duration gives back the supplied average.

</div>
