## Exercise 1

A vehicle travels at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with a constant acceleration of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. How long does it take to brake to rest?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_a &= \frac{v_0}{b}=\frac{18}{3}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 6\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Velocity decreases by $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second and reaches zero after $6\,\mathrm{s}$. The required time is the first instant at which the velocity reaches zero. The equation describes the braking interval only.

## Exercise 3

In a braking test with constant deceleration, the speedometer reads $19\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $1.5\,\mathrm s$ after braking begins and $13\,\mathrm{m/s}$ at $3\,\mathrm s$. Find the total braking time and the time still needed after the second reading.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{13-19}{3-1.5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=-4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\\[6pt]
v_0&=19+4\cdot1.5=25\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
t_a&=\frac{25}{4}\,\mathrm s=6.25\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
t_{\mathrm{restante}}&=(6.25-3)\,\mathrm s=3.25\,\mathrm s
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At both readings the vehicle is still travelling in its initial direction, which we take as positive. The velocity is therefore equal to the speedometer reading, and the two quantities decrease in the same way. The two readings give the acceleration. To work back to the start, add to the first reading what the vehicle lost during the first part of braking.

Total duration starts at that initial instant. To find the remaining time directly, start from the thirteen metres per second recorded in the second reading. At the same deceleration, the vehicle loses that amount in just over three seconds, as found by subtraction.

</div>
