## Exercise 1

A vehicle travels at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with a constant acceleration of magnitude $5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Before braking, it maintains its initial velocity through a reaction time of $1\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the total distance before it stops?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_a &= \frac{v_0}{b}=\frac{15}{5}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 3\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
s_r &= v_0t_r=15\cdot 1\,\mathrm{m}=15\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_f &= \frac{15^2}{2\cdot 5}\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 22.5\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{tot}} &= s_r+s_f \simeq 37.5\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Velocity decreases by $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second and reaches zero after $3\,\mathrm{s}$. During the reaction the velocity stays at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the graph has a rectangle of area $15\,\mathrm{m}$. Add the braking triangle’s area of $22.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The entire duration includes reaction and braking, while the second phase covers less distance than it would at the initial speed.

## Exercise 2

A vehicle travels at $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with a constant acceleration of magnitude $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Before braking, it maintains its initial velocity through a reaction time of $1.2\,\mathrm{s}$. What is the total distance before it stops?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_a &= \frac{v_0}{b}=\frac{16}{4}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 4\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
s_r &= v_0t_r=16\cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m}=19.2\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_f &= \frac{16^2}{2\cdot 4}\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 32\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{tot}} &= s_r+s_f \simeq 51.2\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Velocity decreases by $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second and reaches zero after $4\,\mathrm{s}$. During the reaction the velocity stays at $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so the graph has a rectangle of area $19.2\,\mathrm{m}$. Add the braking triangle’s area of $32\,\mathrm{m}$. The entire duration includes reaction and braking, while the second phase covers less distance than it would at the initial speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The model assigns a trolley $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ during the reaction and a constant deceleration of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ after the command. There are $50\,\mathrm m$ available from the signal. Find the maximum reaction time and the distance left over if the reaction takes $0.75\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s_f&=\frac{15^2}{2\cdot3}\,\mathrm m=37.5\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
t_{r,\max}&=\frac{50-37.5}{15}\,\mathrm s=\frac56\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{tot}}&=15\cdot0.75+37.5=48.75\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{res}}&=(50-48.75)\,\mathrm m=1.25\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Braking always begins at the same velocity and, by assumption, with the same deceleration. Its distance is therefore fixed. The part of the available distance left before braking is available for the reaction stage. Since velocity is constant in this stage, dividing that distance by velocity gives the maximum time.

With a reaction of three quarters of a second, the trolley travels a shorter distance before braking. Add that distance to the braking distance, then subtract the total from the distance available. The positive result means the modelled trolley comes to rest before the stated limit.

</div>
