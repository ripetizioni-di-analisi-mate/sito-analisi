## Exercise 1

An object is thrown upward at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. How far above the launch point does it rise?

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=1.2 ymin=-1.0 ymax=8.2 f="12*x-10*x^2/2" punti="0,0.000000,pieno,I;1.200000,7.200000,pieno,F" nomi="t (s),y (m)" titolo="F is the top of the rise."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_M &= \frac{12}{10}\,\mathrm{s}=1.2\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta h &= \frac{12^2}{2\cdot 10}\,\mathrm{m}=7.2\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

While rising, velocity is positive and acceleration points down. Height is greatest when velocity first reaches zero, after $1.2\,\mathrm{s}$. Set final velocity to zero in the relation without time to get the height gain $7.2\,\mathrm{m}$. The height gain is positive because the apex is above the launch point, while the acceleration is negative.

</div>
