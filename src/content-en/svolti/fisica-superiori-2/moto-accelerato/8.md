## Exercise 1

An object is thrown upward at $12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Take $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. How far above the launch point does it rise?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_M &= \frac{12}{10}\,\mathrm{s}=1.2\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta h &= \frac{12^2}{2\cdot 10}\,\mathrm{m}=7.2\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

While rising, velocity is positive and acceleration points down. Height is greatest when velocity first reaches zero, after $1.2\,\mathrm{s}$. Set final velocity to zero in the relation without time to get the height gain $7.2\,\mathrm{m}$. The height gain is positive because the apex is above the launch point, while the acceleration is negative.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ball is launched upwards at $15\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from a terrace $20\,\mathrm m$ above the ground. With negligible air resistance and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find its maximum height, when it passes the terrace height again and when it hits the ground.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y(t)&=20+15t-5t^2\\[6pt]
v(t)&=15-10t\\[6pt]
v=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ t_M=1.5\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
y_M&=20+15\cdot1.5-5\cdot1.5^2=31.25\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
y=20&\ \Longrightarrow\ 5t(3-t)=0\\[6pt]
y=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ (t-4)(t+1)=0
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With upward positive, gravity gives a negative acceleration. The maximum height occurs when the slope of the height graph becomes zero. Find that instant from the velocity equation and substitute it into the position equation.

To pass terrace height again, the ball must return to a height of twenty metres. Of the two roots, one is the launch instant and the other is three seconds after launch. For the ground, set the height to zero. The negative root precedes the launch and impact occurs at four seconds. This takes longer than returning to the terrace because the ball must fall another twenty metres.

</div>
