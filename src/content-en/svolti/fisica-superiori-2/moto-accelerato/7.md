## Exercise 1

An object is released from rest $20\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Neglecting air resistance and taking $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, when does it reach the ground?

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 20-\frac{10}{2}t^2=0 \\[6pt]
t &= \sqrt{\frac{2\cdot 20}{10}}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 2\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Take height as positive upward. The acceleration is therefore negative, and height starts at $20\,\mathrm{m}$ and then decreases. Impact occurs at zero height. The equation gives two opposite roots; choose the positive one because motion is observed after release.

## Exercise 3

An object A is released from rest at a height of $45\,\mathrm m$. A second object B starts at the same height $1\,\mathrm s$ later and is launched downwards. With negligible air resistance and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, find B's initial velocity so that both reach the ground together. Compare their impact speeds.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_A &=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot45}{10}}\,\mathrm s=3\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
t_B &=(3-1)\,\mathrm s=2\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
45&=2v_{0,B}+\frac12\cdot10\cdot2^2\\[6pt]
v_{0,B}&=\frac{45-20}{2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=12.5\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
v_A&=10\cdot3=30\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
v_B&=12.5+10\cdot2=32.5\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose downward as positive for both objects. A starts with zero velocity, so the distance immediately gives the fall time. B has one second less to reach the same point. Set its duration to two seconds in its motion equation and solve for the velocity needed at launch.

Calculate impact speed separately for each object from its own launch. B gains less velocity because it is in flight for a shorter time. Its starting velocity is nevertheless enough for it to reach the ground moving faster.

</div>
