## Exercise 1

A cart has initial velocity $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and constant acceleration $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its signed displacement in $3\,\mathrm{s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x &= v_0t+\frac12at^2 \\[6pt]
&= (2)\cdot 3+\frac{(2)\cdot 3^2}{2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
&= 15\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial-velocity term contributes $6\,\mathrm{m}$. Acceleration changes the velocity linearly, giving the signed area of a triangle of base $3\,\mathrm{s}$. This second contribution is $9\,\mathrm{m}$. Add them for the displacement. As a check, multiply the mean of the two endpoint velocities, $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, by the duration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart has initial velocity $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and constant acceleration $-1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its signed displacement in $6\,\mathrm{s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x &= v_0t+\frac12at^2 \\[6pt]
&= (8)\cdot 6+\frac{(-1)\cdot 6^2}{2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
&= 30\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial-velocity term contributes $48\,\mathrm{m}$. Acceleration changes the velocity linearly, giving the signed area of a triangle of base $6\,\mathrm{s}$. This second contribution is $-18\,\mathrm{m}$. Add them for the displacement. As a check, multiply the mean of the two endpoint velocities, $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, by the duration.

</div>

## Exercise 3

At $t=2\,\mathrm s$ a trolley is at $3\,\mathrm m$ with velocity $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Its acceleration stays at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until $t=6\,\mathrm s$. Find its final position, displacement and distance travelled from $2$ to $6\,\mathrm s$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau&=t-2,\qquad x(\tau)=3+5\tau-\tau^2\\[6pt]
x(4)&=3+20-16=7\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
\Delta x&=(7-3)\,\mathrm m=4\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
5-2\tau_M=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ \tau_M=2.5\,\mathrm s\\[6pt]
x_M&=3+5\cdot2.5-2.5^2=9.25\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
d&=(9.25-3)+(9.25-7)=8.5\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The time variable in the position equation is the elapsed time since the first reading, denoted by tau. Four seconds have elapsed by the end, so substitute four to find the position. Displacement compares that position with the initial one.

To find distance we need the furthest point reached before the trolley returns. Setting velocity to zero gives a time within the interval. Subtract the initial position from the maximum position, then subtract the final position from that maximum. Both differences are positive. Their sum exceeds the displacement, as expected because the trolley travels back along part of the route.

</div>
