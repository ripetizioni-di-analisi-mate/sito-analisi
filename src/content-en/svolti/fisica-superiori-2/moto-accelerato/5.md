## Exercise 1

A vehicle travels at $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with a constant acceleration of magnitude $2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the distance travelled from braking to rest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_a &= \frac{v_0}{b}=\frac{16}{2}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 8\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
s_f &= \frac{v_0^2}{2b}=\frac{16^2}{2\cdot 2}\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 64\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Velocity decreases by $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second and reaches zero after $8\,\mathrm{s}$. The graph during braking forms a triangle of height $16\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and base $8\,\mathrm{s}$. Its area is $64\,\mathrm{m}$. Isolating displacement in the relation without time, with final velocity zero, gives the same result.

## Exercise 2

A vehicle travels at $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and brakes with a constant acceleration of magnitude $3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Find the distance travelled from braking to rest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_a &= \frac{v_0}{b}=\frac{25}{3}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 8.3333\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
s_f &= \frac{v_0^2}{2b}=\frac{25^2}{2\cdot 3}\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 104.17\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose the direction of travel as positive. Velocity decreases by $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second and reaches zero after $8.3333\,\mathrm{s}$. The graph during braking forms a triangle of height $25\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and base $8.3333\,\mathrm{s}$. Its area is $104.17\,\mathrm{m}$. Isolating displacement in the relation without time, with final velocity zero, gives the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a test a trolley starts at $18\,\mathrm{m/s}$. It brakes for $2\,\mathrm s$ at $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and then at $-2\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until it stops. Find the total braking distance and compare it with the prediction if the first acceleration continued until rest.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_1&=18-3\cdot2=12\,\mathrm{m/s}\\[6pt]
s_1&=\frac{18+12}{2}\cdot2\,\mathrm m=30\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
s_2&=\frac{12^2}{2\cdot2}\,\mathrm m=36\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(30+36)\,\mathrm m=66\,\mathrm m\\[6pt]
s_{\mathrm{c}}&=\frac{18^2}{2\cdot3}\,\mathrm m=54\,\mathrm m
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity reached at the end of the first interval is the starting value for the second. In the first stage, the time is known, so the trapezoid area directly gives the distance. In the second, the starting velocity, deceleration and zero final velocity are known. The distance can therefore be found without first calculating the duration.

Braking in two stages takes twelve metres more. Since the second acceleration has a smaller magnitude, the vehicle loses less velocity per second starting from the same velocity at the stage boundary. To check, the second stage takes six seconds and its average velocity is six metres per second.

</div>
