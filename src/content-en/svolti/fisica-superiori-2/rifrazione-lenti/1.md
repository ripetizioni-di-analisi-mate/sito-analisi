## Exercise 1

A transparent material has refractive index $1.3$. Use $c=3\cdot10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the speed of light in the material?

::grafico{f="3/x" punti="1.3,2.307692,pieno,A" xmin=0.95 xmax=2 ymin=0 ymax=3.3 nomi="n,v (10⁸ m/s)" titolo="At n = 1.3 the speed is about 2.31·10⁸ m/s."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{c}{n} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(3\cdot10^{8}\right)}{\left(1.3\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 2.30769\cdot10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The index $1.3$ is a dimensionless ratio. Divide the speed $c=3\cdot10^{8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ by this ratio. As $n>1$, the speed in the material is below its vacuum value. The result is in metres per second. The graph shows speed varying inversely with $n$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Light has speed $2.25\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in a material. With $c=3.00\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, find the index and estimate travel time over $90\,\mathrm m$.

$$
\begin{aligned}n&=\frac{3.00\cdot10^8}{2.25\cdot10^8}=\frac43\\ t&=\frac{90}{2.25\cdot10^8}\,\mathrm s=0.40\,\mathrm{\mu s}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both speeds share a unit, so their ratio is dimensionless. Find the time with the speed inside the material. In vacuum the same path would take 0.30 microseconds. A delay of a tenth of a microsecond agrees with an index above one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two slabs on a straight path have thicknesses of $20$ and $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ and indices of $1.5$ and $2.0$. At normal incidence and with $c=3.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, what is the crossing time?

$$
\begin{aligned}t&=\frac{1.5\cdot0.20+2.0\cdot0.30}{3.0\cdot10^8}\,\mathrm s\\ &=3.0\cdot10^{-9}\,\mathrm s=3.0\,\mathrm{ns}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Normal incidence keeps the path along the normal. Each layer has its own speed, so add their individual travel times. Substituting c divided by each index for its speed gives the sum of index-weighted thicknesses in the numerator.

</div>
