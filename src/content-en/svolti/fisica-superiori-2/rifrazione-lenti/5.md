## Exercise 1

A thin converging lens has focal length $f=6\,\mathrm{cm}$. A real object is at $p=13.2\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the signed image distance in the paraxial approximation.

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-15.061538,0 12.861538,0;0,-2.86 0,2.86" vettori="-13.2,0,-13.2,2.2;-13.2,2.2,0,2.2;-13.2,2.2,0,0;0,2.2,11,-1.833333;0,0,11,-1.833333;11,0,11,-1.833333" punti="6,0,pieno,F;-6,0,pieno,F′;11,-1.833333,pieno,I;-13.2,2.2,pieno,O" xmin=-16.923077 xmax=14.723077 ymin=-3.146 ymax=3.146 titolo="For p = 13.2 cm and f = 6 cm the image is 11 cm into the output side."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= \frac{f p}{- f + p} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(6\right) \left(13.2\right)}{- \left(6\right) + \left(13.2\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 11\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A converging lens has positive focal length $f=6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The object $p=13.2\,\mathrm{cm}$ lies beyond two focal lengths, so the real image forms between one and two focal lengths on the output side. The reciprocal calculation confirms $q=11\,\mathrm{cm}$. The image is inverted and shorter than the object.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A converging lens with $f=12\,\mathrm{cm}$ views an object at $p=18\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find $q$ and $M$.

$$
\begin{aligned} q&=\frac{12\cdot18}{18-12}\,\mathrm{cm}=36\,\mathrm{cm}\\ M&=-\frac{36}{18}=-2.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object lies between one and two focal lengths. The difference of reciprocals is positive, and the image forms beyond two focal lengths on the other side. The sign of M indicates inversion and magnitude two means the height doubles.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A converging lens has $f=10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the screen is at $q=15\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the distance at which a real object must be placed.

$$
p=\frac{10\cdot15}{15-10}\,\mathrm{cm}=30\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A screen can receive a real image, so q is positive. Image distance is known and object distance is isolated. At thirty centimetres the model predicts a reduced image, consistent with a screen between one and two focal lengths.

</div>
