## Exercise 1

A thin lens forms a real image at $q=12\,\mathrm{cm}$ of a real object at $p=25\,\mathrm{cm}$. What is its signed transverse magnification?

::grafico{assi=0 linee="-27.846154,0 14.846154,0;0,-5.2 0,5.2" vettori="-25,0,-25,4;-25,4,0,4;-25,4,0,0;0,4,12,-1.92;0,0,12,-1.92;12,0,12,-1.92" punti="8.108108,0,pieno,F;-8.108108,0,pieno,F′;12,-1.92,pieno,I;-25,4,pieno,O" xmin=-30.692308 xmax=17.692308 ymin=-5.72 ymax=5.72 titolo="At 12 cm on the output side the ratio to p = 25 cm is 0.48. The image lies below the axis."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= - \frac{q}{p} \\[6pt]
&= - \frac{\left(12\right)}{\left(25\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq -0.48
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since both $q=12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $p=25\,\mathrm{cm}$ have the same unit of length, it cancels in their ratio. The magnitude ratio is $\dfrac{12}{25}=0.48$, so the image height is slightly below half. The minus sign describes inversion of the image, while physical height is the magnitude.

In the diagram the object height is represented by $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Multiplying by the signed value gives $-1.92\,\mathrm{cm}$. This checks both the size of the image and the side of the axis on which it falls.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A lens gives signed image height $-2.5\,\mathrm{cm}$ for an object height of $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. The object is at $p=36\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find $M$ and $q$.

$$
\begin{aligned}M&=\frac{-2.5}{5}=-0.50\\q&=-Mp=-(-0.50)\cdot36\,\mathrm{cm}=18\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two heights give magnification directly. The negative sign comes from inversion of the image. In the equation q and M have opposite signs because p is positive. Positive q locates the image on the output side.

The calculated distances are positive and give a positive sum of reciprocals. A check for consistency with one thin lens gives a focal length of $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the reciprocal of that sum.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A converging lens of focal length $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ is used with an object at $p=8\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find $M$ and explain why no screen is useful.

$$
\begin{aligned}q&=\frac{16\cdot8}{8-16}\,\mathrm{cm}=-16\,\mathrm{cm}\\M&=-\frac{-16}{8}=2.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The object is inside the focal length. The denominator for q is negative, so only backward extensions of the output rays meet the virtual point on the object side. The image looks upright and twice as tall, but a screen placed there does not receive the rays.

For comparison, moving the object to $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ with the same lens would give $q=48\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $M=-2$. The magnitude would be the same, but the opposite signs would indicate a real, inverted image.

</div>
