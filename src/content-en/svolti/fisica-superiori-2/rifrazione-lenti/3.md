## Exercise 1

Light goes from air with $n_1=1$ into glass with $n_2=1.5$. The angle of incidence from the normal is $20^\circ$. What is the refracted angle?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3.4,0 3.4,0" segmenti="0,-3.05,0,3.05" vettori="-0.957656,2.631139,0,0;0,0,0.638438,-2.726242" xmin=-3.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-3.2 ymax=3.2 etichette="-2.4,2.6,n₁;2.4,-2.6,n₂" titolo="Incidence of 20° in air, refraction of about 13.2° in glass."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_2 &= \frac{180 \arcsin{\left(\frac{n_1 \sin{\left(\frac{\pi \alpha}{180} \right)}}{n_2} \right)}}{\pi} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{180 \arcsin{\left(\frac{\left(1\right) \sin{\left(\frac{\pi \left(20\right)}{180} \right)}}{\left(1.5\right)} \right)}}{\pi} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 13.1801{}^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Insert $n_1=1$ and $n_2=1.5$ in Snell’s law. The angle $20^\circ$ is already measured from the normal. Divide by the index of the second medium and take the inverse sine. Moving from the lower-index to the higher-index medium predicts an angle below $20^\circ$.

The transmitted sine is about $0.228$. If the calculator returns an angle in radians, convert it to degrees before comparing it with $20^\circ$. In the drawing the transmitted ray has the smaller angle from the normal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an experiment light enters an unknown material from air of $n=1$. The angles from the normal are $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$. Find the material index.

$$
n_2=\frac{\sin45^\circ}{\sin30^\circ}=\frac{\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}}{\tfrac12}=\sqrt2\simeq1.414.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since both directions are known, Snell’s law can recover a property of the second medium. The refracted ray is nearer the normal, indicating a higher index than air. The calculation gives the square root of two.

Substituting the result gives $\sqrt2\sin30^\circ=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$, the incident sine. The check uses angles from the normal on both sides of the same boundary and preserves the same factor.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ray strikes a slab with $n=1.5$ from air at $60^\circ$ to the surface. The faces are parallel and air is outside on both sides. Find the angle inside and outside the slab, both from the normal.

$$
\begin{aligned}\theta_1&=90^\circ-60^\circ=30^\circ\\ \theta_2&=\arcsin\!\left(\frac{\sin30^\circ}{1.5}\right)\simeq19.5^\circ\\ \theta_3&=\arcsin(1.5\sin\theta_2)=30^\circ.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First convert the angle stated relative to the surface. At the second face the normal has the same orientation and the sine inside is the one just found. Multiplying by the slab index restores the initial sine, so the emerging ray is parallel to the incident ray.

In the last step it is best to keep the exact sine of the interior angle. Rounding to $19.5^\circ$ reports the measurement, while the slab’s index cancels exactly across the two interfaces.

</div>
