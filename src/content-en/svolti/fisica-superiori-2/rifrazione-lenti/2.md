## Exercise 1

Light has vacuum wavelength $430\,\mathrm{nm}$. What is its wavelength in glass of refractive index $1.5$?

::grafico{f="430/x" punti="1.5,286.666667,pieno,A" xmin=0.95 xmax=2 ymin=0 ymax=460 nomi="n,λ (nm)" titolo="Frequency is fixed. Point A gives wavelength in glass."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{\lambda_0}{n} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(430\right)}{\left(1.5\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 286.667\,\mathrm{nm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The frequency set by the source crosses the interface unchanged. Therefore wavelength changes in the same proportion as speed. Divide $430\,\mathrm{nm}$ by the index $1.5$ to get the value in nanometres. The glass wavelength is about two thirds of the vacuum wavelength.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In glass with $n=1.5$ the measured wavelength is $360\,\mathrm{nm}$. Find vacuum wavelength and frequency with $c=3.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}\lambda_0&=1.5\cdot360\,\mathrm{nm}=540\,\mathrm{nm}\\ \nu&=\frac{3.0\cdot10^8}{540\cdot10^{-9}}\,\mathrm{Hz}\simeq5.56\cdot10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Recover vacuum wavelength by multiplying by the index. Frequency is the same in the two media. To use c in metres per second, convert vacuum wavelength to metres. Using the glass speed with the glass wavelength instead gives the same frequency.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the same light the wavelength is $450\,\mathrm{nm}$ in a material of index $1.4$. What is the wavelength in a second material of index $1.75$?

$$
\lambda_2=\lambda_1\frac{n_1}{n_2}=450\frac{1.4}{1.75}\,\mathrm{nm}=360\,\mathrm{nm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product of index and wavelength in each medium gives the same vacuum wavelength, because frequency is unchanged. Therefore the wavelength ratio is the inverse of the index ratio. Index increases and wavelength falls.

</div>
