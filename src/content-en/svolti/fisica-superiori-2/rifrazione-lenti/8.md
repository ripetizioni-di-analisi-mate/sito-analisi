## Exercise 1

A thin converging lens has focal length $0.15\,\mathrm m$. What is its optical power?

::grafico{f="100/x" punti="15,6.666667,pieno,A" xmin=8 xmax=51 ymin=0 ymax=12 nomi="f (cm),D (m⁻¹)" titolo="At f = 0.15 m the power is about 6.67 dioptres."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= \frac{1}{f} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\left(0.15\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 6.66667\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Focal length $0.15\,\mathrm m$ is already in metres. Take its reciprocal and the result is in inverse metres. The converging lens has positive sign. The same focal length as fifteen centimetres must be converted to metres before finding the value in dioptres.

The point on the graph is read at fifteen centimetres, but the dioptre formula uses $0.15\,\mathrm m$. As a dimensionally correct check, the product of focal length in metres and power in inverse metres is one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A lens has optical power $-4\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Express its focal length in centimetres and identify the lens type.

$$
f=\frac1D=-0.25\,\mathrm m=-25\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The reciprocal of inverse metres is metres. The negative sign is preserved and identifies a diverging lens. A physical distance has positive magnitude, while signed focal length encodes the side of its apparent focus.

A magnitude of one quarter of a metre is twenty-five centimetres. Keep the minus sign after conversion because the direction of the distance does not depend on units. A positive value would describe a converging lens.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two thin lenses in contact in air have powers of $+5$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Find total power and equivalent focal length.

$$
\begin{aligned}D_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(5-2)\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}=3\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\\ f_{\mathrm{eq}}&=\frac{1}{3}\,\mathrm m\simeq33.3\,\mathrm{cm}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the model of thin lenses in contact, add their signed powers. The diverging lens reduces the positive power of the converging lens. The system remains converging because the total is positive. Its focal length exceeds that of the positive lens alone, which was twenty centimetres.

Convert from metres to centimetres after taking the reciprocal of the total power. The negative lens makes the system less converging and moves the focus farther away, provided the lenses satisfy the contact model.

</div>
