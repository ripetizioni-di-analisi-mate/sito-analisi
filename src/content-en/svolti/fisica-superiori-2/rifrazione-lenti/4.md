## Exercise 1

Light goes from a material of refractive index $1.5$ into air of refractive index $1$. What is the critical angle, measured from the normal in the incident medium?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3.4,0 3.4,0" segmenti="0,-3.05,0,3.05" vettori="-1.866667,2.086997,0,0;0,0,2.8,-0" xmin=-3.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-3.2 ymax=3.2 etichette="-2.4,2.6,n₁;2.4,-2.6,n₂" titolo="At the critical angle the second ray is tangent to the boundary."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta_c &= \frac{n_2}{n_1} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{1.5}\simeq 0.667 \\[6pt]
\theta_c &= \arcsin(0.667)\simeq 41.8^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ray starts in the medium of index $1.5$ and heads towards $1$. Since the initial index is larger, total internal reflection is possible. The critical value occurs when the transmitted angle reaches ninety degrees and has sine one. The inverse sine of the ratio below one is the threshold, about $41.8^\circ$.

The result is measured from the normal in the initial medium. The resulting transmitted ray grazes the surface. An incidence angle only slightly larger would require the exterior sine to rise above one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In going from glass of $n_1=1.5$ to air of $n_2=1$, incidence is $50^\circ$ from the normal. Determine numerically whether a transmitted ray emerges.

$$
\sin\theta_2=\frac{1.5}{1}\sin50^\circ\simeq1.149>1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sine required by the law is greater than one. No real angle has that sine. In the model of two transparent nonabsorbing media this indicates total internal reflection. The reflected ray stays in glass, fifty degrees from the normal.

The same check can be made by comparing incidence with the threshold. The threshold for glass next to air is about $41.8^\circ$. The measured $50^\circ$ lies in the total reflection range.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The initial medium has index $2.60$. At its interface with a second medium the critical angle is measured as $30^\circ$ from the normal. Find the index of the second medium.

$$
n_2=n_1\sin\theta_c=2.60\cdot0.50=1.30.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the threshold the transmitted ray lies along the surface, so the sine of its angle to the normal is one. Snell’s law then gives the second index by multiplying the initial index by the measured sine. The result is below $2.60$, as required for total internal reflection from that side, and above the index of vacuum. Check by reversing the calculation. The ratio $\dfrac{1.30}{2.60}$ is one half, whose arcsine is $30^\circ$.

</div>
