## Exercise 1

Light goes from a material of refractive index $1.5$ into air of refractive index $1$. What is the critical angle, measured from the normal in the incident medium?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-3.4,0 3.4,0" segmenti="0,-3.05,0,3.05" vettori="-1.866667,2.086997,0,0;0,0,2.8,-0" xmin=-3.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-3.2 ymax=3.2 etichette="-2.4,2.6,n₁;2.4,-2.6,n₂" titolo="At the critical angle the second ray is tangent to the boundary."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_c &= \frac{180 \arcsin{\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1} \right)}}{\pi} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{180 \arcsin{\left(\frac{\left(1\right)}{\left(1.5\right)} \right)}}{\pi} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 41.8103{}^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ray starts in the medium of index $1.5$ and heads towards $1$. Since the initial index is larger, total internal reflection is possible. The critical value occurs when the transmitted angle reaches ninety degrees and has sine one. The inverse sine of the ratio below one is the threshold, about $41.8^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In going from glass of $n_1=1.5$ to air of $n_2=1$, incidence is $50^\circ$ from the normal. Determine numerically whether a transmitted ray emerges.

$$
\sin\theta_2=\frac{1.5}{1}\sin50^\circ\simeq1.149>1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sine required by the law is greater than one. No real angle has that sine. In the model of two transparent nonabsorbing media this indicates total internal reflection. The reflected ray stays in glass, fifty degrees from the normal.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The incident medium has index $1.6$. Its critical angle at the interface with a second material is $30^\circ$. Find the second index.

$$
n_2=n_1\sin\theta_c=1.6\cdot0.50=0.80.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the limit the transmitted sine is one. The law gives the value a second medium would need under the stated conditions. The result is below the vacuum index. It is not one of the ordinary transparent materials assumed in the chapter, so the data need checking if the experiment is about those materials.

</div>
