## Exercise 1

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ object sits on a shelf $4\,\mathrm m$ above the floor. The floor sets the zero height and $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. What is its gravitational potential energy?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,0 4.0,0;1.8,4 4,4" punti="2.6,4,pieno,A" vettori="1.08,0,1.08,4,h" etichette="1.3,2.3,4 m;3.3,-0.42,h = 0;2.91,4.25,2 kg" xmin=-0.2 xmax=4.5 ymin=-0.52 ymax=4.7 titolo="The height of A is measured from the floor, where the zero is set."}

$$
U_g=mgh=2\cdot10\cdot4\,\mathrm J=80\,\mathrm J.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Height is measured vertically from the reference level. The weight has magnitude $20\,\mathrm N$ and the height is positive, so the product gives $80\,\mathrm J$. The shelf fixes the position of the object. For this calculation its height above the floor is the relevant distance.

As a check, descending to the floor would reduce the potential energy by $80\,\mathrm J$. Gravity would do $+80\,\mathrm J$ of work because the force and the vertical part of that displacement both point downwards.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ object lies $2\,\mathrm m$ below the old reference level. The new zero is $3\,\mathrm m$ above the old zero. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what potential energy is assigned to the object with each reference?

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_0&=30\cdot(-2)\,\mathrm J=-60\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
h_1&=(-2-3)\,\mathrm m=-5\,\mathrm m,\\[6pt]
U_1&=30\cdot(-5)\,\mathrm J=-150\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Raising the zero leaves the object's physical position unchanged and decreases the assigned height. Subtract the height of the new zero from the previous value. Since the new height is $-5\,\mathrm m$, multiplying by $30\,\mathrm N$ gives $-150\,\mathrm J$.

The numerical value falls by $90\,\mathrm J$ at any other point in the same field as well. The energy difference between two positions therefore stays the same. Changing the reference adds a constant and does no work on the object.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two ramps, $6$ and $10\,\mathrm m$ long, have the same vertical drop of $2\,\mathrm m$. A $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ sled travels down each ramp under gravity and a constant resistance of magnitude $4\,\mathrm N$ opposing the motion. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the total work done by these two forces on each ramp?

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_g&=3\cdot10\cdot2\,\mathrm J=60\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
W_1&=(60-4\cdot6)\,\mathrm J=36\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
W_2&=(60-4\cdot10)\,\mathrm J=20\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Gravity acts vertically and does the same work on the two ramps because the height difference is the same. The resistance points against the motion throughout the ramp. Its contribution is its magnitude multiplied by the path length, with a negative sign.

The longer ramp adds $4\,\mathrm m$ of travel. The resistance therefore removes another $16\,\mathrm J$. This is exactly the difference between the total amounts of work, even though the two paths start and finish at the same heights.

</div>
