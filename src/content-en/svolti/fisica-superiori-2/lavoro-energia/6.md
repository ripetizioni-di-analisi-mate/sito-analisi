## Exercise 1

An ideal spring with constant $100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is slowly stretched from its natural length to a deformation of $0.10\,\mathrm m$. How much energy does it store?

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_f&=100\cdot0.10\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N,\\[6pt]
U_e&=\frac{0.10\cdot10}{2}\,\mathrm J=0.50\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="100*x" area="100*x;0;0;0.10" punti="0.1,10,pieno,A" segmenti="0.1,0,0.1,10" etichette="0.088,-0.65,0.10 m" xmin=0 xmax=0.13 ymin=0 ymax=13 nomi="x (m),F (N)" titolo="The work done in stretching the spring is the area up to A."}

<div class="spiegazione">

During slow extension the external force grows linearly. On the graph the work is the area of the triangle bounded by the line and the axis. The final height is $10\,\mathrm N$, but the triangle's average height is half as large.

The same value follows from the formula involving the square of the final deformation. The calculation gives $\dfrac12\cdot100\cdot(0.10)^2=0.50\,\mathrm J$. Using the final force as a constant force over the displacement would give twice that value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A spring with constant $160\,\mathrm{N/m}$ goes from a compression of $0.20\,\mathrm m$ to one of $0.10\,\mathrm m$. How much work does the spring force do on its moving end?

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_i&=\frac{160(-0.20)^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=3.2\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
U_f&=\frac{160(-0.10)^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=0.8\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
W_e&=(3.2-0.8)\,\mathrm J=2.4\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The end moves towards the natural length and the spring pushes it in the same direction, so the work must be positive. The spring gives up part of its initial energy and retains the energy associated with the final compression.

As a check, the force during the movement falls from $32$ to $16\,\mathrm N$. Its average over the section is $24\,\mathrm N$. Multiplying by $0.10\,\mathrm m$ gives $2.4\,\mathrm J$. The two energies and the force therefore give the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An ideal spring with $k=180\,\mathrm{N/m}$ is already stretched by $0.10\,\mathrm m$. A slow pull does a further $2.7\,\mathrm J$ of work, all stored in the spring. What is the final extension?

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_i&=\frac{180(0.10)^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=0.9\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
U_f&=(0.9+2.7)\,\mathrm J=3.6\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
x_f&=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot3.6}{180}}\,\mathrm m=0.20\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The added work determines the change in energy. Before the second pull, the spring already held $0.9\,\mathrm J$, so the final configuration holds $3.6\,\mathrm J$. Use this energy to find the final extension, which is measured from the natural length as before.

A ratio provides a check. The final energy is four times the initial energy and energy is proportional to the square of the deformation. The extension must therefore double from $0.10$ to $0.20\,\mathrm m$.

</div>
