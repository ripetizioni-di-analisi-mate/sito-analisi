## Exercise 1

A cart starts from rest and drops through $5\,\mathrm{m}$ without friction. What is its speed at the bottom with $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_f^2 &= v_0^2+2gh = 0^2+2\cdot10\cdot5 \\[6pt]
v_f &= \sqrt{0^2+2\cdot10\cdot5}\,\mathrm{m/s}\simeq 10\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Mechanical energy is conserved without friction because weight is represented by potential energy and the normal force from the stationary surface does no work. The loss in height energy is $mgh$ and becomes an increase in kinetic energy. Add it to the initial kinetic energy from speed $0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The same mass appears on both sides and cancels. The positive square root gives $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$.

</div>
