## Exercise 1

A cart starts at rest and descends a fixed frictionless track. The vertical drop is $5\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is its speed at the end of the descent?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="0,5 0.8,4.8 1.9,4.15 3,3 4.5,1.57 5.9,0.36 7.1,0 7.7,0" punti="0,5,pieno,A;7.1,0,pieno,B" segmenti="-0.45,0,-0.45,5;-0.45,0,7.1,0" etichette="-1.16,2.3,5 m" xmin=-1.3 xmax=8 ymin=-0.65 ymax=5.65 titolo="The cart starts from rest at A. B is at the reference height."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
mgh&=\frac12mv_f^2,\\[6pt]
v_f&=\sqrt{2\cdot10\cdot5}\,\mathrm{m/s}=10\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set the zero of gravitational potential energy at B. The cart starts with gravitational energy only. The force from the fixed track is perpendicular to the displacement, so it does no work and the mechanical energy is conserved. At B, all the energy that was present at the start is kinetic.

Mass appears in both terms and cancels. A check per unit mass gives $gh=50\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, equal to $\dfrac12v_f^2$ for the speed found. The track length does not enter this balance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ sled enters a rough horizontal section at $8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Friction does $-24\,\mathrm J$ of work through that section. The sled then climbs a frictionless ramp. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what maximum height does it reach above the horizontal section?

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_i&=\frac{2\cdot8^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=64\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
K_B&=(64-24)\,\mathrm J=40\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
h_{\max}&=\frac{40}{2\cdot10}\,\mathrm m=2\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The climb starts with the energy left after the rough section. At the end of that section, the positive $40\,\mathrm J$ ensures the sled reaches the ramp. Its mechanical energy is conserved on the smooth ramp, and at the highest point reached before it slides back, its speed is zero.

Using the horizontal track as the reference, the balance over the entire path is $20h_{\max}-64=-24$ in joules. This gives the same height. Ignoring the rough section would give a greater height.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart enters a smooth descent at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and drops $1.5\,\mathrm m$. On the following horizontal section a resistance does $-10\,\mathrm J$ of work. The cart then compresses a spring on a frictionless section and momentarily stops after compressing it by $0.40\,\mathrm m$. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, what is the spring constant?

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_C&=\left(\frac{2\cdot4^2}{2}+2\cdot10\cdot1.5-10\right)\,\mathrm J\\[6pt]
&=36\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
k&=\frac{2\cdot36}{0.40^2}\,\mathrm{N/m}=450\,\mathrm{N/m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The descent converts the loss of gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy. The resistance then removes $10\,\mathrm J$ before the spring begins to compress. The value at C is positive, so the cart reaches the spring. At maximum compression, all $36\,\mathrm J$ is stored in the spring.

The area of a triangle gives a check. The spring force reaches $450\cdot0.40=180\,\mathrm N$. The average force during compression is half as large, so $90\cdot0.40=36\,\mathrm J$.

</div>
