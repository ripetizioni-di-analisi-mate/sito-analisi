## Exercise 1

A cart of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ has speed $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is its kinetic energy?

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=3.66 ymin=0 ymax=13.950000000000001 f="1.0*x^2" punti="3,9,pieno,P" nomi="v (m/s),K (J)" titolo="Kinetic energy grows quadratically with speed."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
K &= \frac12 mv^2 = \frac{2\cdot 3^2}{2}\,\mathrm{J} \\[6pt]
&= 9\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Kinetic energy depends on mass and the square of speed. Square $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ before multiplying by half the mass. The value $9\,\mathrm{J}$ is non-negative, as expected from positive mass and squared velocity. In the product of units, kilograms times squared metres per squared second equals joules. A cart of the same mass moving twice as fast would have four times this energy.

</div>
