## Exercise 1

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart travels at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is its kinetic energy?

$$
K=\frac12mv^2=\frac{2\cdot3^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=9\,\mathrm J.
$$

::grafico{f="x^2" punti="3,9,pieno,A;6,36,pieno,B" xmin=0 xmax=6.7 ymin=0 ymax=44 nomi="v (m/s),K (J)" titolo="For the 2 kg cart, doubling the speed quadruples the vertical coordinate."}

<div class="spiegazione">

It is the speed that is squared. After finding $3^2$, multiply it by half the mass. In this example the factor multiplying the square has numerical value one in the chosen units, so the point at the given speed has vertical coordinate $9\,\mathrm J$ on the graph.

The same cart at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ would have $36\,\mathrm J$. Doubling the speed therefore quadruples the energy needed to bring the cart from rest to that speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Cart A has mass $18\,\mathrm{kg}$ and travels at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Cart B has mass $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ and travels the opposite way at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Which cart has greater kinetic energy?

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_A&=\frac{18\cdot4^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=144\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
K_B&=\frac{8\cdot6^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=144\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The carts have the same kinetic energy. B is faster and lighter. Mass alone cannot settle the comparison because the square of the speed also matters. Direction does not change the comparison either because the square of the velocity component keeps its value when the sign is reversed.

As a check, the ratio of the masses of A and B is $\dfrac94$. The ratio of the squared speeds of B and A is also $\dfrac94$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart has kinetic energy $100\,\mathrm J$. How much kinetic energy must be removed to bring its speed to the limit of $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_{\mathrm{lim}}&=\frac{8\cdot4^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=64\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
K_i-K_{\mathrm{lim}}&=(100-64)\,\mathrm J=36\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For fixed mass, the limiting speed corresponds to a particular energy. First find that energy, then take its difference from the initial $100\,\mathrm J$. The $36\,\mathrm J$ is the energy to be removed. The signed change is $\Delta K=-36\,\mathrm J$.

The initial speed reconstructed from $\sqrt{\dfrac{2K_i}{m}}$ is $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The ratio of the energies is $\dfrac{64}{100}$, equal to the square of the ratio of final to initial speed.

</div>
