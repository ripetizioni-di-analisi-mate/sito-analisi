## Exercise 1

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ object speeds up from $3$ to $7\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the net work done by the forces?

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_i&=\frac{2\cdot3^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=9\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
K_f&=\frac{2\cdot7^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=49\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(49-9)\,\mathrm J=40\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2" punti="3,9,pieno,I;7,49,pieno,F" segmenti="3,9,7,9;7,9,7,49" xmin=0 xmax=8.4 ymin=0 ymax=60 nomi="v (m/s),K (J)" titolo="The vertical segment between the two values represents the change in energy."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Net work is the difference between the kinetic energies of the two states. Find the energy at each endpoint and subtract the initial value from the final one. On the graph this is the change in height between I and F. It is positive because the object speeds up.

An algebraic check uses $v_f^2-v_i^2=(v_f-v_i)(v_f+v_i)$. With the given speeds, the numerical factors are $4$ and $10$, again giving $40\,\mathrm J$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ sled slows from $9$ to $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ over $12\,\mathrm m$ on a horizontal surface. A constant braking force is the only force doing work. What is its magnitude?

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\frac42(3^2-9^2)\,\mathrm J=-144\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
-f\cdot12\,\mathrm m&=-144\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
f&=\frac{144}{12}\,\mathrm N=12\,\mathrm N.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The loss of kinetic energy comes entirely from the braking force. Its work is negative because it points opposite to the displacement. The force magnitude is positive and equals the energy removed divided by the distance covered.

The result can be checked using dynamics. A magnitude of $12\,\mathrm N$ on $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ gives an acceleration of $-3\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ along the direction of motion. The relation $v_f^2=v_i^2+2as$ gives $9\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, the square of the final speed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart initially travels at $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. In the first section the resultant force does $24\,\mathrm J$ of work. In the next horizontal section the only force along the track is friction of magnitude $7\,\mathrm N$, directed against the motion. How far does it travel in this section before stopping?

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_i&=\frac{2\cdot5^2}{2}\,\mathrm J=25\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
K_B&=(25+24)\,\mathrm J=49\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
d&=\frac{49\,\mathrm J}{7\,\mathrm N}=7\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first section increases the kinetic energy from $25$ to $49\,\mathrm J$. On the rough surface, friction removes $7\,\mathrm J$ for each metre travelled. The cart stops once this work has removed all the energy it carried into the section. Divide $49\,\mathrm J$ by $7\,\mathrm N$.

To check the whole path, add the positive work in the first section and the negative work in the second. The result is $24-49=-25\,\mathrm J$, consistent with losing all the original energy.

</div>
