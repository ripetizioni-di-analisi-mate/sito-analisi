## Exercise 1

A machine takes in $500\,\mathrm J$ and supplies $350\,\mathrm J$ of useful energy. What is its percentage efficiency?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 vettori="0,1.5,2.2,1.5,500 J;2.2,1.5,4.3,2.5,350 J;2.2,1.5,4.3,0.5" punti="2.2,1.5,pieno,M" xmin=-0.3 xmax=4.65 ymin=0.15 ymax=2.9 titolo="The input on the left splits into useful output above and other energy below."}

$$
\eta=\frac{350}{500}=0.70,\qquad \eta=70\%.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare the useful contribution with all the energy supplied in the same process. Because both quantities are energies, their units cancel. The quotient $0.70$ corresponds to $70\%$.

The diagram shows that the input splits into useful output and other forms of energy. The latter can be found by subtraction. The non-useful energy is $500-350=150\,\mathrm J$, or $30\%$ of the input. The two percentages add to one hundred percent.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An actuator must supply $1440\,\mathrm J$ of useful work in $6\,\mathrm s$. Its efficiency is $80\%$. What average power must it draw during the operation?

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_i&=\frac{1440}{0.80}\,\mathrm J=1800\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
\mathcal P_i&=\frac{1800}{6}\,\mathrm W=300\,\mathrm W.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Efficiency multiplies the input energy to give the useful energy. To find the input, divide the useful output by $0.80$. The average input power is this energy divided by the $6\,\mathrm s$ over which the operation runs.

The calculations can be reversed. The average useful power is $1440$ divided by $6$, or $240\,\mathrm W$. The ratio of $240$ to $300$ is $0.80$, consistent with the efficiency. The average input power must therefore exceed the useful power.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A first device draws $5000\,\mathrm J$ and passes all its useful output to a second device. Their efficiencies are $80\%$ and $75\%$, respectively. How much useful energy leaves the second device and what is the overall efficiency?

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_1&=0.80\cdot5000\,\mathrm J=4000\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
E_2&=0.75\cdot4000\,\mathrm J=3000\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
\eta&=\frac{3000}{5000}=0.60.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second efficiency applies to the energy leaving the first device. Since the second input is $4000\,\mathrm J$, the final useful output is $3000\,\mathrm J$. Overall efficiency is measured against the original $5000\,\mathrm J$, so it is $60\%$.

As a check, the first device transfers $1000\,\mathrm J$ to other forms and the second transfers another $1000\,\mathrm J$. The total difference of $2000\,\mathrm J$ is also found by comparing the original input and the final output.

</div>
