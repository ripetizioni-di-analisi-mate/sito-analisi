## Exercise 1

A constant force of $20\,\mathrm N$ makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with a straight-line displacement of $3\,\mathrm m$. How much work does it do?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-0.2,0 3.55,0" vettori="0,0,3,0,s;0,0,1.25,2.165,F" segmenti="1.25,0,1.25,2.165" etichette="1.4,-0.28,3 m;0.32,0.18,60°;0.89,1.5,20 N" xmin=-0.4 xmax=3.85 ymin=-0.53 ymax=2.55 titolo="The projection of F along s determines the work."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_\parallel&=20\cos60^\circ\,\mathrm N=10\,\mathrm N,\\[6pt]
W&=10\cdot3\,\mathrm J=30\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The work depends on the part of the force along the displacement. The cosine of $60^\circ$ is positive and equals one half, so the parallel component points along the motion and has half the force magnitude. The perpendicular component does no work because there is no displacement along its direction.

As a check, a force pointing entirely along the motion would give $20\cdot3=60\,\mathrm J$. The result of $30\,\mathrm J$ is half that value, consistent with the projection.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A platform lifts a $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ box vertically through $2\,\mathrm m$ at constant speed. With $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$, how much work do the normal force and gravity do?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=mg=30\,\mathrm N,\\[6pt]
W_N&=30\cdot2\,\mathrm J=+60\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
W_g&=-30\cdot2\,\mathrm J=-60\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The box rises without accelerating, so the platform exerts a force equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to its weight. The normal force points upwards, along the displacement of the box. Gravity points downwards. Its work is negative and has the same magnitude as the work of the platform.

The two contributions add to zero, consistent with constant speed. The support force does work because the support moves in the direction of the force here.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A constant force has components of $8\,\mathrm N$ to the right and $6\,\mathrm N$ upwards. An object moves $3\,\mathrm m$ to the right and then $4\,\mathrm m$ downwards. What is the total work done by this force?

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_1&=8\cdot3\,\mathrm J=+24\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
W_2&=6\cdot(-4)\,\mathrm J=-24\,\mathrm J,\\[6pt]
W&=W_1+W_2=0\,\mathrm J.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal section uses only the horizontal force component, which points along the motion. The vertical section uses the vertical component, which points against the motion. The two products have equal magnitudes and opposite signs, so the total work done by the force is zero.

As a check, the force is constant, so the total displacement with components $3$ and $-4\,\mathrm m$ can be used. Multiplying the matching components and adding them again gives zero.

</div>
