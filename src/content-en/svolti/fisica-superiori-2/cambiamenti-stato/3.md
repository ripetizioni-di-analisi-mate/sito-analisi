## Exercise 1

A mass of water of $0.02\,\mathrm{kg}$, already at $100\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$, vaporises completely at normal atmospheric pressure. Use $L_v=2.26\cdot10^{6}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How much heat is needed?

::grafico{linee="0,100 45.2,100" punti="0,100,pieno;45.2,100,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=50 ymin=95 ymax=106 nomi="Q (kJ),T (°C)" titolo="Energy rises by 45.2 kJ during vaporisation."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= L m \\[6pt]
&= \left(2.26\cdot10^{6}\right) \left(0.02\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 45200\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water starts at $100\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$, so we calculate only the vaporisation energy. The value $L_v=2.26\cdot10^{6}\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ refers to one kilogram, while the available mass is $0.02\,\mathrm{kg}$. This is one fiftieth of a kilogram. Heat is absorbed and the product is in joules.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Water already boiling has mass $0.25\,\mathrm{kg}$. If it absorbs $339\,\mathrm{kJ}$, how much becomes steam and how much remains liquid? Use $L_v=2260\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}m_{\mathrm v}&=\frac{339}{2260}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.15\,\mathrm{kg}\\m_{\mathrm a}&=(0.25-0.15)\,\mathrm{kg}=0.10\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use latent heat because the process starts at the boiling point. The energy is insufficient to vaporise 0.25 kilograms, which would require 565 kilojoules. The quotient gives the part that changes phase. Subtract it from the starting mass to get the liquid part.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Vaporise $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water already boiling, using $500\,\mathrm W$ of useful power. Use $L_v=2260\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. How much time is needed without losses?

$$
\begin{aligned}Q&=0.10\cdot2260\,\mathrm{kJ}=226\,\mathrm{kJ}\\t&=\frac{226000}{500}\,\mathrm s=452\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Latent heat gives the threshold energy, while power sets how quickly it is supplied. Convert the energy to joules before dividing by watts. The result is seven minutes and thirty-two seconds.

</div>
