## Exercise 1

Ice of mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, already at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$, absorbs constant power $250\,\mathrm W$ without heat loss. Use $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How long does complete melting take?

::grafico{f="0.25*x" punti="267.2,66.8,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=280 ymin=0 ymax=76 nomi="t (s),Q (kJ)" titolo="The vertical coordinate at the end of melting is 66.8 kJ."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{L m}{P} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(334000\right) \left(0.2\right)}{\left(250\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 267.2\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ice of mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ is already at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$, so the process begins on the plateau of the heating curve. Multiplying by $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ gives the energy threshold of $66\,800\,\mathrm J$. The power $250\,\mathrm W$ is the energy absorbed each second. The duration is that threshold divided by this slope.

The heater transfers $0.250\,\mathrm{kJ}$ every second. On the graph, the height of $66.8\,\mathrm{kJ}$ identifies the time at which melting ends. Multiplying $267.2\,\mathrm s$ by the useful power recovers the full energy needed for the transition.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $400\,\mathrm W$ device transfers $50\%$ of its power to the ice. With $m=0.12\,\mathrm{kg}$, $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and ice already at zero, find the melting time.

$$
\begin{aligned}P_{\mathrm u}&=0.50\cdot400\,\mathrm W=200\,\mathrm W\\t&=\frac{0.12\cdot334000}{200}\,\mathrm s=200.4\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The threshold depends only on the mass and the substance. Efficiency changes the energy arriving each second. So divide by two hundred useful watts. Using input power would give half the time.

Over $200.4\,\mathrm s$ the apparatus draws $80.16\,\mathrm{kJ}$ of electrical energy. The ice receives half of this, or $40.08\,\mathrm{kJ}$. This check distinguishes the energy drawn by the apparatus from the energy that actually reaches the ice.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Melt $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at zero. The device transfers $250\,\mathrm W$ for $80\,\mathrm s$, then $350\,\mathrm W$. Use $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How many seconds are needed after the power change?

$$
\begin{aligned}Q_{\mathrm{tot}}&=0.15\cdot334000\,\mathrm J=50100\,\mathrm J\\Q_1&=250\cdot80\,\mathrm J=20000\,\mathrm J\\t_2&=\frac{50100-20000}{350}\,\mathrm s=86\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first interval does not complete melting. Subtract the first contribution to find the missing energy. The second duration is that energy divided by the new power. The question asks only about the second interval, while the total time is 166 seconds.

The second interval supplies $350\cdot86\,\mathrm J=30100\,\mathrm J$. Added to the $20000\,\mathrm J$ from the first stage, this reaches the threshold. Changing the power changes the slope of $Q(t)$ while the total energy required by the same mass stays fixed.

</div>
