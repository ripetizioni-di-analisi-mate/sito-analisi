## Exercise 1

A mass of water of $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ freezes completely at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$ at normal atmospheric pressure. With $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, what is the heat for the sample, taken as positive if absorbed?

::grafico{f="-334*x" punti="0.2,-66.8,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=0.26 ymin=-90 ymax=0 nomi="m (kg),Q (kJ)" titolo="In the sample’s balance, Q is negative during freezing."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= - L m \\[6pt]
&= - \left(334000\right) \left(0.2\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq -66800\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ goes from liquid to solid at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$. Its heat-transfer magnitude is the mass times $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. The convention gives a negative sign to released heat. Surroundings taking all this energy would have a positive contribution of the same magnitude in their balance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cooling device removes $100.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$ from water already at zero. With $L_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$, determine the mass frozen and the sign of $Q$ for the water.

$$
Q=-100.2\,\mathrm{kJ},\qquad m=\frac{|Q|}{L_f}=\frac{100.2}{334}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.30\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The specified heat transfer is outward, so it is negative in the signed equation. Mass is positive and follows from the magnitude. If the initial mass were lower than the calculated amount, a further cooling stage beyond freezing would be necessary.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A mass of $0.15\,\mathrm{kg}$ freezes while $0.040\,\mathrm{kg}$ melts in a separate compartment, both at zero. Find the signed net heat for both samples with $L_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$.

$$
Q_{\mathrm{net}}=(-0.15+0.040)\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=-36.74\,\mathrm{kJ}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mass freezing releases energy and the mass melting absorbs it. Since the latent heat is the same in both processes, factor it out. More mass freezes, so the samples together release energy.

</div>
