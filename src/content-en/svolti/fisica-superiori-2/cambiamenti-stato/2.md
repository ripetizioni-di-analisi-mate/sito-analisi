## Exercise 1

Supply $6680\,\mathrm J$ to ice at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$ as it melts. Use $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ and assume enough ice is available. What mass melts?

::grafico{f="x/334" punti="6.68,0.02,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=10 ymin=0 ymax=0.035 nomi="Q (kJ),m (kg)" titolo="Point A gives the mass converted."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{Q}{L} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(6680\right)}{\left(334000\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 0.02\,\mathrm{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sample is already at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$. During the phase change all the energy $6680\,\mathrm J$ converts some of the ice. Dividing by $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ gives the melted mass, twenty grams. That amount is possible because the problem assumes there is at least as much ice available as the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A tray contains $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at its melting point. It absorbs $33.4\,\mathrm{kJ}$ with $L_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. Find the melted mass and corresponding fraction.

$$
\begin{aligned}m_{\mathrm{f}}&=\frac{33.4}{334}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.10\,\mathrm{kg}\\ f&=\frac{0.10}{0.20}=0.50.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The energy units match, so conversion from kilojoules to joules is unnecessary. The heat is one tenth of the amount for one kilogram, so 0.10 kilograms melts. That is half the available mass, and both phases remain at zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A source supplies $36\,\mathrm{kJ}$ to $0.10\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at zero, with $L_f=334\,\mathrm{kJ/kg}$. Find the melted mass and the energy left after melting.

$$
\begin{aligned}Q_{\mathrm{soglia}}&=0.10\cdot334\,\mathrm{kJ}=33.4\,\mathrm{kJ}\\m_{\mathrm f}&=0.10\,\mathrm{kg}\\Q_{\mathrm{resto}}&=(36-33.4)\,\mathrm{kJ}=2.6\,\mathrm{kJ}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the entire supplied energy by latent heat would exceed the mass available. Apply the limit set by the mass present first. Once all the ice has melted, the remaining energy warms the liquid if there are no other losses.

</div>
