## Exercise 1

A mass of ice of $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, already at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$, melts completely at normal atmospheric pressure. Use $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How much heat does it absorb?

::grafico{linee="0,0 66.8,0" punti="0,0,pieno;66.8,0,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=73 ymin=-3 ymax=4 nomi="Q (kJ),T (°C)" titolo="During melting of 0.2 kg, temperature stays at zero."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= L m \\[6pt]
&= \left(334000\right) \left(0.2\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 66800\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required transfer starts at the melting point. The mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ starts at $0\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$, and temperature is constant while ice and water coexist. The value $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ is the energy needed per kilogram. One fifth of a kilogram requires one fifth of it. The product is positive because energy enters the system.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Melt $120\,\mathrm g$ of ice at zero. With $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, express the energy in kilojoules. Compare a second container at the same temperature holding twice the mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}Q_1&=0.120\cdot334000\,\mathrm J=40.08\,\mathrm{kJ}\\ Q_2&=2Q_1=80.16\,\mathrm{kJ}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert the mass in grams because latent heat is per kilogram. For the same material and conditions, energy doubles with mass. Both processes take place at approximately constant temperature during melting.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Melt $40\%$ of a $0.18\,\mathrm{kg}$ quantity of ice at zero. Use $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. Calculate the required energy and how much mass remains solid.

$$
\begin{aligned}m_{\mathrm{f}}&=0.40\cdot0.18\,\mathrm{kg}=0.072\,\mathrm{kg}\\ Q&=0.072\cdot334000\,\mathrm J=24.048\,\mathrm{kJ}\\m_{\mathrm{g}}&=(0.18-0.072)\,\mathrm{kg}=0.108\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The melting term concerns only the mass that changes phase, forty per cent. The remaining ice is the other fraction, sixty per cent. The sum of ice and water conserves the initial mass.

</div>
