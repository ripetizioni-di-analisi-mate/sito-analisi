## Exercise 1

Mix $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $10\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$ with $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at zero degrees Celsius in an insulated vessel of negligible heat capacity. Use $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How much ice melts?

::grafico{f="0.84*x;66.8" punti="10,8.4,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=94 ymin=0 ymax=78 nomi="T_a (°C),Q (kJ)" legenda="Q_\mathrm{avail};Q_\mathrm{melt}" titolo="Point A shows that the energy released by 0.2 kg of water is below the threshold."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{\mathrm{fusa}} &= \frac{T_a c m_a}{L} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(10\right) \left(4200\right) \left(0.2\right)}{\left(334000\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 0.0251497\,\mathrm{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The liquid water of mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ can cool from $10\,\mathrm{^{\circ}C}$ to zero. With $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ it can release $8400\,\mathrm J$. Completely melting $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ would require $66\,800\,\mathrm J$ with $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. The first quantity is smaller than the second. Thus ice and water remain at zero at equilibrium. The available energy divided by $L_f$ gives the melted mass.

As a check, the water would need to start at about $79.5\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ for all the ice to melt, found by dividing $66800\,\mathrm J$ by the water’s heat capacity of $840\,\mathrm{J/K}$. The stated $10\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is far below that threshold.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an insulated vessel of negligible heat capacity, mix $0.4\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice at zero. Use $c_a=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$. How much mass remains as ice?

$$
\begin{aligned}Q_{\mathrm{disp}}&=0.4\cdot4200\cdot25\,\mathrm J=42000\,\mathrm J\\Q_{\mathrm{soglia}}&=0.2\cdot334000\,\mathrm J=66800\,\mathrm J\\m_{\mathrm{resto}}&=\left(0.2-\frac{42000}{334000}\right)\mathrm{kg}\simeq0.0743\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water cannot release enough energy to melt all the ice. The quotient of available heat and latent heat is the melted mass, about 126 grams. The question asks for its complement out of the original 200 grams, about 74 grams.

The remaining mass can also be found from the shortfall in energy for complete melting. The threshold exceeds the available energy by $24800\,\mathrm J$. Dividing this difference by $334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$ again gives about $0.0743\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An insulated vessel of negligible heat capacity contains $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $32\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and ice at zero. With $c_a=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J/kg}$, what is the largest mass of ice that can melt completely?

$$
m_{\mathrm{soglia}}=\frac{0.50\cdot4200\cdot32}{334000}\,\mathrm{kg}\simeq0.201\,\mathrm{kg}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the maximum mass, all the energy released as the water cools to zero goes into melting, with none left over. A greater mass leaves solid ice. A smaller mass can melt fully and leave energy to warm the liquid above zero.

The water releases $67.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$ by cooling to zero. Melting $0.200\,\mathrm{kg}$ of ice would take $66.8\,\mathrm{kJ}$. This comparison confirms that the desired mass is just above $0.200\,\mathrm{kg}$, consistent with the ratio calculated above.

</div>
