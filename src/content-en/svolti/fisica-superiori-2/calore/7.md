## Exercise 1

A plane wall has area $3\,\mathrm{m^2}$, thickness $0.02\,\mathrm m$ and thermal conductivity $0.04\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$. Its faces differ in temperature by $12\,\mathrm K$. What is the power transferred by conduction in steady state?

::grafico{linee="0,22 2,10" punti="0,22,pieno;2,10,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=2.4 ymin=7 ymax=24 nomi="x (cm),T (°C)" titolo="The drop is 12 K over 2 cm. The absolute positions on the temperature scale shown are one compatible example."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P &= \frac{A \Delta T \lambda}{d} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(3\right) \left(12\right) \left(0.04\right)}{\left(0.02\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 72\,\mathrm{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In steady state the power is constant through the thickness $0.02\,\mathrm m$. Divide the difference $12\,\mathrm K$ by the thickness to obtain the magnitude of the temperature gradient. Multiplying the gradient by the conductivity $0.04\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$ gives the power per unit area, which is multiplied by $3\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The positive power here is its magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a plane wall of area $4\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and conductivity $0.04\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$, the steady power is to be limited to $20\,\mathrm W$ across $15\,\mathrm K$. Find the minimum thickness.

$$
d_{\min}=\frac{kA\Delta T}{P_{\max}}=\frac{0.04\cdot4\cdot15}{20}\,\mathrm m=0.12\,\mathrm m.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A limiting power is stated, so isolate the thickness in the conduction equation. Power decreases with thickness, so equality identifies the lower threshold. At 12 centimetres the model gives exactly 20 watts. A greater thickness reduces the power further.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two layers in series have resistances of $0.30$ and $0.70\,\mathrm{K/W}$. The hot face of the first is at $26\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and the cold face of the second at $6\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Find the power and interface temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}P&=\frac{26-6}{0.30+0.70}\,\mathrm W=20\,\mathrm W\\[6pt]\Delta T_1&=20\cdot0.30\,\mathrm K=6\,\mathrm K\\ T_I&=20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since power is the same through both layers, the total drop divides in proportion to their resistances. The first layer has the lower resistance and a six-kelvin drop. The interface is at twenty degrees. The second layer drops by fourteen kelvins and reaches the stated cold temperature.

</div>
