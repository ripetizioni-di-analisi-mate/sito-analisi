## Exercise 1

A mass of water of $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and a calorimeter of heat capacity $300\,\mathrm{J/K}$ are initially in equilibrium. How much heat is needed to warm both by $11\,\mathrm K$? Use $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and neglect heat losses.

::grafico{f="8.4*x;0.3*x;8.7*x" punti="11,92.4,pieno;11,3.3,pieno;11,95.7,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=13 ymin=0 ymax=116 nomi="ΔT (K),Q (kJ)" legenda="\mathrm{water};\mathrm{calorimeter};\mathrm{total}" titolo="The highest point at 11 K includes both contributions."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= \Delta T \left(C + c m\right) \\[6pt]
&= \left(11\right) \left(\left(300\right) + \left(4200\right) \left(2\right)\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 95700\,\mathrm{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two bodies start at the same temperature and end at the same temperature, so both rise by $11\,\mathrm K$. The water of mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ has heat capacity $mc$ using $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Add the calorimeter capacity $300\,\mathrm{J/K}$. If it were neglected, the calculated energy would be smaller by $300\cdot11\,\mathrm J=3300\,\mathrm J$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A calorimeter of heat capacity $150\,\mathrm{J/K}$ is in equilibrium with $0.50\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water. The system absorbs $18000\,\mathrm J$. With $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and no losses, how much does its temperature increase?

$$
\begin{aligned}C_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(0.50\cdot4200+150)\,\mathrm{J/K}=2250\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]\Delta T&=\frac{18000}{2250}\,\mathrm K=8\,\mathrm K.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Divide the supplied energy by the heat capacity of the combined system. Each kelvin needs 2100 joules for the water plus 150 joules for the calorimeter. Therefore 18,000 joules gives eight kelvins. The water alone would warm more with the same energy.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Place $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $70\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ in an empty calorimeter of heat capacity $420\,\mathrm{J/K}$ initially at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Use $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and assume insulation. Find the common temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}C_a&=0.20\cdot4200\,\mathrm{J/K}=840\,\mathrm{J/K}\\[6pt]T_f&=\frac{840\cdot70+420\cdot20}{840+420}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\simeq53.3\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The water enters hot and releases heat to the container. The calorimeter has a weight in the balance of 420 joules per kelvin. The water has twice that heat capacity, so the common temperature is closer to its initial reading. At the unrounded value, the heat released by the water and that absorbed by the calorimeter are both 14 kJ in magnitude.

</div>
