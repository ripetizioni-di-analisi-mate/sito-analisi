## Exercise 1

A $500\,\mathrm W$ heater transfers $80\%$ of its input energy to the water. How long will it take to warm $0.4\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water by $15\,\mathrm K$? Use $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ and assume that the water stays in the same phase.

::grafico{f="0.4*x" punti="63,25.2,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=78 ymin=0 ymax=34 nomi="t (s),Q (kJ)" titolo="The slope is 0.4 kJ/s. The water data give the vertical coordinate 25.2 kJ."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{\Delta T c m}{P \eta} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(15\right) \left(4200\right) \left(0.4\right)}{\left(500\right) \left(0.8\right)} \\[6pt]
&= 63\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First calculate the energy absorbed by the water from its mass $0.4\,\mathrm{kg}$, increase of $15\,\mathrm K$ and $c=4200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Of the heater’s $500\,\mathrm W$, only the fraction $80\%$ reaches the water. The useful power is thus $400\,\mathrm W$. Dividing the required energy by that power gives the operating time in seconds. Greater useful power would deliver the same energy in less time.

In that time the appliance takes in $31.5\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The amount reaching the water is $25.2\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The remaining $6.3\,\mathrm{kJ}$ is lost.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An $800\,\mathrm W$ heater runs for $50\,\mathrm s$. The vessel is measured to absorb $28\,\mathrm{kJ}$. Find the efficiency and average useful power.

$$
\begin{aligned}E_{\mathrm{in}}&=800\cdot50\,\mathrm J=40\,\mathrm{kJ}\\ \eta&=\frac{28}{40}=0.70\\ P_{\mathrm u}&=\frac{28000}{50}\,\mathrm W=560\,\mathrm W.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rated power determines input energy, which is the reference for the energy that reaches the vessel. The ratio of useful heat to input is below one. Thirty percent of the input energy does not reach the vessel. Useful power is the average over the same time interval.

In fifty seconds, $40\,\mathrm{kJ}$ enters and $28\,\mathrm{kJ}$ reaches the vessel. The remaining $12\,\mathrm{kJ}$ corresponds to an average power loss of $240\,\mathrm W$. Adding this to the useful power recovers the rated $800\,\mathrm W$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

To transfer $48\,\mathrm{kJ}$, compare a $600\,\mathrm W$ device with efficiency $0.80$ and a $1000\,\mathrm W$ device with efficiency $0.40$. Which finishes sooner and by how much?

$$
\begin{aligned} P_1&=480\,\mathrm W,\qquad P_2=400\,\mathrm W\\ t_1&=\frac{48000}{480}\,\mathrm s=100\,\mathrm s\\ t_2&=\frac{48000}{400}\,\mathrm s=120\,\mathrm s.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relevant power is the useful power for the required transfer. Although the second device has the higher rated power, its useful power is lower. For the same required energy the time is inversely proportional to useful power. The first device finishes twenty seconds sooner.

The ratio of the second time to the first is $\dfrac{480}{400}=1.20$, the inverse ratio of useful powers. The second takes twenty per cent longer than the first appliance’s one hundred seconds.

</div>
