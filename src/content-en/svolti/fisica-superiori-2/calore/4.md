## Exercise 1

A sample of mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ absorbs $1000\,\mathrm J$ and its temperature rises by $11\,\mathrm K$ without changing phase. Determine its specific heat capacity.

::grafico{f="(1/11)*x" punti="11,1,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=15 ymin=0 ymax=1.5 nomi="ΔT (K),Q (kJ)" titolo="Point A first gives the heat capacity of the entire sample."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= \frac{Q}{\Delta T m} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(1000\right)}{\left(11\right) \left(0.2\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 454.545\,\mathrm{J/(kg K)}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sample absorbs $1000\,\mathrm J$ and stays in the same phase. Dividing the energy by its increase of $11\,\mathrm K$ gives the energy required for a one-kelvin rise of the entire sample. Its mass is $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$, so divide that result by the mass to obtain a value per kilogram. The result is in joules per kilogram per kelvin.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ sample in a container of heat capacity $90\,\mathrm{J/K}$ receives $5400\,\mathrm J$ together with the container. Both warm by $12\,\mathrm K$. Find the sample’s $c$ in the absence of losses.

$$
\begin{aligned}C_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\frac{5400}{12}\,\mathrm{J/K}=450\,\mathrm{J/K}\\ c&=\frac{450-90}{0.30}\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}=1200\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The supplied energy also warmed the container. Determine the combined heat capacity from the measurement and separate the 90 joules per kelvin for the container. The difference of 360 joules per kelvin refers to the whole sample. Dividing by the mass expresses it per kilogram.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ sample cools by $7\,\mathrm K$ and releases $2520\,\mathrm J$. Calculate $c$ and explain its sign.

$$
c=\frac{-2520}{0.80\cdot(-7)}\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}=450\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both the heat for the sample and its temperature change are negative. Their ratio is positive. This means that raising it by one kelvin would require energy input, as expected for the material over the range considered.

</div>
