## Exercise 1

A sample of mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and specific heat capacity $500\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ absorbs $1500\,\mathrm J$ without changing phase. What is its temperature increase?

::grafico{f="10*x" punti="1.5,15,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=2 ymin=0 ymax=25 nomi="Q (kJ),ΔT (K)" titolo="The slope is the reciprocal of heat capacity, after conversion to kJ."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta T &= \frac{Q}{c m} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(1500\right)}{\left(500\right) \left(0.2\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 15\,\mathrm{K}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the mass $0.2\,\mathrm{kg}$ by $500\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ gives a heat capacity of $100\,\mathrm{J/K}$. Each kelvin therefore requires one hundred joules. Since the sample receives $1500\,\mathrm J$ under those conditions and remains in the same phase, divide by one hundred to get the number of kelvins. The quotient is positive because the sample warms.

In the same model a rise of $30\,\mathrm K$ would require $3000\,\mathrm J$. Direct proportionality applies while the specific heat capacity can be treated as constant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object with $C=600\,\mathrm{J/K}$ at $42\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ releases $9000\,\mathrm J$. Find the final temperature.

$$
\begin{aligned}\Delta T&=\frac{Q}{C}=\frac{-9000}{600}\,\mathrm K=-15\,\mathrm K\\ T_f&=27\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The word *releases* means $Q$ is negative for the object. Dividing by the heat capacity gives a negative change, to be added to the initial temperature. A difference of fifteen kelvins has the same numerical size in degrees Celsius.

Subtracting the initial reading from the final reading recovers $-15\,\mathrm K$. Multiplying by $600\,\mathrm{J/K}$ gives $-9000\,\mathrm J$. If the heat magnitude were inserted with a positive sign, it would predict warming, which goes against the direction of transfer.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two samples of the same substance have masses of $0.30$ and $0.90\,\mathrm{kg}$. The first warms by $18\,\mathrm K$ after a heat transfer. How much does the second warm if it receives the same heat and there are no changes of phase?

$$
\begin{aligned}m_1c\Delta T_1&=m_2c\Delta T_2\\[6pt]\Delta T_2&=\frac{0.30}{0.90}\cdot18\,\mathrm K=6\,\mathrm K.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The specific heat capacity is common and the second sample has three times the mass. Raising its temperature by one kelvin takes three times as much energy. At the same energy, its temperature rise is therefore one third of the first sample’s.

To check the result, multiply each mass by its own temperature rise. This gives $0.30\cdot18=5.4\,\mathrm{kg\,K}$ and $0.90\cdot6=5.4\,\mathrm{kg\,K}$. Multiplying both results by the common specific heat capacity recovers equal amounts of heat.

</div>
