## Exercise 1

An object of mass $0.4\,\mathrm{kg}$ has specific heat capacity $900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. Find its heat capacity.

::grafico{f="0.9*x" punti="0.4,0.36,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=0.6 ymin=0 ymax=0.6 nomi="m (kg),C (kJ/K)" titolo="The slope gives the specific heat capacity."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= c m \\[6pt]
&= \left(900\right) \left(0.4\right) \\[6pt]
&\simeq 360\,\mathrm{J/K}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value $900\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$ refers to one kilogram of material. The sample has mass $0.4\,\mathrm{kg}$, so its heat capacity is four tenths of the value for one kilogram. The product is measured in joules per kelvin and is the energy the whole object requires for a one-degree rise.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two parts in thermal equilibrium have heat capacities $220$ and $380\,\mathrm{J/K}$. They warm together by $5\,\mathrm K$. What is their total heat capacity and the heat absorbed?

$$
\begin{aligned}C_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(220+380)\,\mathrm{J/K}=600\,\mathrm{J/K}\\ Q&=600\cdot5\,\mathrm J=3000\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two parts undergo the same temperature rise. Each contribution is its part’s heat capacity times that same interval. Factoring out the interval gives the sum of the heat capacities. As a check, the first part takes 1100 joules and the second 1900.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A container absorbs $4200\,\mathrm J$ while warming by $14\,\mathrm K$. Assuming constant heat capacity, what heat does it release when cooling by $9\,\mathrm K$?

$$
\begin{aligned}C&=\frac{4200}{14}\,\mathrm{J/K}=300\,\mathrm{J/K}\\ Q&=300\cdot(-9)\,\mathrm J=-2700\,\mathrm J.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first measurement gives the coefficient linking energy and temperature for the whole container. In the second process that same coefficient multiplies a negative interval. The heat in the container’s convention is negative and 2700 joules are released.

</div>
