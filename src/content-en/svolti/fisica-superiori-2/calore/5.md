## Exercise 1

Mix $2\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $60\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The system is insulated and the container has negligible heat capacity. What is the final temperature?

::grafico{curve="t,3*(60-t),20,60;t,2*(t-20),20,60" punti="44,48,pieno,E" xmin=20 xmax=68 ymin=0 ymax=125 nomi="Final T (°C),|Q|/c (kg K)" legenda="\mathrm{released};\mathrm{absorbed}" titolo="At the intersection the two parts exchange equal magnitudes of energy."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_e &= \frac{T_1 m_1 + T_2 m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left(20\right) \left(2\right) + \left(60\right) \left(3\right)}{\left(2\right) + \left(3\right)} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 44\,{}^\circ\mathrm C
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since this is the same material, its specific heat capacity is a common factor in both contributions and cancels. The hot mass $3\,\mathrm{kg}$ outweighs the cold mass $2\,\mathrm{kg}$, so the final temperature is closer to $60\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ than to $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. It lies in the required interval. Dividing both magnitudes by $c$ gives $48\,\mathrm{kg\,K}$ on each side of the balance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Water at $40\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is to be obtained by mixing $0.60\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $24\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with water at $64\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The container is insulated and has negligible heat capacity. How much water at $64\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is needed?

$$
\begin{aligned}m_hc(64-40)&=0.60c(40-24)\\[6pt]m_h&=0.60\cdot\frac{16}{24}\,\mathrm{kg}=0.40\,\mathrm{kg}.\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the balance of magnitudes, the hot water loses twenty-four kelvins and the cold water gains sixteen. The specific heat capacity cancels since this is the same substance. A greater interval requires a smaller hot mass for balance. Substitution gives 9.6 kilogram kelvins on both sides before multiplication by specific heat capacity.

The final mixture has mass $1.00\,\mathrm{kg}$. As a separate check, its temperature is recovered by averaging $24$ and $64\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ with respective weights $0.60$ and $0.40$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three amounts of water of $0.20$, $0.50$ and $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $15$, $35$ and $85\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ are combined in an insulated container of negligible heat capacity. Find the equilibrium temperature.

$$
T_f=\frac{0.20\cdot15+0.50\cdot35+0.30\cdot85}{0.20+0.50+0.30}\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=46\,{}^\circ\mathrm C.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each of the three amounts contributes its own mass and initial temperature to the balance. Factor out the common final temperature. The amounts initially at 15 and 35 degrees, both below 46, receive energy. The portion at 85 degrees releases the sum of the other two contributions.

Before multiplying by the common specific heat capacity, the two warming portions give $(0.20\cdot31+0.50\cdot11)\,\mathrm{kg\,K}=11.7\,\mathrm{kg\,K}$. The hot portion gives the same magnitude, $0.30\cdot39\,\mathrm{kg\,K}$. This checks that the opposite contributions balance.

</div>
