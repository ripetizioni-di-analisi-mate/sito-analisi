## Exercise 1

A liquid with $\beta_{\mathrm{liq}}=8.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ fills $200\,\mathrm{mL}$ up to a mark. The container has $\beta_{\mathrm{rec}}=4.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Find the apparent expansion for $\Delta T=25\,\mathrm K$.

::grafico{f="0.16*x;0.008*x;0.152*x" legenda="\text{liquid};\text{container};\text{apparent}" punti="25,4,pieno;25,0.2,pieno;25,3.8,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=31 ymin=-1 ymax=5.5 nomi=",ΔV (mL)" etichette="26,-.76,ΔT (K)" titolo="The increase in the liquid volume exceeds the increase in the container volume by 3.8 mL."}

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta V_{\mathrm{liq}}&\simeq(8.0\cdot10^{-4})\cdot200\cdot25\,\mathrm{mL}=4.0\,\mathrm{mL}\\[6pt] \Delta V_{\mathrm{rec}}&\simeq(4.0\cdot10^{-5})\cdot200\cdot25\,\mathrm{mL}=0.20\,\mathrm{mL}\\[6pt] \Delta V_{\mathrm{app}}&\simeq(4.0-0.20)\,\mathrm{mL}=3.8\,\mathrm{mL}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Initially the volume of the liquid and the volume below the mark coincide. Both change upon heating, so calculate them separately with the appropriate volume coefficients. The apparent change measures how much the new volume of the liquid exceeds the new volume up to the same mark. It is a little smaller than the physical change of the liquid.

When warm, the liquid occupies about $204.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ and the mark encloses about $200.20\,\mathrm{mL}$. Subtracting the two final values gives $3.8\,\mathrm{mL}$ again. This makes clear that the reference is the heated mark and that the capacity increase reduces the visible difference.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a liquid with volume $250\,\mathrm{mL}$, an apparent expansion of $5.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ is read for $\Delta T=40\,\mathrm K$. The container has $\beta_{\mathrm{rec}}=4.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. What is $\beta_{\mathrm{liq}}$?

$$
\begin{aligned} \beta_{\mathrm{app}}&\simeq\frac{5.0}{250\cdot40}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}=5.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}\\[6pt] \beta_{\mathrm{liq}}&\simeq\beta_{\mathrm{app}}+\beta_{\mathrm{rec}}=5.4\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The reading relative to the mark gives the apparent coefficient once divided by $V_0\Delta T$. The physical coefficient is greater because part of the liquid expansion is accompanied by expansion of the container. Add the container coefficient. With the value obtained, the product $\beta_{\mathrm{liq}}V_0\Delta T$ gives $5.4\,\mathrm{mL}$.

To check the reconstruction, calculate how much capacity the container gains up to the mark. The product $(4.0\cdot10^{-5})\cdot250\cdot40$ is $0.4\,\mathrm{mL}$. Subtracting this contribution from the $5.4\,\mathrm{mL}$ physical increase recovers the observed $5.0\,\mathrm{mL}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $400\,\mathrm{mL}$ liquid has $\beta_{\mathrm{liq}}=3.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. The container has $\beta_{\mathrm{rec}}=1.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. At constant pressure the system cools by $20\,\mathrm K$. Find the apparent change.

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta V_{\mathrm{app}}&\simeq(\beta_{\mathrm{liq}}-\beta_{\mathrm{rec}})V_0\Delta T\\[6pt] &=(2.0\cdot10^{-4})\cdot400\cdot(-20)\,\mathrm{mL}=-1.6\,\mathrm{mL}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both the liquid and its container contract. The liquid has the greater volume coefficient, so its volume decreases by more than the volume up to the mark. The apparent difference is negative. Check it by calculating the changes separately, $-2.4\,\mathrm{mL}$ and $-0.8\,\mathrm{mL}$, and subtracting the second from the first.

After cooling, the liquid occupies about $397.6\,\mathrm{mL}$. The mark now represents $399.2\,\mathrm{mL}$. The liquid volume is smaller than that reference volume, so the meniscus lies below the mark. The difference in volume equals the magnitude of $\Delta V_{\mathrm{app}}$.

</div>
