## Exercise 1

An isotropic plate of area $2.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ has $\alpha=2.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. Estimate the area increase for $\Delta T=50\,\mathrm K$.

::grafico{f="0.8*x" punti="50,40,pieno,A" xmin=0 xmax=60 ymin=0 ymax=51 nomi="ΔT (K),ΔA (cm²)" titolo="For this plate the area increases by 40 cm² at 50 K."}

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta A&\simeq2\alpha A_0\Delta T\\[6pt] &=2\cdot(2.0\cdot10^{-5})\cdot2.0\cdot50\,\mathrm{m^2}=0.0040\,\mathrm{m^2}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fractional change of one side is about $0.001$. Since both sides grow in that proportion, the fractional area change is about twice as large. To get the absolute increase, multiply the initial area by $0.002$. The product is in square metres and amounts to $0.2\%$ of the initial area.

The figure gives the same result in square centimetres. One square metre is $10000\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, so $0.0040\,\mathrm{m^2}$ equals $40\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The product of the two linear strains would add only $2\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, or $0.02\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isotropic sheet with a circular hole of area $30\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ warms by $55\,\mathrm K$. With $\alpha=1.2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$, how much does the hole area grow?

$$
\begin{aligned} \frac{\Delta A}{A_0}&\simeq2\alpha\Delta T=2\cdot(1.2\cdot10^{-5})\cdot55\\[6pt] \Delta A&\simeq30\cdot0.00132\,\mathrm{cm^2}=0.0396\,\mathrm{cm^2}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hole is bounded by points on the sheet. Distances between those points grow as though the hole were filled with the same material, so the radius increases. The area depends on the square of the radius, so its fractional change is approximately twice the linear one. Use the initial hole area of thirty square centimetres.

The ratio of final area to initial area is about $1.00132$. It is above one, as expected when the distance between opposite points on the edge increases. A constrained plate could move the rim differently. This calculation assumes uniform free expansion.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An isotropic sheet of area $5\,\mathrm{m^2}$ has $\alpha=2.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. On cooling, its area decreases by $0.006\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Estimate its signed temperature change.

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta T&\simeq \frac{\Delta A}{2\alpha A_0}\\[6pt] &=\frac{-0.006}{2\cdot(2.0\cdot10^{-5})\cdot5}\,\mathrm K=-30\,\mathrm K. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use a negative $\Delta A$ because the final area is smaller than the initial area. The denominator is the area change per kelvin in the approximate equation. Since $\alpha$ is positive, the temperature change and the area change have the same sign. Substituting $-30\,\mathrm K$ into the forward equation recovers the measured decrease.

The slope is $0.0002\,\mathrm{m^2/K}$, so losing $0.006\,\mathrm{m^2}$ requires thirty kelvins of cooling. The plate’s final area would be $4.994\,\mathrm{m^2}$. The change is only $0.12\%$ of the initial area, consistent with the small-strain estimate.

</div>
