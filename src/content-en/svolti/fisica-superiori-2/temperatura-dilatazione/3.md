## Exercise 1

A rod goes from $-9\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to $13\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Find $\Delta T$ in kelvins.

::grafico{f="x+273.15" punti="-9,264.15,pieno,A;13,286.15,pieno,B" xmin=-15 xmax=20 ymin=250 ymax=298 nomi="t_C (°C),T (K)" titolo="From A to B both coordinates increase by 22."}

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta T&=[13-(-9)]\,\mathrm K\\ &=22\,\mathrm K. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference is always the final value minus the initial value. In this case it takes nine degrees Celsius to reach zero and thirteen more to reach the final reading. The total width is twenty-two. On the absolute scale both endpoints increase by $273.15$ in their respective units, and their numerical separation stays the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A probe initially reads $304.15\,\mathrm K$ and finally $18\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Find the signed temperature change.

$$
\begin{aligned} T_f&=(18+273.15)\,\mathrm K=291.15\,\mathrm K\\[6pt] \Delta T&=(291.15-304.15)\,\mathrm K=-13\,\mathrm K. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The readings are measured from different origins, so put them on the same scale before subtracting. Add $273.15$ to the Celsius value for the final reading. The final value is now lower than the initial one. The minus sign indicates cooling, whose magnitude is thirteen kelvins.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A test tube is first warmed by $38\,\mathrm K$, then cooled by $46\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. If it starts at $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, find the final temperature and total change.

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta T_{\mathrm{tot}}&=(38-46)\,\mathrm K=-8\,\mathrm K\\[6pt] t_{C,f}&=(25-8)\,{}^\circ\mathrm C=17\,{}^\circ\mathrm C. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The heating and cooling data are changes, so they can be added with their signs. The second contribution is negative and larger in magnitude, so the sample ends cooler than it started. Applying the same decrease of eight on the Celsius scale gives $17\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. The intermediate temperature would be $63\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$.

</div>
