## Exercise 1

A $4\,\mathrm m$ rod elongates by $2.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ over $50\,\mathrm K$. Estimate its linear expansion coefficient.

::grafico{f="0.048*x" punti="50,2.4,pieno" xmin=0 xmax=60 ymin=0 ymax=3.2 nomi="ΔT (K),ΔL (mm)" titolo="The slope is 0.048 mm/K. Divide it by 4000 mm to obtain the coefficient."}

$$
\begin{aligned} \alpha &\simeq \frac{\Delta L}{L_0\Delta T}\\[6pt] &= \frac{2.4\cdot10^{-3}}{4\cdot50}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}=1.2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The length change must be compared with the initial length. Convert $2.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ to metres and divide it by four metres to get a fractional change of $0.0006$. The rod was heated by fifty kelvins, so the fractional change per kelvin is fifty times smaller. This is the required coefficient.

The graph provides an independent check on the units. The line rises by $2.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ over $50\,\mathrm K$, giving a slope of $0.048\,\mathrm{mm/K}$. Dividing by $4000\,\mathrm{mm}$ recovers the coefficient. Millimetres cancel in the same way as metres in the first calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two readings for an unconstrained rod are $1.5000\,\mathrm m$ at $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ and $1.4988\,\mathrm m$ at $-20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Find $\alpha$.

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta L&=(1.4988-1.5000)\,\mathrm m=-0.0012\,\mathrm m\\[6pt] \Delta T&=(-20-20)\,\mathrm K=-40\,\mathrm K\\[6pt] \alpha&\simeq \frac{-0.0012}{1.5000\cdot(-40)}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}=2.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the same initial instant in both subtractions. The shorter length corresponds to the lower temperature, so both changes are negative. Their quotient is positive. Reversing only the order of the length readings would give the opposite sign and incorrectly suggest expansion on cooling.

With this coefficient the absolute slope is $0.030\,\mathrm{mm/K}$. A fall of $40\,\mathrm K$ therefore gives $-1.20\,\mathrm{mm}$, taking the rod from $1.5000$ to $1.4988\,\mathrm m$. This check uses the same initial length as the measurement.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rod of length $0.80\,\mathrm m$ increases in length by $0.72\,\mathrm{mm}$ over $45\,\mathrm K$. Calculate the coefficient and predict the increase for a $2.00\,\mathrm m$ rod of the same material warmed by $20\,\mathrm K$.

$$
\begin{aligned} \alpha&\simeq\frac{0.00072}{0.80\cdot45}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}=2.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}\\[6pt] \Delta L_2&\simeq(2.0\cdot10^{-5})\cdot2.00\cdot20\,\mathrm m=0.80\,\mathrm{mm}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first measurement determines the material coefficient because both the initial length and the temperature change are known. In the subsequent prediction the coefficient remains the same while both of those quantities change. The second rod is two and a half times as long. It warms by less, so its expansion is only slightly greater than the measured one.

The two extensions can also be compared without finding another coefficient. Their ratio is the product of the length ratio and the heating ratio, $\dfrac{2.00}{0.80}\dfrac{20}{45}=\dfrac{10}{9}$. Multiplying the measured extension by $\dfrac{10}{9}$ gives $0.80\,\mathrm{mm}$ again.

</div>
