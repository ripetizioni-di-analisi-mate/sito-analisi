## Exercise 1

At dawn, an outdoor thermometer reads $-12\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Express the temperature in kelvins.

::grafico{f="x+273.15" punti="-12,261.15,pieno,A;0,273.15,pieno" xmin=-18 xmax=12 ymin=248 ymax=286 nomi="t_C (°C),T (K)" titolo="Point A gives the thermometer reading after conversion."}

$$
\begin{aligned} T&=t_C+273.15\\ T&=(-12+273.15)\,\mathrm K=261.15\,\mathrm K. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The indicated value is below zero Celsius. Absolute zero is lower, so the reading in kelvins is still positive. On the conversion line the point at $-12$ is $12$ units to the left of $0$, and the line has slope one. Its vertical coordinate is therefore $12$ units below $273.15$. The coordinate pair is $(-12, 261.15)$.

As a check, subtract $273.15$ from $261.15$. This recovers the value $-12$ from before the conversion. The interval between the outdoor temperature and zero Celsius is therefore still $12\,\mathrm K$ after the change of scale.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A room sensor has a stated range from $18\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to $22\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. Write the limits in kelvins.

$$
\begin{aligned} T_{\min}&=(18+273.15)\,\mathrm K=291.15\,\mathrm K\\[6pt] T_{\max}&=(22+273.15)\,\mathrm K=295.15\,\mathrm K. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Convert each endpoint because both are temperature values. The shift adds the same constant, so the order of the endpoints is preserved. Check the width of the interval with $295.15-291.15=4$. A range of four degrees Celsius still has a width of four kelvins.

The lower bound becomes $291.15\,\mathrm K$ and the upper bound becomes $295.15\,\mathrm K$. A reading of $293.15\,\mathrm K$ is within range and equally far from both limits. Converting it back gives $20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$, the midpoint of the original interval.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sample at $8\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ is warmed by $17\,\mathrm K$. Write its final temperature in kelvins.

$$
\begin{aligned} T_i&=(8+273.15)\,\mathrm K=281.15\,\mathrm K\\[6pt] T_f&=T_i+\Delta T=(281.15+17)\,\mathrm K=298.15\,\mathrm K. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Celsius reading specifies a point on the scale, so convert it first. The number $17$ gives the distance between the initial and final temperatures on the same scale. Add it to the initial value in kelvins. An alternative check is to add $17$ to the initial Celsius reading and convert $25\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$ to kelvins.

The reverse check compares $298.15\,\mathrm K$ and $281.15\,\mathrm K$. Their difference is exactly $17\,\mathrm K$. Applying the Celsius offset to the heating interval as well would change the difference between the absolute temperatures and fail this check.

</div>
