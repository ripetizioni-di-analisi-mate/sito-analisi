## Exercise 1

An unconstrained brass rod of length $3.0\,\mathrm m$ has $\alpha=2.0\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$. What is its length change when cooled by $25\,\mathrm K$?

::grafico{f="0.06*x" punti="-25,-1.5,pieno,A" xmin=-35 xmax=10 ymin=-2 ymax=0.8 nomi="ΔT (K),ΔL (mm)" titolo="For the stated cooling, point A lies below the horizontal axis."}

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta T&=-25\,\mathrm K\\[6pt] \Delta L&\simeq\alpha L_0\Delta T=(2.0\cdot10^{-5})\cdot3.0\cdot(-25)\,\mathrm m\\[6pt] &=-1.5\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m=-1.5\,\mathrm{mm}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Cooling gives the temperature change a negative sign. Since $\alpha$ is positive, $\Delta L$ is also negative. The rod becomes about one and a half millimetres shorter. The fractional magnitude $\left|\dfrac{\Delta L}{L_0}\right|=0.0005$ is much smaller than one, consistent with the small-deformation model.

Subtracting $0.0015\,\mathrm m$ from the initial length gives about $2.9985\,\mathrm m$. The cooling point lies below the horizontal axis, and the line passes through the origin. Restoring the original temperature would bring the change back to zero in the free-expansion model.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two rods made of the same steel, of lengths $2\,\mathrm m$ and $5\,\mathrm m$, warm by $30\,\mathrm K$. With $\alpha=1.2\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$, by how much do their expansions differ?

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta L_2-\Delta L_1&\simeq\alpha\Delta T(L_{0,2}-L_{0,1})\\[6pt] &=(1.2\cdot10^{-5})\cdot30\cdot(5-2)\,\mathrm m\\[6pt] &=1.08\,\mathrm{mm}. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rods have the same fractional change $\alpha\Delta T$ because they are made of the same material and undergo the same warming. The absolute change is proportional to the initial length. Factor out the common term and multiply it by the difference in lengths. The five-metre rod gains $1.08\,\mathrm{mm}$ more than the two-metre rod.

Check the result by finding both extensions separately. The first is $0.72\,\mathrm{mm}$ and the second is $1.80\,\mathrm{mm}$. Their ratio is $2.5$, the same as that of the initial lengths. Subtracting gives $1.08\,\mathrm{mm}$. This comparison assumes both rods are free and undergo the same uniform temperature change.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $2.5\,\mathrm m$ rod with $\alpha=1.6\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$ is fixed at one end and has a $1.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ gap at the other. At what temperature increase does the gap close?

$$
\begin{aligned} \Delta L&=1.4\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm m\\[6pt] \Delta T&\simeq\frac{\Delta L}{\alpha L_0}\\[6pt] &=\frac{1.4\cdot10^{-3}}{(1.6\cdot10^{-5})\cdot2.5}\,\mathrm K=35\,\mathrm K. \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Until contact the free end can move and the rod expands according to the model. The threshold occurs when the displacement reaches the width of the gap. Use metres both for the displacement and for the initial length in the equation. For increases greater than $35\,\mathrm K$, the wall prevents further free expansion.

The displacement has a slope of $0.040\,\mathrm{mm/K}$. At a rise of $25\,\mathrm K$ the end would advance $1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ and leave a gap of $0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$. The extra $10\,\mathrm K$ produces exactly the remaining displacement.

</div>
