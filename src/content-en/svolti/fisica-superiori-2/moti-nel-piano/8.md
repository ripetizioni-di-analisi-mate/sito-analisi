## Exercise 1

An object moves around a circle of radius $4\,\mathrm{m}$ at constant speed $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. What is the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration?

::grafico{curve="4.0*cos(t),4.0*sin(t),0,6.2831853" xmin=-5.0 xmax=6.0 ymin=-4.8 ymax=4.8 punti="0,0,pieno,O;4.0,0,pieno,P" vettori="4.0,0,4.0,2.6,v;4.0,0,1.2,0,a" uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="At P, v is tangent to the path and a points towards centre O."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_c&=\frac{v^2}{r}=\frac{6^2}{4}\,\mathrm{m/s^2} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At constant speed the velocity vector still changes direction. Acceleration points towards the centre and has magnitude equal to speed squared divided by radius. The squared speed from the data is $36\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Dividing by $4\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $9\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Check that a length factor cancels and leaves metres per second squared.

</div>

The squared speed from the data is $49\,\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$. Dividing by $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$ gives $10.889\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Check that a length factor cancels and leaves metres per second squared.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point rotates at distance $1.6\,\mathrm{m}$ from the centre at constant angular speed $3\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Find the centripetal acceleration.

::grafico{curve="1.6*cos(t),1.6*sin(t),0,6.2831853" xmin=-2.0 xmax=2.4000000000000004 ymin=-1.92 ymax=1.92 punti="0,0,pieno,O;1.6,0,pieno,P" vettori="1.6,0,1.6,1.04,v;1.6,0,0.48,0,a" uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="At P, v is tangent to the path and a points towards centre O."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_t&=\omega r=3\cdot1.6\,\mathrm{m/s}=4.8\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
a_c&=\omega^2r=3^2\cdot 1.6\,\mathrm{m/s^2}=14.4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Tangential velocity is the product of angular speed and radius, giving $4.8\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Radial acceleration is this speed squared divided by radius. Substituting, one radius factor cancels, leaving $r$ times $\omega^2$. The direction is always inward. At fixed angular speed, doubling the radius doubles this acceleration.

</div>
