## Exercise 1

A platform rotates at constant frequency $2\,\mathrm{Hz}$. How long does it take to complete one revolution?

$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac1{2}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 0.5\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta t &= 1\cdot \frac1{2}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 0.5\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point makes 2 revolutions per second. The time for one revolution is the reciprocal $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$. With constant frequency, each revolution takes the same time. Multiply the period by 1 for the requested number of revolutions. Duration times frequency recovers that count.

Two equal turns fit into one second, each lasting half a second. A quarter of a second is therefore half the period and takes the rim point to the opposite side of the disk. The starting position is reached after the other half is complete.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A platform rotates at constant frequency $2.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. How long does it take to complete 2 revolutions?

$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac1{2.5}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 0.4\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
\Delta t &= 2\cdot \frac1{2.5}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 0.8\,\mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point makes 2.5 revolutions per second. The time for one revolution is the reciprocal $0.4\,\mathrm{s}$. With constant frequency, each revolution takes the same time. Multiply the period by 2 for the requested number of revolutions. Duration times frequency recovers that count.

The first turn ends after $0.4\,\mathrm s$. The second takes the same time because the frequency is constant. Over the required $0.8\,\mathrm s$ the radius sweeps through $4\pi\,\mathrm{rad}$ in total. The point's distance from the axis would change the path length while the time for the two turns remains the same.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point on the rim of a wheel is at the right end of the horizontal diameter at $t=0$. The wheel completes $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a turn each second in uniform rotation. What are the first two times when the point is to the left of the centre on the horizontal diameter?

::grafico{f="cos(4*PI*x/3)" punti="0,1,pieno;0.75,-1,pieno;2.25,-1,pieno" segmenti="0.75,0,0.75,-1;2.25,0,2.25,-1" etichette="0.64,-0.2,0.75;2.13,-0.2,2.25" xmin=0 xmax=2.6 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.4 nomi="t (s),x/r" titolo="The minima at 0.75 s and 2.25 s represent the same position and are one period apart."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=\frac{1}{2/3}\,\mathrm s=\frac32\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
t_1&=\frac{T}{2}=0.75\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
t_2&=t_1+T=0.75\,\mathrm s+1.5\,\mathrm s=2.25\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Going from right to left along the diameter takes a half revolution in either direction. Since a full turn takes one and a half seconds, the first passage is at three quarters of a second. Returning to the leftmost point takes another complete revolution. Another half turn would put the point back on the right. On the projection graph, the required times are the first two minima. They are one full period apart, while the first minimum is half a period after the starting time.

</div>
