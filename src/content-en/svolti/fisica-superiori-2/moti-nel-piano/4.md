## Exercise 1

A ball is launched horizontally and is still in flight after $1\,\mathrm{s}$. Ignoring air resistance, what is the magnitude of the vertical component of its velocity then? Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=1.0 ymin=-11.0 ymax=1.0 f="-10*x" punti="0,0.000000,pieno,I;1.000000,-10.000000,pieno,F" nomi="t (s),vᵧ (m/s)" titolo="The slope is −g. The vertical component becomes more negative."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_y &= 0-(10)\cdot 1=-10\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
\lvert v_y\rvert &= 10\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Evaluate the components at the same time $1\,\mathrm{s}$. The vertical equation needs no horizontal component. With upward positive, velocity along $y$ starts at zero and decreases by $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ each second. The negative sign means the ball is falling. The question asks for magnitude, $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The total speed would also require the horizontal component.

</div>
