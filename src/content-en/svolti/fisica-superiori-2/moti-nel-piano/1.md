## Exercise 1

An object has perpendicular velocity components $v_x=3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_y=4\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find its speed.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,3,0;3,0,3,4;0,0,3,4" punti="0,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;3,4,pieno" etichette="1.01,0.37,3 m/s;3.28,1.55,4 m/s;1.5,2.45,v" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=5.2 uguale=1 nomi="vₓ (m/s),vᵧ (m/s)" titolo="The 3 and 4 m/s arrows build the resultant along the diagonal."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\vec v\rvert &= \sqrt{(3)^2+(4)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 5\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the diagram, draw the horizontal component from the origin and start the vertical component at its tip. The diagonal from the start to the endpoint represents the velocity vector. Since the two legs are perpendicular, Pythagoras gives a speed of $5\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The result is less than the sum $3+4$, which would give the speed if both contributions pointed the same way along one axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An object has perpendicular velocity components $v_x=-5\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $v_y=12\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find its speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\vec v\rvert &= \sqrt{(-5)^2+(12)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 13\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The negative horizontal component places the tip of the arrow to the left of the origin. The positive vertical component places it above the origin. Squaring the horizontal component gives $(-5)^2=25$, which is added to the square of the vertical component. The square root gives $13\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Reversing just the horizontal sign would leave the square unchanged and reflect the arrow across the vertical axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A trolley moves at constant velocity. At $t=3\,\mathrm s$ it is at $(4;5)\,\mathrm m$ and at $t=5\,\mathrm s$ it is at $(-12;-25)\,\mathrm m$. Find the components and magnitude of its velocity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\vec r&=(-12-4;-25-5)\,\mathrm m=(-16;-30)\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
\vec v&=(-8;-15)\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[4pt]
|\vec v|&=\sqrt{(-8)^2+(-15)^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=17\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the initial position from the final position and divide each difference by $5-3=2\,\mathrm s$. Since the velocity is constant, the calculated components apply throughout the interval. Both negative signs indicate decreasing coordinates. Use Pythagoras for the magnitude because the components lie along perpendicular axes. As a check, the displacement magnitude $\sqrt{16^2+30^2}\,\mathrm m=34\,\mathrm m$ divided by two seconds gives $17\,\mathrm{m/s}$ again. Using five seconds in the denominator would include time before the first reading.

</div>
