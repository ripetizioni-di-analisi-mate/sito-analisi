## Exercise 1

A person walks $6\,\mathrm{m}$ east and then $8\,\mathrm{m}$ north. What is the magnitude of the resultant displacement?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\Delta\vec r\rvert &= \sqrt{(6)^2+(8)^2}\,\mathrm{m} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 10\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With east and north positive, the two legs of the walk are the perpendicular sides of the triangle. The resultant displacement joins start and finish along the hypotenuse, which is $10\,\mathrm{m}$ long by Pythagoras. The distance travelled along the two legs is $14\,\mathrm{m}$. Swapping their order would take the walker along the other two sides of the same rectangle, with a different intermediate point and the same endpoint.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A person walks $8\,\mathrm m$ west, $15\,\mathrm m$ north and finally $5\,\mathrm m$ east. What is the displacement magnitude? How long is the route?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=(-8+5)\,\mathrm m=-3\,\mathrm m,\qquad \Delta y=15\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
|\Delta\vec r|&=\sqrt{(-3)^2+15^2}\,\mathrm m=3\sqrt{26}\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
d&=(8+15+5)\,\mathrm m=28\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With east and north positive, the first leg contributes minus eight metres to the horizontal coordinate. The last adds five metres, so those two legs partly cancel on the same axis. The vertical component is entirely northward. The segment joining the start and end therefore has legs of three and fifteen metres. Its length, about $15.30\,\mathrm m$, is smaller than the length of the three travelled legs. Applying Pythagoras with eight and fifteen metres would describe the person's position before the final leg. In the total distance, each leg contributes its positive length.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two trolleys move in a plane according to $A(t)=(3t;4t)$ and $B(t)=(12-t;6+2t)$, in SI units with $t\geq0$ on the same clock. Check whether they meet and find the magnitude of A's displacement from the start to the meeting.

::grafico{vettori="0,0,10.5,14;12,6,8,14" punti="0,0,pieno;12,6,pieno;9,12,pieno" etichette="0.59,2.08,A;12.07,6.44,B;9.13,11.1,I" xmin=-1 xmax=14 ymin=-1 ymax=15 uguale=1 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="At 3 s both trolleys are at I with coordinates (9; 12) m."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
3t&=12-t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=3\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
y_A(3)&=12\,\mathrm m=y_B(3),\\[4pt]
A(3)&=B(3)=(9;12)\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
|\Delta\vec r_A|&=\sqrt{9^2+12^2}\,\mathrm m=15\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First equate the horizontal coordinates to find a candidate time. Then check that the vertical coordinates agree at that same time. Both equal twelve metres, so the trolleys occupy the same position after three seconds. A started at the origin, so its displacement components here equal the coordinates of the meeting point. To check the result, use A's speed. Its equations give $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}\,\mathrm{m/s}=5\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so in three seconds it travels $15\,\mathrm m$ along its line. Checking both vertical coordinates prevents treating equal horizontal coordinates alone as a meeting.

</div>
