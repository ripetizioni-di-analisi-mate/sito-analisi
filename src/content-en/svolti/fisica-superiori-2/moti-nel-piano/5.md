## Exercise 1

A wheel makes 12 revolutions in $4\,\mathrm{s}$ at constant frequency. What is the frequency in hertz?

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{N}{\Delta t} = \frac{12}{4}\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 3\,\mathrm{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The duration used is $4\,\mathrm{s}$. Hertz counts revolutions in one second, so divide the number of revolutions by that duration. This gives $3\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Multiplying that figure by $4\,\mathrm{s}$ recovers the 12 observed revolutions. The period would instead be duration divided by revolutions.

The reciprocal of $3\,\mathrm{Hz}$ gives a period of $\dfrac13\,\mathrm s$. Twelve intervals of that duration take exactly the four observed seconds. Revolutions per second and the time for one revolution describe the same motion in two ways.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A wheel makes 75 revolutions in $1\,\mathrm{min}$ at constant frequency. What is the frequency in hertz?

::grafico{f="1.25*x" punti="0,0,pieno;60,75,pieno" segmenti="0,75,60,75;60,0,60,75" etichette="43,76.6,F (60 s, 75 turns)" xmin=0 xmax=64 ymin=0 ymax=85 nomi="t (s),N (turns)" titolo="Seventy-five turns in sixty seconds give 1.25 Hz."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{N}{\Delta t} = \frac{75}{60}\,\mathrm{Hz} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 1.25\,\mathrm{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the duration is given in minutes, first multiply it by 60. The duration used is $60\,\mathrm{s}$. Hertz counts revolutions in one second, so divide the number of revolutions by that duration. This gives $1.25\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Multiplying that figure by $60\,\mathrm{s}$ recovers the 75 observed revolutions. The period would instead be duration divided by revolutions.

The graph of the turn count passes through the point at $60\,\mathrm s$ and vertical value $75$. Its slope measures turns per second. Another sixty seconds would add another seventy-five turns. The frequency is the constant ratio, while the turn count grows with time.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A sensor emits a pulse as each of three equally spaced marks on a steadily rotating wheel passes. The time from the first pulse considered to the pulse after another $150$ pulse intervals is $40\,\mathrm s$. Find the revolution frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=\frac{150}{3}=50,\\[4pt]
f&=\frac{50}{40}\,\mathrm{Hz}=1.25\,\mathrm{Hz},\\[4pt]
T&=\frac{1}{1.25}\,\mathrm s=0.8\,\mathrm s.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Between consecutive pulses the wheel covers a third of its circumference. Three intervals therefore take the time of a full turn, and one hundred fifty intervals represent fifty turns. Divide the number of turns by the forty observed seconds. Dividing one hundred fifty directly by forty would give the number of pulse intervals per second, three times the required frequency. To check, one interval lasts $\dfrac{40}{150}\,\mathrm s$, so a turn takes three intervals or $0.8\,\mathrm s$. The reciprocal again gives $1.25\,\mathrm{Hz}$.

</div>
