## Exercise 1

A point moves in a circle of radius $0.25\,\mathrm{m}$ at frequency $2\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find its tangential speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= 2\pi r f=2\pi\cdot 0.25\cdot 2\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 3.1416\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance in one revolution is the circumference $2\pi r$. The point makes 2 revolutions each second, so multiply that length by the frequency. Instantaneous velocity points along the tangent and has magnitude $3.1416\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Another point at twice the radius on the same disk would make the same number of revolutions per second and travel twice the distance in each revolution.

The period is half a second and the circumference measures $0.5\pi\,\mathrm m$. Dividing the length of one turn by half a second gives $\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}$ again. This avoids confusing the frequency of two turns per second with distance travelled in one second.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point moves in a circle of radius $0.65\,\mathrm{m}$ at frequency $1.5\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Find its tangential speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= 2\pi r f=2\pi\cdot 0.65\cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m/s} \\[6pt]
&\simeq 6.1261\,\mathrm{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance in one revolution is the circumference $2\pi r$. The point makes 1.5 revolutions each second, so multiply that length by the frequency. Instantaneous velocity points along the tangent and has magnitude $6.1261\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Another point at twice the radius on the same disk would make the same number of revolutions per second and travel twice the distance in each revolution.

Check the speed over two seconds. In that time the point completes three turns, each of length $1.3\pi\,\mathrm m$. The total path is $3.9\pi\,\mathrm m$ and dividing by two seconds gives $1.95\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The result is rounded only at the end.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two separate wheels rotate uniformly. Point A is $0.40\,\mathrm m$ from its axis and completes $1.5$ turns per second. Point B lies $0.80\,\mathrm m$ from the other axis and completes $0.75$ turns per second. Compare their tangential and angular speeds.

::grafico{f="3*PI*x;1.5*PI*x" punti="0.4,3.769911,pieno;0.8,3.769911,pieno" segmenti="0,3.769911,0.8,3.769911;0.4,0,0.4,3.769911;0.8,0,0.8,3.769911" etichette="0.37,4.2,A;0.77,4.2,B;0.37,-0.65,0.4;0.77,-0.65,0.8" legenda="A;B" xmin=0 xmax=0.95 ymin=0 ymax=10 nomi="r (m),v (m/s)" titolo="The slopes differ. The speeds agree at the two stated radii."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_A&=2\pi\cdot1.5\,\mathrm{rad/s}=3\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s},\\[4pt]
\omega_B&=2\pi\cdot0.75\,\mathrm{rad/s}=1.5\pi\,\mathrm{rad/s},\\[4pt]
v_A&=(3\pi\cdot0.40)\,\mathrm{m/s}=1.2\pi\,\mathrm{m/s},\\[4pt]
v_B&=(1.5\pi\cdot0.80)\,\mathrm{m/s}=1.2\pi\,\mathrm{m/s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

B completes half as many turns as A in the same time, so its angular speed is half as large. Its radius is double, so each turn of B is twice as long. Angular speed multiplied by radius shows the two effects balance. In two seconds A completes three turns of length $0.8\pi\,\mathrm m$ each. B completes one and a half turns of length $1.6\pi\,\mathrm m$ each. Both cover $2.4\pi\,\mathrm m$ in the same time, confirming equal tangential speeds. Their angular speeds differ because the number of turns differs.

</div>
