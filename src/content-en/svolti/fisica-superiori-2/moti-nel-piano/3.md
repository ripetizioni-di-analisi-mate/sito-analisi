## Exercise 1

A ball is launched horizontally at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from $5\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. What is the horizontal range?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_f &= \sqrt{\frac{2\cdot 5}{10}}\,\mathrm{s}= 1\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
R &= 3\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot5}{10}}\,\mathrm{m}= 3\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose $y=0$ at ground level and measure time from launch. The initial vertical velocity is zero, so the height in metres is $y=5-5t^2$. The time of impact is the first zero with $t>0$. At the same instant, the horizontal position follows from the constant horizontal velocity, $x=3t$.

After half a second the ball is $1.5\,\mathrm m$ from the launch vertical. Its height is still $5-\dfrac12\cdot10\cdot(0.5)^2=3.75$, in metres. The second half of the flight covers the same horizontal distance and a greater drop because the vertical velocity component grows in magnitude.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A ball is launched horizontally at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from $10\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. What is the horizontal range?

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_f &= \sqrt{\frac{2\cdot 10}{10}}\,\mathrm{s}=\sqrt2\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 1.41\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
R &= 4\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot10}{10}}\,\mathrm{m}=4\sqrt2\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 5.66\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Set $10-5t^2=0$ to find when the height reaches the ground and take the positive solution. Over that same interval, motion along the other axis obeys $x=4t$ at constant speed. An alternative is to eliminate time and write the path as $y=10-\dfrac{5}{16}x^2$ in SI units. The positive solution for $y=0$ gives the range directly.

The exact time contains $\sqrt2$, which can be kept until the last step. The range is $4\sqrt2\,\mathrm m$. As a check, that horizontal coordinate gives a time of $\sqrt2\,\mathrm s$, and substituting it gives height $10-5\cdot2=0$. Rounding earlier would lose that exact equality.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two balls are launched horizontally to the right at the same instant along the same vertical line. A starts at $45\,\mathrm m$ at $4\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and B at $20\,\mathrm m$ at $6\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Both land on the same floor. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. Compare their ranges and impact times.

::grafico{curve="t,45-(5/16)*t^2,0,12;t,20-(5/36)*t^2,0,12" punti="0,45,pieno;0,20,pieno;12,0,pieno" etichette="0.44,46.2,A;0.43,21.52,B;12.17,3.1,I" legenda="A;B" xmin=0 xmax=13.4 ymin=0 ymax=50 nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="Both paths end at I, 12 m from the launch vertical. B arrives first."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_A&=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot45}{10}}\,\mathrm s=3\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
t_B&=\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot20}{10}}\,\mathrm s=2\,\mathrm s,\\[4pt]
R_A&=(4\cdot3)\,\mathrm m=12\,\mathrm m,\\[4pt]
R_B&=(6\cdot2)\,\mathrm m=12\,\mathrm m.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A starts higher, so it takes longer to reach the floor. B has a higher horizontal speed and covers the same horizontal distance in less time. Their ranges agree, but B lands one second earlier. To check the distinction, at $t=2\,\mathrm s$ A's horizontal coordinate is only $8\,\mathrm m$ and its height is $45-5\cdot2^2=25\,\mathrm m$. B is already on the floor at $12\,\mathrm m$. The paths share the landing point and reach it at two different times.

</div>
