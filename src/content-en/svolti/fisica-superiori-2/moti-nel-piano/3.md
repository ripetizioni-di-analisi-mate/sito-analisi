## Exercise 1

A ball is launched horizontally at $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ from $5\,\mathrm{m}$ above the ground. Use $g=10\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and neglect air resistance. What is the horizontal range?

::grafico{xmin=0 xmax=3.0 ymin=0 ymax=5.6000000000000005 f="5-(10)*x^2/(2*3^2)" punti="0,5,pieno,L;3.00000000,0,pieno,I" nomi="x (m),y (m)" titolo="Height reaches zero at I. Its horizontal coordinate is the range."}

$$
\begin{aligned}
t_f &= \sqrt{\frac{2\cdot 5}{10}}\,\mathrm{s}\simeq 1\,\mathrm{s} \\[6pt]
R &= 3\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2\cdot5}{10}}\,\mathrm{m}\simeq 3\,\mathrm{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With the vertical axis positive upward, initial vertical velocity is zero. Height drops from $5\,\mathrm{m}$ to zero, so the fall equation gives the positive time $1\,\mathrm{s}$. There is no horizontal acceleration. Velocity $3\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is unchanged throughout the already calculated duration, giving range $3\,\mathrm{m}$. The plot shows the trajectory from launch to ground contact in the same coordinates as the given data.

</div>
