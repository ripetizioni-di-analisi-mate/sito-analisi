## Exercise 1

Study the differentiability of $f(x) = x\,|x|$ on $\mathbb{R}$.

$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^2 & \text{if } x \geq 0 \\[4pt]
-x^2 & \text{if } x < 0
\end{cases}
$$

At $x = 0$, using the definition:
$$
\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(h) - f(0)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{h\,|h|}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} |h| = 0
$$

$$
f'(x) =
\begin{cases}
2x & \text{if } x > 0 \\[4pt]
0 & \text{if } x = 0 \\[4pt]
-2x & \text{if } x < 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \text{that is, } f'(x) = 2\,|x|
$$

$f$ is **differentiable** on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value is removed by distinguishing the sign of $x$: for $x \geq 0$ the function equals $x^2$, for $x < 0$ it equals $-x^2$. At the points $x \neq 0$ the function coincides with a polynomial in a neighborhood of the point, therefore it is **differentiable** with derivative $2x$ or $-2x$. At the **junction point** $x = 0$ one uses the definition: the difference quotient equals $\dfrac{h\,|h|}{h} = |h|$ and tends to $0$, hence $f'(0) = 0$. The three expressions are summarized by the formula $f'(x) = 2\,|x|$. The absolute value makes it necessary to study the junction point, and in this case the check gives a function that is differentiable at every point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the points of non-differentiability of $f(x) = |x^2 - 1|$ and classify them.

$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^2 - 1 & \text{if } x \leq -1 \ \text{or } x \geq 1 \\[4pt]
1 - x^2 & \text{if } -1 < x < 1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
f'(x) =
\begin{cases}
2x & \text{if } |x| > 1 \\[4pt]
-2x & \text{if } |x| < 1
\end{cases}
$$

One-sided limits of the derivative at the junctions:
$$
\lim_{x \to 1^{-}} (-2x) = -2 , \qquad \lim_{x \to 1^{+}} 2x = 2
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -1^{-}} 2x = -2 , \qquad \lim_{x \to -1^{+}} (-2x) = 2
$$

$f$ **is not differentiable** at $x = -1$ and at $x = 1$: both are **corner points**.

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-1)" xmin=-2.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-1.5 ymax=4 punti="-1,0,pieno;1,0,pieno" segmenti="0.4,1.2,1.6,-1.2;0.4,-1.2,1.6,1.2" titolo="$y = |x^2 - 1|$: at $x = 1$ the two one-sided tangent lines have slopes $-2$ and $2$, therefore the point is a corner point; the same happens at $x = -1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value is opened by studying the sign of $x^2 - 1$, positive for $|x| \geq 1$ and negative for $|x| < 1$. The function is **continuous** on $\mathbb{R}$, since it is the composition of the polynomial $x^2 - 1$ with the absolute value, both continuous; continuity at the junctions is the **preliminary condition** for speaking of finite one-sided derivatives. Within each region the function coincides with a polynomial, therefore it is differentiable with the derivatives indicated.

At the points $x = \pm 1$ the function is continuous and the derivative has finite one-sided limits, hence by the **limit-of-the-derivative criterion** these limits are the one-sided derivatives. At $x = 1$ they equal $-2$ and $2$; since they are finite and different, the point is a **corner point**. At $x = -1$ they equal $-2$ and $2$, therefore this point is also a corner point. At all other points $f$ is differentiable.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ so that the function
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^2 & \text{if } x \leq 1 \\[4pt]
a x + b & \text{if } x > 1
\end{cases}
$$
is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$.

Continuity at $x = 1$:
$$
\lim_{x \to 1^{-}} x^2 = 1 , \qquad \lim_{x \to 1^{+}} (a x + b) = a + b
$$

One-sided derivatives at $x = 1$:
$$
\lim_{x \to 1^{-}} 2x = 2 , \qquad \lim_{x \to 1^{+}} a = a
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
a + b = 1 \\[4pt]
a = 2
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\begin{cases}
a = 2 \\[4pt]
b = -1
\end{cases}
$$

The function is **differentiable** on $\mathbb{R}$ for $a = 2$ and $b = -1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Away from the junction the function coincides with a polynomial, therefore it is differentiable for every choice of the parameters; the problem concerns *only* the point $x = 1$. Since a function differentiable at a point is continuous at that point, the first condition is **continuity**: the value $f(1) = 1$ must coincide with the right-hand limit $a + b$. The second condition is the **equality of the one-sided derivatives**: on the left the derivative $2x$ tends to $2$, on the right the derivative is constantly equal to $a$, and by the limit-of-the-derivative criterion these limits are the one-sided derivatives at the junction, since continuity has already been imposed. The system of the two conditions has the unique solution $a = 2$, $b = -1$: the line $y = 2x - 1$ is the **tangent** to the parabola $y = x^2$ at the point $(1, 1)$, therefore the differentiable junction extends the parabola with its tangent.

</div>
