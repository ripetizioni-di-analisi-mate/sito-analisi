## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the tangent line to the graph of $f(x) = x^3 - 2x$ at the point with abscissa $x_0 = 1$.

$$
f(1) = 1 - 2 = -1
$$

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2 , \qquad f'(1) = 3 - 2 = 1
$$

$$
y = f(1) + f'(1)\,(x - 1) = -1 + (x - 1) = x - 2
$$

The tangent line is $y = x - 2$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The tangent line passes through the point $(x_0, f(x_0))$ and has **slope** $f'(x_0)$, therefore the two values $f(1)$ and $f'(1)$ are needed. The value of the function is $f(1) = -1$. The derivative is computed with the **sum and power rules** and equals $3x^2 - 2$, hence the slope is $f'(1) = 1$. Substituting into the **tangent line formula** one obtains $y = -1 + 1 \cdot (x - 1)$, that is, the line $y = x - 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the points of the graph of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ at which the tangent line is horizontal and write the corresponding tangents.

$$
f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3\,(x^2 - 1) = 0 \iff x = -1 \ \text{or} \ x = 1
$$

$$
f(-1) = -1 + 3 = 2 , \qquad f(1) = 1 - 3 = -2
$$

The points are $(-1, 2)$ and $(1, -2)$; the tangents are the lines $y = 2$ and $y = -2$.

::grafico{f="x^3-3*x" xmin=-2.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 punti="-1,2,pieno;1,-2,pieno" segmenti="-2.2,2,0.2,2;-0.2,-2,2.2,-2" titolo="$y = x^3 - 3x$: at the points $(-1, 2)$ and $(1, -2)$ the tangents $y = 2$ and $y = -2$ are horizontal"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A line is **horizontal** when its slope is **zero**, therefore one looks for the points where $f'(x) = 0$. The derivative equals $3x^2 - 3$ and vanishes for $x^2 = 1$, that is, at the two points $x = -1$ and $x = 1$. The corresponding ordinates are $f(-1) = 2$ and $f(1) = -2$. At a point with a horizontal tangent the tangent is the **constant line** through the point, hence the two tangents are $y = 2$ and $y = -2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the point of the graph of $f(x) = \ln x$ at which the tangent line is parallel to the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ and write the tangent.

$$
f'(x) = \frac{1}{x} = \frac{1}{2} \iff x = 2
$$

$$
f(2) = \ln 2
$$

$$
y = \ln 2 + \frac{1}{2}\,(x - 2) = \frac{x}{2} + \ln 2 - 1
$$

The point is $(2, \ln 2)$; the tangent is the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2} + \ln 2 - 1$.

::grafico{f="log(x);x/2;x/2+log(2)-1" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=3.5 uguale=1 punti="2,0.6931,pieno" titolo="The tangent to $y = \ln x$ at the point $(2, \ln 2)$ (red) is parallel to the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ (dashed)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Two lines are **parallel** when they have the *same* slope, therefore one looks for the point where the derivative equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, the slope of the given line. The derivative of the logarithm is $\dfrac{1}{x}$ and the equation $\dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ has the **unique** solution $x = 2$, which belongs to the domain $(0, +\infty)$. The ordinate of the point of tangency is $f(2) = \ln 2$. The tangent passes through $(2, \ln 2)$ with slope $\dfrac{1}{2}$, hence its equation is $y = \dfrac{x}{2} + \ln 2 - 1$, parallel to the given line and *distinct* from it.

</div>
