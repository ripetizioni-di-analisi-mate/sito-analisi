## Exercise 1

Compute the derivative of $f(x) = \sin(3x^2 + 1)$.

$$
f'(x) = \cos(3x^2 + 1) \cdot \bigl( 3x^2 + 1 \bigr)' = 6x \cos(3x^2 + 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is **composite**: the **outer function** is the sine, the argument is $3x^2 + 1$. Since the derivative of the sine is the cosine, one writes $\cos(3x^2 + 1)$ leaving the argument unchanged; the **chain rule** then requires multiplying by the derivative of the argument, which equals $6x$. The derivative is therefore $6x \cos(3x^2 + 1)$, defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the derivative of $f(x) = \ln(x^2 + 4)$.

$$
f'(x) = \frac{1}{x^2 + 4} \cdot \bigl( x^2 + 4 \bigr)' = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polynomial $x^2 + 4$ is positive for every $x$, therefore the function is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$. The **outer function** is the logarithm, whose derivative is the **reciprocal of the argument**, hence the first factor is $\dfrac{1}{x^2 + 4}$; the derivative of the argument equals $2x$. The product of the two factors gives $f'(x) = \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + 4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the derivative of $f(x) = \sqrt{1 + e^{2x}}$.

$$
f'(x) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1 + e^{2x}}} \cdot \bigl( 1 + e^{2x} \bigr)'
= \frac{2\,e^{2x}}{2\sqrt{1 + e^{2x}}}
= \frac{e^{2x}}{\sqrt{1 + e^{2x}}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The composition has **three levels**: the square root, the function $1 + e^{2x}$ and the exponent $2x$. The derivative of the root is $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{\,\cdot\,}}$ with the argument unchanged, therefore the first factor is $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{1 + e^{2x}}}$. To differentiate $1 + e^{2x}$ one applies the **chain rule** again: the derivative of $e^{2x}$ is $e^{2x}$ multiplied by the derivative of the exponent, that is, $2e^{2x}$. The factor $2$ cancels with the $2$ of the root, hence $f'(x) = \dfrac{e^{2x}}{\sqrt{1 + e^{2x}}}$. Since $1 + e^{2x} > 0$ for every $x$, the derivative is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$.

</div>
