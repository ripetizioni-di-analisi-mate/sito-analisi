## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{1 + x} - 1}{x} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{1 + x} - 1}{x}
&= \frac{\left(\sqrt{1 + x} - 1\right)\left(\sqrt{1 + x} + 1\right)}{x\left(\sqrt{1 + x} + 1\right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(1 + x) - 1}{x\left(\sqrt{1 + x} + 1\right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x} + 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{1 + x} - 1}{x}
= \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x} + 1}
= \frac{1}{2}
$$

::grafico{f="(sqrt(1+x)-1)/x" xmin=-1.5 xmax=4 ymin=-0.5 ymax=1.5 punti="0,0.5,vuoto;-1,1,pieno" titolo="$y = \dfrac{\sqrt{1 + x} - 1}{x}$ is not defined at $x = 0$; near $0$ the values approach $\dfrac{1}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ the numerator tends to $\sqrt{1} - 1 = 0$ and the denominator tends to $0$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{1 + x} + 1$, that is, **rationalize**: in the numerator the product $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$ appears with $a = \sqrt{1 + x}$ and $b = 1$, which gives $x$. The factor $x$ cancels since in the limit $x$ is *different* from $0$. The remaining function is **continuous** at $0$, hence the limit is its value at $0$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4}
&= \frac{\left( \sqrt{x} - 2 \right)\left( \sqrt{x} + 2 \right)}{(x - 4)\left( \sqrt{x} + 2 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{x - 4}{(x - 4)\left( \sqrt{x} + 2 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4}
= \lim_{x \to 4} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2}
= \frac{1}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 4$ the numerator tends to $\sqrt{4} - 2 = 0$ and the denominator tends to $0$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{x} + 2$: in the numerator the product $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$ appears with $a = \sqrt{x}$ and $b = 2$, which gives $x - 4$. The factor $x - 4$ cancels since in the limit $x \neq 4$. The function $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2}$ is **continuous** at $4$, hence the limit is its value at $4$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4} + 2} = \dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\sqrt{x + 3} - 2}{\sqrt{x} - 1} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{x + 3} - 2}{\sqrt{x} - 1}
&= \frac{\left( \sqrt{x + 3} - 2 \right)\left( \sqrt{x + 3} + 2 \right)\left( \sqrt{x} + 1 \right)}{\left( \sqrt{x} - 1 \right)\left( \sqrt{x} + 1 \right)\left( \sqrt{x + 3} + 2 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(x - 1)\left( \sqrt{x} + 1 \right)}{(x - 1)\left( \sqrt{x + 3} + 2 \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sqrt{x} + 1}{\sqrt{x + 3} + 2}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\sqrt{x + 3} - 2}{\sqrt{x} - 1}
= \frac{\sqrt{1} + 1}{\sqrt{4} + 2}
= \frac{2}{4}
= \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 1$ the numerator tends to $\sqrt{4} - 2 = 0$ and the denominator tends to $\sqrt{1} - 1 = 0$, therefore we have the indeterminate form $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Roots appear both in the numerator and in the denominator, hence one **rationalizes** *twice*: multiply by $\sqrt{x + 3} + 2$, which turns the numerator into $(x + 3) - 4 = x - 1$, and by $\sqrt{x} + 1$, which turns the denominator into $x - 1$. In both cases the product $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$ is used. The common factor $x - 1$ cancels since in the limit $x \neq 1$. The remaining function is **continuous** at $1$, hence the limit is its value at $1$, that is, $\dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>
