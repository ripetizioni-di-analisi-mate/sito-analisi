## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 5x}{\tan 2x} .
$$

$$
\frac{\sin 5x}{\tan 2x}
= \frac{\sin 5x}{5x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\tan 2x} \cdot \frac{5x}{2x}
= \frac{\sin 5x}{5x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\tan 2x} \cdot \frac{5}{2}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 5x}{5x} = 1, \qquad
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2x}{\tan 2x} = 1
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 5x}{\tan 2x} = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot \frac{5}{2} = \frac{5}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ both $\sin 5x$ and $\tan 2x$ tend to $0$, therefore the ratio is in the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. One multiplies and divides so as to make the ratios $\dfrac{\sin 5x}{5x}$ and $\dfrac{\tan 2x}{2x}$ appear, which are **fundamental limits** after the **change of variable**: setting $y = 5x$, we have $y \to 0$ and $\dfrac{\sin y}{y} \to 1$; setting $z = 2x$, we have $z \to 0$ and $\dfrac{\tan z}{z} \to 1$, therefore the reciprocal $\dfrac{z}{\tan z}$ also tends to $1$. The remaining factor, $\dfrac{5x}{2x}$, is the constant $\dfrac{5}{2}$ for $x \neq 0$. Since the three factors have a *finite* limit, the limit of the product is the product of the limits.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to \pi} \frac{\sin x}{x - \pi} .
$$

$$
\frac{\sin x}{x - \pi}
= \frac{\sin(\pi + y)}{y}
= \frac{-\sin y}{y}
\qquad (y = x - \pi)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to \pi} \frac{\sin x}{x - \pi}
= \lim_{y \to 0} \left( -\frac{\sin y}{y} \right)
= -1
$$

::grafico{f="sin(x)/(x-pi)" xmin=-2 xmax=9 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1 punti="3.1416,-1,vuoto" titolo="$y = \dfrac{\sin x}{x - \pi}$ is not defined at $x = \pi$; near $\pi$ the values approach $-1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to \pi$ the numerator tends to $\sin \pi = 0$ and the denominator tends to $0$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The fundamental sine limit holds for an argument that tends to $0$, whereas here $x$ tends to $\pi$, hence one performs the **change of variable** $y = x - \pi$: when $x \to \pi$ we have $y \to 0$, and $x = \pi + y$. By the **addition formula** $\sin(\pi + y) = \sin \pi \cos y + \cos \pi \sin y = -\sin y$. The ratio becomes $-\dfrac{\sin y}{y}$, and since $\dfrac{\sin y}{y} \to 1$ as $y \to 0$, the limit equals $-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x - \sin x}{x^3} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\tan x - \sin x}{x^3}
&= \frac{1}{x^3} \cdot \sin x \left( \frac{1}{\cos x} - 1 \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{x^3} \cdot \sin x \cdot \frac{1 - \cos x}{\cos x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\sin x}{x} \cdot \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \cdot \frac{1}{\cos x}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1, \qquad
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\cos x} = 1
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x - \sin x}{x^3} = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ both $\tan x - \sin x$ and $x^3$ tend to $0$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}$, the difference in the numerator can be factored as $\sin x \left( \dfrac{1}{\cos x} - 1 \right) = \sin x \cdot \dfrac{1 - \cos x}{\cos x}$. The product is rearranged into three factors, each with a finite limit: $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ tends to $1$ by the **fundamental sine limit**, $\dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2}$ tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ by the **fundamental cosine limit**, and $\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$ tends to $1$ since the cosine is continuous and $\cos 0 = 1$. The limit of the product is the product of the limits, that is, $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>
