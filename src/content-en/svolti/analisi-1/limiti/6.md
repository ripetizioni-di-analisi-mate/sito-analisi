## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x + 1}{x - 1} \right)^{x} .
$$

$$
\frac{x + 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(x - 1) + 2}{x - 1} = 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1}
$$

$$
\left( 1 + \frac{2}{x - 1} \right)^{x}
= \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\dfrac{x - 1}{2}} \right)^{\frac{x - 1}{2}} \right]^{\frac{2x}{x - 1}}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\dfrac{x - 1}{2}} \right)^{\frac{x - 1}{2}} = e, \qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{2x}{x - 1} = 2
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x + 1}{x - 1} \right)^{x} = e^{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to +\infty$ the base tends to $1$ and the exponent tends to $+\infty$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $1^{\infty}$. The base is rewritten as $1$ plus an *infinitesimal* quantity, using the division $x + 1 = (x - 1) + 2$. Set $t = \dfrac{x - 1}{2}$, which tends to $+\infty$, so that the base becomes $1 + \dfrac{1}{t}$. The exponent $x$ is written as $t \cdot \dfrac{x}{t} = t \cdot \dfrac{2x}{x - 1}$, so that the power $\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{t}\right)^{t}$, which tends to $e$, appears raised to $\dfrac{2x}{x - 1}$, which tends to $2$. Since the function $u^{v}$ is **continuous** for $u > 0$, the limit is $e^{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x + 2}{x - 3} \right)^{\frac{x}{2}} .
$$

$$
\frac{x + 2}{x - 3} = \frac{(x - 3) + 5}{x - 3} = 1 + \frac{5}{x - 3}
$$

$$
\left( 1 + \frac{5}{x - 3} \right)^{\frac{x}{2}}
= \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\dfrac{x - 3}{5}} \right)^{\frac{x - 3}{5}} \right]^{\frac{5x}{2(x - 3)}}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{\dfrac{x - 3}{5}} \right)^{\frac{x - 3}{5}} = e, \qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{5x}{2(x - 3)} = \frac{5}{2}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{x + 2}{x - 3} \right)^{\frac{x}{2}} = e^{\frac{5}{2}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to +\infty$ the base tends to $1$ and the exponent tends to $+\infty$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $1^{\infty}$. The base is rewritten as $1$ plus an *infinitesimal* quantity, using the division $x + 2 = (x - 3) + 5$. Set $t = \dfrac{x - 3}{5}$, which tends to $+\infty$, so that the base becomes $1 + \dfrac{1}{t}$. The exponent $\dfrac{x}{2}$ is written as $t \cdot \dfrac{5x}{2(x - 3)}$, since the product $\dfrac{x - 3}{5} \cdot \dfrac{5x}{2(x - 3)}$ equals $\dfrac{x}{2}$. The power $\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{t}\right)^{t}$ tends to $e$, whereas the outer exponent $\dfrac{5x}{2(x - 3)}$ is a ratio of first-degree polynomials and tends to $\dfrac{5}{2}$. Since the function $u^{v}$ is **continuous** for $u > 0$, the limit is $e^{\frac{5}{2}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \cos x \right)^{\frac{1}{x^2}} .
$$

$$
\left( \cos x \right)^{\frac{1}{x^2}}
= \bigl( 1 + (\cos x - 1) \bigr)^{\frac{1}{x^2}}
= \left[ \bigl( 1 + (\cos x - 1) \bigr)^{\frac{1}{\cos x - 1}} \right]^{\frac{\cos x - 1}{x^2}}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \bigl( 1 + (\cos x - 1) \bigr)^{\frac{1}{\cos x - 1}} = e, \qquad
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x - 1}{x^2} = -\frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \cos x \right)^{\frac{1}{x^2}} = e^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}
$$

::grafico{f="cos(x)^(1/x^2)" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-0.2 ymax=1.2 punti="0,0.6065,vuoto" titolo="$y = (\cos x)^{\frac{1}{x^2}}$ as $x \to 0$ approaches $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$, about $0{,}61$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ the base tends to $\cos 0 = 1$ and the exponent $\dfrac{1}{x^2}$ tends to $+\infty$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $1^{\infty}$. The base is rewritten as $1$ plus the quantity $u = \cos x - 1$, which for $0 < |x| < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is negative and *nonzero*, and tends to $0$. Since $u \to 0$, the power $(1 + u)^{\frac{1}{u}}$ tends to $e$ by the **fundamental exponential limit**. The outer exponent equals $\dfrac{\cos x - 1}{x^2} = -\dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2}$ and tends to $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ by the **fundamental cosine limit**. Since the function $u^{v}$ is continuous for $u > 0$, the limit is $e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$.

</div>
