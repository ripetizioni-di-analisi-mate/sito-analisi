## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + x} - x \right) .
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^2 + x} - x
= \frac{\left( \sqrt{x^2 + x} - x \right)\left( \sqrt{x^2 + x} + x \right)}{\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x}
= \frac{x^2 + x - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x}
= \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x}
$$

$$
\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x}
= \frac{x}{x \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} + x}
= \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} + 1}
\qquad (x > 0)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + x} - x \right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 0} + 1} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(x^2+x)-x" xmin=0 xmax=20 ymin=-0.2 ymax=1 asintoti="y=0.5" titolo="$y = \sqrt{x^2 + x} - x$ approaches the line $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to +\infty$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to +\infty$ both terms tend to $+\infty$, therefore the difference is in the **indeterminate form** $+\infty - \infty$. Multiply and divide by the sum $\sqrt{x^2 + x} + x$: in the numerator the product $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$ appears, which **eliminates the root** and leaves $x$. In the denominator one factors $x$ out of the root: $\sqrt{x^2 + x} = \sqrt{x^2}\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} = x\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}}$, where $\sqrt{x^2} = x$ since $x > 0$. After cancelling $x$, the term $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $0$ and the function tends to $\dfrac{1}{1 + 1}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - \sqrt{x^2 + x} \right) .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - \sqrt{x^2 + x}
&= \frac{\left( \sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - \sqrt{x^2 + x} \right)\left( \sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 + x} \right)}{\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 + x}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left( x^2 + 3x \right) - \left( x^2 + x \right)}{\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 + x}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 + x}}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 3x} + \sqrt{x^2 + x}}
= \frac{2x}{x \left( \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{3}{x}} + \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}} \right)}
= \frac{2}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{3}{x}} + \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x}}}
\qquad (x > 0)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 3x} - \sqrt{x^2 + x} \right) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{1} + \sqrt{1}} = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to +\infty$ both roots tend to $+\infty$, therefore the difference is in the **indeterminate form** $+\infty - \infty$. Multiply and divide by the sum of the two roots: in the numerator the product $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$ appears, which **eliminates** *both* roots and leaves $2x$. In each root of the denominator one factors out $x^2$: since $x > 0$, we have $\sqrt{x^2} = x$, and the factored-out $x$ cancels with the numerator. The terms $\dfrac{3}{x}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tend to $0$, hence the denominator tends to $1 + 1 = 2$ and the limit equals $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 2x} + x \right) .
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} + x
= \frac{\left( \sqrt{x^2 + 2x} + x \right)\left( \sqrt{x^2 + 2x} - x \right)}{\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} - x}
= \frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} - x}
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} = |x| \sqrt{1 + \frac{2}{x}} = -x \sqrt{1 + \frac{2}{x}}
\qquad (x < 0)
$$

$$
\frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} - x}
= \frac{2x}{-x \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}} - x}
= \frac{2x}{-x \left( \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}} + 1 \right)}
= -\frac{2}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}} + 1}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^2 + 2x} + x \right) = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{1 + 0} + 1} = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to -\infty$ the root tends to $+\infty$ while $x$ tends to $-\infty$, therefore the sum is in the **indeterminate form** $+\infty - \infty$. Multiply and divide by $\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} - x$: in the numerator the product $(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$ appears, which eliminates the root and leaves $x^2 + 2x - x^2 = 2x$. When factoring under the root we have $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$, and since $x < 0$ we have $|x| = -x$, therefore $\sqrt{x^2 + 2x} = -x\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{2}{x}}$. In the denominator one factors out $-x$ and the factor cancels with the numerator, leaving the **minus sign**. The term $\dfrac{2}{x}$ tends to $0$, hence the function tends to $-\dfrac{2}{1 + 1} = -1$.

</div>
