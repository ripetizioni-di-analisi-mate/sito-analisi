## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{e^{x} + x^{10}}{3^{x} + x^{2}} .
$$

$$
\frac{e^{x} + x^{10}}{3^{x} + x^{2}}
= \frac{e^{x} \left( 1 + \dfrac{x^{10}}{e^{x}} \right)}{3^{x} \left( 1 + \dfrac{x^{2}}{3^{x}} \right)}
= \left( \frac{e}{3} \right)^{x} \cdot \frac{1 + \dfrac{x^{10}}{e^{x}}}{1 + \dfrac{x^{2}}{3^{x}}}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{10}}{e^{x}} = 0, \qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{2}}{3^{x}} = 0, \qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \left( \frac{e}{3} \right)^{x} = 0 \quad \text{since } 0 < \frac{e}{3} < 1
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{e^{x} + x^{10}}{3^{x} + x^{2}} = 0 \cdot \frac{1 + 0}{1 + 0} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator and the denominator both tend to $+\infty$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. In each of them one factors out the term that grows fastest: by the **hierarchy of infinities**, an exponential with base greater than $1$ grows faster than *every* power, therefore $e^{x}$ is factored out in the numerator and $3^{x}$ in the denominator. The ratios $\dfrac{x^{10}}{e^{x}}$ and $\dfrac{x^{2}}{3^{x}}$ tend to $0$ by the hierarchy of infinities, hence the second fraction tends to $1$. The factor $\dfrac{e^{x}}{3^{x}} = \left(\dfrac{e}{3}\right)^{x}$ is an exponential with base between $0$ and $1$, since $e < 3$, and tends to $0$. The limit of the product is $0 \cdot 1 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{2} + \ln^{5} x}{x \ln x} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^{2} + \ln^{5} x}{x \ln x}
= \frac{x^{2} \left( 1 + \dfrac{\ln^{5} x}{x^{2}} \right)}{x \ln x}
= \frac{x}{\ln x} \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{\ln^{5} x}{x^{2}} \right)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\ln^{5} x}{x^{2}} = 0, \qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x}{\ln x} = +\infty
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{2} + \ln^{5} x}{x \ln x} = +\infty
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator and the denominator both tend to $+\infty$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. In the numerator one factors out $x^{2}$, which by the **hierarchy of infinities** dominates every power of the logarithm, and indeed the ratio $\dfrac{\ln^{5} x}{x^{2}}$ tends to $0$. After simplifying with the denominator, what remains is the product of $\dfrac{x}{\ln x}$, which tends to $+\infty$ since every power of $x$ grows faster than every power of the logarithm, and the factor $1 + \dfrac{\ln^{5} x}{x^{2}}$, which tends to $1$. Since the product of a function that tends to $+\infty$ and a function with limit $1$ tends to $+\infty$, the limit is $+\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{e^{x} - x^{5} \, 3^{\frac{x}{2}}}{e^{x} + x^{2}} .
$$

$$
3^{\frac{x}{2}} = \left( \sqrt{3} \right)^{x}, \qquad
\frac{x^{5} \, 3^{\frac{x}{2}}}{e^{x}} = x^{5} \left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{e} \right)^{x} = \frac{x^{5}}{\left( \dfrac{e}{\sqrt{3}} \right)^{x}}
$$

$$
\frac{e^{x} - x^{5} \, 3^{\frac{x}{2}}}{e^{x} + x^{2}}
= \frac{e^{x} \left( 1 - \dfrac{x^{5}}{\left( \dfrac{e}{\sqrt{3}} \right)^{x}} \right)}{e^{x} \left( 1 + \dfrac{x^{2}}{e^{x}} \right)}
= \frac{1 - \dfrac{x^{5}}{\left( \dfrac{e}{\sqrt{3}} \right)^{x}}}{1 + \dfrac{x^{2}}{e^{x}}}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{5}}{\left( \dfrac{e}{\sqrt{3}} \right)^{x}} = 0, \qquad
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{2}}{e^{x}} = 0
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{e^{x} - x^{5} \, 3^{\frac{x}{2}}}{e^{x} + x^{2}} = \frac{1 - 0}{1 + 0} = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator tends to $+\infty$, whereas the numerator is a difference of two terms that both tend to $+\infty$, therefore even before the quotient one must resolve the **indeterminate form** $+\infty - \infty$. The exponential $3^{\frac{x}{2}}$ is rewritten as $\left( \sqrt{3} \right)^{x}$, an exponential with base $\sqrt{3}$. Since $\sqrt{3} < e$, the base of the ratio $\dfrac{e}{\sqrt{3}}$ is greater than $1$, therefore $\left( \dfrac{e}{\sqrt{3}} \right)^{x}$ tends to $+\infty$ and, by the **hierarchy of infinities**, dominates the power $x^{5}$: the ratio $\dfrac{x^{5}}{\left( \frac{e}{\sqrt{3}} \right)^{x}}$ tends to $0$. For this reason the **dominant term** of the numerator is $e^{x}$, and it is factored out; in the denominator $e^{x}$ is again factored out, since $\dfrac{x^{2}}{e^{x}}$ tends to $0$ by the hierarchy of infinities. After cancelling $e^{x}$, two fractions remain that tend to $1$ and to $1$ respectively, hence the limit equals $1$.

</div>
