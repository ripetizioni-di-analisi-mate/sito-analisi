## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x - 1} = x + 1 \qquad (x \neq 1)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = \lim_{x \to 1} (x + 1) = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 1$ the numerator and the denominator both tend to $0$, therefore the ratio is in the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The numerator is a **difference of squares** and factors as $(x - 1)(x + 1)$. Since the limit concerns *only* the points with $x \neq 1$, the factor $x - 1$ cancels and the function coincides, at all these points, with $x + 1$. The function $x + 1$ is **continuous**, hence its limit as $x \to 1$ is the value $1 + 1 = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 - 5x + 6}{x^2 - 4} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^2 - 5x + 6}{x^2 - 4} = \frac{(x - 2)(x - 3)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} = \frac{x - 3}{x + 2} \qquad (x \neq 2)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 - 5x + 6}{x^2 - 4} = \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x - 3}{x + 2} = \frac{2 - 3}{2 + 2} = -\frac{1}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 2$ the numerator tends to $4 - 10 + 6 = 0$ and the denominator tends to $4 - 4 = 0$, therefore the ratio is in the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Both polynomials vanish at $x = 2$, hence *both* contain the factor $x - 2$. The numerator has roots $2$ and $3$ and factors as $(x - 2)(x - 3)$; the denominator is a difference of squares and factors as $(x - 2)(x + 2)$. Since the limit concerns only the points with $x \neq 2$, the factor $x - 2$ cancels. The function $\dfrac{x - 3}{x + 2}$ is **continuous** at $2$, therefore the limit is its value at $2$, that is, $-\dfrac{1}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^3 - 1}{x^2 - 3x + 2} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^3 - 1}{x^2 - 3x + 2} = \frac{(x - 1)\left( x^2 + x + 1 \right)}{(x - 1)(x - 2)} = \frac{x^2 + x + 1}{x - 2} \qquad (x \neq 1)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^3 - 1}{x^2 - 3x + 2} = \frac{1 + 1 + 1}{1 - 2} = -3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 1$ the numerator tends to $1 - 1 = 0$ and the denominator tends to $1 - 3 + 2 = 0$, therefore the ratio is in the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The numerator is a **difference of cubes** and factors as $x^3 - 1 = (x - 1)\left( x^2 + x + 1 \right)$. The denominator has roots $1$ and $2$, hence it factors as $(x - 2)(x - 1)$. Since the limit concerns only the points with $x \neq 1$, the common factor $x - 1$ cancels. The function $\dfrac{x^2 + x + 1}{x - 2}$ is **continuous** at $1$, therefore the limit is its value at $1$: the numerator equals $3$, the denominator equals $-1$, and the limit is $-3$.

</div>
