## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{3x^2 - x + 5}{2x^2 + 7} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3x^2 - x + 5}{2x^2 + 7}
&= \frac{x^2\left(3 - \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{5}{x^2}\right)}{x^2\left(2 + \dfrac{7}{x^2}\right)} \\[8pt]
&= \frac{3 - \dfrac{1}{x} + \dfrac{5}{x^2}}{2 + \dfrac{7}{x^2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{3x^2 - x + 5}{2x^2 + 7} = \frac{3 - 0 + 0}{2 + 0} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

::grafico{f="(3*x^2-x+5)/(2*x^2+7)" xmin=-10 xmax=10 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.5 asintoti="y=1.5" titolo="$y = \dfrac{3x^2 - x + 5}{2x^2 + 7}$ approaches the line $y = \dfrac{3}{2}$ as $x \to +\infty$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to +\infty$ the numerator and the denominator both tend to $+\infty$, therefore the ratio is in the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. In each of them one factors out the **highest-degree power**, $x^2$, which then cancels since $x \neq 0$. The terms $\dfrac{1}{x}$, $\dfrac{5}{x^2}$ and $\dfrac{7}{x^2}$ tend to $0$, hence the ratio tends to $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{3x^2 + 5}{x^3 - 2x} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3x^2 + 5}{x^3 - 2x}
&= \frac{x^2\left(3 + \dfrac{5}{x^2}\right)}{x^3\left(1 - \dfrac{2}{x^2}\right)} \\[8pt]
&= \frac{1}{x} \cdot \frac{3 + \dfrac{5}{x^2}}{1 - \dfrac{2}{x^2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{3x^2 + 5}{x^3 - 2x} = 0 \cdot \frac{3 + 0}{1 - 0} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to +\infty$ the numerator and the denominator both tend to $+\infty$, therefore the ratio is in the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. One factors out the **highest-degree power** of each polynomial: $x^2$ in the numerator and $x^3$ in the denominator. After cancelling, what remains is the factor $\dfrac{1}{x}$, which tends to $0$, multiplied by a fraction that tends to $\dfrac{3}{1} = 3$. Since the product of an infinitesimal function and a function with a finite limit is **infinitesimal**, the limit is $0$. The degree of the denominator exceeds that of the numerator, and this factoring shows why in that case the ratio *always* tends to $0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{x^4 + 2x}{x^3 - 1} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x^4 + 2x}{x^3 - 1}
&= \frac{x^4\left(1 + \dfrac{2}{x^3}\right)}{x^3\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{x^3}\right)} \\[8pt]
&= x \cdot \frac{1 + \dfrac{2}{x^3}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{x^3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{x^4 + 2x}{x^3 - 1} = -\infty
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to -\infty$ the numerator tends to $+\infty$, since the power $x^4$ has an *even* exponent, whereas the denominator tends to $-\infty$, since $x^3$ has an *odd* exponent. One factors out the **highest-degree power** of each polynomial, $x^4$ above and $x^3$ below, and after cancelling what remains is the factor $x$ multiplied by a fraction. The terms $\dfrac{2}{x^3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^3}$ tend to $0$, therefore the fraction tends to $1$. The product of a function that tends to $-\infty$ and a function with limit $1$ tends to $-\infty$, hence the limit is $-\infty$. The sign of the result depends on the **direction of infinity**: as $x \to +\infty$ the same ratio instead tends to $+\infty$.

</div>
