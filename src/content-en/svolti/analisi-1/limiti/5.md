## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x \sin x} .
$$

$$
\frac{1 - \cos x}{x \sin x}
= \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \cdot \frac{x^2}{x \sin x}
= \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \cdot \frac{x}{\sin x}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x}{\sin x} = 1
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x \sin x} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ the numerator tends to $1 - 1 = 0$ and the denominator tends to $0 \cdot 0 = 0$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The numerator $1 - \cos x$ appears in the fundamental limit $\dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$, hence one multiplies and divides by $x^2$. The factor $\dfrac{x^2}{x \sin x}$ simplifies to $\dfrac{x}{\sin x}$, which is the reciprocal of the **fundamental sine limit** and tends to $1$. The limit of the product is the product of the two limits, both finite.

The same result is obtained with **asymptotically equivalent functions**: as $x \to 0$ we have $1 - \cos x \sim \dfrac{x^2}{2}$ and $\sin x \sim x$, and in a *quotient* the substitutions are legitimate, therefore
$$
\frac{1 - \cos x}{x \sin x} \sim \frac{\dfrac{x^2}{2}}{x \cdot x} = \frac{1}{2} .
$$

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos 3x}{x \sin 2x} .
$$

$$
1 - \cos 3x \sim \frac{(3x)^2}{2} = \frac{9x^2}{2}, \qquad
\sin 2x \sim 2x \qquad (x \to 0)
$$

$$
\frac{1 - \cos 3x}{x \sin 2x}
\sim \frac{\dfrac{9x^2}{2}}{x \cdot 2x}
= \frac{9x^2}{4x^2}
= \frac{9}{4}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos 3x}{x \sin 2x} = \frac{9}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ the numerator tends to $1 - 1 = 0$ and the denominator tends to $0$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. Since $3x \to 0$, the **fundamental cosine limit** yields the equivalence $1 - \cos 3x \sim \dfrac{(3x)^2}{2}$, where the argument $3x$ appears in place of $x$. Likewise, since $2x \to 0$, we have $\sin 2x \sim 2x$. The ratio is a *quotient of products*, hence the asymptotic substitutions are **legitimate** and the function is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{9x^2}{4x^2}$. After cancelling $x^2$, which is legitimate since $x \neq 0$, what remains is the constant $\dfrac{9}{4}$, which is the value of the limit.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin\!\left( x^2 \right) \tan 3x}{x \left( 1 - \cos 2x \right)} .
$$

$$
\sin\!\left( x^2 \right) \sim x^2, \qquad
\tan 3x \sim 3x, \qquad
1 - \cos 2x \sim \frac{(2x)^2}{2} = 2x^2 \qquad (x \to 0)
$$

$$
\frac{\sin\!\left( x^2 \right) \tan 3x}{x \left( 1 - \cos 2x \right)}
\sim \frac{x^2 \cdot 3x}{x \cdot 2x^2}
= \frac{3x^3}{2x^3}
= \frac{3}{2}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin\!\left( x^2 \right) \tan 3x}{x \left( 1 - \cos 2x \right)} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0$ every factor of the numerator and of the denominator tends to $0$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{0}{0}$. The fundamental equivalences hold for an infinitesimal argument, *whatever* it is: since $x^2 \to 0$, we have $\sin\!\left( x^2 \right) \sim x^2$; since $3x \to 0$, we have $\tan 3x \sim 3x$; since $2x \to 0$, we have $1 - \cos 2x \sim \dfrac{(2x)^2}{2} = 2x^2$. The function is a quotient of products, hence the three substitutions are **legitimate** and the ratio is asymptotically equivalent to $\dfrac{3x^3}{2x^3}$. After cancelling $x^3$, which is legitimate since $x \neq 0$, what remains is the constant $\dfrac{3}{2}$, which is the value of the limit.

</div>
