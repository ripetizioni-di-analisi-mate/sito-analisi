## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\int \frac{x + 3}{x^2 - x - 2}\, dx .
$$

The denominator factors as $(x - 2)(x + 1)$ and one decomposes into **partial fractions**:
$$
\frac{x + 3}{(x - 2)(x + 1)} = \frac{A}{x - 2} + \frac{B}{x + 1},
\qquad
x + 3 = A(x + 1) + B(x - 2) .
$$

For $x = 2$ we get $5 = 3A$, therefore $A = \dfrac{5}{3}$; for $x = -1$ we get $2 = -3B$, therefore $B = -\dfrac{2}{3}$. Hence
$$
\int \frac{x + 3}{x^2 - x - 2}\, dx
= \frac{5}{3} \ln|x - 2| - \frac{2}{3} \ln|x + 1| + c .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The degree of the numerator is *less* than the degree of the denominator, therefore the division **is not needed**. The trinomial $x^2 - x - 2$ has roots $2$ and $-1$, both real and simple, hence the decomposition contains one term for each root. The constants are found by substituting the roots into the identity $x + 3 = A(x + 1) + B(x - 2)$: each substitution cancels one of the two terms and isolates the other constant. Each partial fraction is integrated with the formula for $\dfrac{f'}{f}$, therefore it gives a **logarithm** with its own coefficient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\int \frac{x + 2}{(x - 1)^2}\, dx .
$$

The root $1$ has multiplicity $2$, therefore the decomposition is
$$
\frac{x + 2}{(x - 1)^2} = \frac{A}{x - 1} + \frac{B}{(x - 1)^2},
\qquad
x + 2 = A(x - 1) + B .
$$

For $x = 1$ we get $B = 3$; the coefficient of $x$ on the left-hand side is $1$ and on the right-hand side is $A$, therefore $A = 1$. Hence
$$
\int \frac{x + 2}{(x - 1)^2}\, dx
= \int \frac{1}{x - 1}\, dx + 3 \int \frac{1}{(x - 1)^2}\, dx
= \ln|x - 1| - \frac{3}{x - 1} + c .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a real root of multiplicity $2$ the decomposition contains **two terms**, one with the denominator to the first power and one with the denominator squared. From the substitution $x = 1$ one immediately obtains $B$; the constant $A$ is obtained by equating the coefficients of $x$, by the **identity principle for polynomials**. The first term gives the logarithm; the second is the integral of $3\,(x - 1)^{-2}$, which by the power formula equals $3 \cdot \dfrac{(x - 1)^{-1}}{-1} = -\dfrac{3}{x - 1}$. Differentiating the result gives $\dfrac{1}{x - 1} + \dfrac{3}{(x - 1)^2} = \dfrac{(x - 1) + 3}{(x - 1)^2} = \dfrac{x + 2}{(x - 1)^2}$, which is the integrand.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\int \frac{2x + 3}{x^2 + 2x + 5}\, dx .
$$

The denominator has negative discriminant. The derivative of the denominator is $2x + 2$ and the numerator can be written as $2x + 3 = (2x + 2) + 1$, therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int \frac{2x + 3}{x^2 + 2x + 5}\, dx
&= \int \frac{2x + 2}{x^2 + 2x + 5}\, dx + \int \frac{1}{(x + 1)^2 + 4}\, dx \\[6pt]
&= \ln\left( x^2 + 2x + 5 \right) + \frac{1}{2} \arctan \frac{x + 1}{2} + c
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discriminant of $x^2 + 2x + 5$ equals $4 - 20 = -16$, negative, therefore the denominator **has no real roots** and the decomposition into fractions with real roots does *not* apply. One isolates in the numerator the derivative of the denominator: the first integral has the form $\dfrac{f'}{f}$ and gives the logarithm, written without absolute value because the denominator is always positive. In the second integral one **completes the square**, $x^2 + 2x + 5 = (x + 1)^2 + 4$, and applies the formula for $\dfrac{1}{u^2 + k^2}$ with $u = x + 1$ and $k = 2$, which gives $\dfrac{1}{2} \arctan \dfrac{x + 1}{2}$. Differentiating the arctangent gives $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{1 + \dfrac{(x+1)^2}{4}} = \dfrac{1}{(x + 1)^2 + 4}$, therefore the result is **correct**.

</div>
