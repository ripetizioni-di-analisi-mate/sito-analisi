## Exercise 1

Compute the area of the region between the parabola $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 2x$.

**Intersections.** From $x^2 = 2x$ it follows that $x(x - 2) = 0$, therefore $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.

**Area.** On $[0, 2]$ the line lies *above* the parabola, because for $x = 1$ we have $2 > 1$, therefore
$$
\mathcal{A} = \int_0^2 \left( 2x - x^2 \right) dx
= \left[ x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^2
= 4 - \frac{8}{3}
= \frac{4}{3} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **intersection points** bound the region, therefore the equation $x^2 = 2x$ is solved first. Between two consecutive intersections the order of the two functions does not change, hence to establish which one lies above a **test value** suffices: at $x = 1$ the line equals $2$ and the parabola equals $1$, therefore on $[0, 2]$ one integrates the difference $2x - x^2$. The integral is computed with the fundamental formula and the result is **positive**, as an area must be.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the area of the region bounded by the curve $y = e^x$, the line $y = e$ and the $y$-axis.

**Intersections.** From $e^x = e$ it follows that $x = 1$; the $y$-axis bounds the region on the left, at $x = 0$.

**Area.** On $[0, 1]$ the line $y = e$ lies above the curve, because $e^x \leq e$ for $x \leq 1$, therefore
$$
\mathcal{A} = \int_0^1 \left( e - e^x \right) dx
= \bigl[ e\,x - e^x \bigr]_0^1
= (e - e) - (0 - 1)
= 1 .
$$

::grafico{f="exp(x);2.71828" area="2.71828;exp(x);0;1" punti="0,1,pieno;1,2.71828,pieno" xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.5 titolo="The region bounded by $y = e^x$, the line $y = e$ (dashed) and the $y$-axis, for $0 \leq x \leq 1$: its area equals $1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The region is bounded by *three* lines: above by the horizontal line $y = e$, below by the graph of the exponential and on the left by the $y$-axis. The **right limit** of integration is the abscissa at which curve and line meet, that is the solution of $e^x = e$, which is $x = 1$ because the exponential is injective. Since $e^x$ is **increasing**, on $[0, 1]$ we have $e^x \leq e$ and one integrates the difference $e - e^x$. An antiderivative is $e\,x - e^x$, since $e$ is a constant, and the evaluation at the limits gives $0 - (-1) = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the area of the region between the curves $y = x^3$ and $y = x$.

**Intersections.** From $x^3 = x$ it follows that $x\,(x^2 - 1) = 0$, therefore $x = -1$, $x = 0$ and $x = 1$.

**Area.** On $[-1, 0]$ the curve $y = x^3$ lies above the line, because for $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ we have $-\dfrac{1}{8} > -\dfrac{1}{2}$; on $[0, 1]$ the line lies above the curve. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}
&= \int_{-1}^0 \left( x^3 - x \right) dx + \int_0^1 \left( x - x^3 \right) dx \\[6pt]
&= \left[ \frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_{-1}^{0} + \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_0^1 \\[6pt]
&= \left( 0 - \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2} \right) \right) + \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4} \right)
= \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4}
= \frac{1}{2} .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^3;x" area="x^3;x;-1;0|x;x^3;0;1" punti="-1,-1,pieno;0,0,pieno;1,1,pieno" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.6 ymax=1.6 titolo="$y = x^3$ and $y = x$ (dashed) meet at $x = -1$, $x = 0$, $x = 1$ and bound two regions symmetric with respect to the origin, each of area $\dfrac{1}{4}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three intersections, therefore the region consists of **two parts** and the interval $[-1, 1]$ is split into $[-1, 0]$ and $[0, 1]$ using the **additivity** of the integral. On each stretch a test value establishes which function lies above: the two curves swap at $0$, hence the difference to integrate changes order on the two stretches. Integrating $x - x^3$ over all of $[-1, 1]$ would give $0$, because the two contributions are opposite, and it would *not* give the area. The sum of the two integrals, both positive, gives the **area** $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>
