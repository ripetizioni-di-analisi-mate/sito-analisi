## Exercise 1

Write in trigonometric and exponential form
$$
z = 1 + i\sqrt{3} .
$$

$$
\rho = \sqrt{1 + 3} = 2
$$
$$
a = 1 > 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \theta = \arctan\sqrt{3} = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$
$$
z = 2\left( \cos\frac{\pi}{3} + i \sin\frac{\pi}{3} \right) = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{\pi}{3}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The modulus is the square root of $a^2 + b^2 = 1 + 3 = 4$, so $\rho = 2$. The point $(1, \sqrt{3})$ has positive real part, therefore it lies in the **first quadrant** and the **principal argument** coincides with the arctangent of the ratio $\dfrac{b}{a} = \sqrt{3}$, that is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, *without* any correction.

As a check, $2 \cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = 2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$ and $2 \sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = 2 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}$, which are the original real part and imaginary part.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write in trigonometric and exponential form
$$
z = -3 + 3i .
$$

$$
\rho = \sqrt{9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
a = -3 < 0, \ b = 3 \geq 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \theta = \arctan(-1) + \pi = -\frac{\pi}{4} + \pi = \frac{3\pi}{4}
$$
$$
z = 3\sqrt{2} \left( \cos\frac{3\pi}{4} + i \sin\frac{3\pi}{4} \right) = 3\sqrt{2}\; e^{\,i \frac{3\pi}{4}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The modulus is $\sqrt{(-3)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$. The point $(-3, 3)$ has negative real part and positive imaginary part, so it lies in the **second quadrant**; the arctangent of $\dfrac{3}{-3} = -1$ equals $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, which is an angle in the fourth quadrant, therefore, according to the cases of the principal argument, $\pi$ is **added** and one obtains $\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.

As a check, $\cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, so $3\sqrt{2} \cos\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = -3$ and $3\sqrt{2} \sin\dfrac{3\pi}{4} = 3$, which coincide with the real part and the imaginary part of $z$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write in trigonometric and exponential form
$$
z = -1 - i\sqrt{3} .
$$

$$
\rho = \sqrt{1 + 3} = 2
$$
$$
a = -1 < 0, \ b = -\sqrt{3} < 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \theta = \arctan\sqrt{3} - \pi = \frac{\pi}{3} - \pi = -\frac{2\pi}{3}
$$
$$
z = 2\left( \cos\!\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) + i \sin\!\left(-\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) \right) = 2\, e^{-i \frac{2\pi}{3}}
$$

::grafico{curve="0.5*cos(t),0.5*sin(t),-2.094,0" xmin=-2 xmax=1.5 ymin=-2.4 ymax=1 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" vettori="0,0,-1,-1.732,z" segmenti="-1,0,-1,-1.732;0,-1.732,-1,-1.732" etichette="0.35,-0.7,θ" titolo="$z = -1 - i\sqrt{3}$ lies in the third quadrant: the principal argument $\theta = -\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is measured clockwise from the positive real semi-axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The modulus is $\sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$, the same as in the first exercise, because changing the sign of the real part and the imaginary part does *not* change the modulus. The point $(-1, -\sqrt{3})$ has both coordinates negative, so it lies in the **third quadrant**; the ratio $\dfrac{b}{a} = \dfrac{-\sqrt{3}}{-1} = \sqrt{3}$ is positive and its arctangent equals $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, an angle in the first quadrant. Since $a < 0$ and $b < 0$, according to the cases of the principal argument $\pi$ is **subtracted** and one obtains $\theta = -\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, which belongs to the interval $(-\pi, \pi]$.

As a check, $\cos\!\left(-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\!\left(-\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $2\cos\theta = -1$ and $2\sin\theta = -\sqrt{3}$, as required.

</div>
