## Exercise 1

Describe the locus of the points $z \in \mathbb{C}$ such that
$$
|z - 1 + i| = 2 .
$$

$$
z = x + iy : \qquad |(x - 1) + i(y + 1)| = 2
$$
$$
\sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + (y + 1)^2} = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x - 1)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 4
$$

The locus is the **circle** with center $(1, -1)$ and radius $2$.

::grafico{curve="1+2*cos(t),-1+2*sin(t),0,6.29" xmin=-1.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-3.6 ymax=1.8 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" punti="1,-1,pieno,1 − i" segmenti="1,-1,3,-1" titolo="$|z - 1 + i| = 2$: circle with center $1 - i$ and radius $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation can be rewritten as $|z - (1 - i)| = 2$: the left-hand side is the **distance** between the point $z$ and the point $1 - i$, so the locus is the set of points at distance $2$ from the point $(1, -1)$, that is a **circle**.

The computation with $z = x + iy$ confirms the **geometric reading**: inside the modulus appears $(x - 1) + i(y + 1)$, whose modulus is $\sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + (y + 1)^2}$. Squaring both sides, which are *nonnegative*, gives the **Cartesian equation** of the circle with center $(1, -1)$ and radius $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Describe the locus of the points $z \in \mathbb{C}$ such that
$$
|z - i| = |z + 1| .
$$

$$
z = x + iy : \qquad x^2 + (y - 1)^2 = (x + 1)^2 + y^2
$$
$$
x^2 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = x^2 + 2x + 1 + y^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
-2y = 2x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = -x
$$

The locus is the line $y = -x$, the **perpendicular bisector** of the segment with endpoints $i$ and $-1$.

::grafico{f="-x" xmin=-2.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-2 ymax=2 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" punti="0,1,pieno,i;-1,0,pieno,−1;-0.5,0.5,vuoto" segmenti="0,1,-1,0" titolo="The line $y = -x$ is the perpendicular bisector of the segment with endpoints $i$ and $-1$: it passes through the midpoint and is perpendicular to the segment"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side is the distance of $z$ from the point $i = (0, 1)$, the right-hand side is the distance of $z$ from the point $-1 = (-1, 0)$: the locus is the set of points **equidistant** from the two points, that is the **perpendicular bisector** of the segment joining them.

Setting $z = x + iy$, both sides, which are *nonnegative*, are squared, and one obtains the equality between the squares of the two distances. The terms $x^2$, $y^2$ and $1$ appear on both sides and cancel; what remains is $-2y = 2x$, that is the line $y = -x$. The line passes through the origin and is **perpendicular** to the segment with endpoints $(0, 1)$ and $(-1, 0)$ at its **midpoint** $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, which indeed satisfies $y = -x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given that $z = 1 + 2i$ is a root of the polynomial
$$
p(z) = z^3 - 3z^2 + 7z - 5 ,
$$
determine all the roots and the factorization of $p$ into real factors.

$$
\text{conjugate roots:} \quad 1 + 2i \ \text{ root} \ \Longrightarrow\ 1 - 2i \ \text{ root}
$$
$$
(z - 1 - 2i)(z - 1 + 2i) = (z - 1)^2 + 4 = z^2 - 2z + 5
$$
$$
z^3 - 3z^2 + 7z - 5 = (z^2 - 2z + 5)(z - 1)
$$

The roots are $1 + 2i$, $1 - 2i$ and $1$; the **real factorization** is $p(z) = (z - 1)(z^2 - 2z + 5)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The polynomial has real coefficients, so by the **theorem on conjugate roots** $\overline{1 + 2i} = 1 - 2i$ is also a root. The product of the two corresponding factors is computed as a **difference of squares** with respect to $z - 1$: $(z - 1)^2 - (2i)^2 = z^2 - 2z + 1 + 4 = z^2 - 2z + 5$, a polynomial with real coefficients and discriminant $4 - 20 = -16 < 0$.

The polynomial $p$ has degree three, so the third factor is of first degree and is obtained by dividing $p$ by $z^2 - 2z + 5$; the quotient is $z - 1$, as is verified by expanding the product: $(z^2 - 2z + 5)(z - 1) = z^3 - z^2 - 2z^2 + 2z + 5z - 5 = z^3 - 3z^2 + 7z - 5$. The **third root** is therefore $z = 1$, and indeed $p(1) = 1 - 3 + 7 - 5 = 0$. The factorization found is the **real** one: a first-degree factor and a second-degree factor with negative discriminant.

</div>
