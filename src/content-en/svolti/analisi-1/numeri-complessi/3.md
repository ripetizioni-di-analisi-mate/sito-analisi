## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
(1 + i)^8 .
$$

$$
1 + i = \sqrt{2} \left( \cos\frac{\pi}{4} + i \sin\frac{\pi}{4} \right)
$$
$$
(1 + i)^8 = (\sqrt{2})^8 \left( \cos\frac{8\pi}{4} + i \sin\frac{8\pi}{4} \right) = 16 \, (\cos 2\pi + i \sin 2\pi) = 16
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $1 + i$ has modulus $\sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$ and positive real part, so its argument is $\arctan 1 = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. By **De Moivre's formula**, in the eighth power the modulus is raised to the eighth power and the argument is multiplied by $8$: the modulus becomes $(\sqrt{2})^8 = 2^4 = 16$ and the argument becomes $2\pi$, which identifies the *same* point as the argument $0$. Therefore the power is the **real number** $16$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
(\sqrt{3} + i)^6 .
$$

$$
\sqrt{3} + i = 2 \left( \cos\frac{\pi}{6} + i \sin\frac{\pi}{6} \right)
$$
$$
(\sqrt{3} + i)^6 = 2^6 \left( \cos\frac{6\pi}{6} + i \sin\frac{6\pi}{6} \right) = 64 \, (\cos\pi + i \sin\pi) = -64
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The modulus of $\sqrt{3} + i$ is $\sqrt{3 + 1} = 2$; the real part is positive, so the argument is $\arctan\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Applying **De Moivre's formula** with $n = 6$, the modulus becomes $2^6 = 64$ and the argument becomes $6 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \pi$. Since $\cos\pi = -1$ and $\sin\pi = 0$, the result is the **real number** $-64$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
(1 - i\sqrt{3})^5 .
$$

$$
1 - i\sqrt{3} = 2 \left( \cos\!\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right) + i \sin\!\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right) \right)
$$
$$
(1 - i\sqrt{3})^5 = 2^5 \left( \cos\!\left(-\frac{5\pi}{3}\right) + i \sin\!\left(-\frac{5\pi}{3}\right) \right)
$$
$$
-\frac{5\pi}{3} + 2\pi = \frac{\pi}{3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(1 - i\sqrt{3})^5 = 32 \left( \cos\frac{\pi}{3} + i \sin\frac{\pi}{3} \right) = 16 + 16\sqrt{3}\, i
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The modulus of $1 - i\sqrt{3}$ is $\sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$; the real part is positive, so the argument is $\arctan(-\sqrt{3}) = -\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, an angle in the fourth quadrant. By **De Moivre's formula** the fifth power has modulus $2^5 = 32$ and argument $-\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$.

The argument $-\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ does *not* belong to the interval $(-\pi, \pi]$, so $2\pi$ is added and one obtains the **principal argument** $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, which identifies the same point. Since $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, the result in **algebraic form** is $32 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} + 32 \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \, i = 16 + 16\sqrt{3}\, i$.

</div>
