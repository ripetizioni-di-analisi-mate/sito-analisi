## Exercise 1

Write in algebraic form
$$
z = (3 + 2i)(1 - i) + (2 - i)^2 .
$$

$$
(3 + 2i)(1 - i) = 3 - 3i + 2i - 2i^2 = 5 - i
$$
$$
(2 - i)^2 = 4 - 4i + i^2 = 3 - 4i
$$
$$
z = (5 - i) + (3 - 4i) = 8 - 5i
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first product is expanded with the **distributive property**: the four terms $3$, $-3i$, $2i$, $-2i^2$ appear. Since $i^2 = -1$, the term $-2i^2$ equals $2$, so the real part is $3 + 2 = 5$ and the imaginary part is $-3 + 2 = -1$.

The square is computed with the formula for the **square of a binomial**: $(2 - i)^2 = 4 - 4i + i^2$. Since $i^2 = -1$, the real part equals $4 - 1 = 3$.

In the final sum the real parts are added together and the imaginary parts are added together, therefore $z = 8 - 5i$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write in algebraic form
$$
z = \frac{1 + 2i}{3 - i} .
$$

$$
z = \frac{(1 + 2i)(3 + i)}{(3 - i)(3 + i)}
= \frac{3 + i + 6i + 2i^2}{9 + 1}
= \frac{1 + 7i}{10}
= \frac{1}{10} + \frac{7}{10}\, i
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To eliminate the imaginary unit from the denominator, numerator and denominator are multiplied by $3 + i$, the **conjugate of the denominator**. In the denominator the product $(3 - i)(3 + i) = 3^2 + 1^2 = 10$ appears, which is **real** because the product of a number and its conjugate is the **square of the modulus**.

In the numerator the product $(1 + 2i)(3 + i)$ gives $3 + i + 6i + 2i^2$; since $2i^2 = -2$, the real part equals $3 - 2 = 1$ and the imaginary part equals $1 + 6 = 7$. Dividing by $10$ separates the real part and the imaginary part of the result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the real part and the imaginary part of
$$
z = \frac{1 + i}{1 - i} + \frac{2}{i} .
$$

$$
\frac{1 + i}{1 - i} = \frac{(1 + i)^2}{(1 - i)(1 + i)} = \frac{1 + 2i + i^2}{2} = \frac{2i}{2} = i
$$
$$
\frac{2}{i} = \frac{2 \cdot (-i)}{i \cdot (-i)} = \frac{-2i}{1} = -2i
$$
$$
z = i - 2i = -i, \qquad \operatorname{Re}(z) = 0, \quad \operatorname{Im}(z) = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first quotient numerator and denominator are multiplied by $1 + i$, the **conjugate of the denominator**. In the numerator $(1 + i)^2 = 1 + 2i + i^2$ appears; since $i^2 = -1$, the real part cancels and $2i$ remains. In the denominator the product equals $1^2 + 1^2 = 2$, so the quotient is $i$.

In the second quotient the conjugate of $i$ is $-i$, and the product $i \cdot (-i) = -i^2 = 1$ makes the denominator **real**; the quotient equals $-2i$.

The sum of the two results is $-i$, a **purely imaginary** number, therefore the real part is $0$ and the imaginary part is $-1$.

</div>
