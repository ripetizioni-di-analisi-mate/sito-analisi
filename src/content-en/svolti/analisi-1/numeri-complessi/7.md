## Exercise 1

Solve in $\mathbb{C}$ the equation
$$
z - 3\bar{z} = 2 + 8i .
$$

$$
z = x + iy : \qquad (x + iy) - 3(x - iy) = -2x + 4iy
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
-2x = 2 \\[4pt]
4y = 8
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x = -1 \\[4pt]
y = 2
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z = -1 + 2i
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation contains $\bar{z}$, so we set $z = x + iy$ with $x$ and $y$ *real* and substitute $\bar{z} = x - iy$. The left-hand side becomes $x + iy - 3x + 3iy = -2x + 4iy$.

Two complex numbers are **equal** *if and only if* their real parts and their imaginary parts coincide, therefore the equation is equivalent to the **system** $-2x = 2$ and $4y = 8$, from which $x = -1$ and $y = 2$. As a check, for $z = -1 + 2i$ we have $z - 3\bar{z} = -1 + 2i - 3(-1 - 2i) = -1 + 2i + 3 + 6i = 2 + 8i$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve in $\mathbb{C}$ the equation
$$
z \bar{z} + 2z = 8 + 6i .
$$

$$
z = x + iy : \qquad (x^2 + y^2) + 2x + 2iy = 8 + 6i
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 + 2x = 8 \\[4pt]
2y = 6
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x^2 + 2x + 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
y = 3
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad z = -1 + 3i
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Setting $z = x + iy$, the product $z\bar{z}$ is the **real number** $x^2 + y^2$, so the left-hand side has real part $x^2 + y^2 + 2x$ and imaginary part $2y$. Equating the **imaginary parts** immediately gives $y = 3$.

Substituting $y = 3$ into the equation of the real parts gives $x^2 + 9 + 2x = 8$, that is $x^2 + 2x + 1 = 0$, which is the square $(x + 1)^2 = 0$ and has the **unique solution** $x = -1$. The solution is therefore $z = -1 + 3i$. As a check, $z\bar{z} = 1 + 9 = 10$ and $2z = -2 + 6i$, whose sum is $8 + 6i$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve in $\mathbb{C}$ the equation
$$
z^2 = \bar{z} .
$$

$$
|z^2| = |\bar{z}| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |z|^2 = |z| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |z| = 0 \ \text{ or } \ |z| = 1
$$
$$
|z| = 1 : \qquad z^2 = \bar{z} \ \Longrightarrow\ z^3 = z \bar{z} = |z|^2 = 1
$$
$$
z = 0, \qquad z = 1, \qquad z = -\frac{1}{2} + i\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \qquad z = -\frac{1}{2} - i\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" punti="0,0,pieno;1,0,pieno;-0.5,0.866,pieno;-0.5,-0.866,pieno" segmenti="1,0,-0.5,0.866;-0.5,0.866,-0.5,-0.866;-0.5,-0.866,1,0" titolo="The solutions of $z^2 = \bar{z}$: the origin and the three cube roots of unity, on the unit circle"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Taking the **modulus** of both sides gives $|z|^2 = |z|$, because the modulus of a square is the square of the modulus and the conjugate has the *same* modulus as the number. The real equation $|z|^2 = |z|$ has the solutions $|z| = 0$, that is $z = 0$, and $|z| = 1$.

In the case $|z| = 1$ both sides of the equation are multiplied by $z$: the left-hand side becomes $z^3$ and the right-hand side becomes $z\bar{z} = |z|^2 = 1$. The equation therefore reduces to $z^3 = 1$, whose solutions are the three **cube roots of unity**, all of modulus $1$ and therefore **acceptable**. In total there are **four** solutions: $z = 0$ and the three cube roots of unity. As a check of the root $z = -\dfrac{1}{2} + i\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$: its square equals $\dfrac{1}{4} - i\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \dfrac{3}{4} = -\dfrac{1}{2} - i\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, which is the conjugate of $z$.

</div>
