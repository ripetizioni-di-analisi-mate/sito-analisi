## Exercise 1

Solve in $\mathbb{C}$ the equation
$$
z^3 = -8 .
$$

$$
-8 = 8\, e^{\,i\pi}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z_k = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{\pi + 2k\pi}{3}}, \qquad k = 0, 1, 2
$$
$$
z_0 = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{\pi}{3}} = 1 + i\sqrt{3}, \qquad
z_1 = 2\, e^{\,i\pi} = -2, \qquad
z_2 = 2\, e^{-i \frac{\pi}{3}} = 1 - i\sqrt{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $-8$ has modulus $8$ and argument $\pi$. By the formula for $n$-th roots with $n = 3$, the solutions have modulus $\sqrt[3]{8} = 2$ and arguments $\dfrac{\pi + 2k\pi}{3}$, that is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\pi$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$; the last one, brought into the interval $(-\pi, \pi]$, is $-\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

The **real solution** is $z_1 = -2$, which is the real cube root of $-8$; the other two are **complex conjugates**, with algebraic form obtained from $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. As a check, $(1 + i\sqrt{3})^2 = 1 + 2\sqrt{3}\,i - 3 = -2 + 2\sqrt{3}\,i$, so $(1 + i\sqrt{3})^3 = (-2 + 2\sqrt{3}\,i)(1 + i\sqrt{3}) = -2 - 2\sqrt{3}\,i + 2\sqrt{3}\,i + 2 \cdot 3\, i^2 = -8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve in $\mathbb{C}$ the equation
$$
z^4 = -1 .
$$

$$
-1 = e^{\,i\pi}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z_k = e^{\,i \frac{\pi + 2k\pi}{4}}, \qquad k = 0, 1, 2, 3
$$
$$
z_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1 + i), \qquad
z_1 = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(-1 + i), \qquad
z_2 = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(-1 - i), \qquad
z_3 = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(1 - i)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $-1$ has modulus $1$ and argument $\pi$, so the four solutions have modulus $\sqrt[4]{1} = 1$ and arguments $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$. *None* of the solutions is real, because a real fourth power is *never* negative.

In algebraic form each solution has coordinates $\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, with the signs given by the quadrant: the four solutions are the vertices of a **square** inscribed in the unit circle, with its diagonals on the bisectors of the quadrants. They form two **conjugate pairs**, $z_0$ with $z_3$ and $z_1$ with $z_2$, in agreement with the fact that the polynomial $z^4 + 1$ has real coefficients.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve in $\mathbb{C}$ the equation
$$
z^3 = 8i .
$$

$$
8i = 8\, e^{\,i \frac{\pi}{2}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z_k = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{\pi + 4k\pi}{6}}, \qquad k = 0, 1, 2
$$
$$
z_0 = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{\pi}{6}} = \sqrt{3} + i, \qquad
z_1 = 2\, e^{\,i \frac{5\pi}{6}} = -\sqrt{3} + i, \qquad
z_2 = 2\, e^{-i \frac{\pi}{2}} = -2i
$$

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.6 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.4 uguale=1 nomi="Re,Im" punti="1.732,1,pieno,z₀;-1.732,1,pieno,z₁;0,-2,pieno,z₂" segmenti="1.732,1,-1.732,1;-1.732,1,0,-2;0,-2,1.732,1" titolo="The solutions of $z^3 = 8i$: an equilateral triangle inscribed in the circle of radius $2$, symmetric with respect to the imaginary axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $8i$ is **purely imaginary** with positive imaginary part, so it has modulus $8$ and argument $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The cube roots have modulus $2$ and arguments $\dfrac{\pi + 4k\pi}{6}$, that is $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$; the last one, brought into the interval $(-\pi, \pi]$, is $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

The algebraic form follows from the values $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $z_0 = \sqrt{3} + i$ and, by **symmetry** with respect to the imaginary axis, $z_1 = -\sqrt{3} + i$. As a check of $z_2$: $(-2i)^3 = -8 i^3 = -8 \cdot (-i) = 8i$. The three solutions are the vertices of an **equilateral triangle** inscribed in the circle of radius $2$.

</div>
