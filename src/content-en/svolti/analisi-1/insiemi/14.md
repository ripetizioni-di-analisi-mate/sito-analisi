## Exercise 1

Solve
$$
\log_2 (x - 1) < 3 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x - 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
x - 1 < 2^3
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x > 1 \\[4pt]
x < 9
\end{cases}
$$

$$
1 < x < 9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **existence condition** requires a positive argument, that is $x > 1$, and it must be put in a **system** with the inequality. Writing $3 = \log_2 2^3 = \log_2 8$, the inequality compares two logarithms to base $2$; the base is greater than $1$, so the logarithm is strictly increasing and the comparison is equivalent to $x - 1 < 8$ in the *same* direction. The system of the two conditions gives the interval $(1, 9)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve
$$
\log_{\frac{1}{3}} (2x + 1) \geq -1 .
$$

$$
-1 = \log_{\frac{1}{3}} \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^{-1} = \log_{\frac{1}{3}} 3
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 1 > 0 \\[4pt]
2x + 1 \leq 3
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x > -\dfrac{1}{2} \\[10pt]
x \leq 1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
-\frac{1}{2} < x \leq 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is written as a logarithm to the same base: $-1 = \log_{\frac{1}{3}} 3$, because $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{-1} = 3$. The base $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is less than $1$, so the logarithm is **strictly decreasing** and the comparison between the logarithms is equivalent to the comparison between the arguments with the **direction reversed**: $2x + 1 \leq 3$.

The **existence condition** $2x + 1 > 0$ enters a **system** with this inequality. The first gives $x > -\dfrac{1}{2}$, the second $x \leq 1$, so the solution is the interval $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}, 1\right]$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve
$$
\left( \ln x \right)^2 - \ln x - 2 > 0 .
$$

$$
x > 0, \qquad t = \ln x
$$

$$
t^2 - t - 2 > 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
(t - 2)(t + 1) > 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
t < -1 \ \text{ or } \ t > 2
$$

$$
\ln x < -1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x < \frac{1}{e},
\qquad\quad
\ln x > 2
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x > e^2
$$

$$
x \in \left( 0,\, \frac{1}{e} \right) \cup \left( e^2,\, +\infty \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **existence condition** is $x > 0$. The **substitution** $t = \ln x$ turns the inequality into a quadratic inequality: the trinomial $t^2 - t - 2$ has roots $-1$ and $2$, because their sum is $1$ and their product is $-2$, and it has a positive leading coefficient, so it is positive *outside* the roots: $t < -1$ or $t > 2$.

We return to the variable $x$ by applying the **exponential**, which is strictly increasing and therefore **preserves the direction** of inequalities: from $\ln x < -1$ it follows that $x < e^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{e}$, and from $\ln x > 2$ it follows that $x > e^2$. Intersecting with the condition $x > 0$ gives the **union** of the two intervals indicated.

</div>
