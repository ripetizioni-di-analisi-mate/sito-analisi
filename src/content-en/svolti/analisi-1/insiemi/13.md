## Exercise 1

Solve
$$
3^{2x - 1} > 27 .
$$

$$
3^{2x - 1} > 3^{3}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
2x - 1 > 3
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x > 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number $27$ is a power of the same base, $27 = 3^3$, so the inequality compares two powers with base $3$. The base is greater than $1$, therefore the exponential is **strictly increasing** and the comparison between the powers is equivalent to the comparison between the exponents, in the **same direction**: $2x - 1 > 3$. Adding $1$ and dividing by $2$ gives $x > 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve
$$
\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{x^2 - 3x} \geq \frac{1}{16} .
$$

$$
\left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{x^2 - 3x} \geq \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^{4}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x^2 - 3x \leq 4
$$

$$
x^2 - 3x - 4 \leq 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
(x - 4)(x + 1) \leq 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
-1 \leq x \leq 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is written as a power of the same base: $\dfrac{1}{16} = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{4}$. The base $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is less than $1$, so the exponential is **strictly decreasing** and the comparison between the powers is equivalent to the comparison between the exponents with the **direction reversed**: $x^2 - 3x \leq 4$.

The quadratic inequality $x^2 - 3x - 4 \leq 0$ is solved by **factorization**: the roots of $x^2 - 3x - 4$ are $-1$ and $4$, because their sum is $3$ and their product is $-4$. The trinomial has a positive leading coefficient, so it is less than or equal to $0$ **between the roots**, that is for $-1 \leq x \leq 4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve
$$
4^{x} - 3 \cdot 2^{x} + 2 \leq 0 .
$$

$$
t = 2^{x} > 0,
\qquad
4^{x} = \left( 2^{x} \right)^2 = t^2
$$

$$
t^2 - 3t + 2 \leq 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
(t - 1)(t - 2) \leq 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
1 \leq t \leq 2
$$

$$
1 \leq 2^{x} \leq 2
\;\Longrightarrow\;
2^{0} \leq 2^{x} \leq 2^{1}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
0 \leq x \leq 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two powers have different bases, but $4^{x} = (2^2)^{x} = (2^{x})^2$, so the **substitution** $t = 2^{x}$ turns the inequality into a quadratic inequality in the variable $t$, with the **constraint** $t > 0$ that comes from the range of the exponential.

The trinomial $t^2 - 3t + 2$ has roots $1$ and $2$ and a positive leading coefficient, so it is less than or equal to $0$ for $1 \leq t \leq 2$; the interval is contained in $(0, +\infty)$, therefore the constraint $t > 0$ is **satisfied**. Returning to the variable $x$, the double inequality $2^{0} \leq 2^{x} \leq 2^{1}$ has a base *greater* than $1$, so it is equivalent to the same double inequality between the exponents: $0 \leq x \leq 1$.

</div>
