## Exercise 1

Given $A = \{1, 2, 3\}$ and $B = \{2, 4\}$, determine $A \cup B$, $A \cap B$, $A \setminus B$, $B \setminus A$ and $A \times B$.

$$
A \cup B = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}, \qquad A \cap B = \{2\}
$$

$$
A \setminus B = \{1, 3\}, \qquad B \setminus A = \{4\}
$$

$$
A \times B = \{(1, 2),\ (1, 4),\ (2, 2),\ (2, 4),\ (3, 2),\ (3, 4)\}
$$

::venn{zone="a" elementi="a:1,3;ab:2;b:4" titolo="$A \setminus B = \{1, 3\}$: the elements of $A$ outside $B$"}

::venn{zone="b" elementi="a:1,3;ab:2;b:4" titolo="$B \setminus A = \{4\}$: the elements of $B$ outside $A$; the common element $2$ lies in the intersection"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **union** collects the elements that lie in *at least one* of the two sets, so it contains $1, 2, 3$ coming from $A$ and $4$ coming from $B$; the element $2$ appears only once, because repetitions do not count in a set. The **intersection** collects the common elements, and the only number that belongs both to $A$ and to $B$ is $2$. The difference $A \setminus B$ contains the elements of $A$ that are not in $B$, that is $1$ and $3$; the difference $B \setminus A$ contains only the elements of $B$ outside $A$, that is $4$: the two differences are **different**, because the order of the sets matters. The Cartesian product is the set of ordered pairs with first component in $A$ and second component in $B$: since $A$ has $3$ elements and $B$ has $2$, there are $3 \cdot 2 = 6$ pairs.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given the intervals $A = [-1, 3)$ and $B = (1, 5]$, determine $A \cup B$, $A \cap B$, $A \setminus B$ and the complement $A^{c}$ with respect to $\mathbb{R}$.

$$
A \cup B = [-1, 5], \qquad A \cap B = (1, 3)
$$

$$
A \setminus B = [-1, 1], \qquad A^{c} = (-\infty, -1) \cup [3, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two intervals overlap in the region between $1$ and $3$, so the union is a **single interval** that goes from the left endpoint of $A$ to the right endpoint of $B$: the point $-1$ is included because it belongs to $A$, the point $5$ is included because it belongs to $B$. The intersection is the **common region**: a point lies in both sets when it is greater than $1$, by the condition of $B$, and less than $3$, by the condition of $A$; the endpoints $1$ and $3$ are **excluded** because $1 \notin B$ and $3 \notin A$. The difference $A \setminus B$ contains the points of $A$ that are not in $B$, that is the points of $[-1, 3)$ with $x \leq 1$: the point $1$ belongs to it, since $1 \in A$ and $1 \notin B$. The complement of $A$ contains the reals less than $-1$, an endpoint included in $A$ and therefore excluded from the complement, and the reals from $3$ onward, since $3 \notin A$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given $A = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : x^2 \leq 9\}$ and $B = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : x > 1\}$, determine $A \cap B$, $A \cup B$ and $B \setminus A$.

$$
A = [-3, 3], \qquad B = (1, +\infty)
$$

$$
A \cap B = (1, 3], \qquad A \cup B = [-3, +\infty), \qquad B \setminus A = (3, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before operating it is convenient to write the two sets as intervals. The inequality $x^2 \leq 9$ is equivalent to $|x| \leq 3$, that is to $-3 \leq x \leq 3$, so $A$ is the **closed interval** $[-3, 3]$; the set $B$ is the **open half-line** $(1, +\infty)$.

The intersection is the set of points that satisfy *both* conditions $x > 1$ and $x \leq 3$, that is $(1, 3]$: the point $3$ is included because it belongs both to $A$ and to $B$, while $1$ is excluded because $1 \notin B$. Since the two sets overlap, the union is a single set that starts from $-3$, included, and is not bounded above, since $B$ contains all the reals greater than $1$. The difference $B \setminus A$ contains the points of $B$ outside $A$, that is the reals with $x > 1$ and $x > 3$: the more **restrictive** condition is $x > 3$, and the endpoint $3$ is excluded because $3 \in A$.

</div>
