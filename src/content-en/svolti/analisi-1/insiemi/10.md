## Exercise 1

Verify that $f(x) = 3x - 2$, from $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$, is bijective and compute its inverse.

$$
f(x_1) = f(x_2)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
3x_1 - 2 = 3x_2 - 2
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x_1 = x_2
$$

$$
y = 3x - 2
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x = \frac{y + 2}{3}
$$

$$
f^{-1}(y) = \frac{y + 2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For injectivity, assume $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$: adding $2$ to both sides and dividing by $3$ gives $x_1 = x_2$, so $f$ is **injective**. For surjectivity, solve the equation $y = 3x - 2$ for $x$: for every $y \in \mathbb{R}$ the solution $x = \dfrac{y + 2}{3}$ exists, therefore every value of the codomain is taken and $f$ is **surjective**. The function is therefore **bijective** and the expression found for $x$ as a function of $y$ is the inverse. Check: $f^{-1}(f(x)) = \dfrac{(3x - 2) + 2}{3} = x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that $f(x) = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 1}$, defined on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}$, is injective; determine its range and its inverse.

$$
y = \frac{2x + 1}{x - 1}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
y x - y = 2x + 1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x (y - 2) = y + 1
$$

$$
x = \frac{y + 1}{y - 2} \qquad (y \neq 2)
$$

$$
f^{-1}(y) = \frac{y + 1}{y - 2},
\qquad
\text{range of } f = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{2\}
$$

::grafico{f="(2*x+1)/(x-1)" xmin=-6 xmax=8 ymin=-6 ymax=10 asintoti="x=1;y=2" punti="0,-1,pieno" titolo="$y = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x - 1}$: every horizontal line $y = c$ with $c \neq 2$ meets the graph at a single point, the line $y = 2$ does not meet it"}

<div class="spiegazione">

We study the equation $y = f(x)$ as $y$ varies. Multiplying both sides by $x - 1$, which is *different* from $0$ in the domain, the equation becomes $y x - y = 2x + 1$; moving the terms with $x$ to the left and factoring gives $x(y - 2) = y + 1$.

For $y \neq 2$ the solution $x = \dfrac{y + 1}{y - 2}$ exists and is unique, and it belongs to the domain because it is different from $1$, since $y + 1 \neq y - 2$; so every value $y \neq 2$ is taken *exactly once*. For $y = 2$ the equation becomes $0 \cdot x = 3$, which is false for every $x$, therefore the value $2$ belongs to the codomain but **not to the range**. The solution, when it exists, is unique, so $f$ is **injective**; the range is $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{2\}$ and the inverse is defined on this set. Check with a point: $f(0) = -1$ and $f^{-1}(-1) = \dfrac{-1 + 1}{-1 - 2} = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Verify that $f(x) = e^{x} - e^{-x}$ is invertible on $\mathbb{R}$ and compute its inverse.

$$
y = e^{x} - e^{-x},
\qquad t = e^{x} > 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
t - \frac{1}{t} = y
\;\Longrightarrow\;
t^2 - y\,t - 1 = 0
$$

$$
t = \frac{y + \sqrt{y^2 + 4}}{2}
$$

$$
f^{-1}(y) = \ln \frac{y + \sqrt{y^2 + 4}}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

$e^{x}$ is strictly increasing and $-e^{-x}$ is strictly increasing, because $e^{-x}$ is strictly decreasing; the sum of strictly increasing functions is strictly increasing, so $f$ is **strictly increasing** on $\mathbb{R}$ and therefore **injective**.

To find the inverse, set $t = e^{x}$, with $t > 0$. The equation $t - \dfrac{1}{t} = y$, multiplied by $t$, becomes the quadratic equation $t^2 - y\,t - 1 = 0$, with solutions $t = \dfrac{y \pm \sqrt{y^2 + 4}}{2}$. Since $\sqrt{y^2 + 4} > \sqrt{y^2} = |y|$, the solution with the minus sign is negative and the one with the plus sign is positive; since $t = e^{x} > 0$, *only* the solution with the plus sign remains. From $e^{x} = t$ it follows that $x = \ln t$, that is the expression of the inverse.

The equation has a positive solution for every $y \in \mathbb{R}$, so the **range** of $f$ is $\mathbb{R}$ and the inverse is defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$. Check with a point: $f(0) = 1 - 1 = 0$ and $f^{-1}(0) = \ln \dfrac{0 + 2}{2} = \ln 1 = 0$.

</div>
