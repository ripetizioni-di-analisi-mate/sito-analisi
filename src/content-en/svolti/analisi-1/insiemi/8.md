## Exercise 1

Compute $\dbinom{8}{3}$ and $\dbinom{8}{5}$, and simplify the expression $\dfrac{(n+1)!}{(n-1)!}$ for $n \geq 1$.

$$
\binom{8}{3} = \frac{8!}{3!\,5!} = \frac{8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 56
$$

$$
\binom{8}{5} = \binom{8}{3} = 56
$$

$$
\frac{(n+1)!}{(n-1)!} = \frac{(n+1) \cdot n \cdot (n-1)!}{(n-1)!} = n\,(n+1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first computation it is convenient to **simplify the factorials** before multiplying: in the ratio $\dfrac{8!}{3!\,5!}$ the factor $5!$ cancels the tail of the product $8!$, so only the factors $8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6$ remain in the numerator, to be divided by $3! = 6$. The result is $56$.

For $\dbinom{8}{5}$ *no* computation is needed. The **symmetry property** gives $\dbinom{n}{k} = \dbinom{n}{n-k}$ and, since $8 - 5 = 3$, the coefficient coincides with $\dbinom{8}{3} = 56$.

In the last expression the **recursive formula** for the factorial is applied *twice*: $(n+1)! = (n+1) \cdot n!$ and $n! = n \cdot (n-1)!$. The factor $(n-1)!$ thus appears both in the numerator and in the denominator and cancels, therefore the ratio equals $n(n+1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand the power $(x - 2)^5$ with the binomial theorem.

$$
(x - 2)^5 = \sum_{k=0}^{5} \binom{5}{k} x^{\,5-k} (-2)^k
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 2)^5
&= x^5 + 5x^4(-2) + 10x^3 \cdot 4 + 10x^2(-8) + 5x \cdot 16 + (-32) \\[6pt]
&= x^5 - 10x^4 + 40x^3 - 80x^2 + 80x - 32
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The power is written as $(x + (-2))^5$, so the **binomial formula** applies with $a = x$, $b = -2$ and $n = 5$. The binomial coefficients of row $n = 5$ of **Pascal's triangle** are $1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1$.

Each term is the product of the binomial coefficient, the decreasing power of $x$ and the increasing power of $-2$: the powers of $-2$ are, in order, $1, -2, 4, -8, 16, -32$. Since the base is negative, the signs of the terms **alternate**: the even powers of $-2$ are positive and the odd ones negative. Multiplying and adding gives the final expansion; as a check, for $x = 1$ the expansion gives $1 - 10 + 40 - 80 + 80 - 32 = -1$, which coincides with $(1 - 2)^5 = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the term independent of $x$ in the expansion of
$$
\left( x + \frac{2}{x} \right)^{8} \qquad (x \neq 0).
$$

$$
\binom{8}{k}\, x^{\,8-k} \left( \frac{2}{x} \right)^{k}
= \binom{8}{k}\, 2^k\, x^{\,8-2k}
$$

$$
8 - 2k = 0 \iff k = 4
$$

$$
\binom{8}{4}\, 2^4 = 70 \cdot 16 = 1120
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **general term** of the expansion is obtained from the binomial formula with $a = x$ and $b = \dfrac{2}{x}$: collecting the powers of $x$, the term with index $k$ contains $x^{8-k} \cdot x^{-k} = x^{8-2k}$, with coefficient $\dbinom{8}{k} 2^k$.

The term independent of $x$ is the one in which the exponent **vanishes**, so we solve $8 - 2k = 0$, which gives $k = 4$. The formula thus identifies the required term *without* having to compute the others. The term with index $k = 4$ equals $\dbinom{8}{4} \cdot 2^4$; since $\dbinom{8}{4} = \dfrac{8 \cdot 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5}{4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1} = 70$ and $2^4 = 16$, the term independent of $x$ is $70 \cdot 16 = 1120$.

</div>
