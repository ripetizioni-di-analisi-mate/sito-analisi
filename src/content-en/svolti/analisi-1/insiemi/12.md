## Exercise 1

Sketch the graph of
$$
f(x) = (x - 2)^2 - 3
$$
starting from that of $y = x^2$, indicating the vertex and the range.

$$
x^2
\;\longrightarrow\;
(x - 2)^2
\;\longrightarrow\;
(x - 2)^2 - 3
$$

Vertex $(2, -3)$, range $[-3, +\infty)$.

::grafico{f="(x-2)^2-3" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=6 punti="2,-3" titolo="$y = (x - 2)^2 - 3$: parabola with vertex $(2, -3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Replacing $x$ with $x - 2$ translates the graph of $x^2$ **to the right** by $2$, so the vertex moves from $(0, 0)$ to $(2, 0)$. Subtracting $3$ translates the graph **downward** by $3$, therefore the vertex ends up at $(2, -3)$. The square $(x - 2)^2$ takes all and only the values greater than or equal to $0$, so $f$ takes all and only the values greater than or equal to $-3$: the **range** is $[-3, +\infty)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Sketch the graph of
$$
f(x) = \left| x^2 - 4 \right|
$$
starting from that of $y = x^2$.

$$
x^2
\;\longrightarrow\;
x^2 - 4
\;\longrightarrow\;
\left| x^2 - 4 \right|
$$

$$
x^2 - 4 < 0
\;\iff\;
-2 < x < 2
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-4)" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=8 punti="-2,0;2,0;0,4" titolo="$y = |x^2 - 4|$: the arc with $-2 < x < 2$ is reflected above the $x$-axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $4$ translates the parabola **downward** by $4$; the new parabola meets the $x$-axis at $-2$ and at $2$ and is negative between these two abscissas. The **absolute value** leaves in place the part of the graph with ordinate greater than or equal to $0$ and reflects above the $x$-axis the part with negative ordinate, so the arc of the parabola between $-2$ and $2$ is flipped and the lowest point $(0, -4)$ becomes the point $(0, 4)$. The resulting function is **even**, because $x^2 - 4$ is even, and its range is $[0, +\infty)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Sketch the graph of
$$
f(x) = 2 - e^{-x} ,
$$
indicating the range and the intersections with the axes.

$$
e^{x}
\;\longrightarrow\;
e^{-x}
\;\longrightarrow\;
-e^{-x}
\;\longrightarrow\;
2 - e^{-x}
$$

$$
f(0) = 2 - 1 = 1,
\qquad
2 - e^{-x} = 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
e^{-x} = 2
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x = -\ln 2
$$

Range $(-\infty, 2)$.

::grafico{f="2-exp(-x)" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 asintoti="y=2" punti="0,1" titolo="$y = 2 - e^{-x}$: the graph approaches the line $y = 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The chain of transformations starts from $e^{x}$. Replacing $x$ with $-x$ **reflects** the graph across the $y$-axis and produces $e^{-x}$, which is decreasing. Changing the sign **reflects** the graph across the $x$-axis and produces $-e^{-x}$, with values in $(-\infty, 0)$. Adding $2$ **translates** the graph upward by $2$.

Since $e^{-x}$ takes all and only the positive values, $2 - e^{-x}$ takes *all and only* the values less than $2$: the **range** is $(-\infty, 2)$ and the graph approaches the line $y = 2$ for large positive $x$, without reaching it. The intersection with the $y$-axis is $f(0) = 1$. For the intersection with the $x$-axis we solve $e^{-x} = 2$: applying the **logarithm**, which is the inverse of the exponential, one obtains $-x = \ln 2$, that is $x = -\ln 2$.

</div>
