## Exercise 1

Determine the upper bounds, lower bounds, supremum, infimum, maximum and minimum of $A = (-2, 5]$.

$$
\text{upper bounds: } [5, +\infty), \qquad \text{lower bounds: } (-\infty, -2]
$$

$$
\sup A = \max A = 5, \qquad \inf A = -2, \qquad \min A \ \text{does not exist}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A number $M$ is an **upper bound** when it is greater than or equal to *every* element of the set. Every $M \geq 5$ is an upper bound, because the elements of $A$ are at most $5$; no $M < 5$ is one, because $5 \in A$ and $5 > M$. Since $5$ is an upper bound and belongs to the set, $5$ is the **maximum**, and the maximum coincides with the **supremum**.

For lower bounds the reasoning is symmetric: every $m \leq -2$ is a lower bound, because the elements of $A$ exceed $-2$; a number $m > -2$ is not one, because between $-2$ and $m$ the interval contains points less than $m$. The infimum, which is the largest of the lower bounds, therefore equals $-2$. The minimum **does not exist**, because $-2 \notin A$ and the minimum of a set must *belong* to it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the supremum, infimum, maximum and minimum of
$$
A = \left\{ \frac{n}{n+1} : n \in \mathbb{N},\ n \geq 1 \right\} .
$$

$$
\frac{n}{n+1} = 1 - \frac{1}{n+1}
$$

$$
\inf A = \min A = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad \sup A = 1, \qquad \max A \ \text{does not exist}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rewriting $\dfrac{n}{n+1} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{n+1}$ makes the behavior of the elements readable: as $n$ increases the term $\dfrac{1}{n+1}$ decreases, so the elements increase. The smallest element is therefore obtained with $n = 1$ and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$: it is a lower bound and belongs to the set, so it is the **minimum**, and it coincides with the **infimum**.

The number $1$ is an upper bound, because $\dfrac{1}{n+1} > 0$ and therefore every element is less than $1$. *No* number $L < 1$ is an upper bound: indeed the inequality $1 - \dfrac{1}{n+1} > L$ is equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{n+1} < 1 - L$, that is to $n + 1 > \dfrac{1}{1 - L}$, and such a large natural number exists because $\mathbb{N}$ is not bounded above. Hence $1$ is the smallest of the upper bounds, that is $\sup A = 1$. The maximum **does not exist**, because $1 \notin A$; indeed the equality $\dfrac{n}{n+1} = 1$ would require $\dfrac{1}{n+1} = 0$, which no natural number achieves.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the supremum, infimum, maximum and minimum of
$$
A = \left\{ (-1)^n + \frac{1}{n} : n \in \mathbb{N},\ n \geq 1 \right\} .
$$

$$
n \ \text{even}: \quad 1 + \frac{1}{n} \qquad\qquad n \ \text{odd}: \quad -1 + \frac{1}{n}
$$

$$
\sup A = \max A = \frac{3}{2}, \qquad \inf A = -1, \qquad \min A \ \text{does not exist}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $(-1)^n$ distinguishes two families of elements, so it is convenient to examine them *separately*. For $n$ even the elements are $1 + \dfrac{1}{n}$: the largest is obtained with the smallest even $n$, that is $n = 2$, and equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$, while the others lie between $1$ and $\dfrac{3}{2}$. For $n$ odd the elements are $-1 + \dfrac{1}{n}$, all less than or equal to $0$, because $\dfrac{1}{n} \leq 1$. Every element of the set is therefore less than or equal to $\dfrac{3}{2}$, and $\dfrac{3}{2}$ belongs to $A$: it is the **maximum**, therefore it is also the **supremum**.

For the infimum: every element exceeds $-1$, because $(-1)^n \geq -1$ and $\dfrac{1}{n} > 0$, so $-1$ is a **lower bound**. No $m > -1$ is a lower bound: indeed for $n$ odd the inequality $-1 + \dfrac{1}{n} < m$ is equivalent to $\dfrac{1}{n} < m + 1$, that is to $n > \dfrac{1}{m+1}$, and such a large odd natural number exists. Hence $\inf A = -1$. The minimum **does not exist**, because $-1 \notin A$; indeed the equality $-1 + \dfrac{1}{n} = -1$ would require $\dfrac{1}{n} = 0$.

</div>
