## Exercise 1

Determine the natural domain of
$$
f(x) = \sqrt{3 - x} + \frac{1}{x + 1} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
3 - x \geq 0 \\[4pt]
x + 1 \neq 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \leq 3 \\[4pt]
x \neq -1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
D = (-\infty, -1) \cup (-1, 3]
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression contains a root of even index and a denominator, so there are *two* **existence conditions**: the argument of the root is greater than or equal to $0$ and the denominator is different from $0$. The two conditions must hold at the same point, therefore they form a **system**. The first gives $x \leq 3$, the second $x \neq -1$; the solution set of the system is the half-line $x \leq 3$ without the point $-1$, that is the **union** of the two intervals indicated.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the natural domain of
$$
f(x) = \frac{\sqrt{x + 2}}{\ln(4 - x)} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2 \geq 0 \\[4pt]
4 - x > 0 \\[4pt]
\ln(4 - x) \neq 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x \geq -2 \\[4pt]
x < 4 \\[4pt]
x \neq 3
\end{cases}
$$

$$
D = [-2, 3) \cup (3, 4)
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(x+2)/log(4-x)" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=8 asintoti="x=3" punti="-2,0,pieno;4,0,vuoto" titolo="$y = \dfrac{\sqrt{x + 2}}{\ln(4 - x)}$ exists for $-2 \leq x < 4$ with $x \neq 3$: at $x = 3$ the denominator vanishes"}

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three conditions. The argument of the root is greater than or equal to $0$, so $x \geq -2$. The argument of the logarithm is greater than $0$, so $x < 4$. The logarithm appears in the denominator, therefore it must be **different** from $0$: since $\ln t = 0$ *only* for $t = 1$, the condition $\ln(4 - x) \neq 0$ is equivalent to $4 - x \neq 1$, that is $x \neq 3$. The **intersection** of the three conditions is the interval $[-2, 4)$ without the point $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the natural domain of
$$
f(x) = \arcsin \frac{2x}{x + 1} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2x}{x + 1} \leq 1 \\[10pt]
\dfrac{2x}{x + 1} \geq -1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\frac{2x}{x + 1} - 1 = \frac{2x - x - 1}{x + 1} = \frac{x - 1}{x + 1} \leq 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
-1 < x \leq 1
$$

$$
\frac{2x}{x + 1} + 1 = \frac{2x + x + 1}{x + 1} = \frac{3x + 1}{x + 1} \geq 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x < -1 \ \text{ or } \ x \geq -\frac{1}{3}
$$

$$
D = \left[ -\frac{1}{3},\; 1 \right]
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The arcsine is defined for arguments in $[-1, 1]$, so the conditions are $\dfrac{2x}{x + 1} \leq 1$ and $\dfrac{2x}{x + 1} \geq -1$, to be imposed *together* in a **system**. At $x = -1$ the expression $\dfrac{2x}{x+1}$ is not defined, so the point $-1$ remains **excluded** from the solutions of the two rational inequalities and the condition on the denominator is already included in the system.

Each inequality is brought into **rational form** with right-hand side $0$, *without* multiplying by the denominator, because the sign of $x + 1$ changes along the real line. The ratio $\dfrac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ is less than or equal to $0$ when numerator and denominator have opposite signs, so for $-1 < x \leq 1$. The ratio $\dfrac{3x + 1}{x + 1}$ is greater than or equal to $0$ when the signs agree, so for $x < -1$ or $x \geq -\dfrac{1}{3}$. The **intersection** of the two solution sets is the interval $\left[-\dfrac{1}{3}, 1\right]$, which is the required domain. At the endpoints the argument equals $-1$ and $1$, values accepted by the arcsine, therefore the endpoints **belong to the domain**.

</div>
