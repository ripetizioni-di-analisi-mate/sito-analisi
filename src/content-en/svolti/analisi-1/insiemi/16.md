## Exercise 1

Determine the range of
$$
f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 5 .
$$

$$
x^2 - 4x + 5 = (x - 2)^2 + 1
$$

$$
f(A) = [1, +\infty)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Completing the square** rewrites the trinomial: $x^2 - 4x + 5 = (x^2 - 4x + 4) + 1 = (x - 2)^2 + 1$. The square $(x - 2)^2$ takes the value $0$ at $x = 2$ and takes every value $t \geq 0$, because the equation $(x - 2)^2 = t$ has the solution $x = 2 + \sqrt{t}$. Hence $f$ takes *all and only* the values of the form $t + 1$ with $t \geq 0$, that is the range is $[1, +\infty)$: the function is **bounded below**, with minimum $1$, and **unbounded above**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the range of
$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2 + 1} .
$$

$$
y = \frac{1}{x^2 + 1}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
y \left( x^2 + 1 \right) = 1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x^2 = \frac{1 - y}{y} \qquad (y \neq 0)
$$

$$
\frac{1 - y}{y} \geq 0
\;\iff\;
0 < y \leq 1
$$

$$
f(A) = (0, 1]
$$

::grafico{f="1/(x^2+1)" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-0.5 ymax=1.5 punti="0,1,pieno" titolo="$y = \dfrac{1}{x^2 + 1}$ takes the maximum value $1$ at $x = 0$ and approaches $0$ without reaching it"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A number $y$ belongs to the range *if and only if* the equation $f(x) = y$ has at least one solution. The left-hand side is a ratio with numerator $1$, so it is different from $0$ for every $x$: the value $y = 0$ must be **excluded**. For $y \neq 0$, multiplying by $x^2 + 1$, which is positive, and dividing by $y$, the equation becomes $x^2 = \dfrac{1 - y}{y}$.

The equation has a solution if and only if the right-hand side is greater than or equal to $0$. The ratio $\dfrac{1 - y}{y}$ is greater than or equal to $0$ when numerator and denominator agree in sign, so for $0 < y \leq 1$. The range is $(0, 1]$: the **maximum** equals $1$ and is attained at $x = 0$, while the value $0$ is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the range of
$$
f(x) = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1} .
$$

$$
y = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
y x^2 - 2x + y = 0
$$

$$
y = 0: \quad x = 0
$$

$$
y \neq 0: \quad \frac{\Delta}{4} = 1 - y^2 \geq 0
\;\iff\;
-1 \leq y \leq 1
$$

$$
f(A) = [-1, 1]
$$

::grafico{f="2*x/(x^2+1)" xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-2 ymax=2 punti="1,1,pieno;-1,-1,pieno" segmenti="-6,1,6,1;-6,-1,6,-1" titolo="$y = \dfrac{2x}{x^2 + 1}$ takes all the values between $-1$ and $1$: the maximum $1$ at $x = 1$, the minimum $-1$ at $x = -1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A number $y$ belongs to the range *if and only if* the equation $f(x) = y$ has at least one real solution. Multiplying by $x^2 + 1$, which is positive for every $x$, the equation becomes $y x^2 - 2x + y = 0$.

For $y = 0$ the equation reduces to $-2x = 0$, with solution $x = 0$, so $0$ **belongs to the range**. For $y \neq 0$ the equation is quadratic in $x$ and has real solutions if and only if the **discriminant** is greater than or equal to $0$: with the reduced formula, $\dfrac{\Delta}{4} = 1 - y^2$, so the condition is $y^2 \leq 1$, that is $-1 \leq y \leq 1$.

The range is $[-1, 1]$ and the function is **bounded**. The endpoints are **attained**: for $y = 1$ the equation becomes $x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2 = 0$, so $f(1) = 1$; for $y = -1$ it becomes $x^2 + 2x + 1 = (x + 1)^2 = 0$, so $f(-1) = -1$.

</div>
