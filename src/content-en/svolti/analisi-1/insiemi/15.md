## Exercise 1

Solve
$$
2 \sin x - 1 = 0 .
$$

$$
\sin x = \frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
x = \frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x = \pi - \frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi = \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 segmenti="-1.5,0.5,1.5,0.5" vettori="0,0,0.866,0.5;0,0,-0.866,0.5" punti="0.866,0.5,pieno;-0.866,0.5,pieno" titolo="The line $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ (dashed) meets the unit circle at the points with angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is brought into the **elementary form** $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$. The value $\dfrac{1}{2}$ belongs to $[-1, 1]$, so the equation **has solutions**; from the notable values, $\arcsin \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. On the unit circle the points with ordinate $\dfrac{1}{2}$ are *two* in each turn, with angles $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$; since the sine is **periodic** with period $2\pi$, $2k\pi$ with $k$ an integer is added to each angle.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve
$$
2 \cos^2 x + \cos x - 1 = 0 .
$$

$$
t = \cos x \in [-1, 1],
\qquad
2t^2 + t - 1 = 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
t = \frac{-1 \pm 3}{4}
$$

$$
\cos x = \frac{1}{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x = \pm\frac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi
$$

$$
\cos x = -1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x = \pi + 2k\pi,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.3 uguale=1 segmenti="0.5,-1.2,0.5,1.2" vettori="0,0,0.5,0.866;0,0,0.5,-0.866;0,0,-1,0" punti="0.5,0.866,pieno;0.5,-0.866,pieno;-1,0,pieno" titolo="On the unit circle the points with abscissa $\dfrac{1}{2}$ have angles $\pm\dfrac{\pi}{3}$; the point with abscissa $-1$ has angle $\pi$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is quadratic in the single unknown $\cos x$, so the **substitution** $t = \cos x$ turns it into $2t^2 + t - 1 = 0$. The quadratic formula with discriminant $1 + 8 = 9$ gives $t = \dfrac{-1 \pm 3}{4}$, that is $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $t = -1$; *both* values belong to $[-1, 1]$, so both elementary equations have solutions.

From $\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, since $\arccos \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and the points of the circle with abscissa $\dfrac{1}{2}$ have **opposite** angles, the solutions $x = \pm\dfrac{\pi}{3} + 2k\pi$ follow. From $\cos x = -1$ it follows that $x = \pi + 2k\pi$, because the point with abscissa $-1$ is **unique** in each turn. The solution set is the **union** of the three families.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve
$$
\sin 2x = \cos x .
$$

$$
2 \sin x \cos x - \cos x = 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\cos x \left( 2 \sin x - 1 \right) = 0
$$

$$
\cos x = 0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi
$$

$$
\sin x = \frac{1}{2}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x = \frac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi
\quad \text{or} \quad
x = \frac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi,
\qquad k \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **double-angle formula** $\sin 2x = 2 \sin x \cos x$ rewrites both sides with the same angle $x$. Moving everything to the left and factoring out the **common factor** $\cos x$, the equation becomes a product equal to $0$, and a product is zero *if and only if* at least one factor is zero; factoring out therefore keeps all the solutions, including those with $\cos x = 0$.

From $\cos x = 0$ the solutions $x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$ follow, because the points of the circle with abscissa $0$ are $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, which are $\pi$ apart. From $2 \sin x - 1 = 0$, that is $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, the two families $x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ and $x = \dfrac{5\pi}{6} + 2k\pi$ follow, as in Exercise 1. The solution set is the **union** of the three families.

</div>
