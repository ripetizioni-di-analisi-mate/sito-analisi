## Exercise 1

Determine for which $x \in \mathbb{R}$ the series
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(x-1)^n}{2^n}
$$
converges and compute its sum.

$$
\frac{(x-1)^n}{2^n} = \left( \frac{x-1}{2} \right)^{\! n},
\qquad q = \frac{x-1}{2}
$$
$$
|q| < 1 \iff |x - 1| < 2 \iff -1 < x < 3
$$
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( \frac{x-1}{2} \right)^{\! n}
= \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{x-1}{2}}
= \frac{2}{3 - x}
\qquad (-1 < x < 3)
$$

::grafico{f="2/(3-x)+0*sqrt((x+1)*(3-x));1+(x-1)/2+((x-1)/2)^2+((x-1)/2)^3" asintoti="x=3" punti="-1,0.5,vuoto" xmin=-2.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1 ymax=6 titolo="On the set of convergence $-1 < x < 3$ the sum of the series is $\dfrac{2}{3 - x}$ (in color); dashed, the partial sum up to $n = 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The series is **geometric** with ratio $q = \dfrac{x-1}{2}$, which depends on the parameter. The convergence condition $|q| < 1$ becomes $|x - 1| < 2$, that is, $-1 < x < 3$. On this interval the sum is $\dfrac{1}{1 - q}$, which after computation equals $\dfrac{2}{3-x}$. Outside the interval the series does *not* converge: for $x \geq 3$ the ratio is greater than or equal to $1$ and the series **diverges** to $+\infty$; for $x \leq -1$ the ratio is less than or equal to $-1$ and the series is **indeterminate**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following series converges:
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^{\alpha} \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right) .
$$

$$
1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \sim \frac{1}{2n^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
n^{\alpha} \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right) \sim \frac{1}{2\, n^{2 - \alpha}}
$$
$$
2 - \alpha > 1 \iff \alpha < 1
$$
The series **converges** for $\alpha < 1$ and **diverges** to $+\infty$ for $\alpha \geq 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The general term is nonnegative for *every* $\alpha$, because the cosine does not exceed $1$, therefore **limit comparison** applies. Since $\dfrac{1}{n} \to 0$, the **standard limit of the cosine** gives $1 - \cos \dfrac{1}{n} \sim \dfrac{1}{2n^2}$, hence the general term is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2}\, n^{\alpha - 2} = \dfrac{1}{2\, n^{2-\alpha}}$. The series therefore has the same behavior as the generalized harmonic series with exponent $2 - \alpha$, which converges if and only if $2 - \alpha > 1$. The condition is equivalent to $\alpha < 1$; for $\alpha \geq 1$ the series, which has nonnegative terms, **diverges** to $+\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Study, as $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the absolute convergence and the convergence of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^{\alpha}} .
$$

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left| \frac{(-1)^n}{n^{\alpha}} \right|
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}
\quad \text{converges} \iff \alpha > 1
$$
$$
0 < \alpha \leq 1 : \quad
b_n = \frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}
\ \text{positive, decreasing, infinitesimal}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{converges (Leibniz)}
$$
$$
\alpha \leq 0 : \quad
\left| \frac{(-1)^n}{n^{\alpha}} \right| = n^{-\alpha} \geq 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{does not converge}
$$

- $\alpha > 1$: **converges absolutely**.
- $0 < \alpha \leq 1$: **converges conditionally**.
- $\alpha \leq 0$: **does not converge**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The series of absolute values is the generalized harmonic series, which converges *if and only if* $\alpha > 1$: in this case the series **converges absolutely**, therefore it converges.

For $0 < \alpha \leq 1$ there is *no* absolute convergence and the **Leibniz test** is applied to $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n^{\alpha}}$: the sequence is positive, it is decreasing because the power $n^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha > 0$ is increasing and lies in the denominator, and it is infinitesimal. The three hypotheses are satisfied, therefore the series converges, and the convergence is **conditional**.

For $\alpha \leq 0$ the absolute value of the general term equals $n^{-\alpha}$ with $-\alpha \geq 0$, therefore it is greater than or equal to $1$ for every $n$ and does not tend to $0$. The **necessary condition** for convergence is not satisfied, hence the series **does not converge**.

</div>
