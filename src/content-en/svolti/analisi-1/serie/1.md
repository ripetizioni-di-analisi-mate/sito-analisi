## Exercise 1

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^{\! n} .
$$

$$
q = \frac{1}{3}, \qquad |q| < 1
$$
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{3} \right)^{\! n}
= \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{1}{3}}
= \frac{1}{\dfrac{2}{3}}
= \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The series is **geometric** with ratio $q = \dfrac{1}{3}$. Since $|q| < 1$, the series **converges** and the sum is the *first term* divided by $1 - q$. The first term is the one with index $n = 0$, that is, $1$, therefore the sum equals $\dfrac{1}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^n}{5^{n+1}} .
$$

$$
\frac{2^n}{5^{n+1}} = \frac{1}{5} \left( \frac{2}{5} \right)^{\! n}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^n}{5^{n+1}}
&= \frac{1}{5} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{2}{5} \right)^{\! n} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{\dfrac{2}{5}}{1 - \dfrac{2}{5}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The general term is *not* yet in the form $q^n$, therefore it is rewritten: $5^{n+1} = 5 \cdot 5^n$, hence $\dfrac{2^n}{5^{n+1}} = \dfrac{1}{5} \left( \dfrac{2}{5} \right)^{\! n}$. The constant $\dfrac{1}{5}$ comes out of the sum by **linearity**. What remains is a **geometric series** with ratio $q = \dfrac{2}{5}$, with $|q| < 1$, starting from $n = 1$: the sum is the first term $\dfrac{2}{5}$ divided by $1 - q = \dfrac{3}{5}$, that is, $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Multiplying by the constant $\dfrac{1}{5}$ gives $\dfrac{2}{15}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n\, 3^n}{4^n} .
$$

$$
\frac{(-1)^n\, 3^n}{4^n} = \left( -\frac{3}{4} \right)^{\! n},
\qquad q = -\frac{3}{4}, \qquad |q| < 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \left( -\frac{3}{4} \right)^{\! n}
&= \frac{\left( -\dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{\! 2}}{1 - \left( -\dfrac{3}{4} \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\dfrac{9}{16}}{\dfrac{7}{4}}
= \frac{9}{16} \cdot \frac{4}{7} = \frac{9}{28}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factors $(-1)^n$, $3^n$ and $4^n$ have the same exponent, therefore the general term can be gathered into a **single power**: the ratio is $q = -\dfrac{3}{4}$. Since $|q| = \dfrac{3}{4} < 1$, the series **converges** *even* with a negative ratio. The index starts from $n = 2$, hence the first term is $q^2 = \dfrac{9}{16}$ and the sum is the first term divided by $1 - q = 1 + \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{7}{4}$. The result is $\dfrac{9}{28}$.

</div>
