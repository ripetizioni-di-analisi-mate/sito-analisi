## Exercise 1

Compute the sum of Mengoli's series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(n+1)} .
$$

$$
\frac{1}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_N &= \sum_{n=1}^{N} \left( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1} \right) \\[6pt]
&= 1 - \frac{1}{N+1} \xrightarrow[N \to +\infty]{} 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(n+1)} = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The general term is decomposed into **partial fractions**: looking for $\dfrac{1}{n(n+1)} = \dfrac{A}{n} + \dfrac{B}{n+1}$ one finds $A = 1$ and $B = -1$. The series is therefore **telescoping** with $b_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$: in the partial sum each term $-\dfrac{1}{n+1}$ cancels with the term $+\dfrac{1}{n+1}$ of the next summand, and *the first and the last* remain. Since $\dfrac{1}{N+1} \to 0$, the partial sums tend to $1$, therefore the series **converges** with sum $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(n+2)} .
$$

$$
\frac{1}{n(n+2)} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+2} \right)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_N &= \frac{1}{2} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \left( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+2} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{2} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{N+1} - \frac{1}{N+2} \right) \\[6pt]
&\xrightarrow[N \to +\infty]{} \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n(n+2)} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The partial fraction decomposition gives $A = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $B = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $\dfrac{1}{2}$ is factored out. Here the cancellation happens **at distance two**: the term $-\dfrac{1}{n+2}$ cancels with the $+\dfrac{1}{n+2}$ that appears two summands later. Hence the *first two* positive terms, $1$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$, survive, together with the last two negative ones, $-\dfrac{1}{N+1}$ and $-\dfrac{1}{N+2}$. The latter tend to $0$, therefore the partial sums tend to $\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \ln\!\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n^2} \right) .
$$

$$
\ln\!\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n^2} \right)
= \ln \frac{(n-1)(n+1)}{n^2}
= \ln \frac{n-1}{n} - \ln \frac{n}{n+1}
$$
$$
b_n = \ln \frac{n-1}{n}, \qquad
\ln\!\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n^2} \right) = b_n - b_{n+1}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_N &= b_2 - b_{N+1}
= \ln \frac{1}{2} - \ln \frac{N}{N+1} \\[6pt]
&\xrightarrow[N \to +\infty]{} \ln \frac{1}{2} - \ln 1 = -\ln 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \ln\!\left( 1 - \frac{1}{n^2} \right) = -\ln 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $n \geq 2$ the argument of the logarithm is positive, therefore the general term is defined. One factors $1 - \dfrac{1}{n^2} = \dfrac{n^2 - 1}{n^2} = \dfrac{(n-1)(n+1)}{n^2}$ and splits the logarithm of the product into the sum of the logarithms. Setting $b_n = \ln \dfrac{n-1}{n}$ one gets $b_{n+1} = \ln \dfrac{n}{n+1}$, therefore the general term is *exactly* $b_n - b_{n+1}$ and the series is **telescoping**. The partial sum equals $b_2 - b_{N+1}$; since $\dfrac{N}{N+1} \to 1$ and the logarithm is continuous, $b_{N+1} \to \ln 1 = 0$, hence the series **converges** with sum $\ln \dfrac{1}{2} = -\ln 2$.

</div>
