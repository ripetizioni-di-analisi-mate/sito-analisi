## Exercise 1

Study the monotonicity of the sequence
$$
a_n = \frac{2n + 1}{n + 3}, \qquad n \geq 1,
$$
and determine its limit, supremum and infimum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n+1} - a_n
&= \frac{2n + 3}{n + 4} - \frac{2n + 1}{n + 3} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(2n + 3)(n + 3) - (2n + 1)(n + 4)}{(n + 3)(n + 4)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\left( 2n^2 + 9n + 9 \right) - \left( 2n^2 + 9n + 4 \right)}{(n + 3)(n + 4)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{5}{(n + 3)(n + 4)} > 0
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{2n + 1}{n + 3} = 2, \qquad
\sup a_n = 2, \qquad
\inf a_n = \min a_n = a_1 = \frac{3}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference between two consecutive terms is positive for every $n$, therefore the sequence is **strictly increasing**. The limit equals $2$, which is the ratio of the leading coefficients. By the monotone sequence theorem the limit of an increasing sequence coincides with the supremum of its terms, hence $\sup a_n = 2$. The value $2$ is not attained by any term, because $\dfrac{2n + 1}{n + 3} = 2$ is equivalent to $1 = 6$; therefore the sequence has *no* maximum. Since it is increasing, the smallest term is the first one, and the infimum is the minimum $a_1 = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Prove that the sequence defined by
$$
a_1 = 1, \qquad a_{n+1} = \frac{a_n + 6}{3}
$$
is convergent and compute its limit.

$$
a_n < 3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_{n+1} = \frac{a_n + 6}{3} < \frac{3 + 6}{3} = 3
$$

$$
a_{n+1} - a_n = \frac{a_n + 6}{3} - a_n = \frac{6 - 2a_n}{3} > 0 \qquad \text{because } a_n < 3
$$

$$
L = \frac{L + 6}{3}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 3L = L + 6
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The general term is not given by an explicit formula, therefore before computing the limit we prove that it exists. By **induction** we prove that $a_n < 3$ for every $n$: the property holds for $a_1 = 1$ and, if it holds for $a_n$, the recurrence relation gives $a_{n+1} < 3$. The difference $a_{n+1} - a_n$ equals $\dfrac{6 - 2a_n}{3}$, which is positive because $a_n < 3$; the sequence is therefore increasing. An increasing sequence that is bounded above is **convergent**, by the monotone sequence theorem. Let $L$ be the limit. The sequence $a_{n+1}$ also tends to $L$, therefore passing to the limit on both sides of the recurrence gives the equation $L = \dfrac{L + 6}{3}$, whose solution is $L = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Prove that the sequence defined by
$$
a_1 = 2, \qquad a_{n+1} = \frac{1}{2} \left( a_n + \frac{2}{a_n} \right)
$$
is convergent and compute its limit.

$$
a_{n+1}^2 - 2
= \frac{1}{4} \left( a_n + \frac{2}{a_n} \right)^{2} - 2
= \frac{1}{4} \left( a_n^2 - 4 + \frac{4}{a_n^2} \right)
= \frac{1}{4} \left( a_n - \frac{2}{a_n} \right)^{2} \geq 0
$$

$$
a_{n+1} - a_n = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{2}{a_n} - a_n \right) = \frac{2 - a_n^2}{2a_n} \leq 0
\qquad \text{because } a_n^2 \geq 2
$$

$$
L = \frac{1}{2} \left( L + \frac{2}{L} \right)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2L^2 = L^2 + 2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L^2 = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L = \sqrt{2}
$$

$$
a_1 = 2, \qquad a_2 = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5, \qquad a_3 = \frac{17}{12} \approx 1.4167, \qquad a_4 = \frac{577}{408} \approx 1.41422
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the terms are positive, because $a_1 > 0$ and the recurrence adds positive quantities. The first computation shows that $a_{n+1}^2 - 2$ is a square, therefore $a_{n+1}^2 \geq 2$ for every $n$; also $a_1^2 = 4 \geq 2$, hence $a_n \geq \sqrt{2}$ for every $n$ and the sequence is **bounded below**. The difference $a_{n+1} - a_n$ equals $\dfrac{2 - a_n^2}{2a_n}$, which is less than or equal to $0$ because $a_n^2 \geq 2$; the sequence is therefore decreasing. By the monotone sequence theorem the limit $L$ exists, and $L \geq \sqrt{2} > 0$. Passing to the limit in the recurrence, which is legitimate because $L \neq 0$, gives $L^2 = 2$; since $L > 0$, the limit is $\sqrt{2}$. The first terms show that the sequence provides increasingly precise rational approximations of $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41421$: this procedure is used to compute the decimal digits of square roots.

</div>
