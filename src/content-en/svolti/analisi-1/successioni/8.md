## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} n^2 \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right) .
$$

$$
n^2 \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right)
= \frac{1 - \cos \dfrac{1}{n}}{\dfrac{1}{n^2}}
= \frac{1 - \cos \varepsilon_n}{\varepsilon_n^2}
\qquad \text{with } \varepsilon_n = \frac{1}{n} \to 0
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} n^2 \left( 1 - \cos \frac{1}{n} \right) = \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $n^2$ diverges and the factor $1 - \cos \dfrac{1}{n}$ tends to $1 - \cos 0 = 0$, therefore the product is in the **indeterminate form** $\infty \cdot 0$. We move $n^2$ to the denominator as $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$ and set $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{1}{n}$, which is an infinitesimal sequence with terms different from $0$. The expression becomes $\dfrac{1 - \cos \varepsilon_n}{\varepsilon_n^2}$. From the standard limit $\dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} \to \dfrac{1}{2}$ as $x \to 0$ and from the **bridge theorem** it follows that the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} n \ln \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n} \right) .
$$

$$
n \ln \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n} \right)
= 3 \cdot \frac{\ln \left( 1 + \dfrac{3}{n} \right)}{\dfrac{3}{n}}
= 3 \cdot \frac{\ln(1 + \varepsilon_n)}{\varepsilon_n}
\qquad \text{with } \varepsilon_n = \frac{3}{n} \to 0
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} n \ln \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n} \right) = 3 \cdot 1 = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $n$ diverges and the logarithm tends to $\ln 1 = 0$, therefore the form is $\infty \cdot 0$. The argument of the logarithm is $1 + \varepsilon_n$ with $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{3}{n}$ infinitesimal. To recognize the standard limit we need $\varepsilon_n$ in the denominator: since $n = \dfrac{3}{\varepsilon_n}$, we multiply and divide by $3$. The ratio $\dfrac{\ln(1 + \varepsilon_n)}{\varepsilon_n}$ tends to $1$ by the standard limit of the logarithm and by the **bridge theorem**, hence the limit equals $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} n \left( \sqrt[n]{2} - 1 \right) .
$$

$$
\sqrt[n]{2} = 2^{\frac{1}{n}} = e^{\frac{\ln 2}{n}}
$$

$$
n \left( \sqrt[n]{2} - 1 \right)
= \ln 2 \cdot \frac{e^{\frac{\ln 2}{n}} - 1}{\dfrac{\ln 2}{n}}
= \ln 2 \cdot \frac{e^{\varepsilon_n} - 1}{\varepsilon_n}
\qquad \text{with } \varepsilon_n = \frac{\ln 2}{n} \to 0
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} n \left( \sqrt[n]{2} - 1 \right) = \ln 2 \cdot 1 = \ln 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the standard limit $\sqrt[n]{2} \to 1$ the second factor tends to $0$, while $n$ diverges: the form is $\infty \cdot 0$. We write the root as a power with base $e$, using the identity $2 = e^{\ln 2}$, and obtain $e^{\varepsilon_n}$ with $\varepsilon_n = \dfrac{\ln 2}{n}$ infinitesimal. Since $n = \dfrac{\ln 2}{\varepsilon_n}$, the expression becomes $\ln 2 \cdot \dfrac{e^{\varepsilon_n} - 1}{\varepsilon_n}$. The ratio tends to $1$ by the standard limit of the exponential and by the **bridge theorem**, therefore the limit equals $\ln 2$. The result refines the limit $\sqrt[n]{2} \to 1$: the difference $\sqrt[n]{2} - 1$ is an infinitesimal of the same order as $\dfrac{1}{n}$.

</div>
