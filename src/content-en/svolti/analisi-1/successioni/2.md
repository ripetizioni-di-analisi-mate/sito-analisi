## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{2n^3 - n + 4}{5n^3 + n^2} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2n^3 - n + 4}{5n^3 + n^2}
&= \frac{n^3 \left( 2 - \dfrac{1}{n^2} + \dfrac{4}{n^3} \right)}{n^3 \left( 5 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2 - \dfrac{1}{n^2} + \dfrac{4}{n^3}}{5 + \dfrac{1}{n}}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{2n^3 - n + 4}{5n^3 + n^2} = \frac{2 - 0 + 0}{5 + 0} = \frac{2}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator and the denominator diverge, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. We factor out $n^3$, which is the highest power both in the numerator and in the denominator, and simplify. The terms $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$, $\dfrac{4}{n^3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{n}$ are infinitesimal, hence the numerator tends to $2$ and the denominator to $5$. By the theorem on the limit of a quotient the limit equals $\dfrac{2}{5}$, that is, the ratio of the leading coefficients.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{\sqrt{4n^2 + n}}{3n + 1} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{4n^2 + n}}{3n + 1}
&= \frac{\sqrt{n^2 \left( 4 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)}}{n \left( 3 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{n \sqrt{4 + \dfrac{1}{n}}}{n \left( 3 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)}
= \frac{\sqrt{4 + \dfrac{1}{n}}}{3 + \dfrac{1}{n}}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{\sqrt{4n^2 + n}}{3n + 1} = \frac{\sqrt{4}}{3} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio is in the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. Under the root we factor out $n^2$; since $n > 0$, we have $\sqrt{n^2} = n$ and the factor $n$ comes out of the root. In the denominator we factor out $n$ and the two factors $n$ cancel. As $n \to +\infty$ the expression under the root tends to $4$, therefore the root tends to $2$, while the denominator tends to $3$. The limit equals $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{n^2 + 3n} - \sqrt{n^2 + 1} \right) .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{n^2 + 3n} - \sqrt{n^2 + 1}
&= \frac{\left( \sqrt{n^2 + 3n} - \sqrt{n^2 + 1} \right)\left( \sqrt{n^2 + 3n} + \sqrt{n^2 + 1} \right)}{\sqrt{n^2 + 3n} + \sqrt{n^2 + 1}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(n^2 + 3n) - (n^2 + 1)}{\sqrt{n^2 + 3n} + \sqrt{n^2 + 1}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{3n - 1}{\sqrt{n^2 + 3n} + \sqrt{n^2 + 1}} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{n \left( 3 - \dfrac{1}{n} \right)}{n \left( \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{3}{n}} + \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{n^2}} \right)}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \sqrt{n^2 + 3n} - \sqrt{n^2 + 1} \right) = \frac{3 - 0}{1 + 1} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two roots diverge to $+\infty$, therefore the difference is in the **indeterminate form** $+\infty - \infty$. We multiply and divide by the sum of the two roots: the numerator becomes the product $(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$, which eliminates the roots and gives $3n - 1$. The new ratio is in the form $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$, hence we factor out $n$ in the numerator and in each root of the denominator. After simplification the numerator tends to $3$ and the denominator to $1 + 1 = 2$, therefore the limit equals $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>
