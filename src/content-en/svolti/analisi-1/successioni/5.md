## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^3 + 2^n}{3^n + n^{10}} .
$$

$$
\frac{n^3 + 2^n}{3^n + n^{10}}
= \frac{2^n \left( \dfrac{n^3}{2^n} + 1 \right)}{3^n \left( 1 + \dfrac{n^{10}}{3^n} \right)}
= \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^{n} \cdot \frac{\dfrac{n^3}{2^n} + 1}{1 + \dfrac{n^{10}}{3^n}}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^3 + 2^n}{3^n + n^{10}} = 0 \cdot \frac{0 + 1}{1 + 0} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator and the denominator diverge, therefore the form is $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$. By the **hierarchy of infinities** an exponential with base greater than $1$ is an infinity of higher order than every power of $n$: we factor out $2^n$ in the numerator and $3^n$ in the denominator. The ratios $\dfrac{n^3}{2^n}$ and $\dfrac{n^{10}}{3^n}$ are infinitesimal, hence the second fraction tends to $1$. The factor $\left( \dfrac{2}{3} \right)^{n}$ is a geometric sequence with common ratio $\dfrac{2}{3}$, which lies between $-1$ and $1$, therefore it tends to $0$. The limit equals $0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
&= \frac{(2n + 2)!}{\left( (n + 1)! \right)^2} \cdot \frac{(n!)^2}{(2n)!} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(2n + 2)(2n + 1)}{(n + 1)^2} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2(2n + 1)}{n + 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = 4 > 1
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2} = +\infty
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The terms are positive and contain factorials, therefore we apply the **ratio test**. In the ratio $\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}$ we use the equalities $(2n + 2)! = (2n + 2)(2n + 1)(2n)!$ and $(n + 1)! = (n + 1)\, n!$: the factorials cancel and $\dfrac{(2n + 2)(2n + 1)}{(n + 1)^2}$ remains. Since $2n + 2 = 2(n + 1)$, one factor $n + 1$ cancels. The ratio tends to $4$, which is greater than $1$, hence by the test the sequence **diverges** to $+\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^n}{3^n\, n!} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
&= \frac{(n + 1)^{n+1}}{3^{n+1}\,(n + 1)!} \cdot \frac{3^n\, n!}{n^n} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{(n + 1)^{n+1}}{3\,(n + 1)\, n^n} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{3} \left( \frac{n + 1}{n} \right)^{n}
= \frac{1}{3} \left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{e}{3} < 1
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^n}{3^n\, n!} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator $n^n$ is an infinity of higher order than both $3^n$ and $n!$, but the denominator contains their product, and the hierarchy of infinities does not establish which of the two terms prevails. We apply the **ratio test**. Simplifying $3^{n+1}$ with $3^n$ and $(n + 1)!$ with $n!$ leaves $\dfrac{(n + 1)^{n+1}}{3(n + 1) n^n}$; one factor $n + 1$ cancels and we obtain $\dfrac{1}{3}\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{n}$. By the definition of the number $e$ the ratio tends to $\dfrac{e}{3}$. Since $e < 3$, the limit of the ratio is less than $1$ and the sequence tends to $0$.

</div>
