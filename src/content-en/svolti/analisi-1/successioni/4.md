## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2n} \right)^{n} .
$$

$$
\left( 1 - \frac{1}{2n} \right)^{n} = \left( 1 + \frac{x}{n} \right)^{n} \qquad \text{with } x = -\frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{2n} \right)^{n} = e^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, therefore we have the **indeterminate form** $1^{\infty}$. The term $-\dfrac{1}{2n}$ is written as $\dfrac{x}{n}$ with $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the exponent is exactly $n$. We therefore apply the limit $\left( 1 + \dfrac{x}{n} \right)^{n} \to e^{x}$, which gives $e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \frac{n + 1}{n - 2} \right)^{n} .
$$

$$
\frac{n + 1}{n - 2} = \frac{(n - 2) + 3}{n - 2} = 1 + \frac{3}{n - 2}
$$

$$
\left( \frac{n + 1}{n - 2} \right)^{n}
= \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n - 2} \right)^{n - 2} \cdot \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n - 2} \right)^{2}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n - 2} \right)^{n - 2} = e^{3}, \qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( 1 + \frac{3}{n - 2} \right)^{2} = 1
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \frac{n + 1}{n - 2} \right)^{n} = e^{3} \cdot 1 = e^{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base tends to $1$ and the exponent to $+\infty$, therefore the form is $1^{\infty}$. We write the base as $1$ plus an infinitesimal term: adding and subtracting $2$ in the numerator gives $1 + \dfrac{3}{n - 2}$. The denominator of the infinitesimal term is $n - 2$, while the exponent is $n$; to recognize the standard limit we write $n = (n - 2) + 2$ and split the power into two factors. In the first factor, setting $m = n - 2$, we find $\left( 1 + \dfrac{3}{m} \right)^{m}$ with $m \to +\infty$, which tends to $e^{3}$. The second factor has the fixed exponent $2$ and a base that tends to $1$, therefore it tends to $1$. By the theorem on the limit of a product the limit equals $e^{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \frac{n^2 + 1}{n^2 - 1} \right)^{n^2} .
$$

$$
\frac{n^2 + 1}{n^2 - 1} = 1 + \frac{2}{n^2 - 1} = 1 + \frac{1}{a_n}
\qquad \text{with } a_n = \frac{n^2 - 1}{2} \to +\infty
$$

$$
\left( \frac{n^2 + 1}{n^2 - 1} \right)^{n^2}
= \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{1}{a_n} \right)^{a_n} \right]^{\frac{n^2}{a_n}},
\qquad
\frac{n^2}{a_n} = \frac{2n^2}{n^2 - 1} \to 2
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \frac{n^2 + 1}{n^2 - 1} \right)^{n^2} = e^{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The form is $1^{\infty}$. We write the base as $1 + \dfrac{1}{a_n}$, where $a_n = \dfrac{n^2 - 1}{2}$ diverges to $+\infty$. To make the power $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{a_n} \right)^{a_n}$ appear, we multiply and divide the exponent $n^2$ by $a_n$. The power $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{a_n} \right)^{a_n}$ tends to $e$, because this limit holds for every divergent sequence $(a_n)$. The outer exponent $\dfrac{n^2}{a_n}$ tends to $2$. A power $b_n^{\,c_n}$ with $b_n \to b > 0$ and $c_n \to c$ tends to $b^{c}$, because $b_n^{\,c_n} = e^{c_n \ln b_n}$ and the exponential and logarithm functions are continuous. Hence the limit equals $e^{2}$.

</div>
