## Exercise 1

Verify from the definition that
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{3n + 2}{n + 1} = 3 .
$$

$$
\left| \frac{3n + 2}{n + 1} - 3 \right|
= \left| \frac{3n + 2 - 3n - 3}{n + 1} \right|
= \frac{1}{n + 1}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{n + 1} < \varepsilon
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n + 1 > \frac{1}{\varepsilon}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n > \frac{1}{\varepsilon} - 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We fix $\varepsilon > 0$ and study the inequality $|a_n - 3| < \varepsilon$. Bringing the terms to a common denominator, the numerator becomes $-1$, therefore the distance of $a_n$ from $3$ equals $\dfrac{1}{n + 1}$. The inequality $\dfrac{1}{n + 1} < \varepsilon$ is equivalent to $n > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon} - 1$, because both sides are positive and we can take reciprocals by reversing the inequality. Hence, having chosen a natural number $N \geq \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon} - 1$, the inequality holds for *every* $n > N$. Since $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, the definition is **satisfied**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify from the definition that
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}} = 0 .
$$

$$
\left| \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}} - 0 \right| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} < \varepsilon
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sqrt{n} > \frac{1}{\varepsilon}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n > \frac{1}{\varepsilon^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $(-1)^n$ has absolute value $1$, therefore the distance of $a_n$ from $0$ equals $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ and the alternating sign plays no role. The inequality $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} < \varepsilon$ is equivalent to $\sqrt{n} > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}$; squaring both sides, which are positive, gives $n > \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2}$. Having chosen $N \geq \dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2}$, the inequality holds for every $n > N$, therefore the sequence is **infinitesimal**. With $\varepsilon = \dfrac{1}{10}$ we can take $N = 100$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Verify from the definition that
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( n^2 - 4n \right) = +\infty .
$$

$$
n^2 - 4n = n(n - 4) \geq n \cdot \frac{n}{2} = \frac{n^2}{2} \qquad \text{for } n \geq 8
$$

$$
\frac{n^2}{2} > M
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n > \sqrt{2M}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We fix $M > 0$ and look for an index beyond which $n^2 - 4n > M$. Solving the quadratic inequality exactly is not necessary: a simpler **lower bound** is enough. For $n \geq 8$ we have $n - 4 \geq \dfrac{n}{2}$, therefore $n^2 - 4n \geq \dfrac{n^2}{2}$. The inequality $\dfrac{n^2}{2} > M$ holds for $n > \sqrt{2M}$. Having chosen a natural number $N$ greater than or equal to both $8$ and $\sqrt{2M}$, for every $n > N$ both conditions hold and therefore $n^2 - 4n \geq \dfrac{n^2}{2} > M$. The definition of the limit $+\infty$ is **satisfied**. The threshold $N$ found is not the smallest possible one, and the definition does not require it.

</div>
