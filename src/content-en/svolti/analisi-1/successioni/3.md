## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n + \cos n}{2n - 1} .
$$

$$
\frac{n + \cos n}{2n - 1}
= \frac{n \left( 1 + \dfrac{\cos n}{n} \right)}{n \left( 2 - \dfrac{1}{n} \right)}
= \frac{1 + \dfrac{\cos n}{n}}{2 - \dfrac{1}{n}}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{\cos n}{n} = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n + \cos n}{2n - 1} = \frac{1 + 0}{2 - 0} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sequence $\cos n$ has no limit, therefore the limit of the numerator cannot be computed term by term. We factor out $n$ in the numerator and in the denominator. The term $\dfrac{\cos n}{n}$ is the product of $\cos n$, which is **bounded** because $|\cos n| \leq 1$, and $\dfrac{1}{n}$, which is infinitesimal; by the theorem on the product of a bounded sequence and an infinitesimal one it tends to $0$. The numerator therefore tends to $1$ and the denominator to $2$, and the limit equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \sqrt[n]{2^n + 3^n} .
$$

$$
3^n \leq 2^n + 3^n \leq 3^n + 3^n = 2 \cdot 3^n
$$

$$
3 \leq \sqrt[n]{2^n + 3^n} \leq 3 \sqrt[n]{2}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} 3 \sqrt[n]{2} = 3 \cdot 1 = 3
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \sqrt[n]{2^n + 3^n} = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Of the two terms under the root, the one that grows faster is $3^n$, therefore we build two estimates that contain it. The sum is greater than or equal to $3^n$, because $2^n > 0$, and it is less than or equal to $2 \cdot 3^n$, because $2^n \leq 3^n$. The $n$-th root is increasing on the positive numbers, therefore it preserves the inequalities and gives $3 \leq a_n \leq 3\sqrt[n]{2}$. By the standard limit $\sqrt[n]{2} \to 1$ the sequence on the right tends to $3$, and the one on the left is constantly equal to $3$. By the **squeeze theorem** the limit equals $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \left( \frac{n}{n^2 + 1} + \frac{n}{n^2 + 2} + \dots + \frac{n}{n^2 + n} \right) .
$$

$$
\frac{n}{n^2 + n} \leq \frac{n}{n^2 + k} \leq \frac{n}{n^2 + 1} \qquad \text{for } k = 1, 2, \dots, n
$$

$$
n \cdot \frac{n}{n^2 + n} \leq a_n \leq n \cdot \frac{n}{n^2 + 1}
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\frac{n^2}{n^2 + n} \leq a_n \leq \frac{n^2}{n^2 + 1}
$$

$$
\lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^2}{n^2 + n} = 1, \qquad \lim_{n \to +\infty} \frac{n^2}{n^2 + 1} = 1
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lim_{n \to +\infty} a_n = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sequence $a_n$ is a sum of $n$ terms, and the number of terms grows with $n$: the theorem on the limit of a sum, which concerns a *fixed* number of terms, cannot be applied. Each term tends to $0$, but it does not follow that the sum tends to $0$. We therefore estimate each term: the fraction $\dfrac{n}{n^2 + k}$ is smaller when the denominator is larger, therefore it lies between the last term and the first. Adding the $n$ inequalities shows that $a_n$ lies between $\dfrac{n^2}{n^2 + n}$ and $\dfrac{n^2}{n^2 + 1}$. Both these sequences tend to $1$, hence by the **squeeze theorem** $a_n \to 1$.

</div>
