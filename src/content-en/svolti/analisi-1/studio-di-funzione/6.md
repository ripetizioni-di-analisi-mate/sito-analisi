## Exercise 1

Study the function
$$
f(x) = x - 2\arctan x
$$
and sketch its qualitative graph.

**Domain.** The arctangent is defined everywhere, therefore $D = \mathbb{R}$.

**Symmetries and periodicity.** The arctangent is odd, therefore $f(-x) = -x - 2\arctan(-x) = -x + 2\arctan x = -f(x)$: the function is **odd** and the graph is symmetric with respect to the origin; it is not periodic.

**Sign and intersections.** $f(0) = 0$, therefore the graph passes through the origin. The equation $x = 2\arctan x$ *cannot* be solved algebraically; the other zeros and the sign are completed after the study of monotonicity, with the **intermediate zero theorem**.

**Limits and asymptotes.** Since $\arctan x \to \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ as $x \to \pm\infty$,
$$
\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \bigl( x - 2\arctan x \bigr) = \pm\infty .
$$
For the oblique asymptotes:
$$
m = \lim_{x \to \pm\infty} \frac{x - 2\arctan x}{x} = 1 - 0 = 1 ,
$$
$$
q = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \bigl( f(x) - x \bigr) = \lim_{x \to +\infty} \bigl( -2\arctan x \bigr) = -\pi ,
$$
and similarly $q = \pi$ as $x \to -\infty$. Therefore $y = x - \pi$ is an **oblique asymptote** as $x \to +\infty$ and $y = x + \pi$ as $x \to -\infty$; there are no vertical asymptotes.

**Continuity.** $f$ is a sum of continuous functions, therefore it is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Differentiability and monotonicity.** $f$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$ and
$$
f'(x) = 1 - \frac{2}{1 + x^2} = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2 + 1} .
$$
The denominator is positive, therefore $f'(x) > 0$ for $|x| > 1$. By the **monotonicity test** $f$ is increasing on $(-\infty, -1]$, decreasing on $[-1, 1]$, increasing on $[1, +\infty)$.

**Maxima and minima.** The stationary points are $\pm 1$. By the **first derivative test** $-1$ is a **relative maximum point** and $1$ a **relative minimum** point, with
$$
f(-1) = -1 + \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2} - 1, \qquad
f(1) = 1 - \frac{\pi}{2} ,
$$
opposite values, in agreement with oddness. The absolute maximum and minimum do *not* exist, because $f$ is unbounded in both directions.

**Completing the sign.** On the half-line $x > 0$ the function starts from $f(0) = 0$, decreases down to $f(1) = 1 - \dfrac{\pi}{2} < 0$ and then increases toward $+\infty$, therefore it vanishes at *only one* other point $x_0 > 1$. Since $\arctan 2 > \arctan\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, we have $f(2) < 2 - \dfrac{2\pi}{3} < 0$; since $\arctan 4 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, we have $f(4) > 4 - \pi > 0$. By the **intermediate zero theorem** $x_0 \in (2, 4)$. Hence $f(x) < 0$ for $0 < x < x_0$ and $f(x) > 0$ for $x > x_0$; by oddness, $f(x) > 0$ for $-x_0 < x < 0$ and $f(x) < 0$ for $x < -x_0$.

**Concavity and inflection points.** Differentiating the quotient,
$$
f''(x) = \frac{2x\,(x^2 + 1) - (x^2 - 1)\,2x}{(x^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{4x}{(x^2 + 1)^2} ,
$$
therefore $f''(x) > 0$ for $x > 0$. The function is **concave** on $(-\infty, 0]$ and **convex** on $[0, +\infty)$; at $0$ the second derivative changes sign, therefore the origin is an **inflection point**, with tangent $y = -x$ because $f'(0) = -1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The sign is the step outside the scheme. The equation $x = 2\arctan x$ has no algebraic solution, therefore it is convenient to postpone the sign until after the monotonicity: the shape of the graph on $(0, +\infty)$, first decreasing below zero and then increasing toward $+\infty$, guarantees that the positive zero is *unique*, and the **intermediate zero theorem** locates it between $2$ and $4$. The two estimates use only known values of the arctangent: from $2 > \sqrt{3}$ it follows that $\arctan 2 > \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, therefore $f(2) < 2 - \dfrac{2\pi}{3} < 0$ because $2\pi > 6$; from $\arctan 4 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ it follows that $f(4) > 4 - \pi > 0$ because $\pi < 4$.

The two oblique asymptotes are **parallel but distinct**, both with slope $1$ and intercepts $-\pi$ and $\pi$. The phenomenon is typical of functions built with the arctangent, because $\arctan x$ has different finite limits in the two directions; the computation of $q$ must therefore be carried out *separately* for $x \to +\infty$ and for $x \to -\infty$.

The tangent at the inflection point is the line $y = -x$, with negative slope, while the function has an inflection point between a maximum and a minimum: the graph descends **crossing its own tangent**, and the change of concavity at $0$ is consistent with the odd symmetry.

</div>
