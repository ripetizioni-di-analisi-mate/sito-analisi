## Exercise 1

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{x + 3}{x^{3} + x + 1}\,dx .
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\dfrac{x + 3}{x^{3} + x + 1}}{\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}}
= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{3} + 3x^{2}}{x^{3} + x + 1}
= 1
$$

$$
\frac{x + 3}{x^{3} + x + 1} \sim \frac{1}{x^{2}} \quad (x \to +\infty),
\qquad \text{the integral converges}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is positive on $[1, +\infty)$, because numerator and denominator are positive for $x \geq 1$, therefore the **comparison tests** apply. Numerator and denominator are polynomials, hence at infinity *the terms of highest degree* are what matter and the candidate benchmark is $\dfrac{x}{x^{3}} = \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$. The limit of the ratio between the integrand and $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ equals $1$, therefore the two functions are asymptotic. The order of infinitesimal is $2 > 1$, the benchmark converges, hence by **limit comparison** the integral **converges**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1}} .
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1}}}{\dfrac{1}{x}}
= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1}}
= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}}}
= 1
$$

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1}} \sim \frac{1}{x} \quad (x \to +\infty),
\qquad \text{the integral diverges}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is positive. Under the root the **dominant term** is $x^{2}$, therefore the candidate benchmark is $\dfrac{1}{x}$. To compute the limit of the ratio one brings $x = \sqrt{x^{2}}$ inside the root (allowed because $x > 0$), and the ratio becomes the reciprocal of $\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{1}{x^{2}}}$, which tends to $1$. The integrand is thus asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x}$, the benchmark with $\alpha = 1$ diverges, hence the integral **diverges** by **limit comparison**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} x^{2} e^{-x}\,dx .
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{2} e^{-x}}{\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}}
= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x^{4}}{e^{x}}
= 0
$$

$$
x^{2} e^{-x} < \frac{1}{x^{2}} \ \text{eventually},
\qquad \text{the integral converges}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is positive. The exponential $e^{-x}$ tends to $0$ faster than *every* power, therefore *no* power is asymptotic to the integrand and one uses the form of the comparison with limit $L = 0$. The ratio between the integrand and the benchmark $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ equals $\dfrac{x^{4}}{e^{x}}$ and tends to $0$ by the **hierarchy of infinities** (chapter on limits). Since the limit is $0$ and the integral of $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ converges, the integrand is eventually less than the benchmark and, by the **comparison test**, the integral **converges**.

</div>
