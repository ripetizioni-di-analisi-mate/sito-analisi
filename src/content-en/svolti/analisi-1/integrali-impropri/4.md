## Exercise 1

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^{\frac{5}{2}}}\,dx .
$$

$$
1 - \cos x \sim \frac{x^{2}}{2} \quad (x \to 0)
$$

$$
\frac{1 - \cos x}{x^{\frac{5}{2}}} \sim \frac{x^{2}}{2\,x^{\frac{5}{2}}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \quad (x \to 0^{+}),
\qquad \text{l'integrale converge}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The singular point is $0$. The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$, because $1 - \cos x > 0$ for $0 < x \leq 1$. The **standard cosine limit** gives $1 - \cos x \sim \dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ and the blow-up order at $0$ is $\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since the order is less than $1$, the corresponding benchmark converges and, by **limit comparison**, the given integral also **converges**; the factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ does *not* change the behavior.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{1} \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{x}\right) dx .
$$

$$
1 + \frac{1}{x} \leq \frac{2}{x} \ \text{per } 0 < x \leq 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
0 \leq \sqrt{x}\,\ln\!\left(1 + \frac{1}{x}\right) \leq \sqrt{x}\,\ln 2 + \sqrt{x}\,\ln\frac{1}{x}
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \sqrt{x}\,\ln\frac{1}{x}
= \lim_{s \to +\infty} \frac{\ln s}{\sqrt{s}}
= 0
\qquad \left( s = \frac{1}{x} \right)
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \frac{\ln\!\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{x}\right)}{\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}} = 0,
\qquad \text{l'integrale converge}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0^{+}$ the argument $1 + \dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $+\infty$, therefore the integrand tends to $+\infty$ and the singular point is $0$; the integrand is positive because the argument of the logarithm is greater than $1$. One compares with the benchmark $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$. For $0 < x \leq 1$ one has $1 \leq \dfrac{1}{x}$, therefore $1 + \dfrac{1}{x} \leq \dfrac{2}{x}$ and, since the logarithm is increasing, $\ln\!\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{x}\right) \leq \ln 2 + \ln\dfrac{1}{x}$. After multiplying by $\sqrt{x}$ both terms tend to $0$, the second by the hierarchy of growth rates after the substitution $s = \dfrac{1}{x}$, hence by the **squeeze theorem** for limits the ratio between the integrand and the benchmark tends to $0$. This is the case $L = 0$ of the comparison, the integral of the benchmark with $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ converges, therefore the given integral **converges**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{e^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1}{x}\,dx .
$$

$$
e^{s} - 1 = e^{c}\,s \ \text{con } c \in (0, s)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e^{s} - 1 \geq s \ \text{per ogni } s > 0
$$

$$
\frac{e^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1}{x} \geq \frac{1}{x^{2}} \ \text{per } 0 < x \leq 1,
\qquad \text{l'integrale diverge}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x \to 0^{+}$ the exponent $\dfrac{1}{x}$ tends to $+\infty$, therefore the integrand tends to $+\infty$ and is positive on $(0, 1]$. No power is asymptotic to an exponential of this kind, hence one looks for a **lower bound**. By the **mean value theorem** applied to $e^{s}$ on the interval $[0, s]$ there exists $c \in (0, s)$ with $e^{s} - 1 = e^{c}\,s$, and since $e^{c} \geq 1$ it follows that $e^{s} - 1 \geq s$ for every $s > 0$. With $s = \dfrac{1}{x}$ one obtains $e^{\frac{1}{x}} - 1 \geq \dfrac{1}{x}$, therefore the integrand is greater than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$. The benchmark with $\alpha = 2$ on $(0, 1]$ diverges, hence by the comparison test the integral **diverges**.

</div>
