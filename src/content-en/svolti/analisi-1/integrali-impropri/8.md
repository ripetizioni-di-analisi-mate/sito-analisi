## Exercise 1

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x^{2}}\,dx .
$$

$$
\left| \frac{\sin x}{x^{2}} \right| \leq \frac{1}{x^{2}},
\qquad
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{2}} \ \text{converges}
$$

$$
\text{the integral converges absolutely, therefore it converges}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand **changes sign**, therefore the tests for nonnegative functions are applied to the *absolute value*. Since $|\sin x| \leq 1$, the absolute value of the integrand is bounded above by $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$; the benchmark with $\alpha = 2$ converges, hence by the comparison test the integral of $\left| \dfrac{\sin x}{x^{2}} \right|$ converges. The given integral thus **converges absolutely** and, by the theorem on absolute convergence, it converges.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the behavior of
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-x} \cos 3x \,dx .
$$

$$
\left| e^{-x} \cos 3x \right| \leq e^{-x},
\qquad
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-x}\,dx = 1
$$

$$
\text{the integral converges absolutely, therefore it converges}
$$

::grafico{f="exp(-x)*cos(3*x);exp(-x);-exp(-x)" area="exp(-x)*cos(3*x);0;0;6" xmin=-0.3 xmax=6 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.2 titolo="The graph of $y = e^{-x}\cos 3x$ stays between $y = e^{-x}$ (dashed) and $y = -e^{-x}$ (in red), therefore the absolute value of the integrand is at most $e^{-x}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At $0$ the integrand is continuous, therefore the *only* critical point is $+\infty$. The cosine is **bounded**, with $|\cos 3x| \leq 1$, hence the absolute value of the integrand is bounded above by $e^{-x}$, whose integral over $[0, +\infty)$ is a convergent notable integral, with value $1$. By the comparison test the integral of $\left| e^{-x} \cos 3x \right|$ converges, therefore the given integral **converges absolutely** and hence converges.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Prove the convergence of
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx .
$$

$$
\int_1^{t} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
= \left[ -\frac{\cos x}{x} \right]_1^{t} - \int_1^{t} \frac{\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx
= \cos 1 - \frac{\cos t}{t} - \int_1^{t} \frac{\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx
$$

$$
\frac{\cos t}{t} \to 0 \quad (t \to +\infty),
\qquad
\left| \frac{\cos x}{x^{2}} \right| \leq \frac{1}{x^{2}}
\ \Longrightarrow\
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx \ \text{converges}
$$

$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
= \cos 1 - \int_1^{+\infty} \frac{\cos x}{x^{2}}\,dx
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bound $\left| \dfrac{\sin x}{x} \right| \leq \dfrac{1}{x}$ has a divergent integral, therefore comparison on the absolute value leaves the behavior *open* and another route is needed, **integration by parts**. One chooses $\sin x$ as the factor to integrate, with antiderivative $-\cos x$, and $\dfrac{1}{x}$ as the factor to differentiate. The term $\dfrac{\cos t}{t}$ tends to $0$, because it is the product of a bounded function and an infinitesimal one (chapter on limits). The integral of $\dfrac{\cos x}{x^{2}}$ converges absolutely by comparison with $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$, therefore it converges. Hence the limit as $t \to +\infty$ of the integral over $[1, t]$ exists and is finite, and the integral **converges**. As stated in the theory, the convergence here is *only* conditional, since the integral of $\dfrac{|\sin x|}{x}$ diverges.

</div>
