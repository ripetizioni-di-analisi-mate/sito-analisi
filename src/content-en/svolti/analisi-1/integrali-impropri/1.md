## Exercise 1

Compute
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{3}} .
$$

$$
\int_1^{t} \frac{dx}{x^{3}}
= \left[ -\frac{1}{2x^{2}} \right]_1^{t}
= \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2t^{2}}
$$

$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{3}}
= \lim_{t \to +\infty} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2t^{2}} \right)
= \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The *only* critical point is $+\infty$, therefore by the definition one integrates over $[1, t]$ and then computes the limit as $t \to +\infty$. An antiderivative of $x^{-3}$ is $\dfrac{x^{-2}}{-2} = -\dfrac{1}{2x^{2}}$, hence the integral over $[1, t]$ equals $\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{2t^{2}}$. As $t \to +\infty$ the term $\dfrac{1}{2t^{2}}$ tends to $0$, therefore the limit exists and is finite, and the integral **converges** to the value $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The result is consistent with the **benchmark of powers**, since the exponent $\alpha = 3$ is greater than $1$ and the predicted value is $\dfrac{1}{\alpha - 1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-2x}\,dx .
$$

$$
\int_0^{t} e^{-2x}\,dx
= \left[ -\frac{e^{-2x}}{2} \right]_0^{t}
= \frac{1}{2} - \frac{e^{-2t}}{2}
$$

$$
\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-2x}\,dx
= \lim_{t \to +\infty} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{e^{-2t}}{2} \right)
= \frac{1}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is continuous on $[0, +\infty)$, therefore the only critical point is $+\infty$. An antiderivative of $e^{-2x}$ is $-\dfrac{e^{-2x}}{2}$, as one checks by differentiating, since the derivative of $-\dfrac{e^{-2x}}{2}$ is $e^{-2x}$. The integral over $[0, t]$ therefore equals $\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{e^{-2t}}{2}$. As $t \to +\infty$ the exponential $e^{-2t}$ tends to $0$, hence the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute
$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{1 + x^{2}} .
$$

$$
\int_0^{t} \frac{dx}{1 + x^{2}}
= \bigl[ \arctan x \bigr]_0^{t}
= \arctan t
$$

$$
\int_0^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{1 + x^{2}}
= \lim_{t \to +\infty} \arctan t
= \frac{\pi}{2}
$$

::grafico{f="1/(1+x^2)" area="1/(1+x^2);0;0;12" xmin=-1 xmax=12 ymin=-0.2 ymax=1.3 titolo="The unbounded region under $y = \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$ for $x \geq 0$ has finite area, equal to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

An antiderivative of $\dfrac{1}{1 + x^{2}}$ is $\arctan x$, and $\arctan 0 = 0$, therefore the integral over $[0, t]$ equals $\arctan t$. As $t \to +\infty$ the arctangent tends to its supremum $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, hence the integral **converges** and equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{2}}$ as $x \to +\infty$, therefore convergence was also predicted by **comparison** with the benchmark of exponent $2$; the explicit computation additionally provides the *value*.

</div>
