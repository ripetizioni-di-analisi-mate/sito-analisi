## Exercise 1

Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{dx}{x^{\alpha}\,\bigl(1 + \sqrt{x}\bigr)} .
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \frac{\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}\,\bigl(1 + \sqrt{x}\bigr)}}{\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}}}
= \lim_{x \to 0^{+}} \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{x}}
= 1
$$

$$
\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}\,\bigl(1 + \sqrt{x}\bigr)} \sim \frac{1}{x^{\alpha}} \quad (x \to 0^{+}),
\qquad \text{the integral converges if and only if } \alpha < 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$ for every $\alpha$ and the critical point is $0$. As $x \to 0^{+}$ the factor $1 + \sqrt{x}$ tends to $1$, therefore the ratio between the integrand and $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}}$ tends to $1$ and the two functions are asymptotic. By **limit comparison** the integral has the same behavior as the benchmark at $0$ of exponent $\alpha$, which **converges** *if and only if* $\alpha < 1$. For $\alpha \geq 1$ the integral **diverges**, since the integrand is positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{\sin x}{x^{\alpha}}\,dx .
$$

$$
\frac{\sin x}{x^{\alpha}}
= \frac{\sin x}{x} \cdot \frac{1}{x^{\alpha - 1}}
\sim \frac{1}{x^{\alpha - 1}} \quad (x \to 0^{+})
$$

$$
\alpha - 1 < 1 \iff \alpha < 2,
\qquad \text{the integral converges if and only if } \alpha < 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On $(0, 1]$ the sine is positive, therefore the integrand is positive. The **standard limit** $\sin x \sim x$ implies that the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha - 1}}$, because the factor $\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ tends to $1$. The benchmark at $0$ with exponent $\alpha - 1$ **converges** *if and only if* $\alpha - 1 < 1$, that is, $\alpha < 2$, and limit comparison transfers the behavior to the given integral. For $\alpha \leq 1$ the exponent $\alpha - 1$ is less than or equal to $0$ and the integrand extends continuously at $0$, therefore the integral is **ordinary**; these values are already included in the condition $\alpha < 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_0^{1} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^{\alpha}}\,dx .
$$

$$
\frac{1 - \cos x}{x^{\alpha}}
\sim \frac{x^{2}}{2\,x^{\alpha}}
= \frac{1}{2\,x^{\alpha - 2}} \quad (x \to 0^{+})
$$

$$
\alpha - 2 < 1 \iff \alpha < 3,
\qquad \text{the integral converges if and only if } \alpha < 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is positive on $(0, 1]$, because $1 - \cos x > 0$ for $0 < x \leq 1$. The **standard limit of the cosine** gives $1 - \cos x \sim \dfrac{x^{2}}{2}$, therefore the integrand is asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2\,x^{\alpha - 2}}$. The factor $\dfrac{1}{2}$ does not change the behavior, hence the integral has the same behavior as the benchmark at $0$ of exponent $\alpha - 2$, which **converges** if and only if $\alpha - 2 < 1$, that is, $\alpha < 3$. Compared with the previous exercise the threshold moves from $2$ to $3$, because $1 - \cos x$ is an **infinitesimal of order** $2$ while $\sin x$ is of order $1$.

</div>
