## Exercise 1

Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \frac{dx}{x^{\alpha}\,(1 + x)} .
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}\,(1 + x)}}{\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha + 1}}}
= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{x}{1 + x}
= 1
$$

$$
\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}\,(1 + x)} \sim \frac{1}{x^{\alpha + 1}} \quad (x \to +\infty),
\qquad \text{the integral converges if and only if } \alpha > 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is positive for $x \geq 1$ and for every $\alpha$. The ratio between the integrand and $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha + 1}}$ equals $\dfrac{x}{1 + x}$ and tends to $1$, therefore the two functions are asymptotic for *every* value of the parameter. By **limit comparison** the integral has the same behavior as the benchmark of exponent $\alpha + 1$, which converges *if and only if* $\alpha + 1 > 1$, that is, $\alpha > 0$. For $\alpha \leq 0$ the integral **diverges**, since the integrand is positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_1^{+\infty} \left( \sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x \right)^{\alpha} dx .
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x
= \frac{\left(\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x\right)\left(\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} + x\right)}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} + x}
= \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} + x}
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x \sim \frac{1}{2x} \quad (x \to +\infty)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\left( \sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x \right)^{\alpha} \sim \frac{1}{2^{\alpha} x^{\alpha}}
$$

$$
\text{the integral converges if and only if } \alpha > 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} - x$ is positive, because $x^{2} + 1 > x^{2}$ and the square root is increasing, therefore the integrand is positive for every $\alpha$. **Rationalization** turns the difference into the reciprocal of $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} + x$; since $\sqrt{x^{2} + 1} \sim x$, the denominator is asymptotic to $2x$ and the difference to $\dfrac{1}{2x}$. Raising to the power $\alpha$ **preserves** asymptotic equivalence, given that the ratio between the two functions raised to the power $\alpha$ tends to $1^{\alpha} = 1$. The integrand is thus asymptotic to $\dfrac{1}{2^{\alpha} x^{\alpha}}$; the constant factor $\dfrac{1}{2^{\alpha}}$ does not change the behavior, therefore the integral has the same behavior as the benchmark of exponent $\alpha$ and **converges if and only if** $\alpha > 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine for which $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ the following integral converges:
$$
\int_e^{+\infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^{\alpha}}\,dx .
$$

**Case $\alpha > 1$.** Setting $\beta = \dfrac{1 + \alpha}{2}$, so that $1 < \beta < \alpha$:
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\dfrac{\ln x}{x^{\alpha}}}{\dfrac{1}{x^{\beta}}}
= \lim_{x \to +\infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^{\alpha - \beta}}
= 0,
\qquad \text{the integral converges}
$$

**Case $\alpha \leq 1$.**
$$
\frac{\ln x}{x^{\alpha}} \geq \frac{1}{x^{\alpha}} \ \text{for } x \geq e,
\qquad \text{the integral diverges}
$$

$$
\text{the integral converges if and only if } \alpha > 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integrand is positive for $x \geq e$, because the logarithm is at least $1$. For $\alpha > 1$ *no* power is asymptotic to the integrand, therefore one compares with the benchmark of exponent $\beta = \dfrac{1 + \alpha}{2}$, strictly between $1$ and $\alpha$. The ratio equals $\dfrac{\ln x}{x^{\alpha - \beta}}$ and tends to $0$ by the **hierarchy of infinities**, since $\alpha - \beta > 0$. This is the case $L = 0$ of the comparison and the benchmark with $\beta > 1$ converges, therefore the integral **converges**. For $\alpha \leq 1$ one has $\ln x \geq 1$ on $[e, +\infty)$, hence the integrand is greater than or equal to $\dfrac{1}{x^{\alpha}}$; the benchmark with exponent $\alpha \leq 1$ diverges, so by the comparison test the integral **diverges**. Overall, convergence holds *if and only if* $\alpha > 1$.

</div>
