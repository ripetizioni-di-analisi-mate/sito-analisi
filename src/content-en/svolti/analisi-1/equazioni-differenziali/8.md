## Exercise 1

Solve the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' + y = 0 \\[4pt]
y(0) = 1 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = 2
\end{cases}
$$

**General solution.** The characteristic equation $r^2 + 1 = 0$ has roots $\pm i$, so
$$
y = C_1 \cos x + C_2 \sin x .
$$

**Initial conditions.** With $y' = -C_1 \sin x + C_2 \cos x$:
$$
\begin{cases}
y(0) = C_1 = 1 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = C_2 = 2
\end{cases}
\quad\implies\quad
y = \cos x + 2 \sin x .
$$

::grafico{f="cos(x)+2*sin(x);1+2*x" punti="0,1,pieno" xmin=-1.5 xmax=7 ymin=-3 ymax=3 titolo="The solution $y = \cos x + 2\sin x$ passes through $(0, 1)$ with slope $2$, that of the tangent line $y = 1 + 2x$ (dashed), and oscillates with amplitude $\sqrt{5}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic equation has **purely imaginary** roots $\pm i$, that is, $\lambda = 0$ and $\mu = 1$, so the general solution is a combination of cosine and sine without an exponential factor. There are *two* initial conditions because there are *two* constants to determine. The first condition is imposed on the general solution and gives $C_1 = 1$, since $\cos 0 = 1$ and $\sin 0 = 0$; the second is imposed **on the derivative** and gives $C_2 = 2$. In this case the system in the constants is already solved, because each condition involves a single constant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' - 4y' + 4y = 0 \\[4pt]
y(0) = 1 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = 0
\end{cases}
$$

**General solution.** The characteristic equation $r^2 - 4r + 4 = (r - 2)^2 = 0$ has the double root $r = 2$, so
$$
y = \left( C_1 + C_2\, x \right) e^{2x} .
$$

**Initial conditions.** With $y' = C_2\, e^{2x} + 2 \left( C_1 + C_2\, x \right) e^{2x}$:
$$
\begin{cases}
y(0) = C_1 = 1 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = C_2 + 2C_1 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\implies\quad
C_1 = 1, \quad C_2 = -2 .
$$

$$
y = \left( 1 - 2x \right) e^{2x}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side of the characteristic equation is the square of $r - 2$, so the root is **double** and the general solution contains the factor $C_1 + C_2 x$. The derivative is computed with the **product rule** and at $x = 0$ equals $C_2 + 2C_1$. From the first condition it follows that $C_1 = 1$; substituting this value into the second gives $C_2 = -2$. The system in the constants is **linear and triangular**, therefore it is solved by substitution from top to bottom.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve the Cauchy problem
$$
\begin{cases}
y'' - y = -2x \\[4pt]
y(0) = 0 \\[4pt]
y'(0) = 1
\end{cases}
$$

**Associated homogeneous equation.** The characteristic equation $r^2 - 1 = 0$ has roots $\pm 1$, so $y_o = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{-x}$.

**Particular solution.** The forcing term is a polynomial of degree $1$ and $0$ is not a root; with $y_p = ax + b$:
$$
0 - (ax + b) = -2x
\quad\implies\quad
a = 2, \quad b = 0
\quad\implies\quad
y_p = 2x .
$$

**General solution.**
$$
y = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{-x} + 2x .
$$

**Initial conditions.** With $y' = C_1 e^{x} - C_2 e^{-x} + 2$:
$$
\begin{cases}
C_1 + C_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
C_1 - C_2 + 2 = 1
\end{cases}
\quad\implies\quad
C_1 = -\frac{1}{2}, \quad C_2 = \frac{1}{2} .
$$

$$
y = \frac{e^{-x} - e^{x}}{2} + 2x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is complete, so before the initial conditions one needs the general solution of the complete equation: the homogeneous equation is solved by the roots $\pm 1$, and for the polynomial forcing term the **method of undetermined coefficients** with trial function $ax + b$ gives $y_p = 2x$. The initial conditions must be imposed on the **complete general solution** and *not* on the homogeneous part alone, because the particular solution also contributes to the values at $x = 0$: indeed the term $2$, which is the derivative of $2x$, appears in the second condition. In the system, adding the two equations gives $2C_1 = -1$, so $C_1 = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and, from the first equation, $C_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

</div>
