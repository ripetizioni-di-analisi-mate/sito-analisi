## Exercise 1

Find the general solution of
$$
y'' - 3y' + 2y = 0 .
$$

Characteristic equation:
$$
r^2 - 3r + 2 = 0
\quad\implies\quad
r = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 - 8}}{2}
\quad\implies\quad
r_1 = 1, \quad r_2 = 2 .
$$

General solution:
$$
y = C_1\, e^{x} + C_2\, e^{2x}, \qquad C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation is homogeneous with constant coefficients, so one writes the **characteristic equation** by substituting $r^2$ for $y''$, $r$ for $y'$, and $1$ for $y$. The discriminant equals $9 - 8 = 1 > 0$, therefore the roots are **real and distinct**. Each root corresponds to the exponential solution $e^{r x}$, and by the **structure theorem** *every* solution is a linear combination of the two: the general solution is $C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{2x}$. The verification is direct, since for example $y = e^{2x}$ gives $4e^{2x} - 6e^{2x} + 2e^{2x} = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the general solution of
$$
y'' + 4y' + 4y = 0 .
$$

Characteristic equation:
$$
r^2 + 4r + 4 = 0
\quad\implies\quad
(r + 2)^2 = 0
\quad\implies\quad
r = -2 \ \text{(double)} .
$$

General solution:
$$
y = \left( C_1 + C_2\, x \right) e^{-2x}, \qquad C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand side of the characteristic equation is the square of $r + 2$, so the discriminant is zero and the root $r = -2$ is **double**. In this case the exponential $e^{-2x}$ alone does *not* suffice to generate all the solutions, because the solution space has dimension $2$; the non-proportional **second solution** is $x e^{-2x}$, as established by the double-root case. The general solution is therefore $(C_1 + C_2 x) e^{-2x}$. As a check, with $y = x e^{-2x}$ one has $y' = (1 - 2x)e^{-2x}$ and $y'' = (4x - 4)e^{-2x}$, and the sum $y'' + 4y' + 4y$ equals $(4x - 4 + 4 - 8x + 4x)e^{-2x} = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the general solution of
$$
y'' + 2y' + 5y = 0 .
$$

Characteristic equation:
$$
r^2 + 2r + 5 = 0
\quad\implies\quad
r = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 20}}{2}
= \frac{-2 \pm 4i}{2}
= -1 \pm 2i .
$$

With $\lambda = -1$ and $\mu = 2$, the general solution is
$$
y = e^{-x} \left( C_1 \cos(2x) + C_2 \sin(2x) \right), \qquad C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discriminant of the characteristic equation equals $4 - 20 = -16 < 0$, so the roots are **complex conjugates**. The square root of $-16$ is written $4i$ using the imaginary unit, therefore the roots are $-1 \pm 2i$: the real part is $\lambda = -1$ and the coefficient of the imaginary part is $\mu = 2$. By **Euler's formula**, the real part and the imaginary part of $e^{(-1 + 2i)x}$ provide the two real solutions $e^{-x}\cos(2x)$ and $e^{-x}\sin(2x)$, and the general solution is their linear combination. The solutions **oscillate** with *decreasing* amplitude $e^{-x}$, since the real part of the roots is negative.

</div>
