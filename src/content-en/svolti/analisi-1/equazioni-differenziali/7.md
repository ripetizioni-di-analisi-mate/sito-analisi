## Exercise 1

Find the general solution of
$$
y'' + y' - 2y = 4x .
$$

**Associated homogeneous equation.** The characteristic equation $r^2 + r - 2 = 0$ has roots $r_1 = 1$ and $r_2 = -2$, so $y_o = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{-2x}$.

**Particular solution.** The forcing term is a polynomial of degree $1$ and $0$ is *not* a root of the characteristic equation, so we look for $y_p = ax + b$:
$$
0 + a - 2(ax + b) = 4x
\quad\implies\quad
-2a\, x + (a - 2b) = 4x
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
-2a = 4 \\[4pt]
a - 2b = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\implies\quad
a = -2, \quad b = -1 .
$$

**General solution.**
$$
y = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{-2x} - 2x - 1, \qquad C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solution follows the **structure theorem**: general solution of the homogeneous equation plus a particular solution of the complete equation. The roots of the characteristic equation are real and distinct, so the homogeneous equation is solved by two exponentials. The forcing term $4x$ is a polynomial of degree $1$; since the number associated with polynomials, namely $0$, is different from the roots $1$ and $-2$, the trial function is a **generic polynomial of the same degree**. Substituting $y_p = ax + b$ into the equation, the derivatives are $y_p' = a$ and $y_p'' = 0$, and the equality between polynomials requires that the coefficients of terms of equal degree coincide; the system yields $a = -2$ and $b = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the general solution of
$$
y'' + 2y' + y = 4 \sin x .
$$

**Associated homogeneous equation.** The characteristic equation $r^2 + 2r + 1 = (r + 1)^2 = 0$ has the double root $r = -1$, so $y_o = (C_1 + C_2\, x)\, e^{-x}$.

**Particular solution.** The number associated with $\sin x$ is $\pm i$, which is *not* a root, so we look for $y_p = a \cos x + b \sin x$:
$$
(-a \cos x - b \sin x) + 2(-a \sin x + b \cos x) + (a \cos x + b \sin x) = 4 \sin x
$$

$$
2b \cos x - 2a \sin x = 4 \sin x
\quad\implies\quad
\begin{cases}
2b = 0 \\[4pt]
-2a = 4
\end{cases}
\quad\implies\quad
a = -2, \quad b = 0 .
$$

**General solution.**
$$
y = \left( C_1 + C_2\, x \right) e^{-x} - 2 \cos x, \qquad C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

::grafico{f="-2*cos(x);3*exp(-x)-2*cos(x);(-1+3*x)*exp(-x)-2*cos(x)" xmin=-0.5 xmax=13 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3.5 titolo="The particular solution $y = -2\cos x$ (in color) and the solutions with $C_1 = 3$, $C_2 = 0$ (dashed) and with $C_1 = -1$, $C_2 = 3$ (in red): the term $(C_1 + C_2 x)e^{-x}$ tends to $0$ and the solutions approach $-2\cos x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic equation has zero discriminant, so the homogeneous equation is solved by the double-root case. The forcing term is a multiple of $\sin x$ with $\omega = 1$; the associated number $\pm i$ does not coincide with the root $-1$, therefore **there is no resonance** and the trial function contains **both the cosine and the sine**, even though *only* the sine appears in the forcing term: the first derivative swaps the two functions, so both are needed. Substituting and collecting the coefficients of $\cos x$ and $\sin x$, the equality must hold for every $x$, therefore the corresponding coefficients on the two sides coincide; the system yields $a = -2$ and $b = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the general solution of
$$
y'' - 3y' + 2y = e^{x} .
$$

**Associated homogeneous equation.** The characteristic equation $r^2 - 3r + 2 = 0$ has roots $r_1 = 1$ and $r_2 = 2$, so $y_o = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{2x}$.

**Particular solution.** The number associated with $e^{x}$ is $\gamma = 1$, a simple root of the characteristic equation: there is **resonance** and the trial function must be multiplied by $x$, that is, $y_p = a\, x e^{x}$. With
$$
y_p' = a (x + 1)\, e^{x}, \qquad y_p'' = a (x + 2)\, e^{x} ,
$$
the substitution gives
$$
a\, e^{x} \bigl( (x + 2) - 3(x + 1) + 2x \bigr) = e^{x}
\quad\implies\quad
-a\, e^{x} = e^{x}
\quad\implies\quad
a = -1 .
$$

**General solution.**
$$
y = C_1 e^{x} + C_2 e^{2x} - x\, e^{x}, \qquad C_1, C_2 \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The forcing term $e^{x}$ is exponential with $\gamma = 1$, and $1$ is a root of the characteristic equation: the trial function $a e^{x}$ would solve the homogeneous equation, so once substituted it would give $0$ and *no* choice of $a$ could produce $e^{x}$. By the **resonance rule** the trial function must be multiplied by $x$. The derivatives of $a x e^{x}$ are computed with the product rule and, after the substitution, the terms containing $x e^{x}$ cancel *precisely* because $1$ is a root; what remains is $-a e^{x}$, from which $a = -1$. The general solution is the sum of the homogeneous solution and the particular solution $-x e^{x}$.

</div>
