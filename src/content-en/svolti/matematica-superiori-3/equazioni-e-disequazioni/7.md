## Exercise 1

Find $m$ so that $x^2-2x+m=0$ has a double root.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta&=(-2)^2-4m=4-4m, \\[6pt]
\Delta=0&\iff m=1,\\[6pt]
x^2-2x+1&=(x-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $x^2$ is always $1$, so the equation is quadratic for every $m$. Its single root is double when the discriminant is zero. With $m=1$, factoring directly shows that the root is $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the number of real solutions of $(m-2)x=m+1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m=2&\implies 0\cdot x=3,&& S=\varnothing, \\[6pt]
m\neq2&\implies x=\frac{m+1}{m-2},&& |S|=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Division by $m-2$ is possible when $m\neq2$. At $m=2$, the coefficient of the unknown is zero but the right-hand side equals $3$. No value of $x$ makes the equality true. For all other parameter values, dividing determines exactly one solution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the number of real solutions of $mx^2-4x+1=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m=0&\implies -4x+1=0\implies x=\frac14, \\[6pt]
m\neq0&\implies \Delta=16-4m=4(4-m), \\[6pt]
m=4&\implies 4x^2-4x+1=(2x-1)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $m=0$, the degree drops to one, so there is one simple solution. For $m<4$ with $m\neq0$, the discriminant is positive and there are two solutions. At $m=4$, the discriminant is zero and there is a double root, $\dfrac12$. For $m>4$, the discriminant is negative and the solution set is empty. Both parameter values $0$ and $4$ give one solution, for different reasons.

</div>
