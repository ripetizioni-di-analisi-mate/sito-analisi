## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x-1}{x-4}\geq0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x & (-\infty,1) & (1,4) & (4,+\infty)\\ \hline
x-1 & - & + & +\\
x-4 & - & - & +\\ \hline
\dfrac{x-1}{x-4} & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
S=(-\infty,1]\cup(4,+\infty).
$$

::grafico{f="(x-1)/(x-4)" xmin=-3 xmax=8 ymin=-6 ymax=7 punti="1,0,pieno" asintoti="x=4" titolo="The numerator makes the fraction zero at $1$ and the denominator makes it undefined at $4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator rules out $4$ from the start. For $x<1$, the numerator and denominator are both negative, whereas for $x>4$ they are both positive. At $1$ only the numerator is zero, so the fraction is defined and equals zero. The solution includes $1$ and excludes $4$.

For $1<x<4$ the numerator is positive and the denominator is negative. At $x=2$, for example, the quotient equals $-\dfrac12$, so the middle interval provides no solutions. Multiplying immediately by $x-4$ without reversing the inequality would also discard the values to the left of $1$. At $x=0$ the fraction equals $\dfrac14>0$, while the inequality $x-1\geq0$ obtained that way would be false. The graph shows the zero at $1$ and the vertical line at $4$. It identifies where the quotient is above the axis and why the two boundary points are treated differently.

An equivalent algebraic check is to multiply by $(x-4)^2>0$ on the domain. This gives $(x-1)(x-4)\geq0$, while $4$ remains excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{x-2}<2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}, \\[6pt]
\frac{x+1}{x-2}-2&=\frac{x+1-2(x-2)}{x-2} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{5-x}{x-2}, \\[6pt]
S&=(-\infty,2)\cup(5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare the difference between the two sides to zero. The numerator $5-x$ is positive for $x<5$, whereas the denominator $x-2$ is negative for $x<2$. The quotient is therefore negative before $2$. After $5$ it is negative again because the numerator is negative and the denominator positive. Exclude $5$ because it gives equality.

On the middle interval, $2<x<5$, the numerator and denominator of the difference are both positive. For example, $x=3$ gives $\dfrac{3+1}{3-2}=4$, which is greater than the threshold $2$. At $x=0$ the fraction is $-\dfrac12<2$, while at $x=6$ it is $\dfrac74<2$. This checks both accepted branches directly in the original inequality. The boundary $x=2$ is excluded in any comparison because it makes the denominator zero. The boundary $x=5$ gives $\dfrac63=2$ and would be allowed for $\leq$.

The constant $2$ becomes $\dfrac{2(x-2)}{x-2}$. Subtracting gives $x+1-2x+4=5-x$ in the numerator. Its negative coefficient explains why it is positive to the left of its zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}\geq0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,3\}, \\[6pt]
\frac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}&=\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+2)} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{x+3}{x+2}\qquad(x\in D),\\[6pt]
S&=(-\infty,-3]\cup(-2,3)\cup(3,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x^2-9)/(x^2-x-6)" xmin=-6 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=5 punti="-3,0,pieno;3,1.2,vuoto" asintoti="x=-2" titolo="The graph meets the axis at $-3$ and has an open circle at $x=3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring the denominator identifies two excluded values, $-2$ and $3$. For $x\in D$, the factor $x-3$ is nonzero and can be canceled. The reduced quotient is positive on both sides of $[-3,-2]$ and equals zero at $-3$. Since $3$ lies in the positive region to the right of $-2$ but outside the domain, that region splits into $(-2,3)$ and $(3,+\infty)$.

At $x=3$ the reduced expression would have y-coordinate $\dfrac65$. The graph therefore shows an open circle there. The vertical line at $-2$ has a different origin, because the factor $x+2$ remains in the denominator after cancellation. The zero $x=-3$ is allowed. In the original denominator, $(-3)^2-(-3)-6=6$, while the numerator is zero. For a check on the excluded middle interval, use $x=-\dfrac52$. The reduced numerator equals $\dfrac12$ and the denominator $-\dfrac12$, so the quotient is $-1$. The reduced expression gives the sign, while the original domain determines the exclusions.

In the reduced form, both factors are positive to the right of $-2$. Removing $3$ splits that branch into two pieces without changing its sign.

</div>
