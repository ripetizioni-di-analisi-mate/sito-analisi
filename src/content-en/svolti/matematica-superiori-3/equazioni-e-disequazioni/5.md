## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x-1}{x-4}\geq0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x & (-\infty,1) & (1,4) & (4,+\infty)\\ \hline
x-1 & - & + & +\\
x-4 & - & - & +\\ \hline
\dfrac{x-1}{x-4} & + & - & +
\end{array}
$$

$$
S=(-\infty,1]\cup(4,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator rules out $4$ from the start. For $x<1$, the numerator and denominator are both negative, whereas for $x>4$ they are both positive. At $1$ only the numerator is zero, so the fraction is defined and equals zero. The solution includes $1$ and excludes $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{x-2}<2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{2\}, \\[6pt]
\frac{x+1}{x-2}-2&=\frac{x+1-2(x-2)}{x-2} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{5-x}{x-2}, \\[6pt]
S&=(-\infty,2)\cup(5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Compare the difference between the two sides to zero. The numerator $5-x$ is positive for $x<5$, whereas the denominator $x-2$ is negative for $x<2$. The quotient is therefore negative before $2$. After $5$ it is negative again because the numerator is negative and the denominator positive. Exclude $5$ because it gives equality.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}\geq0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,3\}, \\[6pt]
\frac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}&=\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+2)} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{x+3}{x+2}\qquad(x\in D),\\[6pt]
S&=(-\infty,-3]\cup(-2,3)\cup(3,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x^2-9)/(x^2-x-6)" xmin=-6 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=5 punti="-3,0,pieno;3,1.2,vuoto" asintoti="x=-2" titolo="The graph meets the axis at $-3$ and has an open circle at $x=3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring the denominator identifies two excluded values, $-2$ and $3$. For $x\in D$, the factor $x-3$ is nonzero and can be canceled. The reduced quotient is positive on both sides of $[-3,-2]$ and equals zero at $-3$. Since $3$ lies in the positive region to the right of $-2$ but outside the domain, that region splits into $(-2,3)$ and $(3,+\infty)$.

</div>
