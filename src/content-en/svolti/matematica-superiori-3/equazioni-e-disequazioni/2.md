## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-1=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-1=0 &\iff (x-1)(x+1)=0 \\[6pt]
&\iff x=1\ \lor\ x=-1,\qquad S=\{-1,1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use $u^2-v^2=(u-v)(u+v)$ with $u=x$ and $v=1$. The term $1$ is a square, so the two factors have opposite constant terms. Both values found have square equal to $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $(2x-3)^2=7$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x-3)^2=7&\iff 2x-3=\pm\sqrt7 \\[6pt]
&\iff x=\frac{3\pm\sqrt7}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square is already isolated. The two real numbers with square equal to $7$ are $\sqrt7$ and $-\sqrt7$. In each case, add $3$ and then divide by $2$. The two values of $x$ are distinct because $\sqrt7>0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2x^2-8x+9=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^2-8x+9&=2(x^2-4x+4)+1 \\[6pt]
&=2(x-2)^2+1\geq1,\qquad S=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*(x-2)^2+1" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-1 ymax=10 punti="2,1,pieno" titolo="The lowest point has y-coordinate $1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

After factoring $2$ from the $x^2$ and $x$ terms, add $4$ inside to complete the square. This addition contributes $8$ to the original expression, so $1$ of the constant term $9$ remains outside. Even at $x=2$, where the square becomes zero, the trinomial is positive. Therefore it has no real roots.

</div>
