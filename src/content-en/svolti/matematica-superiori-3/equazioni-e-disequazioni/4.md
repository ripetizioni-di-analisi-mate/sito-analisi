## Exercise 1

Solve $(x-1)(x-4)>0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x&(-\infty,1)&(1,4)&(4,+\infty)\\ \hline
x-1&-&+&+\\
x-4&-&-&+\\ \hline
(x-1)(x-4)&+&-&+
\end{array}
$$

$$
S=(-\infty,1)\cup(4,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When $x<1$, we also have $x<4$, so both factors are negative. When $x>4$, both are positive. In either case the product is positive. Between the two roots there is exactly one negative factor. The endpoints are excluded because the product is zero there and the inequality requires it to be strictly positive.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $(x-2)^2(x+1)>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-2)^2&>0 && (x\neq2),\\[6pt]
x+1&>0 && (x>-1),\\[6pt]
S&=(-1,2)\cup(2,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square only rules out $x=2$. It stays positive to the left and right of that point, so the direction of the comparison depends on $x+1$. The interval $(-1,+\infty)$ contains $2$, so split it into two open intervals to remove that point.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $-(x+2)(x-1)(x-4)\geq0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
x&(-\infty,-2)&(-2,1)&(1,4)&(4,+\infty)\\ \hline
-(x+2)(x-1)(x-4)&+&-&+&-
\end{array}
$$

$$
S=(-\infty,-2]\cup[1,4].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $x>4$, all three linear factors are positive, so the leading minus sign makes the product negative. Moving from right to left, each zero belongs to only one factor. The sign changes at each critical value, so the product is positive on $(1,4)$ and $(-\infty,-2)$. Include all three zeros because the comparison allows equality.

</div>
