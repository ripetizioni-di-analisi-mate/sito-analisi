## Exercise 1

Solve $(x-1)(x-4)>0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x&(-\infty,1)&(1,4)&(4,+\infty)\\ \hline
x-1&-&+&+\\
x-4&-&-&+\\ \hline
(x-1)(x-4)&+&-&+
\end{array}
$$

$$
S=(-\infty,1)\cup(4,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When $x<1$, we also have $x<4$, so both factors are negative. When $x>4$, both are positive. In either case the product is positive. Between the two roots there is exactly one negative factor. The endpoints are excluded because the product is zero there and the inequality requires it to be strictly positive.

The same intervals can be obtained by solving the conditions for matching signs. If $x-1>0$ and $x-4>0$, we need both $x>1$ and $x>4$, so $x>4$ is sufficient. If $x-1<0$ and $x-4<0$, we need both $x<1$ and $x<4$, so $x<1$ is sufficient. Each branch requires its own conditions simultaneously, while the two branches are joined by a union. As a check, the product equals $4$ at $0$, $-2$ at $2$, and $4$ at $5$. This shows how the rows of the chart give the union of the two rays.

If the comparison allowed equality, there would be a third case in which at least one factor is zero. The strict sign here leaves the two cases in the chart.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $(x-2)^2(x+1)>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-2)^2&>0 && (x\neq2),\\[6pt]
x+1&>0 && (x>-1),\\[6pt]
S&=(-1,2)\cup(2,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2)^2*(x+1)" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-9 ymax=12 punti="-1,0,pieno;2,0,pieno" titolo="The simple zero $-1$ changes the sign and the double zero $2$ separates two positive regions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The square only rules out $x=2$. It stays positive to the left and right of that point, so the direction of the comparison depends on $x+1$. The interval $(-1,+\infty)$ contains $2$, so split it into two open intervals to remove that point.

The two zeros have different roles. Around $-1$, the sign of $x+1$ changes while the square is positive. At $x=0$ the product equals $4>0$, so the positive branch begins to the right of the zero $-1$. Before and after $2$, the factor $x+1$ remains positive. For example, the product equals $2$ at $1$ and $4$ at $3$. The graph touches the axis at $2$ without crossing it. The product is zero there, so the strict inequality excludes the point even though nearby values on both sides are allowed.

The set can also be written as $(-1,+\infty)\setminus\{2\}$. It gives the same solution as a single sign condition, $x>-1$, together with the exclusion $x\neq2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $-(x+2)(x-1)(x-4)\geq0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
x&(-\infty,-2)&(-2,1)&(1,4)&(4,+\infty)\\ \hline
-(x+2)(x-1)(x-4)&+&-&+&-
\end{array}
$$

$$
S=(-\infty,-2]\cup[1,4].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $x>4$, all three linear factors are positive, so the leading minus sign makes the product negative. Moving from right to left, each zero belongs to only one factor. The sign changes at each critical value, so the product is positive on $(1,4)$ and $(-\infty,-2)$. Include all three zeros because the comparison allows equality.

Check the sign by substituting one number from each interval. At $x=-3$ all three linear factors are negative and the leading minus sign makes the result positive. At $x=0$ the factors are $2$, $-1$, and $-4$, so the final value is $-8$. At $x=2$ they are $4$, $1$, and $-2$, giving $8$. At $x=5$ the value is $-28$. Then check the three boundary points separately, since each makes one factor zero. This gives a ray closed on the right and a closed interval.

On the first ray, the product of three negative numbers is negative before the leading minus sign. In the last interval, three positive factors give a positive product before that minus sign is applied.

</div>
