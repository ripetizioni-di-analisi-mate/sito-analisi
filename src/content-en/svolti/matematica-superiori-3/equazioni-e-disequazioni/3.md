## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-5x+4\leq0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x&(-\infty,1)&(1,4)&(4,+\infty)\\ \hline
(x-1)(x-4)&+&-&+
\end{array}
\qquad S=[1,4].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring the trinomial gives $(x-1)(x-4)$. For $1<x<4$, the factor $x-1$ is positive and $x-4$ is negative. Outside $[1,4]$, the two factors have the same sign, so the product is positive. The symbol $\leq$ also allows the zeros $1$ and $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-x^2+7x-10\leq0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^2+7x-10\leq0
&\iff -(x-2)(x-5)\leq0 \\[6pt]
&\iff (x-2)(x-5)\geq0 \\[6pt]
S&=(-\infty,2]\cup[5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-x^2+7*x-10" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-8 ymax=3 punti="2,0,pieno;5,0,pieno" titolo="The nonpositive part is outside the two roots, which are included"}

<div class="spiegazione">

After factoring, multiply by $-1$, so the inequality becomes $\geq$. Now look for the intervals in which the factors have the same sign. At $x=0$, for example, both are negative and the original inequality holds. The point $x=3$ is between the roots and gives $-9+21-10=2>0$, so it is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $4x^2-4x+1\leq0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x^2-4x+1&=(2x-1)^2 \\[6pt]
(2x-1)^2\leq0&\iff 2x-1=0 \\[6pt]
S&=\left\{\frac12\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The trinomial is a perfect square. For every value of $x$, the square is greater than or equal to zero, so the required comparison holds only at equality. The double root $\dfrac12$ gives a single point, rather than an interval of positive length.

</div>
