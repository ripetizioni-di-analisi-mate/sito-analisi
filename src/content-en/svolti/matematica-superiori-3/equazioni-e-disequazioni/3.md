## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-5x+4\leq0$.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
x&(-\infty,1)&(1,4)&(4,+\infty)\\ \hline
(x-1)(x-4)&+&-&+
\end{array}
\qquad S=[1,4].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring the trinomial gives $(x-1)(x-4)$. For $1<x<4$, the factor $x-1$ is positive and $x-4$ is negative. Outside $[1,4]$, the two factors have the same sign, so the product is positive. The symbol $\leq$ also allows the zeros $1$ and $4$.

To build the chart, choose a value in each interval. At $x=0$ the trinomial is $4>0$, at $x=2$ it is $-2<0$, and at $x=5$ it is $4>0$. Once the trinomial has been factored, the signs of the factors are fixed throughout each interval, so the three evaluations represent all its points. The chart describes the open intervals. To decide what to do at the endpoints, substitute $1$ and $4$ separately. Both make the trinomial zero, so the solution is the entire closed interval $[1,4]$.

The roots are simple because each makes just one linear factor zero. Passing across either root changes exactly one sign and therefore reverses the sign of the product.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-x^2+7x-10\leq0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^2+7x-10\leq0
&\iff -(x-2)(x-5)\leq0 \\[6pt]
&\iff (x-2)(x-5)\geq0 \\[6pt]
S&=(-\infty,2]\cup[5,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-x^2+7*x-10" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-8 ymax=3 punti="2,0,pieno;5,0,pieno" titolo="The nonpositive part is outside the two roots, which are included"}

<div class="spiegazione">

After factoring, multiply by $-1$, so the inequality becomes $\geq$. Now look for the intervals in which the factors have the same sign. At $x=0$, for example, both are negative and the original inequality holds. The point $x=3$ is between the roots and gives $-9+21-10=2>0$, so it is excluded.

The effect of the negative coefficient is visible in the graph. The parabola opens downward and lies above the axis between the intersections at $2$ and $5$. The polynomial is positive there, so that section is excluded. Outside the two zeros, the graph is below the axis, as the value $-10$ at $x=0$ confirms. At the zeros, for example $-4+14-10=0$ at $x=2$, equality is allowed. The rays therefore include their finite endpoints. The test value $x=3$ shows that extending a ray toward the center would introduce values of the wrong sign.

The factored form also identifies $-x^2+7x-10$ as the opposite of $x^2-7x+10$. The two polynomials have the same zeros and opposite signs everywhere else.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $4x^2-4x+1\leq0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x^2-4x+1&=(2x-1)^2 \\[6pt]
(2x-1)^2\leq0&\iff 2x-1=0 \\[6pt]
S&=\left\{\frac12\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(2*x-1)^2" xmin=-1.2 xmax=2.2 ymin=-1 ymax=7 punti="0.5,0,pieno;0,1,pieno;1,1,pieno" titolo="The square touches the axis only at $x=\dfrac12$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The trinomial is a perfect square. For every value of $x$, the square is greater than or equal to zero, so the required comparison holds only at equality. The double root $\dfrac12$ gives a single point, rather than an interval of positive length.

To recognize the square, note that $(2x)^2=4x^2$ and that the cross term in $(2x-1)^2$ is $2\cdot2x\cdot(-1)=-4x$. Substituting $x=\dfrac12$ gives $4\left(\dfrac12\right)^2-4\cdot\dfrac12+1=1-2+1=0$. At $x=0$ and $x=1$, however, the square equals $1$, so both values are excluded. The graph touches the axis at $x=\dfrac12$ and rises on both sides. That is why replacing $\leq0$ with the strict inequality $<0$ would give an empty set.

The discriminant is $(-4)^2-4\cdot4\cdot1=0$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is positive, so the nonpositive condition can only hold at the sole zero.

</div>
