## Exercise 1

A rectangle has area $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The longer side is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ longer than the shorter one. Find the side lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(x+3)&=4,\qquad x>0,\\[6pt]
x^2+3x-4&=(x-1)(x+4)=0, \\[6pt]
x&=1,\qquad x+3=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the shorter length measured in centimeters. The information about the difference gives the longer length as $x+3$, so the area is $x(x+3)$. The algebraic roots are $1$ and $-4$. Since a length must be positive, keep $1$. The sides $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ have product $4$ and difference $3$, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has perimeter $22\,\mathrm m$. Which lengths $x$ of one side give an area of at least $24\,\mathrm{m^2}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+(11-x)&=11,\qquad 0<x<11,\\[6pt]
x(11-x)\geq24&\iff x^2-11x+24\leq0 \\[6pt]
&\iff (x-3)(x-8)\leq0, \\[6pt]
x&\in[3,8].
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x*(11-x)+0*sqrt(x*(11-x));24" xmin=-0.5 xmax=11.5 ymin=-5 ymax=35 punti="0,0,vuoto;3,24,pieno;8,24,pieno;11,0,vuoto" legenda="y=x(11-x);y=24" titolo="The portion at height $24$ or above begins at $x=3$ and ends at $x=8$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The semiperimeter gives the sum of the two lengths. If one is $x$, the other is $11-x$. Rearranging the trinomial to make the coefficient of $x^2$ positive reverses the inequality. The algebraic interval $[3,8]$ lies within the domain $0<x<11$, so all of it is admissible. The two endpoints describe the same rectangle with the sides exchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four squares of side length $x$ are cut from a $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ sheet, one at each corner. For which values of $x$ does the rectangle between the cutouts have area at most $24\,\mathrm{cm^2}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&<x<4, \\[6pt]
(10-2x)(8-2x)&\leq24 \\[6pt]
4(x-2)(x-7)&\leq0 \\[6pt]
S&=[2,7]\cap(0,4)=[2,4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each dimension of the base decreases by $x$ at each end. The two dimensions remain positive if $10-2x>0$ and $8-2x>0$. Along with positive cutout side lengths, these conditions give $0<x<4$. The quadratic inequality also allows lengths up to $7$, so intersect its solution set with the geometric domain. At $x=2$, the base measures $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and reaches exactly the specified area.

</div>
