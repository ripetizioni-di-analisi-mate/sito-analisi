## Exercise 1

A rectangle has area $4\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The longer side is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ longer than the shorter one. Find the side lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(x+3)&=4,\qquad x>0,\\[6pt]
x^2+3x-4&=(x-1)(x+4)=0, \\[6pt]
x&=1,\qquad x+3=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the shorter length measured in centimeters. The information about the difference gives the longer length as $x+3$, so the area is $x(x+3)$. The algebraic roots are $1$ and $-4$. Since a length must be positive, keep $1$. The sides $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ have product $4$ and difference $3$, as required.

In the factorization $(x-1)(x+4)$, the cross terms are $4x$ and $-x$, giving the difference of $3$ used in the equation. The root $-4$ would give lengths $-4$ and $-1$. Their product is indeed $4$, but two negative numbers cannot be side lengths of a rectangle. The value $x=1$ instead gives $x+3=4$. In a geometry problem, the choice of unknown matters. If the longer side were the unknown, the product would be $x(x-3)=4$ with $x>3$, which gives length $4$. Both models describe the same rectangle.

Checking the difference also requires the correct order. The longer side must exceed the shorter side by $3$, and the dimensions give exactly $4-1=3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has perimeter $22\,\mathrm m$. Which lengths $x$ of one side give an area of at least $24\,\mathrm{m^2}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+(11-x)&=11,\qquad 0<x<11,\\[6pt]
x(11-x)\geq24&\iff x^2-11x+24\leq0 \\[6pt]
&\iff (x-3)(x-8)\leq0, \\[6pt]
x&\in[3,8].
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x*(11-x)+0*sqrt(x*(11-x));24" xmin=-0.5 xmax=11.5 ymin=-5 ymax=35 punti="0,0,vuoto;3,24,pieno;8,24,pieno;11,0,vuoto" legenda="y=x(11-x);y=24" titolo="The portion at height $24$ or above begins at $x=3$ and ends at $x=8$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The semiperimeter gives the sum of the two lengths. If one is $x$, the other is $11-x$. Rearranging the trinomial to make the coefficient of $x^2$ positive reverses the inequality. The algebraic interval $[3,8]$ lies within the domain $0<x<11$, so all of it is admissible. The two endpoints describe the same rectangle with the sides exchanged.

The constant term $24$ and the sum $3+8=11$ identify the factors $x-3$ and $x-8$. Their product is less than or equal to zero between the two roots. At $x=3$ and $x=8$ the sides are $3$ and $8$ meters and the area is $24\,\mathrm{m^2}$. In between, for example at $x=5$, the other side is $6$ meters and the area is $30\,\mathrm{m^2}$. At $x=2$ the area is $18\,\mathrm{m^2}$, so the condition fails. The graph shows these cases using the area parabola and the threshold line. Requiring both lengths to be positive identifies the relevant segment before the comparison.

The identity $x(11-x)=\dfrac{121}{4}-\bigl(x-\dfrac{11}{2}\bigr)^2$ also explains the symmetry. The two boundary lengths are equally far from $\dfrac{11}{2}$ and give the same area.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four squares of side length $x$ are cut from a $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ sheet, one at each corner. For which values of $x$ does the rectangle between the cutouts have area at most $24\,\mathrm{cm^2}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
0&<x<4, \\[6pt]
(10-2x)(8-2x)&\leq24 \\[6pt]
4(x-2)(x-7)&\leq0 \\[6pt]
S&=[2,7]\cap(0,4)=[2,4).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(10-2*x)*(8-2*x)+0*sqrt(x*(4-x));24" legenda="y=(10-2x)(8-2x);y=24" xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.5 ymin=-10 ymax=86 punti="0,80,vuoto;2,24,pieno;4,0,vuoto" titolo="Base area on the domain $0<x<4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each dimension of the base decreases by $x$ at each end. The two dimensions remain positive if $10-2x>0$ and $8-2x>0$. Along with positive cutout side lengths, these conditions give $0<x<4$. The quadratic inequality also allows lengths up to $7$, so intersect its solution set with the geometric domain. At $x=2$, the base measures $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ by $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and reaches exactly the specified area.

Expanding the product gives $80-36x+4x^2$. Subtracting $24$ and factoring out $4$ gives $4(x^2-9x+14)$, and hence the factors $x-2$ and $x-7$. The threshold $7$ is a root of the algebraic difference, but would give dimensions $10-14=-4$ and $8-14=-6$. Their formal product is $24$, but they cannot be dimensions of the sheet. At $x=4$ the other dimension would be $2$, but the one given by $8-2x$ would be zero and the base would be degenerate. At an allowed value such as $x=3$, both lengths are positive and the area is $4\cdot2=8\,\mathrm{cm^2}$.

The graph shows the area only on the geometric domain. The threshold $24$ meets that part to the left of the zero-area point, at the cutout side length $2$.

</div>
