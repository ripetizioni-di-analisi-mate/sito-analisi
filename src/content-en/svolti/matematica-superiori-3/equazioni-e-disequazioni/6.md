## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}x^2-5x+4\leq0,\\x>2.\end{cases}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-5x+4\leq0&\iff (x-1)(x-4)\leq0 \\[6pt]
&\iff x\in[1,4], \\[6pt]
S&=[1,4]\cap(2,+\infty)=(2,4].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first condition allows the numbers from $1$ to $4$, including both endpoints. The second requires being to the right of $2$, so it removes every value of the first set up to and including $2$. The value $4$ remains because it satisfies both conditions.

Once the two sets are known, points to the left of $1$ are already excluded by the trinomial. The points in $[1,2]$ satisfy the first requirement and fail the second. To the right of $4$ the reverse holds. For example, $x=5$ satisfies $x>2$, but the trinomial equals $25-25+4=4>0$. At $x=2$ the trinomial equals $-2$ and the strict condition fails. At $x=4$ the trinomial equals $0$ and $4>2$, so the right endpoint belongs to the solution. The two endpoints differ because different conditions determine whether they are included.

The intersection can be found by taking the larger lower endpoint and the smaller upper endpoint. Here the first is $2$, from the second condition, and the second is $4$, from the first.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\begin{cases}(x+3)(x-1)\geq0,\\ x^2<16.\end{cases}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1&=(-\infty,-3]\cup[1,+\infty), \\[6pt]
S_2&=(-4,4), \\[6pt]
S&=S_1\cap S_2=(-4,-3]\cup[1,4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first inequality requires factors with the same sign or a zero factor. The second requires the distance from $x$ to zero to be less than $4$. Thus the two intervals in $S_1$ are clipped at the endpoints of $(-4,4)$. The zeros $-3$ and $1$ remain allowed, while $-4$ and $4$ fail the second inequality.

The left branch requires both $x>-4$ and $x\leq-3$, giving $(-4,-3]$. The right branch requires both $x\geq1$ and $x<4$, giving $[1,4)$. Join the results, because satisfying either branch of the first inequality is enough provided the second also holds. As a check, $x=0$ has square less than $16$ but gives $(0+3)(0-1)=-3<0$. At $x=-4$ the product is positive and $x^2=16$, so the other condition fails. The point $x=-3$ gives product zero and square $9$, and therefore belongs to the result.

For the square, factor $x^2-16=(x-4)(x+4)$ and use its negative sign between the roots. This explains why $-4$ and $4$ are excluded even when the first condition holds.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}\geq0,\\ x^2-5x+6\leq0.\end{cases}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1&=(-\infty,-2)\cup[1,+\infty), \\[6pt]
x^2-5x+6&=(x-2)(x-3),\\[6pt]
S_2&=[2,3],\\[6pt]
S&=S_1\cap S_2=[2,3].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is nonnegative when both terms are negative, or when the numerator is nonnegative and the denominator positive. The trinomial imposes the interval $[2,3]$. On this interval the numerator is at least $1$ and the denominator at least $4$, so the entire interval satisfies the first condition. The branch to the left of $-2$ is entirely excluded by the trinomial.

Splitting the two sets into intervals also shows the role of $x=-2$. That value makes the denominator zero, so it is excluded before taking the intersection. The point $x=1$ instead makes the numerator zero and satisfies the first condition. Substituting into the second gives $1-5+6=2>0$, so $1$ is not a solution of the system. At the endpoints $x=2$ and $x=3$ the trinomial is zero, while the fractions are $\dfrac14$ and $\dfrac25$, respectively. Both points are allowed. A point in either set belongs to the solution only if it also passes the other check.

To the left of $-2$, both parts of the fraction are negative. The system excludes that entire branch because no x-coordinate in $[2,3]$ can be less than $-2$.

</div>
