## Exercise 1

Solve $\begin{cases}x^2-5x+4\leq0,\\x>2.\end{cases}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-5x+4\leq0&\iff (x-1)(x-4)\leq0 \\[6pt]
&\iff x\in[1,4], \\[6pt]
S&=[1,4]\cap(2,+\infty)=(2,4].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first condition allows the numbers from $1$ to $4$, including both endpoints. The second requires being to the right of $2$, so it removes every value of the first set up to and including $2$. The value $4$ remains because it satisfies both conditions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\begin{cases}(x+3)(x-1)\geq0,\\ x^2<16.\end{cases}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1&=(-\infty,-3]\cup[1,+\infty), \\[6pt]
S_2&=(-4,4), \\[6pt]
S&=S_1\cap S_2=(-4,-3]\cup[1,4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first inequality requires factors with the same sign or a zero factor. The second requires the distance from $x$ to zero to be less than $4$. Thus the two intervals in $S_1$ are clipped at the endpoints of $(-4,4)$. The zeros $-3$ and $1$ remain allowed, while $-4$ and $4$ fail the second inequality.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\begin{cases}\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}\geq0,\\ x^2-5x+6\leq0.\end{cases}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1&=(-\infty,-2)\cup[1,+\infty), \\[6pt]
x^2-5x+6&=(x-2)(x-3),\\[6pt]
S_2&=[2,3],\\[6pt]
S&=S_1\cap S_2=[2,3].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is nonnegative when both terms are negative, or when the numerator is nonnegative and the denominator positive. The trinomial imposes the interval $[2,3]$. On this interval the numerator is at least $1$ and the denominator at least $4$, so the entire interval satisfies the first condition. The branch to the left of $-2$ is entirely excluded by the trinomial.

</div>
