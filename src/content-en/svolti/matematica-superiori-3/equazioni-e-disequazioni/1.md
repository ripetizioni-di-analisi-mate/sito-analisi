## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-5x+4=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-5x+4&=(x-1)(x-4) \\[6pt]
(x-1)(x-4)=0&\iff x=1\ \lor\ x=4 \\[6pt]
S&=\{1,4\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-5*x+4" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=8 punti="1,0,pieno;4,0,pieno;0,4,pieno" titolo="The trinomial equals zero at $x=1$ and $x=4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant term is positive, so the two numbers $r$ and $s$ in the factorization $(x-r)(x-s)$ must have the same sign. The coefficient of $x$ is $-5$, so the roots must add to $5$. Of the pairs of positive integers with product $4$, only $1$ and $4$ have sum $5$. After factoring, set each factor equal to zero in turn. The pair $2$ and $2$ would give the correct constant term but a coefficient of $x$ equal to $-4$.

Expanding the product explains the signs. The cross terms are $-4x$ and $-x$, so their sum is $-5x$. The constant term is $(-1)(-4)=4$. Direct substitution of the roots gives $1-5+4=0$ and $16-20+4=0$. The graph crosses the $x$-axis at those two x-coordinates. A positive root $r$ corresponds to the factor $x-r$. The factor $x+r$ would instead be zero at $-r$.

The leading term is $x^2$, so the coefficient of $x$ is $1$ in both factors. This is why the roots themselves supply the sum and product.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $x^2-7x+10=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-7x+10 &=x^2-2x-5x+10 \\[6pt]
&=x(x-2)-5(x-2) \\[6pt]
&=(x-5)(x-2),\qquad S=\{2,5\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $-2$ and $-5$ add to $-7$ and have product $10$, we can replace $-7x$ with $-2x-5x$. Factor out $x$ from the first two terms and $-5$ from the last two. This reveals the common factor $x-2$. Even when the factors of a polynomial can be recognized at a glance, grouping shows how they arise.

The sign in the second group matters. The term $+10$ comes from $-5\cdot(-2)$, so the common binomial really is $x-2$. The product is zero when $x-5=0$ or when $x-2=0$. At $x=5$, the first factor is zero and the second equals $3$. At $x=2$ it is the other way around, with $-3$ in the first factor and zero in the second. The equation requires the product to be zero, so the factors become zero independently. The sum $2+5=7$ and product $2\cdot5=10$ can also be checked.

The first step can be checked without expanding everything again. The terms $x(x-2)$ and $-5(x-2)$ already contain the two contributions to $-7x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2x^2+x-6=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^2+x-6&=2x^2+4x-3x-6 \\[6pt]
&=2x(x+2)-3(x+2) \\[6pt]
&=(2x-3)(x+2),\qquad S=\left\{-2,\frac32\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the coefficient of $x^2$ is $2$. Find two integers with sum $1$ and product $2\cdot(-6)=-12$ so that the same binomial can be factored from the two groups. The pair $4$ and $-3$ gives $2x(x+2)-3(x+2)$. The factor $2x-3$ gives $2x=3$, so the positive root is $\dfrac32$. The sum of the roots is indeed $-\dfrac12$.

The product $-12$ arises because, for a trinomial $ax^2+bx+c$, the coefficients of the two cross terms in the factorization have product $ac$. Here, expanding $(2x-3)(x+2)$ gives cross terms $4x$ and $-3x$, which add to $x$. Algebraically $x+2=0$ gives $x=-2$, while $2x-3=0$ gives $x=\dfrac32$. In particular, at $x=\dfrac32$ the trinomial equals $\dfrac92+\dfrac32-6=0$. The roots have product $(-2)\cdot\dfrac32=-3$, as required by the ratio of the constant term to the coefficient of $x^2$.

The discriminant independently checks the number of roots. It equals $1^2-4\cdot2\cdot(-6)=49>0$, so having two distinct solutions agrees with the factors obtained.

</div>
