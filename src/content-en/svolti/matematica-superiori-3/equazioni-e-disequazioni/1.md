## Exercise 1

Solve $x^2-5x+4=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-5x+4&=(x-1)(x-4) \\[6pt]
(x-1)(x-4)=0&\iff x=1\ \lor\ x=4 \\[6pt]
S&=\{1,4\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant term is positive, so the two numbers $r$ and $s$ in the factorization $(x-r)(x-s)$ must have the same sign. The coefficient of $x$ is $-5$, so the roots must add to $5$. Of the pairs of positive integers with product $4$, only $1$ and $4$ have sum $5$. After factoring, set each factor equal to zero in turn. The pair $2$ and $2$ would give the correct constant term but a coefficient of $x$ equal to $-4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $x^2-7x+10=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-7x+10 &=x^2-2x-5x+10 \\[6pt]
&=x(x-2)-5(x-2) \\[6pt]
&=(x-5)(x-2),\qquad S=\{2,5\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $-2$ and $-5$ add to $-7$ and have product $10$, we can replace $-7x$ with $-2x-5x$. Factor out $x$ from the first two terms and $-5$ from the last two. This reveals the common factor $x-2$. Even when the factors of a polynomial can be recognized at a glance, grouping shows how they arise.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2x^2+x-6=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^2+x-6&=2x^2+4x-3x-6 \\[6pt]
&=2x(x+2)-3(x+2) \\[6pt]
&=(2x-3)(x+2),\qquad S=\left\{-2,\frac32\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the coefficient of $x^2$ is $2$. Find two integers with sum $1$ and product $2\cdot(-6)=-12$ so that the same binomial can be factored from the two groups. The pair $4$ and $-3$ gives $2x(x+2)-3(x+2)$. The factor $2x-3$ gives $2x=3$, so the positive root is $\dfrac32$. The sum of the roots is indeed $-\dfrac12$.

</div>
