## Exercise 1

Find the roots of $y=(x-2)^2-4$ and determine where $y<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-2)^2&=4\\
x&=2\pm2\\
x_1&=0,\qquad x_2=4,\\[4pt]
(x-2)^2-4&=x(x-4)<0\quad\iff\quad x\in(0,4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square equals $4$ when the distance from $x$ to $2$ is $2$. This gives two roots, $0$ and $4$. Between the roots the first factor is positive and the second negative, so the product is negative. Outside the roots both factors have the same sign. Since the inequality is strict, the roots are excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-2x^2+4x-2\ge0$ and describe the intersection with the x-axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x^2+4x-2&=-2(x^2-2x+1)\\
&=-2(x-1)^2\le0,\\[4pt]
-2(x-1)^2\ge0&\quad\iff\quad x=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-2*(x-1)^2" xmin=-1.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-8 ymax=1.5 punti="1,0,pieno,V" titolo="The graph touches the x-axis at the vertex"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The square $(x-1)^2$ is positive to the left and right of $1$. Multiplying by $-2$ gives negative ordinates on both sides. The inequality therefore holds only where the square is zero. The parabola touches the axis at the vertex $(1,0)$ and stays below the axis elsewhere.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine which values of $q$ make $y=2x^2-8x+q$ lie strictly above the x-axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^2-8x+q
&=2(x-2)^2+q-8,\\[4pt]
\min y&=q-8,\\[4pt]
q-8>0&\quad\iff\quad q>8 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $2(x-2)^2$ is always nonnegative and equals zero at $x=2$, so $q-8$ is the minimum actually attained. For every ordinate to be positive, that minimum must be positive. When $q=8$, the graph touches the axis, while for $q<8$ the vertex is below the axis.

</div>
