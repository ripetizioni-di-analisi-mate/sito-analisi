## Exercise 1

Find the vertex of $x=(y-2)^2-1$ and its intersections with $x=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=(-1,2),\\
(y-2)^2&=3+1=4,\\
y&=2\pm2,\\
A&=(3,0),\qquad B=(3,4) .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="(t-2)^2-1,t,-1,5" legenda="x=(y-2)^2-1" xmin=-2.5 xmax=6 ymin=-1.2 ymax=5.3 segmenti="3,0,3,4;-2,2,5.5,2" punti="-1,2,pieno,V;3,0,pieno,A;3,4,pieno,B" titolo="A and B have x-coordinate 3 and heights symmetric about the axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The square is zero at $y=2$, so the smallest x-coordinate is $-1$ at ordinate $2$. Substituting $x=3$ fixes the distance of $y$ from $2$ to two units. The points share an x-coordinate, while their ordinates are symmetric about the horizontal axis $y=2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the focus and directrix of $(y+1)^2=-8(x-3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=(3,-1),\\
4p&=-8,\qquad p=-2,\\
F&=(3-2,-1)=(1,-1),\\
d&:x=3-(-2)=5 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The squared variable is $y+1$, so the axis is horizontal and passes through ordinate $-1$. The coefficient $4p$ is negative, which puts the focus to the left of the vertex and the vertical directrix two units to the right. The equation also gives $x\le3$, consistent with opening to the left.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Express the two branches of $x=-2(y-1)^2+5$ as functions of $x$ and specify their domain.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(y-1)^2&=\frac{5-x}{2},\\[4pt]
\frac{5-x}{2}&\ge0\quad\iff\quad x\le5,\\[6pt]
y_+(x)&=1+\sqrt{\frac{5-x}{2}},\\[4pt]
y_-(x)&=1-\sqrt{\frac{5-x}{2}} .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolating the square shows that the right-hand side must be nonnegative. This gives the common domain $(-\infty,5]$. For $x<5$ the root is positive, so one branch is above $y=1$ and the other below it. At $x=5$ the root is zero and the branches meet at the same vertex $(5,1)$.

</div>
