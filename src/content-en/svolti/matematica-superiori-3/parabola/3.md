## Exercise 1

Find the parabola with vertical axis and vertex $V(1,-2)$ through $P(2,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=a(x-1)^2-2,\\
1&=a(2-1)^2-2,\\
a&=3,\\
y&=3(x-1)^2-2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Vertex form incorporates the axis $x=1$ and the height $-2$, so only one unknown remains. The x-coordinate of $P$ is one unit from the axis. Its ordinate is $3$ above the vertex, so $a=3$. The point $Q(0,1)$ also lies on the curve because it is the reflection of $P$ across the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the parabola with vertical axis, roots $-2$ and $4$, and passing through $(0,4)$. Also find its vertex.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=a(x+2)(x-4),\\
4&=a(0+2)(0-4)=-8a,\\[4pt]
y&=-\frac12(x+2)(x-4),\\[4pt]
x_V&=\frac{-2+4}{2}=1,\qquad y_V=-\frac12(3)(-3)=\frac92 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The roots yield the factored form without finding three coefficients separately. The intercept at $x=0$ determines the factor $a$. The roots are on opposite sides of the axis and equally far from it, so the midpoint of their x-coordinates gives $x_V=1$. Substitution in the factored form gives the ordinate $\dfrac92$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether a parabola with vertical axis passes through $(-1,0)$, $(1,4)$ and $(3,8)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{cases}
a-b+c=0\\
a+b+c=4\\
9a+3b+c=8
\end{cases}
&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=2,\\[4pt]
a+c&=2,\qquad 9a+c=2,\\
a&=0,\qquad c=2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the equations for opposite x-coordinates eliminates $a$ and $c$. The linear coefficient is $b=2$. The remaining two equations have the same right-hand side, so subtracting gives $8a=0$. The result is the line $y=2x+2$, which passes through all three points. A parabola requires $a\ne0$, so the data are incompatible.

</div>
