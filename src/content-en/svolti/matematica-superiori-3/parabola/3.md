## Exercise 1

Find the parabola with vertical axis and vertex $V(1,-2)$ through $P(2,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=a(x-1)^2-2,\\
1&=a(2-1)^2-2,\\
a&=3,\\
y&=3(x-1)^2-2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="3*(x-1)^2-2" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=6 asintoti="x=1" punti="0,1,pieno,Q;1,-2,pieno,V;2,1,pieno,P" segmenti="0,1,2,1" titolo="P and Q have the same height and are equally far from the axis on opposite sides"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Vertex form incorporates the axis $x=1$ and the height $-2$, so only one unknown remains. The x-coordinate of $P$ is one unit from the axis. Its ordinate is $3$ above the vertex, so $a=3$. The point $Q(0,1)$ also lies on the curve because it is the reflection of $P$ across the axis.

To check the result in expanded form, calculate
$$
3(x-1)^2-2=3(x^2-2x+1)-2=3x^2-6x+1 .
$$
At $x=2$ the polynomial equals $12-12+1=1$, so it passes through the given point. At $x=1$ it equals $3-6+1=-2$, as required for the vertex. The ratio of the increase in height to the squared distance from the axis is $3$ at every point other than the vertex. The given point has nonzero distance from the axis, so it determines the ratio uniquely. Had the question given only the vertex, the same reasoning would have left $a$ free and many different parabolas would have matched the data.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the parabola with vertical axis, roots $-2$ and $4$, and passing through $(0,4)$. Also find its vertex.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=a(x+2)(x-4),\\
4&=a(0+2)(0-4)=-8a,\\[4pt]
y&=-\frac12(x+2)(x-4),\\[4pt]
x_V&=\frac{-2+4}{2}=1,\qquad y_V=-\frac12(3)(-3)=\frac92 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The roots yield the factored form without finding three coefficients separately. The intercept at $x=0$ determines the factor $a$. The roots are on opposite sides of the axis and equally far from it, so the midpoint of their x-coordinates gives $x_V=1$. Substitution in the factored form gives the ordinate $\dfrac92$.

The point $(0,4)$ is informative because $0$ is different from either given root. Had it been a root, substitution would have produced $y=0$ for every $a$ and left the coefficient undetermined. Here the product at $x=0$ is $-8$, so division by $-8$ is valid and gives a single value.

To verify the type of extreme value, rewrite the product in terms of the distance from $x=1$.
$$
-\frac12(x+2)(x-4)
=-\frac12\bigl((x-1)^2-9\bigr)
=\frac92-\frac12(x-1)^2 .
$$
Subtracting a nonnegative square shows that $\dfrac92$ is indeed the maximum. At $x=0$ the subtracted amount is $\dfrac12$, recovering $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether a parabola with vertical axis passes through $(-1,0)$, $(1,4)$ and $(3,8)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{cases}
a-b+c=0\\
a+b+c=4\\
9a+3b+c=8
\end{cases}
&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=2,\\[4pt]
a+c&=2,\qquad 9a+c=2,\\
a&=0,\qquad c=2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the equations for opposite x-coordinates eliminates $a$ and $c$. The linear coefficient is $b=2$. The remaining two equations have the same right-hand side, so subtracting gives $8a=0$. The result is the line $y=2x+2$, which passes through all three points. A parabola requires $a\ne0$, so the data are incompatible.

A geometric check uses the slopes between consecutive points. From $(-1,0)$ to $(1,4)$ the slope is $\dfrac{4-0}{1-(-1)}=2$. From $(1,4)$ to $(3,8)$ it is $\dfrac{8-4}{3-1}=2$. The slopes agree, so all three points lie on one line.

A parabola with a vertical axis can meet a nonvertical line at most twice, because the difference between their ordinate expressions is a quadratic with nonzero leading coefficient. Here the three points have distinct x-coordinates. To meet all three conditions, the difference from the line must therefore be the zero polynomial, consistent with the value $a=0$ from the system. The data determine a straight line and make a parabola impossible.

</div>
