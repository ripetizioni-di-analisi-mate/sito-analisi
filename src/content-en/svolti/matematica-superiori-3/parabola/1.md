## Exercise 1

Find the vertex and axis of $y=x^2-2x+1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=x^2-2x+1\\[4pt]
&=(x-1)^2,\\[4pt]
V&=(1,0),\qquad x=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The linear term is $-2x$, so the binomial contains $-1$ because $2x\cdot(-1)=-2x$. The constant term is already $(-1)^2=1$, so the quadratic is exactly the square of $x-1$. The square is zero at $x=1$, where $y=0$. At $x=1-s$ and $x=1+s$ the ordinate is the same, $s^2$, so the vertical line $x=1$ is the axis of symmetry.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the vertex and minimum value of $y=x^2-4x+3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=x^2-4x+4-4+3\\[4pt]
 &=(x-2)^2-1,\\[4pt]
V&=(2,-1),\qquad \min y=-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2)^2-1" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-2.5 ymax=7 punti="1,0;3,0;2,-1,pieno,V" titolo="The roots are symmetric about the vertical line through V"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The square needs the term $-2$ to produce $-4x$. But the square of $x-2$ contains the constant term $4$, so add and subtract $4$ before grouping the terms. This leaves $-1$ outside the square. Since $(x-2)^2\ge0$, the smallest ordinate is $-1$, attained only at $x=2$. The points with x-coordinates $1$ and $3$ both have ordinate zero, as the graph shows.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write $y=-3x^2+3x+1$ in vertex form and find the maximum value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=-3(x^2-x)+1\\[4pt]
 &=-3\left[\left(x-\frac12\right)^2-\frac14\right]+1\\[6pt]
 &=-3\left(x-\frac12\right)^2+\frac74,\\[6pt]
V&=\left(\frac12,\frac74\right),\qquad \max y=\frac74 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor out $-3$ before completing the square, so the linear coefficient inside is $-1$. Half that coefficient is $-\dfrac12$, and its square is $\dfrac14$. The compensating term $-\dfrac14$ is multiplied by $-3$, so it adds $\dfrac34$ to the ordinate. The square has a negative coefficient, so the vertex ordinate $\dfrac74$ is a maximum in this case.

</div>
