## Exercise 1

Find the vertex and axis of $y=x^2-2x+1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=x^2-2x+1\\[4pt]
&=(x-1)^2,\\[4pt]
V&=(1,0),\qquad x=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The linear term is $-2x$, so the binomial contains $-1$ because $2x\cdot(-1)=-2x$. The constant term is already $(-1)^2=1$, so the quadratic is exactly the square of $x-1$. The square is zero at $x=1$, where $y=0$. At $x=1-s$ and $x=1+s$ the ordinate is the same, $s^2$, so the vertical line $x=1$ is the axis of symmetry.

The axis is a line, so its description is the equation $x=1$. The vertex is a point and also needs an ordinate. To recover it directly from the original quadratic, calculate $f(1)=1^2-2\cdot1+1=0$.

To check the symmetry, take x-coordinates $0$ and $2$, both one unit from the axis. The first leaves the constant term $1$. The second gives $2^2-2\cdot2+1=1$. The point $(1,0)$ is also the only intersection with the x-axis because $(x-1)^2=0$ forces the two roots to coincide at $1$. The square stays positive on both sides, so the curve touches the axis and then moves up away from it.

To find the part of the graph with ordinate at most $1$, solve $(x-1)^2\le1$ and obtain $0\le x\le2$. The points at height $1$ mark both endpoints.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the vertex and minimum value of $y=x^2-4x+3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=x^2-4x+4-4+3\\[4pt]
 &=(x-2)^2-1,\\[4pt]
V&=(2,-1),\qquad \min y=-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2)^2-1" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-2.5 ymax=7 punti="1,0;3,0;2,-1,pieno,V" titolo="The roots are symmetric about the vertical line through V"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The square needs the term $-2$ to produce $-4x$. But the square of $x-2$ contains the constant term $4$, so add and subtract $4$ before grouping the terms. This leaves $-1$ outside the square. Since $(x-2)^2\ge0$, the smallest ordinate is $-1$, attained only at $x=2$. The points with x-coordinates $1$ and $3$ both have ordinate zero, as the graph shows.

The term $-1$ is the vertex height, so it is the signed vertical displacement from the horizontal axis. To recover the roots, set the entire ordinate to zero.
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-2)^2-1&=0,\\
(x-2)^2&=1,\\
x-2&=\pm1 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The two x-coordinates are one unit from the axis of symmetry. Check the minimum ordinate by substituting $x=2$ into the original polynomial to get $4-8+3=-1$. At $x=0$ the intercept is $3$. The two values have different roles because the first occurs at the lowest point and the second at the point with x-coordinate zero. Here the vertex lies between the two roots, as expected for a parabola with minimum below the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write $y=-3x^2+3x+1$ in vertex form and find the maximum value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=-3(x^2-x)+1\\[4pt]
 &=-3\left[\left(x-\frac12\right)^2-\frac14\right]+1\\[6pt]
 &=-3\left(x-\frac12\right)^2+\frac74,\\[6pt]
V&=\left(\frac12,\frac74\right),\qquad \max y=\frac74 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="-3*(x-0.5)^2+1.75" legenda="y=-3(x-\dfrac12)^2+\dfrac74" xmin=-1.1 xmax=2.1 ymin=-3.5 ymax=2.5 asintoti="x=0.5" punti="0,1,pieno,A;1,1,pieno,B;0.5,1.75,pieno,V" segmenti="0,1,1,1" titolo="The points at height 1 have x-coordinates symmetric about the vertical line through V"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor out $-3$ before completing the square, so the linear coefficient inside is $-1$. Half that coefficient is $-\dfrac12$, and its square is $\dfrac14$. The compensating term $-\dfrac14$ is multiplied by $-3$, so it adds $\dfrac34$ to the ordinate. The square has a negative coefficient, so the vertex ordinate $\dfrac74$ is a maximum in this case.

The expanded form provides an independent check on both vertex coordinates. The formula for the x-coordinate gives $-\dfrac{3}{2(-3)}=\dfrac12$. Substituting into the original quadratic gives the same ordinate.
$$
f\left(\frac12\right)=-\frac34+\frac32+1=\frac74 .
$$
In vertex form, the term $-3\left(x-\dfrac12\right)^2$ is always at most zero. The value $\dfrac74$ is therefore the maximum and is attained only once. At $x=0$ and $x=1$, the distance to the axis is $\dfrac12$, so subtracting $\dfrac34$ from the maximum leaves $1$ in both cases. The two points at the same height make it possible to check the position and orientation of the vertex from the diagram.

</div>
