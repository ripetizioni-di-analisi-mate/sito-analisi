## Exercise 1

Find the tangent to $y=x^2$ at the point with x-coordinate $1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-mx-q&=(x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1,\\[4pt]
m&=2,\qquad q=-1,\\
y&=2x-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tangent must give the double root $1$ in the intersection equation. Since the coefficient of $x^2$ is $1$, the difference between parabola and line must be $(x-1)^2$. Equating coefficients gives $m=2$ from the linear term and $q=-1$ from the constant term. Substitution of $x=1$ gives $y=1$ on the line, the same as on the parabola.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the tangent to $y=-x^2+4x$ parallel to $y=2x+5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2&=2(-1)t+4,\qquad t=1,\\[4pt]
y(1)&=-1+4=3,\\[4pt]
y-3&=2(x-1),\\
y&=2x+1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Parallelism fixes the slope at $2$. The slope of the tangent at x-coordinate $t$ is $-2t+4$, so $t=1$. Substituting into the parabola gives the point of tangency $(1,3)$. An algebraic check subtracts $2x+1$ from $-x^2+4x$, giving $-(x-1)^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the tangents to $y=x^2$ through $P(1,-3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3&=2t\cdot1-t^2,\\
t^2-2t-3&=(t-3)(t+1)=0,\\
t&=3,\qquad t=-1,\\[4pt]
y&=6x-9,\qquad y=-2x-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The x-coordinate $1$ of the given point is used to impose the condition of passing through $P$. The x-coordinate of tangency is the unknown $t$ in $y=2tx-t^2$. The point condition gives two values of $t$, hence two lines. Substituting $x=1$ into each gives $-3$. Subtracting the lines from $x^2$ gives $(x-3)^2$ and $(x+1)^2$, respectively.

</div>
