## Exercise 1

Find the points common to $y=x^2$ and $y=4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2&=4,\\
x&=\pm2,\\
A&=(-2,4),\qquad B=(2,4) .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal line sets the ordinate to $4$ at every point, so look for where the square equals $4$ on the parabola. Both x-coordinates $-2$ and $2$ satisfy the equation. Since their ordinates agree, the chord is horizontal and its midpoint lies on the axis of the parabola.

The number $4$ sets the height of the segment between the intersections. Check the points by substituting their x-coordinates into the parabola to get $(-2)^2=4$ and $2^2=4$. The horizontal line has height $4$ everywhere, so both pairs satisfy both equations. The chord has length $2-(-2)=4$ and midpoint $(0,4)$.

Here the line can also be classified by comparing its height with the minimum ordinate of the parabola. The minimum is $0$ and $4>0$, so there is one point on either side of the axis. The line at height zero would instead be tangent at the origin, because $x^2=0$ has a double root.

The chord's endpoints are on opposite sides of the axis. The segment joins two points on the curve, while its midpoint is at the height of the line and lies above the vertex $(0,0)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the endpoints and the length of the chord cut by $y=x+1$ on $y=x^2-1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-1&=x+1,\\
(x-2)(x+1)&=0,\\
A&=(-1,0),\qquad B=(2,3),\\[4pt]
AB&=\sqrt{(2-(-1))^2+(3-0)^2}=3\sqrt2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-1;x+1" xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.1 ymin=-2 ymax=7 punti="-1,0,pieno,A;2,3,pieno,B" segmenti="-1,0,2,0;2,0,2,3" titolo="Chord AB and its horizontal and vertical projections"}

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the endpoints, equate the two expressions for $y$. The roots give only the x-coordinates, so substitute into the line to complete each pair. The ordinates differ, so the length uses both the horizontal and vertical differences. The segment advances by $3$ in both directions and has length $3\sqrt2$.

The intersection coordinates can be checked in the second equation. Substituting $x=-1$ into $y=x^2-1$ gives $0$, and substituting $x=2$ gives $3$. The triangle formed by the chord and its two projections therefore has legs both of length $3$. The distance is the hypotenuse, so it equals $\sqrt{3^2+3^2}=\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2$.

The diagram distinguishes the difference in x-coordinates, which measures only the horizontal projection, from the actual distance between the points. The line has slope $1$, so each unit of horizontal advance gives the same vertical rise. This is why the two legs have equal length here.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Classify the position of the line $y=4x+q$ relative to $y=x^2+2$ as $q$ varies.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+2&=4x+q,\\
(x-2)^2&=q+2,\\[4pt]
&\begin{array}{c|c}
q<-2 & 0\\
q=-2 & 1\\
q>-2 & 2
\end{array}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2+2;4*x-2;4*x-1" xmin=-1 xmax=4.1 ymin=-6 ymax=13 punti="2,6,pieno,T;1,3,pieno,A;3,11,pieno,B" titolo="For q = −2 the line is tangent at T. For q = −1 the secant meets the curve at A and B"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Completing the square isolates $(x-2)^2$ so it can be compared with $q+2$. A negative number gives a line that misses the parabola. If $q=-2$, the only root $x=2$ is double and the line is tangent at $(2,6)$. If $q>-2$, the distance from $2$ is positive and gives two x-coordinates, so the line is a secant.

The threshold $q=-2$ can also be found using the discriminant of the intersection equation $x^2-4x+2-q=0$.
$$
\Delta=(-4)^2-4(2-q)=4(q+2) .
$$
The two methods give the same threshold because $4$ is positive and the discriminant has the same sign as $q+2$. For $q>-2$, the two x-coordinates are $2-\sqrt{q+2}$ and $2+\sqrt{q+2}$. For example, with $q=-1$ these are $1$ and $3$, and the ordinates are $3$ and $11$. As $q$ varies with the slope fixed at $4$, the line moves vertically and the chord endpoints remain horizontally symmetric about $2$. This differs from the parabola's own axis $x=0$.

</div>
