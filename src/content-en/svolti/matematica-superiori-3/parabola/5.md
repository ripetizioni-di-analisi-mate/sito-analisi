## Exercise 1

Find the points common to $y=x^2$ and $y=4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2&=4,\\
x&=\pm2,\\
A&=(-2,4),\qquad B=(2,4) .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal line sets the ordinate to $4$ at every point, so look for where the square equals $4$ on the parabola. Both x-coordinates $-2$ and $2$ satisfy the equation. Since their ordinates agree, the chord is horizontal and its midpoint lies on the axis of the parabola.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the endpoints and the length of the chord cut by $y=x+1$ on $y=x^2-1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-1&=x+1,\\
(x-2)(x+1)&=0,\\
A&=(-1,0),\qquad B=(2,3),\\[4pt]
AB&=\sqrt{(2-(-1))^2+(3-0)^2}=3\sqrt2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-1;x+1" xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.1 ymin=-2 ymax=7 punti="-1,0,pieno,A;2,3,pieno,B" segmenti="-1,0,2,0;2,0,2,3" titolo="Chord AB and its horizontal and vertical projections"}

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the endpoints, equate the two expressions for $y$. The roots give only the x-coordinates, so substitute into the line to complete each pair. The ordinates differ, so the length uses both the horizontal and vertical differences. The segment advances by $3$ in both directions and has length $3\sqrt2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Classify the position of the line $y=4x+q$ relative to $y=x^2+2$ as $q$ varies.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+2&=4x+q,\\
(x-2)^2&=q+2,\\[4pt]
&\begin{array}{c|c}
q<-2 & 0\\
q=-2 & 1\\
q>-2 & 2
\end{array}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Completing the square isolates $(x-2)^2$ so it can be compared with $q+2$. A negative number gives a line that misses the parabola. If $q=-2$, the only root $x=2$ is double and the line is tangent at $(2,6)$. If $q>-2$, the distance from $2$ is positive and gives two x-coordinates, so the line is a secant.

</div>
