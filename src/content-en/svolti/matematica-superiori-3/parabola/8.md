## Exercise 1

A rectangle has perimeter $24\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the dimensions that maximize its area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x)&=x(12-x),\qquad 0<x<12,\\[4pt]
&=36-(x-6)^2,\\[4pt]
A_{\max}&=36\,\mathrm{m}^2,\qquad x=6\,\mathrm{m} .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perimeter counts each side twice, so the two dimensions add to $12\,\mathrm{m}$. Choosing $x$ as one side gives $12-x$ for the other, and their product gives the area. The square subtracts a nonnegative quantity from $36$, vanishing at the permitted value $x=6$. The other side is also $6\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A $20\,\mathrm{m}$ fence encloses three sides of a rectangle against a wall. Each side perpendicular to the wall is at most $4\,\mathrm{m}$ due to available space. Find the maximum area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x)&=x(20-2x)=50-2(x-5)^2,\\
&\qquad 0<x\le4,\\[4pt]
A(4)&=50-2(4-5)^2=48\,\mathrm{m}^2,\\[4pt]
20-2\cdot4&=12\,\mathrm{m} .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fence covers two sides of length $x$ and the side parallel to the wall. The vertex of the area graph has x-coordinate $5$, but permitted values go only up to $4$. From $0$ to $4$, increasing $x$ decreases the distance $5-x$, so the area increases. The maximum occurs at the permitted endpoint, with side lengths $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and $12\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The height of a water jet, in metres, is $h(t)=5-2(t-2)^2$ at time $t$ in seconds. A camera records it for $2\le t\le3$. Find the minimum and maximum heights and the time spent at or above $4{,}5\,\mathrm{m}$ during the recording.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(2)&=5,\qquad h(3)=3,\\[4pt]
5-2(t-2)^2\ge 4{,}5
&\quad\iff\quad(t-2)^2\le\frac14,\\[4pt]
[2,3]\cap\left[\frac32,\frac52\right]
&=\left[2,\frac52\right],\\[4pt]
\frac52-2&=\frac12\,\mathrm{s} .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Over the recorded interval, $t-2$ starts at zero and increases to $1$. The term subtracted from $5$ increases, so the maximum is $5\,\mathrm{m}$ at the start and the minimum is $3\,\mathrm{m}$ at the end. The threshold of $4{,}5\,\mathrm{m}$ requires a distance of at most $\dfrac12$ from $t=2$. Times before $2$ are outside the recording, so only the following half-second counts.

</div>
