## Exercise 1

Find the vertex, focus and directrix of $y=2(x-1)^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2&=\frac12 y,\\[4pt]
4p&=\frac12,\qquad p=\frac18,\\[4pt]
V&=(1,0),\quad F=\left(1,\frac18\right),\quad d:y=-\frac18 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertex can be read from the given form because the square vanishes at $x=1$. To identify $p$, isolate the square and compare the coefficient of $y$ with $4p$. The focus is thus one eighth of a unit above the vertex, and the directrix equally far below. The coefficient of $(x-1)^2$ is positive, so the upward opening agrees with the position of the focus.

The quadratic coefficient $2$ and the parameter $p$ play different roles. The first scales the ordinates at a given horizontal distance from the axis. The parameter $p$ is a displacement along the axis and comes from comparing coefficients after isolating the square.

Use $P(2,2)$ to check the result. It is on the parabola because $2=2(2-1)^2$. Its distance from the directrix $y=-\dfrac18$ is $2+\dfrac18=\dfrac{17}{8}$. For the focus just found,
$$
PF^2=(2-1)^2+\left(2-\frac18\right)^2
=1+\frac{225}{64}=\frac{289}{64}.
$$
Taking the positive square root again gives $\dfrac{17}{8}$. The equal distances verify both the focus ordinate and that of the directrix.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the equation of the parabola with focus $F(2,-1)$ and directrix $y=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\left(2,\frac{-1+3}{2}\right)=(2,1),\\[4pt]
p&=-1-1=-2,\\[4pt]
(x-2)^2&=-8(y-1),\\[4pt]
y&=1-\frac18(x-2)^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="1-(x-2)^2/8;3" legenda="y=1-\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{8};y=3" xmin=-3 xmax=8 ymin=-3.3 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="2,1,pieno,V;2,-1,pieno,F;6,-1,pieno,P;6,3,pieno,H" segmenti="2,-1,6,-1;6,-1,6,3" titolo="The focus is on the side of the opening. At P both distances are 4"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is the perpendicular to the directrix through the focus, so it is the vertical line $x=2$. The vertex is halfway between the focus and the point where the axis meets the directrix. The displacement from the vertex to the focus is $-2$ in the y-coordinate, so $p$ is negative. The equation gives only ordinates $y\le1$, consistent with opening towards the focus.

The same equation can be derived by comparing distances from a general point. For $P(x,y)$, the squared distance to $F$ is $(x-2)^2+(y+1)^2$. The squared distance to the directrix is $(y-3)^2$. Equating them gives
$$
(x-2)^2=(y-3)^2-(y+1)^2=8-8y .
$$
This recovers the coefficient $-8$ of $y-1$. For example, at height $y=-1$ the equation gives $(x-2)^2=16$. The point $(6,-1)$ is $4$ units from the focus horizontally and $4$ from the directrix vertically. This check uses the original geometric data and confirms the curve's position away from the axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Use distances to check whether $P(4,2)$ lies on the parabola with focus $F(0,2)$ and directrix $y=-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
PF&=\sqrt{(4-0)^2+(2-2)^2}=4,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{dist}(P,d)&=|2-(-2)|=4,\\[4pt]
PF&=\operatorname{dist}(P,d) .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2/8;-2" xmin=-5 xmax=6 ymin=-3 ymax=4.3 uguale=1 segmenti="0,2,4,2;4,-2,4,2" punti="0,2,pieno,F;4,2,pieno,P;4,-2,pieno,H" titolo="The horizontal segment PF and vertical segment PH have equal length"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The segment from $P$ to the focus is horizontal, so its length is the absolute difference of the x-coordinates. The shortest segment from $P$ to the directrix is vertical and ends at $H(4,-2)$. Since the two distances both equal $4$, $P$ satisfies the definition of the locus.

The definition compares distances from the *same point* to the two objects. The segment to the focus is horizontal only because $P$ and $F$ both have height $2$. At a different height, the difference in ordinates would also enter the calculation. For example, at $(4,1)$ the distance to the focus would be $\sqrt{17}$, whereas the distance to the directrix would be $3$. The distances are unequal, so that point lies off the curve.

As an algebraic check, the given data give $V=(0,0)$ and $p=2$. The focus-directrix equation is $x^2=8y$. At the given point it gives $4^2=8\cdot2$. Checking the equation agrees with comparing the distances.

</div>
