## Exercise 1

Find the vertex, focus and directrix of $y=2(x-1)^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2&=\frac12 y,\\[4pt]
4p&=\frac12,\qquad p=\frac18,\\[4pt]
V&=(1,0),\quad F=\left(1,\frac18\right),\quad d:y=-\frac18 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertex can be read from the given form because the square vanishes at $x=1$. To identify $p$, isolate the square and compare the coefficient of $y$ with $4p$. The focus is thus one eighth of a unit above the vertex, and the directrix equally far below. The coefficient of $(x-1)^2$ is positive, so the upward opening agrees with the position of the focus.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the equation of the parabola with focus $F(2,-1)$ and directrix $y=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V&=\left(2,\frac{-1+3}{2}\right)=(2,1),\\[4pt]
p&=-1-1=-2,\\[4pt]
(x-2)^2&=-8(y-1),\\[4pt]
y&=1-\frac18(x-2)^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis is the perpendicular to the directrix through the focus, so it is the vertical line $x=2$. The vertex is halfway between the focus and the point where the axis meets the directrix. The displacement from the vertex to the focus is $-2$ in the y-coordinate, so $p$ is negative. The equation gives only ordinates $y\le1$, consistent with opening towards the focus.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Use distances to check whether $P(4,2)$ lies on the parabola with focus $F(0,2)$ and directrix $y=-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
PF&=\sqrt{(4-0)^2+(2-2)^2}=4,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{dist}(P,d)&=|2-(-2)|=4,\\[4pt]
PF&=\operatorname{dist}(P,d) .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2/8;-2" xmin=-5 xmax=6 ymin=-3 ymax=4.3 uguale=1 segmenti="0,2,4,2;4,-2,4,2" punti="0,2,pieno,F;4,2,pieno,P;4,-2,pieno,H" titolo="The horizontal segment PF and vertical segment PH have equal length"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The segment from $P$ to the focus is horizontal, so its length is the absolute difference of the x-coordinates. The shortest segment from $P$ to the directrix is vertical and ends at $H(4,-2)$. Since the two distances both equal $4$, $P$ satisfies the definition of the locus.

</div>
