## Exercise 1

A point on $\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$ is $7$ units from one focus. Find its distance from the other, and verify that the value is possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=5,\qquad c=\sqrt{25-9}=4,\\
d_1+d_2&=2a=10,\\
d_2&=10-7=3,\\
|d_1-d_2|&=4<2c=8<d_1+d_2=10.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The semi-major axis fixes the sum of the two distances. Subtract the known distance to get $3$. To check the data really describe a point, take the two foci and the point as vertices of a triangle. The side between the foci measures $8$, strictly between the difference $4$ and the sum $10$ of the other sides. A triangle thus exists with its third vertex off the focal axis. That vertex has distance sum $10$ and lies on the ellipse.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Identify the locus of points for which $PF_1+PF_2=6$, with $F_1(-3,0)$ and $F_2(3,0)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
PF_1+PF_2&\geq F_1F_2=6,\\
PF_1+PF_2=6&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\in\overline{F_1F_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\{(x,0)\mid -3\leq x\leq3\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given value equals the distance between the foci. Equality in the triangle inequality requires the point to lie between the endpoints, including the endpoints themselves. For a point outside the segment, the two legs from one focus to the other via $P$ have total length greater than $6$. In terms of semi-axes, $a=c=3$ and hence $b^2=0$. The locus reduces to a segment without dividing by the square of the semi-minor axis.

</div>
