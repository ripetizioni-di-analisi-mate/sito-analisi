## Exercise 1

A point on $\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$ is $7$ units from one focus. Find its distance from the other, and verify that the value is possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=5,\qquad c=\sqrt{25-9}=4,\\
d_1+d_2&=2a=10,\\
d_2&=10-7=3,\\
|d_1-d_2|&=4<2c=8<d_1+d_2=10.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="5*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="-4,0,pieno,F_1;4,0,pieno,F_2;2.5,2.598076211,pieno,P;2.5,-2.598076211,pieno,Q" segmenti="-4,0,2.5,2.598076211;4,0,2.5,2.598076211;2.5,-2.598076211,2.5,2.598076211" legenda="\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1" titolo="Each of $P$ and $Q$ has focal distances $7$ and $3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The semi-major axis fixes the sum of the two distances. Subtract the known distance to get $3$. To check the data really describe a point, take the two foci and the point as vertices of a triangle. The side between the foci measures $8$, strictly between the difference $4$ and the sum $10$ of the other sides. A triangle thus exists with its third vertex off the focal axis. That vertex has distance sum $10$ and lies on the ellipse.

One can also construct an explicit example. Set $F_1=(-4,0)$ and $F_2=(4,0)$ and choose $PF_1=7$ and $PF_2=3$. The difference of the squared distances is $49-9=40$. In coordinates it is $(x+4)^2-(x-4)^2=16x$. This gives $x=\dfrac52$, then $y^2=9-\left(\dfrac52-4\right)^2=\dfrac{27}{4}$. The vertical coordinates are real and opposite, with values $\pm\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2}$. The two points explicitly demonstrate feasibility and are symmetric about the focal line. At either point the lengths $7$ and $3$ give the required sum.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Identify the locus of points for which $PF_1+PF_2=6$, with $F_1(-3,0)$ and $F_2(3,0)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
PF_1+PF_2&\geq F_1F_2=6,\\
PF_1+PF_2=6&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\in\overline{F_1F_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\{(x,0)\mid -3\leq x\leq3\}.
$$

::grafico{xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="-3,0,pieno,F_1;3,0,pieno,F_2;1,0,pieno,P;1,2,pieno,Q" linee="-3,0 3,0" segmenti="-3,0,1,2;3,0,1,2" titolo="The path through $P$ has length $6$. The two-part path through $Q$ is longer."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The given value equals the distance between the foci. Equality in the triangle inequality requires the point to lie between the endpoints, including the endpoints themselves. For a point outside the segment, the two legs from one focus to the other via $P$ have total length greater than $6$. In terms of semi-axes, $a=c=3$ and hence $b^2=0$. The locus reduces to a segment without dividing by the square of the semi-minor axis.

Along the focal line the condition can be read without the theorem. For $-3\leq x\leq3$, the distances are $x+3$ and $3-x$, which add to $6$. If $x>3$, they are $x+3$ and $x-3$, with sum $2x>6$. If $x<-3$, they are $-x-3$ and $3-x$, with sum $-2x>6$. A point off the line gives a strict triangle inequality because the three points are non-collinear. These checks explain both why the entire segment belongs to the locus and why its extensions do not.

</div>
