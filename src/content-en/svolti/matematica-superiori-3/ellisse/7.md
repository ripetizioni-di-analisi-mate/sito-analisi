## Exercise 1

Find the tangent at the left-hand vertex of $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=(-4,0),\\[2pt]
\frac{-4x}{16}+\frac{0\cdot y}{9}&=1,\\[4pt]
x&=-4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand vertex is the horizontal endpoint with negative $x$-coordinate, so it is $(-4,0)$. The term containing $y$ vanishes in the tangent formula because the point has $y$-coordinate zero, leaving a vertical line. Substituting $x=-4$ into the ellipse equation gives $1+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$. The only common point therefore has $y=0$, as required for the tangent.

The vertical direction can be justified from the position of the other points of the ellipse. The vertical term is non-negative, so $\dfrac{x^2}{16}\leq1$ at every point. This gives $x\geq-4$. The line $x=-4$ leaves the whole curve in one half-plane and reaches only one point, since equality requires $y=0$. The symmetric line $x=4$ touches the right-hand vertex. A line in the same direction but with horizontal coordinate $-3$ would be secant, since the vertical term would be $1-\dfrac9{16}>0$, giving two vertical coordinates.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that $P\left(3,-\dfrac{12}{5}\right)$ lies on $\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$, and find the tangent at $P$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3^2}{25}+\frac{(-\frac{12}{5})^2}{9}
&=\frac9{25}+\frac{16}{25}=1,\\[4pt]
\frac{3x}{25}-\frac{4y}{15}&=1,\\[4pt]
9x-20y&=75.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(9*x-75)/20" curve="5*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-6 xmax=7 ymin=-6 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="3,-2.4,pieno,P" legenda="9x-20y=75;\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1" titolo="The line meets the ellipse only at $P$ and has positive slope."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum of $1$ verifies the hypothesis of the formula. In the tangent equation, divide the coordinates of the contact point by the corresponding squares of the semi-axes. In particular, its $y$-coordinate is negative, so the coefficient of $y$ is $-\dfrac4{15}$. Multiplying by $75$ clears both denominators. The point $P$ must also lie on the resulting line. Indeed, $9\cdot3-20\left(-\dfrac{12}{5}\right)=27+48=75$.

The system checks uniqueness. On the line, $y=\dfrac{9x-75}{20}$. Substituting into the ellipse and multiplying by $400$ gives $16x^2+(3x-25)^2=400$. Expanding gives $25x^2-150x+225=0$, or $25(x-3)^2=0$. This is a double root and the only possible horizontal coordinate is $3$. The line then gives vertical coordinate $-\dfrac{12}{5}$. This check distinguishes tangency from the mere fact that the point lies on the line. The slope is positive, since solving for $y$ gives coefficient $\dfrac9{20}$ for $x$.

</div>
