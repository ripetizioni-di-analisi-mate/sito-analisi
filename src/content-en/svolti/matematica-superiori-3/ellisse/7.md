## Exercise 1

Find the tangent at the left-hand vertex of $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=(-4,0),\\[2pt]
\frac{-4x}{16}+\frac{0\cdot y}{9}&=1,\\[4pt]
x&=-4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand vertex is the horizontal endpoint with negative $x$-coordinate, so it is $(-4,0)$. The term containing $y$ vanishes in the tangent formula because the point has $y$-coordinate zero, leaving a vertical line. Substituting $x=-4$ into the ellipse equation gives $1+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$. The only common point therefore has $y=0$, as required for the tangent.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that $P\left(3,-\dfrac{12}{5}\right)$ lies on $\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$, and find the tangent at $P$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3^2}{25}+\frac{(-\frac{12}{5})^2}{9}
&=\frac9{25}+\frac{16}{25}=1,\\[4pt]
\frac{3x}{25}-\frac{4y}{15}&=1,\\[4pt]
9x-20y&=75.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum of $1$ verifies the hypothesis of the formula. In the tangent equation, divide the coordinates of the contact point by the corresponding squares of the semi-axes. In particular, its $y$-coordinate is negative, so the coefficient of $y$ is $-\dfrac4{15}$. Multiplying by $75$ clears both denominators. The point $P$ must also lie on the resulting line. Indeed, $9\cdot3-20\left(-\dfrac{12}{5}\right)=27+48=75$.

</div>
