## Exercise 1

Find the four endpoints of the axes and their lengths for $\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{25}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ x^2=9\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\pm3,\\
x=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ y^2=25\ \Longrightarrow\ y=\pm5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(\pm3,0),\quad (0,\pm5),\qquad 2a=10,\quad 2b=6.
$$

::grafico{curve="3*cos(t),5*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-3,0;3,0;0,-5;0,5" legenda="\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{25}=1" titolo="The endpoints of the ellipse’s axes lie at its intersections with the coordinate axes."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal endpoints are found by setting the $y$-coordinate to zero. Their $x$-coordinates are the two opposite square roots of $9$. Likewise, set the $x$-coordinate to zero and take the two opposite square roots of $25$ to find the vertical endpoints. The vertical semi-axis measures $5$, so the major axis is vertical and has length $10$. The horizontal axis measures $6$. The figure uses the same scale on both axes so their relative lengths are visible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the directions and lengths of the axes of $4x^2+25y^2=100$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4x^2}{100}+\frac{25y^2}{100}&=1,\\[4pt]
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{4}&=1,\\[4pt]
2a=2\sqrt{25}&=10,\qquad 2b=2\sqrt4=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First divide by $100$ so the equation shows the squares of the semi-axes. The coefficient of $x^2$ was $4$, so after division the denominator of $x^2$ is $100$ divided by $4$, which is $25$. Since $25>4$, the semi-major axis is horizontal. The square roots of the denominators give the semi-axes and doubling them gives the axes. Check the result at the endpoints. The point $(5,0)$ gives $4\cdot25=100$ and $(0,2)$ gives $25\cdot4=100$.

</div>
