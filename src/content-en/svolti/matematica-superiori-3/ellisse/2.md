## Exercise 1

Find the foci and the distance between them for $\dfrac{x^2}{36}+\dfrac{y^2}{20}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2&=36,\qquad b^2=20,\\
c^2&=a^2-b^2=36-20=16,\\
c&=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_1=(-4,0),\qquad F_2=(4,0),\qquad F_1F_2=8.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The larger denominator is under $x^2$, so the major axis and the two foci are horizontal. Subtract the squares of the semi-axes and take the positive square root to get the distance from each focus to the centre. The foci are symmetric about the origin, so the distance between them is $2c=8$. As a check, the vertex $(6,0)$ is $10$ and $2$ units from the two foci. Their sum $12$ equals the length of the major axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ellipse is centred at the origin with a vertical major axis of length $14$ and a minor axis of length $2\sqrt{24}$. Find the foci and the sum of the distances from any point on the ellipse to the foci.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{14}{2}=7,\qquad b=\sqrt{24},\\
c&=\sqrt{7^2-24}=\sqrt{25}=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_1=(0,-5),\qquad F_2=(0,5),\qquad PF_1+PF_2=14 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given lengths are for the full axes, so halve them before applying $c^2=a^2-b^2$. The value $c=5$ is a distance from the centre, so the two foci have opposite $y$-coordinates. Both $x$-coordinates are zero because the major axis is vertical. The sum of the focal distances is $2a=14$. At the upper vertex $(0,7)$, the distances $12$ and $2$ provide a direct check.

</div>
