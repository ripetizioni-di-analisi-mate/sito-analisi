## Exercise 1

Find the foci and the distance between them for $\dfrac{x^2}{36}+\dfrac{y^2}{20}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2&=36,\qquad b^2=20,\\
c^2&=a^2-b^2=36-20=16,\\
c&=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_1=(-4,0),\qquad F_2=(4,0),\qquad F_1F_2=8.
$$

::grafico{curve="6*cos(t),sqrt(20)*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-7 xmax=7 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-4,0,pieno,F_1;4,0,pieno,F_2;0,0,pieno,C;0,4.472135955,pieno,P" segmenti="-4,0,0,4.472135955;4,0,0,4.472135955" legenda="\dfrac{x^2}{36}+\dfrac{y^2}{20}=1" titolo="Both foci are $6$ units from $P$. They are $8$ units apart."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The larger denominator is under $x^2$, so the major axis and the two foci are horizontal. Subtract the squares of the semi-axes and take the positive square root to get the distance from each focus to the centre. The foci are symmetric about the origin, so the distance between them is $2c=8$. As a check, the vertex $(6,0)$ is $10$ and $2$ units from the two foci. Their sum $12$ equals the length of the major axis.

Another check uses a minor-axis endpoint, for example $(0,\sqrt{20})$. That point is equally distant from both foci by symmetry about the vertical line, and its triangle with the centre has legs of lengths $\sqrt{20}$ and $4$. The distance to a focus is therefore $\sqrt{20+16}=6$. This equals $a$ because the two equal distances must add to $2a$. The triangle also shows why the formula starts from $a^2-b^2$. Both $a$ and $b$ are half-lengths, while $c$ is the horizontal leg. The focal separation is twice that leg.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ellipse is centred at the origin with a vertical major axis of length $14$ and a minor axis of length $2\sqrt{24}$. Find the foci and the sum of the distances from any point on the ellipse to the foci.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\frac{14}{2}=7,\qquad b=\sqrt{24},\\
c&=\sqrt{7^2-24}=\sqrt{25}=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_1=(0,-5),\qquad F_2=(0,5),\qquad PF_1+PF_2=14 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given lengths are for the full axes, so halve them before applying $c^2=a^2-b^2$. The value $c=5$ is a distance from the centre, so the two foci have opposite $y$-coordinates. Both $x$-coordinates are zero because the major axis is vertical. The sum of the focal distances is $2a=14$. At the upper vertex $(0,7)$, the distances $12$ and $2$ provide a direct check.

The corresponding equation is $\dfrac{x^2}{24}+\dfrac{y^2}{49}=1$. The denominator $49$ goes under the vertical term because the given length $14$ refers to the full vertical segment. The sum can also be checked at the horizontal endpoint $(\sqrt{24},0)$. Its displacement to either focus has horizontal component of magnitude $\sqrt{24}$ and vertical component of magnitude $5$. Both distances are therefore $\sqrt{24+25}=7$, half the sum. Moving from the vertical endpoint to the horizontal one changes the individual distances from $12$ and $2$ to $7$ and $7$, while their sum remains $14$.

</div>
