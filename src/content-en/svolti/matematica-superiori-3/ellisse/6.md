## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the ellipse with centre at the origin, major-axis vertex $(5,0)$ and focus $(4,0)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=5,\qquad c=4,\\
b^2&=a^2-c^2=25-16=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The major-axis vertex and the focus are both on the $x$-axis, so the semi-major axis is horizontal. Their distances from the centre have different meanings. The vertex gives $a$, while the focus gives $c$. The relation $a^2=b^2+c^2$ supplies the missing vertical measurement. As a check, $(0,3)$ is $5$ units from each focus $(\pm4,0)$, so the sum equals $2a=10$.

The triangle's lengths can also be obtained from the definition, without assuming the relation among the semi-axes. On the minor axis, $P=(0,b)$ is equally distant from the foci. At vertex $(5,0)$ the focal sum is $9+1=10$. Hence $PF_1=PF_2=5$. The distance formula gives $16+b^2=25$, so $b^2=9$. This recovers the vertical denominator directly from the locus. The focal value $4$ is thus a coordinate on the major axis and must be distinguished from the vertical half-length $3$ and the semi-major axis $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the ellipse with foci $(-2,-1)$ and $(-2,7)$ for which the sum of the distances to the foci is $10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=\left(-2,\frac{-1+7}{2}\right)=(-2,3),\\[4pt]
c&=\frac{7-(-1)}2=4,\qquad a=\frac{10}2=5,\\[4pt]
b^2&=25-16=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{(x+2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y-3)^2}{25}=1.
$$

::grafico{curve="-2+3*cos(t),3+5*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-7 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=9 uguale=1 punti="-2,3,pieno,C;-2,-1,pieno,F_1;-2,7,pieno,F_2;1,3,pieno,Q;-2,8,pieno,V" segmenti="-2,-1,1,3;-2,7,1,3" legenda="\dfrac{(x+2)^2}{9}+\dfrac{(y-3)^2}{25}=1" titolo="The minor-axis endpoint $Q$ is equidistant from the foci. Vertex $V$ lies above both foci."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The foci have the same $x$-coordinate, so their line is vertical and contains the major axis. Their midpoint is the centre. The distance $8$ between the foci gives $2c$, while the given sum $10$ gives $2a$. Since $5>4$, the semi-minor axis is positive. The denominator $25$ belongs under $(y-3)^2$. At the upper vertex $(-2,8)$, the distances to the foci are $9$ and $1$, which verifies the given sum.

The horizontal part of the equation can also be checked with the focal distance sum. The right endpoint has horizontal coordinate $-2+3=1$ and vertical coordinate $3$. From $(1,3)$ to either focus, the horizontal difference has magnitude $3$ and the vertical difference magnitude $4$. The Pythagorean theorem gives $5$ for each distance and $10$ for their sum. Checking both the upper vertex and a minor-axis endpoint verifies the two directions separately. Switching the denominators would no longer make the first term equal to $1$ at $(1,3)$.

</div>
