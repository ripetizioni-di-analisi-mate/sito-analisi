## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the ellipse with centre at the origin, major-axis vertex $(5,0)$ and focus $(4,0)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=5,\qquad c=4,\\
b^2&=a^2-c^2=25-16=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The major-axis vertex and the focus are both on the $x$-axis, so the semi-major axis is horizontal. Their distances from the centre have different meanings. The vertex gives $a$, while the focus gives $c$. The relation $a^2=b^2+c^2$ supplies the missing vertical measurement. As a check, $(0,3)$ is $5$ units from each focus $(\pm4,0)$, so the sum equals $2a=10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the ellipse with foci $(-2,-1)$ and $(-2,7)$ for which the sum of the distances to the foci is $10$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=\left(-2,\frac{-1+7}{2}\right)=(-2,3),\\[4pt]
c&=\frac{7-(-1)}2=4,\qquad a=\frac{10}2=5,\\[4pt]
b^2&=25-16=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{(x+2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y-3)^2}{25}=1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The foci have the same $x$-coordinate, so their line is vertical and contains the major axis. Their midpoint is the centre. The distance $8$ between the foci gives $2c$, while the given sum $10$ gives $2a$. Since $5>4$, the semi-minor axis is positive. The denominator $25$ belongs under $(y-3)^2$. At the upper vertex $(-2,8)$, the distances to the foci are $9$ and $1$, which verifies the given sum.

</div>
