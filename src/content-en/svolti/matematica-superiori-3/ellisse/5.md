## Exercise 1

Read off the centre and foci of $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{4}+\dfrac{(y+1)^2}{16}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=(2,-1),\\
c&=\sqrt{16-4}=2\sqrt3,\\
F_\pm&=(2,-1\pm2\sqrt3).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The centre makes both differences $x-2$ and $y+1$ zero. The larger denominator is under the square containing $y$, so the foci are $c$ units above and below the centre. Their $x$-coordinate remains $2$. Since $2\sqrt3<4$, both foci have $y$-coordinates between those of the vertical vertices, which are $-1-4=-5$ and $-1+4=3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the centre, major-axis vertices and foci of $4x^2+9y^2-16x+18y-11=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(x^2-4x)+9(y^2+2y)&=11,\\
4(x-2)^2+9(y+1)^2&=11+16+9=36,\\[4pt]
\frac{(x-2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+1)^2}{4}&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{gathered}
C=(2,-1), \quad V_1=(-1,-1), \quad V_2=(5,-1),\\
F_\pm=(2\pm\sqrt5,-1).
\end{gathered}
$$

::grafico{curve="2+3*cos(t),-1+2*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="2,-1,pieno,C;-1,-1,pieno,V_1;5,-1,pieno,V_2" legenda="\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{9}+\dfrac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1" titolo="The major-axis vertices lie on the line with $y$-coordinate $-1$, which passes through the centre $C$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor out the coefficients $4$ and $9$ before completing the squares. The term added to the right for the square in $x$ is therefore $4\cdot4=16$, while the term for $y$ is $9\cdot1=9$. Dividing by $36$ gives the translated form with horizontal semi-major axis $3$ and vertical semi-axis $2$. From the centre add and subtract $3$ to find the vertices and $\sqrt{9-4}=\sqrt5$ to find the foci. All four points have $y$-coordinate $-1$.

</div>
