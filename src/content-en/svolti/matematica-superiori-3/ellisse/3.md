## Exercise 1

Find the eccentricity of $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{25}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=5,\qquad b=4,\\
c&=\sqrt{25-16}=3,\\
e&=\frac{c}{a}=\frac35.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The semi-major axis is vertical because the denominator $25$ is under $y^2$. The letter $a$ therefore denotes $5$. Find $c=3$ from the difference of the squares and divide it by $a$. The ratio has no units, so it describes the shape independently of the unit of length. Rotating the ellipse by a right angle would leave its semi-axes at $5$ and $4$, with the same eccentricity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two horizontal ellipses have the same semi-minor axis $b=4$ and eccentricities $\dfrac35$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$. Find their horizontal widths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^2&=a^2(1-e^2),\\
a_1^2&=\frac{16}{1-\frac{9}{25}}=25,\\[4pt]
a_2^2&=\frac{16}{1-\frac34}=64.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
2a_1=10,\qquad 2a_2=16.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the semi-minor axis is fixed, isolate $a^2$ in the relation $b^2=a^2(1-e^2)$. The factor $1-e^2$ is positive in both cases, so we can divide by it. For the first ellipse it is $\dfrac{16}{25}$ and for the second it is $\dfrac14$. A smaller factor requires a larger semi-major axis to preserve the height. The second ellipse is $16$ units wide and $8$ high, while the first is $10$ units wide and $8$ high.

</div>
