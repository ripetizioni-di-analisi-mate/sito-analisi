## Exercise 1

Find the zeros, the $y$-intercept and the intervals on which $f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x+3}$ is positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_f&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-3\},\\[4pt]
x-1=0&\iff x=1\in D_f,\\[4pt]
f(0)&=\frac{-1}{3},\\[4pt]
f(x)>0&\iff x\in(-\infty,-3)\cup(1,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is zero at $-3$, so this input is excluded. The numerator is zero at $1$, which belongs to the domain and gives the intercept $(1,0)$. At $0$ the value is $-\dfrac{1}{3}$, so the vertical intercept is $\left(0,-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)$. To determine the sign, use $-3$ and $1$ as the interval endpoints. To the left of $-3$ both factors are negative. Between $-3$ and $1$ they have opposite signs. To the right of $1$ both are positive. The quotient is positive on the first and third intervals.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the inputs at which $f(x)=\dfrac{(x-2)(x+1)}{x-2}$ is zero or positive, and identify the missing point on the graph.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_f&=\mathbb R\setminus\{2\},\\[4pt]
f(x)&=x+1\qquad(x\neq 2),\\[4pt]
f(x)=0&\iff x=-1,\\[4pt]
f(x)>0&\iff x\in(-1,2)\cup(2,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
\qquad P=(2,3) .
$$

::grafico{f="x+1" xmin=-4 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=7 punti="-1,0;2,3,vuoto" titolo="The zero $-1$ is in the domain and the input $2$ is excluded."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $x-2$ can be cancelled because it is nonzero throughout the domain. The sign is then the sign of $x+1$, with a zero at $-1$ and positive values at inputs greater than $-1$. Those inputs include $2$, which is still excluded by the original formula. The line $y=x+1$ would have vertical coordinate $3$ at $2$, so the point to leave out of the graph of the function is $(2,3)$.

</div>
