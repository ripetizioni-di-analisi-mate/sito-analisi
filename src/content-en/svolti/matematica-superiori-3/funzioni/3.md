## Exercise 1

Find the zeros, the $y$-intercept and the intervals on which $f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x+3}$ is positive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_f&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-3\},\\[4pt]
x-1=0&\iff x=1\in D_f,\\[4pt]
f(0)&=\frac{-1}{3},\\[4pt]
f(x)>0&\iff x\in(-\infty,-3)\cup(1,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
f(-4)=\frac{-5}{-1}=5,\qquad f(2)=\frac{1}{5}>0 .
$$

::grafico{f="(x-1)/(x+3)" xmin=-8 xmax=6 ymin=-5 ymax=5 punti="1,0;0,-0.333333" asintoti="x=-3" titolo="The outputs are negative between the excluded input and the zero."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is zero at $-3$, so this input is excluded. The numerator is zero at $1$, which belongs to the domain and gives the intercept $(1,0)$. At $0$ the value is $-\dfrac{1}{3}$, so the vertical intercept is $\left(0,-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)$. To determine the sign, use $-3$ and $1$ as the interval endpoints. To the left of $-3$ both factors are negative. Between $-3$ and $1$ they have opposite signs. To the right of $1$ both are positive. The quotient is positive on the first and third intervals.

A direct check is to evaluate the quotient once in each interval. The sign is constant within each interval because the two linear factors cross zero only at the marked boundaries. In particular, $x=0$ gives a negative result, consistent with the vertical intercept lying below the horizontal axis. The sign of a quotient agrees with the sign of the product of its factors, since dividing by a negative number and multiplying by it both reverse the sign. This comparison applies only where the denominator is nonzero, so $-3$ remains outside each solution set.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the inputs at which $f(x)=\dfrac{(x-2)(x+1)}{x-2}$ is zero or positive, and identify the missing point on the graph.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_f&=\mathbb R\setminus\{2\},\\[4pt]
f(x)&=x+1\qquad(x\neq 2),\\[4pt]
f(x)=0&\iff x=-1,\\[4pt]
f(x)>0&\iff x\in(-1,2)\cup(2,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
\qquad P=(2,3) .
$$

::grafico{f="x+1" xmin=-4 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=7 punti="-1,0;2,3,vuoto" titolo="The zero $-1$ is in the domain and the input $2$ is excluded."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $x-2$ can be cancelled because it is nonzero throughout the domain. The sign is then the sign of $x+1$, with a zero at $-1$ and positive values at inputs greater than $-1$. Those inputs include $2$, which is still excluded by the original formula. The line $y=x+1$ would have vertical coordinate $3$ at $2$, so the point to leave out of the graph of the function is $(2,3)$.

Check the difference between a zero and the missing point in the original formula. At $x=-1$, the factor $x+1$ is zero while the denominator is $-3$. At $x=2$, the common factor is zero in the numerator and denominator, so the quotient would involve division by zero. It is the original rule that determines which points of the line describe the function. In particular, the sign stays positive just before and just after $2$. The open point splits the positive inputs into two intervals because the function is undefined at that single input, even though the extended line is above the horizontal axis.

</div>
