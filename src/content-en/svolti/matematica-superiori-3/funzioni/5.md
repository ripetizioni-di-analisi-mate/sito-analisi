## Exercise 1

Given $f(x)=2x+1$ and $g(x)=x^2+1$, find both compositions on $\mathbb R$ and compare their values at $x=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)(x)&=g(2x+1)\\[4pt]
&=(2x+1)^2+1=4x^2+4x+2,\\[4pt]
(f\circ g)(x)&=f(x^2+1)\\[4pt]
&=2(x^2+1)+1=2x^2+3 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)(0)&=2,\\[4pt]
(f\circ g)(0)&=3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="4*x^2+4*x+2;2*x^2+3" legenda="y=(g\circ f)(x);y=(f\circ g)(x)" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=12 punti="0,2;0,3" titolo="At $x=0$, the compositions have outputs $2$ and $3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

In $g\circ f$, first evaluate $2x+1$. This entire value becomes the argument of $g$, so square it before adding $1$. In the reverse composition, double the value $x^2+1$ and then add $1$. Both compositions are defined at every real number because the two rules are polynomials. At $x=0$, the first gives $2$ and the second gives $3$. This one input is sufficient to show that the compositions differ.

Check the first expansion using $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$ with $a=2x$ and $b=1$. The linear term is then $4x$, and the constant is $1$ before the final $1$ is added. In the reverse order, the factor $2$ applies both to $x^2$ and to the inner constant $1$, giving $2x^2+2+1$. Comparing at $x=0$ distinguishes the functions without checking every input in their common domain. Two different functions can still agree at individual inputs. Here equality requires $2x^2+4x-1=0$, which is equivalent to $(x+1)^2=\dfrac32$. The two solutions are $-1\pm\sqrt{\dfrac32}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f(x)=\sqrt{x-2}$ and $g(t)=\dfrac{1}{t-2}$. Find the formula and domain of $g\circ f$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)(x)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x-2}-2},\\[6pt]
x-2\geq 0&\iff x\geq 2,\\[4pt]
\sqrt{x-2}=2&\iff x-2=4\\[4pt]
&\iff x=6 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad D_{g\circ f}=[2,6)\cup(6,+\infty) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inner function has a value for $x\geq 2$. The outer function excludes the input $2$, so find the inputs at which $f$ returns exactly $2$. Because $\sqrt{x-2}$ and $2$ are both nonnegative, squaring preserves equivalence and gives $x=6$. Remove this input from $[2,+\infty)$. At $x=2$, the inner function gives $0$, which is an allowed input for $g$, so the endpoint $2$ lies in the domain of the composition.

The numbers $2$ and $6$ play different roles. The first is forbidden as an input to $g$, whereas the second is the input that produces it under $f$. An input such as $x=5$ belongs to the domain because $f(5)=\sqrt3<2$ and negative denominators are allowed. At $x=7$, however, $f(7)=\sqrt5>2$, so the denominator is positive. At $6$ the denominator is zero and no output is assigned. Checking these three cases shows why the inner threshold removes one point from the composition's domain and why both neighbouring intervals contain allowed inputs.

</div>
