## Exercise 1

Given $f(x)=2x+1$ and $g(x)=x^2+1$, find both compositions on $\mathbb R$ and compare their values at $x=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)(x)&=g(2x+1)\\[4pt]
&=(2x+1)^2+1=4x^2+4x+2,\\[4pt]
(f\circ g)(x)&=f(x^2+1)\\[4pt]
&=2(x^2+1)+1=2x^2+3 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)(0)&=2,\\[4pt]
(f\circ g)(0)&=3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In $g\circ f$, first evaluate $2x+1$. This entire value becomes the argument of $g$, so square it before adding $1$. In the reverse composition, double the value $x^2+1$ and then add $1$. Both compositions are defined at every real number because the two rules are polynomials. At $x=0$, the first gives $2$ and the second gives $3$. This one input is sufficient to show that the compositions differ.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f(x)=\sqrt{x-2}$ and $g(t)=\dfrac{1}{t-2}$. Find the formula and domain of $g\circ f$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(g\circ f)(x)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x-2}-2},\\[6pt]
x-2\geq 0&\iff x\geq 2,\\[4pt]
\sqrt{x-2}=2&\iff x-2=4\\[4pt]
&\iff x=6 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad D_{g\circ f}=[2,6)\cup(6,+\infty) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inner function has a value for $x\geq 2$. The outer function excludes the input $2$, so find the inputs at which $f$ returns exactly $2$. Because $\sqrt{x-2}$ and $2$ are both nonnegative, squaring preserves equivalence and gives $x=6$. Remove this input from $[2,+\infty)$. At $x=2$, the inner function gives $0$, which is an allowed input for $g$, so the endpoint $2$ lies in the domain of the composition.

</div>
