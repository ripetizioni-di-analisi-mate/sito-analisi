## Exercise 1

Determine whether $f(x)=x^4+x^2+1$ is even or odd on $\mathbb R$ and state the symmetry of the graph.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-x)&=(-x)^4+(-x)^2+1\\[4pt]
&=x^4+x^2+1=f(x),\\[4pt]
f(0)&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rule is defined on the whole real line. Therefore, whenever $x$ is allowed, so is $-x$. Substituting $-x$ into the two even powers removes the negative sign in both terms, while the constant remains $1$. Opposite inputs give the same output. This makes the function **even**, and its graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis. In particular, $f(0)=1$. An odd function defined at $0$ would have value $0$ there.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the symmetry of $g(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2-4}$ on its real domain.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-4=0&\iff x\in\{-2,2\},\\[4pt]
D_g&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,2\},\\[4pt]
g(-x)&=\frac{-x}{(-x)^2-4}\\[4pt]
&=-\frac{x}{x^2-4}=-g(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x/(x^2-4)" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 asintoti="x=-2;x=2" punti="1,-0.333333,pieno,P;-1,0.333333,pieno,Q" titolo="The points $P$ and $Q$ are symmetric about the origin."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator excludes both $2$ and $-2$. Therefore the opposite of each allowed input is still in the domain. Under the substitution $x\mapsto -x$, the denominator stays the same because it contains only a square and a constant. The numerator changes sign, so the entire quotient changes sign. The function is **odd**. If the graph contains $(x,g(x))$, it therefore also contains $(-x,-g(x))$, the point reflected through the origin.

</div>
