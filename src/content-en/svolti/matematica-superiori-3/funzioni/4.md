## Exercise 1

Determine whether $f(x)=x^4+x^2+1$ is even or odd on $\mathbb R$ and state the symmetry of the graph.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-x)&=(-x)^4+(-x)^2+1\\[4pt]
&=x^4+x^2+1=f(x),\\[4pt]
f(0)&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^4+x^2+1" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=9 punti="-1,3,pieno,A;1,3,pieno,B;0,1" segmenti="-1,3,1,3" titolo="Points $A$ and $B$ share an output and have opposite inputs."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The rule is defined on the whole real line. Therefore, whenever $x$ is allowed, so is $-x$. Substituting $-x$ into the two even powers removes the negative sign in both terms, while the constant remains $1$. Opposite inputs give the same output. This makes the function **even**, and its graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis. In particular, $f(0)=1$. An odd function defined at $0$ would have value $0$ there.

An illustrative numerical check uses $x=\pm1$, where both outputs equal $1+1+1=3$. On the graph the two points have the same height and the same distance from the vertical axis. That check alone would concern just one pair. The proof instead makes the same substitution for arbitrary $x$, so it holds for every pair of opposite inputs. The test using $f(0)$ rules out oddness here because zero belongs to the domain and equals its own opposite. An odd function's output there would have to be its own negative. The constant $1$ makes that condition impossible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the symmetry of $g(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2-4}$ on its real domain.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-4=0&\iff x\in\{-2,2\},\\[4pt]
D_g&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,2\},\\[4pt]
g(-x)&=\frac{-x}{(-x)^2-4}\\[4pt]
&=-\frac{x}{x^2-4}=-g(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x/(x^2-4)" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 asintoti="x=-2;x=2" punti="1,-0.333333,pieno,P;-1,0.333333,pieno,Q" titolo="The points $P$ and $Q$ are symmetric about the origin."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator excludes both $2$ and $-2$. Therefore the opposite of each allowed input is still in the domain. Under the substitution $x\mapsto -x$, the denominator stays the same because it contains only a square and a constant. The numerator changes sign, so the entire quotient changes sign. The function is **odd**. If the graph contains $(x,g(x))$, it therefore also contains $(-x,-g(x))$, the point reflected through the origin.

Here the excluded inputs form a pair of opposite coordinates. If just one of them were excluded, the comparison of $g(-x)$ and $g(x)$ might be undefined at some allowed inputs even with the same formula. To check the signs, at $x=1$ the denominator is $-3$ and the numerator is $1$, giving $g(1)=-\dfrac13$. At $x=-1$ the same denominator divides the numerator $-1$, giving $g(-1)=\dfrac13$. Reflection through the origin exchanges these two points. Since the two outputs are opposite and nonzero, this pair also rules out evenness.

</div>
