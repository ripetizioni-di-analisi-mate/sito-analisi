## Exercise 1

Evaluate $f(-2)$ for $f(x)=x^2-2x+1$. State the coordinates of the point on the graph with horizontal coordinate $-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-2)&=(-2)^2-2(-2)+1\\[5pt]
&=4+4+1\\[5pt]
&=9 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad P=(-2,9) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The required point has a given horizontal coordinate. To find its vertical coordinate, substitute $-2$ for both occurrences of $x$. The square is $4$ because it multiplies $-2$ by itself. The linear term is also $4$ because the coefficient $-2$ multiplies the number $-2$. Adding these terms and the constant gives $9$. At $P=(-2,9)$, the value $-2$ is the input and $9$ is the image.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f$ be defined on $[-2,4]$ by $f(x)=x+2$ for $x<1$ and $f(x)=4-x$ for $x\geq 1$. Evaluate $f(-1)$ and $f(1)$, then find the preimages of $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-1)&=-1+2=1,\\[5pt]
f(1)&=4-1=3.\\[5pt]
x+2=2&\iff x=0\in[-2,1),\\[5pt]
4-x=2&\iff x=2\in[1,4].
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\{x\mid f(x)=2\}=\{0,2\} .
$$

::grafico{curve="t,t+2,-2,1;t,4-t,1,4" legenda="y=x+2;y=4-x" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=4 segmenti="-2,2,4,2" punti="0,2,pieno,A;2,2,pieno,B;1,3" titolo="The line $y=2$ meets the graph at $A$ and $B$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

At $-1$, use the rule $x+2$ because $-1<1$. At the boundary $x=1$, the second rule applies because it includes equality. To find the preimages of $2$, work backwards. Once the output is fixed at $2$, each piece yields a candidate. The value $0$ belongs to the first interval, whereas $2$ belongs to the second. Both are valid. In the graph, the horizontal line $y=2$ meets the two pieces exactly at $(0,2)$ and $(2,2)$.

</div>
