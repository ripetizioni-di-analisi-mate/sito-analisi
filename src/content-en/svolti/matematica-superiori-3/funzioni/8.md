## Exercise 1

Find the range of $f(x)=(x-2)^2+1$ on $\mathbb R$ and state where its minimum is attained.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-2)^2&\geq 0,\\[4pt]
f(x)&\geq 1,\\[4pt]
f(2)&=1,\\[4pt]
f(2+\sqrt{y-1})&=y\qquad(y\geq 1).
\end{aligned}
\qquad f(\mathbb R)=[1,+\infty) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quadratic term is the square of the distance between $x$ and $2$. Since this distance is always nonnegative, the smallest value occurs when the distance is zero, at $x=2$. The output there is $1$. To show that the range contains *every* value from $1$ upwards, fix $y\geq 1$ and take the input $2+\sqrt{y-1}$. The square is then $y-1$, so the function takes the value $y$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the range, maximum and minimum, where they exist, of $g(x)=5-(x+1)^2$ on $(-3,0]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3<x\leq 0&\Longrightarrow -2<x+1\leq 1,\\[4pt]
0\leq(x+1)^2<4&\Longrightarrow 1<g(x)\leq 5,\\[4pt]
g(-1)&=5,\\[4pt]
g(-1-\sqrt{5-y})&=y\qquad(1<y\leq 5).
\end{aligned}
\qquad g((-3,0])=(1,5] .
$$

::grafico{curve="t,5-(t+1)^2,-3,0" legenda="y=5-(x+1)^2" xmin=-4 xmax=1 ymin=0 ymax=6 punti="-3,1,vuoto;-1,5,pieno,V;0,4" titolo="The point with vertical coordinate $1$ is open, while the vertex is included."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance from $-1$ is less than $2$ on the domain and is zero at $x=-1$. The negative sign in front of the square makes zero distance give the maximum value $5$. As $x$ approaches $-3$ from the right, the square approaches $4$, so the outputs approach $1$. The value $1$ would require a distance of exactly $2$, which never occurs on the domain. Every output $y$ with $1<y\leq 5$ is attained on the left side of the vertex, at the input $-1-\sqrt{5-y}$. Thus there is no minimum.

</div>
