## Exercise 1

Find the real domain of $f(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x-1}}{x-4}$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x-1\geq 0\\
x-4\neq 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x\geq 1\\
x\neq 4
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_f=[1,4)\cup(4,+\infty) .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned} f(2)&=\frac{\sqrt{2-1}}{2-4}=-\frac12,\\[4pt] f(5)&=\frac{\sqrt{5-1}}{5-4}=2 .\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(x-1)/(x-4)" legenda="y=\dfrac{\sqrt{x-1}}{x-4}" xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-4 ymax=4 punti="1,0" asintoti="x=4" titolo="The graph begins at $x=1$, and input $4$ is excluded."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The radicand $x-1$ may be zero because the square root is in the numerator. The condition $x\geq 1$ therefore gives the interval $[1,+\infty)$. Remove $4$ from that same interval because it makes the denominator zero. Removing an interior point splits the interval into two pieces that are open at that point. The endpoint $1$ is still included because the numerator is zero there and the denominator is $-3$. In particular, $f(1)=0$.

To read this result on the number line, first mark the half-line starting at $1$, including that endpoint. Then remove the one point with coordinate $4$. For example, at $x=0$ the denominator would be nonzero, but the radicand would be $-1$. At $x=2$, both the square root exists and the denominator is nonzero, so the quotient is $-\dfrac12$. At $x=5$ both operations again exist, giving $2$. These two check values lie on opposite sides of the excluded point. The domain concerns whether the quotient is defined, and it includes inputs with positive and negative outputs.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the real domain of $g(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{4-x}}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4-x\geq 0 &\iff x\leq 4,\\[4pt]
x^2-1>0 &\iff (x-1)(x+1)>0\\[4pt]
&\iff x\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
\qquad D_g=(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,4] .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two square roots have different roles. The numerator only requires $4-x\geq 0$. The square root in the denominator must exist and be nonzero, so impose $x^2-1>0$. The product $(x-1)(x+1)$ is positive when both factors are negative or when both are positive. This yields two intervals, from which we keep only the inputs satisfying $x\leq 4$. The point $4$ is allowed because it makes only the numerator zero, whereas $-1$ and $1$ make the denominator zero.

Checking the endpoints distinguishes a zero denominator from an undefined square root. Between $-1$ and $1$, for example at $x=0$, the denominator's radicand is $-1$. At $x=-1$ and $x=1$ it is zero, so the square root exists but cannot be used as a divisor. To the right of $4$, the numerator is not real even though the denominator is positive. An interior point of the left branch such as $x=-2$ satisfies both $4-x=6$ and $x^2-1=3$. The two requirements therefore also account for that branch of the domain.

</div>
