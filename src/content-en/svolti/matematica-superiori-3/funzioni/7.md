## Exercise 1

The graph of $g(x)=(x-1)^2-2$ is a translation of the graph of $f(x)=x^2$. Find where the vertex $V(0,0)$ and the point $A(-1,1)$ are sent.

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x)&=f(x-1)-2,\\[4pt]
x-1=u&\iff x=u+1,\\[4pt]
(u,f(u))&\longmapsto (u+1,f(u)-2),\\[4pt]
V(0,0)&\longmapsto V'(1,-2),\\[4pt]
A(-1,1)&\longmapsto A'(0,-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;(x-1)^2-2" xmin=-3 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=6 punti="0,0,pieno,V;1,-2,pieno,V';-1,1,pieno,A;0,-1,pieno,A'" titolo="The vertex and the second point shift $1$ unit right and $2$ units down."}

<div class="spiegazione">

To recover the original input $u$, solve $x-1=u$. The new horizontal coordinate is therefore $1$ greater than the starting coordinate. The new vertical coordinate is found by subtracting $2$ from the starting value. The vertex goes to $(1,-2)$ because $f(0)=0$. The point with horizontal coordinate $-1$ goes to $(0,-1)$ because $f(-1)=1$ and $1-2=-1$. The same shift acts on every corresponding pair of points on the two curves.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The graph of $f$ contains the point $P(-2,3)$. Find the corresponding point on $g(x)=-f(1-x)+2$ and describe the effect on the coordinates.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1-x&=-2\\[4pt]
x&=3,\\[4pt]
g(3)&=-f(1-3)+2\\[4pt]
&=-f(-2)+2\\[4pt]
&=-3+2=-1 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad P'=(3,-1) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula for $f$ is unknown, but the given point tells us that $f(-2)=3$. The inner argument $1-x$ must therefore equal $-2$, which gives $x=3$. For the vertical coordinate, reverse the sign of $3$ and add $2$ to obtain $-1$. In general, the horizontal coordinate $u$ becomes $1-u$ and the vertical coordinate $v$ becomes $2-v$. The midpoint between $(u,v)$ and its transformed point is therefore always $\left(\dfrac{1}{2},1\right)$.

</div>
