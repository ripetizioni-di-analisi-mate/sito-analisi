## Exercise 1

Find the intersections of $\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{16}=1$ with $x=5$ and the length of the chord.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{y^2}{16}&=\frac{25}{9}-1\\
y^2&=16\cdot\frac{16}{9}=\frac{256}{9}\\
y&=\pm\frac{16}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
A=\left(5,\frac{16}{3}\right),\qquad B=\left(5,-\frac{16}{3}\right).
$$

$$
AB=\left|\frac{16}{3}-\left(-\frac{16}{3}\right)\right|=\frac{32}{3}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The abscissa $5$ is greater than the right vertex abscissa $3$, so the vertical line can cross that branch. Substitution gives a positive value for $y^2$. The roots are opposites because the curve is symmetric about the horizontal axis. Both points lie on the same branch because both abscissas are positive. The joining segment is vertical and crosses the $x$-axis halfway along, so its length is twice the distance of either point from that axis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find all vertical lines cutting a chord of length $8$ on $x^2-y^2=9$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x=s&\ \Longrightarrow\ y^2=s^2-9\\
2\sqrt{s^2-9}&=8,\qquad |s|>3\\
s^2-9&=16\\
s^2&=25 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
x=-5\qquad\text{and}\qquad x=5 .
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(x^2-9);-sqrt(x^2-9)" xmin=-7 xmax=7 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-5,4;-5,-4;5,4;5,-4" segmenti="-5,-4,-5,4;5,-4,5,4" legenda="y=\sqrt{x^2-9};y=-\sqrt{x^2-9}" titolo="A chord of length $8$ occurs once on each branch."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $s$ be the abscissa of the vertical line being sought. A chord of positive length requires two distinct points, so we need $s>3$ or $s<-3$. The ordinates are $\sqrt{s^2-9}$ and $-\sqrt{s^2-9}$. Setting their difference equal to $8$ gives $s^2=25$. Both roots satisfy $|s|>3$. Substituting either into the original equation gives ordinates $4$ and $-4$, confirming the required length.

</div>
