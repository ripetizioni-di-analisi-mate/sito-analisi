## Exercise 1

Find the foci of $\dfrac{y^2}{25}-\dfrac{x^2}{144}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\sqrt{25}=5\\
b&=\sqrt{144}=12\\
c&=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\\
 &=\sqrt{25+144}=13 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_1=(0,-13),\qquad F_2=(0,13).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the positive term is the $y$ term, the line through the foci is the vertical axis. The upper and lower foci therefore have abscissa zero. The distance from either focus to the center follows by adding the squares of the semiaxes, since the focal definition of a hyperbola gives $b^2=c^2-a^2$. The value $13$ exceeds the distance $5$ from the center to either vertex, so the foci lie beyond the vertices on the same axis. Their separation is $26$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The foci are $(-5,0)$ and $(5,0)$, and the hyperbola passes through $P=(4,0)$. Find its standard equation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=\frac{5-(-5)}{2}=5\\
PF_1&=4-(-5)=9,\qquad PF_2=5-4=1\\
2a&=|9-1|=8,\qquad a=4\\
b^2&=c^2-a^2=25-16=9 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The midpoint of the foci is the origin. Since both have ordinate zero, the equation must have a horizontal transverse axis. One point on the curve is given, so measuring its distances from the foci determines the constant $2a$. We have $0<8<10$, so the condition defines a hyperbola. Each focus is $5$ units from the center, so the square of the conjugate semiaxis is $25-16=9$. As a check, at the given point the first term is $\dfrac{16}{16}=1$ and the second term is zero.

</div>
