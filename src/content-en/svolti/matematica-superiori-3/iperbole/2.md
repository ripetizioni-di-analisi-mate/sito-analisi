## Exercise 1

Find the foci of $\dfrac{y^2}{25}-\dfrac{x^2}{144}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a&=\sqrt{25}=5\\
b&=\sqrt{144}=12\\
c&=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\\
 &=\sqrt{25+144}=13 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
F_1=(0,-13),\qquad F_2=(0,13).
$$

::grafico{f="5*sqrt(1+x^2/144);-5*sqrt(1+x^2/144)" xmin=-12 xmax=12 ymin=-15 ymax=15 uguale=1 punti="0,5,pieno,V_2;0,-5,pieno,V_1;0,13,pieno,F_2;0,-13,pieno,F_1" legenda="y=5\sqrt{1+\dfrac{x^2}{144}};y=-5\sqrt{1+\dfrac{x^2}{144}}" titolo="Along the vertical axis the upper vertex is $8$ units from the focus above it and $18$ from the opposite focus."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the positive term is the $y$ term, the line through the foci is the vertical axis. The upper and lower foci therefore have abscissa zero. The distance from either focus to the center follows by adding the squares of the semiaxes, since the focal definition of a hyperbola gives $b^2=c^2-a^2$. The value $13$ exceeds the distance $5$ from the center to either vertex, so the foci lie beyond the vertices on the same axis. Their separation is $26$.

For a check, start from the upper vertex at $(0,5)$. Its distance from $F_2=(0,13)$ is $13-5=8$, and its distance from $F_1=(0,-13)$ is $5+13=18$. Their absolute difference is $18-8=10=2a$, the defining constant. Placing a focus at distance $12$ would confuse $b$ with $c$. In that case the two distances from the vertex would still differ by $10$, because both foci would still be outside the vertices. The curve equation is also needed to distinguish the values, since it requires $c^2=25+144=169$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The foci are $(-5,0)$ and $(5,0)$, and the hyperbola passes through $P=(4,0)$. Find its standard equation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=\frac{5-(-5)}{2}=5\\
PF_1&=4-(-5)=9,\qquad PF_2=5-4=1\\
2a&=|9-1|=8,\qquad a=4\\
b^2&=c^2-a^2=25-16=9 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The midpoint of the foci is the origin. Since both have ordinate zero, the equation must have a horizontal transverse axis. One point on the curve is given, so measuring its distances from the foci determines the constant $2a$. We have $0<8<10$, so the condition defines a hyperbola. Each focus is $5$ units from the center, so the square of the conjugate semiaxis is $25-16=9$. As a check, at the given point the first term is $\dfrac{16}{16}=1$ and the second term is zero.

To check the focal data off the axis, take the right focus’s abscissa $x=5$. The resulting equation gives $\dfrac{y^2}{9}=\dfrac{25}{16}-1=\dfrac{9}{16}$. The upper point is $Q=(5,\dfrac94)$. Its distances from the two foci are $\dfrac{41}{4}$ and $\dfrac94$, so their difference is $\dfrac{32}{4}=8$, just as for $P$. This comparison also shows that the focal line itself picks out only the vertices, while the curve has many other points. Both distances for the vertex are measured along the axis. For $Q$, one distance is the hypotenuse of a right triangle.

</div>
