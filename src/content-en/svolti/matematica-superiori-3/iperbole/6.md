## Exercise 1

Find the hyperbola with the coordinate axes as asymptotes that passes through $P=(-2,3)$. State which quadrants contain its branches.

$$
\begin{aligned}
xy&=K\\
K&=(-2)\cdot3=-6\\
y&=-\frac6x,\qquad x\ne0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

When the axes are the asymptotes, the equation has the form $xy=K$. The known point determines the product without first finding the semiaxes. Since $K=-6$ is negative, the coordinates of every point on the graph have opposite signs. The given point is in the second quadrant, and its reflection through the origin, $(2,-3)$, is in the fourth. Both satisfy $xy=-6$. Points on the $x$-axis or the $y$-axis have product zero, so none are on the graph.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangular photograph has area $48\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. Derive the relation between its width $x$ and height $y$, then find the widths for which the ratio $\dfrac{x}{y}$ is at least $3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
xy&=48,\qquad x,y>0\\
y&=\frac{48}{x}\\
\frac{x}{y}&=\frac{x^2}{48}\\[4pt]
\frac{x^2}{48}\geq3&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x^2\geq144\\
&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x\geq12 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
x\geq12\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both lengths are positive, so the model uses only the first-quadrant branch of $xy=48$. To compare the proportions with the ratio $3$, substitute $y=\dfrac{48}{x}$. The ratio is now a function of the width alone. The inequality $x^2\geq144$ reduces to $x\geq12$ on the domain of positive lengths. A width of $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ gives a height of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the ratio is exactly $3$ at the boundary. Larger widths produce a wider rectangle, while the height decreases to preserve the area.

</div>
