## Exercise 1

Find the eccentricity of $\dfrac{y^2}{16}-\dfrac{x^2}{9}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2&=16,\qquad b^2=9\\
c&=\sqrt{16+9}=5\\
e&=\frac{c}{a}=\frac54 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive term is the $y$ term, so $a$ is $4$ even though its direction is vertical. The upper focus is $5$ units from the center and the upper vertex is $4$ units away. Their ratio is therefore $\dfrac54$, which is greater than one. These two distances can be read along the vertical line through the foci. The number $3$ is the length of the conjugate semiaxis and enters the calculation of the focal distance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An origin-centered horizontal hyperbola has transverse semiaxis $6$ and eccentricity $\dfrac53$. Find its equation and the slope of its rising asymptote.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=ae=6\cdot\frac53=10\\
b^2&=c^2-a^2=100-36=64\\
b&=8,\qquad m=\frac86=\frac43 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{36}-\frac{y^2}{64}=1,\qquad m=\frac43 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the given quantity is the ratio $\dfrac ca$. Multiplying it by $a$ gives the focal distance. The relation $c^2=a^2+b^2$ then determines the denominator of the negative term. With $b=8$, the rectangle rises by $8$ units from the center to its upper-right corner and moves $6$ units to the right. The ratio $\dfrac86$ gives the slope. Halving both lengths would give the same ratio, although the distances of the vertices and foci from the center would change.

</div>
