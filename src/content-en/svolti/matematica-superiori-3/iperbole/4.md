## Exercise 1

Find the eccentricity of $\dfrac{y^2}{16}-\dfrac{x^2}{9}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2&=16,\qquad b^2=9\\
c&=\sqrt{16+9}=5\\
e&=\frac{c}{a}=\frac54 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive term is the $y$ term, so $a$ is $4$ even though its direction is vertical. The upper focus is $5$ units from the center and the upper vertex is $4$ units away. Their ratio is therefore $\dfrac54$, which is greater than one. These two distances can be read along the vertical line through the foci. The number $3$ is the length of the conjugate semiaxis and enters the calculation of the focal distance.

A check on $e$ starts from the semiaxis ratio. Dividing $c^2=a^2+b^2$ by $a^2=16$ gives $e^2=1+\dfrac{9}{16}=\dfrac{25}{16}$. Eccentricity is positive, so it equals $\dfrac54$. The ratio $\dfrac34$ instead equals $\dfrac ba$. In the vertical form it compares the horizontal and vertical half-sides of the reference rectangle, while the focal ratio compares the location of the focus with the location of the vertex. Geometrically, the distance from the center to the focus is $5$, one unit greater than the distance from the center to the vertex.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An origin-centered horizontal hyperbola has transverse semiaxis $6$ and eccentricity $\dfrac53$. Find its equation and the slope of its rising asymptote.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=ae=6\cdot\frac53=10\\
b^2&=c^2-a^2=100-36=64\\
b&=8,\qquad m=\frac86=\frac43 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{36}-\frac{y^2}{64}=1,\qquad m=\frac43 .
$$

::grafico{f="4*sqrt(x^2-36)/3;-4*sqrt(x^2-36)/3" xmin=-12 xmax=12 ymin=-10 ymax=10 uguale=1 punti="-10,0,pieno,F_1;10,0,pieno,F_2;-6,0,pieno,V_1;6,0,pieno,V_2" legenda="y=\dfrac43\sqrt{x^2-36};y=-\dfrac43\sqrt{x^2-36}" titolo="The right focus has abscissa $10$, while the right vertex has abscissa $6$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the given quantity is the ratio $\dfrac ca$. Multiplying it by $a$ gives the focal distance. The relation $c^2=a^2+b^2$ then determines the denominator of the negative term. With $b=8$, the rectangle rises by $8$ units from the center to its upper-right corner and moves $6$ units to the right. The ratio $\dfrac86$ gives the slope. Halving both lengths would give the same ratio, although the distances of the vertices and foci from the center would change.

The given data already show the link between eccentricity and asymptote slope. Squaring $e=\dfrac ca$ gives $e^2=1+\dfrac{b^2}{a^2}$. Subtracting $1$ from $\dfrac{25}{9}$ leaves $\dfrac{16}{9}$, so $\dfrac ba=\dfrac43$ can be found without calculating $b$ separately. Eccentricity therefore determines the slope, but writing the equation also requires one length. As a check, the vertices are $(\pm6,0)$ and the foci are $(\pm10,0)$. At the right vertex the focal distances are $16$ and $4$, whose difference is $12$, as required by $2a$.

</div>
