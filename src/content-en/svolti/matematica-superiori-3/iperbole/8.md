## Exercise 1

For $f(x)=\dfrac{2x+3}{x-1}$, find the center, asymptotes, and intercepts.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x+3&=2(x-1)+5\\
f(x)&=2+\frac{5}{x-1},\qquad x\ne1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C=(1,2),\qquad x=1,\qquad y=2 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)=0&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ 2x+3=0
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x=-\frac32\\
f(0)&=\frac{3}{-1}=-3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
A=\left(-\frac32,0\right),\qquad B=(0,-3).
$$

::grafico{f="2+5/(x-1)" xmin=-5 xmax=7 ymin=-5 ymax=9 uguale=1 asintoti="x=1;y=2" punti="1,2,vuoto,C;-1.5,0,pieno,A;0,-3,pieno,B" titolo="The asymptotes and the two axis intercepts."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $2(x-1)$ has the same coefficient of $x$ as the numerator. Subtracting it leaves $5$, giving a constant term and a translated reciprocal term. The denominator vanishes at $x=1$, where the magnitude of the reciprocal grows without bound, and the reciprocal approaches zero for large $|x|$. This gives both asymptotes. Their intersection is the center. To find the point on the $x$-axis, set the numerator to zero and check $-\dfrac32\ne1$. To find the point on the $y$-axis, evaluate the function at zero, which is in the domain.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A linear fractional graph has asymptotes $x=-2$ and $y=3$ and passes through $P=(0,1)$. Find $f(x)$ and determine whether the branch with $x>-2$ is above or below $y=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)&=3+\frac{K}{x+2}\\
1&=3+\frac K2\\
K&=2(1-3)=-4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
f(x)=3-\frac4{x+2}.
$$

$$
x>-2\ \Longrightarrow\ x+2>0
\ \Longrightarrow\ f(x)-3=-\frac4{x+2}<0 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical asymptote determines the denominator $x+2$, while the horizontal asymptote fixes the constant term $3$. That leaves a single parameter $K$. The given point is to the right of the center and below the horizontal asymptote, so its relative coordinates have opposite signs and their product should be negative. Substitution confirms $K=-4$. Along the whole right branch $x+2$ is positive, so the term subtracted from $3$ is positive and the branch lies below that line. The left branch has negative denominator and lies above it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Describe the graph of $g(x)=\dfrac{6x-12}{3x-6}$. Identify any missing point and determine whether the excluded vertical line is an asymptote.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x-6=0&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x=2\\
g(x)&=\frac{6(x-2)}{3(x-2)}=2,\qquad x\ne2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\{(x,2)\mid x\in\mathbb R,\ x\ne2\}.
$$

::grafico{f="2" xmin=-3 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=5 punti="2,2,vuoto,P" titolo="The point $P=(2,2)$ is missing from the graph line."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator of the given expression is zero at $x=2$. At every other value the numerator and denominator can be divided by $x-2$, leaving the constant $2$. In particular, on both sides of the excluded value the ordinates are always $2$. The graph therefore has a missing point at $(2,2)$ and no vertical asymptote. This time the numerator and denominator vanish at the same abscissa, so cancellation leaves no reciprocal term to make the graph rise or fall without bound near $2$.

</div>
