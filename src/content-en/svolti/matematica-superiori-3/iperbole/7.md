## Exercise 1

Find the center and vertices of $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{9}-\dfrac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-2=0,\quad y+1=0
&\ \Longrightarrow\ C=(2,-1)\\
y=-1
&\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{(x-2)^2}{9}=1\\
&\ \Longrightarrow\ x=2\pm3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V_1=(-1,-1),\qquad V_2=(5,-1).
$$

::grafico{f="-1+2*sqrt((x-2)^2-9)/3;-1-2*sqrt((x-2)^2-9)/3" xmin=-5 xmax=9 ymin=-5 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="2,-1,vuoto,C;-1,-1,pieno,V_1;5,-1,pieno,V_2" segmenti="-1,-1,5,-1" legenda="y=-1+\dfrac23\sqrt{(x-2)^2-9};y=-1-\dfrac23\sqrt{(x-2)^2-9}" titolo="The vertices lie three units from the center on the line $y=-1$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The differences $x-2$ and $y+1$ measure displacement from the center. Both vanish at $(2,-1)$. The $x-2$ term is positive, so the vertices lie on the horizontal line through the center, namely $y=-1$. On that line a square is equal to $9$, giving offsets $+3$ and $-3$ from the abscissa $2$. The midpoint of the resulting vertices is $\left(\dfrac{-1+5}{2},-1\right)=(2,-1)$, as expected from the translated coordinates.

To see where the branches lie, write $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{9}=1+\dfrac{(y+1)^2}{4}\geq1$. Hence $|x-2|\geq3$ and the abscissas on the two branches satisfy $x\leq-1$ and $x\geq5$, respectively. Equality holds at the vertices. The center $(2,-1)$ lies in the strip between the branches and gives zero on the left-hand side. Reading the signs directly from the binomial constants could suggest $(-2,1)$, but the resulting offsets would be $-4$ and $2$ instead of vanishing. Substitution in the two binomials immediately exposes this sign error.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write $4y^2-9x^2+8y+36x-68=0$ in standard form and find its center and asymptotes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(y^2+2y)-9(x^2-4x)&=68\\
4\big((y+1)^2-1\big)-9\big((x-2)^2-4\big)&=68\\
4(y+1)^2-9(x-2)^2&=36\\
\frac{(y+1)^2}{9}-\frac{(x-2)^2}{4}&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C=(2,-1),\qquad y+1=\pm\frac32(x-2).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor $4$ from the $y$ terms and $-9$ from the $x$ terms. Completing the square in $x$ subtracts $4$ within the quadratic expression, so its contribution to the overall constant is $(-9)(-4)=36$. The contribution from $y$ is $4(-1)=-4$. Moving these constants to the right leaves $36$, and dividing gives standard form with the positive $y$ term. The center is $(2,-1)$, the vertical half-side is $3$, and the horizontal half-side is $2$. The diagonals pass through the center with slopes $\pm\dfrac32$.

The orientation can be checked by taking one vertex and substituting it into the original equation. At $x=2$, standard form gives $(y+1)^2=9$, so $y=2$ or $y=-4$. At $(2,2)$ the original left-hand side is $16-36+16+72-68=0$. For the asymptotes, at $x=4$ the horizontal offset from the center is $2$, so their vertical offsets must be $+3$ and $-3$, giving points $(4,2)$ and $(4,-4)$. Since the positive term is the $y$ term, these are the corners on the right side of the rectangle. The vertices of the hyperbola lie along $x=2$.

</div>
