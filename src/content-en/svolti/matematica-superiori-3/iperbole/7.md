## Exercise 1

Find the center and vertices of $\dfrac{(x-2)^2}{9}-\dfrac{(y+1)^2}{4}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-2=0,\quad y+1=0
&\ \Longrightarrow\ C=(2,-1)\\
y=-1
&\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{(x-2)^2}{9}=1\\
&\ \Longrightarrow\ x=2\pm3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V_1=(-1,-1),\qquad V_2=(5,-1).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The differences $x-2$ and $y+1$ measure displacement from the center. Both vanish at $(2,-1)$. The $x-2$ term is positive, so the vertices lie on the horizontal line through the center, namely $y=-1$. On that line a square is equal to $9$, giving offsets $+3$ and $-3$ from the abscissa $2$. The midpoint of the resulting vertices is $\left(\dfrac{-1+5}{2},-1\right)=(2,-1)$, as expected from the translated coordinates.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write $4y^2-9x^2+8y+36x-68=0$ in standard form and find its center and asymptotes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(y^2+2y)-9(x^2-4x)&=68\\
4\big((y+1)^2-1\big)-9\big((x-2)^2-4\big)&=68\\
4(y+1)^2-9(x-2)^2&=36\\
\frac{(y+1)^2}{9}-\frac{(x-2)^2}{4}&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C=(2,-1),\qquad y+1=\pm\frac32(x-2).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor $4$ from the $y$ terms and $-9$ from the $x$ terms. Completing the square in $x$ subtracts $4$ within the quadratic expression, so its contribution to the overall constant is $(-9)(-4)=36$. The contribution from $y$ is $4(-1)=-4$. Moving these constants to the right leaves $36$, and dividing gives standard form with the positive $y$ term. The center is $(2,-1)$, the vertical half-side is $3$, and the horizontal half-side is $2$. The diagonals pass through the center with slopes $\pm\dfrac32$.

</div>
