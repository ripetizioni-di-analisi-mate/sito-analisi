## Exercise 1

Find the vertices of $\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{16}=1$ and state the orientation of the branches.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{x^2}{9}=1\\
&\ \Longrightarrow\ x^2=9\\
&\ \Longrightarrow\ x=\pm3.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V_1=(-3,0),\qquad V_2=(3,0).
$$

::grafico{f="4*sqrt(x^2-9)/3;-4*sqrt(x^2-9)/3" xmin=-7 xmax=7 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-3,0,pieno,V_1;3,0,pieno,V_2" legenda="y=\dfrac43\sqrt{x^2-9};y=-\dfrac43\sqrt{x^2-9}" titolo="The two points of the hyperbola on the $x$-axis are the vertices."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive term contains $x$, so the transverse axis is the line $y=0$. Setting $y$ to zero gives both intersections with that axis. If $y$ is allowed to vary, the equation gives $x^2=9+\dfrac{9y^2}{16}\geq9$. Thus the abscissas of the right branch are at least $3$, and those of the left branch are at most $-3$. The strip between the vertices is empty. The denominator $16$ affects how widely the branches open, while the vertices are determined by the denominator of the positive term.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the vertices of $4x^2-9y^2=-36$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x^2-9y^2&=-36\\[4pt]
\frac{y^2}{4}-\frac{x^2}{9}&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x=0&\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{y^2}{4}=1\\
&\ \Longrightarrow\ y=\pm2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V_1=(0,-2),\qquad V_2=(0,2).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is negative. Dividing by $-36$ makes it $1$ and changes the sign of both squared terms. This is the form from which to read the orientation. The $y$ term is positive, so one branch lies above the origin and the other below. The transverse axis is the vertical line $x=0$. The section along that line gives $y^2=4$, so each vertex is $2$ units from the center. Although $9$ is the larger denominator, it belongs to the negative term and determines the horizontal half-width of the reference rectangle.

</div>
