## Exercise 1

Solve $|x-1|+|x-4|=5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x<1&\implies 5-2x=5 \iff x=0,\\[4pt]
1\leq x\leq4&\implies 3=5,\\[4pt]
x>4&\implies 2x-5=5 \iff x=5,\\[4pt]
S&=\{0,5\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Between $1$ and $4$, the distances add to the length of the segment, which is $3$. To reach $5$, two additional units are needed. Moving one unit to the left or right of the segment adds those two units. The calculation on the outer branches gives exactly $0$ and $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $|x+2|-|x-2|=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x<-2&\implies -4=1,\\[4pt]
-2\leq x\leq2&\implies 2x=1 \iff x=\frac12,\\[4pt]
x>2&\implies 4=1,\\[4pt]
S&=\left\{\frac12\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x+2)-abs(x-2);1" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-5 ymax=5 punti="-2,-4,pieno;0.5,1,pieno;2,4,pieno" titolo="Only the increasing branch meets the line at height $1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

To the left of $-2$, both arguments are negative and the difference between their absolute values is always $-4$. To the right of $2$, both are positive and the difference is always $4$. Between $-2$ and $2$ we have $(x+2)-(2-x)=2x$. The candidate $\dfrac12$ belongs to the branch on which it was found.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $|x-1|+2|x-3|\leq4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x<1&\implies 7-3x\leq4\iff x\geq1,\\[4pt]
1\leq x\leq3&\implies 5-x\leq4\iff x\geq1,\\[4pt]
x>3&\implies 3x-7\leq4\iff x\leq\frac{11}{3},\\[4pt]
S&=\left[1,\frac{11}{3}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expressions inside the absolute values change sign at $1$ and $3$. The first branch is $x<1$ and its condition $x\geq1$ is incompatible. The middle branch satisfies the inequality throughout $[1,3]$. On the right branch, intersecting with $x>3$ gives $\left(3,\dfrac{11}{3}\right]$. The two allowed pieces form the stated interval.

</div>
