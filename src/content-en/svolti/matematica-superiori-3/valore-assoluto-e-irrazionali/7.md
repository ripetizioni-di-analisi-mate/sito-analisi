## Exercise 1

Solve $\sqrt{x-1}\leq3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0\leq x-1\leq9 &\iff 1\leq x\leq10,\\[4pt]
S&=[1,10].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lower bound $x\geq1$ comes from the domain. The upper bound comes from $x-1\leq9$. The point $1$ gives a zero square root, while at $10$ we have $\sqrt9=3$. Both satisfy the comparison.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\sqrt{x^2-1}\leq\sqrt3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0\leq x^2-1\leq3 &\iff 1\leq x^2\leq4,\\[4pt]
S&=[-2,-1]\cup[1,2].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With both sides nonnegative, the upper bound is equivalent to $x^2-1\leq3$. Existence of the root on the left also requires $x^2-1\geq0$. Thus the allowed values have distance from zero between $1$ and $2$. The inner endpoints give a zero radicand and remain included.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\sqrt{2x+3}>x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\left[-\frac32,+\infty\right),\\[4pt]
x\in\left[-\frac32,0\right)&\implies \sqrt{2x+3}>x,\\[4pt]
x\geq0,\quad \sqrt{2x+3}>x &\iff 2x+3>x^2,\\[4pt]
(x+1)(x-3)&<0\implies x\in[0,3),\\[4pt]
S&=\left[-\frac32,3\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="sqrt(2*x+3);x" xmin=-2.5 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=5 punti="-1.5,0,pieno;3,3,pieno" titolo="The root lies above the line from $-\dfrac32$, included, to $3$, excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For $x<0$, any real square root is greater than $x$, so the domain is sufficient. For $x\geq0$, the squares can be compared. The product $(x+1)(x-3)$ is negative on $(-1,3)$, which becomes $[0,3)$ in the second case. At $3$ the root equals the right-hand side, so $3$ is excluded.

</div>
