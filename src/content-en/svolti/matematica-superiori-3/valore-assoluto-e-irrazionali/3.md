## Exercise 1

Solve $|x-1|>3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-1<-3 &\iff x<-2,\\[4pt]
x-1>3 &\iff x>4,\\[4pt]
S&=(-\infty,-2)\cup(4,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The allowed points are more than three units from $1$. On the number line, they are before $-2$ or after $4$. A value only needs to satisfy one of the two comparisons, so take the union.

Removing the absolute value requires distinguishing the location relative to the center. To the left of $1$, the distance is $1-x$, so $1-x>3$ gives $x<-2$. To the right it is $x-1$, so $x-1>3$ gives $x>4$. The inequalities apply on different sides, and the solution is their union. At $x=-3$ the distance is $4$, while at $x=0$ it is $1$. This check distinguishes the exterior from the middle interval. At $x=-2$ and $x=4$ the distance is exactly $3$, so both endpoints are excluded. With a non-strict threshold, both would be included.

The inequality $-2<x<4$ instead describes points less than $3$ units away. Both strict comparisons exclude the boundaries, and the direction decides whether to use the inside or the outside region.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $|x^2-1|\geq1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-1\leq-1 &\iff x^2\leq0 \iff x=0,\\[4pt]
x^2-1\geq1 &\iff x^2\geq2,\\[4pt]
S&=(-\infty,-\sqrt2]\cup\{0\}\cup[\sqrt2,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-1);1" xmin=-2.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-1 ymax=5 punti="-1.4142135624,1,pieno;0,1,pieno;1.4142135624,1,pieno;-1,0,pieno;1,0,pieno" titolo="Height $1$ is also reached at the isolated point with x-coordinate $0$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The branch $x^2-1\leq-1$ is possible only at $0$ because a square is nonnegative. The branch $x^2-1\geq1$ requires a distance from zero of at least $\sqrt2$. Keep the isolated point $0$ because its image equals $|-1|=1$.

The condition concerns how far $x^2$ is from $1$. To differ by at least $1$ downward, the square must be at most zero. To differ by at least $1$ upward, it must reach at least $2$. At $x=0$ the deviation is $1$, and equality is allowed. At $x=1$, which lies between $0$ and $\sqrt2$, the absolute value is instead $|1-1|=0$. At $x=\sqrt2$ we have $|2-1|=1$. These checks distinguish the isolated point $0$ from the two rays. The graph also shows that the absolute value is zero when $x^2=1$, at the symmetric points $x=-1$ and $x=1$.

In this case, replacing $\geq$ by $>$ would remove the isolated point and both irrational boundaries. The first branch would require $x^2<0$, which is impossible.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $|x-2|>x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x<2&\implies 2-x>x \iff x<1,\\[4pt]
x\geq2&\implies x-2>x \iff -2>0,\\[4pt]
S&=(-\infty,1).
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x-2);x" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=6 punti="1,1,pieno;2,0,pieno" titolo="To the right of $2$ the two lines have the same slope"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For $x<2$, $x-2$ is negative and we use its opposite. The condition $x<1$ already lies within that branch, so it requires no further restriction there. For $x\geq2$, use $x-2$, which is always less than $x$. The point $1$ gives equality and is excluded.

To the left of $2$, the comparison $2-x>x$ becomes $2>2x$, hence $x<1$. This interval is entirely to the left of $2$, so no part of the provisional result is lost. On the right branch the two expressions always differ by $-2$. In particular, at $x=2$ the absolute value equals $0$, and the right-hand side equals $2$. To check the first branch, at $x=0$ we have $2>0$, while at $x=\dfrac32$ we have $\dfrac12<\dfrac32$. Negative numbers are allowed because the absolute value is nonnegative and the right-hand side is negative. This also accounts for the infinite interval to the left.

In the graph, the left branch meets $y=x$ just once, at $(1,1)$. The right branch has the same slope as the line and a y-coordinate $2$ units lower.

</div>
