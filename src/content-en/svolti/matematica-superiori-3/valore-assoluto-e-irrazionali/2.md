## Exercise 1

Solve $|x-1|\leq3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x-1|\leq3 &\iff -3\leq x-1\leq3 \\[4pt]
&\iff -2\leq x\leq4,\\[4pt]
S&=[-2,4].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown must lie in the segment centered at $1$ with radius $3$. Adding $1$ to each part gives the endpoints. The absolute value at either endpoint is exactly $3$, so the allowed equality includes them.

Writing $-3\leq x-1\leq3$ imposes a lower and an upper bound at the same time. The first gives $x\geq-2$ and the second $x\leq4$. Intersect the two sets and retain both boundaries. At the center $x=1$ the absolute value is zero, and all displacements of length at most $3$ are allowed. For an outside check, take $x=5$. Its distance from $1$ is $4$, so it is excluded. The graph of $y=|x-1|$ shows the same effect. Between the two x-coordinates where it meets $y=3$, the ordinates are less than $3$, and at the endpoints they are equal. The inequality $\leq$ includes that entire portion.

The result can also be expressed as the two simultaneous conditions $x+2\geq0$ and $4-x\geq0$. They require the point to remain to the right of the left boundary and to the left of the right boundary.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $|7-3x|<2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2 < 7-3x < 2
&\iff -9 < -3x < -5\\[4pt]
&\iff 3 > x > \frac53,\\[4pt]
S&=\left(\frac53,3\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After subtracting $7$, the coefficient of $x$ is $-3$. Dividing by this number reverses both comparisons. The number $3$ becomes the upper endpoint and $\dfrac53$ the lower one. The inequality is strict, so both endpoints are excluded.

The first comparison $-2<7-3x$ leads to $-9<-3x$ and hence $x<3$. The second, $7-3x<2$, leads to $-3x<-5$ and thus $x>\dfrac53$. Doing each step separately shows why the endpoints reverse order after division by a negative number. An inside check is $x=2$, where the absolute value is $|7-6|=1$. At $x=\dfrac53$ the argument is $2$, and at $x=3$ it is $-2$. The arguments have opposite signs but the same absolute value $2$. Neither satisfies the strict inequality. The center is $\dfrac73$, where the argument is zero.

Rewriting the absolute value as $3\left|x-\dfrac73\right|$, the comparison requires the distance from $\dfrac73$ to be less than $\dfrac23$. This gives the same two endpoints.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $|x^2-6|\leq3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x^2-6|\leq3 &\iff -3\leq x^2-6\leq3 \\[4pt]
&\iff 3\leq x^2\leq9,\\[4pt]
S&=[-3,-\sqrt3]\cup[\sqrt3,3].
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x^2-6);3" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=8 punti="-3,3,pieno;-1.7320508076,3,pieno;1.7320508076,3,pieno;3,3,pieno" titolo="The two portions at height at most $3$ are symmetric about the $y$-axis"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The bounded value is $x^2-6$. Adding $6$ requires the square to lie between $3$ and $9$, so the distance from $x$ to zero must lie between $\sqrt3$ and $3$. The lower bound removes the central segment that would satisfy $x^2\leq9$ alone. All four endpoints satisfy the non-strict inequalities.

Solve the bounds separately. The condition $x^2\leq9$ gives $-3\leq x\leq3$. The condition $x^2\geq3$ gives $x\leq-\sqrt3$ or $x\geq\sqrt3$. Intersect the first interval with each of the two rays. For example, $x=0$ satisfies the upper bound but gives $|0-6|=6$ and fails the comparison. The values $x=\pm3$ give $|9-6|=3$, while $x=\pm\sqrt3$ give $|3-6|=3$. In the graph the four intersections with the threshold identify the two useful portions. Symmetry follows because $x$ and $-x$ have the same square.

Substituting $x=\sqrt6$ makes the argument zero. It lies between $\sqrt3$ and $3$, so the check confirms that this point on the right-hand branch is inside the solution.

</div>
