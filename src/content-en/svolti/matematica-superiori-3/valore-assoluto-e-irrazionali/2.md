## Exercise 1

Solve $|x-1|\leq3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x-1|\leq3 &\iff -3\leq x-1\leq3 \\[4pt]
&\iff -2\leq x\leq4,\\[4pt]
S&=[-2,4].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown must lie in the segment centered at $1$ with radius $3$. Adding $1$ to each part gives the endpoints. The absolute value at either endpoint is exactly $3$, so the allowed equality includes them.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $|7-3x|<2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2 < 7-3x < 2
&\iff -9 < -3x < -5\\[4pt]
&\iff 3 > x > \frac53,\\[4pt]
S&=\left(\frac53,3\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After subtracting $7$, the coefficient of $x$ is $-3$. Dividing by this number reverses both comparisons. The number $3$ becomes the upper endpoint and $\dfrac53$ the lower one. The inequality is strict, so both endpoints are excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $|x^2-6|\leq3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|x^2-6|\leq3 &\iff -3\leq x^2-6\leq3 \\[4pt]
&\iff 3\leq x^2\leq9,\\[4pt]
S&=[-3,-\sqrt3]\cup[\sqrt3,3].
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bounded value is $x^2-6$. Adding $6$ requires the square to lie between $3$ and $9$, so the distance from $x$ to zero must lie between $\sqrt3$ and $3$. The lower bound removes the central segment that would satisfy $x^2\leq9$ alone. All four endpoints satisfy the non-strict inequalities.

</div>
