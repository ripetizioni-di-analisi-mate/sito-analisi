## Exercise 1

Solve $\sqrt{x-1}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x-1}=1 &\iff x-1=1 \\[4pt]
&\iff x=2,\\[4pt]
S&=\{2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side is positive, so we can square both sides. At $x=2$, the radicand equals $1$ and the root equals $\sqrt1=1$, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $2-\sqrt{3x+1}=-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2-\sqrt{3x+1}=-2 &\iff \sqrt{3x+1}=4 \\[4pt]
&\iff 3x+1=16\\[4pt]
&\iff x=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Isolating the square root gives the positive right-hand side $4$. Square at this point to avoid expanding the square of a difference that still contains the root. At $x=5$ the original expression equals $2-\sqrt{16}=-2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\sqrt{2x+5}+3=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2x+5}&=1-3=-2,\\[4pt]
\sqrt{2x+5}&\geq0 \qquad (x\geq-\tfrac52),\\[4pt]
S&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain contains the values for which $2x+5\geq0$. Throughout that domain the side containing the square root is nonnegative, so equality with $-2$ is impossible. Squaring would make the squares of $2$ and $-2$ equal and introduce an extraneous candidate.

</div>
