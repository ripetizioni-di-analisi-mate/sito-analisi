## Exercise 1

Solve $\sqrt{x+2}=x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x+2}=x &\iff \begin{cases}x\geq0,\\ x^2=x+2.\end{cases}\\[4pt]
x^2-x-2&=(x-2)(x+1),\\[4pt]
S&=\{-1,2\}\cap[0,+\infty)=\{2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Squaring does not preserve the sign. At $-1$, the square root equals $\sqrt1=1$, while the right-hand side equals $-1$. The condition $x\geq0$ therefore removes that candidate. At $2$, both sides equal $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\sqrt{3x+1}=|x-3|$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x+1&=(x-3)^2,\\[4pt]
x^2-9x+8&=(x-1)(x-8)=0,\\[4pt]
S&=\{1,8\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value on the right is nonnegative for every real $x$, so once the domain has been imposed we can square both sides. Both candidates satisfy $3x+1\geq0$. At $1$, both sides equal $2$. At $8$, they both equal $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\sqrt{x+9}-\sqrt x=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&\geq0,\\[4pt]
\sqrt{x+9}&=1+\sqrt x,\\[4pt]
x+9&=1+2\sqrt x+x,\\[4pt]
\sqrt x&=4\iff x=16.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The common domain is $x\geq0$. After moving $\sqrt x$ to the right, both sides are nonnegative. Squaring the right-hand side gives the cross term $2\sqrt x$, and the terms in $x$ cancel. Substitution gives $\sqrt{25}-\sqrt{16}=5-4=1$, so the solution is $16$.

</div>
