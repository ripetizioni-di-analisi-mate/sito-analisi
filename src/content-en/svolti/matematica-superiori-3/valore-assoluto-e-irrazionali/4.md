## Exercise 1

Find the real domain of $f(x)=\dfrac1{\sqrt{x-1}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-1\geq0,\quad \sqrt{x-1}\neq0
&\iff x-1>0,\\[4pt]
D&=(1,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square root requires a nonnegative radicand, and the denominator must be nonzero. A zero radicand would make the root zero as well, so the radicand must be strictly positive. This excludes the point $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the real domain of $f(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x+1}}{\sqrt{4-x}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+1\geq0&\iff x\geq-1,\\[4pt]
4-x>0&\iff x<4,\\[4pt]
D&=[-1,+\infty)\cap(-\infty,4)=[-1,4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two roots have different conditions because one is in the numerator and the other is in the denominator. The endpoint $-1$ gives a zero numerator but a positive denominator. The endpoint $4$ would give a zero denominator. Therefore the first is included and the second is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the real domain of $f(x)=\dfrac1{\sqrt{\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}}}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x-1}{x+2}>0&\iff
x<-2\ \lor\ x>1,\\[4pt]
D&=(-\infty,-2)\cup(1,+\infty).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inner denominator excludes $-2$. Since the root itself is in a denominator, the radicand must be strictly positive. The quotient is positive when numerator and denominator are both negative or both positive. At $x=1$ the quotient is zero, so this endpoint is excluded as well.

</div>
