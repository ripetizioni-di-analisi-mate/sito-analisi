## Exercise 1

Solve $|x-1|=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-1=3 &\iff x=4,\\[4pt]
x-1=-3 &\iff x=-2,\\[4pt]
S&=\{-2,4\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="abs(x-1);3" xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=6 punti="-2,3,pieno;4,3,pieno" titolo="The two points at height $3$ have x-coordinates $-2$ and $4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving three units to the right of $1$ gives $4$. Moving three units to the left gives $-2$. These are the only points at distance $3$ from the center, because a real displacement with absolute value $3$ is either $3$ or $-3$.

The two linear equations distinguish the directions of displacement from the center. Starting from $x-1=3$, adding $1$ gives $4$. Starting from $x-1=-3$, the same step gives $-2$. Substitution gives $|4-1|=|3|=3$ and $|-2-1|=|-3|=3$. In the graph the two points have height $3$. The point with x-coordinate $1$ instead has height zero and is the vertex. The two x-coordinates are symmetric because each branch rises by one unit for each unit of distance from $1$. The comparison asks for distance *equal* to $3$, so the segment between $-2$ and $4$ also contains values that do not solve the equation. For example, the x-coordinate $0$ gives distance $1$.

The result can also be written $x=1\pm3$. The sign distinguishes the direction on the number line. The distance is identical for both points.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $|3x+2|=4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x+2=4 &\iff x=\frac23,\\[4pt]
3x+2=-4 &\iff x=-2,\\[4pt]
S&=\left\{-2,\frac23\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="abs(3*x+2);4" xmin=-3 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=8 punti="-2,4,pieno;0.6666666667,4,pieno;-0.6666666667,0,pieno" titolo="Two intersections at height $4$, on either side of $-\dfrac23$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression $3x+2$ must be $4$ units from zero. Subtract $2$ and divide each case by $3$ to get the two x-coordinates. On the number line, rewriting $|3x+2|=3|x+\dfrac23|$ shows that both solutions are $\dfrac43$ units from $-\dfrac23$.

To separate the two cases, subtract $2$ to obtain $3x=2$ and $3x=-6$. Dividing by the same coefficient gives the two x-coordinates. At $x=\dfrac23$, the argument of the absolute value is $3\cdot\dfrac23+2=4$. At $x=-2$ the argument is $-6+2=-4$, so the absolute value is $4$ there as well. In the graph, the intersections with $y=4$ lie on either side of $-\dfrac23$, where the inner expression is zero. That inner expression increases by $3$ per unit of $x$. Thus two inner values that differ by $8$ require x-coordinates that differ by $\dfrac83$. Indeed, $\dfrac23-(-2)=\dfrac83$.

The horizontal distance from the center is $\dfrac43$ in both cases. This value is also obtained by dividing height $4$ by the absolute value of the coefficient $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $|x+1|=2x-1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x-1\geq0&\iff x\geq\frac12,\\[4pt]
x\geq\frac12&\implies x+1>0,\\[4pt]
x+1=2x-1&\iff x=2,\\[4pt]
S&=\{2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The right-hand side must be nonnegative, so work only where $x\geq\dfrac12$. On this ray, $x+1$ is positive and its absolute value equals $x+1$. The solution $2$ satisfies the constraint and gives $|2+1|=2\cdot2-1=3$.

The two branches of the absolute value also show what happens outside the admissible region. If $x<-1$, the left-hand side is $-x-1>0$, whereas $2x-1$ is negative on the same region. That branch cannot supply a solution. If $-1\leq x<\dfrac12$, the left side remains nonnegative and the right side is still negative. From $\dfrac12$ onward both sides are nonnegative and the first one equals $x+1$. At $x=\dfrac12$, the line on the right-hand side has height zero and the curve on the left-hand side has height $\dfrac32$. Their ordinates agree only later, at $x=2$ and height $3$. The two branches explain why the absolute value equation gives just one solution here.

Checking by branches can also start from $-x-1=2x-1$. This gives $x=0$, which must be discarded because $-x-1$ describes the absolute value only for $x<-1$.

</div>
