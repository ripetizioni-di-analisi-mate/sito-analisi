## Exercise 1

Classify the locus $-x^2-y^2+4x-6y-9=0$ and find its center and radius if they exist.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+y^2-4x+6y+9&=0\\
(x-2)^2-4+(y+3)^2-9+9&=0\\
(x-2)^2+(y+3)^2&=4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C=(2,-3),\qquad r=2 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both quadratic coefficients are negative. Multiplying the entire equation by $-1$ makes both terms positive without changing the solutions. Since the two squares have the same coefficient, the locus is determined by the sum of the squared coordinate offsets from the center. The right-hand side $4$ is positive, so a real circle exists. The value $4$ is the squared radius and gives radius $2$. One point on the curve is $(4,-3)$, because a horizontal offset of $2$ and a vertical offset of zero give the required distance.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For every $t\in\mathbb R$, classify $x^2+y^2-6x-2y+t=0$. Give the center and radius in the circle cases.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-3)^2-9+(y-1)^2-1+t&=0\\
(x-3)^2+(y-1)^2&=10-t .
\end{aligned}
$$

- If $t<10$, a circle centered at $(3,1)$ with radius $\sqrt{10-t}$.
- If $t=10$, just the point $(3,1)$.
- If $t>10$, the empty locus.

<div class="spiegazione">

The linear terms are independent of the parameter, so all real members of the family with positive radius have the same center. The right-hand side $10-t$ determines whether the radius exists. For instance, when $t=6$ it equals $4$ and the radius is $2$. When $t=10$ the sum of squares is zero, so each square must vanish. Finally, when $t>10$, the right-hand side is negative, while any sum of real squares is nonnegative. In that case there are no real solutions.

</div>
