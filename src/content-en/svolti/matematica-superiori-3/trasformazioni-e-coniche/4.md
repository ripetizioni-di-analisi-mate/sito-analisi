## Exercise 1

Find the center and semiaxes of $9x^2+4y^2-18x+16y-11=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
9(x^2-2x)+4(y^2+4y)&=11\\
9((x-1)^2-1)+4((y+2)^2-4)&=11\\
9(x-1)^2+4(y+2)^2&=36\\
\frac{(x-1)^2}{4}+\frac{(y+2)^2}{9}&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C=(1,-2),\qquad a_x=2,\qquad a_y=3 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After factoring out the quadratic coefficients, the linear terms in the groups are $-2x$ and $4y$. Halving their coefficients gives $-1$ and $2$, so the squares are $(x-1)^2$ and $(y+2)^2$. To preserve equality, subtract $1$ inside the first group and $4$ inside the second. Multiplying by the outside factors gives constants $-9$ and $-16$. Moving them to the right gives $11+9+16=36$. Since the squared terms have positive coefficients and $36>0$, the curve is an ellipse. After dividing, the larger denominator belongs to the $y$ square, so the semiaxis of length $3$ is vertical.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write $x^2-4y^2-6x-16y-11=0$ in standard form and identify its center, transverse axis, and vertices.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2-6x)-4(y^2+4y)&=11\\
(x-3)^2-9-4((y+2)^2-4)&=11\\
(x-3)^2-4(y+2)^2&=4\\
\frac{(x-3)^2}{4}-(y+2)^2&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C=(3,-2),\qquad
V_1=(1,-2),\qquad V_2=(5,-2).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor out $-4$ from the $y$ group, giving the linear term $+4y$. The square is therefore $(y+2)^2$, and subtracting $4$ inside produces $+16$ outside. The $x$ group gives $-9$. The net constant left with the squares is $+7$, so the right-hand side is $11-7=4$. Dividing by that value leaves the $x$ term positive. The transverse axis is therefore $y=-2$. Along that line the square $(x-3)^2$ equals $4$, giving two vertices $2$ units from the center.

</div>
