## Exercise 1

Dilate $P=(4,3)$ about $C=(2,1)$ by factor $-2$. Find $CP'$ and compare it with $CP$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{CP}&=(4-2,3-1)=(2,2)\\
\overrightarrow{CP'}&=-2(2,2)=(-4,-4)\\
P'&=(2,1)+(-4,-4)=(-2,-3).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
CP=\sqrt{2^2+2^2}=2\sqrt2,\qquad
CP'=\sqrt{(-4)^2+(-4)^2}=4\sqrt2.
$$

::grafico{xmin=-4 xmax=7 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="2,1,pieno,C;4,3,pieno,P;-2,-3,pieno,P'" segmenti="-2,-3,4,3" titolo="The negative sign places $P'$ on the opposite side of $C$ from $P$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale factor multiplies the components measured from $C$. Multiplying $4$ and $3$ directly would use the origin as the center. The appropriate vector goes from $C$ to $P$ and has two positive components. Multiplying by $-2$ makes both negative, so the image lies on the opposite ray. Distance is the magnitude of the vector and stays positive. The calculation confirms that the final distance is twice the initial one.

A check on the scale factor comes from recovering $P$ from $P'$. The coordinates of $P'$ relative to the center are $(-4,-4)$. Multiplying by $-\dfrac12$ gives $(2,2)$, and adding the center gives $(4,3)$. The sign can also be checked geometrically. Point $C$ is between $P$ and $P'$, and the three points lie on $y=x-1$. Substitution of all three pairs verifies that equation. The positive factor $2$ would instead give $(6,5)$ on the same ray from $C$ as $P$, although the distance would be the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A circle centered at $A=(4,1)$ encloses area $16\pi$. Dilate it about $C=(1,1)$ by factor $-\dfrac12$. Find the image equation and the enclosed area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi r^2&=16\pi,\qquad r=4\\
A'&=(1,1)-\frac12(3,0)=\left(-\frac12,1\right)\\
r'&=\frac12\cdot4=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\left(x+\frac12\right)^2+(y-1)^2=4,
\qquad
\pi(r')^2=4\pi.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original area gives radius $4$. Treat the center of the circle like any other point and multiply the vector from $C$ to $A$ by $-\dfrac12$. The image center lies to the left of $C$. The radius is a length and becomes half its original value. The area therefore becomes one-fourth of the original, also given by $\left(-\dfrac12\right)^2$. The image equation uses the square of the new radius, so its right-hand side is $4$.

The new center and radius can be checked together using the original point $B=(8,1)$, which lies $4$ units from $A$. The vector $B-C=(7,0)$ gives $B'=\left(-\dfrac52,1\right)$. This point is $2$ units from the new center $A'=\left(-\dfrac12,1\right)$, giving $(x+\dfrac12)^2=4$ in the image equation. The point originally to the right of the circle’s center moves to the left of the image center. The negative factor reverses this direction, while its square determines the reduction in area. Multiplying the area by $\dfrac12$ would give $8\pi$, corresponding to a radius other than $2$.

</div>
