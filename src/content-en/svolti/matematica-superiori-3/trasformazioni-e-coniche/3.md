## Exercise 1

Dilate $P=(4,3)$ about $C=(2,1)$ by factor $-2$. Find $CP'$ and compare it with $CP$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{CP}&=(4-2,3-1)=(2,2)\\
\overrightarrow{CP'}&=-2(2,2)=(-4,-4)\\
P'&=(2,1)+(-4,-4)=(-2,-3).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
CP=\sqrt{2^2+2^2}=2\sqrt2,\qquad
CP'=\sqrt{(-4)^2+(-4)^2}=4\sqrt2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale factor multiplies the components measured from $C$. Multiplying $4$ and $3$ directly would use the origin as the center. The appropriate vector goes from $C$ to $P$ and has two positive components. Multiplying by $-2$ makes both negative, so the image lies on the opposite ray. Distance is the magnitude of the vector and stays positive. The calculation confirms that the final distance is twice the initial one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A circle centered at $A=(4,1)$ encloses area $16\pi$. Dilate it about $C=(1,1)$ by factor $-\dfrac12$. Find the image equation and the enclosed area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi r^2&=16\pi,\qquad r=4\\
A'&=(1,1)-\frac12(3,0)=\left(-\frac12,1\right)\\
r'&=\frac12\cdot4=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\left(x+\frac12\right)^2+(y-1)^2=4,
\qquad
\pi(r')^2=4\pi.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original area gives radius $4$. Treat the center of the circle like any other point and multiply the vector from $C$ to $A$ by $-\dfrac12$. The image center lies to the left of $C$. The radius is a length and becomes half its original value. The area therefore becomes one-fourth of the original, also given by $\left(-\dfrac12\right)^2$. The image equation uses the square of the new radius, so its right-hand side is $4$.

</div>
