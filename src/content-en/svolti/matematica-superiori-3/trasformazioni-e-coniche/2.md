## Exercise 1

Reflect $y=(x-2)^2+1$ across the $x$-axis. Find the vertex and orientation of the image parabola.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=x,\qquad v=-y\\
-y&=(x-2)^2+1\\
y&=-(x-2)^2-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V=(2,1)\quad\longmapsto\quad V'=(2,-1).
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2)^2+1;-(x-2)^2-1" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="2,1,pieno,V;2,-1,pieno,V'" segmenti="2,-1,2,1" titolo="The two vertices lie on the same vertical line, equally far from the $x$-axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Reflection across the $x$-axis preserves the horizontal coordinate and changes the sign of the vertical coordinate. This changes the sign of the whole ordinate, including the constant term $1$. Since the square is nonnegative, the maximum ordinate of the reflected curve is $-1$ at $x=2$. Reflecting $(2,1)$ directly gives the same vertex and confirms the algebra. The original parabola opens upward, while its image opens downward.

Points away from the vertex clarify the sign change too. On the original parabola the abscissas $1$ and $3$ both have ordinate $2$, because both are one unit from $2$. Their images have ordinate $-2$, as the new equation predicts. Changing only the sign of the square while keeping $+1$ would leave the image vertex at height $1$. That would describe reflection across $y=1$. Here the midpoint between $(2,1)$ and $(2,-1)$ is $(2,0)$, on the required axis. The segments joining the other point pairs are perpendicular to that axis as well.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Reflect the ellipse $\dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9}+\dfrac{(y+2)^2}{4}=1$ across $y=x$. Find the image center and semiaxes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=y,\qquad v=x\\
\frac{(y-1)^2}{9}+\frac{(x+2)^2}{4}&=1\\
\frac{(x+2)^2}{4}+\frac{(y-1)^2}{9}&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C'=(-2,1),\qquad a_x=2,\qquad a_y=3.
$$

::grafico{curve="1+3*cos(t),-2+2*sin(t),0,6.283185307;-2+2*cos(t),1+3*sin(t),0,6.283185307" f="x" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="4,-2,pieno,P;-2,4,pieno,P'" segmenti="4,-2,-2,4" legenda="y=x;\dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9}+\dfrac{(y+2)^2}{4}=1;\dfrac{(x+2)^2}{4}+\dfrac{(y-1)^2}{9}=1" titolo="The right vertex of the first ellipse becomes the upper vertex of its reflection."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Reflection across the bisector swaps the coordinates. The center $(1,-2)$ therefore moves to $(-2,1)$. The horizontal span of the original ellipse becomes the vertical span of its reflection, because horizontal segments become vertical. Consistently, after swapping symbols the denominator $9$ stays with the old abscissa, which is now $y$. The lengths of the semiaxes are preserved, but the semiaxis of length $3$ becomes vertical. For example, the right vertex $(4,-2)$ moves to the upper vertex $(-2,4)$, which is $3$ units from the new center.

The midpoint between $(4,-2)$ and $(-2,4)$ is $(1,1)$, on the angle bisector. Their joining segment has slope $\dfrac{4-(-2)}{-2-4}=-1$, so it is perpendicular to the line of slope $1$. Both vertices are the same distance from the midpoint. This geometric check confirms the coordinate swap and distinguishes reflection across the bisector from reflection across the $y$-axis, which would leave the major axis horizontal. The new rightmost point $(0,1)$ is also $2$ units from the new center and is the image of the original point $(1,0)$.

</div>
