## Exercise 1

Reflect $y=(x-2)^2+1$ across the $x$-axis. Find the vertex and orientation of the image parabola.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=x,\qquad v=-y\\
-y&=(x-2)^2+1\\
y&=-(x-2)^2-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
V=(2,1)\quad\longmapsto\quad V'=(2,-1).
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2)^2+1;-(x-2)^2-1" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="2,1,pieno,V;2,-1,pieno,V'" segmenti="2,-1,2,1" titolo="The two vertices lie on the same vertical line, equally far from the $x$-axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Reflection across the $x$-axis preserves the horizontal coordinate and changes the sign of the vertical coordinate. This changes the sign of the whole ordinate, including the constant term $1$. Since the square is nonnegative, the maximum ordinate of the reflected curve is $-1$ at $x=2$. Reflecting $(2,1)$ directly gives the same vertex and confirms the algebra. The original parabola opens upward, while its image opens downward.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Reflect the ellipse $\dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9}+\dfrac{(y+2)^2}{4}=1$ across $y=x$. Find the image center and semiaxes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=y,\qquad v=x\\
\frac{(y-1)^2}{9}+\frac{(x+2)^2}{4}&=1\\
\frac{(x+2)^2}{4}+\frac{(y-1)^2}{9}&=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
C'=(-2,1),\qquad a_x=2,\qquad a_y=3.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Reflection across the bisector swaps the coordinates. The center $(1,-2)$ therefore moves to $(-2,1)$. The horizontal span of the original ellipse becomes the vertical span of its reflection, because horizontal segments become vertical. Consistently, after swapping symbols the denominator $9$ stays with the old abscissa, which is now $y$. The lengths of the semiaxes are preserved, but the semiaxis of length $3$ becomes vertical. For example, the right vertex $(4,-2)$ moves to the upper vertex $(-2,4)$, which is $3$ units from the new center.

</div>
