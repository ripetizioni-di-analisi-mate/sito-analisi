## Exercise 1

Find the tangent line of slope $4$ to $y=x^2$ and its contact point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2&=4x+q\\
x^2-4x-q&=0\\
\Delta&=(-4)^2-4(1)(-q)\\
&=16+4q.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\Delta=0\ \Longrightarrow\ q=-4.
$$

$$
(x-2)^2=0\ \Longrightarrow\ x=2,\quad y=4 .
$$

$$
y=4x-4,\qquad T=(2,4).
$$

::grafico{f="x^2;4*x-4;4*x" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=18 punti="2,4,pieno,T;0,0;4,16,pieno,Q" legenda="y=x^2;y=4x-4;y=4x" titolo="The dashed tangent passes through $T$. The parallel secant with zero intercept meets the parabola at O and Q."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope is given, so the only unknown line parameter is its intercept $q$. Substitution gives a quadratic with leading coefficient $1$ for every $q$. The discriminant can therefore be set to zero, giving a repeated root in the intersection equation. At $q=-4$ that root is $2$. The line ordinate $4\cdot2-4=4$ agrees with the ordinate of the parabola. Thus the curves share a single point, where the difference of their ordinates is the vanishing square $(x-2)^2$.

The intercept also follows from the difference $x^2-(4x+q)=(x-2)^2-(4+q)$. Its smallest value occurs at $x=2$. For $q=-4$ that minimum is zero, so the line touches the parabola without crossing it. If the intercept were $q=0$, the intersection equation would be $(x-2)^2=4$ and give abscissas $0$ and $4$. If $q=-5$, the square would have to be $-1$, so the line would stay below the parabola. The three positions explain why a zero discriminant identifies tangency at a fixed slope.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the tangents to $y=x^2$ through $(0,-1)$. Determine the contact points.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=mx-1\\
x^2-mx+1&=0\\
\Delta&=m^2-4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\Delta=0\ \Longrightarrow\ m=-2\quad\text{or}\quad m=2.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m=2&:\quad (x-1)^2=0,\qquad T_1=(1,1)\\
m=-2&:\quad (x+1)^2=0,\qquad T_2=(-1,1).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
y=2x-1,\qquad y=-2x-1 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given point fixes the vertical intercept at $-1$ for every nonvertical line in the family. The vertical line $x=0$ also passes through it but has a simple intersection at $(0,0)$, because substituting $x=0$ leaves $y=0$. For the other lines, tangency requires a repeated root of the quadratic in $x$. The discriminant depends on the square of the slope, so the two solutions are opposites. The lines share their intercept and the contact points are symmetric about the vertical axis of the parabola.

To check tangency, subtract each line ordinate from the parabola value. For slope $2$ this gives $x^2-(2x-1)=(x-1)^2$, and for slope $-2$ it gives $x^2-(-2x-1)=(x+1)^2$. Both differences are always nonnegative, and each vanishes only at its contact point. Both tangents also pass through $(0,-1)$ because both intercepts equal $-1$. Keeping only $m=2$ would lose the other line, which can also be found by reflecting the first across the $y$-axis that contains the given point.

</div>
