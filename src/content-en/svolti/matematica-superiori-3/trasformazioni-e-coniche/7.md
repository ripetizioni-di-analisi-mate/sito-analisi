## Exercise 1

Find the tangent line of slope $4$ to $y=x^2$ and its contact point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2&=4x+q\\
x^2-4x-q&=0\\
\Delta&=(-4)^2-4(1)(-q)\\
&=16+4q.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\Delta=0\ \Longrightarrow\ q=-4.
$$

$$
(x-2)^2=0\ \Longrightarrow\ x=2,\quad y=4 .
$$

$$
y=4x-4,\qquad T=(2,4).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope is given, so the only unknown line parameter is its intercept $q$. Substitution gives a quadratic with leading coefficient $1$ for every $q$. The discriminant can therefore be set to zero, giving a repeated root in the intersection equation. At $q=-4$ that root is $2$. The line ordinate $4\cdot2-4=4$ agrees with the ordinate of the parabola. Thus the curves share a single point, where the difference of their ordinates is the vanishing square $(x-2)^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the tangents to $y=x^2$ through $(0,-1)$. Determine the contact points.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=mx-1\\
x^2-mx+1&=0\\
\Delta&=m^2-4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\Delta=0\ \Longrightarrow\ m=-2\quad\text{or}\quad m=2.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m=2&:\quad (x-1)^2=0,\qquad T_1=(1,1)\\
m=-2&:\quad (x+1)^2=0,\qquad T_2=(-1,1).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
y=2x-1,\qquad y=-2x-1 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given point fixes the vertical intercept at $-1$ for every nonvertical line in the family. The vertical line $x=0$ also passes through it but has a simple intersection at $(0,0)$, because substituting $x=0$ leaves $y=0$. For the other lines, tangency requires a repeated root of the quadratic in $x$. The discriminant depends on the square of the slope, so the two solutions are opposites. The lines share their intercept and the contact points are symmetric about the vertical axis of the parabola.

</div>
