## Exercise 1

Translate $P=(-3,4)$ and $Q=(1,1)$ by $(2,-5)$. Find the distance between the points before and after translation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P'&=(-3+2,4-5)=(-1,-1)\\
Q'&=(1+2,1-5)=(3,-4)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ&=\sqrt{(1-(-3))^2+(1-4)^2}\\
&=\sqrt{4^2+(-3)^2}=5\\
P'Q'&=\sqrt{(3-(-1))^2+(-4-(-1))^2}\\
&=\sqrt{4^2+(-3)^2}=5 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-3,4,pieno,P;1,1,pieno,Q;-1,-1,pieno,P';3,-4,pieno,Q'" linee="-3,4 1,1;-1,-1 3,-4" vettori="-3,4,-1,-1;1,1,3,-4" titolo="The arrows represent the same vector. The two segments are equal in length."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The vector is a shift two units right and five units down. Applying the same shift to both points changes their positions, but the difference in their abscissas is still $4$ and the difference in their ordinates is still $-3$. The distance calculation therefore uses the same two legs of lengths $4$ and $3$ before and after translation. In the figure $P'Q'$ is parallel to $PQ$ because the two coordinate differences remain proportional with ratio $1$. Their common length is $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Translate $y=2x^2$ by $(-3,2)$. Write the image equation and find where $(1,2)$ moves.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x=u-3&\ \Longrightarrow\ u=x+3\\
y=v+2&\ \Longrightarrow\ v=y-2\\
v=2u^2&\ \Longrightarrow\ y-2=2(x+3)^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
y=2(x+3)^2+2 .
$$

$$
P'=(1-3,2+2)=(-2,4).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $(1,2)$ moves by direct addition of the vector. For the equation, however, start from an image point $(x,y)$ and recover its original coordinates $(u,v)$. This explains why $x+3$ appears inside the square even though the translation moves left. Substituting $x=-2$ in the new equation gives $y=2(-2+3)^2+2=4$, so the image point satisfies the new curve. The vertex has also shifted by $(-3,2)$ and the opening coefficient $2$ has stayed the same.

</div>
