## Exercise 1

Find the intersections of $y=x^2+1$ and $y=9-x^2$ and determine which curve is higher at $x=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+1&=9-x^2\\
2x^2&=8\\
x&=\pm2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
A=(-2,5),\qquad B=(2,5).
$$

$$
x=0:\qquad 9-0^2=9>1=0^2+1.
$$

::grafico{f="x^2+1;9-x^2" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=10 uguale=1 punti="-2,5,pieno,A;2,5,pieno,B;0,1;0,9" titolo="The parabolas meet at $A$ and $B$. Their ordinates on the vertical axis are $1$ and $9$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

A common point must have the same abscissa and ordinate on both curves. At a given abscissa each formula gives a single ordinate, so the two values can be equated. The resulting equation gives two distinct abscissas. Both give $y=5$, which also satisfies $9-x^2=9-4=5$. Comparing heights at a specified abscissa instead requires evaluating the ordinates separately. At $x=0$ the downward-opening curve has value $9$ and stands $8$ units above the other one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find all common points of $x^2+y^2=8$ and $y=x^2-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+(x^2-2)^2&=8\\
s+(s-2)^2&=8,\qquad s=x^2\geq0\\
s^2-3s-4&=0\\
(s-4)(s+1)&=0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
s=4,\qquad x=\pm2,\qquad y=4-2=2 .
$$

$$
A=(-2,2),\qquad B=(2,2).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substitute the ordinate given by the parabola into the circle equation. The resulting equation contains $x^2$ and its square, so write $s$ for $x^2$. The new equation has roots $4$ and $-1$, but the latter cannot be the square of a real number and must be discarded. The root $4$ gives two real abscissas. The parabola gives ordinate $2$ for both, and they satisfy $4+4=8$ on the circle. The point $(2,-2)$ is on the circle, but the parabola requires the positive ordinate.

</div>
