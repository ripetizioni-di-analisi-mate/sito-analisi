## Exercise 1

The midpoint of $AB$ is $M(1,2)$ and the known endpoint is $A(3,6)$. Find $B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_B&=2\cdot1-3=-1,\\[4pt]
y_B&=2\cdot2-6=-2 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad B=(-1,-2) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From $A$ to $M$, the horizontal coordinate decreases by $2$ and the vertical coordinate by $4$. Repeating the same displacement from $M$ to $B$ gives $B=(-1,-2)$. Algebraically, twice the average coordinate is the sum of the two endpoint coordinates, so subtract the known coordinate. Averaging again with $\dfrac{3-1}{2}=1$ and $\dfrac{6-2}{2}=2$ recovers $M$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The endpoints $A$ and $B$ of a vertical segment are $6$ units apart. Its midpoint is $M(2,-1)$. Find both endpoints, given that $A$ is the lower one.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AM=MB&=\frac{6}{2}=3,\\[4pt]
x_A=x_B&=2,\\[4pt]
y_A&=-1-3=-4,\\[4pt]
y_B&=-1+3=2.
\end{aligned}
\qquad A=(2,-4),\quad B=(2,2) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a vertical segment the horizontal coordinate is the same at every point, so both endpoints have horizontal coordinate $2$. The midpoint divides the length $6$ into two lengths of $3$. To find the lower endpoint $A$, subtract $3$ from the vertical coordinate of $M$. To find $B$, add $3$. The resulting vertical coordinates differ by $6$ and average to $-1$, as required.

</div>
