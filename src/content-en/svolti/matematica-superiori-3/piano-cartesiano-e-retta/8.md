## Exercise 1

The midpoint of $AB$ is $M(1,2)$ and the known endpoint is $A(3,6)$. Find $B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_B&=2\cdot1-3=-1,\\[4pt]
y_B&=2\cdot2-6=-2 .
\end{aligned}
\qquad B=(-1,-2) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From $A$ to $M$, the horizontal coordinate decreases by $2$ and the vertical coordinate by $4$. Repeating the same displacement from $M$ to $B$ gives $B=(-1,-2)$. Algebraically, twice the average coordinate is the sum of the two endpoint coordinates, so subtract the known coordinate. Averaging again with $\dfrac{3-1}{2}=1$ and $\dfrac{6-2}{2}=2$ recovers $M$.

The construction can also be read as point symmetry about $M$. The point $A$ is two units right and four units above $M$, so $B$ is two units left and four units below. This is why both components relative to the centre change sign. The line containing the segment offers another check. From $M$ to $A$, the displacement ratio is $4$ to $2$, giving slope $2$. The rule through $M(1,2)$ is $y=2x$, which at $x=-1$ produces the output $-2$ of $B$. Both checks confirm the position found. Interchanging $A$ and $B$ gives the same construction. From endpoint $B$, first add $(2,4)$ to reach $M$, then add the same displacement to reach $A$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The endpoints $A$ and $B$ of a vertical segment are $6$ units apart. Its midpoint is $M(2,-1)$. Find both endpoints, given that $A$ is the lower one.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AM=MB&=\frac{6}{2}=3,\\[4pt]
x_A=x_B&=2,\\[4pt]
y_A&=-1-3=-4,\\[4pt]
y_B&=-1+3=2.
\end{aligned}
\qquad A=(2,-4),\quad B=(2,2) .
$$

$$
\begin{cases} y_A+y_B=-2\\ y_B-y_A=6\end{cases}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y_A=-4,\quad y_B=2 .
$$

::grafico{curve="2,t,-4,2" legenda="x=2" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-5 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="2,-4,pieno,A;2,-1,pieno,M;2,2,pieno,B" titolo="$A$ and $B$ are three units from $M$ in opposite directions."}

<div class="spiegazione">

In a vertical segment the horizontal coordinate is the same at every point, so both endpoints have horizontal coordinate $2$. The midpoint divides the length $6$ into two lengths of $3$. To find the lower endpoint $A$, subtract $3$ from the vertical coordinate of $M$. To find $B$, add $3$. The resulting vertical coordinates differ by $6$ and average to $-1$, as required.

The requirement that $A$ is lower fixes the order of the endpoints and assigns the smaller output to $A$. Algebraically, the vertical coordinates must have sum $2(-1)=-2$ and difference $y_B-y_A=6$. Adding gives $2y_B=4$, so $y_B=2$ and $y_A=-4$. The common horizontal coordinate is $2$ because the segment is vertical and passes through the midpoint. The distance check uses difference $2-(-4)=6$ and zero horizontal difference. Although one endpoint has negative output and the other positive output, length depends on their difference, while their sum locates the midpoint.

</div>
