## Exercise 1

Find the distance from the origin to the line $3x+4y-5=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{|3\cdot0+4\cdot0-5|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}\\[5pt]
&=\frac{5}{\sqrt{25}}=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At $P(0,0)$ the coordinate terms are zero, leaving the constant $-5$. Its absolute value is $5$ because the distance is a length. The quantity $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$ is the length of the normal vector $(3,4)$. Dividing by $5$ measures the displacement from the line in units of length. In this case it is $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $P(1,4)$ and $r:y=x$. Find the foot $H$ of the perpendicular from $P$ and calculate the distance $PH$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y-4&=-(x-1),\\[4pt]
x=-x+5&\iff x=\frac{5}{2},\\[4pt]
H&=\left(\frac{5}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right),\\[4pt]
PH&=\sqrt{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2+
                \left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)^2}
=\frac{3\sqrt 2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x" segmenti="1,4,2.5,2.5" punti="1,4,pieno,P;2.5,2.5,pieno,H" xmin=-1 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=6 uguale=1 titolo="The segment $PH$ meets $r$ at a right angle."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The line $r$ has slope $1$, so the perpendicular has slope $-1$. Requiring it to pass through $P$ gives $y=-x+5$. The point $H$ also lies on $r$, so its coordinates agree and it can be found by equating $x$ and $-x+5$. From $P$ to $H$ the displacement is $\left(\dfrac{3}{2},-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$. The Pythagorean theorem then gives $PH=\dfrac{3\sqrt 2}{2}$. The distance formula applied to $x-y=0$ gives the same value, $\dfrac{|1-4|}{\sqrt{2}}$.

</div>
