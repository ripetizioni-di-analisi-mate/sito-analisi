## Exercise 1

Find the distance between $A(1,1)$ and $B(4,5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x&=4-1=3,\\[4pt]
\Delta y&=5-1=4,\\[4pt]
AB&=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=\sqrt{25}=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{segmenti="1,1,4,1;4,1,4,5" linee="1,1 4,5" punti="1,1,pieno,A;4,5,pieno,B;4,1,pieno,C" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=7 uguale=1 titolo="The direct path is the hypotenuse of triangle $ACB$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving from $A$ to $B$ takes $3$ units horizontally and $4$ vertically. The point $C(4,1)$ splits the movement into two perpendicular segments. These segments are the legs of triangle $ACB$, and the direct segment $AB$ is its hypotenuse. This is why we add the squares of the legs and then take the square root. The distance is $5$ units.

The choice of $C$ keeps the horizontal coordinate of $B$ and the vertical coordinate of $A$. Therefore $AC$ has only a horizontal component, and $CB$ only a vertical one. One could also go via $(1,5)$, drawing the vertical leg first. The leg lengths would still be $4$ and $3$, giving the same square root. Reversing the direction also leaves the distance unchanged because the differences become $-3$ and $-4$, whose squares stay $9$ and $16$. The number $7$ obtained by adding the legs would instead measure the broken route via $C$. Subtracting the square of the horizontal displacement from the squared distance gives $25-9=16$. This recovers the vertical leg's length, $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $P(t,-1)$. Find the values of $t$ for which $P$ is $5$ units away from $A(-2,3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AP&=\sqrt{(t+2)^2+(-1-3)^2},\\[4pt]
AP=5&\iff (t+2)^2+16=25\\[4pt]
&\iff (t+2)^2=9\\[4pt]
&\iff t+2=\pm3.
\end{aligned}
\qquad t\in\{-5,1\} .
$$

::grafico{curve="-2+5*cos(t),3+5*sin(t),0,6.28318530718" f="-1" legenda="y=-1;(x+2)^2+(y-3)^2=25" xmin=-8 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=9 uguale=1 punti="-2,3,pieno,A;-5,-1,pieno,P_1;1,-1,pieno,P_2" segmenti="-2,3,-5,-1;-2,3,1,-1" titolo="The line meets the circle at the two points at distance $5$ from $A$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The vertical coordinate of $P$ is fixed, so the vertical separation from $A$ is always $4$. To make the hypotenuse $5$ units long, the horizontal length must be $3$, since $25-16=9$. The expression $t+2$ is the signed horizontal displacement. It can equal $3$ to the right or $-3$ to the left. Both inputs give a triangle with legs $3$ and $4$, so each gives the required distance.

Squaring preserves equality because both quantities being compared are nonnegative lengths. The line containing $P$ is horizontal and its vertical separation from $A$ is always $4$. At input $-2$, the point would lie directly below $A$, at distance $4$, the smallest possible on that line. To increase the distance to $5$, a horizontal displacement of magnitude $3$ suffices. The two inputs found are symmetric about $-2$. On the graph both positions lie on the circle of centre $A$ and radius $5$, so the horizontal line goes through exactly the two points at the required distance.

</div>
