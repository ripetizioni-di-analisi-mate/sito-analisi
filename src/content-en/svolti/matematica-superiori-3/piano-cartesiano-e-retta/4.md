## Exercise 1

Find the line parallel to $y=2x+7$ through $P(2,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=2x+q,\\[4pt]
1&=2\cdot2+q,\\[4pt]
q&=-3,\qquad y=2x-3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope $2$ is kept to preserve the direction. The intercept is fixed by requiring the horizontal coordinate $2$ to have output $1$. Substitution gives $q=-3$, which differs from the original line's intercept $7$. Hence the lines are distinct. At every horizontal coordinate, their two outputs differ by $7-(-3)=10$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the line through $P(-2,4)$ parallel to the vertical line $3x-9=0$. Verify that the two lines are distinct.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x-9=0&\iff x=3,\\[4pt]
r&:\ x=x_P=-2,\\[4pt]
r\cap \{x=3\}&=\varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation $3x-9=0$ fixes the horizontal coordinate and leaves the vertical one free. To retain the vertical direction, the required line must also have a fixed horizontal coordinate. Since it passes through $P$, that coordinate must be $-2$. No point can have both $3$ and $-2$ as its horizontal coordinate, so the two lines are distinct and parallel.

</div>
