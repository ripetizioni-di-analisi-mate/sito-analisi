## Exercise 1

Find the line parallel to $y=2x+7$ through $P(2,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y&=2x+q,\\[4pt]
1&=2\cdot2+q,\\[4pt]
q&=-3,\qquad y=2x-3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The slope $2$ is kept to preserve the direction. The intercept is fixed by requiring the horizontal coordinate $2$ to have output $1$. Substitution gives $q=-3$, which differs from the original line's intercept $7$. Hence the lines are distinct. At every horizontal coordinate, their two outputs differ by $7-(-3)=10$.

The same line follows from point-slope form. Since the slope is $2$, moving from $P(2,1)$ to a point $(x,y)$ gives the vertical change $y-1=2(x-2)$. Expanding recovers $y=2x-3$. Comparing with the given line gives an output difference of $10$ at every input. At $x=2$, for instance, the given line outputs $11$, and the required line outputs $1$. The vertical separation is constant, but the distance between slanted lines must be measured along a perpendicular segment, which generally has a different length. The distance from $P$ to the given line $2x-y+7=0$ is $\dfrac{10}{\sqrt5}=2\sqrt5$. The two values measure different segments between the same lines.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the line through $P(-2,4)$ parallel to the vertical line $3x-9=0$. Verify that the two lines are distinct.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x-9=0&\iff x=3,\\[4pt]
r&:\ x=x_P=-2,\\[4pt]
r\cap \{x=3\}&=\varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned} 3(-2)+c=0&\iff c=6,\\[4pt] 3x+6=0&\iff x=-2 .\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="-2,t,-2,6;3,t,-2,6" legenda="x=-2;x=3" xmin=-4 xmax=5 ymin=-2 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-2,4,pieno,P;3,4,pieno,Q" segmenti="-2,4,3,4" titolo="The horizontal segment $PQ$ is perpendicular to both vertical lines."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation $3x-9=0$ fixes the horizontal coordinate and leaves the vertical one free. To retain the vertical direction, the required line must also have a fixed horizontal coordinate. Since it passes through $P$, that coordinate must be $-2$. No point can have both $3$ and $-2$ as its horizontal coordinate, so the two lines are distinct and parallel.

To check the result in standard form, keep $3$ as the coefficient of $x$ and require $3(-2)+c=0$. This gives $c=6$ and hence $3x+6=0$, or $x=-2$. With no variable $y$, every vertical coordinate is allowed on each line. At height $4$, for instance, the given line contains $(3,4)$ and the required line contains $(-2,4)$. These points share a height and are separated horizontally by $|3-(-2)|=5$ units. Their segment is perpendicular to the two vertical lines and also measures their distance. The same reasoning works at any height other than that of $P$.

</div>
