## Exercise 1

Find the perpendicular to $y=2x-3$ through $P(4,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2m_\perp&=-1,\qquad m_\perp=-\frac{1}{2},\\[4pt]
y-1&=-\frac{1}{2}(x-4),\\[4pt]
y&=-\frac{1}{2}x+3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given line has slope $2$, so a perpendicular slope must be $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ to make the product $-1$. Its output must be $1$ at the horizontal coordinate $4$. This is why the vertical displacement $y-1$ equals the slope times the horizontal displacement $x-4$. Expanding gives an intercept of $3$. Substituting $4$ into the rule returns the output $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the standard form of the perpendicular to $2x-3y+6=0$ through $P(1,2)$ and verify the coefficient condition.

$$
\begin{aligned}
r&:\ 3x+2y+c=0,\\[4pt]
3\cdot1+2\cdot2+c&=0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad c=-7,\\[4pt]
2\cdot3+(-3)\cdot2&=0.
\end{aligned}
\qquad r:\ 3x+2y-7=0 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of the required line must satisfy $2a-3b=0$. The pair $(3,2)$ satisfies this equation and fixes the direction, while the constant is still free. Passing through $P(1,2)$ requires adding $-7$ to $3+4$. The final identity confirms that the coefficients do give perpendicularity. The slopes $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $-\dfrac{3}{2}$ give the same check.

</div>
