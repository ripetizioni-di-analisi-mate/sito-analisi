## Exercise 1

Find the midpoint of the segment joining $A(1,-1)$ and $B(5,3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_M&=\frac{1+5}{2}=3,\\[4pt]
y_M&=\frac{-1+3}{2}=1.
\end{aligned}
\qquad M=(3,1) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving from $A$ to $B$ adds $4$ to both coordinates. Halfway along the segment, therefore, $2$ is added to each coordinate. Averaging the coordinates gives exactly this displacement and produces $M=(3,1)$. Starting at $M$ and moving another $2$ units horizontally and $2$ vertically reaches $B$, confirming that the two parts have the same length.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $A(2,-3)$ and $B(t,7)$. Find $t$ so that the midpoint of $AB$ lies on the line $y=x-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=\left(\frac{2+t}{2},\frac{-3+7}{2}\right)
=\left(\frac{2+t}{2},2\right),\\[4pt]
2&=\frac{2+t}{2}-2,\\[4pt]
8&=2+t,\qquad t=6.
\end{aligned}
\qquad M=(4,2) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The average vertical coordinate is $2$ for every value of $t$. On the given line, the output $2$ occurs only at $x=4$, because $2=x-2$. Therefore set the average horizontal coordinate to $4$. The value $t=6$ gives $M=(4,2)$, which satisfies $2=4-2$.

</div>
