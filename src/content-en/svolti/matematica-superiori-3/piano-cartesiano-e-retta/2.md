## Exercise 1

Find the midpoint of the segment joining $A(1,-1)$ and $B(5,3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_M&=\frac{1+5}{2}=3,\\[4pt]
y_M&=\frac{-1+3}{2}=1.
\end{aligned}
\qquad M=(3,1) .
$$

::grafico{curve="1+4*t,-1+4*t,0,1" legenda="y=x-2" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-3 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="1,-1,pieno,A;3,1,pieno,M;5,3,pieno,B" segmenti="1,-1,3,-1;3,-1,3,1;3,1,5,1;5,1,5,3" titolo="From $A$ to $M$ and from $M$ to $B$, the displacement $(2,2)$ is repeated."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Moving from $A$ to $B$ adds $4$ to both coordinates. Halfway along the segment, therefore, $2$ is added to each coordinate. Averaging the coordinates gives exactly this displacement and produces $M=(3,1)$. Starting at $M$ and moving another $2$ units horizontally and $2$ vertically reaches $B$, confirming that the two parts have the same length.

The midpoint does not depend on the choice of starting endpoint, because swapping the addends leaves the averages unchanged. If we start at $B$, the displacement towards $A$ is now $(-4,-4)$, and halfway along we add $(-2,-2)$ to $B$. We reach $(3,1)$ once again. Compare the displacements to check collinearity. The displacement from $A$ to $M$ is $(2,2)$, and from $M$ to $B$ it is again $(2,2)$. The pairs agree, so the direction is the same, and both segments have length $\sqrt{2^2+2^2}=2\sqrt2$. The point $(3,1)$ also lies within the rectangle with opposite vertices $A$ and $B$. Its coordinates lie strictly inside both intervals set by the endpoints, as expected for an interior point of this segment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $A(2,-3)$ and $B(t,7)$. Find $t$ so that the midpoint of $AB$ lies on the line $y=x-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=\left(\frac{2+t}{2},\frac{-3+7}{2}\right)
=\left(\frac{2+t}{2},2\right),\\[4pt]
2&=\frac{2+t}{2}-2,\\[4pt]
8&=2+t,\qquad t=6.
\end{aligned}
\qquad M=(4,2) .
$$

$$
(4,2)+(2,5)=(6,7)=B .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The average vertical coordinate is $2$ for every value of $t$. On the given line, the output $2$ occurs only at $x=4$, because $2=x-2$. Therefore set the average horizontal coordinate to $4$. The value $t=6$ gives $M=(4,2)$, which satisfies $2=4-2$.

As $t$ varies, the point $B$ moves along the horizontal line $y=7$. The midpoint also moves along a horizontal line, at height $2$. So the task is to intersect the line of possible midpoints with $y=x-2$. After finding input $4$, recover endpoint $B$ by repeating the displacement from $A$ to $M$. Going from $(2,-3)$ to $(4,2)$ adds $(2,5)$. Adding it again reaches $(6,7)$, with the vertical coordinate stated in the question. Since $y=x-2$ meets $y=2$ only once, no other value of $t$ works. Algebraically, rewriting $y=x-2$ as $x-y=2$ checks the midpoint immediately, since $4-2=2$. Endpoint $A$ need not satisfy this equation because it is imposed only on $M$.

</div>
