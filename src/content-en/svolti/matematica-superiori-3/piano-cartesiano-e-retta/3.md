## Exercise 1

Find the equation of the line through $A(1,1)$ and $B(3,5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=\frac{5-1}{3-1}=2,\\[4pt]
y-1&=2(x-1),\\[4pt]
y&=2x-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x-1" segmenti="1,1,3,1;3,1,3,5" punti="1,1,pieno,A;3,5,pieno,B;0,-1" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=7 titolo="From $A$ to $B$, the rise is $4$ and the horizontal displacement is $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Between the two points, the horizontal coordinate increases by $2$ and the vertical coordinate increases by $4$. The line therefore rises by $2$ for each horizontal unit. Using $A$ as the starting point, the horizontal displacement is $x-1$ and the vertical displacement is $y-1$, which gives the second line of working. At $x=3$, the equation gives $5$, the vertical coordinate of the second point.

Using $B$ as the starting point gives the same equation because the ratio of displacements is constant along the line. The equation $y-5=2(x-3)$ indeed gives $y=2x-1$. The intercept $-1$ can also be reconstructed on the graph from $A$. Reaching the vertical axis requires decreasing the input by one unit, and with slope $2$ the output drops two units, from $1$ to $-1$. The data therefore fix both direction and position. The reversed ratio $\dfrac24$ would instead give a rise of $1$ for a horizontal change of $2$, inconsistent with the actual separation of $A$ and $B$. The standard form $2x-y-1=0$ gives a further check without isolating $y$. Both $2\cdot1-1-1$ and $2\cdot3-5-1$ equal zero, so both points satisfy the equation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the line through $A(-2,1)$ and $B(-2,5)$ and determine its axis intercepts, if they exist.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_A=x_B&=-2,\qquad y_B-y_A=4,\\[4pt]
r&:\ x=-2,\\[4pt]
r\cap\{y=0\}&=\{(-2,0)\},\\[4pt]
r\cap\{x=0\}&=\varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="-2,t,-2,6" legenda="x=-2" xmin=-4 xmax=2 ymin=-2 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-2,1,pieno,A;-2,5,pieno,B;-2,0,pieno,Q" titolo="The vertical line meets the $x$-axis at $Q$ and stays to the left of the $y$-axis."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The points are distinct because their vertical coordinates differ. Their horizontal coordinates agree, so the joining line is vertical and every point on it has horizontal coordinate $-2$. For the horizontal intercept, set $y=0$ and keep $x=-2$. The vertical axis would require $x=0$, which is impossible on the given line. In this case the form $y=mx+q$ would require dividing by zero to find a slope, whereas $x=-2$ describes all the points with the same horizontal coordinate.

The distance between $A$ and $B$ here is the absolute difference of their vertical coordinates, namely $4$, because they share the same horizontal coordinate. This distinguishes a vertical line from a horizontal line, which would require zero vertical difference and allow the horizontal coordinate to vary. On the graph, the given vertical line is parallel to and to the left of the $y$-axis. It crosses the $x$-axis at height $0$. In standard form it is $x+2=0$. Substituting either $A$ or $B$ gives zero on the left, while any point on the vertical axis has $x=0$ and gives $2$.

</div>
