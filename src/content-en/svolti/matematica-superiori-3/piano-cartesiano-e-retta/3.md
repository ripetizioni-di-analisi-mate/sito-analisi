## Exercise 1

Find the equation of the line through $A(1,1)$ and $B(3,5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=\frac{5-1}{3-1}=2,\\[4pt]
y-1&=2(x-1),\\[4pt]
y&=2x-1 .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2*x-1" segmenti="1,1,3,1;3,1,3,5" punti="1,1,pieno,A;3,5,pieno,B;0,-1" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=7 titolo="From $A$ to $B$, the rise is $4$ and the horizontal displacement is $2$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Between the two points, the horizontal coordinate increases by $2$ and the vertical coordinate increases by $4$. The line therefore rises by $2$ for each horizontal unit. Using $A$ as the starting point, the horizontal displacement is $x-1$ and the vertical displacement is $y-1$, which gives the second line of working. At $x=3$, the equation gives $5$, the vertical coordinate of the second point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the line through $A(-2,1)$ and $B(-2,5)$ and determine its axis intercepts, if they exist.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_A=x_B&=-2,\qquad y_B-y_A=4,\\[4pt]
r&:\ x=-2,\\[4pt]
r\cap\{y=0\}&=\{(-2,0)\},\\[4pt]
r\cap\{x=0\}&=\varnothing .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The points are distinct because their vertical coordinates differ. Their horizontal coordinates agree, so the joining line is vertical and every point on it has horizontal coordinate $-2$. For the horizontal intercept, set $y=0$ and keep $x=-2$. The vertical axis would require $x=0$, which is impossible on the given line. In this case the form $y=mx+q$ would require dividing by zero to find a slope, whereas $x=-2$ describes all the points with the same horizontal coordinate.

</div>
