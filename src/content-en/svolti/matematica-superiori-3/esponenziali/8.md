## Exercise 1

Find the range of $f(x)=2^{x+1}+4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^{x+1}>0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x)>4,\\[4pt]
y>4&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x=\log_2(y-4)-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Im}(f)=(4,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every value of $f$ exceeds $4$ because a positive power is added to the level $4$. To establish that the range is the entire interval, we also need the converse. For any fixed $y>4$, the argument $y-4$ is positive, so the logarithm giving $x$ is defined. Substituting that value of $x$ gives precisely $f(x)=y$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the range and monotonicity of $g(x)=3-2^x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^x>0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g(x)<3,\\[4pt]
y<3&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\log_2(3-y),\\[4pt]
x_1<x_2&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3-2^{x_1}>3-2^{x_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Im}(g)=(-\infty,3).
$$

::grafico{f="3-2^x" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=4 asintoti="y=3" punti="0,2;1,1" titolo="The graph of $g$ decreases and approaches the asymptote $y=3$ from below"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The power $2^x$ takes every positive value. Subtracting it from $3$ therefore gives every value below $3$. As $2^x$ increases with $x$, the amount being subtracted grows and $g$ decreases. As $x$ decreases without bound, the term being subtracted tends to zero, so the graph has asymptote $y=3$ and stays entirely below it.

</div>
