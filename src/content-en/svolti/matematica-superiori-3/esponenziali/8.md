## Exercise 1

Find the range of $f(x)=2^{x+1}+4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^{x+1}>0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x)>4,\\[4pt]
y>4&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x=\log_2(y-4)-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Im}(f)=(4,+\infty).
$$

$$
f(-1)=5,\qquad f(1)=8.
$$

::grafico{f="2^(x+1)+4" xmin=-4 xmax=2.3 ymin=0 ymax=13.8 asintoti="y=4" punti="-1,5,pieno,A;1,8,pieno,B" legenda="2^{x+1}+4" titolo="The curve stays above $y=4$. Points $A$ and $B$ give two heights that are actually attained"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The range consists of the heights that the function actually attains. Because $2^{x+1}>0$, every height of $f(x)=2^{x+1}+4$ exceeds $4$. To show that every higher value occurs, fix $y>4$ and solve $2^{x+1}=y-4$. The difference $y-4$ is positive, so the logarithm gives $x=\log_2(y-4)-1$.

For example, $y=5$ requires $2^{x+1}=1$ and occurs at $x=-1$. Height $y=8$ requires $2^{x+1}=4$ and occurs at $x=1$. Height $4$ would require the exponential to equal zero, a value it never takes.

As the inputs decrease, the graph approaches $y=4$ from above. The asymptote gives the lower boundary. Solving $2^{x+1}=y-4$ shows why every height beyond that boundary is attained. Solving for $x$ from $y$ checks both parts of the range. Heights at or below $4$ give no positive argument for the logarithm, while each height above $4$ corresponds to exactly one input.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the range and monotonicity of $g(x)=3-2^x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^x>0&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g(x)<3,\\[4pt]
y<3&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=\log_2(3-y),\\[4pt]
x_1<x_2&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3-2^{x_1}>3-2^{x_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\operatorname{Im}(g)=(-\infty,3).
$$

::grafico{f="3-2^x" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=4 asintoti="y=3" punti="0,2;1,1" titolo="The graph of $g$ decreases and approaches the asymptote $y=3$ from below"}

$$
g(-1)=3-\frac12=\frac52,\qquad g(2)=3-4=-1.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponential $2^x$ takes every positive value. Subtracting it from $3$ gives only values below $3$. Conversely, for any fixed height $y<3$, the difference $3-y$ is positive and $x=\log_2(3-y)$ produces that height. The range is therefore $(-\infty,3)$.

If $x_1<x_2$, then $2^{x_1}<2^{x_2}$ because the base-$2$ exponential is increasing. Subtracting both values from $3$ reverses their order. This gives $g(x_1)>g(x_2)$, so $g$ is strictly decreasing. The points with $g(0)=2$ and $g(1)=1$ illustrate this direction.

For increasingly negative inputs, the subtracted power approaches zero. The curve therefore approaches $y=3$ from below, while moving right gives increasingly negative values. This is consistent with the range being open at $3$. A very negative height is also in the range. For example, taking $y=-5$ gives $3-y=8$, so $x=3$. Height $3$ is excluded because it would require $2^x=0$, not because the function is undefined at any input.

</div>
