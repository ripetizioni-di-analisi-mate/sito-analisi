## Exercise 1

Find the horizontal asymptote and the point at $x=1$ for $f(x)=2^{x-1}-3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(1)&=2^0-3=-2,\\[4pt]
y_{\mathrm{as}}&=-3.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2^(x-1)-3" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 asintoti="y=-3" punti="1,-2,pieno,P" titolo="The point $P(1,-2)$ is one unit above the asymptote $y=-3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Replacing $x$ with $x-1$ shifts the graph of $2^x$ one unit right, so the exponent is zero at $x=1$. Subtracting $3$ lowers each ordinate, including the height of the asymptote. As $x$ decreases, the power $2^{x-1}$ approaches zero through positive values, so the graph approaches $y=-3$ from above.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A function has the form $f(x)=C\cdot3^x+k$. Its graph has asymptote $y=2$ and passes through $(0,5)$. Find $C$ and $k$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=2,\\[4pt]
5&=C\cdot3^0+2,\\[4pt]
C&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As $x$ decreases without bound, $3^x$ approaches zero. The term $C\cdot3^x$ therefore does the same for every fixed $C$. The height of the asymptote determines $k=2$. At $x=0$, the power is one and the ordinate is $C+2$. The given point yields $C=3$, determining $f(x)=3\cdot3^x+2$.

</div>
