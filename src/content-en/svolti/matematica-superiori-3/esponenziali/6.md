## Exercise 1

Find the horizontal asymptote and the point at $x=1$ for $f(x)=2^{x-1}-3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(1)&=2^0-3=-2,\\[4pt]
y_{\mathrm{as}}&=-3.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2^(x-1)-3" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 asintoti="y=-3" punti="1,-2,pieno,P" titolo="The point $P(1,-2)$ is one unit above the asymptote $y=-3$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(0)&=\frac12-3=-\frac52,\\[4pt]
f(-1)&=\frac14-3=-\frac{11}{4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Replacing $x$ by $x-1$ shifts $2^x$ one unit to the right. The exponent is zero at $x=1$, where the power equals $1$. Subtracting $3$ gives $f(1)=1-3=-2$. Every height is lowered by $3$, including the level that the graph approaches.

As the inputs become more negative, $2^{x-1}$ approaches zero and $f(x)$ approaches $-3$. The power is always positive. Therefore $f(x)>-3$ everywhere and the asymptote is approached from above.

The two check heights are $\dfrac12$ and $\dfrac14$ above $y=-3$, respectively. Moving one step left halves the distance. The point $P(1,-2)$ is one unit above the asymptote. At inputs greater than $1$, the distance above the asymptote exceeds $1$. At $x=2$, for example, it is $2$ and the height of $f$ is $-1$. The difference between the heights of $f$ and the level $-3$ follows the original exponential.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A function has the form $f(x)=C\cdot3^x+k$. Its graph has asymptote $y=2$ and passes through $(0,5)$. Find $C$ and $k$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=2,\\[4pt]
5&=C\cdot3^0+2,\\[4pt]
C&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
f(-1)=3\cdot\frac13+2=3,\qquad f(1)=3\cdot3+2=11.
$$

::grafico{f="3*3^x+2" xmin=-2.5 xmax=1.2 ymin=-0.4 ymax=14 asintoti="y=2" punti="-1,3;0,5,pieno,P;1,11" segmenti="0,2,0,5" legenda="3\cdot3^x+2" titolo="At $x=0$, the vertical distance from $P$ to the asymptote is $3$, the coefficient $C$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For increasingly negative $x$, $3^x$ approaches zero, and so does $C\cdot3^x$ for fixed $C$. The horizontal asymptote is therefore $y=k$, and the given line gives $k=2$. The point $(0,5)$ fixes the value at zero. Since $3^0=1$, we get $5=C+2$, hence $C=3$.

The coefficient $C$ is the difference between the height at zero and the asymptote. Its positive sign means that the curve lies above the asymptote. Had the given point been below the line, the same subtraction would give $C<0$ and the curve would lie below $y=2$.

The inputs $-1,0,1$ give distances from the line equal to $1,3,9$. The constant ratio $3$ between those distances confirms that the background $2$ must be subtracted before comparing the heights. For this particular point the coefficient cannot be zero, since a constant value of $2$ would not go through $(0,5)$.

</div>
