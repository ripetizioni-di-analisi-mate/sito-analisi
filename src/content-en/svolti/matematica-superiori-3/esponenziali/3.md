## Exercise 1

Solve $3^{2x-1}\leq27$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^{2x-1}\leq3^3
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2x-1\leq3\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2x\leq4\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\leq2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We recognise $27=3^3$. Because the base $3$ exceeds $1$, a smaller exponent gives a smaller power, so the comparison preserves its direction. Dividing by $2$ also preserves it since $2>0$. At $x=2$ the exponent equals $3$, so the endpoint giving equality is included.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $2^{\frac{1}{x-1}}>4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{1\},\\[4pt]
2^{\frac{1}{x-1}}>2^2
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{x-1}>2\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{3-2x}{x-1}>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
S=\left(1,\frac32\right).
$$

::grafico{f="2^(1/(x-1));4" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=8 asintoti="x=1" punti="1.5,4,pieno,P" legenda="2^{\frac1{x-1}};4" titolo="On the branch to the right of $x=1$, the graph exceeds the level $y=4$ up to $P$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponent has a denominator, so $x=1$ is excluded before comparing the powers. Because the exponential is increasing, we can compare the exponent directly with $2$. Moving all terms to the left gives a quotient whose numerator is positive for $x<\dfrac32$ and denominator positive for $x>1$. The quotient is positive when the signs agree. This happens between $1$ and $\dfrac32$, with the first endpoint excluded by the domain and the second by the strict inequality.

</div>
