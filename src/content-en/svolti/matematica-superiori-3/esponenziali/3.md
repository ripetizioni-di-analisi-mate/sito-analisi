## Exercise 1

Solve $3^{2x-1}\leq27$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^{2x-1}\leq3^3
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2x-1\leq3\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2x\leq4\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\leq2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
3^{2\cdot1-1}=3<27,\qquad 3^{2\cdot2-1}=27.
$$

::grafico{f="3^(2*x-1);27" xmin=-0.2 xmax=2.4 ymin=-1 ymax=46 punti="1,3;2,27,pieno,P" segmenti="2,0,2,27" legenda="3^{2x-1};27" titolo="The solution includes the inputs to the left of $P$ and the input of $P$ itself"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The threshold $27$ is a power of $3$, namely $3^3$. Thus both powers have the same base. Since $3$ exceeds $1$, its exponential is increasing and the comparison is equivalent to $2x-1\leq3$. Adding $1$ gives $2x\leq4$. Division by positive $2$ preserves the direction, so $x\leq2$.

The first comparison uses monotonicity of $u\mapsto3^u$. The second uses the sign of the coefficient of $x$. These are separate steps. With a negative coefficient, the exponential comparison would still preserve direction but dividing by the coefficient would reverse it.

In the graph the curve increases because the exponent $2x-1$ increases. It lies below the horizontal line at height $27$ for every input less than $2$ and reaches it at $2$. The symbol $\leq$ includes the intersection. Even an input slightly greater than $2$ makes the exponent strictly greater than $3$. At $x=\dfrac52$, for example, the exponent is $4$ and the power is $81$, so that side of the intersection is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $2^{\frac{1}{x-1}}>4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{1\},\\[4pt]
2^{\frac{1}{x-1}}>2^2
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{x-1}>2\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{3-2x}{x-1}>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
S=\left(1,\frac32\right).
$$

::grafico{f="2^(1/(x-1));4" xmin=0 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=8 asintoti="x=1" punti="1.5,4,vuoto,P" legenda="2^{\frac1{x-1}};4" titolo="The curve is above $y=4$ between $x=1$ and $P$. The heights agree at $P$"}

$$
2^{\frac{1}{\frac54-1}}=2^4=16,\qquad 2^{\frac{1}{\frac32-1}}=2^2=4.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponent is defined for $x\ne1$. Since $4=2^2$ and $2^u$ is increasing, the inequality becomes $\dfrac1{x-1}>2$. Multiplying by $x-1$ requires knowing its sign. Alternatively, for $x<1$ the exponent is negative, so the power is below $1$ and the inequality fails.

For $x>1$ the denominator is positive. Multiply without reversing the inequality to obtain $1>2(x-1)$, so $x<\dfrac32$. The two conditions give $1<x<\dfrac32$. Studying the fraction $\dfrac{3-2x}{x-1}$ separates the same two endpoints.

The point at $\dfrac54$ lies inside the solution because its height $16$ exceeds $4$. The point at $\dfrac32$ gives equality and is excluded. The input $1$ is outside the domain, as marked by the dashed vertical line. On that same branch, increasing $x$ decreases the reciprocal $\dfrac1{x-1}$. Beyond $\dfrac32$ the exponent is therefore below $2$ and the value of the power falls below $4$.

</div>
