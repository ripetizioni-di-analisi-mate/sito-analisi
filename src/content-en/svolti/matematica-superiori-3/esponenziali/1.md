## Exercise 1

Evaluate $81^{\frac34}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
81^{\frac34}
 &=\left(\sqrt[4]{81}\right)^3\\[4pt]
 &=3^3=27.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $4$ of the exponent indicates the fourth root, which is $3$ because $3^4=81$. The numerator $3$ then indicates the cube of the result. Writing $81$ as $3^4$ also lets us use the power-of-a-power law and obtain $3^{4\cdot\frac34}=3^3$ directly.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\dfrac{27^{-\frac23}\cdot9^{\frac32}}{\sqrt3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
27^{-\frac23}&=(3^3)^{-\frac23}=3^{-2},\\[4pt]
9^{\frac32}&=(3^2)^{\frac32}=3^3,\\[4pt]
\frac{27^{-\frac23}\cdot9^{\frac32}}{\sqrt3}
 &=3^{-2+3-\frac12}\\[4pt]
 &=3^{\frac12}=\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $27$ and $9$ are powers of $3$, so every term can use the same base, including $\sqrt3=3^{\frac12}$. The product in the numerator adds the exponents $-2$ and $3$. The radical in the denominator subtracts $\dfrac12$. The total is $\dfrac12$, indicating the square root of $3$.

</div>
