## Exercise 1

Evaluate $81^{\frac34}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
81^{\frac34}
 &=\left(\sqrt[4]{81}\right)^3\\[4pt]
 &=3^3=27.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3^4)^{\frac34}&=3^{4\cdot\frac34}=27,\\[4pt]
27^4&=(3^3)^4=3^{12}=(3^4)^3=81^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="81^x" xmin=-0.15 xmax=1.05 ymin=0 ymax=100 punti="0.5,9;0.75,27,pieno,P;1,81" segmenti="0.75,0,0.75,27" legenda="81^x" titolo="The height of $P$ lies between the heights at exponents one half and one"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the exponent $\dfrac34$, the denominator $4$ calls for a fourth root and the numerator $3$ for a cube. The fourth root of $81$ is $3$ because $3^4=81$. Since $81$ is positive, take the nonnegative root. The next step gives $3^3=27$.

The same power can be evaluated by immediately writing $81=3^4$. The power-of-a-power law multiplies the exponents, giving $4\cdot\dfrac34=3$. The two approaches agree because the fourth root undoes raising a positive number to the fourth power.

The last identity checks the result using the definition of $81^{\frac34}$ as the positive fourth root of $81^3$. The height $27$ lies between the heights at exponents $\dfrac12$ and $1$, namely $9$ and $81$. This check uses only the increasing power with base $81$. Both numbers in this check are positive. Equality of their fourth powers therefore guarantees equality of the values under consideration, without introducing a negative root of the equation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\dfrac{27^{-\frac23}\cdot9^{\frac32}}{\sqrt3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
27^{-\frac23}&=(3^3)^{-\frac23}=3^{-2},\\[4pt]
9^{\frac32}&=(3^2)^{\frac32}=3^3,\\[4pt]
\frac{27^{-\frac23}\cdot9^{\frac32}}{\sqrt3}
 &=3^{-2+3-\frac12}\\[4pt]
 &=3^{\frac12}=\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sqrt[3]{27})^2&=3^2=9,&(\sqrt9)^3&=3^3=27,\\[4pt]
\frac{\frac19\cdot27}{\sqrt3}&=\frac3{\sqrt3}=\sqrt3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Recognise the powers $27=3^3$ and $9=3^2$. Writing $\sqrt3=3^{\frac12}$ as well gives all the factors the same base. By the power-of-a-power law, the first factor becomes $3^{-2}$ and the second becomes $3^3$. Multiplication adds the exponents to give $3^{1}$. The denominator then subtracts $\dfrac12$ from the exponent, leaving $3^{\frac12}=\sqrt3$.

A check uses the values of the two factors. We have $27^{-\frac23}=\dfrac19$ and $9^{\frac32}=27$. Their product is $3$, so the quotient is $3$ divided by its square root, giving the square root again.

The minus sign in the first exponent gives the reciprocal of the $9$ just calculated. The denominator is a power with positive exponent $\dfrac12$, so its contribution to the quotient is subtracted. The result must be positive because all factors are positive. It is also below $3$, because the numerator is $3$ and the denominator is $\sqrt3>1$. The value $\sqrt3$ satisfies both conditions and squares to $3$.

</div>
