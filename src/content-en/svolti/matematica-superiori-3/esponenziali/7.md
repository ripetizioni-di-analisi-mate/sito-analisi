## Exercise 1

A quantity of $80$ units doubles every $3$ hours. Find the value predicted after $6$ hours.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N(t)&=80\cdot 2^{\frac t3},\\[4pt]
N(6)&=80\cdot2^{\frac63}\\[4pt]
 &=80\cdot 4=320.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
N(0)=80,\qquad N(3)=160,\qquad N(6)=320.
$$

::grafico{f="80*2^(x/3)" xmin=-0.25 xmax=7 ymin=0 ymax=430 nomi="t,N" punti="0,80;3,160,pieno,A;6,320,pieno,B" segmenti="3,0,3,160;6,0,6,320" legenda="80\cdot2^{\frac{t}{3}}" titolo="From $A$ to $B$ another three-hour period passes and the height doubles from $160$ to $320$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The initial value is $80$ and the factor per period is $2$. Each period lasts $3$ hours, so the exponent is the number of hours divided by $3$. The model is $N(t)=80\cdot2^{\frac t3}$. At $6$ hours the exponent is $2$. This gives two doublings and the value $80\cdot4=320$.

The values at the ends of the periods can also be found in order. After $3$ hours there are $160$ units. A second period multiplies that value by $2$ and gives $320$. The factor $2$ stays fixed, while the increase changes from $80$ to $160$ units.

The three sample inputs on the graph are equally spaced. Their heights do not have a constant difference. This distinguishes exponential growth from a linear model, which always adds the same amount. The doublings every three hours determine the stated sample values. The value between samples comes from choosing an exponential model. For instance, halfway through the first period the model uses the factor $\sqrt2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A quantity decays according to $N(t)=N_0q^t$, where $q>0$ is the daily factor. After $2$ days, $64\%$ of the initial quantity remains. Find the daily percentage decrease.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{N(2)}{N_0}&=q^2=0{,}64,\\[4pt]
q&=\sqrt{0{,}64}=0{,}8,\\[4pt]
1-q&=0{,}2=20\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
100\cdot0{,}8&=80,\\[4pt]
80\cdot0{,}8&=64.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The given data mean that $\dfrac{N(2)}{N_0}$ is $0{,}64$. In the model this ratio is $q^2$, since the daily factor is applied twice. As $q>0$, take the positive square root of $0{,}64$, which is $0{,}8$. One day therefore leaves $80\%$ and loses $20\%$ of the value at that day's start.

A check can start with $100$ units. Day one leaves $80$ and day two reduces those $80$ units to $64$. The two losses are $20$ and $16$ units because the second percentage uses a new reference amount.

The total decrease is $36\%$, while the daily rate is $20\%$. Dividing $36\%$ by two would give $18\%$. That rate would leave $0{,}82^2=0{,}6724$ of the initial amount. The daily ratio can be recovered from any day because $N(t+1)=qN(t)$. The estimate of $20\%$ depends on the assumption that both days use the same factor. With two different factors, the final value of $64$ would not determine the decrease on the first day.

</div>
