## Exercise 1

Solve $\left(\dfrac12\right)^x\geq\dfrac18$.

$$
\left(\frac12\right)^x\geq\left(\frac12\right)^3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\leq3.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The threshold $\dfrac18$ is the third power of $\dfrac12$. Since the exponential is decreasing, power values at least as large as the threshold correspond to exponents at most $3$. For example, $x=2$ gives $\dfrac14$, which exceeds the threshold. Equality holds at $x=3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\left(\dfrac13\right)^{x^2-4}>\dfrac19$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac13\right)^{x^2-4}>\left(\frac13\right)^2
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2-4<2\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2<6\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -\sqrt6<x<\sqrt6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base $\dfrac13$ belongs to $(0,1)$, so exceeding $\dfrac19$ requires an exponent below $2$. Adding $4$ gives a comparison between the square of $x$ and $6$. The square depends on the distance of $x$ from zero, so the solution includes both positive values below $\sqrt6$ and negative values above $-\sqrt6$. The endpoints give exactly $\dfrac19$ and are excluded.

</div>
