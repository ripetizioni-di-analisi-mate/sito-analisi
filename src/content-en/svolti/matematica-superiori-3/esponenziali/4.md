## Exercise 1

Solve $\left(\dfrac12\right)^x\geq\dfrac18$.

$$
\left(\frac12\right)^x\geq\left(\frac12\right)^3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\leq3.
$$

$$
\left(\frac12\right)^2=\frac14>\frac18,\qquad \left(\frac12\right)^4=\frac1{16}<\frac18.
$$

::grafico{f="0.5^x;0.125" xmin=-0.25 xmax=5 ymin=-0.1 ymax=0.65 punti="2,0.25;3,0.125,pieno,P;4,0.0625" segmenti="3,0,3,0.125" legenda="\left(\frac12\right)^x;\frac18" titolo="The curve is above the threshold to the left of $P$. The point $P$ is included"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the threshold in the given base. Since $\dfrac18=\left(\dfrac12\right)^3$, the comparison is between values of the same decreasing exponential. A height at least $\dfrac18$ requires an exponent at most $3$. The result is $x\leq3$. Here the exponent is just $x$, so no further algebra is needed.

The same direction follows from writing $\left(\dfrac12\right)^x=2^{-x}$ and $\dfrac18=2^{-3}$. The exponential with base $2$ increases, giving $-x\geq-3$. Multiplication by $-1$ then reverses the direction and again gives $x\leq3$.

The two checks place the curve above the line at $x=2$ and below at $x=4$. Equality at horizontal coordinate $3$ is allowed, so the intersection is marked with a filled point. The threshold is positive, like every value of the exponential. The solution is not restricted to $x\geq0$, since the function is also defined at negative exponents. For instance, $x=-1$ gives height $2$, which is above the threshold.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\left(\dfrac13\right)^{x^2-4}>\dfrac19$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac13\right)^{x^2-4}>\left(\frac13\right)^2
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2-4<2\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2<6\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -\sqrt6<x<\sqrt6.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\left(\frac13\right)^{3^2-4}=\left(\frac13\right)^5=\frac1{243}<\frac19.
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-4;2" xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-5 ymax=6 punti="-2.44948974,2,vuoto,A;2.44948974,2,vuoto,B" legenda="x^2-4;2" titolo="Between $A$ and $B$, the exponent is below $2$ and the power is above the threshold"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The threshold is $\dfrac19=\left(\dfrac13\right)^2$. Since the base-$\dfrac13$ exponential decreases, a larger power requires a smaller exponent. Thus $x^2-4<2$, or $x^2<6$. Because the square root of $6$ is positive, the allowed inputs are those less than distance $\sqrt6$ from zero.

The exponent contains a square, so the expression has the same height at $x$ and $-x$. The solution is therefore a symmetric interval. At $x=0$ the exponent is $-4$ and the power is $81$, above the threshold. At $x=\pm\sqrt6$ the exponent is $2$ and the value is exactly the threshold.

Substituting $x=3$ checks an outside value. The input $x=-3$ is also outside because it has the same square. The strict inequality excludes $-\sqrt6$ and $\sqrt6$, so the required set is $\left(-\sqrt6,\sqrt6\right)$. The result also follows from comparing the graph of $x^2-4$ with the horizontal line at height $2$. Between the intersections the parabola lies below the line. Since the exponential with base $\dfrac13$ is decreasing, that portion gives power values above the threshold.

</div>
