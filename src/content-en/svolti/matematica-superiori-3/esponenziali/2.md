## Exercise 1

Solve $2^{x+1}=8$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^{x+1}&=2^3\\[4pt]
x+1&=3\\[4pt]
x&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $8$ is the cube of $2$, both sides can be written with the same base. The function $2^x$ is strictly increasing and therefore one-to-one. The powers are equal only when their exponents agree. From $x+1=3$ we get $x=2$, which gives $2^{2+1}=8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $4^x=8^{x-1}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2^2)^x&=(2^3)^{x-1}\\[4pt]
2^{2x}&=2^{3x-3}\\[4pt]
2x&=3x-3\\[4pt]
x&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases $4$ and $8$ differ, so first write them as powers of $2$. The exponent $x-1$ multiplies the whole exponent $3$ of base $8$, giving $3x-3$. Injectivity of $2^x$ then leads to a linear equation. As a check, $4^3=64$ and $8^{3-1}=64$.

</div>
