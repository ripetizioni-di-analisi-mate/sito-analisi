## Exercise 1

Solve $2^{x+1}=8$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^{x+1}&=2^3\\[4pt]
x+1&=3\\[4pt]
x&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^{2+1}&=8,\\[4pt]
2^{3+1}&=16>8.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="2^(x+1);8" xmin=-1 xmax=3.5 ymin=-1 ymax=16 punti="2,8,pieno,P;0,2" segmenti="2,0,2,8" legenda="2^{x+1};8" titolo="The line at height $8$ meets the curve at $P$. Its projection onto the horizontal axis is $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain is $\mathbb R$ because $x+1$ is defined for every $x$. The right-hand side is $8=2^3$. Both sides are now values of the same increasing function, so they can agree only at equal exponents. The exponential equation reduces to $x+1=3$, with one solution $x=2$.

On the graph of $2^{x+1}$, the $+1$ in the exponent means that height $8$ is attained at $x=2$. The basic function $2^x$ reaches that same height at $x=3$. The one-unit difference is the horizontal shift.

Substituting $x=3$ confuses the required input with the exponent of the threshold. The horizontal line at height $8$ meets the increasing curve once. The input of that point is the solution of the equation. A second comparison uses the value at $x=1$, where the power is $4$. From there to $x=2$ the exponent increases by one and the height doubles, reaching $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $4^x=8^{x-1}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2^2)^x&=(2^3)^{x-1}\\[4pt]
2^{2x}&=2^{3x-3}\\[4pt]
2x&=3x-3\\[4pt]
x&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{8^{x-1}}{4^x}&=2^{3(x-1)-2x}=2^{x-3},\\[4pt]
\frac{8^{x-1}}{4^x}=1&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two sides have different bases. Write $4=2^2$ and $8=2^3$, so the power-of-a-power law gives $2^{2x}=2^{3(x-1)}$. The exponential is one-to-one, so equate the exponents. In the second exponent, $3$ multiplies both $x$ and $-1$. This gives $2x=3x-3$, hence $x=3$.

Taking a ratio yields the same equation. Because the denominator $4^x$ is positive, equality requires the ratio of the right side to the left side to equal $1$. In base $2$ the ratio reduces to a single power.

The ratio equals $1$ only at exponent zero. At $x=3$ both sides are $64$. If $x$ were greater than $3$, the ratio would exceed $1$, so the right-hand side would be larger than the left. The order can also be checked on either side of the crossing. At $x=2$ the left side is $16$ and the right side $8$. At $x=4$ these values are $256$ and $512$. The ratio predicts the change from a smaller to a larger right-hand value because it crosses $1$ at $x=3$.

</div>
