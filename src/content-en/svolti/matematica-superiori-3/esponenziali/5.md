## Exercise 1

Solve $4^x-5\cdot2^x+4=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=2^x>0,\\[4pt]
u^2-5u+4&=(u-1)(u-4)=0,\\[4pt]
u=1&\Longrightarrow x=0,\\[4pt]
u=4&\Longrightarrow x=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4^0-5\cdot2^0+4&=1-5+4=0,\\[4pt]
4^2-5\cdot2^2+4&=16-20+4=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-5*x+4" xmin=-0.4 xmax=5.5 ymin=-3 ymax=9 nomi="u,y" punti="1,0,pieno,A;4,0,pieno,B;2.5,-2.25" legenda=0 titolo="Points $A$ and $B$ are the zeros of the quadratic in $u$. Both have positive horizontal coordinates"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $4^x$ is the square of $2^x$. Set $u=2^x$ to get a quadratic in $u$. The numbers $1$ and $4$ have product $4$ and sum $5$, so the polynomial factors as $(u-1)(u-4)$. Both roots are positive and can occur as exponential values.

The root $u=1$ corresponds to $x=0$ because $2^0=1$. The root $u=4$ corresponds to $x=2$. The roots in $u$ are therefore different from the original inputs. The graph in $u$ crosses at $1$ and $4$. Each gives a unique input for the exponential.

These substitutions check the results in the original equation. The quadratic has only two roots, and the substitution gives a unique exponent for each, confirming the entire solution set $\{0,2\}$. For example, using $x=4$ by mistake from $u=4$ would give back $u=2^4=16$ in the substitution, which is not a zero of the quadratic.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $9^x+2\cdot3^x-3=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=3^x>0,\\[4pt]
u^2+2u-3&=(u+3)(u-1)=0,\\[4pt]
\{-3,1\}\cap(0,+\infty)&=\{1\},\\[4pt]
3^x=1&\Longrightarrow x=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(3^x+3)(3^x-1)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 3^x-1=0 .
$$

::grafico{f="x^2+2*x-3" xmin=-4.6 xmax=2.5 ymin=-4.5 ymax=9 nomi="u,y" punti="-3,0,vuoto,A;1,0,pieno,B" legenda=0 titolo="Only the horizontal coordinate of $B$ lies in the range of $3^x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $9^x=(3^x)^2$, set $u=3^x>0$. The resulting quadratic is $u^2+2u-3=(u+3)(u-1)$. Its roots are $-3$ and $1$. The first is an algebraic root, but no real exponent makes the base-$3$ power negative. This leaves only $u=1$.

The exponential is $1$ when the exponent is zero. The candidate is therefore $x=0$, which satisfies the original formula. The same exclusion follows by factoring without a substitution. In that form $3^x+3$ is always positive, so only the other factor can be zero.

The check separates the roots of the polynomial from those of the exponential equation. Although the polynomial has two distinct zeros, the substitution has range $(0,+\infty)$, so only the positive one is usable. On the graph in $u$, the vertical axis separates possible inputs from excluded ones. The root at $u=-3$ lies on the side that the substitution cannot reach. At $u=1$ the graph crosses the axis and the corresponding exponent is $0$. The value $u=0$ is never attained, but it bounds the range of $3^x$.

</div>
