## Exercise 1

Solve $4^x-5\cdot2^x+4=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=2^x>0,\\[4pt]
u^2-5u+4&=(u-1)(u-4)=0,\\[4pt]
u=1&\Longrightarrow x=0,\\[4pt]
u=4&\Longrightarrow x=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $4^x$ equals $(2^x)^2$. This lets us express the dependence on $x$ through one new unknown $u=2^x$. Both roots are positive, so both lie in the range of the exponential. To return to base $2$, use $1=2^0$ and $4=2^2$. The solution set is therefore $\{0,2\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $9^x+2\cdot3^x-3=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
u&=3^x>0,\\[4pt]
u^2+2u-3&=(u+3)(u-1)=0,\\[4pt]
\{-3,1\}\cap(0,+\infty)&=\{1\},\\[4pt]
3^x=1&\Longrightarrow x=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $9^x=(3^x)^2$, the substitution $u=3^x$ produces a quadratic. The root $-3$ solves the quadratic but cannot be a value of $3^x$, so discard it. The only admissible root is $u=1$. The equation $3^x=1$ has the single solution $x=0$, which gives $1+2-3=0$.

</div>
