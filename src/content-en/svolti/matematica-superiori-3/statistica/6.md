## Exercise 1

Find the covariance for $(1,5)$, $(2,4)$ and $(3,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{1+2+3}{3}=2,\\[4pt]
\bar y&=\frac{5+4+1}{3}=\frac{10}{3},\\[4pt]
\operatorname{cov}(x,y)
&=\frac{(-1)\dfrac53+0+1\left(-\dfrac73\right)}{3}
=-\frac43.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=6 punti="1,5;2,4;3,1" asintoti="x=2;y=3.3333333333333335" titolo="The deviations at the first and third points have opposite signs."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first point has an x-coordinate below the mean and a y-coordinate above the mean, so the product of its deviations is negative. The third point also has deviations with opposite signs. The x-coordinate of the second point equals the mean, so its contribution is zero. The covariance is therefore negative.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the linear correlation coefficient for the pairs $(1,1)$, $(2,3)$ and $(3,2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\bar y=2,\\
\operatorname{cov}(x,y)&=\frac{(-1)(-1)+0\cdot1+1\cdot0}{3}
=\frac13,\\[4pt]
\sigma_x^2&=\sigma_y^2=\frac{1+1}{3}=\frac23,\\[4pt]
r&=\frac{\dfrac13}{\sqrt{\dfrac23}\sqrt{\dfrac23}}
=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The y-coordinates are a rearrangement of the x-coordinates, so both variables have the same variance. Only the first pair contributes to the sum of the products of deviations. The correlation is positive, but the three points are not collinear, and the coefficient is less than $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the correlation for $(-2,4)$, $(-1,1)$, $(1,1)$ and $(2,4)$, and describe the relationship between the variables.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=0,\qquad \bar y=\frac{4+1+1+4}{4}=\frac52,\\[4pt]
4\operatorname{cov}(x,y)
&=(-2)\frac32+(-1)\left(-\frac32\right)\\
&\quad+1\left(-\frac32\right)+2\cdot\frac32=0,\\[4pt]
r&=0,\qquad y=x^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-0.6 ymax=5.4 punti="-2,4;-1,1;1,1;2,4" titolo="Opposite x-coordinates share the same y-coordinate."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Every x-coordinate has its opposite at the same y-coordinate. The products cancel in pairs. Since both variables take at least two distinct values, the coefficient exists and equals zero. All the points nevertheless lie on the same parabola.

</div>
