## Exercise 1

Find the covariance for $(1,5)$, $(2,4)$ and $(3,1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{1+2+3}{3}=2,\\[4pt]
\bar y&=\frac{5+4+1}{3}=\frac{10}{3},\\[4pt]
\operatorname{cov}(x,y)
&=\frac{(-1)\dfrac53+0+1\left(-\dfrac73\right)}{3}
=-\frac43.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=6 punti="1,5;2,4;3,1" asintoti="x=2;y=3.3333333333333335" titolo="The deviations at the first and third points have opposite signs."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first point has an x-coordinate below the mean and a y-coordinate above the mean, so the product of its deviations is negative. The third point also has deviations with opposite signs. The x-coordinate of the second point equals the mean, so its contribution is zero. The covariance is therefore negative.

With $\bar x=2$ and $\bar y=\dfrac{10}{3}$, the first point has deviations $-1$ and $\dfrac53$, the second has $0$ and $\dfrac23$, and the third has $1$ and $-\dfrac73$. The two mean lines in the figure allow us to read the signs. For a numerical check, subtract the product of the means from the mean of the products. Here the mean of the products is $\dfrac{1\cdot5+2\cdot4+3\cdot1}{3}=\dfrac{16}{3}$. The product of the means is $2\cdot\dfrac{10}{3}=\dfrac{20}{3}$, giving difference $-\dfrac43$. Both methods use the same pairs. Swapping just two y-coordinates can change covariance because it changes which deviations are multiplied together.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the linear correlation coefficient for the pairs $(1,1)$, $(2,3)$ and $(3,2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\bar y=2,\\
\operatorname{cov}(x,y)&=\frac{(-1)(-1)+0\cdot1+1\cdot0}{3}
=\frac13,\\[4pt]
\sigma_x^2&=\sigma_y^2=\frac{1+1}{3}=\frac23,\\[4pt]
r&=\frac{\dfrac13}{\sqrt{\dfrac23}\sqrt{\dfrac23}}
=\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.6 ymin=-0.4 ymax=4.55 punti="1,1;2,3;3,2" asintoti="x=2;y=2" segmenti="1,1,2,1;1,1,1,2" titolo="The point $(1,1)$ contributes positively. The other two lie on the mean lines."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The y-coordinates are a rearrangement of the x-coordinates, so both variables have the same variance. Only the first pair contributes to the sum of the products of deviations. The correlation is positive, but the three points are not collinear, and the coefficient is less than $1$.

The deviations in $x$ are $-1$, $0$ and $1$. Those in $y$, in the order of the pairs, are $-1$, $1$ and $0$. These lists let us check numerator and denominator separately. Covariance uses the mean of the paired products and equals $\dfrac13$. The standard deviations both equal $\sqrt{\dfrac23}$, so their product is $\dfrac23$. The ratio is $\dfrac12$. On the plot the point at x-coordinate $2$ lies on the vertical mean line, while the point with y-coordinate $2$ lies on the horizontal one. For both, the product of deviations is zero. The positive sign therefore comes from the other contribution, and the value below $1$ agrees with the three points being non-collinear.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the correlation for $(-2,4)$, $(-1,1)$, $(1,1)$ and $(2,4)$, and describe the relationship between the variables.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=0,\qquad \bar y=\frac{4+1+1+4}{4}=\frac52,\\[4pt]
4\operatorname{cov}(x,y)
&=(-2)\frac32+(-1)\left(-\frac32\right)\\
&\quad+1\left(-\frac32\right)+2\cdot\frac32=0,\\[4pt]
r&=0,\qquad y=x^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-2.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-0.6 ymax=5.4 punti="-2,4;-1,1;1,1;2,4" titolo="Opposite x-coordinates share the same y-coordinate."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Every x-coordinate has its opposite at the same y-coordinate. The products cancel in pairs. Since both variables take at least two distinct values, the coefficient exists and equals zero. All the points nevertheless lie on the same parabola.

We can check directly that both variances are positive. For $x$, the sum of squared deviations is $4+1+1+4=10$, so $\sigma_x^2=\dfrac52$. For $y$, the deviations from the mean are $\dfrac32,-\dfrac32,-\dfrac32,\dfrac32$, so $\sigma_y^2=\dfrac94$. The denominator of the correlation is therefore nonzero. The zero in the numerator comes from cancellation between symmetric points and indicates zero linear association in these data. Each y-coordinate is still determined exactly by the quadratic rule $y=x^2$. In particular, moving away from $0$ in either direction increases the y-coordinate. The plot shows both this increase and the symmetry that makes covariance zero.

</div>
