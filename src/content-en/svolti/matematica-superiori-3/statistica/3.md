## Exercise 1

Find the median and mode of $8,3,7,3,5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{(1)},\ldots,x_{(5)})&=(3,3,5,7,8),\\
\operatorname{Med}&=x_{(3)}=5,\\
\max_{v} n_v&=n_3=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With five observations there are two positions on either side of the third, so the middle value is $5$. The value $3$ occupies two positions, while each other value occurs once. The median and mode therefore answer different questions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The values $2$, $5$ and $9$ occur $4$, $2$ and $2$ times, respectively. Find the median without listing all the observations.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=4+2+2=8,\\
x_{(4)}&=2,\qquad x_{(5)}=5,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{Med}&=\frac{2+5}{2}=\frac72.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two middle positions are the fourth and fifth. The cumulative frequency at $2$ is $4$, so the fourth observation is $2$. The fifth lies among the observations with value $5$. The median is an intermediate point and need not be an observed value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the ordered data $1,3,4,7,9,12,20$, find $Q_1$, $Q_3$ and the interquartile range, excluding the overall median from both halves.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_1&=\operatorname{Med}(1,3,4)=3,\\
Q_3&=\operatorname{Med}(9,12,20)=12,\\
Q_3-Q_1&=12-3=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The middle value $7$ separates the data into two sets of three. Take the middle observation of each set. Even if $20$ were replaced by $40$, the middle positions of the two halves would contain the same values and the interquartile range would stay at $9$.

</div>
