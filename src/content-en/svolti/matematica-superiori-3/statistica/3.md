## Exercise 1

Find the median and mode of $8,3,7,3,5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{(1)},\ldots,x_{(5)})&=(3,3,5,7,8),\\
\operatorname{Med}&=x_{(3)}=5,\\
\max_{v} n_v&=n_3=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.45 xmax=9.1 ymin=-0.4 ymax=3.3 nomi="x,count" poligoni="2.75,0 3.25,0 3.25,2 2.75,2;4.75,0 5.25,0 5.25,1 4.75,1;6.75,0 7.25,0 7.25,1 6.75,1;7.75,0 8.25,0 8.25,1 7.75,1" titolo="The tallest bar identifies the mode. The median comes from ordering the recorded values with their frequencies."}

<div class="spiegazione">

With five observations there are two positions on either side of the third, so the middle value is $5$. The value $3$ occupies two positions, while each other value occurs once. The median and mode therefore answer different questions.

To find the centre, the **positions** matter. The first and second are both occupied by $3$, while the fourth and fifth are occupied by $7$ and $8$. There are two observations strictly below $5$ and two strictly above it. The mode instead comes from the tallies attached to the values, without locating the middle position. In the figure the bar at $3$ is twice the height of the others. Another check is to compare the result with the mean. Here the sum is $26$, so the mean is $\dfrac{26}{5}=5{,}2$. The three numbers differ because frequency, order and sum answer three different questions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The values $2$, $5$ and $9$ occur $4$, $2$ and $2$ times, respectively. Find the median without listing all the observations.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=4+2+2=8,\\
x_{(4)}&=2,\qquad x_{(5)}=5,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{Med}&=\frac{2+5}{2}=\frac72.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two middle positions are the fourth and fifth. The cumulative frequency at $2$ is $4$, so the fourth observation is $2$. The fifth lies among the observations with value $5$. The median is an intermediate point and need not be an observed value.

The cumulative counts are $4$ at value $2$, $6$ at value $5$, and $8$ at value $9$. Each value occupies the positions immediately after the previous cumulative count up to its own. Thus $5$ occupies the fifth and sixth positions, and $9$ the seventh and eighth. For an even number of observations, reaching exactly half in one row does not let us stop at that row's value. Here the count of $4$ at $2$ makes $2$ the first middle value. The second is in the next row. Averaging the two middle values gives $3{,}5$. As a check, four observations lie below $3{,}5$ and four above it.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the ordered data $1,3,4,7,9,12,20$, find $Q_1$, $Q_3$ and the interquartile range, excluding the overall median from both halves.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_1&=\operatorname{Med}(1,3,4)=3,\\
Q_3&=\operatorname{Med}(9,12,20)=12,\\
Q_3-Q_1&=12-3=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.45 xmax=22 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.6 nomi="x," poligoni="3,0.7 12,0.7 12,1.65 3,1.65" linee="1,1.17 3,1.17;12,1.17 20,1.17;1,0.9 1,1.45;20,0.9 20,1.45;7,0.7 7,1.65" etichette="3,1.87,Q_1;7,1.87,Me;12,1.87,Q_3" titolo="The box runs from $3$ to $12$. The outer segments show the minimum $1$ and maximum $20$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The middle value $7$ separates the data into two sets of three. Take the middle observation of each set. Even if $20$ were replaced by $40$, the middle positions of the two halves would contain the same values and the interquartile range would stay at $9$.

Counting positions shows which observations determine the result. The fourth observation is the median $7$. In the lower half the second position gives $3$. In the upper half the middle position is the sixth of the full list and gives $12$. If the measure of spread used both extremes, the distance from minimum to maximum would be $20-1=19$. The interquartile range is instead the distance $12-3=9$ between the two interior positions. The maximum can therefore increase without affecting the answer. The rule excluding the median matters. If $7$ were included in both halves, the quartiles would be $\dfrac{3+4}{2}$ and $\dfrac{9+12}{2}$, with a difference of $7$.

</div>
