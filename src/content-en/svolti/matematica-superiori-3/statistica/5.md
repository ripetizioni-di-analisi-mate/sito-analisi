## Exercise 1

A scale overestimates each mass by $2\,\mathrm{g}$. Its readings have mean $48\,\mathrm{g}$ and standard deviation $3\,\mathrm{g}$. Find both measures after correction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_i&=x_i-2,\\
\bar y&=48-2=46\,\mathrm{g},\\
\sigma_y&=|1|\cdot 3=3\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The correction subtracts $2\,\mathrm{g}$ from both each reading and the mean. The difference between a reading and the mean stays the same, so the standard deviation does as well.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sensor transforms its measurements according to $y=-2x+10$. The original data have $\bar x=3$ and $\sigma_x^2=4$. Find the mean, variance and standard deviation of $y$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar y&=-2\cdot3+10=4,\\
\sigma_y^2&=(-2)^2\cdot4=16,\\
\sigma_y&=\sqrt{16}=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The negative factor reverses the order of the observations. Their distances from the new mean double because they depend on the absolute value of the factor. The squared distances quadruple, and the term $10$ cancels when the deviation is calculated.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Apply $y=ax+b$ with $a>0$. The means are $\bar x=10$ and $\bar y=35$, and the standard deviations are $2$ and $6$. Find $a$ and $b$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6&=a\cdot2, & a&=3,\\
35&=3\cdot10+b, & b&=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The standard deviation depends only on the scale factor, so find $a$ first. The condition $a>0$ means that $|a|$ can be replaced by $a$. Once the scale is known, use the mean to find the shift.

</div>
