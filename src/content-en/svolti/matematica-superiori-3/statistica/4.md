## Exercise 1

Find the mean, descriptive variance and standard deviation of the lengths $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{2+4+6}{3}=4\,\mathrm{cm},\\[5pt]
\sigma^2&=\frac{(2-4)^2+(4-4)^2+(6-4)^2}{3}\\[4pt]
&=\frac83\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[4pt]
\sigma&=\sqrt{\frac83}\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The deviations from the mean are $-2$, $0$ and $2$ centimetres. Their sum would be zero, while the sum of the squares is $8\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. Descriptive variance divides this total by the number of observations. Taking the square root expresses the spread in centimetres.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare the spread of group A with observations $2,5,8$ and group B with observations $4,5,6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x_A&=\bar x_B=5,\\
\sigma_A^2&=\frac{9+0+9}{3}=6,\\[4pt]
\sigma_B^2&=\frac{1+0+1}{3}=\frac23,\\[4pt]
\frac{\sigma_A}{\sigma_B}&=\sqrt{\frac{6}{\dfrac23}}=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both groups are centred on $5$. The two extreme values of A are three units from the centre, while those of B are one unit away. The ratio of the variances is $9$, so the standard deviation of A is three times that of B.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four measurements have sum $20$ and sum of squares $116$. Find the descriptive variance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{20}{4}=5,\\
\frac14\sum_i x_i^2&=\frac{116}{4}=29,\\[4pt]
\sigma^2&=29-5^2=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The individual measurements are unknown, but the two totals suffice for the variance formula. The first total gives the square of the mean, $25$, and the second gives the mean of squares, $29$. Their difference gives the required measure of spread.

</div>
