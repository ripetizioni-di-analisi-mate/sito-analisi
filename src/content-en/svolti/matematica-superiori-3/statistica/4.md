## Exercise 1

Find the mean, descriptive variance and standard deviation of the lengths $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{2+4+6}{3}=4\,\mathrm{cm},\\[5pt]
\sigma^2&=\frac{(2-4)^2+(4-4)^2+(6-4)^2}{3}\\[4pt]
&=\frac83\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\[4pt]
\sigma&=\sqrt{\frac83}\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The deviations from the mean are $-2$, $0$ and $2$ centimetres. Their sum would be zero, while the sum of the squares is $8\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. Descriptive variance divides this total by the number of observations. Taking the square root expresses the spread in centimetres.

Squaring is what distinguishes variance from the sum of deviations. The two extreme measurements lie equally far from the mean, but the one on the left gives a negative deviation. In a signed sum the two distances cancel. In the variance each contributes $4\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. The middle value counts in the divisor because the data set contains three observations, even though it contributes zero to the sum. An alternative check uses the mean of squares, $\dfrac{2^2+4^2+6^2}{3}=\dfrac{56}{3}$ in square centimetres. Subtracting $4^2=16$ gives $\dfrac83$ again. The standard deviation is slightly greater than $1{,}6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and less than the distance from either extreme to the centre, which is $2\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare the spread of group A with observations $2,5,8$ and group B with observations $4,5,6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x_A&=\bar x_B=5,\\
\sigma_A^2&=\frac{9+0+9}{3}=6,\\[4pt]
\sigma_B^2&=\frac{1+0+1}{3}=\frac23,\\[4pt]
\frac{\sigma_A}{\sigma_B}&=\sqrt{\frac{6}{\dfrac23}}=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.45 xmax=10 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.8 nomi="x,group" punti="2,2;5,2;8,2;4,1;5,1;6,1" etichette="0.55,2.03,A;0.55,1.03,B" asintoti="x=5" segmenti="2,2,8,2;4,1,6,1" titolo="The two rows share the mean $5$. Their endpoints lie at different distances from $5$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Both groups are centred on $5$. The two extreme values of A are three units from the centre, while those of B are one unit away. The ratio of the variances is $9$, so the standard deviation of A is three times that of B.

The factor of three between the standard deviations can also be read directly from the data. Around the common mean $5$, group B has deviations $-1,0,1$. Group A has deviations $-3,0,3$, each three times the corresponding deviation in B. The squares are therefore multiplied by $9$. The groups have the same size, so dividing each sum by three preserves their ratio. In the figure, the two rows identify different groups on the same value axis. The central point has the same horizontal coordinate, but group A's endpoints are further away. Variance captures this difference even though the mean alone assigns the same value to both groups.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four measurements have sum $20$ and sum of squares $116$. Find the descriptive variance.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{20}{4}=5,\\
\frac14\sum_i x_i^2&=\frac{116}{4}=29,\\[4pt]
\sigma^2&=29-5^2=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The individual measurements are unknown, but the two totals suffice for the variance formula. The first total gives the square of the mean, $25$, and the second gives the mean of squares, $29$. Their difference gives the required measure of spread.

We can check the calculation by expanding the expression for the deviations. With mean $5$, the sum of the squared deviations is
$$
\sum_i(x_i-5)^2
=116-10\cdot20+4\cdot25=16.
$$
Dividing by the four observations gives $4$ again. The expression contains both the sum of the data and the sum of their squares because the cross term is $-10x_i$ for each observation. The value $29$ is the mean of the squares of the raw data. It measures the average squared distance from the origin, while variance measures deviations from the mean. The two totals also determine the standard deviation, which is $2$, but they do not generally determine the individual observations.

</div>
