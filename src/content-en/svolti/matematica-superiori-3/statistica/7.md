## Exercise 1

A set of pairs has $\bar x=4$, $\bar y=7$, $\sigma_x^2=3$ and $\operatorname{cov}(x,y)=6$. Find the regression line of $y$ on $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m&=\frac63=2,\\[4pt]
q&=7-2\cdot4=-1,\\
\hat y&=2x-1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The covariance is positive and the variance of $x$ is $3$, so the slope is positive and equals $2$. Find the intercept by requiring the line to pass through the point of means. Substituting $x=4$ gives $2cdot4-1=7$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the regression line for $(1,2)$, $(2,5)$ and $(3,5)$ and the residual at the middle point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=2,\qquad \bar y=4,\\
\sigma_x^2&=\frac{1+0+1}{3}=\frac23,\\[4pt]
\operatorname{cov}(x,y)&=\frac{(-1)(-2)+0+1\cdot1}{3}=1,\\[4pt]
m&=\frac{1}{\dfrac23}=\frac32,\qquad q=4-\frac32\cdot 2=1,\\[4pt]
\hat y&=\frac32x+1,\qquad e_2=5-(3+1)=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="1.5*x+1" xmin=-0.4 xmax=4.4 ymin=-0.4 ymax=6.8 punti="1,2;2,5;3,5" segmenti="2,4,2,5" titolo="The segment at $x=2$ connects the prediction $4$ to the observation $5$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Divide covariance by the variance of the x-coordinates to find the slope. Choose the intercept so that the mean x-coordinate $2$ gives the mean y-coordinate $4$. The observed point with that x-coordinate has y-coordinate $5$, so it lies above the line and its residual equals $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $(0,1)$, $(1,3)$ and $(2,2)$, compare $y=x+1$ and $y=2$ using the sum of squared residuals.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_i e_{i,1}^2&=(1-1)^2+(3-2)^2+(2-3)^2=2,\\[4pt]
\sum_i e_{i,2}^2&=(1-2)^2+(3-2)^2+(2-2)^2=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both lines have the same sum of squares, even though their residuals differ. For $y=x+1$ the point at $x=0$ lies on the line, while for $y=2$ the point at $x=2$ does. The criterion assigns both candidates the same value. The least squares line, which was not one of the two candidates, is $\hat y=\dfrac{x}{2}+\dfrac32$ and has sum of squares $\dfrac32$.

</div>
