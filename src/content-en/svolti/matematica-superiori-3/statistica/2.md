## Exercise 1

Four times, in minutes, are $6,9,5,8$. Calculate their mean.

$$
\bar x=\frac{6+9+5+8}{4}=\frac{28}{4}=7\,\mathrm{min}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each time carries the same weight. The four times sum to $28\,\mathrm{min}$, so sharing the total equally gives $7\,\mathrm{min}$ per observation. The sum would remain $28\,\mathrm{min}$ if each duration were replaced by the mean.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A student averages $7$ points over four tests. What score is needed on the fifth test to reach an average of $7{,}4$ points?

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_4&=4\cdot7=28,\\
S_5&=5\cdot7{,}4=37,\\
x_5&=S_5-S_4=37-28=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The target mean refers to five results, so the new total must be $37$ points. The missing score is the difference between this total and the existing one. A score of $9$ increases the total by exactly the amount needed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

One group of $6$ students has mean score $8$ points. Another group of $14$ students has mean score $6$ points. Calculate the mean for all the students.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=6\cdot8+14\cdot6=132,\\[4pt]
\bar x&=\frac{132}{20}=6{,}6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply each group's mean by its size to recover its total score. Divide the sum by $20$, since each student must contribute equally. The combined mean is closer to $6$ because the second group has more than twice as many students.

</div>
