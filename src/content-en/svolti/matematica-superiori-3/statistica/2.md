## Exercise 1

Four times, in minutes, are $6,9,5,8$. Calculate their mean.

$$
\bar x=\frac{6+9+5+8}{4}=\frac{28}{4}=7\,\mathrm{min}.
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.45 xmax=5.05 ymin=-0.5 ymax=11 nomi="x,min" punti="1,6;2,9;3,5;4,8" asintoti="y=7" segmenti="1,6,1,7;2,7,2,9;3,5,3,7;4,7,4,8" titolo="The inputs $1$ through $4$ number the trials. Distances from the mean line balance when their signs are taken into account."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each time carries the same weight. The four times sum to $28\,\mathrm{min}$, so sharing the total equally gives $7\,\mathrm{min}$ per observation. The sum would remain $28\,\mathrm{min}$ if each duration were replaced by the mean.

We can check the result with the **deviations from the mean**. Subtracting $7$ minutes from each time gives $-1$, $2$, $-2$ and $1$. Their sum is $-1+2-2+1=0$, so the two negative deviations balance the two positive ones. This check is equivalent to recovering the total as $4\cdot7=28$ minutes. The mean need not equal any observed duration, as this example shows. Its value depends on every observation, while the centre of the sorted list uses just the two middle positions. Correcting a single duration upwards by $4$ minutes would raise the total by $4$ and the mean by $1$ minute, since the number of measurements would stay the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A student averages $7$ points over four tests. What score is needed on the fifth test to reach an average of $7{,}4$ points?

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_4&=4\cdot7=28,\\
S_5&=5\cdot7{,}4=37,\\
x_5&=S_5-S_4=37-28=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The target mean refers to five results, so the new total must be $37$ points. The missing score is the difference between this total and the existing one. A score of $9$ increases the total by exactly the amount needed.

One can also measure each result relative to the starting mean. If the mean over all five tests were $7$ points, the reference total would be $35$. To reach the required mean, the five tests need an extra $0{,}4$ points each on average, a total increase of $5\cdot0{,}4=2$. The first four already have a fixed total, so the two extra points must come entirely from the fifth test. The required score is $7+2=9$. Reversing the calculation checks both the sum and the divisor, since $\dfrac{28+9}{5}=\dfrac{37}{5}=7{,}4$. A score of $8$ would instead produce a mean of only $\dfrac{28+8}{5}=7{,}2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

One group of $6$ students has mean score $8$ points. Another group of $14$ students has mean score $6$ points. Calculate the mean for all the students.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=6\cdot8+14\cdot6=132,\\[4pt]
\bar x&=\frac{132}{20}=6{,}6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply each group's mean by its size to recover its total score. Divide the sum by $20$, since each student must contribute equally. The combined mean is closer to $6$ because the second group has more than twice as many students.

A second calculation starts from the $6$ points of the larger group. If all $20$ students had $6$ points each, the total would be $120$. The first group has $6$ students and its mean is $2$ points higher, so it adds $6\cdot2=12$ points to that reference total. Dividing the extra amount by $20$ gives $\dfrac{12}{20}=0{,}6$ more points per student, or $6{,}6$. Averaging the two group means gives $7$, which would be appropriate for equally sized groups. To check the result, the deviations of the two group means from $6{,}6$ must cancel once weighted by the group sizes. Indeed, $6(8-6{,}6)+14(6-6{,}6)=0$.

</div>
