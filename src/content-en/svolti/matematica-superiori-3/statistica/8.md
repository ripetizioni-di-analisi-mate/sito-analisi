## Exercise 1

The measurements $2$, $4$ and $7$ occur $3$, $2$ and $5$ times, respectively. Find the mean from the frequency table.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=3+2+5=10,\\
S&=3\cdot2+2\cdot4+5\cdot7=49,\\[4pt]
\bar x&=\frac{49}{10}=4{,}9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The table lists three distinct values but summarises ten measurements. The value $7$ contributes five times, so its row adds $35$ to the sum. Dividing by ten distributes the sum across all observed measurements.

Listing all the observations would give $2,2,2,4,4,7,7,7,7,7$, with the same sum of $49$. The table is a shorthand for precisely this repetition. Using relative frequencies, the values $2$, $4$ and $7$ get weights $\dfrac3{10}$, $\dfrac2{10}$ and $\dfrac5{10}$, which add to $1$. Multiplying each value by its weight again gives $2\cdot\dfrac3{10}+4\cdot\dfrac2{10}+7\cdot\dfrac5{10}=\dfrac{49}{10}$. The mode is $7$, the value with the highest frequency. The mean is below $7$ because the other five observations have values $2$ and $4$. Weighting all three rows equally would instead give $\dfrac{2+4+7}{3}=\dfrac{13}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a histogram of durations, classes $[0,3)$ and $[3,7)$ have heights $4$ and $2$, measured in observations per minute of interval width. How many observations does each class represent?

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_1&=(3-0)\cdot4=12,\\
n_2&=(7-3)\cdot2=8,\\
N&=12+8=20.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.7 xmax=8 ymin=-0.45 ymax=5 nomi="min,density" poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,4 0,4;3,0 7,0 7,2 3,2" etichette="1.2,1.7,12;4.8,0.8,8" titolo="The frequencies $12$ and $8$ are the rectangle areas."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Height is a frequency density and width is measured in minutes. Their product gives the count. The second interval spans more of the horizontal axis, but it contains fewer observations because its area is smaller.

The heights $4$ and $2$ express concentrations per unit of horizontal width. Saying that the first bar is twice as high compares those concentrations. Counts require multiplying each height by its own width. Their ratio is $\dfrac{12}{8}=\dfrac32$, which differs from the height ratio $\dfrac42=2$. The areas also give the proportions $\dfrac{12}{20}=\dfrac35$ and $\dfrac8{20}=\dfrac25$. As a check, these fractions add up to $1$. The boundary between the classes is $3$ minutes. Under the interval convention, a duration of exactly $3$ belongs only to the second class and is counted once.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A table lists $8$ times in $[0,4)$ and $12$ times in $[4,10)$, in minutes. Estimate the mean duration using the class midpoints.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1&=\frac{0+4}{2}=2,\qquad c_2=\frac{4+10}{2}=7,\\[4pt]
\widehat{\bar x}&=\frac{8\cdot2+12\cdot7}{8+12}\\[4pt]
&=\frac{100}{20}=5\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Represent every duration in the first class by $2$ minutes, and each duration in the second by $7$ minutes. These representative values have weights $8$ and $12$. The estimated mean is $5$ minutes. It equals the actual mean if each class mean equals its midpoint.

A comparison of weights checks the estimate. The second midpoint, $7$, has weight $12$ and the first, $2$, has weight $8$. The estimated mean must therefore be closer to $7$ than to $2$, as $5$ is. The frequencies also give bounds on the actual total. The first class contributes at least $8\cdot0$ and less than $8\cdot4$. The second contributes at least $12\cdot4$ and less than $12\cdot10$. The actual mean therefore satisfies
$$
\frac{48}{20}\le \bar x<\frac{152}{20},
\qquad 2{,}4\le\bar x<7{,}6.
$$
The estimate is at the midpoint of this interval. The table determines the bounds but leaves the actual mean's precise location unknown.

</div>
