## Exercise 1

A survey of $32$ students records $12$ sign-ups for a lab. Find the relative frequency and the percentage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{12}{32}=\frac38,\\[5pt]
p&=100\cdot\frac38\,\%=37{,}5\,\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.5 xmax=8.7 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.1 nomi="x,share" poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,1 0,1" linee="3,0 8,0 8,1 3,1 3,0" segmenti="1,0,1,1;2,0,2,1;4,0,4,1;5,0,5,1;6,0,6,1;7,0,7,1" titolo="Three of the eight units are shaded to represent the lab sign-ups."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Each student appears once in the survey, so the total is $32$. Sign-ups account for $3$ observations in every $8$. Multiplying the proportion by $100$ expresses the same ratio per hundred students.

The percentage lets us compare this response with that of a larger class. For instance, in a class of $64$ students, $24$ would have to sign up for the proportion to stay the same. Both counts double, so the ratio remains $\dfrac38$. In the diagram, sign-ups account for three units out of eight. The other five account for $\dfrac58=62{,}5\,\%$. As an independent check, $32-12=20$ students did not sign up and $\dfrac{20}{32}=\dfrac58$. The two proportions add to $1$ because each questionnaire has a single outcome. Dividing $12$ by $20$ would compare the two groups with each other and would use a different reference total.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In two classes, $9$ out of $24$ students and $12$ out of $40$ take the train. In which class is this choice more common? Find the combined proportion as well.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_A&=\frac9{24}=\frac38=37{,}5\,\%,\\[5pt]
f_B&=\frac{12}{40}=\frac3{10}=30\,\%,\\[5pt]
f_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\frac{9+12}{24+40}=\frac{21}{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first class has the greater proportion because $37{,}5,%>30,%$. The $12$ students in the second class are compared with a total of $40$. To combine the two classes, first add the counts and total sizes. The combined proportion is closer to that of the second class, which has more students.

To compare the fractions without using decimals, give them denominator $40$. We obtain $\dfrac38=\dfrac{15}{40}$ against $\dfrac3{10}=\dfrac{12}{40}$. For classes of $40$ students, the proportions would therefore represent $15$ and $12$ train travellers. The same gap is $7{,}5$ percentage points. To check the combined proportion $\dfrac{21}{64}$, distinguish the average of the two percentages from the proportion across individual students. A simple average would give $33{,}75\,\%$, weighting the classes equally. The combined proportion is $32{,}8125\,\%$, counting each student once. The difference between the results comes from the larger weight of the second class.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In one week, $5$ households placed $0$ orders, $7$ placed $1$, $4$ placed $2$ and $4$ placed $3$. Find the proportion of households that placed more than one order.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=5+7+4+4=20,\\
N_{\le1}&=5+7=12,\\
N_{>1}&=20-12=8,\\[4pt]
f_{>1}&=\frac8{20}=40\,\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

The two bars to the right of $1$ represent the households in the required group."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The cumulative count up to one order includes households with zero orders and those with exactly one. Removing both groups from the total leaves only those with two or three orders. The denominator remains the number of households.

The cumulative counts at zero, one, two and three orders are $5$, $12$, $16$ and $20$. The result can be read in two ways. Above one order, $20-12=8$ households remain. Starting from the frequencies of individual values, add the counts for two and three orders to get $4+4=8$. This check makes the difference between *more than one* and *at least one* explicit. The second question would also include the seven households that placed one order, giving $20-5=15$. In the diagram, only the bars with horizontal coordinate strictly greater than $1$ are included. The percentage compares those eight households with all twenty observed.

</div>
