## Exercise 1

A survey of $32$ students records $12$ sign-ups for a lab. Find the relative frequency and the percentage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f&=\frac{12}{32}=\frac38,\\[5pt]
p&=100\cdot\frac38\,\%=37{,}5\,\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each student appears once in the survey, so the total is $32$. Sign-ups account for $3$ observations in every $8$. Multiplying the proportion by $100$ expresses the same ratio per hundred students.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In two classes, $9$ out of $24$ students and $12$ out of $40$ take the train. In which class is this choice more common? Find the combined proportion as well.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_A&=\frac9{24}=\frac38=37{,}5\,\%,\\[5pt]
f_B&=\frac{12}{40}=\frac3{10}=30\,\%,\\[5pt]
f_{\mathrm{tot}}&=\frac{9+12}{24+40}=\frac{21}{64}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first class has the greater proportion because $37{,}5,%>30,%$. The $12$ students in the second class are compared with a total of $40$. To combine the two classes, first add the counts and total sizes. The combined proportion is closer to that of the second class, which has more students.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In one week, $5$ households placed $0$ orders, $7$ placed $1$, $4$ placed $2$ and $4$ placed $3$. Find the proportion of households that placed more than one order.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=5+7+4+4=20,\\
N_{\le1}&=5+7=12,\\
N_{>1}&=20-12=8,\\[4pt]
f_{>1}&=\frac8{20}=40\,\%.
\end{aligned}
$$
