## Exercise 1

Solve $\log_{\frac12}(x+1)\geq-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac12\right)^{-2}&=4,\\[6pt]
\begin{cases}
x+1>0,\\
x+1\leq4
\end{cases}
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -1<x\leq3.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\log_{\frac12}(1+1)=-1,\qquad \log_{\frac12}(7+1)=-3.
$$

::grafico{f="log(x+1)/log(0.5);-2" xmin=-1.5 xmax=8 ymin=-3.7 ymax=3 asintoti="x=-1" punti="1,-1;3,-2,pieno,P;7,-3" legenda="\log_{\frac12}(x+1);-2" titolo="Between the asymptote and $P$ the curve lies above the line at height $-2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Level $-2$ is reached at argument $\left(\dfrac12\right)^{-2}=4$. Since the logarithm with base $\dfrac12$ is decreasing, a greater height corresponds to a smaller argument. The non-strict inequality therefore requires $x+1\leq4$. Together with $x+1>0$, this gives $-1<x\leq3$.

The endpoints are considered separately. At $x=-1$ the argument is zero so the function is undefined. At $x=3$ the argument is $4$ and the logarithm equals $-2$, as allowed by $\geq$.

Checking two inputs in the same domain confirms the direction. The value $x=1$ works because it gives $-1>-2$. The value $x=7$ fails because it gives $-3<-2$. On the graph the two points lie on opposite sides of the horizontal line. The inputs are distinct from the logarithm's arguments. Level $-2$ is reached at argument $4$, but the corresponding horizontal coordinate is $3$ because the argument is $x+1$. This is the shift of the base curve shown in the graph.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\log_{\frac13}\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=(-\infty,-2)\cup(1,+\infty),\\[4pt]
\log_{\frac13}\frac{x-1}{x+2}>0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0<\frac{x-1}{x+2}<1,\\[4pt]
\frac{x-1}{x+2}-1&=\frac{-3}{x+2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
S=D\cap(-2,+\infty)=(1,+\infty).
$$

$$
\left.\frac{x-1}{x+2}\right|_{x=-3}=4,\qquad \left.\frac{x-1}{x+2}\right|_{x=2}=\frac14.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fractional argument must be positive and its denominator must be nonzero. The quotient is positive on $x<-2$ and $x>1$, where numerator and denominator have the same sign. On this domain, the base-$\dfrac13$ logarithm is positive when the argument lies between $0$ and $1$. It remains to compare the quotient with $1$.

Subtracting $1$ from the quotient gives $\dfrac{-3}{x+2}$. The numerator is always negative. The fraction is negative when the denominator is positive, meaning $x>-2$. Intersecting with the domain leaves only $x>1$.

Both example inputs are in the domain. The first gives an argument greater than $1$, so its logarithm is negative. The second gives an argument between $0$ and $1$, so its logarithm is positive. The two conditions could also be written as a system of rational inequalities. Both involve the point $-2$ because they have the same denominator, while the only zero of the argument is $1$. Positivity of the quotient alone would include the left-hand branch, where the quotient is greater than $1$.

</div>
