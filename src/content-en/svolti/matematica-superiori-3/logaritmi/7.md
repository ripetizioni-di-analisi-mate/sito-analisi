## Exercise 1

Solve $\log_{\frac12}(x+1)\geq-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac12\right)^{-2}&=4,\\[6pt]
\begin{cases}
x+1>0,\\
x+1\leq4
\end{cases}
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -1<x\leq3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value $-2$ is the logarithm of $4$ in base $\dfrac12$. Since this base gives a decreasing logarithm, the arguments that produce a value at least $-2$ are at most $4$. Positivity of the argument excludes $x=-1$. The endpoint $x=3$ is allowed and gives exactly the required level.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\log_{\frac13}\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=(-\infty,-2)\cup(1,+\infty),\\[4pt]
\log_{\frac13}\frac{x-1}{x+2}>0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0<\frac{x-1}{x+2}<1,\\[4pt]
\frac{x-1}{x+2}-1&=\frac{-3}{x+2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
S=D\cap(-2,+\infty)=(1,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To determine where the quotient is positive, compare the signs of its factors, which change at $-2$ and $1$. Since the base is less than $1$, the logarithm is positive when its argument is between $0$ and $1$. To compare the quotient with $1$, bring everything to the left. The numerator $-3$ is negative, so the fraction is negative only where the denominator is positive, meaning $x>-2$. Intersecting with the domain leaves $x>1$.

</div>
