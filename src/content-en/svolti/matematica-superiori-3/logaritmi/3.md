## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\log_9 27$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_9 27
 &=\frac{\log_3 27}{\log_3 9}\\[4pt]
 &=\frac{3}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
9^{\frac32}=(\sqrt9)^3=3^3=27.
$$

::grafico{f="9^x;27" xmin=-0.3 xmax=2.2 ymin=-2 ymax=90 punti="1,9;1.5,27,pieno,P;2,81" segmenti="1.5,0,1.5,27" legenda="9^x;27" titolo="The intersection at height $27$ lies between the horizontal coordinates $1$ and $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The answer is the exponent of $9$ that gives $27$. Since $9=3^2$ and $27=3^3$, base $3$ is a natural common base. In the change-of-base formula, the numerator is the logarithm of the argument, equal to $3$, and the denominator is the logarithm of the original base, equal to $2$.

The result $\dfrac32$ lies between $1$ and $2$. Indeed, $9^1=9$ and $9^2=81$, with the argument $27$ between them. Graphically, the result is the horizontal coordinate where the base-$9$ curve reaches level $27$.

The check applies the definition of a fractional power. Reversing the quotient to $\dfrac23$ would give an exponent below $1$ and hence a power below $9$, which cannot give $27$. Calculation in the original base starts from $9^u=27$, gives $(3^2)^u=3^3$ and then $2u=3$. This shows that the denominator $2$ is the exponent that expresses base $9$ in base $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\dfrac{\ln 20-\ln 5}{\ln 8}$ exactly without a calculator.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\ln 20-\ln 5}{\ln 8}
 &=\frac{\ln 4}{\ln 8}\\[4pt]
 &=\log_8 4\\[4pt]
 &=\frac{\log_2 4}{\log_2 8}
 =\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
8^{\frac23}=(\sqrt[3]8)^2=2^2=4.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is a difference of natural logarithms with positive arguments. It simplifies to $\ln\dfrac{20}{5}=\ln4$. The ratio of $\ln4$ to $\ln8$ is therefore $\log_8 4$. The denominator is nonzero since $8>1$. We can now choose base $2$, because both $4$ and $8$ are powers of $2$.

An alternative is to label the result $u$ and write $8^u=4$. Substituting the powers of $2$ gives $2^{3u}=2^2$, so $u=\dfrac23$. This approach shows directly why the two exponents appear in the quotient in that order.

Because $1<4<8$, the required exponent lies between $0$ and $1$. Its size rules out $\dfrac32$ without further calculation. Dividing the numerator by $\ln8$ is thus a separate step from the subtraction in the numerator. Before transforming the quotient, its sign can be checked. The numerator is positive because $20>5$ and $\ln$ increases. The denominator is positive because $\ln8>\ln1=0$. The result must therefore be positive.

</div>
