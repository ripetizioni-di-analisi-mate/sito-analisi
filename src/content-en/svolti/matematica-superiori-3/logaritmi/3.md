## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\log_9 27$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_9 27
 &=\frac{\log_3 27}{\log_3 9}\\[4pt]
 &=\frac{3}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Choose base $3$ because $27=3^3$ and $9=3^2$. The logarithm of the argument goes in the numerator and the logarithm of the original base in the denominator. The result is the fraction $\dfrac32$, verified directly by $9^{\frac32}=(\sqrt9)^3=27$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\dfrac{\ln 20-\ln 5}{\ln 8}$ exactly without a calculator.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\ln 20-\ln 5}{\ln 8}
 &=\frac{\ln 4}{\ln 8}\\[4pt]
 &=\log_8 4\\[4pt]
 &=\frac{\log_2 4}{\log_2 8}
 =\frac23.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the numerator is a difference of logarithms, it is the logarithm of $20$ divided by $5$, giving $\ln 4$. Dividing by $\ln 8$ therefore gives a logarithm in base $8$. Using base $2$ makes the exponents $2$ and $3$ apparent. The argument $4$ lies between $1$ and $8$, so the result should be between $0$ and $1$. That check rules out the inverted quotient.

</div>
