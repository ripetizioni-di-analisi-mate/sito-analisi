## Exercise 1

Solve $\log_2(x-1)\leq2$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x-1>0,\\
x-1\leq 2^2
\end{cases}
\Longleftrightarrow
\begin{cases}
x>1,\\
x\leq5.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
S=(1,5].
$$

::grafico{f="log(x-1)/log(2);2" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-3 ymax=3 asintoti="x=1" punti="5,2,pieno,P" legenda="\log_2(x-1);2" titolo="To the right of $x=1$, the curve stays below $y=2$ up to $P(5,2)$"}

$$
\log_2(3-1)=1<2,\qquad \log_2(5-1)=2.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is defined for $x>1$. Level $2$ is $\log_2 4$, and base $2$ makes the logarithm increasing. Comparing the heights therefore comes down to comparing their arguments in the same direction. This gives $x-1\leq4$. Combining it with $x-1>0$ yields $1<x\leq5$.

The two endpoints play different roles. At $x=1$ the argument vanishes, so the graph has a vertical asymptote there and no point. At $x=5$ the logarithm is defined and equals $2$. The point $P$ is therefore included, together with all allowed lower heights.

The value $x=3$ checks an interior solution. In contrast, $x=6$ is in the domain but has argument $5>4$, so its height exceeds the allowed level. The graph shows the same distinction relative to the horizontal line. Writing the answer as the two conditions $x>1$ and $x\leq5$ recovers the domain and the threshold comparison even without the figure.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\log_3(x+1)>\log_3(x^2-1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+1>0\ \land\ x^2-1>0
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x>1,\\[4pt]
x+1>x^2-1
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (x-2)(x+1)<0,\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -1<x<2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
S=(1,2).
$$

$$
\left.x+1\right|_{x=2}=3,\qquad \left.x^2-1\right|_{x=2}=3.
$$

::grafico{f="log(x+1)/log(3);log(x^2-1)/log(3)" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 punti="2,1,vuoto,P" asintoti="x=1" legenda="\log_3(x+1);\log_3(x^2-1)" titolo="On their common domain the first curve lies above the second up to intersection $P$, which the inequality excludes"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The left-hand logarithm exists for $x>-1$. The right-hand logarithm requires $x^2-1>0$, hence $x<-1$ or $x>1$. Their intersection leaves only $x>1$. On this interval base $3$ preserves the direction of comparison, so solve $x+1>x^2-1$.

Moving everything to the right gives $x^2-x-2=(x-2)(x+1)$. On the domain, the factor $x+1$ is positive. The product is negative exactly when $x-2$ is negative, that is, when $x<2$. Together with $x>1$ this yields $(1,2)$. At $2$ the arguments agree, so the strict inequality excludes that point.

At $x=1$ the first argument is already positive, but the second is zero. The portion shown in the figure is read on the common domain, which starts to the right of $1$. For example, at $x=\dfrac32$ the two arguments are $\dfrac52$ and $\dfrac54$. At $x=3$ they are $4$ and $8$. These values confirm the order in the two parts of the domain.

</div>
