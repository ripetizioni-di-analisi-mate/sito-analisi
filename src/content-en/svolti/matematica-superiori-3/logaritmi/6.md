## Exercise 1

Solve $\log_2(x-1)\leq2$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x-1>0,\\
x-1\leq 2^2
\end{cases}
\Longleftrightarrow
\begin{cases}
x>1,\\
x\leq5.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
S=(1,5].
$$

::grafico{f="log(x-1)/log(2);2" xmin=0 xmax=7 ymin=-3 ymax=3 asintoti="x=1" punti="5,2,pieno,P" legenda="\log_2(x-1);2" titolo="To the right of $x=1$, the curve stays below $y=2$ up to $P(5,2)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Write $2$ as $\log_2 4$. Because the logarithm is increasing, comparing the function values is equivalent to comparing $x-1$ and $4$ in the same direction. Intersect the result with $x>1$. The graph meets the level $2$ at $x=5$, which belongs to the solution because $\leq$ includes equality.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\log_3(x+1)>\log_3(x^2-1)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+1>0\ \land\ x^2-1>0
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x>1,\\[4pt]
x+1>x^2-1
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (x-2)(x+1)<0,\\[4pt]
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad -1<x<2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
S=(1,2).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The condition $x^2-1>0$ also allows $x<-1$, but those values make the argument of the first logarithm negative. The comparison is therefore restricted to $x>1$. Because the logarithm is increasing, comparing the arguments gives a quadratic inequality. The product $(x-2)(x+1)$ is negative between its roots. Intersecting that interval with the domain gives $1<x<2$.

</div>
