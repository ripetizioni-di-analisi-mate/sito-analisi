## Exercise 1

Find the domain of $f(x)=\log_2\dfrac{x-3}{x+1}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x-3}{x+1}>0
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x<-1 \ \text{or}\ x>3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quotient is positive when numerator and denominator have the same sign. For $x<-1$ both factors are negative. For $-1<x<3$ the numerator is negative and the denominator positive. For $x>3$ both are positive. At $-1$ the denominator is zero, and at $3$ the argument is zero. The domain is $(-\infty,-1)\cup(3,+\infty)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the domain of $f(x)=\log_{x-1}(4-x)$.

$$
\begin{cases}
x-1>0,\\
x-1\ne1,\\
4-x>0
\end{cases}
\Longleftrightarrow
\begin{cases}
x>1,\\
x\ne2,\\
x<4.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
D=(1,2)\cup(2,4).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here both the base and the argument depend on $x$. Positivity of the base requires $x>1$, while positivity of the argument requires $x<4$. We also exclude $x=2$ because the base is $1$ there. The value of $4-x$ at $x=2$ is positive, so the excluded point lies inside the interval and splits it in two.

</div>
