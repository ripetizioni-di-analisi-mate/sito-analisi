## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\log_2 64$ and $\log_2\dfrac1{64}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
64&=2^6 &\Longrightarrow\quad \log_2 64&=6,\\[6pt]
\frac1{64}&=2^{-6} &\Longrightarrow\quad \log_2\frac1{64}&=-6.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^6\cdot2^{-6}&=2^{6-6}=1,\\[5pt]
\log_2 64+\log_2\frac1{64}&=\log_2 1=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="log(x)/log(2)" xmin=-2 xmax=67 ymin=-6.8 ymax=7 punti="1,0;64,6,pieno,P" segmenti="64,0,64,6" asintoti="x=0" legenda="\log_2 x" titolo="Point $P$ shows $\log_2 64=6$. Arguments between $0$ and $1$ have negative height"}

<div class="spiegazione">

We are looking for two exponents of the same base. For the first, start with $2^3=8$. Squaring gives $2^6=64$, so the height $6$ on the graph corresponds to argument $64$. The second argument is the reciprocal of the first. To obtain the reciprocal of a positive power, reverse the sign of the exponent, so $2^{-6}=\dfrac1{64}$.

The minus sign belongs to the exponent. The value $\dfrac1{64}$ remains positive and lies in the domain of the logarithm. Adding the two logarithms provides a check. Their arguments are reciprocals, so their product is $1$ and the sum must be $\log_2 1=0$.

In the figure, horizontal coordinate $1$ separates arguments with positive logarithms from those with negative logarithms. The curve for base $2$ is increasing, so the point with the larger horizontal coordinate has the larger height.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\log_{\frac14} 8$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac14\right)^u&=8\\[4pt]
(2^{-2})^u&=2^3\\[4pt]
-2u&=3\\[4pt]
u&=-\frac32.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.25^x;8" xmin=-2.3 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=12 punti="-1.5,8,pieno,P" segmenti="-1.5,0,-1.5,8" legenda="\left(\frac14\right)^x;8" titolo="The horizontal coordinate of $P$ is the exponent of $\dfrac14$ that gives $8$"}

$$
\left(\frac14\right)^{-\frac32}=4^{\frac32}=(\sqrt4)^3=2^3=8.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $u$ be the exponent that gives $8$ in base $\dfrac14$. The base is $2^{-2}$ and the argument is $2^3$, so the exponents of the two powers of $2$ can be compared. The factor $-2$ multiplies $u$, giving $-2u=3$. Dividing by $-2$ gives $u=-\dfrac32$.

The sign agrees with the order of the values. The function $\left(\dfrac14\right)^u$ is decreasing and equals $1$ at $u=0$. Reaching $8$, which exceeds $1$, therefore requires a negative exponent. On the graph, the horizontal coordinate where the curve meets the horizontal line at height $8$ is exactly that value.

The check also shows how to handle the fraction and its sign together. The negative sign takes the reciprocal of the fractional base, the denominator $2$ in the exponent gives a square root, and the numerator $3$ cubes the result. For comparison, the positive exponent $\dfrac32$ would give $\dfrac18$. The two opposite exponents produce reciprocal heights, so only the negative one reaches height $8$ on the graph.

</div>
