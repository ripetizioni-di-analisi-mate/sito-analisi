## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\log_2 64$ and $\log_2\dfrac1{64}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
64&=2^6 &\Longrightarrow\quad \log_2 64&=6,\\[6pt]
\frac1{64}&=2^{-6} &\Longrightarrow\quad \log_2\frac1{64}&=-6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We want two exponents of the same base. Since $64$ is the sixth power of $2$, the first logarithm is $6$. The second argument is the reciprocal of $2^6$, obtained by reversing the sign of the exponent. The two values are therefore opposites. The number $\dfrac1{64}$ is positive, so its logarithm is defined and may have a negative value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\log_{\frac14} 8$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac14\right)^u&=8\\[4pt]
(2^{-2})^u&=2^3\\[4pt]
-2u&=3\\[4pt]
u&=-\frac32.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="0.25^x;8" xmin=-2.3 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=12 punti="-1.5,8,pieno,P" segmenti="-1.5,0,-1.5,8" legenda="\left(\frac14\right)^x;8" titolo="The horizontal coordinate of $P$ is the exponent of $\dfrac14$ that gives $8$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The definition turns evaluation of the logarithm into an equation for the exponent $u$. The base is $2^{-2}$ and the argument is $2^3$, so we can compare the exponents of two powers of $2$. The negative value is consistent with the original base $\dfrac14$, because the logarithm of an argument greater than $1$ is negative when the base is between $0$ and $1$. As a check, $\left(\dfrac14\right)^{-\frac32}=4^{\frac32}=8$.

</div>
