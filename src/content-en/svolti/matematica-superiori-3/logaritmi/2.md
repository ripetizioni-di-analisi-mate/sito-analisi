## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\log_2 24-\log_2 3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2 24-\log_2 3
 &=\log_2\frac{24}{3}\\[4pt]
 &=\log_2 8\\[4pt]
 &=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_2 24&=\log_2(3\cdot2^3)\\[4pt]
&=\log_2 3+3,\\[4pt]
\log_2 3-\log_2 24&=\log_2\frac18=-3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both logarithms have the same base and positive arguments, so their difference is the logarithm of the quotient. The first argument $24$ goes in the numerator because it is the minuend's argument. The second argument $3$ goes in the denominator because its logarithm is subtracted. The quotient is $8$, the third power of $2$.

The result can also be found without introducing a quotient. The number $24$ is the product of $3$ and $2^3$, so its logarithm is $3$ greater than $\log_2 3$. The common term $\log_2 3$ cancels in the difference.

Reversing the quotient also reverses the sign. The sign can be determined before calculating, because $24>3$ and the base-$2$ logarithm is increasing. The difference must therefore be positive. If the logarithms had different bases, this transformation would not apply directly. Here both $\log_2 24$ and $\log_2 3$ measure exponents of the same base, so their difference gives the exponent of the quotient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write $2\log_3x-\dfrac12\log_3 y+\log_3 9$ as a single logarithm for $x,y>0$. Evaluate it at $x=2$ and $y=16$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\log_3x-\frac12\log_3 y+\log_3 9
 &=\log_3(x^2)-\log_3(\sqrt y)+\log_3 9\\[4pt]
 &=\log_3\frac{9x^2}{\sqrt y},\\[6pt]
\log_3\frac{9\cdot2^2}{\sqrt{16}}
 &=\log_3 9=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_3\frac{9\cdot2^2}{\sqrt{16}}&=\log_3\frac{9\cdot4}{4}\\[4pt]
&=\log_3(3^2)=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the power laws first. The coefficient $2$ changes $x$ to the square $x^2$. The coefficient $\dfrac12$ changes $y$ to the root $\sqrt y$. The logarithm of this root is still subtracted, so the root goes in the denominator when the terms are combined. The term $\log_3 9$ is added, so its argument multiplies $x^2$ in the numerator.

The conditions $x,y>0$ make all these transformations valid. At $x=2$ and $y=16$, we get $x^2=4$ and $\sqrt y=4$. Those two factors give a quotient of $1$, so the combined argument is $9$.

The original expression provides another check of the cancellation. The value $y=16=2^4$ gives $\dfrac12\log_3 16=2\log_3 2$. The first two terms therefore have equal magnitude and opposite signs. Only $\log_3 9$ remains. Even before substitution we can tell when the expression equals $2$. We need $\dfrac{9x^2}{\sqrt y}=9$, or $y=x^4$. The supplied values satisfy this relation.

</div>
