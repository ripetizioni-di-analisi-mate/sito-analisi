## Exercise 1

Solve $\log_3(2x-1)=2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x-1>0 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x>\frac12,\\[6pt]
\log_3(2x-1)=2 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2x-1=3^2,\\[4pt]
2x&=10\\[4pt]
x&=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\log_3(2\cdot5-1)=\log_3(10-1)=\log_3 9=2.
$$

::grafico{f="log(2*x-1)/log(3);2" xmin=0 xmax=7.5 ymin=-3 ymax=3.3 asintoti="x=0.5" punti="1,0;5,2,pieno,P" segmenti="5,0,5,2" legenda="\log_3(2x-1);2" titolo="The candidate is the horizontal coordinate of $P$, where the curve has height $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality $2x-1>0$ gives domain $x>\dfrac12$. On that domain, the definition turns logarithm value $2$ into the exponent of base $3$. The argument must be $3^2=9$. From $2x-1=9$ we get $2x=10$, hence $x=5$. The required value is the unknown $x$, while $9$ is the value of the argument.

The graph is defined only to the right of $x=\dfrac12$ and increases because both the argument $2x-1$ and the base-$3$ logarithm increase. The line at height $2$ meets it exactly once.

Substitution checks the domain and the equality together. For example, $x=1$ lies in the domain but has argument $1$ and gives height zero. Being in the domain is necessary, and the required height must also be reached. The curve reaches height $2$ before $x=6$, because the argument at $x=6$ is $11$, greater than $9$. Together with monotonicity, this confirms that there are no other intersections on the defined branch.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\log_2(x-2)+\log_2(x+2)=\log_2 12$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{cases}x-2>0,\\x+2>0\end{cases}
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x>2,\\[6pt]
\log_2\bigl((x-2)(x+2)\bigr)&=\log_2 12,\\[4pt]
(x-2)(x+2)&=12,\\[4pt]
x^2&=16.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\{-4,4\}\cap(2,+\infty)=\{4\}.
$$

$$
\log_2(4-2)+\log_2(4+2)=\log_2(2\cdot6)=\log_2 12.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Before combining the terms, require $x-2$ and $x+2$ to be positive. The first condition, $x>2$, implies the second. On the common domain the sum is the logarithm of the product. Injectivity of the base-$2$ logarithm lets us set that product equal to $12$. Expanding $(x-2)(x+2)$ gives $x^2-4=12$, hence $x=\pm4$.

Both roots are candidates for the algebraic equation. At $x=-4$ the original arguments are $-6$ and $-2$. Their product is positive, but each separate logarithm is undefined. Only $x=4$, on $x>2$, remains.

The check is made in the original sum. Using only the domain of $\log_2(x^2-4)$ would enlarge the admissible set to include $x<-2$ and would incorrectly admit the negative root. Uniqueness can also be established before calculating. On $x>2$, the product $(x-2)(x+2)$ increases because both factors are positive and increasing. For instance, at $x=3$ the product is $5$, while at $x=5$ it is $21$. The threshold $12$ must be reached at an input between those two values.

</div>
