## Exercise 1

Write the tangent to $x^2+y^2=25$ at $P(3,4)$ in the form $\alpha x+\beta y=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^2+4^2&=25,\\
3(x-3)+4(y-4)&=0,\\
3x+4y&=25,\\
\frac{3}{25}x+\frac{4}{25}y&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equality verifies that the point is on the circle. The radius from the centre to $P$ is perpendicular to the tangent and has components $(3,4)$, which become the coefficients of $x$ and $y$. Write the equation of the line through $P$ and expand the products. Finally, divide the entire equation by $25$ to obtain the required right-hand side. Substituting the coordinates of $P$ into the final form gives $\dfrac{9+16}{25}=1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the tangents with slope $-2$ to the circle $(x-1)^2+(y-3)^2=5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_q &\colon 2x+y-q=0,\\
d(C,\ell_q)&=\frac{|2+3-q|}{\sqrt{2^2+1^2}}
=\frac{|5-q|}{\sqrt5},\\
\frac{|5-q|}{\sqrt5}&=\sqrt5
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |5-q|=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
y=-2x \,,\qquad y=-2x+10.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A line in the required direction has equation $y=-2x+q$. The term $q$ remains free while the line can move parallel to itself. The centre is $(1,3)$ and the radius is $\sqrt5$, so require the line to be exactly $\sqrt5$ units from the centre. The absolute value gives one tangent on either side of the parallel line through the centre. That line has intercept $5$, the mean of the two intercepts $0$ and $10$.

</div>
