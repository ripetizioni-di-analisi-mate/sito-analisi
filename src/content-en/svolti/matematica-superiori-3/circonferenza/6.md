## Exercise 1

Write the tangent to $x^2+y^2=25$ at $P(3,4)$ in the form $\alpha x+\beta y=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^2+4^2&=25,\\
3(x-3)+4(y-4)&=0,\\
3x+4y&=25,\\
\frac{3}{25}x+\frac{4}{25}y&=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equality verifies that the point is on the circle. The radius from the centre to $P$ is perpendicular to the tangent and has components $(3,4)$, which become the coefficients of $x$ and $y$. Write the equation of the line through $P$ and expand the products. Finally, divide the entire equation by $25$ to obtain the required right-hand side. Substituting the coordinates of $P$ into the final form gives $\dfrac{9+16}{25}=1$.

Normalising the right-hand side to $1$ is possible because the tangent here is $5$ units from the origin and therefore does not pass through it. As an independent check, the distance from the centre to $3x+4y-25=0$ is $25$ divided by the length of $(3,4)$, or $5$. This verifies both the point on the line and its distance from the centre. In particular, the ratio of the coefficients gives a negative slope, as perpendicularity to the radius predicts.

The right-hand side can be reduced to $1$ in two steps. The first divides by the length $5$ of $(3,4)$ and uses the unit normal $\left(\dfrac35,\dfrac45\right)$ and the right-hand side would become $5$. Dividing by $5$ once more gives the requested coefficients.

$$
d(C,\ell)=\frac{|0+0-25|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}=5.
$$

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the tangents with slope $-2$ to the circle $(x-1)^2+(y-3)^2=5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_q &\colon 2x+y-q=0,\\
d(C,\ell_q)&=\frac{|2+3-q|}{\sqrt{2^2+1^2}}
=\frac{|5-q|}{\sqrt5},\\
\frac{|5-q|}{\sqrt5}&=\sqrt5
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |5-q|=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
y=-2x \,,\qquad y=-2x+10.
$$

::grafico{f="-2*x;-2*x+10" curve="1+sqrt(5)*cos(t),3+sqrt(5)*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-1 ymax=7 uguale=1 punti="1,3,pieno,C;-1,2,pieno,P;3,4,pieno,Q" segmenti="-1,2,3,4" legenda="y=-2x;y=-2x+10;(x-1)^2+(y-3)^2=5" titolo="The contact points $P$ and $Q$ are the endpoints of a diameter."}

<div class="spiegazione">

A line in the required direction has equation $y=-2x+q$. The term $q$ remains free while the line can move parallel to itself. The centre is $(1,3)$ and the radius is $\sqrt5$, so require the line to be exactly $\sqrt5$ units from the centre. The absolute value gives one tangent on either side of the parallel line through the centre. That line has intercept $5$, the mean of the two intercepts $0$ and $10$.

An algebraic check substitutes the vertical coordinates of the two lines into the circle equation. For $q=0$ the system reduces to $5(x+1)^2=0$, so the contact is $(-1,2)$. For $q=10$ it gives $5(x-3)^2=0$, yielding contact $(3,4)$. Both are at distance $\sqrt5$ from the centre and their vectors from the centre are opposite. This is why the parallel lines touch opposite sides of the circle.

The middle line $y=-2x+5$ passes through the centre, as $-2\cdot1+5=3$ confirms. This parallel line is therefore at zero distance from the centre and contains a diameter, while the other two have just one point each.

</div>
