## Exercise 1

Determine which of $P(4,4)$, $Q(1,4)$ and $R(6,2)$ lie on the circle with centre $C(1,0)$ and radius $5$. Classify the other two points as well.

$$
\begin{aligned}
CP^2&=(4-1)^2+4^2=25,\\
CQ^2&=(1-1)^2+4^2=16<25,\\
CR^2&=(6-1)^2+2^2=29>25.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="1+5*cos(t),5*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-5 xmax=8 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="1,0,pieno,C;4,4,pieno,P;1,4,pieno,Q;6,2,pieno,R" legenda="(x-1)^2+y^2=25" titolo="The points $P$, $Q$ and $R$ lie on the circle, inside it and outside it, respectively."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance formula subtracts $1$ from the $x$-coordinate because the centre is away from the origin. All three squared distances are compared with $5^2=25$. Thus $P$ lies on the circle, $Q$ is inside, and $R$ is outside. We can also locate $R$ by starting from $(6,0)$, the right endpoint of the horizontal diameter. Keeping the same $x$-coordinate and changing the $y$-coordinate adds a positive square, so the distance exceeds the radius.

To decide membership, comparing squares is sufficient because distances are non-negative. This also gives the exact distance $4$ for $Q$, while the square root $\sqrt{29}$ is unnecessary for the comparison at $R$. The figure shows that $Q$ can be checked by moving only vertically from $C$. For $P$, both coordinate differences matter, and the triangle with legs $3$ and $4$ has hypotenuse exactly $5$.

Direct substitution of $Q$ into $(x-1)^2+y^2=25$ gives $16$ on the left. The value $25-16=9$ is the difference of the squared distances. The distance from $Q$ to the boundary along the radius through $Q$ is instead $5-4=1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For which $t\in\mathbb R$ does the point $S(t,3)$ lie in the closed disk bounded by $(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=20$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(t+2)^2+(3-1)^2&\leq 20,\\
(t+2)^2 &\leq 16,\\
-4\leq t+2&\leq 4,\\
-6\leq t&\leq 2.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{curve="-2+sqrt(20)*cos(t),1+sqrt(20)*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-8 xmax=4 ymin=-5 ymax=7 uguale=1 punti="-2,1,pieno,C;-6,3,pieno,A;2,3,pieno,B;-2,3,pieno,M" segmenti="-2,1,-2,3" linee="-6,3 2,3" legenda="(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=20" titolo="The admissible horizontal coordinates form the segment $AB$, including its endpoints."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $S$ moves along the horizontal line $y=3$, always at vertical distance $2$ from the centre. That leaves at most $16$ for the square of the horizontal difference. Since $(t+2)^2\leq16$ is equivalent to $|t+2|\leq4$, the $x$-coordinate can be at most $4$ units from $-2$. The endpoints $-6$ and $2$ are included because points on the circle satisfy the condition. All points strictly between them are inside.

The section of the disk along $y=3$ is a line segment. Its midpoint $(-2,3)$ lies vertically above the centre, and its length is $2-(-6)=8$. If the question concerned only the circle, equality would be required and only the two endpoints would remain. For example, $t=-2$ gives $(t+2)^2=0$ and an interior point, whereas $t=3$ gives $(t+2)^2=25>16$ and is excluded.

The inequalities $t+2\geq-4$ and $t+2\leq4$ must both hold. Treating them as alternatives would admit exterior points as well. At the two endpoints, direct substitution gives $(-6+2)^2+2^2=(2+2)^2+2^2=20$.

</div>
