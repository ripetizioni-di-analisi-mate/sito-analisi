## Exercise 1

Identify the locus represented by $x^2+y^2+4x-6y+13=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+4x+y^2-6y&=-13,\\
(x+2)^2+(y-3)^2&=-13+4+9=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(x,y)=(-2,3).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Complete the square in $x$ with $4$ and in $y$ with $9$. These two additions exactly balance the term $-13$. The sum of the squares is zero only when both are zero, so the locus consists solely of $(-2,3)$. A circle as a locus at fixed distance requires a positive radius. Here the fixed distance is zero and every point of the locus coincides with the centre.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For which $m\in\mathbb R$ does $x^2+y^2-6x+2y+m=0$ represent a circle with radius at least $2$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-3)^2+(y+1)^2&=9+1-m=10-m,\\
r\geq 2
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 10-m\geq 4,\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\leq 6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The linear terms stay fixed, so the centre is always $(3,-1)$ whenever the curve exists. The parameter changes the right-hand side. A radius of at least $2$ requires a squared radius of at least $4$. This also guarantees that the radius is positive. At $m=6$ the radius is exactly $2$, so the boundary value is included. For $6<m<10$ there is still a circle, but its radius is less than $2$.

</div>
