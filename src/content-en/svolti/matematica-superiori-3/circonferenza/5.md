## Exercise 1

Find the common points of the circle $x^2+y^2=25$ and the line $y=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+3^2&=25,\\
x^2&=16,\\
x&=\pm4.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
A=(-4,3),\qquad B=(4,3).
$$

::grafico{curve="5*cos(t),5*sin(t),0,6.283185307" f="3" xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="-4,3,pieno,A;4,3,pieno,B;0,3,pieno,H;0,0,pieno,C" segmenti="0,0,0,3;0,0,4,3" legenda="y=3;x^2+y^2=25" titolo="The perpendicular $CH$ divides the chord $AB$ into two segments of length $4$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Every point on the line has $y$-coordinate $3$, so substitute that value into the equation containing both coordinates. The square of $x$ must equal $16$, which gives two opposite $x$-coordinates. Each gives one point since its $y$-coordinate is already fixed by the line. Symmetry about the $y$-axis confirms that the other point has opposite $x$-coordinate and the same $y$-coordinate.

The two roots can be explained by projecting the centre onto the line. The projection is $(0,3)$, at distance $3<5$ from the origin. From the midpoint of the chord, move horizontally by $\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=4$ to reach each intersection. One then has negative horizontal coordinate and the other positive. Had the given height been $5$, the same substitution would have given $x^2=0$ and just one point of contact.

Both points can finally be checked separately. At $A$ we have $(-4)^2+3^2=25$, and at $B$ we have $4^2+3^2=25$. Both have vertical coordinate $3$, so they solve the same system.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the common points of $(x-1)^2+(y+1)^2=10$ and $y=x-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2+(x-2+1)^2&=10,\\
2(x-1)^2&=10,\\
x&=1\pm\sqrt5,\\
y&=-1\pm\sqrt5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P_{\pm}=(1\pm\sqrt5,-1\pm\sqrt5).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every point on the line satisfies $y+1=x-1$, so the two squares in the circle equation are equal. The signs in the final pair must match, because the $y$-coordinate is the $x$-coordinate minus $2$. The line passes through the centre $(1,-1)$, and the two pairs differ from the centre by opposite vectors. Geometrically, the points are therefore the endpoints of a diameter. At either point the two squares are both $5$, so their sum is $10$.

The equal squares can also be explained using coordinates relative to the centre. On the line, a horizontal displacement $u$ from the centre is accompanied by the same vertical displacement $u$. The squared distance is therefore $u^2+u^2$. Combining the positive sign for one coordinate with the negative sign for the other would still give a point on the circle, but on the opposite diagonal through the centre. The condition $y=x-2$ selects only the desired diagonal.

The centre is the midpoint, as the coordinates confirm. Averaging the horizontal coordinates of $P_\pm$ gives $1$, and averaging their vertical coordinates gives $-1$. The distance between the endpoints is $2\sqrt{10}$, which is twice the radius.

</div>
