## Exercise 1

Find the common points of the circle $x^2+y^2=25$ and the line $y=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+3^2&=25,\\
x^2&=16,\\
x&=\pm4.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
A=(-4,3),\qquad B=(4,3).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every point on the line has $y$-coordinate $3$, so substitute that value into the equation containing both coordinates. The square of $x$ must equal $16$, which gives two opposite $x$-coordinates. Each gives one point since its $y$-coordinate is already fixed by the line. Symmetry about the $y$-axis confirms that the other point has opposite $x$-coordinate and the same $y$-coordinate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the common points of $(x-1)^2+(y+1)^2=10$ and $y=x-2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-1)^2+(x-2+1)^2&=10,\\
2(x-1)^2&=10,\\
x&=1\pm\sqrt5,\\
y&=-1\pm\sqrt5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
P_{\pm}=(1\pm\sqrt5,-1\pm\sqrt5).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every point on the line satisfies $y+1=x-1$, so the two squares in the circle equation are equal. The signs in the final pair must match, because the $y$-coordinate is the $x$-coordinate minus $2$. The line passes through the centre $(1,-1)$, and the two pairs differ from the centre by opposite vectors. Geometrically, the points are therefore the endpoints of a diameter. At either point the two squares are both $5$, so their sum is $10$.

</div>
