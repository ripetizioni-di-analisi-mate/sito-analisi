## Exercise 1

Find the equation of the circle with diameter $AB$ for $A(-2,0)$ and $B(4,0)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=\left(\frac{-2+4}{2},0\right)=(1,0),\\[4pt]
r&=\frac{4-(-2)}{2}=3,\\[4pt]
(x-1)^2+y^2&=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $A$ and $B$ are the endpoints of a diameter, the centre must be halfway between them. The $y$-coordinate stays $0$ and the $x$-coordinate is the mean of $-2$ and $4$. The endpoints have the same $y$-coordinate, so the diameter is the positive difference of their $x$-coordinates, which is $6$. The radius is half this length. As a direct check, substituting $A$ gives $(-2-1)^2=9$ and substituting $B$ gives $(4-1)^2=9$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the circle through $A(0,1)$, $B(4,1)$ and $Q(4,3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_C&=\frac{0+4}{2}=2,\\
y_C&=\frac{1+3}{2}=2,\\
C&=(2,2),\\
r^2&=(4-2)^2+(3-2)^2=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
(x-2)^2+(y-2)^2=5.
$$

::grafico{curve="2+sqrt(5)*cos(t),2+sqrt(5)*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-1 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="0,1,pieno,A;4,1,pieno,B;4,3,pieno,Q;2,2,pieno,C" segmenti="2,0,2,4;1,2,5,2" legenda="(x-2)^2+(y-2)^2=5" titolo="The centre $C$ is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of $AB$ and $BQ$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The centre must be the same distance from $A$ and $B$. Because $AB$ is horizontal, its perpendicular bisector is the vertical line through its midpoint, $x=2$. Similarly, $BQ$ is vertical, so its perpendicular bisector is the horizontal line $y=2$. These two conditions fix the centre at $(2,2)$. A single distance then gives the radius. In this figure the triangle $ABQ$ is right-angled at $B$, and the centre is also the midpoint of the hypotenuse $AQ$.

</div>
