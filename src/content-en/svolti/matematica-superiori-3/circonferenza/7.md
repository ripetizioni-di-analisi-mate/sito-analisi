## Exercise 1

Find the length of the chord cut by the line $3x+4y=15$ on the circle $x^2+y^2=25$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{|3\cdot0+4\cdot0-15|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}=3,\\
\frac L2&=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=\sqrt{16}=4,\\
L&=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="(15-3*x)/4" curve="5*cos(t),5*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-6 xmax=6 ymin=-6 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,C;1.8,2.4,pieno,H;-1.4,4.8,pieno,A;5,0,pieno,B" segmenti="0,0,1.8,2.4;0,0,-1.4,4.8" legenda="3x+4y=15;x^2+y^2=25" titolo="The foot $H$ is the midpoint of the chord $AB$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The perpendicular from the centre to the line divides the chord into two equal parts. The distance formula from a point to a line first gives $d=3$. Since $3<5$, the line meets the circle at two points. The radius is the hypotenuse of the right triangle formed by the centre, the foot of the perpendicular and an endpoint. The side along the line measures $4$, so the full chord has length $8$.

The foot of the perpendicular is a multiple of the normal vector $(3,4)$. Writing it as $(3t,4t)$ and substituting into the line gives $25t=15$, hence $H=\left(\dfrac95,\dfrac{12}{5}\right)$. Along the chord, the vector $\left(\dfrac45,-\dfrac35\right)$ has length $1$ and is perpendicular to the normal. Move four times this vector from $H$ in either direction. This gives $(5,0)$ and $\left(-\dfrac75,\dfrac{24}{5}\right)$, both points on the line.

The endpoint coordinates give another length computation without using the right triangle for the half-chord. The displacements between them are $\dfrac{32}{5}$ and $-\dfrac{24}{5}$, so the squared distance is $\dfrac{1024+576}{25}=64$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the horizontal lines that cut a chord of length $6$ in the circle $(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=25$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^2&=5^2-\left(\frac62\right)^2=25-9=16,\\
d&=4,\\
|q-1|&=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
y=5\,,\qquad y=-3.
$$

::grafico{f="5;-3" curve="-2+5*cos(t),1+5*sin(t),0,6.283185307" xmin=-8 xmax=4 ymin=-5 ymax=7 uguale=1 punti="-2,1,pieno,C;-5,5;1,5;-5,-3;1,-3" segmenti="-2,-3,-2,5" legenda="y=5;y=-3;(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=25" titolo="The two horizontal chords are at the same distance from the centre $C$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the chord length is given, so use the Pythagorean relation to find the side perpendicular to the line. From $d^2=16$, take the positive square root because $d$ is a distance. For a line $y=q$, the distance from the centre is the absolute difference between the $y$-coordinates. One line is $4$ units above the centre and the other $4$ below. On both lines, the endpoints have $x$-coordinates $-5$ and $1$ because the half-chord measures $3$ and the centre has $x$-coordinate $-2$.

One could also start directly from the equation. On $y=q$, the square of the horizontal distance from the centre is $25-(q-1)^2$. For a chord of length $6$ both horizontal coordinates must lie $3$ units from the central value $-2$. The square must therefore be $9$. This method also gives $(q-1)^2=16$. The two lines are reflections across $y=1$, so the horizontal coordinates of their intersections are the same relative to the centre.

The figure shows equal lengths because the two lines have equal distances from the centre. If the given length had been $10$, the distance would have been $0$ and only the line $y=1$ would have remained.

</div>
