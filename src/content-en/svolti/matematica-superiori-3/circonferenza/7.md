## Exercise 1

Find the length of the chord cut by the line $3x+4y=15$ on the circle $x^2+y^2=25$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{|3\cdot0+4\cdot0-15|}{\sqrt{3^2+4^2}}=3,\\
\frac L2&=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=\sqrt{16}=4,\\
L&=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perpendicular from the centre to the line divides the chord into two equal parts. The distance formula from a point to a line first gives $d=3$. Since $3<5$, the line meets the circle at two points. The radius is the hypotenuse of the right triangle formed by the centre, the foot of the perpendicular and an endpoint. The side along the line measures $4$, so the full chord has length $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the horizontal lines that cut a chord of length $6$ in the circle $(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=25$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d^2&=5^2-\left(\frac62\right)^2=25-9=16,\\
d&=4,\\
|q-1|&=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
y=5\,,\qquad y=-3.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the chord length is given, so use the Pythagorean relation to find the side perpendicular to the line. From $d^2=16$, take the positive square root because $d$ is a distance. For a line $y=q$, the distance from the centre is the absolute difference between the $y$-coordinates. One line is $4$ units above the centre and the other $4$ below. On both lines, the endpoints have $x$-coordinates $-5$ and $1$ because the half-chord measures $3$ and the centre has $x$-coordinate $-2$.

</div>
