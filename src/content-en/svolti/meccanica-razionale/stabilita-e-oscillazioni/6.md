## Exercise 1

Two point particles, of mass $m$ and $2m$, slide without friction on a fixed horizontal guide and are connected to the two walls and to each other by three equal ideal springs, of stiffness $k$. Determine the natural angular frequencies of the small oscillations and the normal modes of the system in the figure.

::struttura{poligoni="2,0 3,0 3,1 2,1;5,0 6,0 6,1.2 5,1.2" linee="0,0 8,0;0,0 0,1.6;8,0 8,1.6;0,0.5 0.4,0.75 0.9,0.25 1.4,0.75 1.9,0.5 2,0.5;3,0.5 3.4,0.75 3.9,0.25 4.4,0.75 4.9,0.25 5,0.5;6,0.5 6.4,0.75 6.9,0.25 7.4,0.75 7.9,0.5 8,0.5" etichette="1,1.15,k;4,1.15,k;7,1.15,k;2.5,0.5,m;5.5,0.6,2m" frecce="2.5,1.6,3.3,1.6,x_1;5.5,1.8,6.3,1.8,x_2" titolo="The two point particles and the three springs, with the abscissas measured from the equilibrium positions"}

The abscissas $x_1$ and $x_2$ of the two particles, measured from the equilibrium configuration, in which all the springs are unloaded, are taken as Lagrangian coordinates.

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,2m\,\dot{x}_2^2 ,
\qquad
V = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(x_2 - x_1)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x_2^2
$$
$$
\mathbb{A} =
\begin{bmatrix}
m & 0 \\
0 & 2m
\end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbb{V} = k
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & -1 \\
-1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\det\big( \mathbb{V} - \omega^2\,\mathbb{A} \big)
= \big( 2k - m\omega^2 \big)\big( 2k - 2m\omega^2 \big) - k^2
= 2m^2\,\omega^4 - 6km\,\omega^2 + 3k^2 = 0
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{6km \pm \sqrt{36k^2m^2 - 24k^2m^2}}{4m^2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega_1^2 = \frac{\big( 3 - \sqrt{3} \big)\,k}{2m} ,
\quad
\omega_2^2 = \frac{\big( 3 + \sqrt{3} \big)\,k}{2m}
$$
$$
\big( 2k - m\,\omega^2 \big)\,u_1 - k\,u_2 = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{u_2}{u_1} = 2 - \frac{m\,\omega^2}{k}
$$
$$
u^{(1)} = \left( 1,\ \frac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{2} \right) ,
\qquad
u^{(2)} = \left( 1,\ \frac{1 - \sqrt{3}}{2} \right)
$$

Check with the sum and the product of the roots of the characteristic equation:
$$
\omega_1^2\,\omega_2^2 = \frac{\det \mathbb{V}}{\det \mathbb{A}} = \frac{3k^2}{2m^2} ,
\qquad
\omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2 = \frac{2k}{m} + \frac{2k}{2m} = \frac{3k}{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has two degrees of freedom, one for each particle. In the equilibrium configuration the three springs are unloaded, because an unloaded spring exerts no force; measuring the abscissas from such a configuration, the elongation of the left spring is $x_1$, that of the central spring is $x_2 - x_1$ and that of the right spring is $-x_2$, whose square is $x_2^2$.

The kinetic energy is already a quadratic form with constant coefficients, hence the kinetic matrix $\mathbb{A}$ is diagonal with entries $m$ and $2m$. The potential energy is quadratic as well, therefore the Hessian matrix $\mathbb{V}$ can be read directly from the coefficients: the term $-k\,x_1 x_2$ that comes from the expansion of $(x_2 - x_1)^2$ gives the off-diagonal entries.

The characteristic equation $\det(\mathbb{V} - \omega^2\mathbb{A}) = 0$ is of second degree in $\omega^2$ and has two positive roots, since $\mathbb{A}$ and $\mathbb{V}$ are both positive definite. The angular frequencies are $\omega_1 < \omega_2$, and the different masses prevent the modes from being symmetric.

The normal modes are obtained by substituting each angular frequency into the homogeneous system. In the first mode the ratio $\dfrac{u_2}{u_1}$ is positive, hence the two particles move in the same direction; in the second one it is negative and they move in opposite directions, with the central spring deforming more. In the first mode the particle of mass $2m$ has larger amplitude than the particle of mass $m$, because $\dfrac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2} \simeq 1.37$.

The check uses the relations between the coefficients and the roots of a second-degree equation. The product of the roots equals $\dfrac{3k^2}{2m^2}$, which coincides with $\dfrac{\det \mathbb{V}}{\det \mathbb{A}}$; the sum equals $\dfrac{3k}{m}$, which coincides with the trace of $\mathbb{A}^{-1}\mathbb{V}$. Both relations are satisfied by the values found, since $\big(3-\sqrt{3}\big)\big(3+\sqrt{3}\big) = 6$ and the sum of the two numbers equals $6$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two equal pendulums, each formed by a point particle of mass $m$ hanging from a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $\ell$, are hinged at the fixed points $O_1$ and $O_2$, placed at the same height and at distance $d$. The two point particles are connected by an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and natural length $d$. Determine the natural angular frequencies of the small oscillations of the system in the figure about the configuration with both pendulums vertical.

::struttura{nodi="O_1,0,4;P_1,0,1;O_2,3.2,4;P_2,3.2,1" aste="O_1-P_1;O_2-P_2" vincoli="O_1,cerniera,90;O_2,cerniera,90" linee="0.00,1.00 0.68,1.28 0.88,0.72 1.09,1.28 1.29,0.72 1.50,1.28 1.70,0.72 1.91,1.28 2.11,0.72 2.32,1.28 2.52,0.72 3.20,1.00" tratteggi="0,4 0,0.6;3.2,4 3.2,0.6" punti="0,1,,pieno;3.2,1,,pieno" quote="O_1,O_2,d,-0.6" etichette="1.6,1.75,k;0.3,2.3,ϑ_1;3.5,2.3,ϑ_2" titolo="The two pendulums connected by the spring of natural length $d$"}

The angles $\vartheta_1$ and $\vartheta_2$ that the two rods form with the downward vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. In the configuration $(0,0)$ the spring has length $d$, hence it is unloaded and the configuration is an equilibrium configuration.

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}_2^2 ,
\qquad
L = \overline{P_1P_2} ,
\qquad
L - d \simeq \ell\,\big( \vartheta_2 - \vartheta_1 \big)
$$
$$
V \simeq \text{constant} + \frac{m g \ell}{2}\,\big( \vartheta_1^2 + \vartheta_2^2 \big) + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2\,\big( \vartheta_2 - \vartheta_1 \big)^2
$$
$$
\mathbb{A} = m\ell^2
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbb{V} =
\begin{bmatrix}
m g \ell + k\ell^2 & -k\ell^2 \\[4pt]
-k\ell^2 & m g \ell + k\ell^2
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\det\big( \mathbb{V} - \omega^2\,\mathbb{A} \big) = \big( m g \ell + k\ell^2 - m\ell^2\omega^2 \big)^2 - k^2\ell^4 = 0
$$
$$
m g \ell + k\ell^2 - m\ell^2\,\omega^2 = \pm\,k\ell^2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega_1^2 = \frac{g}{\ell} ,
\qquad
\omega_2^2 = \frac{g}{\ell} + \frac{2k}{m}
$$
$$
u^{(1)} = (1,\,1) ,
\qquad
u^{(2)} = (1,\,-1)
$$

Check of the two modes with a direct argument. In the first mode $\vartheta_1 = \vartheta_2$, hence
$$
P_2 - P_1 = (d,\,0) ,
\qquad
L = d ,
$$
and the spring stays unloaded, hence each pendulum oscillates as a simple pendulum, with $\omega^2 = \dfrac{g}{\ell}$. In the second mode $\vartheta_2 = -\vartheta_1$, hence the midpoint of the spring stays at rest and each pendulum is connected to a fixed point by half a spring, of stiffness $2k$, which gives
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{g}{\ell} + \frac{2k}{m} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rods have negligible mass, therefore the kinetic energy is that of the two point particles, each in circular motion of radius $\ell$ with speed $\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}_i$. The kinetic matrix is diagonal and constant.

For the potential energy one expands to second order about the vertical configuration. The term of the weight of each pendulum is $-m g \ell \cos\vartheta_i$, whose expansion gives $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\,\vartheta_i^2$ up to a constant. The elastic term is obtained by observing that the change in length of the spring, to first order, is $\ell\,(\vartheta_2 - \vartheta_1)$, because the point $P_i$ moves horizontally by $\ell\,\vartheta_i$.

The characteristic equation is the square of a binomial set equal to $k^2\ell^4$, therefore it is solved by taking the square root: the two angular frequencies correspond to the signs $+$ and $-$. The first one does not contain $k$, the second one increases with the stiffness of the spring.

The normal modes are $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$, as happens in every system symmetric under the exchange of the two coordinates: in the **in-phase mode** the two pendulums oscillate together, in the **out-of-phase mode** they oscillate in opposite directions.

The check is physical and does not use the determinant. In the first mode the distance between the two particles stays exactly $d$, because the quadrilateral $O_1P_1P_2O_2$ stays a parallelogram; the spring does not intervene and the angular frequency is that of the simple pendulum. In the second mode the motion is symmetric with respect to the midline, hence the midpoint of the spring does not move and each half behaves as a spring of halved length, therefore of stiffness $2k$, with one end fixed. The addition of the term $\dfrac{2k}{m}$ is the one found with the computation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, has the end $A$ hinged to a collar that slides without friction on a fixed horizontal guide; the rod rotates in the vertical plane about $A$. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ connects the collar to the fixed point $O$ of the guide and is unloaded when the collar is at $O$. Determine the natural angular frequencies of the small oscillations of the system in the figure about the stable equilibrium configuration, and compute their value in the case $k = \dfrac{m g}{\ell}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0.4,3;A,2.2,3;B,3.6,0.4" aste="A-B" vincoli="O,incastro,180;A,carrello,90" linee="0,3 4.4,3;0.40,3.00 0.80,3.22 0.94,2.78 1.08,3.22 1.23,2.78 1.37,3.22 1.52,2.78 1.66,3.22 1.80,2.78 2.20,3.00" tratteggi="2.2,3 2.2,0.8" punti="2.9,1.7,G,baricentro" forze="2.9 1.7,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="1.3,3.5,k;2.5,1.6,ϑ" quote="O,A,x,-0.9" titolo="The rod with the end $A$ on the collar, pulled back by the spring toward the fixed point $O$"}

The abscissa $x$ of $A$ measured from $O$ and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. The center of mass is
$$
G = \left( x + \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta ,\ -\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta \right) .
$$

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\left( \dot{x}^2 + \ell\,\dot{x}\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta + \frac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 \right) + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{m\ell^2}{12}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
V(x, \vartheta) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial x} = k\,x = 0 \\[10pt]
\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial \vartheta} = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
(x, \vartheta) = (0, 0)
$$
$$
\mathbb{A} =
\begin{bmatrix}
m & \dfrac{m\ell}{2} \\[8pt]
\dfrac{m\ell}{2} & \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}
\end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbb{V} =
\begin{bmatrix}
k & 0 \\[4pt]
0 & \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\det\big( \mathbb{V} - \omega^2\,\mathbb{A} \big)
= \big( k - m\omega^2 \big)\left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - \frac{m\ell^2}{3}\,\omega^2 \right) - \frac{m^2\ell^2}{4}\,\omega^4 = 0
$$
$$
m\,\ell\,\omega^4 - \big( 6 m g + 4 k \ell \big)\,\omega^2 + 6\,g\,k = 0
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{3 m g + 2 k \ell \mp \sqrt{9m^2g^2 + 6 m g k \ell + 4k^2\ell^2}}{m\,\ell}
$$
$$
k = \frac{m g}{\ell}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ell^2\,\omega^4 - 10\,g\,\ell\,\omega^2 + 6\,g^2 = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega_1^2 = \frac{\big( 5 - \sqrt{19} \big)\,g}{\ell} ,
\quad
\omega_2^2 = \frac{\big( 5 + \sqrt{19} \big)\,g}{\ell}
$$

Check with the sum and the product of the roots in the case $k = \dfrac{m g}{\ell}$:
$$
\omega_1^2\,\omega_2^2 = \frac{\det \mathbb{V}}{\det \mathbb{A}} = \frac{\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cdot\dfrac{m g}{\ell}}{\dfrac{m^2\ell^2}{12}} = \frac{6\,g^2}{\ell^2} ,
\qquad
\omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2 = \frac{10\,g}{\ell}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has two degrees of freedom: the translation of the collar along the guide and the rotation of the rod about $A$. The guide and the hinge are smooth, hence the constraints are ideal.

The kinetic energy is computed with König's theorem: the term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2$ with $v_G^2 = \dot{x}^2 + \ell\,\dot{x}\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta + \dfrac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, plus the term of the rotation about the center of mass with $I_G = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$. Evaluating the coefficients at $\vartheta = 0$ gives the matrix $\mathbb{A}$, which here **is not diagonal**: the translation and the rotation are coupled by the inertia, not by the forces.

The potential energy, on the contrary, is the sum of a term in $x$ and of a term in $\vartheta$, therefore $\mathbb{V}$ is diagonal. The equilibrium configuration is the one with the spring unloaded and the rod vertical and downward, and the Hessian is positive definite there, hence the equilibrium is stable.

The characteristic equation is obtained by expanding the determinant; the term $-\dfrac{m^2\ell^2}{4}\,\omega^4$ comes from the square of the off-diagonal entry of $\mathbb{A}$ and reduces the coefficient of $\omega^4$ from $\dfrac{m^2\ell^2}{3}$ to $\dfrac{m^2\ell^2}{12}$, which is the determinant of $\mathbb{A}$.

In the case $k = \dfrac{m g}{\ell}$ the numbers become manageable and the two angular frequencies $\omega_1 \simeq 0.80\,\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$ and $\omega_2 \simeq 3.06\,\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$ are obtained. The lower one corresponds to a motion in which the collar and the rod move in the same direction, the higher one to a rapid motion in which they oppose each other.

The check compares the roots found with the product and the sum that can be read directly in the matrices. The product is the ratio of the determinants and equals $\dfrac{6g^2}{\ell^2}$, which coincides with $(5-\sqrt{19})(5+\sqrt{19})\dfrac{g^2}{\ell^2} = \dfrac{6g^2}{\ell^2}$; the sum equals $\dfrac{10 g}{\ell}$, as the sum of the two values found.

</div>
