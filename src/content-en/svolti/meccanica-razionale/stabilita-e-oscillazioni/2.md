## Exercise 1

A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to the smooth fixed guide of equation $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, with $a > 0$, contained in a vertical plane. The weight and the constant horizontal force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, act on the particle. Determine the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and study their stability.

::grafico{f="x^2/2" legenda=0 xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.6 ymin=-0.6 ymax=3.4 punti="1,0.5,pieno,P" vettori="1,0.5,1,-0.3,mg;1,0.5,1.95,0.5,F" unicolore=1 titolo="The particle $P$ on the guide $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, with the weight and the force $\vec{F}$"}

The particle has a single degree of freedom and the Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the abscissa $x$, since the height is determined by the equation of the guide.

$$
U(x) = -m g\,y + F\,x = -\frac{m g}{2a}\,x^2 + F\,x
$$
$$
U'(x) = -\frac{m g}{a}\,x + F = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x_0 = \frac{a F}{m g}
$$
$$
U''(x) = -\frac{m g}{a} < 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
V''(x_0) = \frac{m g}{a} > 0
$$

Check with the equilibrium condition of a particle constrained to a smooth curve, that is, with the vanishing of the tangential component of the active forces. A vector tangent to the guide is $\vec{t} = \left( 1,\ \dfrac{x}{a} \right)$, hence
$$
\big( F,\ -m g \big) \cdot \left( 1,\ \frac{x}{a} \right) = F - \frac{m g}{a}\,x = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x_0 = \frac{a F}{m g}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide is smooth, bilateral and fixed, therefore the constraint is ideal and the search for equilibrium reduces to the stationary points of the potential. The coordinate $x$ identifies the configuration, since $y$ is obtained from the equation of the guide.

The potential of the weight is $-m g\,y$, and substituting $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$ gives $-\dfrac{m g}{2 a}\,x^2$. The potential of the constant force $\vec F$ is $F\,x$, because the force has the direction of the $x$-axis and constant magnitude.

The potential is a second-degree polynomial with negative leading coefficient, hence it has a single stationary point, which is a maximum. The equilibrium configuration is therefore unique and, since $V'' > 0$, it is **stable** by the Lagrange-Dirichlet theorem.

The result can be read off the drawing, because the particle settles where the guide is inclined just enough for the normal reaction, together with the weight, to balance the force $\vec F$. For $F \to 0$ the configuration tends to the vertex of the parabola, and for large $F$ it moves to the right proportionally to $F$.

The check uses a route different from the potential, because the reaction of the smooth guide is normal to the curve and therefore at equilibrium the resultant of the active forces must be normal as well, that is, its component along the tangent must be zero. The scalar product between the resultant of the active forces and the tangent vector gives the same configuration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to a smooth fixed circular guide of center $O$ and radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects $P$ to the lowest point $A$ of the guide. Determine the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and study their stability.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" punti="1.41,-1.41,P,pieno;0,0,O;0,-2,A" linee="0.00,-2.00 0.25,-1.72 0.48,-1.97 0.47,-1.63 0.71,-1.88 0.70,-1.53 0.94,-1.78 0.93,-1.44 1.16,-1.69 1.41,-1.41" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2;0,0 1.41,-1.41" forze="1.41 -1.41,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="0.3,-1.15,ϑ;0.75,-2.3,k" titolo="The particle $P$ on the circular guide, with the spring connecting it to the lowest point $A$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the angle $\vartheta$ between the radius $OP$ and the downward vertical $OA$, positive counterclockwise, so that
$$
P - O = R\,(\sin\vartheta,\,-\cos\vartheta) ,
\qquad
\overline{PA}^{\,2} = 2R^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta) .
$$

$$
U(\vartheta) = -m g\,y_P - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\overline{PA}^{\,2}
= m g R \cos\vartheta - k R^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)
$$
$$
U(\vartheta) = \big( m g R + k R^2 \big)\cos\vartheta - k R^2
$$
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\big( m g R + k R^2 \big)\sin\vartheta = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\vartheta = 0 ,
\quad
\vartheta = \pi
$$
$$
U''(\vartheta) = -\big( m g R + k R^2 \big)\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
U''(0) = -\big( m g R + k R^2 \big) < 0 ,
\qquad
U''(\pi) = m g R + k R^2 > 0
$$

Check with the vanishing of the tangential component of the active forces. The unit tangent vector to the guide is $\vec{t} = (\cos\vartheta,\,\sin\vartheta)$ and the elastic force is $k\,(A - P) = k R\,(-\sin\vartheta,\ \cos\vartheta - 1)$, hence
$$
\big( 0,\,-m g \big) \cdot \vec{t} + k R \big( -\sin\vartheta,\ \cos\vartheta - 1 \big) \cdot \vec{t}
= -m g \sin\vartheta - k R \sin\vartheta
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The circular guide leaves a single degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ identifies the configuration. The length of the spring is computed with the law of cosines in the triangle $OPA$, which has the two sides $OP$ and $OA$ equal to $R$ and the included angle $\vartheta$; from this $\overline{PA}^{\,2} = 2R^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)$.

Since the natural length is zero, the potential of the spring is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\overline{PA}^{\,2}$, which is written directly with the square of the distance, without taking the square root. Adding the potential of the weight gives a potential of the form $c\,\cos\vartheta$ with $c = m g R + k R^2 > 0$.

The equilibrium configurations are $\vartheta = 0$, with the particle in the lowest position, and $\vartheta = \pi$, with the particle in the highest position. At $\vartheta = 0$ we have $V'' = m g R + k R^2 > 0$, hence the equilibrium is **stable**; at $\vartheta = \pi$ we have $V'' < 0$ and the equilibrium is **unstable**.

Weight and spring cooperate, since both tend to bring the particle back toward $A$, and indeed they appear with the same sign in the coefficient $c$. The spring makes the potential well deeper, and in the study of the small oscillations it will be seen that it also increases its angular frequency.

In the check it is imposed that the active forces have zero tangential component, because the reaction of the smooth guide is directed along the radius. The total tangential component equals $-(m g + k R)\sin\vartheta$, which vanishes for $\sin\vartheta = 0$, that is, in the same two configurations. Note that such an expression is $\dfrac{1}{R}\,U'(\vartheta)$, as it must be, because the virtual displacement of $P$ has magnitude $R\,\delta\vartheta$ and is tangent to the guide.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane. A constant couple of moment $\mathcal{M} > 0$, counterclockwise, is applied to the rod. Determine the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure, discussing their existence, and study their stability.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3.4;B,2.3,1.1" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,3.4 0,1.2" punti="1.15,2.25,G,baricentro" forze="1.15 2.25,0,-0.9,mg" coppie="1.7 1.7,1,M" etichette="0.4,2.5,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$ with the constant couple $\mathcal{M}$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical, positive counterclockwise. The virtual work of the couple is $\delta L = \mathcal{M}\,\delta\vartheta$, hence the Lagrangian component of the force system is $Q_\vartheta = \mathcal{M}$ and the corresponding potential is $\mathcal{M}\,\vartheta$.

$$
U(\vartheta) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + \mathcal{M}\,\vartheta
$$
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta + \mathcal{M} = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin\vartheta = \frac{2\mathcal{M}}{m g \ell} = \lambda
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\lambda > 1 & \text{no equilibrium configuration} \\[4pt]
\lambda = 1 & \vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[4pt]
\lambda < 1 & \vartheta_1 = \arcsin\lambda , \quad \vartheta_2 = \pi - \arcsin\lambda
\end{cases}
$$
$$
U''(\vartheta) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
U''(\vartheta_1) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2} < 0 ,
\qquad
U''(\vartheta_2) = +\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sqrt{1 - \lambda^2} > 0
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to the pole $A$:
$$
M_A = \mathcal{M} - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates about the fixed hinge $A$ and has a single degree of freedom. The potential of the weight is $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, as for the hinged rod with no other force system.

For the couple one starts from the virtual work. In a virtual rotation $\delta\vartheta$ the couple does the work $\mathcal{M}\,\delta\vartheta$, therefore $Q_\vartheta = \mathcal{M}$; since $\mathcal{M}$ is constant, the condition $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = \mathcal{M}$ is satisfied by $U = \mathcal{M}\,\vartheta$. The force system is therefore conservative.

The equation $U'(\vartheta) = 0$ is rewritten as $\sin\vartheta = \lambda$ with the dimensionless parameter $\lambda = \dfrac{2\mathcal{M}}{m g \ell}$, which compares the applied moment with the maximum moment of the weight with respect to $A$. For $\lambda > 1$ the moment of the couple exceeds that of the weight in every configuration, therefore equilibrium does not exist and the rod can only rotate. For $\lambda < 1$ the equilibrium configurations in the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ are two, symmetric with respect to the horizontal direction.

In the first one, $\vartheta_1 = \arcsin\lambda$, the cosine is positive, therefore $U'' < 0$ and $V'' > 0$, hence the equilibrium is **stable**. In the second one, $\vartheta_2 = \pi - \arcsin\lambda$, the cosine is negative and the equilibrium is **unstable**.

In the limiting case $\lambda = 1$ the two configurations coincide at $\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and we obtain $U''(\vartheta_0) = 0$, hence the second derivative criterion does not apply. In such a case one observes that $U'(\vartheta) = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\,(1 - \sin\vartheta) \ge 0$, therefore $U$ is increasing and $\vartheta_0$ is not a maximum, hence the equilibrium is unstable.

The check with the cardinal moment equation with respect to $A$ gives the same equation, because the moment of the couple equals $\mathcal{M}$ with respect to every pole and that of the weight equals $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$.

</div>
