## Exercise 1

A double pendulum is formed by two rigid rods of negligible mass and length $\ell$ each, with a point particle of mass $m$ at the end of each rod: the first rod is hinged at the fixed point $O$ and carries the particle $P_1$, the second one is hinged at $P_1$ and carries the particle $P_2$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Determine the natural angular frequencies and the normal modes of the small oscillations about the vertical configuration.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,4.4;P_1,0.9,2.74;P_2,2.65,2.04" aste="O-P_1;P_1-P_2" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" cerniere="P_1" tratteggi="0,4.4 0,2.6;0.9,2.74 0.9,1.3" punti="0.9,2.74,,pieno;2.65,2.04,,pieno" etichette="0.25,3.5,ϑ_1;1.2,1.95,ϑ_2;1.1,2.95,m;2.8,1.79,m" titolo="Double pendulum with the two point particles of mass $m$"}

The angles $\vartheta_1$ and $\vartheta_2$ that the two rods form with the downward vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates.

$$
P_1 - O = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta_1,\,-\cos\vartheta_1) ,
\qquad
P_2 - P_1 = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta_2,\,-\cos\vartheta_2)
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\left[ 2\,\dot{\vartheta}_1^2 + 2\,\dot{\vartheta}_1\dot{\vartheta}_2\cos(\vartheta_1 - \vartheta_2) + \dot{\vartheta}_2^2 \right]
$$
$$
V = -m g \ell\,\big( 2\cos\vartheta_1 + \cos\vartheta_2 \big)
$$
$$
\mathbb{A} = m\ell^2
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 1 \\
1 & 1
\end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbb{V} = m g \ell
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\lambda = \frac{\ell\,\omega^2}{g} ,
\qquad
\det\big( \mathbb{V} - \omega^2 \mathbb{A} \big) = m^2g^2\ell^2\left[ (2 - 2\lambda)(1 - \lambda) - \lambda^2 \right] = 0
$$
$$
\lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 2 = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega_1^2 = \big( 2 - \sqrt{2} \big)\frac{g}{\ell} ,
\qquad
\omega_2^2 = \big( 2 + \sqrt{2} \big)\frac{g}{\ell}
$$
$$
(2 - 2\lambda)\,u_1 - \lambda\,u_2 = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{u_2}{u_1} = \frac{2 - 2\lambda}{\lambda}
$$
$$
u^{(1)} = \big( 1,\ \sqrt{2} \big) ,
\qquad
u^{(2)} = \big( 1,\ -\sqrt{2} \big)
$$

Check with the orthogonality of the modes with respect to the kinetic matrix:
$$
u^{(1)} \cdot \mathbb{A}\,u^{(2)}
= m\ell^2\,\big( 1,\ \sqrt{2} \big) \cdot
\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix}
= m\ell^2\left[ \big( 2 - \sqrt{2} \big) + \sqrt{2}\,\big( 1 - \sqrt{2} \big) \right] = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rods have negligible mass, hence the kinetic energy is that of the two particles. The velocity of $P_1$ has magnitude $\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}_1$; that of $P_2$ is the sum of the velocity of $P_1$ and of the velocity of $P_2$ relative to the second rod, therefore its square contains the cross term $\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}_1\dot{\vartheta}_2\cos(\vartheta_1 - \vartheta_2)$. Evaluating the coefficients in the configuration $(0,0)$ gives the matrix $\mathbb{A}$, which is not diagonal.

The potential energy is the sum of the two terms of the weight, with $y_{P_1} = -\ell\cos\vartheta_1$ and $y_{P_2} = -\ell\cos\vartheta_1 - \ell\cos\vartheta_2$; the first angle appears twice, because the height of both particles depends on the first rod. The Hessian at $(0,0)$ is diagonal and positive definite, hence the vertical configuration is stable.

It is convenient to introduce the pure number $\lambda = \dfrac{\ell\,\omega^2}{g}$, which turns the characteristic equation into a second-degree equation with integer coefficients. Its roots $2 \mp \sqrt{2}$ give the two angular frequencies: $\omega_1 \simeq 0.77\,\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$ and $\omega_2 \simeq 1.85\,\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$, the first one slower and the second one faster than the simple pendulum of length $\ell$.

In the **first mode** the two rods rotate in the same direction and the second one oscillates with amplitude $\sqrt{2}$ times that of the first: the double pendulum moves as a single arc. In the **second mode** the two rods rotate in opposite directions, with the same ratio of amplitudes. The general motion is the superposition
$$
\vartheta_1 = c_1\cos(\omega_1 t + \varphi_1) + c_2\cos(\omega_2 t + \varphi_2) ,
\qquad
\vartheta_2 = \sqrt{2}\,c_1\cos(\omega_1 t + \varphi_1) - \sqrt{2}\,c_2\cos(\omega_2 t + \varphi_2) ,
$$
with the four constants determined by the initial data.

The check verifies a general property of the normal modes: eigenvectors relative to distinct angular frequencies are orthogonal with respect to the kinetic matrix. Computing the product $u^{(1)} \cdot \mathbb{A}\,u^{(2)}$ gives zero, therefore the two modes found are correct. A second check comes from the product of the roots: $\omega_1^2\,\omega_2^2 = \dfrac{\det \mathbb{V}}{\det \mathbb{A}} = \dfrac{2g^2}{\ell^2}$, and indeed $\big(2-\sqrt{2}\big)\big(2+\sqrt{2}\big) = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is supported in a vertical plane by two equal vertical ideal springs, of stiffness $k$, attached to its ends and to the ground. The rod can translate vertically and rotate by small angles. Determine the natural angular frequencies, the normal modes and the normal coordinates of the system in the figure.

::struttura{nodi="A,0.6,2;B,4.4,2" aste="A-B" linee="0.60,2.00 0.82,1.65 0.38,1.52 0.82,1.39 0.38,1.26 0.82,1.14 0.38,1.01 0.82,0.88 0.38,0.75 0.60,0.40;4.40,2.00 4.62,1.65 4.18,1.52 4.62,1.39 4.18,1.26 4.62,1.14 4.18,1.01 4.62,0.88 4.18,0.75 4.40,0.40;0,0.4 5,0.4" punti="2.5,2,G,baricentro" frecce="2.5,2.5,2.5,3.1,y" etichette="1,1.2,k;4.8,1.2,k;2.9,1.75,ϑ" titolo="The rod resting on the two equal vertical springs"}

The vertical displacement $y$ of the center of mass, measured from the equilibrium configuration, and the angle $\vartheta$ by which the rod rotates with respect to the horizontal, positive counterclockwise, are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. For small angles the elongations of the two springs with respect to equilibrium are
$$
\delta_A = y - \frac{\ell}{2}\,\vartheta ,
\qquad
\delta_B = y + \frac{\ell}{2}\,\vartheta .
$$

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{m\ell^2}{12}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
V = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\delta_A^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\delta_B^2 = k\,y^2 + \frac{k\,\ell^2}{4}\,\vartheta^2
$$
$$
\mathbb{A} =
\begin{bmatrix}
m & 0 \\[4pt]
0 & \dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}
\end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbb{V} =
\begin{bmatrix}
2k & 0 \\[4pt]
0 & \dfrac{k\ell^2}{2}
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\big( 2k - m\,\omega^2 \big)\left( \frac{k\ell^2}{2} - \frac{m\ell^2}{12}\,\omega^2 \right) = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega_1^2 = \frac{2k}{m} ,
\qquad
\omega_2^2 = \frac{6k}{m}
$$
$$
u^{(1)} = (1,\,0) ,
\qquad
u^{(2)} = (0,\,1)
$$

Check with the cardinal equations applied separately to the two modes. In the translation mode, with $\vartheta = 0$, the two springs act in parallel:
$$
m\,\ddot{y} = -2k\,y
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega_1^2 = \frac{2k}{m}
$$
In the rotation mode, with $y = 0$, the moment with respect to the center of mass equals
$$
M_G = -k\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\vartheta \cdot \frac{\ell}{2} - k\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\vartheta \cdot \frac{\ell}{2} = -\frac{k\ell^2}{2}\,\vartheta
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{m\ell^2}{12}\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -\frac{k\ell^2}{2}\,\vartheta ,
\qquad
\omega_2^2 = \frac{6k}{m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Measuring the coordinates from the equilibrium configuration, the weight no longer appears in the potential energy: its effect is already contained in the initial compression of the springs, and only the elastic terms remain in the quadratic expansion. This is the reason why it is always convenient to choose the origin of the coordinates in the equilibrium configuration.

The elongations are obtained from the rigid motion of the rod: a point of the rod at distance $s$ from the center of mass moves vertically by $y + s\,\vartheta$ for small angles, hence the end $A$, which has $s = -\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, moves by $y - \dfrac{\ell}{2}\vartheta$ and the end $B$ by $y + \dfrac{\ell}{2}\vartheta$. Adding the two elastic terms, the cross terms cancel, because the two springs are equal and symmetric with respect to the center of mass.

Both $\mathbb{A}$ and $\mathbb{V}$ are diagonal, hence the characteristic equation splits and the angular frequencies can be read one per coordinate. The normal modes are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$: in the first one the rod translates while staying horizontal, in the second one it rotates about the center of mass, which stays at rest. The coordinates $y$ and $\vartheta$ are already **normal coordinates**, because each one satisfies a harmonic equation independent of the other.

The uncoupling comes from the symmetry of the system with respect to the center of mass. With two springs of different stiffness the off-diagonal entries of $\mathbb{V}$ would not be zero and the modes would mix translation and rotation.

The check obtains the same two angular frequencies from the cardinal equations. In the translation motion the springs, both deformed by $y$, give a total force $-2k\,y$ on the mass $m$. In the rotation motion the two elastic forces $k\dfrac{\ell}{2}\vartheta$ have opposite direction and moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, hence their moments with respect to $G$ add up; the angular momentum equation with respect to $G$ gives the second angular frequency.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two point particles of mass $m$ slide without friction on a fixed horizontal guide. The first one is connected to the left wall by a spring of stiffness $k$, the second one to the right wall by an equal spring, and the two particles are connected to each other by a spring of stiffness $2k$. At the initial instant the first particle is displaced by $a$ from its equilibrium position, the second one is at its own equilibrium position and both are at rest. Determine the normal coordinates and the motion of the system in the figure.

::struttura{poligoni="2,0 3,0 3,1 2,1;5,0 6,0 6,1 5,1" linee="0,0 8,0;0,0 0,1.6;8,0 8,1.6;0,0.5 0.4,0.75 0.9,0.25 1.4,0.75 1.9,0.5 2,0.5;3,0.5 3.4,0.8 3.9,0.2 4.4,0.8 4.9,0.2 5,0.5;6,0.5 6.4,0.75 6.9,0.25 7.4,0.75 7.9,0.5 8,0.5" etichette="1,1.15,k;4,1.2,2k;7,1.15,k;2.5,0.5,m;5.5,0.5,m" frecce="2.5,1.6,3.3,1.6,x_1;5.5,1.6,6.3,1.6,x_2" titolo="The two point particles with the springs $k$, $2k$ and $k$"}

$$
\mathbb{A} = m
\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbb{V} = k
\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\big( 3k - m\omega^2 \big)^2 - 4k^2 = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega_1^2 = \frac{k}{m} ,
\qquad
\omega_2^2 = \frac{5k}{m}
$$
$$
u^{(1)} = (1,\,1) ,
\qquad
u^{(2)} = (1,\,-1)
$$
$$
\xi = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{2} ,
\qquad
\eta = \frac{x_1 - x_2}{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\begin{cases}
\ddot{\xi} + \omega_1^2\,\xi = 0 \\[4pt]
\ddot{\eta} + \omega_2^2\,\eta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\xi(0) = \frac{a}{2} ,
\quad
\eta(0) = \frac{a}{2} ,
\quad
\dot{\xi}(0) = \dot{\eta}(0) = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\xi = \frac{a}{2}\cos\omega_1 t ,
\quad
\eta = \frac{a}{2}\cos\omega_2 t
$$
$$
x_1(t) = \frac{a}{2}\,\big( \cos\omega_1 t + \cos\omega_2 t \big) ,
\qquad
x_2(t) = \frac{a}{2}\,\big( \cos\omega_1 t - \cos\omega_2 t \big)
$$

::grafico{f="0.5*(cos(x)+cos(2.2360679*x));0.5*(cos(x)-cos(2.2360679*x))" xmin=0 xmax=14 ymin=-1.2 ymax=1.2 nomi="t,x" titolo="The abscissas $x_1$ (solid) and $x_2$ (dashed) as functions of time, with $a = 1$ and $\omega_1 = 1$"}

Check by substitution into the first equation of motion, $m\,\ddot{x}_1 = -3k\,x_1 + 2k\,x_2$:
$$
m\,\ddot{x}_1 = -\frac{a}{2}\,m\,\big( \omega_1^2\cos\omega_1 t + \omega_2^2\cos\omega_2 t \big)
= -\frac{a}{2}\,k\,\big( \cos\omega_1 t + 5\cos\omega_2 t \big)
$$
$$
-3k\,x_1 + 2k\,x_2 = -\frac{a}{2}\,k\,\big( 3\cos\omega_1 t + 3\cos\omega_2 t \big) + \frac{a}{2}\,k\,\big( 2\cos\omega_1 t - 2\cos\omega_2 t \big)
= -\frac{a}{2}\,k\,\big( \cos\omega_1 t + 5\cos\omega_2 t \big)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The abscissas are measured from the equilibrium positions, in which all the springs are unloaded. The elongation of the central spring is $x_2 - x_1$, hence the potential energy equals $\dfrac{1}{2}k x_1^2 + k (x_2 - x_1)^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}k x_2^2$, from which the entries of $\mathbb{V}$ can be read.

The system is symmetric under the exchange of the two particles, therefore the normal modes are $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$. In the first one the two particles move together and the central spring stays unloaded, hence only the outer spring counts and the angular frequency is $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$. In the second one they move in opposition, the central spring stretches by twice the displacement of each particle and the angular frequency rises to $\sqrt{\dfrac{5k}{m}}$.

The normal coordinates are the half-sum and the half-difference of the abscissas. Adding and subtracting the two equations of motion gives two uncoupled harmonic equations, one for $\xi$ and one for $\eta$, each with its own angular frequency.

The initial data give $\xi(0) = \eta(0) = \dfrac{a}{2}$ with zero velocities, therefore both normal coordinates start from their maximum elongation and oscillate as cosines. Going back to the abscissas gives the motion written above, in which **both modes are present with the same amplitude**.

The ratio of the two angular frequencies equals $\sqrt{5}$, which is irrational, hence the motion is not periodic, because the two harmonics never return to the initial phase at the same time. The graph shows the first instants, with the second particle setting into motion even though it started at rest at its own equilibrium position, because the central spring transmits to it the motion of the first one.

The check substitutes the functions found into the first equation of motion. The two sides coincide for every $t$, since the coefficients of $\cos\omega_1 t$ and of $\cos\omega_2 t$ are equal separately.

</div>
