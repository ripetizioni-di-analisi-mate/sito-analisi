## Exercise 1

The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the point $O$ of the rod placed at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{6}$ from the end $A$, and oscillates in a vertical plane, as the diagram in the figure shows. Determine the angular frequency and the period of the small oscillations about the stable equilibrium configuration, and the length of the simple pendulum that oscillates with the same period.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,3.4;A,-0.5,4.27;B,2.5,-0.93" aste="A-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,3.4 0,0.9" punti="1,1.67,G,baricentro" forze="1 1.67,0,-0.9,mg" etichette="0.22,2.15,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$, with the center of mass $G$ at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{3}$ from the hinge"}

The center of mass $G$ is the midpoint of the rod, hence its distance from the hinge is
$$
d = \frac{\ell}{2} - \frac{\ell}{6} = \frac{\ell}{3} .
$$
The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical.

$$
I_O = \frac{m\ell^2}{12} + m\,d^2 = \frac{m\ell^2}{12} + \frac{m\ell^2}{9} = \frac{7\,m\ell^2}{36}
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,I_O\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
V(\vartheta) = -m g\,d\,\cos\vartheta = -\frac{m g \ell}{3}\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
V'(\vartheta) = \frac{m g \ell}{3}\sin\vartheta = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\vartheta = 0 ,
\quad
\vartheta = \pi
$$
$$
V''(0) = \frac{m g \ell}{3} > 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\text{stable equilibrium}
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{V''(0)}{I_O} = \frac{\dfrac{m g \ell}{3}}{\dfrac{7\,m\ell^2}{36}} = \frac{12\,g}{7\,\ell} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{7\,\ell}{12\,g}}
$$
$$
L = \frac{g}{\omega^2} = \frac{7\,\ell}{12}
$$

Check with the cardinal moment equation with respect to the fixed pole $O$:
$$
I_O\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -m g\,d\,\sin\vartheta
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{7\,m\ell^2}{36}\,\ddot{\vartheta} + \frac{m g \ell}{3}\,\vartheta = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates about the fixed axis through $O$, hence it has a single degree of freedom and the kinetic energy is that of a rotational state of motion, $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_O\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$.

The moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $O$ is obtained from the Huygens-Steiner theorem starting from the value $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$ relative to the axis through the center of mass, adding $m\,d^2$ with $d = \dfrac{\ell}{3}$.

The potential energy of the weight is $m g\,y_G$ with $y_G = -d\cos\vartheta$, therefore $V = -m g d \cos\vartheta$. The configuration $\vartheta = 0$, with the center of mass below the hinge, has $V'' > 0$ and is **stable**; the one with the center of mass above the hinge has $V'' < 0$ and is unstable.

The angular frequency of the small oscillations is the ratio between the stiffness $V''(0)$ and the inertia $I_O$, under a square root. Comparing $\omega^2$ with the angular frequency $\dfrac{g}{L}$ of the simple pendulum gives the **reduced length** $L = \dfrac{7\ell}{12}$, hence the rod oscillates like a simple pendulum of that length.

The check does not use the potential. The cardinal moment equation with respect to $O$ is written, a pole at which the reaction of the hinge has zero moment, and $\sin\vartheta \simeq \vartheta$ is substituted for small values of the angle. The equation obtained is that of a harmonic motion with the same angular frequency.

A check on the known case confirms the procedure, because if the hinge is at the end $A$ we obtain $d = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$, $I_O = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$ and $L = \dfrac{2\ell}{3}$, which is the reduced length of the compound pendulum formed by the rod hinged at one end.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects the center $C$ to the fixed point $D$, placed at the same height as $C$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Determine the angular frequency and the period of the small oscillations about the equilibrium configuration.

::struttura{nodi="D,0.3,1" cerchi="4,1,1" vincoli="D,incastro,180" linee="0,0 8,0;0.30,1.00 1.08,1.30 1.32,0.70 1.56,1.30 1.79,0.70 2.03,1.30 2.27,0.70 2.51,1.30 2.74,0.70 2.98,1.30 3.22,0.70 4.00,1.00" punti="4,1,C,baricentro;4,0,H,centro" quote="0.3 1,4 1,x" titolo="The rolling disk with the spring between the center $C$ and the fixed point $D$"}

Rolling without slipping ties the rotation to the translation of the center; the Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the abscissa $x$ of $C$ measured from $D$, so that the rotation angle $\varphi$ satisfies $x = R\,\varphi$.

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\dot{\varphi}^2
= \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{m R^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\dot{x}^2}{R^2}
= \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2
$$
$$
V(x) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 ,
\qquad
V'(x) = k\,x = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x_0 = 0
$$
$$
V''(x_0) = k > 0 ,
\qquad
a = \frac{3}{2}\,m
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{V''(x_0)}{a} = \frac{k}{\dfrac{3}{2}\,m} = \frac{2k}{3m} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{3m}{2k}}
$$

Check with the cardinal moment equation with respect to the contact point $H$, which is the instantaneous center of rotation:
$$
I_H\,\ddot{\varphi} = -k\,x\,R ,
\qquad
I_H = \frac{m R^2}{2} + m R^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,m R^2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} + k\,x = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The state of motion of the disk is rotational about the contact point, therefore the kinetic energy can be written either as the sum of the translation term of the center of mass and the rotation term about $G$, or directly as $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_H\,\dot{\varphi}^2$. The two forms give the same coefficient $a = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m$, because $I_H = I_C + m R^2$ by the Huygens-Steiner theorem.

The height of the center stays constant during the motion, hence the weight does not appear in the potential energy, which reduces to the elastic term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$. The equilibrium configuration is $x_0 = 0$, with the spring unloaded, and it is **stable** because $V'' = k > 0$.

The angular frequency turns out to be $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{2k}{3m}}$. The factor $\dfrac{3}{2}$ in the denominator is the additional inertia due to the rotation, since the same disk, if it slid without rotating, would oscillate with angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$, hence more rapidly.

The check is made with the cardinal moment equation with respect to $H$. The pole $H$ is moving, but its velocity is parallel to that of the center, therefore the term $\vec{v}_H \wedge \vec{Q}$ is zero and the moment equation has the same form it would have with a fixed pole. The weight, the normal reaction and the friction force have zero moment with respect to such a pole, because the first two have a line of action through $H$ and the third is applied at $H$. There remains the moment of the elastic force, with moment arm $R$, and the same equation of harmonic motion is obtained.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A uniform pulley of mass $M$ and radius $R$ rotates without friction about its fixed center $O$. An inextensible rope, of negligible mass, which does not slip, is wound on the groove of the pulley; on one side the rope supports a body of mass $m$ that moves along the vertical, on the other it is connected to an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ fixed to the ground. Determine the equilibrium configuration and the period of the small oscillations of the system in the figure.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,1.6,foro" nodi="O,0,0,-;E,-1.6,0,-;S,-1.6,-3.2,-;D,1.6,0,-;N,1.6,-2,-" punti="0,0,O,baricentro" catene="E-S;D-N" poligoni="-2.2,-3.2 -1,-3.2 -1,-4.2 -2.2,-4.2" linee="1.60,-2.00 1.90,-2.35 1.30,-2.48 1.90,-2.61 1.30,-2.74 1.90,-2.86 1.30,-2.99 1.90,-3.12 1.30,-3.25 1.60,-3.60;0.7,-3.6 2.5,-3.6" vincoli="O,incastro,0" etichette="-1.6,-3.75,m;2.4,-2.75,k;0.75,0.3,R;-0.15,-0.75,M" forze="-1.6 -5.7,0,-0.9,mg" tratteggi="0,0 1.6,0" titolo="The pulley with the body of mass $m$ hanging from the rope and the spring connected to the ground"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the downward displacement $x$ of the body measured from the configuration in which the spring is unloaded; the rope is inextensible and does not slip, hence the spring stretches by $x$ and the pulley rotates by the angle $\varphi = \dfrac{x}{R}$.

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I_O\,\dot{\varphi}^2
= \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{M R^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\dot{x}^2}{R^2}
= \frac{1}{2}\left( m + \frac{M}{2} \right)\dot{x}^2
$$
$$
V(x) = -m g\,x + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2
$$
$$
V'(x) = -m g + k\,x = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x_0 = \frac{m g}{k}
$$
$$
V''(x_0) = k > 0 ,
\qquad
a = m + \frac{M}{2} = \frac{2m + M}{2}
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{k}{\dfrac{2m + M}{2}} = \frac{2k}{2m + M} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2m + M}{2k}}
$$

Check with the cardinal equations written separately for the two bodies, with $\Phi$ the tension of the rope on the side of the body and $\Psi$ on the side of the spring:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{x} = m g - \Phi \\[4pt]
\dfrac{M R^2}{2}\,\ddot{\varphi} = \Phi\,R - \Psi\,R \\[4pt]
\Psi = k\,x
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\left( m + \frac{M}{2} \right)\ddot{x} = m g - k\,x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is formed by three parts constrained to one another: the body that translates, the pulley that rotates and the spring. The rope is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, therefore the downward displacement of the body, the elongation of the spring and the arc $R\varphi$ are equal, and a single degree of freedom remains.

The kinetic energy adds the translation term of the body and the rotation term of the pulley about its fixed axis, with $I_O = \dfrac{M R^2}{2}$. Substituting $\dot{\varphi} = \dfrac{\dot{x}}{R}$ gives the coefficient $a = m + \dfrac{M}{2}$, which does not depend on the radius.

The potential energy contains the term of the weight, $m g\,y = -m g\,x$ because the body descends when $x$ increases, and the elastic term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$. The equilibrium configuration is $x_0 = \dfrac{m g}{k}$, that is, the static elongation that the spring must have to support the weight, and it is **stable** because $V'' = k > 0$.

The angular frequency does not depend on the equilibrium position, since $V''$ is constant, and the system oscillates about $x_0$ with $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{2k}{2m+M}}$. For $M \to 0$ we recover $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ of the plain mass-spring system, whereas a heavy pulley slows the oscillations down.

The check writes the cardinal equations for each body. The first one is the vertical translation of the hanging body, the second one is the moment with respect to the fixed axis of the pulley, the third one expresses that the tension on the side of the spring equals the elastic force, since the spring is ideal. Substituting $\ddot{\varphi} = \dfrac{\ddot{x}}{R}$ and adding, the tensions cancel and the equation of motion is recovered, whose linear part about $x_0$ has the angular frequency computed above.

</div>
