## Exercise 1

A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to a smooth fixed circular guide of center $O$ and radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects $P$ to the highest point $A$ of the guide. Determine the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and discuss their stability as the spring constant varies.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" punti="1.29,1.53,P,pieno;0,0,O;0,2,A" linee="0.00,2.00 0.34,2.05 0.33,1.71 0.54,1.97 0.54,1.63 0.75,1.90 0.75,1.56 0.96,1.82 0.95,1.48 1.29,1.53" tratteggi="0,0 0,2;0,0 1.29,1.53" forze="1.29 1.53,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="0.3,1.05,ϑ;0.95,2.1,k" titolo="The particle $P$ on the circular guide, with the spring connecting it to the highest point $A$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the angle $\vartheta$ between the radius $OP$ and the upward vertical $OA$, so that
$$
P - O = R\,(\sin\vartheta,\,\cos\vartheta) ,
\qquad
\overline{PA}^{\,2} = 2R^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta) .
$$

$$
U(\vartheta) = -m g R \cos\vartheta - k R^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)
= \big( k R^2 - m g R \big)\cos\vartheta - k R^2
$$
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -R\,\big( k R - m g \big)\sin\vartheta = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\vartheta = 0 ,
\quad
\vartheta = \pi
$$
$$
U''(\vartheta) = -R\,( k R - m g )\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{k R}{m g}
$$
$$
U''(0) = m g R\,(1 - \lambda) ,
\qquad
U''(\pi) = m g R\,(\lambda - 1)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\lambda > 1 & \vartheta = 0 \ \text{stable} , \quad \vartheta = \pi \ \text{unstable} \\[4pt]
\lambda < 1 & \vartheta = 0 \ \text{unstable} , \quad \vartheta = \pi \ \text{stable} \\[4pt]
\lambda = 1 & U \ \text{constant, neutral equilibrium}
\end{cases}
$$

Check in the case $\lambda = 1$ with the direct computation of the resultant of the active forces:
$$
k\,(A - P) + m\vec{g} = k R\,\big( -\sin\vartheta,\ 1 - \cos\vartheta \big) - m g\,\hat{\jmath}
= -m g\,\big( \sin\vartheta,\ \cos\vartheta \big)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide leaves a single degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$, measured here from the upward vertical, identifies the configuration. The length of the spring is obtained from the law of cosines in the isosceles triangle $OPA$, of sides $R$ and vertex angle $\vartheta$.

The potential of the weight equals $-m g\,y_P = -m g R \cos\vartheta$ and that of the spring equals $-k R^2 (1 - \cos\vartheta)$. Adding them gives a potential of the form $c\,\cos\vartheta$ with $c = k R^2 - m g R$, and on the sign of $c$ depends which of the two vertical configurations is stable.

The equilibrium configurations are always the two vertical ones, $\vartheta = 0$ with the particle at the top and $\vartheta = \pi$ with the particle at the bottom. Here weight and spring act in opposite directions, because the weight tends to bring $P$ downward and the spring tends to bring it to $A$, which is at the top. The dimensionless parameter $\lambda = \dfrac{k R}{m g}$ compares the two actions.

For $\lambda > 1$ the spring prevails and the **stable** configuration is the upper one; for $\lambda < 1$ the weight prevails and the stable configuration is the lower one. On passing through $\lambda = 1$ the two configurations exchange their roles, and this is an exchange of stability.

The value $\lambda = 1$ deserves attention, because the potential is constant and every configuration is an equilibrium configuration. The check gives the reason for this, because when $k R = m g$ the resultant of the active forces equals $-m g\,(\sin\vartheta, \cos\vartheta)$, that is, it is directed along the radius $OP$ toward the center. The reaction of the smooth guide is radial as well, therefore it can balance it in every configuration. The equilibrium is then neutral.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to the smooth fixed guide of equation $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, with $a > 0$, contained in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects $P$ to the fixed point $D = (0, h)$ of the $y$-axis, with $h > a$. Determine the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and discuss their stability as $k$ varies.

::grafico{f="x^2/2" legenda=0 xmin=-3.2 xmax=3.2 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3.6 punti="1.6,1.28,pieno,P;0,2.6,pieno,D" segmenti="1.6,1.28,0,2.6" etichette="0.75,2.2,spring k" titolo="The particle $P$ on the guide $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, connected by the spring to the fixed point $D$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the abscissa $x$ of $P$, and we set
$$
c = k\,(h - a) - m g .
$$

$$
U(x) = -\frac{m g}{2a}\,x^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,k\left[ x^2 + \left( \frac{x^2}{2a} - h \right)^{2} \right]
$$
$$
U'(x) = -\frac{x}{2a^2}\,\big( k\,x^2 - 2 a c \big)
$$
$$
x = 0 ,
\qquad
x_{\pm} = \pm\sqrt{\frac{2 a c}{k}} = \pm\sqrt{2 a (h - a)\,(1 - \lambda)} ,
\qquad
\lambda = \frac{m g}{k\,(h - a)}
$$
$$
U''(x) = \frac{c}{a} - \frac{3k}{2a^2}\,x^2
$$
$$
U''(0) = \frac{c}{a} ,
\qquad
U''(x_{\pm}) = \frac{c}{a} - \frac{3k}{2a^2}\cdot\frac{2ac}{k} = -\,\frac{2c}{a}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\lambda > 1 & x = 0 \ \text{only configuration, stable} \\[4pt]
\lambda < 1 & x = 0 \ \text{unstable}, \quad x_{\pm} \ \text{stable}
\end{cases}
$$

::grafico{f="0" curve="1-t^2,t,-1.35,1.35" xmin=-0.1 xmax=2.4 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 nomi="λ,x" segmenti="1,-1.45,1,1.45" etichette="1.75,0.18,x = 0;0.12,1.25,branch x > 0;0.12,-1.3,branch x < 0;1.07,-1.2,λ = 1" titolo="The equilibrium configurations as $\lambda$ varies: at $\lambda = 1$ the two branches $x_{\pm}$ detach from the configuration $x = 0$"}

Check with the vanishing of the tangential component of the active forces. A vector tangent to the guide is $\vec{t} = \left( 1,\ \dfrac{x}{a} \right)$ and the elastic force is $k\,(D - P) = k\left( -x,\ h - \dfrac{x^2}{2a} \right)$, hence
$$
\big( 0,\,-m g \big) \cdot \vec{t} + k\,(D - P) \cdot \vec{t}
= \frac{x}{a}\left( c - \frac{k}{2a}\,x^2 \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential is written by adding the term of the weight, $-m g\,y$ with $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, and the elastic term $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\overline{PD}^{\,2}$, where $\overline{PD}^{\,2} = x^2 + \left( \dfrac{x^2}{2a} - h \right)^2$. The natural length is zero, hence there is no need to take the square root.

Differentiating and factoring out $x$ gives the product $-\dfrac{x}{2a^2}\,(k x^2 - 2 a c)$, which vanishes for $x = 0$ and, when $c > 0$, for the two opposite values $x_{\pm}$. The number of the equilibrium configurations therefore depends on the sign of $c = k(h-a) - m g$, that is, on the comparison between the action of the spring and that of the weight, measured by the dimensionless parameter $\lambda$.

For $\lambda > 1$ the weight prevails, the only configuration is the vertex of the parabola and the second derivative $U''(0) = \dfrac{c}{a}$ is negative, hence the equilibrium is **stable**. For $\lambda < 1$ the spring prevails, the vertex becomes **unstable** and the two symmetric configurations $x_{\pm}$ appear, in which $U'' = -\dfrac{2c}{a} < 0$ and the equilibrium is stable.

The graph shows the equilibrium configurations as a function of $\lambda$. At the bifurcation value $\lambda = 1$ the two lateral branches are born from the branch $x = 0$, which at that point loses stability; the configurations go from one to three.

The check does not use the potential, because the reaction of the smooth guide is normal to the curve and therefore at equilibrium the tangential component of the active forces must vanish. Computing the scalar product with the tangent vector reproduces the same factor $c - \dfrac{k}{2a}\,x^2$ and the same configurations.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects the end $B$ to the fixed point $D$, placed on the vertical at distance $\ell$ above $A$. Discuss the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and their stability as $k$ varies, and compute the angular frequency of the small oscillations about the stable configuration.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,2.6;B,2.25,1.3;D,0,5.2" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,180" linee="0.00,5.20 0.69,4.50 0.40,4.00 0.98,4.00 0.69,3.50 1.27,3.50 0.98,3.00 1.56,3.00 1.27,2.50 1.85,2.50 1.56,2.00 2.25,1.30" tratteggi="0,2.6 0,0.9" punti="1.125,1.95,G,baricentro" forze="1.125 1.95,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="0.45,1.85,ϑ;2.1,3.4,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$ with the spring between $B$ and the fixed point $D$ placed above $A$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical. From the law of cosines in the triangle $ABD$, which has the sides $AB$ and $AD$ equal to $\ell$ and the included angle $\pi - \vartheta$, it follows that $\overline{BD}^{\,2} = 2\ell^2\,(1 + \cos\vartheta)$.

$$
U(\vartheta) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - k\,\ell^2\,(1 + \cos\vartheta)
= \left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2 \right)\cos\vartheta - k\,\ell^2
$$
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2 \right)\sin\vartheta = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\vartheta = 0 ,
\quad
\vartheta = \pi
$$
$$
\mu = \frac{2 k \ell}{m g} ,
\qquad
\frac{m g \ell}{2} - k \ell^2 = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\,(1 - \mu)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\mu < 1 & \vartheta = 0 \ \text{stable} , \quad \vartheta = \pi \ \text{unstable} \\[4pt]
\mu > 1 & \vartheta = 0 \ \text{unstable} , \quad \vartheta = \pi \ \text{stable} \\[4pt]
\mu = 1 & U \ \text{constant: neutral equilibrium}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{m \ell^2}{3}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
V'' = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\,|1 - \mu| = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\big| m g - 2 k \ell \big|
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{V''}{\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}} = \frac{3\,\big| m g - 2 k \ell \big|}{2\,m\,\ell}
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to the pole $A$. The elastic force applied at $B$ equals $k\,(D - B) = k\ell\,(-\sin\vartheta,\ 1 + \cos\vartheta)$, hence
$$
M_A = \big( k\,\ell^2 - \frac{m g \ell}{2} \big)\sin\vartheta = U'(\vartheta)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential is built as in the previous cases. The elastic term requires the distance $\overline{BD}$: since $D$ is above $A$ and $B$ below, the angle at $A$ of the triangle $ABD$ is $\pi - \vartheta$, and the law of cosines gives $\overline{BD}^{\,2} = 2\ell^2(1 + \cos\vartheta)$.

Weight and spring act in opposite directions: the weight keeps the rod downward, the spring pulls it upward, that is, toward $D$. The parameter $\mu = \dfrac{2k\ell}{m g}$ compares the two actions and the coefficient of $\cos\vartheta$ in the potential changes sign at $\mu = 1$.

For a weak spring, $\mu < 1$, the **stable** configuration is the one with the rod downward; for a stiff spring, $\mu > 1$, the stable configuration becomes the one with the rod upward, which the spring manages to support. In the case $\mu = 1$ the potential is constant and every configuration is an equilibrium configuration.

For the small oscillations one uses the kinetic energy of the rod rotating about $A$, with $I_A = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$, and the second derivative of the potential energy in the stable configuration, which in both cases equals $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,|m g - 2 k \ell|$. The angular frequency vanishes as $\mu \to 1$: on approaching the bifurcation value the potential well flattens and the period of the oscillations grows beyond any bound.

The check with the cardinal moment equation with respect to $A$ gives the same result. The moment of the elastic force equals $k\ell^2 \sin\vartheta$, that of the weight equals $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$, and their sum coincides with $U'(\vartheta)$, as it must be, since the resultant moment with respect to $A$ is the Lagrangian component of the force system.

</div>
