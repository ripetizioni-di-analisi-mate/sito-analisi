## Exercise 1

A uniform disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide. The center $C$ is connected to the left wall by an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length, and to the right wall by a viscous damper that exerts on $C$ the force $-h\,\dot{x}\,\hat{\imath}$, with $h > 0$. Write the equation of the small oscillations of the system in the figure, determine the critical value of $h$ and the pseudo angular frequency in the oscillatory case.

::struttura{nodi="D,0.3,1,-;E,7.8,1,-" cerchi="4,1,1" vincoli="D,incastro,180;E,incastro,0" linee="0,0 8,0;0.30,1.00 0.89,1.30 1.11,0.70 1.33,1.30 1.54,0.70 1.76,1.30 1.97,0.70 2.19,1.30 2.41,0.70 3.00,1.00;3,1 4,1;5,1 6.6,1;6.6,0.75 7.5,0.75 7.5,1.25 6.6,1.25;6.6,0.78 6.6,1.22;7.5,1 7.8,1" punti="4,1,C,baricentro;4,0,H,centro" etichette="1.65,1.65,k;7,1.6,h" quote="0.3 1,4 1,x" titolo="The rolling disk, with the spring on the left and the viscous damper on the right"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the abscissa $x$ of the center $C$, measured from the configuration in which the spring is unloaded.

$$
T = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 ,
\qquad
V = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 ,
\qquad
Q_x = -h\,\dot{x}
$$
$$
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} + h\,\dot{x} + k\,x = 0
\qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{x} + 2\beta\,\dot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = 0
$$
$$
\beta = \frac{h}{3m} ,
\qquad
\omega_0^2 = \frac{2k}{3m}
$$
$$
\beta = \omega_0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{h^2}{9m^2} = \frac{2k}{3m}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
h_c = \sqrt{6\,k\,m}
$$
$$
h < h_c
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x(t) = C\,e^{-\beta t}\cos(\omega t + \varphi) ,
\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{3m} - \frac{h^2}{9m^2}}
$$

Check with the kinetic energy theorem, according to which the derivative of the mechanical energy is the power of the nonconservative forces:
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 \right) = -h\,\dot{x}^2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{x}\left( \frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} + k\,x + h\,\dot{x} \right) = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The kinetic energy of the disk rolling without slipping equals $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2$, as for the disk with the spring alone: the term $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m$ that appears in the equation of motion is the total inertia of translation and rotation.

The force of the damper is not conservative, therefore it does not enter the potential, and its Lagrangian component is computed from the virtual work, $\delta L = -h\,\dot{x}\,\delta x$, whence $Q_x = -h\,\dot{x}$. The Lagrange equation then gives the linear second-order equation with constant coefficients written above.

Dividing by $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m$ one recognizes the damping coefficient $\beta$ and the natural angular frequency $\omega_0$ of the undamped system. The comparison between the two numbers decides the type of motion, and the critical value is obtained by imposing $\beta = \omega_0$.

The **critical damping** equals $h_c = \sqrt{6 k m}$. For $h < h_c$ the motion is oscillatory with amplitude that decreases like $e^{-\beta t}$, for $h \ge h_c$ the disk returns to the equilibrium configuration without passing beyond it. In every case the configuration $x = 0$ is asymptotically stable, because the viscous friction removes energy.

The check uses the kinetic energy theorem instead of the Lagrange equations. The power of the viscous force equals $-h\,\dot{x}^2$ and that of the elastic force is already contained in the derivative of the potential energy; carrying out the derivative and factoring out $\dot{x}$ one recovers the same equation of motion, since the equality holds at every instant and $\dot{x}$ is not identically zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and oscillates in a vertical plane. A viscous damper that exerts the force $-h\,\vec{v}_B$, with $h > 0$, is connected to the end $B$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Write the equation of the small oscillations about the vertical configuration and determine the critical value of $h$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3.4;B,1.16,0.21" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" linee="1.16,0.21 2.3,0.21;2.3,-0.04 3.1,-0.04 3.1,0.46 2.3,0.46;2.3,-0.01 2.3,0.43;3.1,0.21 3.4,0.21;3.4,-0.15 3.4,0.57" tratteggi="0,3.4 0,0.4" punti="0.58,1.8,G,baricentro" forze="0.58 1.8,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="0.3,2,ϑ;2.7,0.85,h" titolo="The compound pendulum with the viscous damper applied at the end $B$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical. For small values of the angle the velocity of $B$ is horizontal and equals $\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}$, hence the moment of the viscous force with respect to $A$ equals $-h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}$.

$$
I_A = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} ,
\qquad
V''(0) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{m\ell^2}{3}\,\ddot{\vartheta} + h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta} + \frac{m g \ell}{2}\,\vartheta = 0
\qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} + \frac{3h}{m}\,\dot{\vartheta} + \frac{3g}{2\ell}\,\vartheta = 0
$$
$$
\beta = \frac{3h}{2m} ,
\qquad
\omega_0^2 = \frac{3g}{2\ell}
$$
$$
\beta = \omega_0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{9h^2}{4m^2} = \frac{3g}{2\ell}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
h_c = m\sqrt{\frac{2g}{3\ell}}
$$

Check with the Lagrange equations. From the virtual work of the viscous force, $\delta L = -h\,\ell\dot{\vartheta}\cdot\ell\,\delta\vartheta$, it follows that $Q_\vartheta = -h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}$, hence
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} - \frac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta}
= \frac{m\ell^2}{3}\,\ddot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} + Q_\vartheta = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta - h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates about the fixed axis through $A$, hence the equation of motion is written with the angular momentum with respect to $A$ and the resultant moment with respect to the same pole. The weight contributes with $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$, which for small angles equals $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\vartheta$.

The force of the damper is proportional to the velocity of $B$ and opposite to it. For small oscillations $\vec{v}_B$ is horizontal with magnitude $\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}$, hence the force equals $-h\,\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}$ and its moment with respect to $A$ has moment arm $\ell$: the contribution is $-h\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}$.

Dividing by $I_A$ gives the equation in canonical form, with $\beta = \dfrac{3h}{2m}$ and $\omega_0^2 = \dfrac{3g}{2\ell}$. Note that $\beta$ does not depend on the length of the rod, whereas $\omega_0$ does.

The critical value $h_c = m\sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{3\ell}}$ separates the damped oscillatory motion, for $h < h_c$, from the return without oscillations, for $h \ge h_c$. A longer rod has a smaller $\omega_0$, therefore a weaker damper is enough to prevent it from oscillating.

The check repeats the computation with the Lagrange equations instead of the cardinal moment equation. The kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_A\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, hence the left-hand side equals $I_A\,\ddot{\vartheta}$; the right-hand side adds the Lagrangian component of the conservative forces and that of the viscous force. The equation obtained coincides with the one written above.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point particle of mass $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides without friction on a horizontal guide, connected to a spring of stiffness $k = 50\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and to a viscous damper of constant $h = 4\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Determine the critical damping, the pseudo angular frequency, the pseudo-period, the ratio between two successive maximum amplitudes and the time needed for the amplitude to reduce to one tenth of the initial one.

::struttura{nodi="D,0.4,1,-;E,5.6,1,-" poligoni="2.4,0.5 3.4,0.5 3.4,1.5 2.4,1.5" vincoli="D,incastro,180;E,incastro,0" linee="0,0.5 6,0.5;0.40,1.00 0.84,1.30 1.00,0.70 1.16,1.30 1.32,0.70 1.48,1.30 1.64,0.70 1.80,1.30 1.96,0.70 2.40,1.00;3.4,1 4.3,1;4.3,0.72 5.2,0.72 5.2,1.28 4.3,1.28;4.3,0.75 4.3,1.25;5.2,1 5.6,1" etichette="1.4,1.65,k;4.75,1.65,h;2.9,1,m" frecce="2.9,1.9,3.7,1.9,x" titolo="The point particle with the spring and the viscous damper"}

$$
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{50}{2}} = 5\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\beta = \frac{h}{2m} = \frac{4}{4} = 1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}
$$
$$
h_c = 2\,m\,\omega_0 = 2\sqrt{k\,m} = 2\sqrt{100} = 20\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \beta^2} = \sqrt{25 - 1} = 2\sqrt{6} \simeq 4.90\,\mathrm{rad/s}
$$
$$
\mathcal{T} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{2\sqrt{6}} \simeq 1.28\,\mathrm{s}
$$
$$
\delta = \beta\,\mathcal{T} \simeq 1 \cdot 1.28 = 1.28 ,
\qquad
\frac{x_n}{x_{n+1}} = e^{\delta} \simeq 3.61
$$
$$
e^{-\beta t} = \frac{1}{10}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
t = \frac{\ln 10}{\beta} \simeq 2.30\,\mathrm{s}
$$

::grafico{f="exp(-x)*cos(4.899*x);exp(-x)" xmin=0 xmax=4 ymin=-1.1 ymax=1.1 punti="0,1,pieno;1.283,0.277,pieno;2.566,0.077,pieno" nomi="t,x" titolo="The motion $x(t) = e^{-\beta t}\cos\omega t$ with the first three maxima, in the ratio $e^{\delta} \simeq 3.61$"}

Check of the ratio of the amplitudes with the computed values:
$$
x(0) = 1 ,
\qquad
x(\mathcal{T}) = e^{-\beta \mathcal{T}} = e^{-1.28} \simeq 0.277 ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{0.277} \simeq 3.61
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation of motion is $m\,\ddot{x} + h\,\dot{x} + k\,x = 0$, which in canonical form has $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ and $\beta = \dfrac{h}{2m}$. With the assigned data we obtain $\beta = 1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, much smaller than $\omega_0 = 5\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, hence the motion is damped oscillatory.

The critical damping is obtained from $\beta = \omega_0$, that is, $h_c = 2\,m\,\omega_0 = 2\sqrt{k m} = 20\,\mathrm{N\,s/m}$. The assigned value is one fifth of the critical one, therefore the damping is weak and the pseudo angular frequency $\omega = 2\sqrt{6}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ differs little from $\omega_0$.

The pseudo-period is the interval between two successive passages through the same phase and equals $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} \simeq 1.28\,\mathrm{s}$. The **logarithmic decrement** $\delta = \beta\,\mathcal{T}$ is the logarithm of the ratio between two successive maxima, because in one pseudo-period the factor $e^{-\beta t}$ is reduced by $e^{-\delta}$; here each oscillation has amplitude equal to about one third of the previous one.

The time needed for the amplitude to reduce to one tenth is obtained from the equation $e^{-\beta t} = \dfrac{1}{10}$ and equals about $2.30\,\mathrm{s}$, that is, a little less than two pseudo-periods.

The check compares the ratio $e^{\delta}$ with the direct computation of the envelope at the two instants $t = 0$ and $t = \mathcal{T}$, which gives the same value. The graph shows the first three maxima, whose ordinates are in the predicted ratio.

</div>
