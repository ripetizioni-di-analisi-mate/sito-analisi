## Exercise 1

A collar $P$ of mass $m$ slides without friction along a fixed vertical straight guide. An ideal spring of spring constant $k$ and natural length $\ell_0$ connects $P$ to the fixed point $O$ of the guide, as the diagram in the figure shows. The system moves in a vertical plane. Determine the equilibrium configuration and study its stability.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,4,-;P,0,1.4,-" vincoli="O,incastro,90" linee="0,4 0.3,3.65 -0.3,3.25 0.3,2.85 -0.3,2.45 0.3,2.05 0,1.75 0,1.4" tratteggi="0,3.9 0,0.5" punti="0,4,,pieno;0,1.4,,pieno" forze="P,0,-0.9,mg" etichette="-0.45,3.9,O;0.2,1.25,P;0.62,2.85,k" quote="O,P,s" titolo="The collar $P$ on the vertical guide, with the spring connecting it to the fixed point $O$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the distance $s > 0$ of $P$ from $O$, measured along the guide downward, so that the height of $P$ with respect to $O$ is $y_P = -s$ and the length of the spring is $s$.

$$
U(s) = -m g\,y_P - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(s - \ell_0)^2 = m g s - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(s - \ell_0)^2
$$
$$
U'(s) = m g - k\,(s - \ell_0) = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
s_0 = \ell_0 + \frac{m g}{k}
$$
$$
U''(s) = -k < 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
V''(s_0) = k > 0
$$

Check with the cardinal equation of statics for a point particle:
$$
\vec{\Phi} + m\vec{g} + \vec{F}_{\text{el}} = \vec{0}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
-m g + k\,(s_0 - \ell_0) = -m g + k \cdot \frac{m g}{k} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has a single degree of freedom, since the collar is constrained to the guide, and the coordinate $s$ identifies the configuration. The guide is smooth and bilateral, hence the constraint is ideal and one can work with the potential.

The potential of the weight is $U = -m g\,y_P$ with the convention $\vec{F} = \operatorname{grad} U$; since $y_P = -s$, we obtain $U = m g s$, which increases with $s$ because the weight tends to make the collar descend. The potential of the spring is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,(s - \ell_0)^2$, since the length of the spring coincides with $s$.

The equilibrium configurations are the stationary points of the potential, therefore $U'(s)$ is set equal to zero. The only solution is $s_0 = \ell_0 + \dfrac{m g}{k}$, that is, the configuration in which the spring is stretched by $\dfrac{m g}{k}$ with respect to the natural length. Such an elongation is the one that produces an elastic force of magnitude $m g$, equal to the weight.

The second derivative of the potential equals $-k$ in every configuration, hence $V'' = k > 0$ and by the second derivative criterion the equilibrium is **stable**.

The check does not use the potential, since it directly imposes that the sum of the forces applied to the collar be zero. The reaction of the guide is horizontal, because the guide is smooth, hence the vertical component of the equation contains only the weight and the elastic force, and it returns the same configuration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A uniform disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length connects the center $C$ to the fixed point $D$, placed at the same height as $C$; moreover, the constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, is applied at the center of the disk. Determine the equilibrium configuration of the system in the figure.

::struttura{nodi="D,0.3,1" cerchi="4,1,1" vincoli="D,incastro,180" linee="0,0 8,0;0.3,1 0.75,1.3 1.3,0.7 1.85,1.3 2.4,0.7 2.95,1.3 3.3,1 4,1" punti="4,1,C,baricentro;4,0,H,centro" forze="5.7 1,0.8,0,F" etichette="2,1.65,k" quote="0.3 1,4 1,x" titolo="The rolling disk, with the spring between $C$ and $D$ and the force $\vec{F}$ applied at $C$"}

Rolling without slipping leaves a single degree of freedom; the Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the abscissa $x$ of the center $C$ measured from $D$, which is also the length of the spring.

$$
U(x) = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 + F\,x
$$
$$
U'(x) = -k\,x + F = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
x_0 = \frac{F}{k}
$$
$$
U''(x) = -k < 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
V''(x_0) = k > 0
$$

Check with the cardinal equations of statics, taking the contact point $H$ as the pole:
$$
M_H = -\big( F - k\,x_0 \big)\,R = -\left( F - k \cdot \frac{F}{k} \right) R = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk rolls without slipping, hence the rotation is tied to the translation of the center and a single degree of freedom remains. The height of $C$ is constant and equals $R$, therefore the weight has constant potential and does not appear in the equilibrium condition.

The potential of the spring is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$, because the natural length is zero and the length of the spring is $x$. The constant force $\vec F = F\,\hat\imath$ admits the potential $U = F\,x$, since its Lagrangian component is $Q_x = F$ and $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(F x) = F$.

Setting $U'(x)$ equal to zero gives $x_0 = \dfrac{F}{k}$, that is, the configuration in which the spring pulls the center back with a force equal and opposite to $\vec{F}$. Since $V'' = k > 0$, the equilibrium is **stable**.

In the check the moment equation with respect to the contact point $H$ is used. The weight and the normal reaction have a vertical line of action through $H$, hence zero moment; the friction force is applied at $H$ and has zero moment as well. There remain the force $\vec F$ and the elastic force, both horizontal and applied at $C$, with moment arm $R$ with respect to $H$. The vanishing of their resultant moment gives the same configuration.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The uniform rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the fixed end $A$ and moves in a vertical plane. The constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, horizontal and of assigned magnitude $F > 0$, is applied at the end $B$. Determine the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and study their stability.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3.4;B,1.7,0.44" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,3.4 0,0.6" punti="0.85,1.92,G,baricentro" forze="0.85 1.92,0,-0.9,mg;1.7 0.44,0.9,0,F" etichette="0.35,2.4,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$ with the horizontal force $\vec{F}$ applied at $B$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate is taken to be the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical, positive counterclockwise, so that
$$
B - A = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta,\,-\cos\vartheta) ,
\qquad
G - A = \frac{\ell}{2}\,(\sin\vartheta,\,-\cos\vartheta) .
$$

$$
U(\vartheta) = -m g\,y_G + F\,x_B = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + F\,\ell\,\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta + F\,\ell\,\cos\vartheta = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\tan\vartheta = \frac{2F}{m g}
$$
$$
\vartheta_1 = \arctan\frac{2F}{m g} ,
\qquad
\vartheta_2 = \vartheta_1 + \pi
$$
$$
U''(\vartheta) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - F\,\ell\,\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
U''(\vartheta_1) = -\frac{\ell}{2}\,\sqrt{m^2 g^2 + 4F^2} < 0 ,
\qquad
U''(\vartheta_2) = +\frac{\ell}{2}\,\sqrt{m^2 g^2 + 4F^2} > 0
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to the pole $A$:
$$
M_A = (G - A) \wedge (-m g\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot \hat{k} + (B - A) \wedge (F\,\hat{\imath}) \cdot \hat{k}
= -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta + F \ell \cos\vartheta
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates about the fixed hinge $A$, hence it has a single degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ identifies its configuration. The hinge is smooth and bilateral, therefore the constraint is ideal.

The potential of the weight is $-m g\,y_G$ with $y_G = -\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, hence it equals $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$. The constant force $\vec F = F\,\hat\imath$ admits the potential $U = F\,x_B$, because its gradient with respect to the position of $B$ is $\vec F$; substituting $x_B = \ell \sin\vartheta$ gives the second summand.

The equation $U'(\vartheta) = 0$ is written as $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta = F \ell \cos\vartheta$. The solutions with $\cos\vartheta = 0$ are excluded, since for $\cos\vartheta = 0$ the left-hand side is not zero, hence one can divide by $\cos\vartheta$ and obtain $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2F}{m g}$. In the interval $[0, 2\pi)$ the equation has the two solutions $\vartheta_1$ and $\vartheta_2 = \vartheta_1 + \pi$, which correspond to the two opposite configurations on the same line.

To evaluate $U''$ in the two configurations one uses $\cos\vartheta_1 = \dfrac{m g}{\sqrt{m^2g^2 + 4F^2}}$ and $\sin\vartheta_1 = \dfrac{2F}{\sqrt{m^2g^2 + 4F^2}}$, which follow from $\tan\vartheta_1 = \dfrac{2F}{mg}$ with $\vartheta_1$ in the first quadrant. We obtain $U''(\vartheta_1) < 0$, therefore $V''(\vartheta_1) > 0$ and the configuration $\vartheta_1$ is **stable**; in the opposite configuration sine and cosine change sign, hence $U''(\vartheta_2) > 0$ and the equilibrium is **unstable**.

The stable configuration is the one in which the rod hangs on the side toward which the force pushes. For $F \to 0$ the angle $\vartheta_1$ tends to zero and the rod vertical and downward is recovered.

The check uses the cardinal moment equation with respect to $A$, a pole at which the reaction of the hinge has zero moment. The moment of the weight equals $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$, that of the force $\vec F$ equals $F\ell\cos\vartheta$, and their sum coincides with $U'(\vartheta)$, hence the two routes give the same equilibrium equation.

</div>
