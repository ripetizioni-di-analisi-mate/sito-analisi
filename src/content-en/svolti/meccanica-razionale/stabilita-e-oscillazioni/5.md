## Exercise 1

A point particle $P$ of mass $m$ moves without constraints in a vertical plane, subject to the weight and to an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and zero natural length that connects it to the fixed point $O$. Determine the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and study their stability.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0" punti="1.2,-2,P,pieno" linee="0.00,0.00 0.44,-0.31 0.14,-0.67 0.60,-0.57 0.30,-0.92 0.75,-0.82 0.45,-1.18 0.90,-1.08 0.60,-1.43 1.06,-1.33 0.76,-1.69 1.20,-2.00" vincoli="O,incastro,90" tratteggi="0,-2 1.2,-2;1.2,0 1.2,-2" forze="1.2 -2,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="1.1,-0.75,k;0.6,-2.3,x;1.5,-1,y" titolo="The particle $P$ in the vertical plane, connected to $O$ by the spring"}

The particle is free in the plane, hence it has two degrees of freedom; the components $x$ and $y$ of the vector $P - O$ are taken as Lagrangian coordinates.

$$
V(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\big( x^2 + y^2 \big) + m g\,y
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial x} = k\,x = 0 \\[10pt]
\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial y} = k\,y + m g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
P_0 = \left( 0,\ -\frac{m g}{k} \right)
$$
$$
\mathbb{V} =
\begin{bmatrix}
k & 0 \\
0 & k
\end{bmatrix} ,
\qquad
\det \mathbb{V} = k^2 > 0 ,
\qquad
V_{11} = k > 0
$$

Check with the cardinal equation of statics for a point particle:
$$
k\,(O - P) + m\vec{g} = \vec{0}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\begin{cases}
-k\,x = 0 \\[4pt]
-k\,y - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The particle is not constrained, therefore its position in the plane requires two coordinates and the system has two degrees of freedom. The potential energy adds the elastic term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\overline{OP}^{\,2}$ and the term of the weight $m g\,y$.

The gradient of $V$ vanishes at a single point, which lies on the vertical through $O$ at distance $\dfrac{m g}{k}$ below it: it is the static elongation of the spring, the one that produces an elastic force of magnitude $m g$.

The Hessian matrix is $k$ times the identity matrix, hence it is positive definite, since the determinant and the entry in position $(1,1)$ are positive. By the Hessian criterion the configuration is a **strict minimum** of the potential energy, therefore the equilibrium is **stable**.

The two eigenvalues of the Hessian coincide. This says that the potential well has the same curvature in all directions: the particle, displaced slightly in any direction, oscillates with the same angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$, and every direction is the direction of a normal mode.

The check writes the equilibrium of the particle as the vanishing of the resultant of the active forces, since the particle is free. The two components of the vector equation return the same configuration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two uniform rods form a double pendulum in a vertical plane: the rod $OA$, of mass $2m$ and length $2\ell$, is hinged at the fixed point $O$; the rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the end $A$ of the first one. The hinges are smooth. Determine all the equilibrium configurations of the system in the figure and study their stability.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,4.4;A,1.37,1.9;B,2.71,1.42" aste="O-A;A-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" cerniere="A" tratteggi="0,4.4 0,2;1.37,1.9 1.37,0.5" punti="0.685,3.15,G_1,baricentro;2.04,1.66,G_2,baricentro" etichette="0.3,3.3,ϑ_1;1.65,1.1,ϑ_2" titolo="Double pendulum formed by the rod $OA$ of mass $2m$ and by the rod $AB$ of mass $m$"}

The angles $\vartheta_1$ and $\vartheta_2$ that the two rods form with the downward vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. The centers of mass have heights
$$
y_{G_1} = -\ell\cos\vartheta_1 ,
\qquad
y_{G_2} = -2\ell\cos\vartheta_1 - \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta_2 .
$$

$$
V = 2 m g\,y_{G_1} + m g\,y_{G_2}
= -4\,m g \ell \cos\vartheta_1 - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta_2
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial \vartheta_1} = 4\,m g \ell \sin\vartheta_1 = 0 \\[10pt]
\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial \vartheta_2} = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta_2 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\vartheta_1 \in \{0, \pi\} ,
\quad
\vartheta_2 \in \{0, \pi\}
$$
$$
\mathbb{V} =
\begin{bmatrix}
4\,m g \ell \cos\vartheta_1 & 0 \\[4pt]
0 & \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta_2
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
(0, 0) & \text{eigenvalues } 4 m g \ell \ \text{and} \ \dfrac{m g \ell}{2} : \ \text{stable} \\[8pt]
(0, \pi), \ (\pi, 0), \ (\pi, \pi) & \text{one negative eigenvalue} : \ \text{unstable}
\end{cases}
$$

Check with the cardinal equations of statics. For the rod $AB$ alone, with the pole at the internal hinge $A$, and for the whole system, with the pole at $O$:
$$
\begin{cases}
-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta_2 = 0 \\[10pt]
-4\,m g \ell \sin\vartheta_1 - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta_2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has two degrees of freedom, since each rod can rotate about its own hinge. The hinges are smooth and bilateral, hence the constraints are ideal.

The center of mass of the first rod lies at distance $\ell$ from $O$, since the rod has length $2\ell$. The center of mass of the second one is found by adding the position of $A$, which depends on $\vartheta_1$, and the vector $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ along the second rod, which depends on $\vartheta_2$.

The potential energy is the sum of a term that depends only on $\vartheta_1$ and of a term that depends only on $\vartheta_2$, therefore the Hessian is diagonal and its entries are the eigenvalues. The equilibrium configurations are the four combinations of rod downward and rod upward.

The only configuration with both eigenvalues positive is $(0,0)$, with the two rods vertical and downward, and by the Hessian criterion the equilibrium is **stable**. In each of the other three at least one eigenvalue is negative, hence the equilibrium is **unstable**: it is enough that one of the two rods be upward.

The check proceeds by taking the system apart. First the moment equation is written for the rod $AB$ alone with respect to the internal hinge $A$, at which the reaction exchanged between the two rods has zero moment: only the moment of the weight of $AB$ remains, which vanishes for $\sin\vartheta_2 = 0$. Then the moment equation is written for the whole system with respect to $O$, which contains the weights of both rods. The two equations coincide, up to sign, with the partial derivatives of the potential energy.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two equal uniform rods, $O_1A$ and $O_2B$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, are hinged to the ground at the fixed points $O_1$ and $O_2$, at distance $d$, and rotate in a vertical plane. The upper ends $A$ and $B$ are connected by an ideal spring of stiffness $k$ and natural length $d$. Study the stability of the configuration with both rods vertical, shown in the figure.

::struttura{nodi="O_1,0.52,0;A,0.52,3.95;O_2,3.48,0;B,3.48,3.95" aste="O_1-A;O_2-B" vincoli="O_1,cerniera;O_2,cerniera" linee="0.52,3.95 1.15,4.25 1.34,3.65 1.53,4.25 1.72,3.65 1.91,4.25 2.09,3.65 2.28,4.25 2.47,3.65 2.66,4.25 2.85,3.65 3.48,3.95;0,0 4,0" punti="0.52,1.97,G_1,baricentro;3.48,1.97,G_2,baricentro" quote="O_1,O_2,d" etichette="2,4.6,k" titolo="The two vertical rods connected at the upper ends by the spring of natural length $d$"}

The angles $\vartheta_1$ and $\vartheta_2$ that the two rods form with the upward vertical are taken as Lagrangian coordinates. With $O_1$ at the origin,
$$
A - O_1 = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta_1,\ \cos\vartheta_1) ,
\qquad
B - O_1 = \big( d + \ell\sin\vartheta_2,\ \ell\cos\vartheta_2 \big) .
$$

$$
V = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\,\big( \cos\vartheta_1 + \cos\vartheta_2 \big) + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\big( L - d \big)^2 ,
\qquad
L = \overline{AB}
$$

In the configuration $(0,0)$ we have $L = d$, hence the gradient of the elastic term is zero and the gradient of $V$ is zero: the configuration is an equilibrium configuration. For small values of the angles
$$
L - d \simeq \ell\,\big( \sin\vartheta_2 - \sin\vartheta_1 \big) \simeq \ell\,\big( \vartheta_2 - \vartheta_1 \big)
$$
$$
V \simeq \text{constant} - \frac{m g \ell}{4}\,\big( \vartheta_1^2 + \vartheta_2^2 \big) + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2\,\big( \vartheta_2 - \vartheta_1 \big)^2
$$
$$
\mathbb{V} =
\begin{bmatrix}
k\ell^2 - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2} & -k\ell^2 \\[8pt]
-k\ell^2 & k\ell^2 - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
\det\big( \mathbb{V} - \sigma\,\mathbb{I} \big) = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sigma_1 = -\frac{m g \ell}{2} \ \ \text{with} \ u_1 = (1, 1) ,
\qquad
\sigma_2 = 2k\ell^2 - \frac{m g \ell}{2} \ \ \text{with} \ u_2 = (1, -1)
$$

The eigenvalue $\sigma_1$ is negative for every value of $k$, hence the vertical configuration is **unstable** however the spring is chosen.

Direct check on the line $\vartheta_1 = \vartheta_2 = \vartheta$:
$$
B - A = \big( d,\ 0 \big) ,
\qquad
L = d ,
\qquad
V(\vartheta, \vartheta) = m g \ell \cos\vartheta
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has two degrees of freedom, one for each rod. The potential energy adds the weights of the two rods, whose centers of mass have height $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta_i$, and the elastic term, which depends on the distance $L$ between the upper ends.

In the vertical configuration the spring has length $d$, that is, it is unloaded, therefore its first derivative is zero and the configuration is an equilibrium configuration. To build the Hessian it is convenient to expand $V$ to second order: the elastic term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,(L-d)^2$ has second derivative $k\,\left( \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \vartheta_h} \right)\left( \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \vartheta_k} \right)$ in the configuration in which $L = d$, and in the case at hand $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \vartheta_1} = -\ell$ and $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \vartheta_2} = \ell$.

The Hessian matrix is symmetric with equal diagonal entries, therefore its eigenvectors are $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ and the eigenvalues are obtained by adding and subtracting the off-diagonal term. The eigenvalue relative to $(1,1)$ equals $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$ and does not contain $k$.

The reason can be read in the check. If the two rods rotate by the same angle, the vector $B - A$ stays equal to $(d, 0)$, hence the spring is not deformed and the potential energy reduces to that of the two weights, $m g \ell \cos\vartheta$, which has a strict maximum at $\vartheta = 0$. Along that direction the system behaves as two inverted pendulums without a spring, and by the instability theorem the equilibrium is **unstable** whatever the stiffness.

In the direction $(1,-1)$, in which the two rods tilt in opposite directions, the spring is deformed and the eigenvalue $2k\ell^2 - \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$ is positive for $k$ large enough: that mode of deformation is stabilized by the spring, but the stability of the equilibrium requires that all the eigenvalues be positive.

</div>
