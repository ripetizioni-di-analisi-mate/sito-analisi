## Exercise 1

The rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $\ell$, is placed along the $x$ axis with $A$ at the origin and carries the distributed load of intensity
$$
q(s) = q_0\,\Big( 1 + \frac{s}{\ell} \Big) ,
\qquad 0 \leq s \leq \ell ,
$$
directed along $-\hat{\jmath}$, where $s$ is the coordinate measured from $A$ and $q_0 > 0$. Determine the resultant of the load and the abscissa of the point through which its line of action passes.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" carichi="A,B,0.6,1.2,q" reazioni="3.333 1.5,0,-1.4," quote="A,3.333 0,s_C,1" etichette="3.6,1.45,R" riferimento=0 titolo="The trapezoidal load from $q_0$ at $A$ to $2q_0$ at $B$ and its resultant"}

$$
R = \int_0^\ell q_0\,\Big( 1 + \frac{s}{\ell} \Big)\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ s + \frac{s^2}{2\ell} \Big]_0^\ell = q_0\,\Big( \ell + \frac{\ell}{2} \Big) = \frac{3\,q_0\,\ell}{2}
$$
$$
\int_0^\ell s\,q(s)\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ \frac{s^2}{2} + \frac{s^3}{3\ell} \Big]_0^\ell = q_0\,\Big( \frac{\ell^2}{2} + \frac{\ell^2}{3} \Big) = \frac{5\,q_0\,\ell^2}{6}
$$
$$
s_C = \frac{1}{R}\int_0^\ell s\,q(s)\,ds = \frac{5\,q_0\,\ell^2}{6} \cdot \frac{2}{3\,q_0\,\ell} = \frac{5\ell}{9}
$$
The load is equivalent to the force $-\dfrac{3\,q_0\,\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at the point of abscissa $\dfrac{5\ell}{9}$.

Check by splitting the load into a uniform part and a triangular part and using the additivity of the center:
$$
R_1 = q_0\,\ell , \quad s_1 = \frac{\ell}{2} ;
\qquad
R_2 = \frac{q_0\,\ell}{2} , \quad s_2 = \frac{2\ell}{3}
$$
$$
R = R_1 + R_2 = \frac{3\,q_0\,\ell}{2} ,
\qquad
s_C = \frac{q_0\,\ell \cdot \dfrac{\ell}{2} + \dfrac{q_0\,\ell}{2} \cdot \dfrac{2\ell}{3}}{\dfrac{3\,q_0\,\ell}{2}} = \frac{\dfrac{5\,q_0\,\ell^2}{6}}{\dfrac{3\,q_0\,\ell}{2}} = \frac{5\ell}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load is a system of infinitely many vectors applied parallel to $-\hat{\jmath}$, hence its resultant is the integral of the intensity and its line of action passes through the center of the system, obtained by replacing the sums with integrals in the formula for the center of a system of parallel vectors.

In the first integral the primitive of $1 + \dfrac{s}{\ell}$ is $s + \dfrac{s^2}{2\ell}$; evaluated between $0$ and $\ell$ it gives $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$. In the second integral the intensity is multiplied by $s$ before integrating, hence the integrand is $s + \dfrac{s^2}{\ell}$.

The point found lies slightly beyond the middle of the rod, on the side where the load is larger, as is to be expected for a load increasing towards $B$.

The check splits the load into a **uniform** part of intensity $q_0$, with resultant $q_0\,\ell$ at the midpoint, and into a **triangular** part growing from $0$ to $q_0$, with resultant $\dfrac{q_0\,\ell}{2}$ at the point of abscissa $\dfrac{2\ell}{3}$. The two resultants form a system of two parallel vectors, whose center is the weighted average of the two abscissas; the result coincides with the one obtained by integration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $\ell$, placed as in the previous exercise, carries the distributed load of intensity
$$
q(s) = q_0\,\Big( 1 - \frac{s^2}{\ell^2} \Big) ,
\qquad 0 \leq s \leq \ell ,
$$
directed along $-\hat{\jmath}$. Determine the resultant and the abscissa of its line of action.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 0,1.5 0.25,1.497 0.5,1.49 0.75,1.477 1,1.458 1.25,1.435 1.5,1.406 1.75,1.372 2,1.333 2.25,1.289 2.5,1.24 2.75,1.185 3,1.125 3.25,1.06 3.5,0.99 3.75,0.914 4,0.833 4.25,0.747 4.5,0.656 4.75,0.56 5,0.458 5.25,0.352 5.5,0.24 5.75,0.122 6,0" nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" reazioni="2.25 1.9,0,-1.5," quote="A,2.25 0,s_C,1" etichette="2.5,1.85,R;0.3,1.72,q_0" riferimento=0 titolo="The parabolic load of maximum intensity $q_0$ at $A$ and zero at $B$"}

$$
R = q_0 \int_0^\ell \Big( 1 - \frac{s^2}{\ell^2} \Big)\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ s - \frac{s^3}{3\ell^2} \Big]_0^\ell = q_0\,\Big( \ell - \frac{\ell}{3} \Big) = \frac{2\,q_0\,\ell}{3}
$$
$$
\int_0^\ell s\,q(s)\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ \frac{s^2}{2} - \frac{s^4}{4\ell^2} \Big]_0^\ell = q_0\,\Big( \frac{\ell^2}{2} - \frac{\ell^2}{4} \Big) = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{4}
$$
$$
s_C = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{4} \cdot \frac{3}{2\,q_0\,\ell} = \frac{3\ell}{8}
$$

Check with the moment about the point of abscissa $s_C$, which must vanish:
$$
\int_0^\ell \Big( s - \frac{3\ell}{8} \Big)\,q(s)\,ds = \int_0^\ell s\,q(s)\,ds - \frac{3\ell}{8}\,R = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{4} - \frac{3\ell}{8} \cdot \frac{2\,q_0\,\ell}{3} = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{4} - \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{4} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The procedure is the same as in the previous exercise and uses the primitives of $1 - \dfrac{s^2}{\ell^2}$ and of $s - \dfrac{s^3}{\ell^2}$.

The load is maximum at $A$ and zero at $B$, hence the line of action of the resultant falls in the first half of the rod. The value $\dfrac{3\ell}{8}$ is greater than $\dfrac{\ell}{3}$, which would be obtained with a triangular load decreasing from the same maximum, since the parabolic load stays high for longer and moves the center to the right.

The check computes the moment of the load about the point found. The moment is the integral of the distance $s - s_C$ multiplied by the intensity; expanding it one obtains the difference between the moment about $A$ and the product $s_C\,R$, which vanishes by the very definition of $s_C$. The numerical computation confirms that the two terms cancel out, hence the point of abscissa $\dfrac{3\ell}{8}$ belongs to the **line of action of the resultant**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $\ell$, carries in two distinct cases the loads of intensity
$$
q_a(s) = q_0 \sin\frac{\pi s}{\ell} ,
\qquad
q_b(s) = q_0 \sin\frac{2\pi s}{\ell} ,
$$
directed along $-\hat{\jmath}$, with $0 \leq s \leq \ell$. In the second case the intensity is negative in the second half of the rod, that is the load points upwards. Reduce each of the two loads.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 0,0 0.25,0.196 0.5,0.388 0.75,0.574 1,0.75 1.25,0.913 1.5,1.061 1.75,1.19 2,1.299 2.25,1.386 2.5,1.449 2.75,1.487 3,1.5 3.25,1.487 3.5,1.449 3.75,1.386 4,1.299 4.25,1.19 4.5,1.061 4.75,0.913 5,0.75 5.25,0.574 5.5,0.388 5.75,0.196 6,0" nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" reazioni="3 1.9,0,-1.5," quote="A,3 0,s_C,1" etichette="3.25,1.85,R;2.0,1.62,q_0" riferimento=0 titolo="The first load, with maximum intensity $q_0$ at midspan"}

$$
R_a = q_0 \int_0^\ell \sin\frac{\pi s}{\ell}\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ -\frac{\ell}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi s}{\ell} \Big]_0^\ell = \frac{2\,q_0\,\ell}{\pi}
$$
$$
\int_0^\ell s\,q_0 \sin\frac{\pi s}{\ell}\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ -\frac{\ell s}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi s}{\ell} + \frac{\ell^2}{\pi^2}\sin\frac{\pi s}{\ell} \Big]_0^\ell = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{\pi}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s_C = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{\pi} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2\,q_0\,\ell} = \frac{\ell}{2}
$$

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 0,0 0.25,0.336 0.5,0.65 0.75,0.919 1,1.126 1.25,1.256 1.5,1.3 1.75,1.256 2,1.126 2.25,0.919 2.5,0.65 2.75,0.336 3,0;3,0 3,0 3.25,-0.336 3.5,-0.65 3.75,-0.919 4,-1.126 4.25,-1.256 4.5,-1.3 4.75,-1.256 5,-1.126 5.25,-0.919 5.5,-0.65 5.75,-0.336 6,0" nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" coppie="3 1.3,1,ℳ" riferimento=0 titolo="The second load, downwards in the first half and upwards in the second: the system is equivalent to the couple $\mathcal{M}$"}

$$
R_b = q_0 \int_0^\ell \sin\frac{2\pi s}{\ell}\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ -\frac{\ell}{2\pi}\cos\frac{2\pi s}{\ell} \Big]_0^\ell = 0
$$
$$
\int_0^\ell s\,q_0 \sin\frac{2\pi s}{\ell}\,ds = q_0\,\Big[ -\frac{\ell s}{2\pi}\cos\frac{2\pi s}{\ell} + \frac{\ell^2}{4\pi^2}\sin\frac{2\pi s}{\ell} \Big]_0^\ell = -\,\frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{2\pi}
$$
$$
\vec{M}_A = -\,\hat{k} \int_0^\ell s\,q_b(s)\,ds = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{2\pi}\,\hat{k}
$$
The second load is equivalent to a counterclockwise **couple** of magnitude $\dfrac{q_0\,\ell^2}{2\pi}$.

Check of the second case with the pole $B$:
$$
\vec{M}_B = -\,\hat{k} \int_0^\ell (s - \ell)\,q_b(s)\,ds = -\,\hat{k}\,\Big( -\frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{2\pi} - \ell \cdot 0 \Big) = \frac{q_0\,\ell^2}{2\pi}\,\hat{k}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first case the load is positive along the whole rod and the resultant differs from zero, hence the load is equivalent to the force $-\dfrac{2\,q_0\,\ell}{\pi}\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at the point of abscissa $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$. The value $s_C = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$ can also be read on the graph of the intensity, which is symmetric with respect to midspan, and by the property of **symmetry** of the center the center belongs to the axis of symmetry.

The integral $\displaystyle\int s \sin\frac{\pi s}{\ell}\,ds$ is computed by parts, choosing the sine as the factor to integrate; the primitive indicated is checked by differentiating it.

In the second case the intensity changes sign at midspan and the integral over the whole rod vanishes, hence the resultant vanishes and the center does not exist. By the **reduction theorem** the load is then equivalent to a couple, whose moment is computed as the resultant moment about an arbitrary pole. The result is positive, because the part of the load directed upwards acts in the right half of the rod and tends to produce a counterclockwise rotation.

The check computes the moment about $B$ and finds the same value, as it must be for a couple, since the additional term $\ell \displaystyle\int_0^\ell q_b(s)\,ds$ vanishes precisely because the resultant vanishes.

</div>
