## Exercise 1

In the plane $Oxy$ the applied vector $(P, \vec{v})$ is given, with $P - O = a\,(3\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ and $\vec{v} = v\,(\hat{\imath} - 2\hat{\jmath})$, where $a > 0$ and $v > 0$. Compute the moment about $O$, the arm of the line of action with respect to $O$ and the moment about the pole $O'$ with $O' - O = 2a\,\hat{\jmath}$, as the diagram in the figure shows.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;O',0,2;P,3,1" forze="4.2 -1.4,1.2,-2.4,v" tratteggi="2.4,2.2 5,-3;0,0 2.8,1.4" punti="0,0;0,2;3,1;2.8,1.4" etichette="1.3,0.95,b" titolo="The vector applied at $P$, its line of action, the arm $b$ with respect to $O$ and the second pole $O'$"}

$$
\vec{M}_O = (P - O) \wedge \vec{v} = a\,(3\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}) \wedge v\,(\hat{\imath} - 2\hat{\jmath}) = a v\,\big[ 3 \cdot (-2) - 1 \cdot 1 \big]\,\hat{k} = -\,7\,a v\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}| = v\sqrt{1^2 + 2^2} = v\sqrt{5} ,
\qquad
b = \frac{|\vec{M}_O|}{|\vec{v}|} = \frac{7\,a v}{v\sqrt{5}} = \frac{7\sqrt{5}}{5}\,a
$$
$$
\vec{M}_{O'} = \vec{M}_O + (O - O') \wedge \vec{v} = -\,7\,a v\,\hat{k} + (-\,2a\,\hat{\jmath}) \wedge v\,(\hat{\imath} - 2\hat{\jmath}) = -\,7\,a v\,\hat{k} + 2\,a v\,\hat{k} = -\,5\,a v\,\hat{k}
$$

Check by direct computation about $O'$:
$$
(P - O') \wedge \vec{v} = a\,(3\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath}) \wedge v\,(\hat{\imath} - 2\hat{\jmath}) = a v\,\big[ 3 \cdot (-2) - (-1) \cdot 1 \big]\,\hat{k} = -\,5\,a v\,\hat{k}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moment is obtained from the cross product of the position vector $P - O$ and the vector $\vec{v}$. Both lie in the plane $Oxy$, therefore the product is parallel to $\hat{k}$ and reduces to the number $(x_P - x_O)\,v_y - (y_P - y_O)\,v_x$. The result is negative, therefore the applied vector tends to produce a **clockwise** rotation about $O$.

The arm is obtained from the **theorem on the moment and the arm**, which relates the magnitude of the moment to the magnitude of the vector. Since $|\vec{M}_O| = |\vec{v}|\,b$, the former is divided by the latter. The value $\dfrac{7\sqrt{5}}{5}\,a$ is the distance of $O$ from the line of action.

For the second pole the **change of pole formula** is used, with $O - O' = -\,2a\,\hat{\jmath}$. The additional term equals $(-\,2a\,\hat{\jmath}) \wedge v\,\hat{\imath} = 2\,a v\,\hat{k}$, since $\hat{\jmath} \wedge \hat{\imath} = -\hat{k}$ and the term with $\hat{\jmath} \wedge \hat{\jmath}$ vanishes. The direct computation with the position vector $P - O' = a\,(3\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath})$ gives the same value, and this confirms the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The applied vector $(P, \vec{v})$ has $P - O = a\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$ and $\vec{v} = F\,(2\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath} + 2\hat{k})$, with $a > 0$ and $F > 0$; the point $P$ is a vertex of the cube of side $a$ in the figure, with three edges on the coordinate axes. Compute the moment about $O$, the arm with respect to $O$, the axial moment about the $z$ axis and the axial moment about the line $r$ through $O$ with unit vector $\hat{u} = \dfrac{\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}}{\sqrt{3}}$, which is the diagonal of the cube.

::struttura{frecce="0,0,-1.15,-0.828,x;0,0,2.3,0,y;0,0,0,2.3,z" tratteggi="0.0,0.0 -0.7,-0.504 0.7,-0.504 1.4,0.0 0.0,0.0;0.0,1.4 -0.7,0.896 0.7,0.896 1.4,1.4 0.0,1.4;-0.7,-0.504 -0.7,0.896;0.7,-0.504 0.7,0.896;1.4,0.0 1.4,1.4;0.0,0.0 0.0,1.4;0.0,0.0 0.7,0.896" punti="0.7,-0.504" forze="-0.235 0.091,-0.935,0.595," etichette="0.16,-0.34,O;0.85,-0.72,P;-0.32,0.2,v;0.82,1.0,r" riferimento=0 titolo="The cube of side $a$, the vector $\vec{v}$ applied at the vertex $P$ and the diagonal $r$"}

$$
\vec{M}_O = a\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}) \wedge F\,(2\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath} + 2\hat{k})
= a F\,\big( 2\hat{\imath} - 2\hat{\jmath} - 3\hat{k} \big)
$$
$$
|\vec{M}_O| = a F\sqrt{4 + 4 + 9} = a F\sqrt{17} ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}| = F\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3F ,
\qquad
b = \frac{a F\sqrt{17}}{3F} = \frac{\sqrt{17}}{3}\,a
$$
$$
M_z = \hat{k} \cdot \vec{M}_O = -\,3\,a F ,
\qquad
M_r = \hat{u} \cdot \vec{M}_O = \frac{a F\,(2 - 2 - 3)}{\sqrt{3}} = -\sqrt{3}\,a F
$$

Check of $M_z$ with Varignon's theorem, decomposing $\vec{v}$ into the three components applied at $P$:
$$
M_z = \underbrace{-\,a \cdot 2F}_{2F\hat{\imath}} + \underbrace{a \cdot (-F)}_{-F\hat{\jmath}} + \underbrace{0}_{2F\hat{k}} = -\,2\,a F - a F = -\,3\,a F
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross product is computed component by component. With $P - O = a\,(1, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{v} = F\,(2, -1, 2)$ one obtains the first component $a F\,(1 \cdot 2 - 0 \cdot (-1)) = 2\,a F$, the second $a F\,(0 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 2) = -\,2\,a F$ and the third $a F\,(1 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 2) = -\,3\,a F$.

The arm is the distance of $O$ from the line of action and is obtained from the ratio of the magnitude of the moment to the magnitude of the vector, by the **theorem on the moment and the arm**.

The axial moment about $z$ is the third component of the polar moment, because $O$ belongs to the $z$ axis and the unit vector of the axis is $\hat{k}$. The axial moment about the diagonal is obtained with the same pole $O$, which belongs to that line as well, by projecting $\vec{M}_O$ onto $\hat{u}$.

In the check the vector has been decomposed into the three components $2F\hat{\imath}$, $-F\hat{\jmath}$ and $2F\hat{k}$, all applied at $P$; by **Varignon's theorem** the axial moment of the vector is the sum of the axial moments of the components. The component along $\hat{k}$ is parallel to the $z$ axis, hence it gives no contribution; the other two have arm $a$ with respect to the $z$ axis and both tend to produce a clockwise rotation as seen from the positive $z$ semiaxis. The sum coincides with the value already found.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $r$ be the line parallel to the $z$ axis through the point $A$ with $A - O = d\,\hat{\imath}$, oriented along $\hat{k}$, and let $(P, \vec{v})$ be the applied vector with $P - O = b\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{v} = v\,\hat{\jmath}$, with $b > d > 0$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Compute the axial moment $M_r$, check that it does not depend on the point chosen on the axis and determine for which directions of $\vec{v}$ in the plane $Oxy$ the axial moment vanishes.

::struttura{frecce="0,0,-1.7,-1.224,x;0,0,2.2,0,y;0,0,0,2.2,z" punti="-0.5,-0.36;-1.3,-0.936" tratteggi="-0.5,-0.9 -0.5,1.9;0,0 -1.3,-0.936" forze="0.1 -0.936,1.4,0," etichette="0.16,-0.34,O;-0.36,-0.62,A;-1.18,-1.22,P;-0.38,1.95,r;0.25,-1.05,v" riferimento=0 titolo="The axis $r$ parallel to $\hat{k}$ through $A$ and the vector $\vec{v} = v\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $P$"}

$$
\vec{M}_A = (P - A) \wedge \vec{v} = (b - d)\,\hat{\imath} \wedge v\,\hat{\jmath} = v\,(b - d)\,\hat{k}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
M_r = \hat{k} \cdot \vec{M}_A = v\,(b - d)
$$
With $\vec{v} = v\,(\cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath})$:
$$
\vec{M}_A = (b - d)\,\hat{\imath} \wedge v\,(\cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath}) = v\,(b - d)\sin\alpha\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
M_r = v\,(b - d)\sin\alpha
$$
$$
M_r = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sin\alpha = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha = 0 \ \text{ or } \ \alpha = \pi
$$

Check with the second point $A'$ of the axis, with $A' - O = d\,\hat{\imath} + c\,\hat{k}$ and $c$ arbitrary:
$$
\vec{M}_{A'} = \big[ (b - d)\,\hat{\imath} - c\,\hat{k} \big] \wedge v\,\hat{\jmath} = v\,(b - d)\,\hat{k} + c v\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
\hat{k} \cdot \vec{M}_{A'} = v\,(b - d)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pole for the axial moment must belong to the axis, therefore $A$ is chosen. The vector $P - A$ is parallel to $\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{v}$ is parallel to $\hat{\jmath}$, hence the cross product is parallel to $\hat{k}$ and its projection onto $\hat{k}$ is the whole moment. The value $v\,(b - d)$ is the product of the magnitude by the distance between the line of action and the axis, which is the arm measured in the plane orthogonal to the axis.

In the general case the component of $\vec{v}$ parallel to $\hat{\imath}$ is parallel to the vector $P - A$, hence its contribution to the cross product vanishes; only the component $v\sin\alpha$ orthogonal to $P - A$ remains. The axial moment therefore vanishes when $\vec{v}$ is parallel to $\hat{\imath}$, that is when the line of action and the axis $r$ are coplanar.

The check uses a second point of the axis, displaced by $c\,\hat{k}$. The polar moment changes, since the term $c v\,\hat{\imath}$ appears, whereas its component along $\hat{k}$ stays $v\,(b - d)$. The result confirms the **theorem on the independence of the axial moment of the point of the axis**, and it also shows that the component of the moment orthogonal to the axis does depend on the point chosen.

</div>
