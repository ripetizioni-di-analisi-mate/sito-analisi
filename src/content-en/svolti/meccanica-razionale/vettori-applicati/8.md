## Exercise 1

In the plane $Oxy$ the applied vectors $(A, F\,\hat{\imath})$ and $(C, 2F\,\hat{\jmath})$ act on a rigid body, with $A = O$ and $C - A = \ell\,\hat{\imath}$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Determine the vector applied at $B$, with $B - A = 2\ell\,\hat{\imath}$, and the couple of moment $\mathcal{M}\,\hat{k}$ that make the overall system balanced.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,1,0;B,2,0" aste="A-B" forze="A,1,0,F;C,0,1.3,2F" quote="A,C,ℓ,0.9;C,B,ℓ,0.9" titolo="The two known vectors on the rod $AB$; the unknown vector is sought at $B$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
\vec{R} = \vec{0} \\[4pt]
\vec{M}_A = \vec{0}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_B = -\,\big( F\,\hat{\imath} + 2F\,\hat{\jmath} \big) = -F\,\hat{\imath} - 2F\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
M_A = \underbrace{0}_{(A, F\hat{\imath})} + \underbrace{\ell \cdot 2F}_{(C, 2F\hat{\jmath})} + \underbrace{2\ell \cdot (-\,2F)}_{(B, \vec{v}_B)} + \mathcal{M} = 2F\ell - 4F\ell + \mathcal{M} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal{M} = 2F\ell
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,1,0;B,2,0" aste="A-B" forze="A,1,0,F;C,0,1.3,2F" reazioni="B,-0.7,-1.4,v_B" coppie="1.5 0.9,1,ℳ" quote="A,C,ℓ,0.9;C,B,ℓ,0.9" titolo="The balanced system: in black the known vectors, in the second color the unknown vector at $B$ and the counterclockwise couple $\mathcal{M} = 2F\ell$"}

Check with the pole $B$:
$$
M_B = \underbrace{-\,(0 - 0)\,F}_{(A, F\hat{\imath})} + \underbrace{(\ell - 2\ell) \cdot 2F}_{(C, 2F\hat{\jmath})} + \underbrace{0}_{(B, \vec{v}_B)} + 2F\ell = -\,2F\ell + 2F\ell = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A system is **balanced** when the resultant and the resultant moment both vanish; in the plane the conditions are three scalar equations. The couple does not contribute to the resultant, hence the first condition alone determines the unknown vector: $\vec{v}_B$ must be opposite to the sum of the two known vectors.

The second condition is written by choosing $A$ as pole, because the vector applied at $A$ then has zero moment. The vector applied at $C$ has vertical component $2F$ and arm $\ell$, hence counterclockwise moment $2F\ell$; the vector at $B$ has vertical component $-\,2F$ and arm $2\ell$, hence clockwise moment $-\,4F\ell$. The horizontal component of $\vec{v}_B$ has its line of action along the rod, which passes through $A$, therefore it does not enter the moment equation.

The couple required equals $2F\ell$ and is counterclockwise. Without the couple the system would have zero resultant and moment $-\,2F\ell$, that is it would reduce to a clockwise couple of that value, which the added couple cancels.

The check repeats the computation of the moment about $B$. The result is zero, as it must be for a balanced system, in which the resultant moment vanishes about every pole by the **change of pole formula**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On the rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $3a$ and placed along the $x$ axis with $A$ at the origin, the vector $-\,3F\,\hat{\jmath}$ acts at the point $D$ with $D - A = 2a\,\hat{\imath}$. Determine the two vectors parallel to $\hat{\jmath}$, applied at $A$ and at $B$, that make the system balanced; then establish where the known vector should act for the two unknown vectors to be equal.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,2,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" forze="D,0,-1.5,3F" quote="A,D,2a,1;D,B,a,1" titolo="The rod with the known vector at $D$; two vertical vectors are sought at $A$ and at $B$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_A + \Phi_B - 3F = 0 \\[4pt]
3a\,\Phi_B - 2a \cdot 3F = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_B = \frac{2a \cdot 3F}{3a} = 2F ,
\qquad
\Phi_A = 3F - 2F = F
$$
The two vectors are $F\,\hat{\jmath}$ at $A$ and $2F\,\hat{\jmath}$ at $B$, both pointing upwards. If the known vector acts at the point of abscissa $x$, the same equations give
$$
\Phi_B = \frac{3F x}{3a} = \frac{F x}{a} ,
\qquad
\Phi_A = 3F - \frac{F x}{a} ,
$$
and the equality $\Phi_A = \Phi_B$ requires $\dfrac{F x}{a} = 3F - \dfrac{F x}{a}$, that is $x = \dfrac{3a}{2}$, the midpoint of the rod.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,2,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" forze="D,0,-1.5,3F" reazioni="A,0,1,Φ_A;B,0,1.5,Φ_B" quote="A,D,2a,1;D,B,a,1" titolo="The balanced system: $\Phi_A = F$ and $\Phi_B = 2F$, both pointing upwards"}

Check with the center of the parallel vectors: the two unknown vectors, by themselves, have resultant $3F\,\hat{\jmath}$ and center of abscissa
$$
x_C = \frac{F \cdot 0 + 2F \cdot 3a}{3F} = 2a ,
$$
hence their resultant is opposite to the known vector and has the same line of action; by the theorem on balanced systems of two vectors the system is balanced.

<div class="spiegazione">

The three vectors are all parallel to $\hat{\jmath}$, hence the condition $\vec{R} = \vec{0}$ reduces to a single scalar equation and the resultant moment has only the component along $\hat{k}$. The two equations of the system are the sum of the vertical components and the moment about $A$, chosen as pole because $\Phi_A$ then has zero moment.

The unknown vector at $B$ equals $2F$ and the one at $A$ equals $F$: the point of application of the known vector is closer to $B$, hence the larger part is carried by $B$. The formula $\Phi_B = \dfrac{F x}{a}$ shows that the magnitude at $B$ grows in proportion to the distance of the known vector from $A$, and that the two unknown vectors are equal only when the known vector acts at midspan.

The check uses the **center of a system of parallel vectors** applied to the two unknown vectors alone. Their resultant equals $3F\,\hat{\jmath}$ and passes through the point of abscissa $2a$, that is through $D$. The complete system is then formed by two opposite vectors with the same line of action, which by the **theorem on balanced systems of two vectors** is balanced.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The applied vectors $(P_1, F\,\hat{k})$ with $P_1 - O = a\,\hat{\imath}$ and $(P_2, F\,\hat{\imath})$ with $P_2 - O = a\,\hat{\jmath}$ are given, as the diagram in the figure shows. Establish whether a single applied vector exists that makes the system balanced, and determine the balancing system formed by an applied vector and by a couple of minimum moment.

::struttura{frecce="0,0,-1.1,-0.792,x;0,0,2.2,0,y;0,0,0,2.2,z" punti="-0.7,-0.504,P_1;1.4,0,P_2" forze="-0.7 0.696,0,1.2,;0.8 -0.432,-0.6,-0.432," etichette="0.2,-0.3,O;-0.62,0.78,F;0.98,-0.5,F" riferimento=0 titolo="The two applied vectors, with skew lines of action"}

$$
\vec{R} = F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k}) ,
\qquad
\vec{M}_O = \underbrace{a\,\hat{\imath} \wedge F\,\hat{k}}_{(P_1, F\hat{k})} + \underbrace{a\,\hat{\jmath} \wedge F\,\hat{\imath}}_{(P_2, F\hat{\imath})} = -\,a F\,\hat{\jmath} - a F\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
I = \vec{R} \cdot \vec{M}_O = F \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot (-\,a F) + F \cdot (-\,a F) = -\,a F^2 \neq 0 ,
$$
therefore no applied vector, by itself, balances the system.
$$
\vec{R} \wedge \vec{M}_O = F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k}) \wedge \big( -\,a F\,(\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}) \big) = a F^2\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath} - \hat{k})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
Q - O = \frac{a}{2}\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath} - \hat{k})
$$
$$
\vec{M}_Q = \frac{I}{|\vec{R}|^2}\,\vec{R} = \frac{-\,a F^2}{2F^2}\,F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k}) = -\,\frac{a F}{2}\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k})
$$
The balancing system is formed by the vector $(Q, -\vec{R})$, that is $-F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k})$ applied at $Q$, and by the couple of moment
$$
-\vec{M}_Q = \frac{a F}{2}\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k}) ,
\qquad
|\vec{M}_Q| = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,a F .
$$

Check of the equilibrium with the pole $O$:
$$
(Q - O) \wedge (-\vec{R}) = \frac{a}{2}\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath} - \hat{k}) \wedge \big( -F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k}) \big) = -\,\frac{a F}{2}\,\hat{\imath} + a F\,\hat{\jmath} + \frac{a F}{2}\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\vec{M}_O^{\,\text{(total)}} = \big( -\,a F\,\hat{\jmath} - a F\,\hat{k} \big) + \Big( -\frac{a F}{2}\,\hat{\imath} + a F\,\hat{\jmath} + \frac{a F}{2}\,\hat{k} \Big) + \frac{a F}{2}\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{k}) = \vec{0}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two moments are obtained with $\hat{\imath} \wedge \hat{k} = -\hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{\jmath} \wedge \hat{\imath} = -\hat{k}$.

To balance the system with a single applied vector $(Q, -\vec{R})$ the moment of that vector about $O$ must be $-\vec{M}_O$, that is $\vec{M}_O = (Q - O) \wedge \vec{R}$. The right-hand side is orthogonal to $\vec{R}$, hence the condition requires $\vec{R} \cdot \vec{M}_O = 0$. Here the invariant equals $-\,a F^2$, therefore the condition is not satisfied and a single applied vector does not suffice.

The simplest balancing system is then obtained from the equivalent minimal system. The given system is equivalent to the vector $\vec{R}$ applied at a point $Q$ of the **central axis** together with the couple of moment $\vec{M}_Q$, parallel to $\vec{R}$; to balance it the sign of both is changed. The couple so obtained has the moment of smallest magnitude among all the couples that, together with an applied vector, balance the system.

The check adds the moments about $O$: that of the given system, that of the balancing vector applied at $Q$ and that of the couple. The sum is the zero vector, and the overall resultant vanishes as well, therefore the complete system is **balanced**.

</div>
