## Exercise 1

The homogeneous disk in the figure, of radius $R$ and center $G$, rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, in the vertical plane $Oxy$. The abscissa of the center is $s(t)$. Compute the angular velocity of the disk, the velocity of the point $T$ diametrically opposite to the contact and the acceleration of the contact point $C$.

::struttura{cerchi="2,1,1" linee="-0.3,0 4.8,0" punti="2,1,G,baricentro;2,0,C,centro;2,2,T" frecce="2,1,3,1,v_G" coppie="1.35 1.5,-1,ω" quote="0 0,2 0,s,0.45" titolo="Disk of radius $R$ rolling without slipping on the horizontal guide"}

$$
G - O = s\,\hat{\imath} + R\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_G = \dot{s}\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
C - G = -\,R\,\hat{\jmath}
$$

Condition of rolling without slipping, with $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$:
$$
\vec{v}_C = \vec{v}_G + \omega\,\hat{k} \wedge (-R\,\hat{\jmath}) = (\dot{s} + \omega R)\,\hat{\imath} = \vec{0}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega = -\,\frac{\dot{s}}{R}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_T = \vec{v}_G + \omega\,\hat{k} \wedge (R\,\hat{\jmath}) = \dot{s}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\dot{s}}{R}\,R\,\hat{\imath} = 2\dot{s}\;\hat{\imath}
$$

Acceleration of the contact point, with $\vec{a}_G = \ddot{s}\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = -\dfrac{\ddot{s}}{R}\,\hat{k}$:
$$
\vec{a}_C = \vec{a}_G + \dot{\vec{\omega}} \wedge (C - G) - \omega^2\,(C-G)
= \ddot{s}\,\hat{\imath} - \ddot{s}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\dot{s}^{\,2}}{R}\;\hat{\jmath}
= \frac{\dot{s}^{\,2}}{R}\;\hat{\jmath}
$$

Check by the instantaneous center of rotation:
$$
|\vec{v}_T| = |\omega|\,|T - C| = \frac{|\dot{s}|}{R}\,(2R) = 2\,|\dot{s}| ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_G| = \frac{|\dot{s}|}{R}\,R = |\dot{s}|
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center always stays at height $R$, therefore its velocity is horizontal and equals $\dot{s}$. **Rolling without slipping** on a fixed guide requires the point of the disk in contact to have zero velocity; writing this condition by means of the fundamental formula one obtains $\omega = -\dfrac{\dot{s}}{R}$. The minus sign says that a disk advancing to the right turns in the clockwise sense.

For $T$ one applies the same formula with $T - G = R\,\hat{\jmath}$. The term $\omega\,\hat{k} \wedge (R\,\hat{\jmath})$ equals $-\omega R\,\hat{\imath} = \dot{s}\,\hat{\imath}$, hence the velocity of the topmost point is **twice** that of the center.

In the acceleration of $C$ the tangential term $\dot{\vec{\omega}} \wedge (C-G)$ equals $-\ddot{s}\,\hat{\imath}$ and cancels $\vec{a}_G$; only the centripetal term $-\omega^2(C-G)$ remains, directed from $C$ toward $G$, that is, upward. The contact point therefore has zero velocity and acceleration $\dfrac{\dot{s}^{\,2}}{R}$ directed toward the center: the motion of the material point touching the guide reverses its direction, and this is possible only with a nonzero acceleration.

The check uses the fact that $C$ is the instantaneous center of rotation: every velocity equals $|\omega|$ times the distance from $C$. The distances $|T - C| = 2R$ and $|G - C| = R$ give back the magnitudes found.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The disk of the previous exercise, of radius $R$, rolls without slipping on the horizontal guide with angular velocity of constant magnitude $\dot{\varphi}$, where $\varphi$ is the rotation angle of the disk, measured from the instant at which the point $P$ of the rim touches the guide at the origin. Determine the trajectory of $P$, its velocity and its acceleration.

::struttura{cerchi="1.57,1,1" linee="-0.3,0 5.4,0" punti="1.57,1,G,baricentro;1.57,0,C,centro;0.57,1,P" frecce="0.57,1,1.57,2,v_P" tratteggi="1.57,1 0.57,1;0.57,1 1.57,0" etichette="1.03,1.15,R" quote="0 0,1.57 0,s,0.45" titolo="The point $P$ of the rim after a quarter of a turn: $\vec{v}_P$ is orthogonal to $PC$"}

Rolling condition integrated, with $s = R\varphi$:
$$
P - O = R\,(\varphi - \sin\varphi)\;\hat{\imath} + R\,(1 - \cos\varphi)\;\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = R\dot{\varphi}\,(1 - \cos\varphi)\;\hat{\imath} + R\dot{\varphi}\sin\varphi\;\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = R\dot{\varphi}\,\sqrt{(1-\cos\varphi)^2 + \sin^2\varphi} = R\dot{\varphi}\,\sqrt{2 - 2\cos\varphi} = 2R\dot{\varphi}\,\left| \sin\frac{\varphi}{2} \right|
$$
$$
\vec{a}_P = R\dot{\varphi}^{\,2}\sin\varphi\;\hat{\imath} + R\dot{\varphi}^{\,2}\cos\varphi\;\hat{\jmath} = \dot{\varphi}^{\,2}\,(G - P)
$$

::grafico{curve="t-sin(t),1-cos(t),0,12.57" xmin=-0.6 xmax=13 ymin=-0.6 ymax=2.6 uguale=1 titolo="The cycloid described by the point $P$ of the rim, with $R = 1$: two arches correspond to two turns of the disk"}

Check by the instantaneous center of rotation:
$$
P - C = -\,R\sin\varphi\;\hat{\imath} + R\,(1 - \cos\varphi)\;\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
|P - C| = R\sqrt{2-2\cos\varphi} = 2R\left| \sin\frac{\varphi}{2} \right|
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P \cdot (P - C) = -R^2\dot{\varphi}\sin\varphi\,(1-\cos\varphi) + R^2\dot{\varphi}\sin\varphi\,(1-\cos\varphi) = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The condition of pure rolling on a straight guide integrates to $s = R\varphi$, hence the center is at $(R\varphi, R)$. The point $P$ lies on the rim in the direction that from the center points toward the contact, rotated by $\varphi$ in the clockwise sense, therefore $P - G = -R\sin\varphi\;\hat{\imath} - R\cos\varphi\;\hat{\jmath}$. Adding, one obtains the position of $P$, which describes a **cycloid**.

Differentiating one obtains $\vec{v}_P$, and the magnitude simplifies by means of $(1-\cos\varphi)^2 + \sin^2\varphi = 2 - 2\cos\varphi$ and of the half-angle formula $1 - \cos\varphi = 2\sin^2\dfrac{\varphi}{2}$. For $\varphi = 0$ the velocity is zero, because $P$ is at the contact point; for $\varphi = \pi$ it equals $2R\dot{\varphi}$, that is, twice the velocity of the center, because $P$ is the topmost point. In the graph the points of zero velocity are the cusps of the cycloid.

The acceleration, with $\dot{\varphi}$ constant, turns out to be equal to $\dot{\varphi}^{\,2}\,(G-P)$: it is directed from $P$ toward the center $G$ and has constant magnitude $R\dot{\varphi}^{\,2}$, as for a point in uniform circular motion with respect to $G$. The center indeed translates with constant velocity, hence the motion of $P$ with respect to $G$ is uniform circular motion.

The check verifies that $\vec{v}_P$ is orthogonal to $P - C$ and that its magnitude is $|\omega|\,|P-C|$, as the property of the instantaneous center of rotation requires.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The disk in the figure, of radius $r$ and center $G$, rolls without slipping while staying in contact with the inner wall of a fixed circular guide of radius $R > r$ and center $O$. The position of the disk is located by the angle $\varphi$ that the radius $OG$ forms with the $x$ axis. Compute the velocity of the center $G$, the angular velocity of the disk and the angle through which the disk turns about itself while $G$ makes a complete turn about $O$.

::struttura{cerchi="1.2,0.9,1.5" linee="3,0 2.99,0.26 2.95,0.52 2.9,0.78 2.82,1.03 2.72,1.27 2.6,1.5 2.46,1.72 2.3,1.93 2.12,2.12 1.93,2.3 1.72,2.46 1.5,2.6 1.27,2.72 1.03,2.82 0.78,2.9 0.52,2.95 0.26,2.99 0,3 -0.26,2.99 -0.52,2.95 -0.78,2.9 -1.03,2.82 -1.27,2.72 -1.5,2.6 -1.72,2.46 -1.93,2.3 -2.12,2.12 -2.3,1.93 -2.46,1.72 -2.6,1.5 -2.72,1.27 -2.82,1.03 -2.9,0.78 -2.95,0.52 -2.99,0.26 -3,0 -2.99,-0.26 -2.95,-0.52 -2.9,-0.78 -2.82,-1.03 -2.72,-1.27 -2.6,-1.5 -2.46,-1.72 -2.3,-1.93 -2.12,-2.12 -1.93,-2.3 -1.72,-2.46 -1.5,-2.6 -1.27,-2.72 -1.03,-2.82 -0.78,-2.9 -0.52,-2.95 -0.26,-2.99 0,-3 0.26,-2.99 0.52,-2.95 0.78,-2.9 1.03,-2.82 1.27,-2.72 1.5,-2.6 1.72,-2.46 1.93,-2.3 2.12,-2.12 2.3,-1.93 2.46,-1.72 2.6,-1.5 2.72,-1.27 2.82,-1.03 2.9,-0.78 2.95,-0.52 2.99,-0.26 3,0" punti="0,0,O;1.2,0.9,G,baricentro;2.4,1.8,C,centro" tratteggi="0,0 2.4,1.8;0,0 2.6,0" etichette="1,0.25,φ" riferimento=0 titolo="Disk of radius $r$ rolling inside the circular guide of radius $R$"}

$$
G - O = (R-r)\,\big( \cos\varphi\;\hat{\imath} + \sin\varphi\;\hat{\jmath} \big) ,
\qquad
C - O = R\,\big( \cos\varphi\;\hat{\imath} + \sin\varphi\;\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_G = (R-r)\,\dot{\varphi}\,\big( -\sin\varphi\;\hat{\imath} + \cos\varphi\;\hat{\jmath} \big) ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_G| = (R-r)\,|\dot{\varphi}|
$$

Condition of rolling without slipping on the fixed guide, with $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$ and $C - G = r\,(\cos\varphi\;\hat{\imath} + \sin\varphi\;\hat{\jmath})$:
$$
\vec{v}_C = \vec{v}_G + \omega\,\hat{k} \wedge (C-G) = \big[ (R-r)\dot{\varphi} + \omega r \big]\,\big( -\sin\varphi\;\hat{\imath} + \cos\varphi\;\hat{\jmath} \big) = \vec{0}
$$
$$
\omega = -\,\frac{(R-r)\,\dot{\varphi}}{r}
$$

Angle through which the disk turns in one turn of $G$, with $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}$:
$$
\dot{\vartheta} = -\,\frac{R-r}{r}\,\dot{\varphi}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
|\Delta\vartheta| = \frac{R-r}{r}\,2\pi
$$

Check by the lengths of the arcs rolled:
$$
R\,|\Delta\varphi| = r\,|\Delta\vartheta - \Delta\varphi| ,
\qquad
\Delta\vartheta - \Delta\varphi = -\,\frac{R-r}{r}\,\Delta\varphi - \Delta\varphi = -\,\frac{R}{r}\,\Delta\varphi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center $G$ travels along the circle with center $O$ and radius $R - r$, because the disk stays in contact with the guide; the contact point $C$ lies instead on the guide, at distance $R$ from $O$, in the same direction as $G$. Differentiating the position of $G$ one obtains a velocity tangent to that circle, of magnitude $(R-r)\,|\dot{\varphi}|$.

The vector $C - G$ is radial, hence $\hat{k} \wedge (C-G)$ is tangential and parallel to $\vec{v}_G$: the two terms of the fundamental formula have the same direction and the condition $\vec{v}_C = \vec{0}$ reduces to a single scalar equation, which gives $\omega$.

The ratio $\dfrac{R-r}{r}$ is greater than $1$ when $r < \dfrac{R}{2}$, therefore a small disk makes **more** than one turn about itself while its center makes one turn about $O$. The point of the rim describes a hypocycloid.

The check is the balance of the arcs: the arc of guide traveled by the contact point, of length $R\,|\Delta\varphi|$, must be equal to the arc of the rim of the disk that lies on it, of length $r$ times the rotation of the disk *relative to the radius* $OG$, that is, $|\Delta\vartheta - \Delta\varphi|$. Substituting the value found one obtains $\dfrac{R}{r}\,|\Delta\varphi|$ multiplied by $r$, that is, $R\,|\Delta\varphi|$, and the equality is verified. In the limiting case $r \to R$ the disk fills the guide, the center does not move and both $\vec{v}_G$ and $\omega$ tend to zero.

</div>
