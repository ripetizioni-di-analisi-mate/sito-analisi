## Exercise 1

A rigid body turns about the fixed axis $z$ and its configuration is located by the angle $\vartheta(t)$ between the first attached unit vector $\hat{e}_1$ and the $x$ axis. Verify Poisson's formulas for the three attached unit vectors and compute the velocity and the acceleration of the point $P$ of the body with $P - \Omega = a\,\hat{e}_1$, where $\Omega$ is the fixed point on the axis.

::struttura{linee="-2.6,0 2.6,0;0,-2.6 0,2.6;0,0 2.3,1.33" frecce="0,0,1,0.58,e_1;0,0,-0.58,1,e_2" punti="0,0,Ω;2.3,1.33,P" etichette="2.7,-0.1,x;0.1,2.7,y;1.35,0.25,ϑ" riferimento=0 titolo="Rotation about the $z$ axis: the attached basis is rotated by the angle $\vartheta$ with respect to the fixed one"}

$$
\hat{e}_1 = \cos\vartheta\;\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\;\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{e}_2 = -\sin\vartheta\;\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\;\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{e}_3 = \hat{k}
$$
$$
\dot{\hat{e}}_1 = \dot{\vartheta}\,\big( -\sin\vartheta\;\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\;\hat{\jmath} \big) = \dot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_2 ,
\qquad
\dot{\hat{e}}_2 = \dot{\vartheta}\,\big( -\cos\vartheta\;\hat{\imath} - \sin\vartheta\;\hat{\jmath} \big) = -\,\dot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\dot{\hat{e}}_3 = \vec{0}
$$

Comparison with $\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_i$, having set $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{e}_3$:
$$
\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_1 = \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_1 = \dot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_2 ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_2 = \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_2 = -\,\dot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_3 = \vec{0}
$$

Velocity and acceleration of $P$:
$$
\vec{v}_P = \frac{d}{dt}\,(a\,\hat{e}_1) = a\,\dot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{a}_P = a\,\ddot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_2 + a\,\dot{\vartheta}\;\dot{\hat{e}}_2 = a\,\ddot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_2 - a\,\dot{\vartheta}^{\,2}\;\hat{e}_1
$$

Check by the formula of the acceleration field:
$$
\vec{a}_P = \ddot{\vartheta}\,\hat{e}_3 \wedge (a\,\hat{e}_1) - \dot{\vartheta}^{\,2}\,(a\,\hat{e}_1) = a\,\ddot{\vartheta}\;\hat{e}_2 - a\,\dot{\vartheta}^{\,2}\;\hat{e}_1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The attached basis is obtained by rotating the fixed one by the angle $\vartheta$ about $z$, therefore the first two unit vectors have the components of the sine and of the cosine and the third coincides with $\hat{k}$. Differentiating with respect to time one finds expressions that are recognized as multiples of the unit vectors themselves.

The comparison with $\vec{\omega} \wedge \hat{e}_i$ uses the rules $\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_1 = \hat{e}_2$ and $\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_2 = -\hat{e}_1$, valid because the attached triad is orthonormal and right-handed. The results coincide with the derivatives computed, hence **Poisson's formulas** are verified with $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{e}_3$.

The point $P$ has constant components in the attached basis, therefore its velocity is computed by differentiating the unit vector alone: it equals $a\dot{\vartheta}$ and is orthogonal to $\Omega P$, as for a circular motion of radius $a$. Differentiating again and using Poisson once more one obtains the tangential term $a\ddot{\vartheta}$ and the centripetal one $a\dot{\vartheta}^{\,2}$, directed from $P$ toward $\Omega$.

The check applies the formula of the acceleration field of a rigid body with $\vec{a}_\Omega = \vec{0}$, and gives the same result: differentiating in the attached basis with Poisson's formulas and using the general formula are two routes to the same computation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rigid body with a fixed point $\Omega$ moves in such a way that the nutation angle stays constant, $\vartheta = 60^\circ$, whereas the precession and the proper rotation have constant velocities $\dot{\psi} = 2\omega_0$ and $\dot{\varphi} = \omega_0$, with $\omega_0 > 0$. Compute the components of the angular velocity in the attached basis at the instants at which $\varphi = 0$ and $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and the magnitude of $\vec{\omega}$.

::struttura{linee="0,0 3.2,0;0,0 0,2.8;0,0 -1.5,-1.1;0,0 1.1,2.3;0,0 2.6,-0.8" tratteggi="-1.5,-1.1 2.6,-0.8" punti="0,0,Ω" etichette="3.3,0.1,x;-1.9,-1.3,y;0.1,2.9,z;1.15,2.45,ζ;2.75,-0.85,N;0.45,0.5,ϑ;1.3,-0.35,ψ" riferimento=0 titolo="Euler angles: nutation $\vartheta$ constant, precession $\psi$ and proper rotation $\varphi$ with constant derivatives"}

$$
\begin{cases}
\omega_\xi = \dot{\psi}\sin\vartheta\sin\varphi + \dot{\vartheta}\cos\varphi \\[4pt]
\omega_\eta = \dot{\psi}\sin\vartheta\cos\varphi - \dot{\vartheta}\sin\varphi \\[4pt]
\omega_\zeta = \dot{\psi}\cos\vartheta + \dot{\varphi}
\end{cases}
$$

With $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$, $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{1}{2}$:
$$
\varphi = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\;
\qquad
\omega_\xi = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega_\eta = 2\omega_0\,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}\,\omega_0 ,
\qquad
\omega_\zeta = 2\omega_0\,\frac{1}{2} + \omega_0 = 2\,\omega_0
$$
$$
\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\;
\qquad
\omega_\xi = \sqrt{3}\,\omega_0 ,
\qquad
\omega_\eta = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega_\zeta = 2\,\omega_0
$$
$$
|\vec{\omega}| = \sqrt{3\omega_0^2 + 4\omega_0^2} = \sqrt{7}\;\omega_0
$$

Check by the formula of the magnitude in a regular precession:
$$
|\vec{\omega}|^2 = \dot{\psi}^{\,2} + \dot{\varphi}^{\,2} + 2\,\dot{\psi}\dot{\varphi}\cos\vartheta
= 4\omega_0^2 + \omega_0^2 + 2 \cdot 2\omega_0 \cdot \omega_0 \cdot \frac{1}{2}
= 7\,\omega_0^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is a **regular precession**: the nutation angle is constant, hence $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ and in the three components only the terms with $\dot{\psi}$ and $\dot{\varphi}$ remain.

In the two configurations considered the components along $\xi$ and $\eta$ exchange, because the factor $\sin\varphi$ and the factor $\cos\varphi$ exchange on passing from $\varphi = 0$ to $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The component along $\zeta$ does not depend on $\varphi$ and stays $2\omega_0$.

The magnitude is the same at the two instants, and this is understood from the decomposition $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\psi}\,\hat{k} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3$: the two vectors have constant magnitudes and form the constant angle $\vartheta$, hence their sum has constant magnitude as well.

The check computes the same magnitude by the general formula of the regular precession, which is obtained from the law of cosines applied to the two vectors $\dot{\psi}\,\hat{k}$ and $\dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3$. The two results coincide.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the rigid body of the previous exercise, with $\vartheta = 60^\circ$, $\dot{\psi} = 2\omega_0$ and $\dot{\varphi} = \omega_0$, determine the angle $\alpha$ that the axis of instantaneous rotation forms with the fixed axis $z$, the angle $\beta$ that it forms with the attached axis $\zeta$ and the velocity of the point $P$ of the axis $\zeta$ at distance $\ell$ from the fixed point.

::struttura{linee="0,0 0,3;0,0 2.6,1.5;0,0 0.78,2.27" tratteggi="0,0 -0.78,2.27;0,0 2.36,-0.46" punti="0,0,Ω" etichette="-0.3,3.1,z;2.7,1.55,ζ;0.75,2.45,ω;0.17,2.1,α;1.15,1.85,β;-1.6,2.3,fixed cone;2.1,-0.75,moving cone" riferimento=0 titolo="The axis of instantaneous rotation describes the fixed cone about $z$ and the moving cone about $\zeta$"}

$$
\vec{\omega} = \dot{\psi}\,\hat{k} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3 ,
\qquad
|\vec{\omega}| = \sqrt{7}\;\omega_0
$$
$$
\cos\alpha = \frac{\vec{\omega} \cdot \hat{k}}{|\vec{\omega}|} = \frac{\dot{\psi} + \dot{\varphi}\cos\vartheta}{|\vec{\omega}|}
= \frac{2\omega_0 + \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}}{\sqrt{7}\,\omega_0}
= \frac{5}{2\sqrt{7}} = \frac{5\sqrt{7}}{14} \approx 0.94
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\alpha \approx 19.11^\circ
$$
$$
\cos\beta = \frac{\vec{\omega} \cdot \hat{e}_3}{|\vec{\omega}|} = \frac{\dot{\psi}\cos\vartheta + \dot{\varphi}}{|\vec{\omega}|}
= \frac{\omega_0 + \omega_0}{\sqrt{7}\,\omega_0}
= \frac{2}{\sqrt{7}} = \frac{2\sqrt{7}}{7} \approx 0.76
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\beta \approx 40.89^\circ
$$

Velocity of the point $P$ with $P - \Omega = \ell\,\hat{e}_3$:
$$
\vec{v}_P = \vec{\omega} \wedge (\ell\,\hat{e}_3) = \ell\,\dot{\psi}\,\big( \hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_3 \big) ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_P| = \ell\,\dot{\psi}\sin\vartheta = 2\omega_0\,\ell\,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}\;\omega_0\,\ell
$$

Check by the sum of the angles:
$$
\alpha + \beta \approx 19.11^\circ + 40.89^\circ = 60^\circ = \vartheta
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a regular precession the angular velocity is the sum of two vectors of constant magnitude, $\dot{\psi}\,\hat{k}$ and $\dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3$, which form the constant angle $\vartheta$; hence $\vec{\omega}$ always belongs to the plane determined by the two axes and forms a constant angle with each of them.

The angles are obtained from the scalar products, using $\hat{k} \cdot \hat{e}_3 = \cos\vartheta$. The axis of instantaneous rotation passes through the fixed point $\Omega$, because the kinematic invariant vanishes when a point is at rest, and it has the direction of $\vec{\omega}$.

With respect to the fixed frame the axis turns about $z$ keeping the angle $\alpha$, therefore it describes a cone of half-angle $\alpha$, the **fixed cone**; with respect to the body it turns about $\zeta$ keeping the angle $\beta$ and describes the **moving cone**, of half-angle $\beta$. The motion is realized by rolling the moving cone without slipping on the fixed cone, because the points of the common generator are at rest.

The point $P$ lies on the axis $\zeta$, hence the term $\dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_3$ vanishes and only the contribution of the precession remains: $P$ describes a circle of radius $\ell\sin\vartheta$ about the axis $z$ with angular velocity $\dot{\psi}$.

The check is the sum $\alpha + \beta$, which must give back the angle $\vartheta$ between the two axes, since $\vec{\omega}$ lies between $\hat{k}$ and $\hat{e}_3$ in their plane. The check is satisfied.

</div>
