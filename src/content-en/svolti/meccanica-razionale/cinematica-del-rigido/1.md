## Exercise 1

The rigid rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $\ell$, moves in the vertical plane $Oxy$ and forms the angle of $30^\circ$ with the $x$ axis. At the instant considered the endpoint $A$ has velocity $\vec{v}_A = v\,\hat{\imath}$ and the rod has angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$, with $v > 0$ and $\omega > 0$. Compute the velocity of $B$ and determine the point of the plane attached to the rod that has zero velocity.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3.46,2" aste="A-B" punti="0,0;3.46,2" frecce="0,0,1.2,0,v_A" tratteggi="0,0 2,0" etichette="1.1,0.3,30°" coppie="2.6 0.85,1,ω" titolo="Rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ inclined at $30^\circ$, with $\vec{v}_A = v\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$"}

$$
B - A = \ell\cos 30^\circ\;\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin 30^\circ\;\hat{\jmath} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\ell\;\hat{\imath} + \frac{1}{2}\,\ell\;\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{\omega} \wedge (B - A) = \omega\,\hat{k} \wedge \left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\ell\;\hat{\imath} + \frac{1}{2}\,\ell\;\hat{\jmath} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega\ell\;\hat{\jmath} - \frac{1}{2}\,\omega\ell\;\hat{\imath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_B = \vec{v}_A + \vec{\omega} \wedge (B - A) = \left( v - \frac{\omega\ell}{2} \right)\hat{\imath} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega\ell\;\hat{\jmath}
$$

Point $C$ with $\vec{v}_C = \vec{0}$, having set $C - A = x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath}$:
$$
\begin{cases}
v - \omega y = 0 \\[4pt]
\omega x = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
C - A = \frac{v}{\omega}\;\hat{\jmath}
$$

Check by equiprojectivity:
$$
(\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A) \cdot (B - A) = \left( -\frac{\omega\ell}{2} \right) \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\ell + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega\ell \cdot \frac{\ell}{2} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body is rigid, therefore the velocity of $B$ is obtained from that of $A$ by the **fundamental formula of rigid kinematics**, in which the velocity of one point and the angular velocity appear. The vector $B - A$ is written with the components of the given angle, and the cross product is computed by means of $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{\imath} = \hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{\jmath} = -\hat{\imath}$.

The horizontal component of $\vec{v}_B$ is smaller than that of $\vec{v}_A$ and the vertical component is positive, since $\omega > 0$ and the counterclockwise rotation carries $B$ upward and to the left with respect to $A$.

The point with zero velocity is sought by imposing $\vec{v}_A + \vec{\omega} \wedge (C - A) = \vec{0}$, which in the plane is a system of two equations in the two unknowns $x$ and $y$. The second equation gives $x = 0$ and the first gives $y = \dfrac{v}{\omega}$, hence $C$ lies on the vertical line through $A$, that is, on the line through $A$ orthogonal to $\vec{v}_A$, as the construction of the instantaneous center of rotation predicts. The point $C$ belongs to the plane attached to the rod, whereas the segment $AB$ forms the angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal, therefore $C$ does not belong to the segment.

The check uses **equiprojectivity**: the difference $\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A$ must turn out to be orthogonal to $B - A$, and the scalar product vanishes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The same rod $AB$ of the previous exercise, inclined at $30^\circ$, has at the instant considered $\vec{v}_A = v\,\hat{\imath}$, $\vec{a}_A = a\,\hat{\imath}$, angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$ and angular acceleration $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \alpha\,\hat{k}$. Compute the acceleration of the endpoint $B$ and that of the midpoint $M$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3.46,2" aste="A-B" punti="0,0;3.46,2;1.73,1,M" frecce="0,0,1.2,0,v_A" reazioni="A,0.9,0,a_A" tratteggi="0,0 2,0" etichette="1.1,0.3,30°" coppie="2.4 0.6,1,α" titolo="Data of the exercise: $\vec{v}_A$, $\vec{a}_A$, $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$ and $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \alpha\,\hat{k}$"}

$$
\vec{a}_B = \vec{a}_A + \alpha\,\hat{k} \wedge (B - A) - \omega^2\,(B - A)
$$
$$
\alpha\,\hat{k} \wedge (B-A) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\alpha\ell\;\hat{\jmath} - \frac{1}{2}\,\alpha\ell\;\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
-\,\omega^2\,(B-A) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega^2\ell\;\hat{\imath} - \frac{1}{2}\,\omega^2\ell\;\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_B = \left( a - \frac{\alpha\ell}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega^2\ell \right)\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \sqrt{3}\,\alpha - \omega^2 \big)\;\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
M - A = \frac{1}{2}\,(B - A)
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\vec{a}_M = \vec{a}_A + \frac{1}{2}\,\big( \vec{a}_B - \vec{a}_A \big) = \frac{\vec{a}_A + \vec{a}_B}{2}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_M = \left( a - \frac{\alpha\ell}{4} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\,\omega^2\ell \right)\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{4}\,\big( \sqrt{3}\,\alpha - \omega^2 \big)\;\hat{\jmath}
$$

Check in the case $\alpha = 0$ and $a = 0$:
$$
\vec{a}_B = -\,\omega^2\,(B-A) ,
\qquad
|\vec{a}_B| = \omega^2\ell
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is plane, therefore the angular velocity is orthogonal to the vector $B - A$ and the vector triple product reduces to $-\omega^2\,(B-A)$. The acceleration of $B$ thus has three contributions: the acceleration of $A$, the tangential term $\alpha\,\hat{k} \wedge (B-A)$, orthogonal to the rod, and the centripetal term $-\omega^2\,(B-A)$, directed from $B$ toward $A$.

The midpoint $M$ lies at half of the vector $B - A$, and both the tangential term and the centripetal one depend linearly on $B - A$; therefore the acceleration of $M$ is the **average** of the accelerations of the endpoints. The same linearity holds for the velocities.

The check is made in the case in which $A$ is at rest with zero acceleration and the angular velocity is constant: the rod turns uniformly about $A$, hence $B$ travels along a circle of radius $\ell$ with angular velocity $\omega$ and its acceleration is purely centripetal, of magnitude $\omega^2\ell$ and directed toward the center $A$. The general result reduces precisely to this.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rigid body moves in space. At the instant considered its point $A$, coinciding with the origin $O$, has velocity $\vec{v}_A = v\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath} + \hat{k})$ and the angular velocity is $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,(2\,\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath} + 2\,\hat{k})$. Compute the velocity of the point $B$ of the body with $B - A = \ell\,\hat{\imath} + 2\ell\,\hat{k}$ and check equiprojectivity.

::struttura{linee="0,0 2.8,0;0,0 1.13,0.8;0,0 0,3;0,0 1.2,2.4" punti="0,0,A;1.2,2.4,B" frecce="0,0,1.3,1.19,v_A;-1.7,0.4,-0.95,1.21,ω" etichette="2.9,-0.05,x;1.25,0.9,y;0.05,3.1,z" riferimento=0 titolo="Axonometric view of the data: the point $A$ at the origin, the point $B$ and the vectors $\vec{v}_A$ and $\vec{\omega}$"}

$$
\vec{\omega} \wedge (B - A) = \omega\ell\,\big( (-1)\cdot 2 - 2 \cdot 0,\;\; 2 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 2,\;\; 2 \cdot 0 - (-1)\cdot 1 \big)
= \omega\ell\,\big( -2\,\hat{\imath} - 2\,\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k} \big)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_B = \vec{v}_A + \vec{\omega} \wedge (B-A) = (v - 2\omega\ell)\,\hat{\imath} + (v - 2\omega\ell)\,\hat{\jmath} + (v + \omega\ell)\,\hat{k}
$$

Check by equiprojectivity:
$$
(\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A) \cdot (B - A) = (-2\omega\ell)\,\ell + (-2\omega\ell)\cdot 0 + (\omega\ell)\,(2\ell) = -2\omega\ell^2 + 2\omega\ell^2 = 0
$$

Kinematic invariant:
$$
I = \vec{\omega} \cdot \vec{v}_A = \omega v\,(2 - 1 + 2) = 3\,\omega v ,
\qquad
|\vec{\omega}| = \omega\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3\,\omega
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fundamental formula holds in space without modifications, and the cross product is computed by means of the symbolic determinant. The first two components of the product equal $(-1)(2) - (2)(0) = -2$ and $(2)(1) - (2)(2) = -2$, the third equals $(2)(0) - (-1)(1) = 1$, all multiplied by $\omega\ell$.

Equiprojectivity is the check of the exercise: the difference $\vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A$ coincides with $\vec{\omega} \wedge (B-A)$, which is orthogonal to $B - A$ by the very definition of the cross product, therefore the scalar product vanishes. A value different from zero would signal an error in the computation of the components.

The kinematic invariant turns out to be different from zero, hence the state of motion is **helical** and no point of the body is at rest. The minimum velocity has magnitude $|\vec{v}_{\min}| = \dfrac{|I|}{|\vec{\omega}|} = \dfrac{3\,\omega v}{3\,\omega} = v$, and the search for the Mozzi axis on which it is attained is the subject of the sheet on states of motion in space.

</div>
