## Exercise 1

A particle of mass $m$ slides down the smooth and fixed inclined plane in the figure, which makes the angle $\alpha = 30^\circ$ with the horizontal. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at rest at $P_0$, at distance $d$ from the base $O$ measured along the line of steepest slope. Determine the acceleration, the reaction of the plane, the velocity with which the particle arrives at $O$ and the time taken.

::struttura{linee="0,0 5,2.887 5,0 0,0" nodi="O,0,0;P,3.248,1.875" punti="3.248,1.875" etichette="0.95,0.22,α" quote="O,P,d,-0.85" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle at rest on the smooth inclined plane at distance $d$ from the base, with $\alpha = 30^\circ$"}

$$
\sigma = \text{abscissa along the line of steepest slope, increasing downwards}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{\sigma} = m g\sin\alpha \\[4pt]
0 = \Phi - m g\cos\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\ddot{\sigma} = g\sin\alpha = \frac{g}{2} ,
\qquad
\Phi = m g\cos\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,m g
$$
$$
\sigma(t) = \frac{1}{2}\,g\sin\alpha\,t^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_O = \sqrt{\frac{2 d}{g\sin\alpha}} = 2\sqrt{\frac{d}{g}}
$$
$$
v_O = g\sin\alpha\,t_O = \sqrt{2 d g\sin\alpha} = \sqrt{g d}
$$

Check with the conservation of mechanical energy between $P_0$ and $O$, whose difference in height is $h = d\sin\alpha$:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_O^2 = m g\,d\sin\alpha
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_O = \sqrt{2 d g\sin\alpha} ,
$$
and with the limiting case $\alpha = 90^\circ$, in which free fall $v_O = \sqrt{2 g d}$ is recovered.

<div class="spiegazione">

The trajectory is rectilinear, hence the radius of curvature is infinite and the centripetal term of the intrinsic equations is zero. The first equation is the projection onto the line of steepest slope, where the weight has component $m g\sin\alpha$ and the reaction, **orthogonal** to the smooth plane, does not appear. The second equation is the projection onto the normal to the plane, where the weight has component $-m g\cos\alpha$ and the acceleration is zero.

The acceleration $g\sin\alpha$ is constant and does not depend on the mass, because both the weight and the inertia are proportional to $m$. The motion is therefore uniformly accelerated, and one integrates twice with the initial data $\sigma(0) = 0$ and $\dot{\sigma}(0) = 0$.

The time of descent is obtained by imposing $\sigma(t_O) = d$, and the final velocity is obtained by substituting $t_O$ into the velocity. With $\alpha = 30^\circ$ one has $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\cos\alpha = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, whence the numerical values of the coefficients.

The check is independent because it does not use the law of motion. The weight is conservative and the reaction of the smooth and fixed plane has zero power, hence the mechanical energy is conserved and the final velocity depends only on the difference in height $h = d\sin\alpha$. The limiting case $\alpha = 90^\circ$ gives back free fall, as must be the case.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle of mass $m$ rests against the free end of an ideal spring of constant $k$, placed along the line of steepest slope of a smooth and fixed inclined plane of angle $\alpha$, as in the figure. The other end of the spring is fixed to the base of the plane. At the instant $t = 0$ the spring has its rest length and the particle is at rest. Determine the equilibrium position, the period of the oscillations, the maximum compression of the spring and the maximum velocity of the particle.

::struttura{linee="0,0 5,3.75 5,0 0,0;0.22,0.54 0.58,0.06;0.4,0.3 0.45,0.65 1.15,0.55 1.25,1.25 1.95,1.15 2.05,1.85 2.75,1.75 2.8,2.1" nodi="P,2.8,2.1" punti="2.8,2.1" etichette="1.3,0.3,α;1.0,1.85,k" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle resting against the spring on the smooth inclined plane, in the configuration with the spring at rest"}

$$
s = \text{compression of the spring, measured downwards along the plane}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{s} = m g\sin\alpha - k\,s \\[4pt]
0 = \Phi - m g\cos\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
$$
s_e = \frac{m g\sin\alpha}{k} ,
\qquad
u = s - s_e
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ddot{u} + \frac{k}{m}\,u = 0 ,
\qquad
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
u(0) = -s_e ,
\quad
\dot{u}(0) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
s(t) = \frac{m g\sin\alpha}{k}\left( 1 - \cos\left( \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}\,t \right) \right)
$$
$$
s_{\max} = \frac{2 m g\sin\alpha}{k} ,
\qquad
v_{\max} = s_e\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = g\sin\alpha\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$

Check with the conservation of mechanical energy between the initial configuration and the configuration of maximum compression, in both of which the velocity is zero:
$$
m g\,s_{\max}\sin\alpha = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s_{\max}^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
s_{\max} = \frac{2 m g\sin\alpha}{k} ,
$$
and with the limiting case $\alpha \to 0$, in which $s_e \to 0$ and the particle remains at rest in the initial configuration.

<div class="spiegazione">

One takes as coordinate the compression $s$ of the spring, positive downwards along the plane. In this direction the weight has component $m g\sin\alpha$ and the spring reacts with $-k\,s$, because a compressed spring pushes the particle upwards along the plane. The reaction of the plane is orthogonal to it, therefore it does not appear in the first equation and is obtained from the second, where the normal acceleration is zero because the trajectory is rectilinear.

The equation contains the constant term $m g\sin\alpha$, hence it is not yet that of the harmonic oscillator. One therefore looks for the equilibrium configuration, by imposing $\ddot{s} = 0$: it turns out that $s_e = \dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{k}$. Setting $u = s - s_e$ the constant term disappears and the equation becomes $\ddot{u} + \dfrac{k}{m}\,u = 0$, therefore the motion is harmonic about the equilibrium configuration with period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$, independent of the inclination.

The particle starts from rest with the spring at rest length, that is, at distance $s_e$ from the equilibrium; the amplitude is therefore $s_e$ and the compression oscillates between $0$ and $2 s_e$. The maximum compression is therefore **twice** the equilibrium one, and the maximum velocity is reached at the passage through the equilibrium configuration.

The check uses the energy balance between the two configurations in which the velocity is zero: the work of the weight along the descent $s_{\max}$ is $m g\,s_{\max}\sin\alpha$ and the energy stored by the spring is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,s_{\max}^2$. Equating them one recovers the same maximum compression.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle of mass $m$ hangs at $O$ from an inextensible massless string of length $\ell$, and moves in the vertical plane $Oxy$. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at rest with the string taut, inclined by $\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ with respect to the downward vertical, as in the figure. Determine the velocity at the lowest point, the tension of the string as a function of $\vartheta$, its maximum value and the period of the small oscillations about the equilibrium position.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,3.4;P,2.252,2.1" catene="O-P" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,3.4 0,1.2" etichette="0.35,2.45,ϑ_0" quote="O,P,ℓ,-0.6" punti="2.252,2.1" riferimento=0 titolo="Simple pendulum of length $\ell$, at rest with the string inclined by $\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ell\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -m g\sin\vartheta \\[4pt]
m\,\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \Phi - m g\cos\vartheta
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 - m g \ell\cos\vartheta = -m g \ell\cos\vartheta_0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = 2 g \ell\,( \cos\vartheta - \cos\vartheta_0 )
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \ell\,|\dot{\vartheta}|_{\vartheta = 0} = \sqrt{2 g \ell\,( 1 - \cos\vartheta_0 )} = \sqrt{g\,\ell}
$$
$$
\Phi(\vartheta) = m g\cos\vartheta + m\,\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = m g\,( 3\cos\vartheta - 2\cos\vartheta_0 ) = m g\,( 3\cos\vartheta - 1 )
$$
$$
\Phi_{\max} = \Phi(0) = 2 m g ,
\qquad
T_{\text{small}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}}
$$

Check with the value of the tension in the starting position, where the velocity is zero: the second equation gives $\Phi(\vartheta_0) = m g\cos\vartheta_0$, and the general formula gives
$$
\Phi(\vartheta_0) = m g\,( 3\cos\vartheta_0 - 2\cos\vartheta_0 ) = m g\cos\vartheta_0 ,
$$
that is, the same value, which with $\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ is $\dfrac{m g}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible and taut, therefore the particle travels along the circle of center $O$ and radius $\ell$; one takes as coordinate the angle $\vartheta$ with the downward vertical. The arc length is $s = \ell\,\vartheta$ and the radius of curvature is $\ell$, constant.

The first equation is the projection onto the tangent: the weight has component $-m g\sin\vartheta$ and the tension, directed along the string, does not appear. The second is the projection onto the principal normal, directed from $P$ towards $O$: the tension enters with the positive sign because the string pulls towards $O$, the weight with $-m g\cos\vartheta$, and the left-hand side is the mass times the centripetal acceleration $\dfrac{v^2}{\ell} = \ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$.

The weight is conservative and the tension has zero power, since it is orthogonal to the velocity; therefore the mechanical energy is conserved. Imposing $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ at $\vartheta_0$ one determines the constant and obtains $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ as a function of $\vartheta$. The modulus of the velocity is maximum at the lowest point, where $\cos\vartheta = 1$.

Substituting $\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = 2 g\,(\cos\vartheta - \cos\vartheta_0)$ into the second equation one obtains the tension as a function of the angle alone. It is maximum at the lowest point, where it equals $2 m g$: the string must support **twice** the weight, because besides balancing the weight it provides the centripetal force. For $|\vartheta|$ small one replaces $\sin\vartheta$ with $\vartheta$ and the first equation becomes that of the harmonic oscillator of angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{\ell}}$.

The check compares two routes for the tension in the initial position: the second equation with $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ and the general formula evaluated at $\vartheta_0$. The two values coincide, hence the expression of $\Phi(\vartheta)$ is correct.

</div>
