## Exercise 1

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure. It is connected to the wall by an ideal spring of constant $k$ and by a damper that exerts the force $-h\,\dot{x}\,\hat{\imath}$, with $h = \sqrt{m k}$; the abscissa $x$ is measured from the position in which the spring has its rest length. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at rest at $x = d > 0$. Determine the type of motion, the pseudo angular frequency, the pseudo-period, the law of motion and the ratio between two successive maxima of the elongation.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;P,3.6,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180" linee="0,0 0.3,0.35 0.9,-0.35 1.5,0.35 2.1,-0.35 2.7,0.35 3.3,-0.35 3.6,0;3.6,0 6.2,0;3.6,0 3.6,-0.8;0,-0.8 0.6,-0.8;1.2,-0.8 3.6,-0.8;0.6,-1.15 1.8,-1.15 1.8,-0.45 0.6,-0.45 0.6,-1.15;1.2,-1.05 1.2,-0.55" punti="3.6,0" etichette="1.8,0.75,k;1.2,-1.5,h" tratteggi="2.6,-0.3 2.6,0.75" quote="2.6 0,P,d,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth guide with a spring of constant $k$ and a damper of constant $h$"}

$$
m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x - h\,\dot{x}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\,\dot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} ,
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{h}{2m} = \frac{\omega_0}{2}
$$
$$
\gamma < \omega_0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{underdamping}
$$
$$
\omega_s = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega_0 ,
\qquad
T_s = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_s} = \frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}\,\omega_0} = \frac{4\sqrt{3}\,\pi}{3}\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
x(t) = d\,e^{-\gamma t}\left( \cos(\omega_s t) + \frac{\gamma}{\omega_s}\sin(\omega_s t) \right)
= d\,e^{-\frac{\omega_0}{2}t}\left( \cos(\omega_s t) + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\sin(\omega_s t) \right)
$$
$$
\frac{x(t + T_s)}{x(t)} = e^{-\gamma T_s} = e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}} = e^{-\frac{2\sqrt{3}\,\pi}{3}} \approx 0.027
$$

Check with the initial acceleration. Differentiating the law of motion one obtains
$$
\dot{x}(t) = -d\,\frac{\omega_0^2}{\omega_s}\,e^{-\gamma t}\sin(\omega_s t) ,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\dot{x}(0) = 0 ,
\qquad
\ddot{x}(0) = -d\,\omega_0^2 ,
$$
and the same value is obtained from the equation of motion evaluated at $t = 0$, where $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ and $x(0) = d$ give $\ddot{x}(0) = -\omega_0^2\,d$.

<div class="spiegazione">

On the particle there act the elastic force $-k\,x$ and the force of the damper $-h\,\dot{x}$, while the weight is balanced by the reaction of the smooth guide. Dividing by $m$ one obtains the equation of the damped oscillator, with $\omega_0$ the natural angular frequency and $\gamma$ the damping coefficient.

With $h = \sqrt{m k}$ it turns out that $\gamma = \dfrac{\sqrt{m k}}{2m} = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$, hence $\gamma$ is *smaller* than $\omega_0$ and the damping is weak, therefore the particle oscillates with an amplitude that decreases exponentially. The pseudo angular frequency is $\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$, that is, $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega_0$, therefore the pseudo-period is larger than the period that one would have without the damper, in the ratio $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$.

The law of motion is obtained from the general form $e^{-\gamma t}\,(c_1\cos\omega_s t + c_2\sin\omega_s t)$ by imposing the initial data: from $x(0) = d$ it follows that $c_1 = d$, and from $\dot{x}(0) = -\gamma c_1 + \omega_s c_2 = 0$ it follows that $c_2 = \dfrac{\gamma\,d}{\omega_s}$, that is, $\dfrac{d}{\sqrt{3}}$.

The factor $e^{-\gamma t}$ multiplies a periodic function of period $T_s$, therefore two values separated by $T_s$ are in the constant ratio $e^{-\gamma T_s}$, which here equals about $0.027$: after a single oscillation the elongation has been reduced to less than a thirtieth.

The check compares two routes for the initial acceleration. Differentiating the law of motion the cosine terms cancel, a single sine term remains and one reads $\ddot{x}(0) = -d\,\omega_0^2$. The same value is obtained directly from the equation of motion, because at the initial instant the velocity is zero and only the elastic force $-k\,d$ acts.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The system is the one of the previous exercise, with the same mass $m$, the same spring $k$ and the same initial data $x(0) = d$, $\dot{x}(0) = 0$. Determine the value $h_c$ of the constant of the damper for which the damping is critical, the law of motion in that case and the law of motion when $h = \dfrac{5}{2}\sqrt{m k}$, saying in both cases whether the particle crosses the origin.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;P,3.6,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180" linee="0,0 0.3,0.35 0.9,-0.35 1.5,0.35 2.1,-0.35 2.7,0.35 3.3,-0.35 3.6,0;3.6,0 6.2,0;3.6,0 3.6,-0.8;0,-0.8 0.6,-0.8;1.2,-0.8 3.6,-0.8;0.6,-1.15 1.8,-1.15 1.8,-0.45 0.6,-0.45 0.6,-1.15;1.2,-1.05 1.2,-0.55" punti="3.6,0" etichette="1.8,0.75,k;1.2,-1.5,h" tratteggi="2.6,-0.3 2.6,0.75" quote="2.6 0,P,d,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="The same system, with the constant $h$ of the damper to be determined"}

$$
\gamma = \omega_0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\frac{h}{2m} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
h_c = 2\sqrt{m k}
$$
$$
x(t) = (c_1 + c_2\,t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t} ,
\qquad
x(0) = d ,
\quad
\dot{x}(0) = c_2 - \omega_0\,c_1 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x(t) = d\,( 1 + \omega_0\,t )\,e^{-\omega_0 t}
$$
$$
h = \frac{5}{2}\sqrt{m k}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\gamma = \frac{5}{4}\,\omega_0 ,
\qquad
\lambda_{1,2} = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2} = -\frac{\omega_0}{2} ,\ -2\,\omega_0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
c_1 + c_2 = d \\[4pt]
-\dfrac{\omega_0}{2}\,c_1 - 2\,\omega_0\,c_2 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
c_1 = \frac{4 d}{3} ,
\qquad
c_2 = -\frac{d}{3}
$$
$$
x(t) = \frac{4 d}{3}\,e^{-\frac{\omega_0}{2}t} - \frac{d}{3}\,e^{-2\,\omega_0 t}
$$

Check with the initial acceleration, which by the equation of motion with $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ must equal $-\omega_0^2\,d$ in both cases:
$$
\ddot{x}(0)\Big|_{\text{critical}} = d\,\big( \omega_0^2 - 2\,\omega_0^2 \big) = -d\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
\ddot{x}(0)\Big|_{h = \frac{5}{2}\sqrt{mk}} = \frac{4 d}{3}\cdot\frac{\omega_0^2}{4} - \frac{d}{3}\cdot 4\,\omega_0^2 = -d\,\omega_0^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The damping is critical when the discriminant of the characteristic equation vanishes, that is, when $\gamma = \omega_0$. Substituting $\gamma = \dfrac{h}{2m}$ and $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ and solving for $h$ one obtains $h_c = 2\sqrt{m k}$.

In the critical case the root $-\omega_0$ is double, therefore the general integral contains also the term $t\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$. The initial data give $c_1 = d$ and $c_2 = \omega_0\,d$. The function $d\,(1 + \omega_0 t)\,e^{-\omega_0 t}$ is positive for every $t \geq 0$, hence the particle **does not cross** the origin and only tends to it as $t \to +\infty$.

With $h = \dfrac{5}{2}\sqrt{m k}$ it turns out that $\gamma = \dfrac{5}{4}\,\omega_0 > \omega_0$, hence the damping is strong. The radical equals $\sqrt{\dfrac{25}{16} - 1}\,\omega_0 = \dfrac{3}{4}\,\omega_0$, whence the two real and negative roots. The initial data give the system of two equations in the constants $c_1$ and $c_2$, solved by substitution.

In this case too the particle does not cross the origin: $x(t) = 0$ requires $4\,e^{-\frac{\omega_0}{2}t} = e^{-2\omega_0 t}$, that is, $e^{\frac{3}{2}\omega_0 t} = \dfrac{1}{4}$, which has no solutions with $t \geq 0$ because the left-hand side is greater than or equal to $1$. For the same initial data, strong damping brings the particle back towards the origin more slowly than critical damping, because the dominant term decays as $e^{-\frac{\omega_0}{2}t}$ instead of as $e^{-\omega_0 t}$.

The check is the same for the two cases: the equation of motion with $x(0) = d$ and $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ imposes $\ddot{x}(0) = -\omega_0^2\,d$, whatever $h$ is. Differentiating the two laws of motion twice one obtains the same value, therefore the constants are correct.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The system is the one of exercise 1, with $h = \sqrt{m k}$, and on the particle there acts also the force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F_0 > 0$, as in the figure. Determine the amplitude of the steady-state motion as a function of $\Omega$, the resonant angular frequency, the maximum amplitude and the comparison with the amplitude obtained for $\Omega = \omega_0$ and for $\Omega$ close to zero.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;P,3.6,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180" linee="0,0 0.3,0.35 0.9,-0.35 1.5,0.35 2.1,-0.35 2.7,0.35 3.3,-0.35 3.6,0;3.6,0 6.2,0;3.6,0 3.6,-0.8;0,-0.8 0.6,-0.8;1.2,-0.8 3.6,-0.8;0.6,-1.15 1.8,-1.15 1.8,-0.45 0.6,-0.45 0.6,-1.15;1.2,-1.05 1.2,-0.55" punti="3.6,0" forze="4.9 0,1.3,0," etichette="1.8,0.75,k;1.2,-1.5,h;4.4,0.42,F_0 cos(Ωt)" riferimento=0 titolo="The same system with the forcing $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$ applied to the particle"}

$$
\ddot{x} + 2\gamma\,\dot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos(\Omega t) ,
\qquad
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} ,
\qquad
\gamma = \frac{\omega_0}{2}
$$
$$
A(\Omega) = \frac{\dfrac{F_0}{m}}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2)^2 + 4\gamma^2\,\Omega^2}}
= \frac{\dfrac{F_0}{m}}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \Omega^2)^2 + \omega_0^2\,\Omega^2}}
$$
$$
\Omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2} = \frac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\omega_0
$$
$$
A(\Omega_r) = \frac{F_0}{2m\,\gamma\sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}}
= \frac{F_0}{2m\cdot\dfrac{\omega_0}{2}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\omega_0}
= \frac{2 F_0}{\sqrt{3}\,m\,\omega_0^2}
= \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}\,\frac{F_0}{k} \approx 1.155\,\frac{F_0}{k}
$$
$$
A(\omega_0) = \frac{\dfrac{F_0}{m}}{\sqrt{\omega_0^2\,\omega_0^2}} = \frac{F_0}{m\,\omega_0^2} = \frac{F_0}{k} ,
\qquad
\lim_{\Omega \to 0^+} A(\Omega) = \frac{F_0}{m\,\omega_0^2} = \frac{F_0}{k}
$$

Check with the static case: a constant force $F_0$ applied to the particle brings it to the equilibrium position in which the spring balances it, that is, to $x = \dfrac{F_0}{k}$. The value $\displaystyle\lim_{\Omega \to 0^+} A(\Omega) = \dfrac{F_0}{k}$ coincides with this static displacement, as must be the case, because for small $\Omega$ the forcing varies slowly.

<div class="spiegazione">

To the equation of exercise 1 one adds the forcing term $\dfrac{F_0}{m}\cos(\Omega t)$. The general integral of the homogeneous equation tends to zero, because the damping is weak but present; therefore after a transient only the steady-state motion remains, which has the same angular frequency as the forcing and amplitude $A(\Omega)$.

With $\gamma = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$ the term $4\gamma^2\Omega^2$ becomes $\omega_0^2\,\Omega^2$, and the condition $\gamma < \dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$ is satisfied, because $\dfrac{1}{2} < \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$; there exists therefore a resonant angular frequency, which equals $\dfrac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{2}}$ and is *smaller* than the natural angular frequency.

The maximum amplitude is obtained by substituting $\Omega_r$ into the expression of $A$, and equals $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$ times the static displacement $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$. With this damping the resonance is therefore not very pronounced, since the maximum amplitude exceeds the static one only by about $15\%$. The amplitude at $\Omega = \omega_0$ coincides with the static one, because with $\gamma = \dfrac{\omega_0}{2}$ the denominator equals $\omega_0^2$ in both cases.

The check compares the limit of $A(\Omega)$ as $\Omega \to 0^+$ with the displacement produced by a constant force $F_0$, which is computed directly from the balance between the applied force and the elastic force. The two values coincide, therefore the expression of the amplitude is correct.

</div>
