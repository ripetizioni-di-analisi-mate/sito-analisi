## Exercise 1

A particle of mass $m$ rests at the vertex $S$ of a smooth and fixed spherical cap of radius $R$, with the center $C$ on the vertical, as in the figure. The constraint is unilateral, because the cap can only push the particle outwards. The particle is released with negligible velocity and slides along a meridian. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle between the reversed $C - S$ and the segment joining $C$ to the particle, that is, the angle measured from the upward vertical, determine the constraint reaction as a function of $\vartheta$, the angle of detachment, the velocity and the height at that instant.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" nodi="C,0,0;S,0,2" linee="-2.6,-2 2.6,-2" punti="1.286,1.532,P;0,2;0,0" tratteggi="0,0 0,2.55;0,0 1.64,1.954" etichette="0.3,1.35,ϑ" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle sliding on the smooth spherical cap of radius $R$, having started from rest at the vertex $S$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
m\,R\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m g\sin\vartheta \\[4pt]
m\,\dfrac{v^2}{R} = m g\cos\vartheta - \Phi
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 = m g\,( R - R\cos\vartheta )
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v^2 = 2 g R\,( 1 - \cos\vartheta )
$$
$$
\Phi(\vartheta) = m g\cos\vartheta - \frac{m\,v^2}{R} = m g\,( 3\cos\vartheta - 2 )
$$
$$
\Phi(\vartheta_d) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\cos\vartheta_d = \frac{2}{3} ,
\qquad
\vartheta_d = \arccos\frac{2}{3} \approx 48.19^\circ
$$
$$
v_d^2 = 2 g R\left( 1 - \frac{2}{3} \right) = \frac{2 g R}{3} ,
\qquad
v_d = \sqrt{\frac{2 g R}{3}} ,
\qquad
y_d = R\cos\vartheta_d = \frac{2 R}{3}
$$

Check of the velocity of detachment with the normal equation alone. At the instant of detachment the reaction is zero, therefore the normal component of the weight alone provides the centripetal acceleration:
$$
m\,\frac{v_d^2}{R} = m g\cos\vartheta_d = \frac{2}{3}\,m g
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_d^2 = \frac{2 g R}{3} ,
$$
that is, the same value obtained from the conservation of energy, which is a relation independent of the normal equation.

<div class="spiegazione">

As long as the particle remains on the cap its trajectory is an arc of a circle of radius $R$, therefore the intrinsic equations hold with radius of curvature $R$. The first equation is the projection onto the tangent, where the weight has component $m g\sin\vartheta$ in the direction of increasing $\vartheta$ and the reaction does not appear, because the cap is smooth. The second is the projection onto the principal normal, directed towards the center $C$: the weight has component $m g\cos\vartheta$ towards the center and the reaction $\Phi$, which pushes outwards, enters with the opposite sign.

The weight is conservative and the reaction of a smooth and fixed constraint has zero power, hence the mechanical energy is conserved. The height of the particle is $R\cos\vartheta$, therefore the descent is $R\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)$ and one obtains at once $v^2$ as a function of the angle, without integrating the tangential equation.

Substituting $v^2$ into the second equation one obtains $\Phi(\vartheta) = m g\,(3\cos\vartheta - 2)$, which decreases as $\vartheta$ grows and vanishes for $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{2}{3}$. The constraint is unilateral, hence it can only push and $\Phi \geq 0$ must hold: for $\vartheta > \vartheta_d$ the formula would give a negative reaction, therefore the particle **detaches** at $\vartheta_d$. The angle of detachment depends neither on $m$ nor on $R$.

After the detachment the particle moves as a free body under gravity, with the position and the velocity reached at the instant of detachment as initial data, therefore it describes an arc of a parabola.

The check uses the normal equation alone with $\Phi = 0$, whereas the value of $v_d$ had been obtained from the energy integral; the two routes give the same result. A second check is the initial value $\Phi(0) = m g$, which is the reaction that the cap exerts on a particle at rest at the vertex.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle of mass $m$ slides inside a smooth and fixed circular guide of radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane, as in the figure. The guide is unilateral, because it can only push the particle towards the center $C$. The particle starts from the lowest point $A$ with horizontal velocity $v_0$. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle measured from the downward vertical, determine the constraint reaction as a function of $\vartheta$, the minimum value of $v_0$ for which the particle completes the full loop and the reaction at $A$ in that case.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" nodi="C,0,0;A,0,-2" punti="0,-2;0,0;1.667,1.106,P" frecce="0.2,-2.25,1.7,-2.25,v_0" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.55;0,0 1.667,1.106" etichette="0.62,-0.55,ϑ" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle inside a smooth circular guide of radius $R$, launched from $A$ with velocity $v_0$"}

$$
m\,\frac{v^2}{R} = \Phi - m g\cos\vartheta
\qquad\text{(projection onto the normal, directed towards }C\text{)}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = -m g R\,( 1 - \cos\vartheta )
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v^2 = v_0^2 - 2 g R\,( 1 - \cos\vartheta )
$$
$$
\Phi(\vartheta) = \frac{m\,v^2}{R} + m g\cos\vartheta = \frac{m\,v_0^2}{R} - 2 m g + 3 m g\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
\Phi(\pi) = \frac{m\,v_0^2}{R} - 5 m g \geq 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_0^2 \geq 5 g R ,
\qquad
v_{0,\min} = \sqrt{5 g R}
$$
$$
\Phi(0)\Big|_{v_0^2 = 5 g R} = \frac{m \cdot 5 g R}{R} - 2 m g + 3 m g = 6 m g
$$

Check of the condition for the full loop with the normal equation alone at the highest point. There the velocity is $v_{\text{top}}$ and the normal is directed downwards, therefore
$$
m\,\frac{v_{\text{top}}^2}{R} = \Phi + m g
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\Phi \geq 0 \iff v_{\text{top}}^2 \geq g R ,
$$
and the conservation of energy gives $v_0^2 = v_{\text{top}}^2 + 4 g R \geq 5 g R$, that is, the same condition.

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide is smooth, hence the reaction is orthogonal to it, that is, radial; moreover it is unilateral and can only push towards the center, therefore the condition to be imposed is $\Phi \geq 0$. The projection onto the tangent is not needed, because the velocity is obtained from the conservation of energy, since the weight is conservative and the reaction has zero power.

The height of the particle with respect to $A$ is $R\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)$, therefore the potential energy grows with $\vartheta$ up to the highest point. Substituting $v^2$ into the normal equation one obtains $\Phi(\vartheta)$, which is a decreasing function of $\vartheta$ on the interval $[0, \pi]$, since $\cos\vartheta$ decreases. The smallest value therefore occurs at the highest point, and it suffices to impose the sign condition there.

The condition $v_0 \geq \sqrt{5 g R}$ is more restrictive than the simple requirement that the particle reach the top with non-negative velocity, which would give $v_0 \geq 2\sqrt{g R}$. In order to stay in contact with the guide the particle must reach the top with centripetal acceleration $\dfrac{v^2}{R}$ at least equal to $g$, since the reaction provides only the part that exceeds the weight.

With the minimum value of $v_0$ the reaction at the lowest point equals $6 m g$, that is, six times the weight, therefore the most stressed stretch of the guide is the lower one, where the reaction must balance the weight and at the same time provide the maximum centripetal acceleration.

The check repeats the computation at the highest point using directly the normal equation written there, with the normal turned downwards, and the conservation of energy between $A$ and the highest point. The same condition is recovered, hence the expression of $\Phi(\vartheta)$ is correct.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A bead of mass $m$ is threaded on a smooth and fixed circular guide of radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane, as in the figure. The constraint is now **bilateral**, because the guide can push the bead both towards the center and outwards. The bead starts from the lowest point $A$ with velocity $v_0$ such that $v_0^2 = \dfrac{9}{2}\,g R$. Determine the constraint reaction as a function of $\vartheta$, verify that the bead completes the full loop and find the position at which the reaction changes direction.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" nodi="C,0,0;A,0,-2" punti="0,-2;0,0;-1.667,1.106,P" frecce="0.2,-2.25,1.7,-2.25,v_0" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.55;0,0 -1.667,1.106" etichette="-0.62,-0.55,ϑ" riferimento=0 titolo="Bead threaded on a smooth circular guide, launched from $A$ with velocity $v_0$"}

$$
v^2(\vartheta) = v_0^2 - 2 g R\,( 1 - \cos\vartheta ) = \frac{9}{2}\,g R - 2 g R + 2 g R\cos\vartheta
= g R\left( \frac{5}{2} + 2\cos\vartheta \right)
$$
$$
v^2(\pi) = g R\left( \frac{5}{2} - 2 \right) = \frac{g R}{2} > 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{the full loop takes place}
$$
$$
\Phi(\vartheta) = \frac{m\,v^2}{R} + m g\cos\vartheta = m g\left( \frac{5}{2} + 2\cos\vartheta \right) + m g\cos\vartheta
= m g\left( \frac{5}{2} + 3\cos\vartheta \right)
$$
$$
\Phi(\vartheta^*) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\cos\vartheta^* = -\frac{5}{6} ,
\qquad
\vartheta^* = \arccos\left( -\frac{5}{6} \right) \approx 146.44^\circ
$$
$$
\Phi(0) = \frac{11}{2}\,m g ,
\qquad
\Phi(\pi) = -\frac{1}{2}\,m g
$$

Check with the limiting case $v_0^2 = 5 g R$, in which the unilateral guide of the previous exercise is just sufficient. The general formula $\Phi(\vartheta) = \dfrac{m\,v_0^2}{R} - 2 m g + 3 m g\cos\vartheta$ gives
$$
\cos\vartheta^* = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{v_0^2}{3 g R} = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{5}{3} = -1 ,
$$
that is, $\vartheta^* = \pi$: the reaction vanishes exactly at the highest point, in agreement with the result of exercise 2.

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide is smooth, hence the reaction remains radial; being bilateral, its component $\Phi$ towards the center can have any sign. The conservation of energy gives the velocity as a function of the angle, as in the previous exercise, and the substitution into the normal equation gives the reaction.

To establish whether the bead completes the loop it suffices to check that $v^2$ remains positive, because with a bilateral constraint there is no detachment: the minimum value of $v^2$ occurs at the highest point and turns out to be $\dfrac{g R}{2} > 0$, therefore the bead passes the highest point and the motion is a complete rotation.

The reaction $\Phi(\vartheta) = m g\left( \dfrac{5}{2} + 3\cos\vartheta \right)$ vanishes for $\cos\vartheta^* = -\dfrac{5}{6}$, that is, at about $146^\circ$ from the downward vertical. For $\vartheta < \vartheta^*$ the reaction is positive and the guide pushes the bead towards the center, as an outer wall would do; for $\vartheta > \vartheta^*$ the reaction is negative and the guide pushes outwards, with the inner wall. At the highest point it equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,m g$, therefore there the bead is supported by the inner side of the guide.

The check examines the case in which the two exercises must give the same result: with $v_0^2 = 5 g R$ the reaction vanishes only at the highest point, which is exactly the condition of imminent detachment found for the unilateral guide.

</div>
