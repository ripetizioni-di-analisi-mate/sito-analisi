## Exercise 1

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure, in the vertical plane $Oxy$. The particle is connected to the wall by an ideal spring of elastic constant $k$ and rest length $\ell_0$; the abscissa $x$ is measured from the position in which the spring has its rest length. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at rest at $x = d > 0$. Determine the equation of motion, the period of the oscillations, the law of motion and the maximum velocity reached.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;P,3.6,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180" linee="0,0 0.3,0.35 0.9,-0.35 1.5,0.35 2.1,-0.35 2.7,0.35 3.3,-0.35 3.6,0;3.6,0 6.4,0" punti="3.6,0" etichette="1.8,0.75,k;2.6,-0.95,x = 0" tratteggi="2.6,-0.75 2.6,0.75" quote="2.6 0,P,d,-0.45" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth horizontal guide pulled back by a spring of constant $k$"}

$$
m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ddot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_0} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
x(0) = d ,
\quad
\dot{x}(0) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x(t) = d\cos\big( \omega_0\,t \big) ,
\qquad
\dot{x}(t) = -d\,\omega_0\sin\big( \omega_0\,t \big)
$$
$$
v_{\max} = d\,\omega_0 = d\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

Check with the conservation of mechanical energy between the initial position and the passage through $x = 0$:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,k\,d^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_{\max}^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_{\max} = d\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} ,
$$
which coincides with the value obtained from the law of motion.

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring is ideal, hence it exerts on the particle the force $-k\,x\,\hat{\imath}$, proportional to the elongation and directed towards the position of rest length. The weight is balanced by the reaction of the guide, which is smooth and therefore orthogonal to the guide; along $x$ only the elastic force acts, and the equation of motion is that of the **harmonic oscillator**.

The natural angular frequency $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ grows with the stiffness of the spring and decreases with the mass, therefore the period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$ gets longer if the particle is heavier. The period does not depend on $d$: the initial amplitude changes the velocity but not the duration of an oscillation.

The initial data determine the solution: from $x(0) = d$ and $\dot{x}(0) = 0$ one finds that the solution is a cosine of amplitude $d$ and zero phase. The modulus of the velocity is maximum when $|\sin(\omega_0 t)| = 1$, that is, at the passage through $x = 0$, and equals $d\,\omega_0$.

The check uses another route: the potential of the spring is $U = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$, hence the potential energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$, the reaction of the smooth and fixed guide has zero power and the mechanical energy is conserved. In the initial position the energy is entirely potential, at the passage through the origin it is entirely kinetic, therefore the two values are equal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An ideal spring of constant $k$ and rest length $\ell_0$ hangs from the ceiling at the point $A$; at the other end a particle of mass $m$ is attached, which moves along the vertical. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at rest in the position in which the spring has length $\ell_0$. Determine the equilibrium position, the period of the oscillations, the maximum elongation of the spring, the maximum velocity and the maximum tension of the spring.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,4.4;P,0,1.6" vincoli="A,incastro,90" linee="0,4.4 0.22,4.17 -0.22,3.73 0.22,3.29 -0.22,2.85 0.22,2.63 0,2.4;0,2.4 0,1.6" punti="0,1.6" etichette="0.5,3.4,k" tratteggi="-0.9,1.6 0.9,1.6" quote="A,P,ℓ_0,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Vertical spring of rest length $\ell_0$ with the particle $P$ in the initial position"}

$$
s = \text{elongation of the spring, measured downwards}
$$
$$
m\,\ddot{s} = m g - k\,s
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ddot{s} + \frac{k}{m}\left( s - \frac{m g}{k} \right) = 0
$$
$$
\delta = \frac{m g}{k} ,
\qquad
u = s - \delta
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ddot{u} + \omega_0^2\,u = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} ,
\qquad
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
u(0) = -\delta ,
\quad
\dot{u}(0) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
u(t) = -\delta\cos\big( \omega_0\,t \big) ,
\qquad
s(t) = \frac{m g}{k}\Big( 1 - \cos\big( \omega_0\,t \big) \Big)
$$
$$
s_{\max} = 2\,\delta = \frac{2 m g}{k} ,
\qquad
v_{\max} = \delta\,\omega_0 = g\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} ,
\qquad
\Phi_{\max} = k\,s_{\max} = 2 m g
$$

Check with the conservation of mechanical energy between the initial position and the position of maximum elongation, where the velocity is zero:
$$
0 = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s_{\max}^2 - m g\,s_{\max}
= \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\frac{4 m^2 g^2}{k^2} - m g\,\frac{2 m g}{k}
= \frac{2 m^2 g^2}{k} - \frac{2 m^2 g^2}{k} = 0 ,
$$
hence the value $s_{\max} = \dfrac{2 m g}{k}$ is compatible with the initial mechanical energy, which is zero with this choice of the reference level for the potential energy.

<div class="spiegazione">

One chooses as coordinate the elongation $s$ of the spring, positive downwards. In this direction the weight has component $m g$ and the elastic force has component $-k\,s$, because the spring pulls upwards when it is stretched. No other force is applied to the particle, since the motion is vertical and there is no guide.

The equation obtained is not that of the harmonic oscillator, because it contains the constant term $m g$. One eliminates the constant term by translating the coordinate: one sets $u = s - \delta$ with $\delta = \dfrac{m g}{k}$, and the value $\delta$ is exactly the **static elongation**, that is, the elongation for which the elastic force balances the weight. In the new coordinate the equation is that of the harmonic oscillator, therefore the motion is harmonic about the equilibrium position and the period is the same as it would be without the weight.

The particle starts from the position of rest length, which is at distance $\delta$ from the equilibrium position, and starts from rest; therefore the amplitude is $\delta$ and the elongation oscillates between $0$ and $2\delta$. The maximum tension of the spring therefore equals $2 m g$, that is, twice the weight supported, so releasing the load from rest on the unstretched spring doubles the stress with respect to the case in which it is accompanied down to the equilibrium position.

The check computes the mechanical energy in the configuration of maximum elongation, where the velocity is zero, and compares it with the initial one, which also has zero velocity and an unstretched spring. The total potential energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2 - m g\,s$, therefore both configurations have zero energy, as must be the case.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure and is connected to the two walls by two ideal springs, of constants $k$ and $2k$. In the configuration of the drawing both springs have their rest length. The particle is launched from that configuration with velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$. Determine the period of the oscillations, the amplitude and the maximum modulus of the resultant elastic force.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,8,0;P,4,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,incastro,0" linee="0,0 0.25,0.35 0.75,-0.35 1.25,0.35 1.75,-0.35 2.25,0.35 2.75,-0.35 3.2,0;4.8,0 5.2,0.35 5.7,-0.35 6.2,0.35 6.7,-0.35 7.2,0.35 7.6,-0.35 8,0;3.2,0 4.8,0" punti="4,0" etichette="1.6,0.75,k;6.4,0.75,2k" frecce="4,0.6,5.4,0.6,v_0" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle between two springs of constants $k$ and $2k$, launched from the rest configuration"}

$$
m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x - 2k\,x = -3k\,x
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ddot{x} + \omega_0^2\,x = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{3k}{m}}
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_0} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{3k}}
$$
$$
x(0) = 0 ,
\quad
\dot{x}(0) = v_0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x(t) = \frac{v_0}{\omega_0}\sin\big( \omega_0\,t \big) ,
\qquad
A = \frac{v_0}{\omega_0} = v_0\sqrt{\frac{m}{3k}}
$$
$$
F_{\max} = 3k\,A = v_0\sqrt{3 k m}
$$

Check with the conservation of mechanical energy between the initial configuration and the configuration of maximum elongation:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,(3k)\,A^2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
A = v_0\sqrt{\frac{m}{3k}} ,
$$
and with the check of the system deprived of the spring of constant $k$. In that system only the spring of constant $2k$ remains, therefore the period equals $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{2k}}$, that is, the value of the general formula with $3k$ replaced by $2k$.

<div class="spiegazione">

One measures $x$ from the configuration in which both springs have their rest length. If the particle moves by $x > 0$ to the right, the left spring stretches by $x$ and pulls to the left with intensity $k\,x$, while the right one shortens by $x$ and pushes to the left with intensity $2k\,x$. The two forces therefore have the *same* direction, and their sum is $-3k\,x$, that is, the force of a single spring of constant $k + 2k$.

The equation of motion is that of the harmonic oscillator with $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\dfrac{3k}{m}}$. The initial data are zero position and velocity $v_0$, therefore the solution is a sine of amplitude $\dfrac{v_0}{\omega_0}$: the particle crosses the initial position with the maximum velocity and stops at the two extreme positions $\pm A$.

The resultant elastic force has modulus $3k\,|x|$, hence it is maximum at the extreme positions and equals $3k\,A$. Substituting the amplitude one obtains $v_0\sqrt{3 k m}$: a greater mass or stiffer springs produce a greater maximum force, for the same launch velocity.

The check compares the initial kinetic energy with the elastic potential energy at the position of maximum elongation, where the velocity is zero, and recovers the amplitude. The last check confirms the formula, because by removing one of the two springs the system becomes that of exercise 1, with the constant of the remaining spring.

</div>
