## Exercise 1

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure, in the vertical plane $Oxy$. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at $O$ with velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$, with $v_0 > 0$, and from that instant it is subject to the constant force $F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, besides the weight. Determine the law of motion, the reaction of the guide, the velocity with which the particle passes through the abscissa $d > 0$ and the time taken to reach it.

::struttura{linee="-0.5,0 7,0" nodi="O,0,0" punti="3,0,P" forze="3 0,1.3,0,F" frecce="3,0.55,4.3,0.55,v_0" quote="O,3 0,d,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth horizontal guide with the constant force $F$ and the initial velocity $v_0$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{x} = F \\[4pt]
m\,\ddot{y} = \Phi - m g
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\ddot{x} = \frac{F}{m}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\dot{x}(t) = v_0 + \frac{F}{m}\,t ,
\qquad
x(t) = v_0\,t + \frac{F}{2m}\,t^2
$$
$$
y(t) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\Phi = m g
$$
$$
t_d = \frac{m}{F}\left( \sqrt{v_0^2 + \frac{2 F d}{m}} - v_0 \right) ,
\qquad
v(d) = v_0 + \frac{F}{m}\,t_d = \sqrt{v_0^2 + \frac{2 F d}{m}}
$$

Check with the kinetic energy theorem, applied between $O$ and the abscissa $d$:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v(d)^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\frac{2 F d}{m} = F d = L ,
$$
and $L = F d$ is exactly the work of the constant force over the displacement $d$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The particle stays on the guide, hence its ordinate is constant and zero; therefore $\ddot{y} = 0$ and the second equation gives at once $\Phi = m g$, that is, the guide supports the particle. The guide is smooth, hence the reaction is **orthogonal** to the guide and has no component along $x$: in the first equation only the force $F$ appears.

The acceleration along $x$ is constant, therefore one integrates twice with respect to the time. The first integration, with the condition $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$, gives the velocity; the second, with $x(0) = 0$, gives the law of motion. The motion is therefore **uniformly accelerated**.

To find $t_d$ one solves the quadratic equation $\dfrac{F}{2m}\,t^2 + v_0\,t - d = 0$ and chooses the positive root, because the time sought comes after the initial instant. Substituting $t_d$ into the velocity one obtains $v(d)$, where the initial velocity appears only through $v_0^2$.

The check is independent of the computation carried out, because the kinetic energy theorem links the change of $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2$ to the work of the force, without passing through the law of motion. The weight and the reaction do no work, since they are orthogonal to the displacement, hence the total work is $F d$ and the expression of $v(d)$ is recovered.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure. Besides the weight, from the instant $t = 0$ the force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{\imath}$ acts, with $F_0 > 0$ and $\Omega > 0$. At the initial instant the particle is at rest at $O$. Determine the law of motion, the maximum distance from $O$ and the maximum velocity reached.

::struttura{linee="-0.5,0 7,0" nodi="O,0,0" punti="3,0,P" forze="3 0,1.3,0,F_0 cos(Ωt)" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth horizontal guide with the force $F_0\cos(\Omega t)$"}

$$
m\,\ddot{x} = F_0\cos(\Omega t) ,
\qquad
x(0) = 0 ,
\qquad
\dot{x}(0) = 0
$$
$$
\dot{x}(t) = \int_0^t \frac{F_0}{m}\cos(\Omega\tau)\,d\tau = \frac{F_0}{m\,\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)
$$
$$
x(t) = \int_0^t \frac{F_0}{m\,\Omega}\sin(\Omega\tau)\,d\tau = \frac{F_0}{m\,\Omega^2}\big( 1 - \cos(\Omega t) \big)
$$
$$
x_{\max} = \frac{2 F_0}{m\,\Omega^2} ,
\qquad
v_{\max} = \frac{F_0}{m\,\Omega}
$$

Check with the kinetic energy theorem, applied between $t = 0$ and $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2\Omega}$, the instant at which the velocity is maximum:
$$
L = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2\Omega}} F_0\cos(\Omega t)\,\dot{x}(t)\,dt
= \frac{F_0^2}{m\,\Omega}\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2\Omega}} \sin(\Omega t)\cos(\Omega t)\,dt
= \frac{F_0^2}{2m\,\Omega^2} ,
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_{\max}^2 - 0 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\frac{F_0^2}{m^2\,\Omega^2} = \frac{F_0^2}{2m\,\Omega^2} = L
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force depends only on the time, therefore the equation of motion is integrated directly twice: the first integration gives the velocity, the second the position. The constants of integration are zero, because the particle starts from rest from the origin and the integrals have already been written between $0$ and $t$.

The velocity $\dfrac{F_0}{m\,\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)$ is *always* non-negative in the first half-period and changes sign afterwards; the motion is therefore an oscillation, but not about $O$: the position $x(t) = \dfrac{F_0}{m\,\Omega^2}\big( 1 - \cos(\Omega t) \big)$ remains non-negative and oscillates between $0$ and $\dfrac{2F_0}{m\,\Omega^2}$, which is the value attained when $\cos(\Omega t) = -1$. The maximum velocity occurs when $\sin(\Omega t) = 1$, that is, at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2\Omega}$, and equals $\dfrac{F_0}{m\,\Omega}$.

Both maxima **decrease** as $\Omega$ grows: a force that changes direction rapidly does not manage to displace the particle much, because it reverses its own direction before the velocity has grown.

In the check one computes the work of the force up to the instant of maximum velocity; the integral is solved with the substitution $u = \sin(\Omega t)$, which gives $\dfrac{F_0^2}{m\,\Omega}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2\Omega}$. The result coincides with the final kinetic energy, because the particle starts from rest.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle of mass $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure, immersed in a fluid that exerts the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_P$, with $h = 4\,\mathrm{kg/s}$. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at $O$ with velocity $v_0 = 10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ in the positive direction of the $x$ axis. Determine the law of motion, the total distance travelled and the instant at which the velocity has halved.

::struttura{linee="-0.5,0 7,0" nodi="O,0,0" punti="3,0,P" forze="3 0,-1.3,0,h v_P" frecce="3,0.55,4.3,0.55,v_0" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth horizontal guide with the viscous resistance $-h\,\vec{v}_P$"}

$$
m\,\ddot{x} = -h\,\dot{x} ,
\qquad
x(0) = 0 ,
\qquad
\dot{x}(0) = v_0
$$
$$
\dot{x}(t) = v_0\,e^{-\frac{h}{m}t} ,
\qquad
x(t) = \frac{m\,v_0}{h}\left( 1 - e^{-\frac{h}{m}t} \right)
$$
$$
x_\infty = \lim_{t \to +\infty} x(t) = \frac{m\,v_0}{h} = \frac{2 \cdot 10}{4}\,\mathrm{m} = 5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
Denoting by $t_m$ the instant at which the velocity has halved, the condition $\dot{x}(t_m) = \dfrac{v_0}{2}$ becomes

$$
e^{-\frac{h}{m}t_m} = \frac{1}{2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
t_m = \frac{m\ln 2}{h} = \frac{2\ln 2}{4}\,\mathrm{s} = 0.35\,\mathrm{s}
$$

Check with the kinetic energy theorem over the whole motion: the work of the resistance equals
$$
L = \int_0^{+\infty} (-h\,\dot{x})\,\dot{x}\,dt = -h\,v_0^2\int_0^{+\infty} e^{-\frac{2h}{m}t}\,dt = -h\,v_0^2\,\frac{m}{2h} = -\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 ,
$$
and it coincides with the change of kinetic energy, which is $0 - \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2$ because the final velocity is zero.

<div class="spiegazione">

The resistance is directed opposite to the velocity, hence along the guide it has component $-h\,\dot{x}$. The equation of motion is linear of the first order in the velocity, because it contains $\dot{x}$ and $\ddot{x}$ but not $x$; one separates the variables and integrates, or one recognizes the equation $\dot{v} = -\dfrac{h}{m}\,v$ with solution $v_0\,e^{-\frac{h}{m}t}$. Integrating the velocity between $0$ and $t$ one obtains the law of motion.

The velocity is positive for every $t$ and tends to zero, hence the particle always advances and its abscissa grows up to the limiting value $\dfrac{m\,v_0}{h}$: the distance travelled is **finite** even though the motion lasts indefinitely. The characteristic time $\dfrac{m}{h}$ equals $0.5\,\mathrm{s}$, and in that time the velocity is reduced to the factor $\dfrac{1}{e}$ of the initial value.

The check is independent because it computes the work of the resistance with an integral, without using the distance travelled. The velocity tends to zero, hence the final kinetic energy is zero and all the initial kinetic energy has been dissipated.

</div>
