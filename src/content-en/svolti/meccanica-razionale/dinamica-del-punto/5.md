## Exercise 1

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure and is subject to the elastic force of a spring of constant $k$, with rest length equal to the distance from the wall to the origin $O$, and to the constant force $F_0\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F_0 > 0$. At the instant $t = 0$ the particle is at rest at $O$. Determine the potential energy, the equilibrium configuration, the turning points, the period and the maximum velocity.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;P,4,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180" linee="0,0 0.35,0.3 1.0,-0.3 1.65,0.3 2.3,-0.3 2.95,0.3 3.6,-0.3 4,0;4,0 6.4,0" punti="4,0" forze="5.3 0,1.3,0," tratteggi="2.6,-0.55 2.6,0.6" etichette="1.4,0.7,k;2.6,-0.8,x = 0;4.65,0.42,F_0" quote="2.6 0,P,x,-1.1" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth guide subject to the spring of constant $k$ and to the constant force $F_0$"}

$$
F(x) = -k\,x + F_0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
V(x) = \frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 - F_0\,x
$$
$$
V'(x) = k\,x - F_0 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x_e = \frac{F_0}{k} ,
\qquad
V''(x_e) = k > 0 ,
\qquad
V(x_e) = -\frac{F_0^2}{2k}
$$
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}\,m\cdot 0^2 + V(0) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2 - F_0\,x = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x_1 = 0 ,
\qquad
x_2 = \frac{2 F_0}{k}
$$
$$
m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x + F_0 ,
\qquad
u = x - \frac{F_0}{k}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\ddot{u} + \frac{k}{m}\,u = 0 ,
\qquad
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$
$$
v_{\max} = \frac{F_0}{k}\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \frac{F_0}{\sqrt{m k}}
$$

::grafico{f="x^2/2-x;0" legenda=0 punti="0,0,pieno;2,0,pieno;1,-0.5,pieno" xmin=-1 xmax=3 ymin=-0.85 ymax=1.1 nomi="x,V" titolo="Potential energy in units of $\dfrac{F_0^2}{k}$, with $x$ in units of $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$: at the level $E = 0$ the turning points are $0$ and $\dfrac{2 F_0}{k}$"}

Check with the conservation of mechanical energy at the passage through the equilibrium configuration:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_{\max}^2 + V(x_e) = E = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_{\max}^2 = \frac{F_0^2}{2k}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
v_{\max} = \frac{F_0}{\sqrt{m k}} ,
$$
which coincides with the value obtained from the amplitude and the angular frequency.

<div class="spiegazione">

The total force along the guide is the sum of the elastic force $-k\,x$ and the constant force $F_0$. The potential energy is obtained by integrating with the sign changed, because $F = -V'$; the additive constant is chosen to be zero, so that $V(0) = 0$.

The equilibrium configuration is the stationary point of $V$, that is, $x_e = \dfrac{F_0}{k}$: in that position the spring balances the constant force. The second derivative is $k > 0$, hence $V$ has a **minimum** and the equilibrium is stable.

The particle starts from rest at $x = 0$, therefore the mechanical energy is $E = V(0) = 0$. The turning points are the solutions of $V(x) = 0$, that is, $x = 0$ and $x = \dfrac{2 F_0}{k}$, and the motion takes place between them. The equilibrium configuration is the midpoint of the two, as must be the case for a potential energy of the second degree.

The equation of motion, written in the coordinate $u$ that measures the displacement from the equilibrium, is that of the harmonic oscillator; therefore the motion is harmonic with amplitude $\dfrac{F_0}{k}$ and period $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$, the same that one would have without the constant force. The maximum velocity is the product of the amplitude by the angular frequency.

The check obtains the maximum velocity by another route: in the equilibrium configuration the potential energy equals $-\dfrac{F_0^2}{2k}$ and the total energy is zero, hence all the kinetic energy available is $\dfrac{F_0^2}{2k}$. The two values coincide.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the horizontal, smooth and fixed rectilinear guide in the figure, and is subject to the force
$$
F(x) = -c\,x\,( x^2 - \ell^2 ) ,
\qquad
c > 0 ,
\qquad
\ell > 0 .
$$
Determine the potential energy, the equilibrium configurations with their nature, the angular frequency of the small oscillations about the stable configurations and the turning points for the energy levels $E_1 = -\dfrac{c\,\ell^4}{8}$ and $E_2 = \dfrac{c\,\ell^4}{4}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,3,0;P,4.4,0" linee="-0.2,0 6.6,0" punti="4.4,0" forze="P,-1.2,0,F(x)" quote="O,P,x,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on a smooth horizontal guide subject to the force $F(x) = -c\,x\,(x^2 - \ell^2)$"}

$$
V(x) = -\int F(x)\,dx = c\left( \frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{\ell^2\,x^2}{2} \right)
$$
$$
V'(x) = c\,x\,( x^2 - \ell^2 ) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x = 0 ,
\qquad
x = \pm\,\ell
$$
$$
V''(x) = c\,( 3x^2 - \ell^2 ) ,
\qquad
V''(0) = -c\,\ell^2 < 0 ,
\qquad
V''(\pm\ell) = 2\,c\,\ell^2 > 0
$$
$$
V(0) = 0 \ \text{(maximum)} ,
\qquad
V(\pm\ell) = -\frac{c\,\ell^4}{4} \ \text{(minima)}
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{V''(\pm\ell)}{m}} = \ell\sqrt{\frac{2c}{m}}
$$
$$
E_1 = -\frac{c\,\ell^4}{8} :
\qquad
\frac{x^4}{4} - \frac{\ell^2\,x^2}{2} = -\frac{\ell^4}{8}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x^2 = \ell^2\left( 1 \pm \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)
$$
$$
x = \pm\,\ell\sqrt{1 - \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} \approx \pm\,0.541\,\ell ,
\qquad
x = \pm\,\ell\sqrt{1 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} \approx \pm\,1.307\,\ell
$$
$$
E_2 = \frac{c\,\ell^4}{4} :
\qquad
x^4 - 2\,\ell^2\,x^2 - \ell^4 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x = \pm\,\ell\sqrt{1 + \sqrt{2}} \approx \pm\,1.554\,\ell
$$

::grafico{f="x^4/4-x^2/2;-0.125;0.25" legenda=0 punti="-1.3066,-0.125,pieno;-0.5412,-0.125,pieno;0.5412,-0.125,pieno;1.3066,-0.125,pieno;-1.5538,0.25,pieno;1.5538,0.25,pieno" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-0.5 ymax=0.7 nomi="x,V" titolo="Potential energy in units of $c\,\ell^4$, with $x$ in units of $\ell$: at the level $E_1$ the motion remains in one of the two wells, at the level $E_2$ it crosses the origin"}

Check of the angular frequency of the small oscillations with the linearization of the equation of motion. Setting $x = \ell + \xi$ with $|\xi|$ small,
$$
m\,\ddot{\xi} = -c\,( \ell + \xi )\big( ( \ell + \xi )^2 - \ell^2 \big) = -c\,( \ell + \xi )\,( 2\ell\,\xi + \xi^2 ) = -2\,c\,\ell^2\,\xi + o(\xi) ,
$$
hence $\ddot{\xi} + \dfrac{2 c\,\ell^2}{m}\,\xi = 0$ and the angular frequency is $\ell\sqrt{\dfrac{2c}{m}}$, as obtained from the second derivative of the potential.

<div class="spiegazione">

The potential energy is obtained by integrating the force with the sign changed, and the constant is chosen so that $V(0) = 0$. The function $V$ is even, therefore the graph is symmetric with respect to the axis of the ordinates and the two wells have the same depth.

The equilibrium configurations are the stationary points of $V$, that is, the solutions of $V'(x) = 0$. The sign of the second derivative distinguishes the two cases: at $x = 0$ the potential has a maximum, hence the equilibrium is unstable; at $x = \pm\ell$ it has two minima, hence the two equilibria are stable. About a minimum the motion with energy slightly higher is harmonic with angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{V''}{m}}$, as is seen by expanding $V$ with Taylor's formula up to the second order.

With the level $E_1 = -\dfrac{c\,\ell^4}{8}$, which lies between the minimum $-\dfrac{c\,\ell^4}{4}$ and the maximum $0$, the set $\{ V \leq E_1 \}$ is formed by two separate intervals, one in each well. The particle remains in the one that contains the initial position, because in order to cross the origin it would have to pass through positions with $V > E_1$: the motion is periodic between $0.541\,\ell$ and $1.307\,\ell$ if it starts in the right-hand well.

With the level $E_2 = \dfrac{c\,\ell^4}{4}$, greater than the maximum of $V$, the set $\{ V \leq E_2 \}$ is a single interval and the particle oscillates between $-1.554\,\ell$ and $1.554\,\ell$, crossing the origin with non-zero velocity.

The level $E = 0$ separates the two behaviors. At that level both $V - E$ and $V'$ vanish at $x = 0$, therefore the particle tends to that position without reaching it in a finite time.

The check obtains the angular frequency without passing through the potential: one writes the equation of motion, substitutes $x = \ell + \xi$ and neglects the terms of order higher than the first in $\xi$. One obtains the same angular frequency, therefore the computation of the second derivative is correct.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A particle of mass $m$ slides along the half-line $x > 0$ of a smooth and fixed rectilinear guide, as in the figure, and is subject to the force
$$
F(x) = -\frac{2\,V_0\,\ell\,( x - \ell )}{x^3} ,
\qquad
V_0 > 0 ,
\qquad
\ell > 0 ,
$$
whose potential energy is $V(x) = V_0\left( \dfrac{\ell^2}{x^2} - \dfrac{2\ell}{x} \right)$. Determine the equilibrium configuration and its nature, the angular frequency of the small oscillations, the turning points for $E = -\dfrac{V_0}{2}$ and the values of $E$ for which the motion is bounded.

::struttura{nodi="O,0.4,0;P,3.6,0" linee="0.4,0 6.6,0" punti="3.6,0" forze="P,-1.2,0,F(x)" quote="O,P,x,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="Particle on the half-line $x > 0$ of a smooth guide, subject to the force $F(x)$"}

$$
V'(x) = V_0\left( -\frac{2\,\ell^2}{x^3} + \frac{2\,\ell}{x^2} \right) = \frac{2\,V_0\,\ell\,( x - \ell )}{x^3} = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x_e = \ell
$$
$$
V(\ell) = V_0\,( 1 - 2 ) = -V_0 ,
\qquad
V''(x) = V_0\left( \frac{6\,\ell^2}{x^4} - \frac{4\,\ell}{x^3} \right) ,
\qquad
V''(\ell) = \frac{2\,V_0}{\ell^2} > 0
$$
$$
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{V''(\ell)}{m}} = \frac{1}{\ell}\sqrt{\frac{2\,V_0}{m}}
$$
$$
E = -\frac{V_0}{2} :
\qquad
V_0\left( \frac{\ell^2}{x^2} - \frac{2\ell}{x} \right) = -\frac{V_0}{2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x^2 - 4\,\ell\,x + 2\,\ell^2 = 0
$$
$$
x_1 = ( 2 - \sqrt{2} )\,\ell \approx 0.586\,\ell ,
\qquad
x_2 = ( 2 + \sqrt{2} )\,\ell \approx 3.414\,\ell
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to +\infty} V(x) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{the motion is bounded if and only if} \quad -V_0 \leq E < 0
$$

::grafico{f="1/x^2-2/x;-0.5" legenda=0 punti="0.5858,-0.5,pieno;3.4142,-0.5,pieno;1,-1,pieno" xmin=0.3 xmax=6 ymin=-1.3 ymax=1.6 nomi="x,V" titolo="Potential energy in units of $V_0$, with $x$ in units of $\ell$: at the level $E = -\dfrac{V_0}{2}$ the motion is periodic between the two turning points"}

Check of the angular frequency with the linearization of the force. Setting $x = \ell + \xi$ with $|\xi|$ small,
$$
F(\ell + \xi) = -\frac{2\,V_0\,\ell\,\xi}{( \ell + \xi )^3} = -\frac{2\,V_0}{\ell^2}\,\xi + o(\xi) ,
$$
hence $m\,\ddot{\xi} = -\dfrac{2\,V_0}{\ell^2}\,\xi$ and the angular frequency is $\dfrac{1}{\ell}\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,V_0}{m}}$, in agreement with the computation carried out starting from $V''$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The derivative of $V$ is computed term by term and collected, and the numerator vanishes only for $x = \ell$, because the denominator $x^3$ is positive on the half-line considered. The value $V(\ell) = -V_0$ is the minimum of the function, since $V''(\ell)$ is positive, hence the equilibrium is **stable**.

For the energy $E = -\dfrac{V_0}{2}$ one solves $V(x) = E$. Multiplying by $-\dfrac{2 x^2}{V_0}$ one obtains the quadratic equation $x^2 - 4\,\ell\,x + 2\,\ell^2 = 0$, whose roots are both positive. The motion is periodic between them, because between $x_1$ and $x_2$ one has $V(x) < E$ and at both endpoints the derivative of $V$ is not zero.

The behavior at infinity decides the boundedness of the motion. The function $V$ tends to zero from negative values as $x$ grows, and its minimum equals $-V_0$. If $E \geq 0$, the inequality $V(x) \leq E$ is satisfied for every sufficiently large $x$, therefore the particle can move away indefinitely; if $-V_0 \leq E < 0$, the set $\{ V \leq E \}$ is a closed and bounded interval and the motion remains in it.

The check obtains the angular frequency from the expression of the force alone: one substitutes $x = \ell + \xi$, observes that the numerator is already proportional to $\xi$ and replaces the denominator with $\ell^3$, which is its value for $\xi = 0$. The coefficient obtained coincides with $V''(\ell)$, therefore the angular frequency is confirmed.

</div>
