## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at the endpoint $A$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane, with a smooth hinge, and is released at rest in the horizontal position, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the reaction of the hinge as a function of the angle $\vartheta$ by which the rod has descended below the horizontal.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.6,-1.5" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 3,0" punti="1.3,-0.75,G,baricentro" forze="1.3 -0.75,0,-0.9,mg" reazioni="A,1,0,Φ_Ax;A,0,1,Φ_Ay" etichette="0.85,-0.22,ϑ" titolo="Free body diagram of the rod in the generic configuration"}

Motion of the rod, from the conservation of the mechanical energy and from the second cardinal equation with the pole at the fixed hinge $A$:
$$
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{3\,g\,\sin\vartheta}{\ell} ,
\qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g\,\cos\vartheta}{2\,\ell}
$$
Position and acceleration of the center of mass:
$$
G - A = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \frac{\ell}{2}\,\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_G = -\frac{9}{8}\,g\,\sin 2\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{3}{4}\,g\,\big( 3\sin^2\vartheta - 1 \big)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
First cardinal equation, $m\,\vec{a}_G = \vec{\Phi}_A - m\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} = -\dfrac{9}{8}\,m\,g\,\sin 2\vartheta \\[8pt]
\Phi_{Ay} = \dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,g\,\big( 9\sin^2\vartheta + 1 \big)
\end{cases}
$$
Components along the rod and orthogonal to the rod, with $\vec{e}_r$ directed from $A$ toward $B$ and $\vec{e}_t$ in the direction of the motion:
$$
\vec{\Phi}_A \cdot \vec{e}_r = -\frac{5}{2}\,m\,g\,\sin\vartheta ,
\qquad
\vec{\Phi}_A \cdot \vec{e}_t = -\frac{1}{4}\,m\,g\,\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
|\vec{\Phi}_A| = \frac{m\,g}{4}\,\sqrt{1 + 99\sin^2\vartheta}
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation about the pole $G$, about which the weight has vanishing moment:
$$
(A - G) \wedge \vec{\Phi}_A = \Big( -\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\,\Phi_{Ay} - \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\,\Phi_{Ax} \Big)\,\hat{k} = -\frac{1}{8}\,m\,g\,\ell\,\cos\vartheta\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\dot{\vec{K}}_G = -\frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} = -\frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2 \cdot \frac{3\,g\,\cos\vartheta}{2\,\ell}\,\hat{k} = -\frac{1}{8}\,m\,g\,\ell\,\cos\vartheta\,\hat{k}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod has only one degree of freedom and the Lagrangian coordinate is the angle $\vartheta$, zero in the initial configuration. The reaction of the hinge has two unknown components, $\Phi_{Ax}$ and $\Phi_{Ay}$, drawn in the positive direction of the axes in the **free body diagram**.

The motion is determined first, because the acceleration of $G$ depends on $\dot{\vartheta}$ and on $\ddot{\vartheta}$. The conservation of the mechanical energy gives $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ as a function of $\vartheta$, and the second cardinal equation with the pole at the fixed hinge gives $\ddot{\vartheta}$, because in that equation the reaction $\vec{\Phi}_A$ does not appear, since its line of action passes through $A$.

The acceleration of $G$ is obtained by differentiating the position twice and substituting the values of $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and $\ddot{\vartheta}$ found. The first cardinal equation then relates $\vec{a}_G$ to the external forces alone, which are the weight and the reaction of the hinge; isolating $\vec{\Phi}_A$ one obtains the two components.

The result is read better along the rod and orthogonally to the rod. The component along the rod equals $-\dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,g\,\sin\vartheta$, hence it is directed **from $B$ toward $A$**: the hinge pulls the rod toward itself in order to provide the centripetal acceleration. The orthogonal component equals $-\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,g\,\cos\vartheta$, hence it is opposite to the direction of the motion. In the initial configuration, $\vartheta = 0$, the reaction is vertical and equals $\dfrac{m\,g}{4}$ upward; at the passage through the vertical, $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, it equals $\dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,g$ upward, two and a half times the weight of the rod.

The check uses the second cardinal equation with the pole at $G$, which was not used in the computation. The weight is applied at $G$ and has vanishing moment about that pole, hence the moment of the reaction alone must be equal to $\dot{\vec{K}}_G$. The two sides coincide for every $\vartheta$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two bodies of masses $m$ and $2m$, which can be treated as material points, are joined by an inextensible string of negligible mass passing over an ideal pulley, of negligible mass and friction, held by a fixed support, as in the diagram in the figure. The system is left free to move. Determine the acceleration of the bodies, the tension of the string and the force that the system exerts on the support during the motion.

::struttura{nodi="S,0,3.2,-" vincoli="S,incastro,90" cerchi="0,2.6,0.6" punti="0,2.6,,centro" linee="-0.6,2.6 -0.6,1.3;0.6,2.6 0.6,0.7" poligoni="-1,0.7 -0.2,0.7 -0.2,1.3 -1,1.3;0.2,0.1 1,0.1 1,0.7 0.2,0.7" reazioni="0 2.6,0,0.9,Φ" etichette="-0.6,1,m;0.6,0.4,2m" titolo="Ideal pulley: the reaction $\vec{\Phi}$ of the support is the unknown"}

$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a = T - m\,g \\[4pt]
2m\,a = 2m\,g - T
\end{cases}
$$
$$
3m\,a = m\,g
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
a = \frac{g}{3} ,
\qquad
T = m\,\Big( g + \frac{g}{3} \Big) = \frac{4}{3}\,m\,g
$$
The pulley is ideal, hence the two portions of string have the same tension and the reaction of the support balances the two tensions:
$$
\Phi = 2\,T = \frac{8}{3}\,m\,g
$$

Check with the first cardinal equation applied to the whole system. The body of mass $m$ rises with acceleration $a$ and the one of mass $2m$ descends with the same acceleration, hence
$$
a_{Gy} = \frac{m\,a + 2m\,(-a)}{3m} = -\frac{a}{3} = -\frac{g}{9} ,
$$
$$
3m\,a_{Gy} = \Phi - 3m\,g
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\Phi = 3m\,g - 3m\,\frac{g}{9} = \frac{8}{3}\,m\,g
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible, hence the two bodies have accelerations of equal magnitude $a$ and opposite directions; the system has only one degree of freedom. The unknowns are $a$, the tension $T$ and the reaction $\vec{\Phi}$ of the support.

The first two equations are Newton's equations of the two bodies, projected onto the vertical, with the convention that $a$ is positive when the body of mass $2m$ descends. The pulley is ideal, that is of negligible mass and friction, hence its moment equation reduces to $(T_2 - T_1)\,R = 0$ and the two portions of string have the same tension $T$.

Summing the two equations the tension cancels and one obtains $a = \dfrac{g}{3}$, hence $T = \dfrac{4}{3}\,m\,g$. The tension is **larger** than $m\,g$ and **smaller** than $2m\,g$, as it must be because the light body rises and the heavy one descends.

The reaction of the support holds the pulley, on which the string exerts two vertical downward pulls of intensity $T$ each; by the action and reaction principle the system presses on the support with the force $\dfrac{8}{3}\,m\,g$ downward. At rest the force would be $3\,m\,g$, whereas during the motion it is **smaller**, because the center of mass accelerates downward.

The check applies the first cardinal equation to the whole system, without going through the tension. The acceleration of the center of mass equals $-\dfrac{a}{3}$, and the external forces are the total weight $3m\,g$ and the reaction $\vec{\Phi}$ of the support. The same value is recovered.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is suspended in the horizontal position from two inextensible vertical strings of negligible mass, attached at the endpoints, as in the diagram in the figure. The string attached at $B$ is cut. Determine the tension of the string attached at $A$ immediately after the cut and the acceleration of the center of mass at that instant.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,0,1.6,-;D,3,1.6,-" aste="A-B" catene="C-A;D-B" vincoli="C,incastro,90;D,incastro,90" punti="1.5,0,G,baricentro" forze="1.5 0,0,-0.8,mg" reazioni="A,0,0.8,T" etichette="3.45,0.8,cut string" quote="A,B,ℓ,0.6" titolo="Rod hanging from two vertical strings: the string attached at $B$ is cut"}

At the instant following the cut the rod is still at rest, hence $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$; the string at $A$ is taut and the endpoint $A$ moves on a circle, hence its acceleration is horizontal and $a_{Ay} = 0$. From the formula for the acceleration in rigid motion, with $\vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$,
$$
\vec{a}_G = \vec{a}_A + \vec{\alpha} \wedge (G - A)
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
a_{Gy} = \alpha\,\frac{\ell}{2}
$$
Cardinal equations, with the pole $G$ for the second one:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a_{Gy} = T - m\,g \\[8pt]
\dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\alpha = -\,T\,\dfrac{\ell}{2} \\[8pt]
a_{Gy} = \alpha\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\alpha = -\frac{6\,T}{m\,\ell}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
m\,\Big( -\frac{6\,T}{m\,\ell} \Big)\frac{\ell}{2} = T - m\,g
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
-3\,T = T - m\,g
$$
$$
T = \frac{m\,g}{4} ,
\qquad
a_{Gy} = -\frac{3}{4}\,g ,
\qquad
\alpha = -\frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation about the pole $A$. At the instant considered $\vec{v}_A = \vec{v}_G = \vec{0}$, hence the term $\vec{v}_A \wedge m\,\vec{v}_G$ vanishes and $\dot{\vec{K}}_A = \vec{M}_A^{(e)}$ holds. The tension is applied at $A$ and has vanishing moment about $A$, hence
$$
\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\alpha = -\,m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\alpha = -\frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell} ,
\qquad
T = m\,g + m\,\alpha\,\frac{\ell}{2} = m\,g - \frac{3}{4}\,m\,g = \frac{m\,g}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As long as the two strings are taut the rod is at rest and each string carries $\dfrac{m\,g}{2}$. Right after the cut the system has two degrees of freedom, because only one string is left, and the unknowns of the initial instant are the tension $T$, the acceleration $a_{Gy}$ and the angular acceleration $\alpha$.

The rod is still at rest, hence the angular velocity vanishes and in the formula for the acceleration of the rigid motion the centripetal term $\vec{\omega} \wedge (\vec{\omega} \wedge (G - A))$ vanishes. The endpoint $A$ stays attached to the string, which is inextensible, hence $A$ moves on a circle whose center is the attachment point; starting from rest, its acceleration is tangent to the circle, that is horizontal, and its vertical component vanishes. The third equation of the system translates this condition.

The first equation is the first cardinal equation projected onto the vertical, the second one is the second cardinal equation with the pole at $G$, in which the weight gives no moment because it is applied at $G$, whereas the tension, applied at $A$ and directed upward, gives a clockwise moment, that is negative along $\hat{k}$.

Solving, one obtains $T = \dfrac{m\,g}{4}$, hence the tension of the remaining string **is reduced to half** of the value it had before the cut, and it does not double. The center of mass accelerates downward with $\dfrac{3}{4}\,g$ and the rod starts rotating clockwise.

The check uses the second cardinal equation with the pole at $A$, legitimate in the simple form because at that instant both $A$ and $G$ have zero velocity. About $A$ the tension has vanishing moment, hence the equation contains $\alpha$ alone and gives the same value; bringing it back into the first cardinal equation one recovers $T = \dfrac{m\,g}{4}$.

</div>
