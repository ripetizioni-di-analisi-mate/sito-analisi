## Exercise 1

Two bodies, which can be treated as material points of masses $m$ and $3m$, rest on a smooth horizontal plane and are held in contact with a compressed ideal spring of negligible mass, as in the diagram in the figure. The system starts from rest. When the spring reaches its rest length the two bodies separate and the body of mass $m$ has a velocity of magnitude $v$ directed to the right. Determine the velocity of the body of mass $3m$ and the elastic energy initially stored in the spring.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 1.2,0 1.2,0.9 0,0.9;3.4,0 5.2,0 5.2,0.9 3.4,0.9" linee="-0.8,0 6,0;1.2,0.45 1.47,0.72 1.92,0.18 2.37,0.72 2.82,0.18 3.13,0.45 3.4,0.45" etichette="0.6,0.45,3m;4.3,0.45,m;2.3,1.15,k" titolo="The two bodies pressed by the compressed spring on a smooth horizontal plane"}

$$
\vec{R}^{(e)} \cdot \hat{\imath} = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
Q_x = 0 \ \text{ at every instant}
$$
$$
m\,v - 3m\,u = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
u = \frac{v}{3}
$$
$$
E_{\text{el}} = T_{\text{fin}} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,\Big( \frac{v}{3} \Big)^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \frac{1}{6}\,m\,v^2 = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,v^2
$$

Check on the position of the center of mass: with $\vec{v}_1 = v\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{v}_2 = -\dfrac{v}{3}\,\hat{\imath}$,
$$
\vec{v}_G = \frac{m\,v - 3m\,\dfrac{v}{3}}{4m}\,\hat{\imath} = \vec{0} ,
$$
hence the center of mass stays at rest in the initial position, as it must be for a system that starts from rest and has no horizontal external forces.

<div class="spiegazione">

As the system one takes the set of the two bodies and the spring. The external forces are the weights and the reactions of the smooth plane, all vertical, hence the horizontal component of the external resultant vanishes and from the first cardinal equation it follows that $Q_x$ is constant. The system starts from rest, hence $Q_x = 0$ at every instant.

The forces that the spring exerts on the two bodies are internal to the system, and their resultant vanishes, hence the spring accelerates the two bodies in opposite directions without altering the total linear momentum.

Calling $u$ the magnitude of the velocity of the body of mass $3m$, directed to the left, the condition $Q_x = 0$ gives $u = \dfrac{v}{3}$. The heavier body moves away with a velocity **three times smaller**, in the inverse ratio of the masses.

The plane is smooth and the spring is ideal, hence the mechanical energy is conserved. In the initial configuration the kinetic energy vanishes and all the energy lies in the spring; when the spring reaches its rest length its potential energy vanishes, hence the initial elastic energy is equal to the final kinetic energy, which equals $\dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,v^2$.

The check computes the velocity of the center of mass with the two values found and gives $\vec{v}_G = \vec{0}$, consistent with the theorem on the motion of the center of mass, according to which $\vec{a}_G$ vanishes as long as the horizontal external resultant vanishes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cart of mass $3m$ has a smooth upper profile, which starts at the height $h$ and ends horizontal at the base, and can slide without friction on a horizontal guide, as in the diagram in the figure. A material point of mass $m$ is released at rest at the highest point of the profile and slides down to the base. Determine the velocities of the point and of the cart at the instant in which the point leaves the cart.

::struttura{poligoni="1.3,2 1.4,1.42 1.7,0.91 2.15,0.53 2.71,0.33 3,0.3 4.2,0.3 4.2,0 1.3,0" linee="-0.4,0 5.6,0" punti="1.3,2,P,pieno" quote="1.3 0.3,1.3 2,h,-0.7" etichette="3.1,1,3m;0.95,2.25,m" titolo="The point starts from rest at the height $h$ and slides on the smooth profile of the cart"}

We denote by $v$ the horizontal component of the velocity of the point and by $u$ that of the cart, both in the final configuration, in which the velocity of the point is horizontal.
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,v + 3m\,u = 0 \\[4pt]
m\,g\,h = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,u^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
u = -\frac{v}{3}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
m\,g\,h = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \frac{1}{6}\,m\,v^2 = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,v^2
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{3\,g\,h}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{6\,g\,h} ,
\qquad
u = -\frac{1}{6}\,\sqrt{6\,g\,h}
$$

Check by substituting the two values into the energy balance:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,\frac{6\,g\,h}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,\frac{6\,g\,h}{36} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,g\,h + \frac{1}{4}\,m\,g\,h = m\,g\,h
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the system one takes the point and the cart together. The external forces are the two weights and the reaction of the smooth guide, all vertical, hence $Q_x$ is conserved; the system starts from rest, hence $Q_x = 0$ at every instant. The reactions that the profile of the cart and the point exchange are internal to the system.

The profile and the guide are smooth and the weight is conservative, hence the mechanical energy is conserved as well. At the final instant the profile is horizontal, hence the velocity of the point relative to the cart is horizontal and the absolute velocity of the point is horizontal too, so at that instant the two unknowns are the components $v$ and $u$ alone.

The first equation of the system is the conservation of the horizontal linear momentum, the second one is the conservation of the mechanical energy between the initial instant and the final one, with the drop $h$ of the point. Substituting $u = -\dfrac{v}{3}$ into the second one obtains an equation in $v$ alone.

The point leaves **to the right** with velocity $\dfrac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{6\,g\,h}$ and the cart recedes **to the left** with a velocity three times smaller. The value of $v$ is smaller than $\sqrt{2\,g\,h}$, which would be obtained with the cart locked, because part of the energy goes to the cart.

The check substitutes the two values into the energy balance, and the two contributions equal $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,g\,h$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,g\,h$ and their sum is $m\,g\,h$, that is the potential energy lost.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A person of mass $m$ stands still at the end of a boat of mass $4m$ and length $\ell$, at rest on still water, as in the diagram in the figure. The person walks to the other end. Neglecting the resistance of the water, determine the displacement of the boat and the displacement of the person relative to the shore.

::struttura{poligoni="0.2,0 3.8,0 3.5,0.7 0.5,0.7" linee="-0.9,0 5.4,0" punti="0.75,0.7,P,pieno" frecce="1.1,1.15,3.2,1.15" quote="0.2 0,3.8 0,ℓ,0.55" etichette="2,0.33,4m;0.6,1.2,m" titolo="The person walks from the stern to the bow of a boat free to slide on the water"}

$$
\vec{R}^{(e)} \cdot \hat{\imath} = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
Q_x = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
x_G = \text{constant}
$$

Calling $X$ the displacement of the boat relative to the shore, the displacement of the person relative to the shore equals $X + \ell$, because relative to the boat the person advances by $\ell$:
$$
4m\,X + m\,(X + \ell) = 0
$$
$$
X = -\frac{\ell}{5} ,
\qquad
X + \ell = \frac{4\,\ell}{5}
$$

Check on the relative displacement and on the center of mass:
$$
\frac{4\,\ell}{5} - \Big( -\frac{\ell}{5} \Big) = \ell ,
\qquad
4m\,\Big( -\frac{\ell}{5} \Big) + m\,\frac{4\,\ell}{5} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is formed by the person and the boat. The horizontal external forces are neglected by hypothesis, because the resistance of the water is assumed to vanish, and the weight and the hydrostatic thrust are vertical. From the first cardinal equation it follows that $Q_x$ is conserved, and since the system starts from rest we have $Q_x = 0$, hence the abscissa of the center of mass stays the same throughout the walk.

The actions that the person exerts on the bottom of the boat, and the boat on the person, are internal to the system, hence they do not move the center of mass.

The displacement of the person relative to the shore is the sum of the displacement of the boat and of the relative displacement $\ell$. Imposing that $x_G$ is constant one obtains $X = -\dfrac{\ell}{5}$, hence the boat moves by $\dfrac{\ell}{5}$ **in the direction opposite** to the one in which the person walks, who advances by $\dfrac{4\,\ell}{5}$ relative to the shore. The two displacements are in the inverse ratio of the masses, $1 : 4$.

The check verifies that the difference between the two displacements is precisely $\ell$, as the datum of the problem requires, and that the sum weighted by the masses vanishes, that is that the center of mass has not moved.

</div>
