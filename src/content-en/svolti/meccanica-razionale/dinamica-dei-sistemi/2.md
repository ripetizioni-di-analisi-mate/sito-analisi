## Exercise 1

Two material points of masses $m$ and $2m$ occupy the positions $P_1 = (\ell, 0)$ and $P_2 = (0, 2\ell)$ of the fixed frame $(O; x, y)$, as in the diagram in the figure, and have velocities $\vec{v}_1 = v\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{v}_2 = -v\,\hat{\imath}$. Compute the angular momentum $\vec{K}_O$ about the origin and the angular momentum $\vec{K}_G$ about the center of mass.

::struttura{punti="2,0,P_1,pieno;0,4,P_2,pieno;0.667,2.667,G,baricentro" frecce="2,0,2,1.6;0,4,-1.6,4" etichette="2.5,0.9,v;-1,4.3,v;2.4,-0.12,m;0.45,4.45,2m" quote="0 0,2 0,ℓ,0.5;0 0,0 4,2ℓ,0.5" titolo="The two points and their center of mass $G$"}

$$
\vec{K}_O = (P_1 - O) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_1 + (P_2 - O) \wedge 2m\,\vec{v}_2
$$
$$
\vec{K}_O = \big( \ell\,\hat{\imath} \big) \wedge \big( m\,v\,\hat{\jmath} \big) + \big( 2\ell\,\hat{\jmath} \big) \wedge \big( -2m\,v\,\hat{\imath} \big)
= m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k} + 4\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k} = 5\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
G - O = \frac{m\,\ell\,\hat{\imath} + 2m \cdot 2\ell\,\hat{\jmath}}{3m} = \frac{\ell}{3}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{4\,\ell}{3}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_G = \frac{m\,v\,\hat{\jmath} - 2m\,v\,\hat{\imath}}{3m} = -\frac{2\,v}{3}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{v}{3}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{K}_G = \vec{K}_O - (G - O) \wedge 3m\,\vec{v}_G = 5\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k} - 3\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k} = 2\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k}
$$

Check with the direct computation of $\vec{K}_G$:
$$
P_1 - G = \frac{2\,\ell}{3}\,\hat{\imath} - \frac{4\,\ell}{3}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
P_2 - G = -\frac{\ell}{3}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{2\,\ell}{3}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{K}_G = \Big( \frac{2\,\ell}{3}\,\hat{\imath} - \frac{4\,\ell}{3}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big) \wedge m\,v\,\hat{\jmath} + \Big( -\frac{\ell}{3}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{2\,\ell}{3}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big) \wedge \big( -2m\,v\,\hat{\imath} \big)
= \frac{2}{3}\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k} + \frac{4}{3}\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k} = 2\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is planar, therefore the vectors $\vec{K}_O$ and $\vec{K}_G$ are parallel to $\hat{k}$ and it suffices to compute their component. The unknowns are the two components, and they are obtained from the definition of angular momentum and from König's theorem.

In the first cross product one uses $\hat{\imath} \wedge \hat{\jmath} = \hat{k}$, therefore the contribution of $P_1$ is $m v \ell\,\hat{k}$, positive because the point turns about $O$ counterclockwise. In the second one uses $\hat{\jmath} \wedge \hat{\imath} = -\hat{k}$, and the factor $-2mv$ in front of $\hat{\imath}$ changes the sign, hence the contribution of $P_2$ is positive as well and equals $4 m v \ell\,\hat{k}$. The two points turn about $O$ in the same sense, and the total angular momentum is $5\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k}$, **counterclockwise**.

The center of mass and its velocity are computed as averages weighted by the masses. **König's theorem** for the angular momentum gives $\vec{K}_O = \vec{K}_G + (G - O) \wedge 3m\,\vec{v}_G$, and the second term is the moment of the total linear momentum thought of as applied at $G$, and it equals
$$
3m \Big( \frac{\ell}{3} \cdot \frac{v}{3} + \frac{4\,\ell}{3} \cdot \frac{2\,v}{3} \Big) \hat{k} = 3\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k} .
$$
Subtracting it from $\vec{K}_O$ one obtains $\vec{K}_G = 2\,m\,v\,\ell\,\hat{k}$.

The check recomputes $\vec{K}_G$ from the definition, with the position vectors measured from $G$ and the absolute velocities, and gives the same value. The computation with the absolute velocities is legitimate because the additional terms contain $\sum_i m_i\,(P_i - G) = \vec{0}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rotates in a vertical plane about the fixed hinge $A$, as in the diagram in the figure, with angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}$. Compute the angular momentum about $A$ and about the center of mass $G$, and verify König's theorem.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.6,-1.5" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" punti="1.3,-0.75,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 3,0" coppie="2.1 -0.55,1,ω" quote="A,B,ℓ,0.75" titolo="Rod hinged at the endpoint $A$: the state of motion is rotatory about $A$"}

$$
I_A = \int_0^{\ell} \frac{m}{\ell}\,s^2\,ds = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_G = \int_{-\frac{\ell}{2}}^{\frac{\ell}{2}} \frac{m}{\ell}\,s^2\,ds = \frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2
$$
$$
\vec{K}_A = I_A\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\vec{K}_G = I_G\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} = \frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
v_G = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
(G - A) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_G = m\,\frac{\ell}{2} \cdot \frac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}
$$

Verification of König's theorem:
$$
\vec{K}_G + (G - A) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_G = \Big( \frac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{4} \Big)\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} = \vec{K}_A
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is a rigid body with the point $A$ fixed, therefore it has only one degree of freedom and as a Lagrangian coordinate one takes the angle $\vartheta$ that the rod forms with the horizontal. The state of motion is rotatory about $A$.

The two moments of inertia are computed with the integral over the linear density $\dfrac{m}{\ell}$, taking the abscissa $s$ from $A$ in the first case and from $G$ in the second. The two values are found in the topic on the geometry of masses and are related by the **Huygens-Steiner theorem**, since $\dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,\Big( \dfrac{\ell}{2} \Big)^2 = \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$.

For a planar rigid body rotating about a point $P$ of the body, the angular momentum about that point equals $I_P\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}$, hence $\vec{K}_A$ is obtained with $I_A$ and $\vec{K}_G$ with $I_G$. The velocity of the center of mass is orthogonal to the rod and has magnitude $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}$, therefore the cross product $(G - A) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_G$ has magnitude $m\,\dfrac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}$ and is directed along $\hat{k}$.

The verification sums the two contributions of König's theorem and recovers exactly $\vec{K}_A$. The comparison shows that the angular momentum about the hinge is four times the one about the center of mass, because the transport term equals three times the proper term.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, as in the diagram in the figure. The center $G$ has velocity $\vec{v}_G = v\,\hat{\imath}$, with $v > 0$. Compute the angular velocity of the disk, the angular momentum about $G$ and the angular momentum about the point $C$ of the guide that at the instant considered coincides with the contact point.

::struttura{cerchi="3,1.5,1.5" linee="0,0 7,0" punti="3,1.5,G,baricentro;3,0,C,centro" frecce="3,1.5,5.1,1.5,v_G" coppie="3 2.45,-1,ω" tratteggi="3,0 3,1.5" etichette="3.3,0.72,R" titolo="Disk rolling without slipping: the contact point has zero velocity"}

$$
\vec{v}_C = \vec{v}_G + \vec{\omega} \wedge (C - G) = \vec{0}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\vec{\omega} = -\frac{v}{R}\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 ,
\qquad
\vec{K}_G = I_G\,\vec{\omega} = -\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R\,v\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\vec{K}_C = \vec{K}_G + (G - C) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_G = -\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R\,v\,\hat{k} + \big( R\,\hat{\jmath} \big) \wedge \big( m\,v\,\hat{\imath} \big) = -\frac{3}{2}\,m\,R\,v\,\hat{k}
$$

Check with the moment of inertia about the axis through $C$. The Huygens-Steiner theorem gives $I_C = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$, and since at the instant considered the contact point has zero velocity,
$$
\vec{K}_C = I_C\,\vec{\omega} = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2 \cdot \Big( -\frac{v}{R} \Big)\,\hat{k} = -\frac{3}{2}\,m\,R\,v\,\hat{k}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk rolls without slipping, therefore the point of the disk in contact with the guide has zero velocity. Imposing this condition in the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics, with $C - G = -R\,\hat{\jmath}$, one obtains $v\,\hat{\imath} + \omega_z\,\hat{k} \wedge (-R\,\hat{\jmath}) = v\,\hat{\imath} + \omega_z\,R\,\hat{\imath} = \vec{0}$, whence $\omega_z = -\dfrac{v}{R}$. The angular velocity turns out to be **clockwise**, because the disk advances to the right.

The moment of inertia of a homogeneous disk about the axis through the center orthogonal to the plane equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$, a value obtained in the topic on the geometry of masses. The angular momentum about $G$ is therefore $I_G\,\vec{\omega}$, clockwise as well.

For the angular momentum about $C$ one applies **König's theorem**: to the term $\vec{K}_G$ one adds the moment of the linear momentum $m\,\vec{v}_G$ applied at $G$, about the pole $C$. The product $\big( R\,\hat{\jmath} \big) \wedge \big( m\,v\,\hat{\imath} \big)$ equals $-m\,R\,v\,\hat{k}$, because $\hat{\jmath} \wedge \hat{\imath} = -\hat{k}$, hence the two contributions have the same sign and add up.

The check computes $\vec{K}_C$ in an independent way, using the fact that at the instant considered the state of motion is rotatory about $C$, and then the angular momentum about $C$ equals $I_C\,\vec{\omega}$, with $I_C$ given by the Huygens-Steiner theorem. The two results coincide.

</div>
