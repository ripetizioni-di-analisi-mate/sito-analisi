## Exercise 1

Two bodies, which can be treated as material points of masses $m$ and $3m$, move on a smooth horizontal guide, as in the diagram in the figure. The body of mass $m$ has velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$ and strikes the body of mass $3m$, which is initially at rest; the impact is elastic and takes place along the guide. Determine the velocities of the two bodies after the impact.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 0.9,0 0.9,0.7 0,0.7;2.6,0 3.8,0 3.8,0.9 2.6,0.9" linee="-0.8,0 4.8,0" frecce="1.05,0.35,2.2,0.35,v_0" etichette="0.45,0.3,m;3.2,0.4,3m" titolo="Head-on impact on a smooth horizontal guide"}

$$
\begin{cases}
m\,v_0 = m\,v_1 + 3m\,v_2 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,v_2^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
v_1 = \frac{m - 3m}{m + 3m}\,v_0 = -\frac{v_0}{2} ,
\qquad
v_2 = \frac{2\,m}{m + 3m}\,v_0 = \frac{v_0}{2}
$$

Check by substituting the two values into the two conditions:
$$
m\,\Big( -\frac{v_0}{2} \Big) + 3m\,\frac{v_0}{2} = m\,v_0 ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,\frac{v_0^2}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,\frac{v_0^2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

During the impact the only external forces are the weights and the reaction of the guide, both vertical and not impulsive, hence the horizontal component of the linear momentum is conserved. The first equation of the system expresses this conservation.

The impact is elastic by hypothesis, hence the kinetic energies immediately before and immediately after the impact are equal, and this is the second equation of the system. The system admits two solutions, and the one with $v_1 = v_0$ and $v_2 = 0$ describes the configuration before the impact; the other one is therefore taken.

The body of mass $m$ **bounces back** with velocity $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$, because it strikes a body of larger mass, and the body of mass $3m$ advances with velocity $\dfrac{v_0}{2}$. The velocity of the center of mass, which equals $\dfrac{v_0}{4}$, stays the same before and after the impact, as the conservation of the linear momentum requires.

The check substitutes the two results into the two starting conditions, and the linear momentum comes back to $m\,v_0$ and the kinetic energy comes back to $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2$, hence both conditions are satisfied.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two bodies, which can be treated as material points of masses $m$ and $3m$, move on a smooth horizontal guide, as in the diagram in the figure. The body of mass $m$ has velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$ and strikes the body of mass $3m$, which is initially at rest; the impact is perfectly inelastic and the two bodies carry on joined together. Determine the common velocity after the impact and the kinetic energy lost.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 0.9,0 0.9,0.7 0,0.7;2.6,0 3.8,0 3.8,0.9 2.6,0.9" linee="-0.8,0 4.8,0" frecce="1.05,0.35,2.2,0.35,v_0" etichette="0.45,0.3,m;3.2,0.4,3m" titolo="The two bodies stay joined after the impact"}

$$
m\,v_0 = (m + 3m)\,v^+
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
v^+ = \frac{v_0}{4}
$$
$$
T^- = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 ,
\qquad
T^+ = \frac{1}{2}\,(4m)\,\frac{v_0^2}{16} = \frac{1}{8}\,m\,v_0^2
$$
$$
T^- - T^+ = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 - \frac{1}{8}\,m\,v_0^2 = \frac{3}{8}\,m\,v_0^2 = \frac{3}{4}\,T^-
$$

Check with König's theorem, using $\vec{v}_G = \dfrac{v_0}{4}\,\hat{\imath}$, which does not change in the impact:
$$
T^- = \frac{1}{2}\,(4m)\,v_G^2 + T'^- = \frac{1}{8}\,m\,v_0^2 + T'^-
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
T'^- = \frac{3}{8}\,m\,v_0^2 ,
$$
and after the impact the two bodies are at rest relative to the center of mass, hence $T'^+ = 0$ and the energy lost is precisely $T'^-$.

<div class="spiegazione">

During the impact the external forces are not impulsive, hence the linear momentum is conserved. The impact is perfectly inelastic, that is the two bodies have the same velocity after the impact, hence the only unknown is the common value $v^+$.

From the conservation of the linear momentum it follows that $v^+ = \dfrac{v_0}{4}$, which coincides with the velocity of the center of mass before the impact, as it must be.

The kinetic energy after the impact is a quarter of the one before, hence the impact dissipates **three quarters** of the initial kinetic energy. The energy lost is turned into permanent deformation and heat and is not recovered.

The check uses König's theorem. The kinetic energy is written as the sum of the term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,M\,v_G^2$, which does not change because $\vec{v}_G$ is conserved, and of the kinetic energy relative to the center of mass. In the perfectly inelastic impact the two bodies end up at rest with respect to each other, hence also with respect to $G$, and $T'$ vanishes. The energy lost is then the whole initial relative kinetic energy, $\dfrac{3}{8}\,m\,v_0^2$, the same value found by the direct route.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $3m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at the endpoint $A$ to a fixed point of a vertical plane, with a smooth hinge, and stands still in the vertical position, as in the diagram in the figure. A material point of mass $m$, moving horizontally with velocity $v_0$, strikes the endpoint $B$ and sticks to it. Determine the angular velocity of the system right after the impact, the maximum angle reached and the impulse of the reaction of the hinge.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3;B,0,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" punti="-1.8,0,P,pieno;0,1.5,G,baricentro" frecce="-1.8,0,-0.35,0" quote="A,B,ℓ,-0.6" etichette="-2.1,0.35,m;-0.75,2.2,3m;-1.05,0.3,v_0" titolo="The point strikes the endpoint $B$ of the rod and sticks to it"}

Angular momentum about $A$, conserved in the impact because the reaction of the hinge has vanishing moment about $A$:
$$
K_A^- = m\,v_0\,\ell ,
\qquad
I_A = \frac{1}{3}\,(3m)\,\ell^2 + m\,\ell^2 = 2\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
K_A^+ = 2\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega
$$
$$
m\,v_0\,\ell = 2\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega = \frac{v_0}{2\,\ell}
$$
After the impact the mechanical energy is conserved; calling $\vartheta$ the angle by which the system rotates starting from the vertical,
$$
T^+ = \frac{1}{2}\,(2\,m\,\ell^2)\,\frac{v_0^2}{4\,\ell^2} = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,v_0^2 ,
\qquad
\Delta V = 3m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,(1 - \cos\vartheta) + m\,g\,\ell\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)
$$
$$
\frac{1}{4}\,m\,v_0^2 = \frac{5}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\,(1 - \cos\vartheta_{\max})
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\cos\vartheta_{\max} = 1 - \frac{v_0^2}{10\,g\,\ell}
$$
Impulse of the reaction of the hinge, from the first cardinal equation integrated over the duration of the impact:
$$
Q^+ = m\,(\omega\,\ell) + 3m\,\Big( \omega\,\frac{\ell}{2} \Big) = \frac{5}{2}\,m\,\omega\,\ell = \frac{5}{4}\,m\,v_0 ,
\qquad
J_A = Q^+ - Q^- = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,v_0
$$

Check by writing the impulsive equations of the two bodies separately. We denote by $J_B$ the magnitude of the impulse that the two bodies exchange at $B$, directed opposite to $v_0$ on the point and in the same direction as $v_0$ on the rod,
$$
m\,(\omega\,\ell) - m\,v_0 = -J_B ,
\qquad
\frac{1}{3}\,(3m)\,\ell^2\,\omega = J_B\,\ell
$$
$$
J_B = m\,\ell\,\omega
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
m\,\ell\,\omega = m\,v_0 - m\,\ell\,\omega
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega = \frac{v_0}{2\,\ell}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the system one takes the rod and the point. During the impact the only impulsive external force is the reaction of the hinge, applied at $A$, whose moment about $A$ vanishes, whereas the weight is not impulsive and gives a negligible impulse. From the integrated second cardinal equation it then follows that the **angular momentum about $A$** is conserved in the impact.

Before the impact the only body in motion is the point, whose velocity is horizontal and whose arm about $A$ equals $\ell$, hence the angular momentum equals $m\,v_0\,\ell$. After the impact the point stays attached to $B$, hence the system is a single rigid body rotating about $A$, with a moment of inertia given by the sum of the contribution of the rod, $\dfrac{1}{3}\,(3m)\,\ell^2$, and of the contribution of the point, $m\,\ell^2$.

The equality of the two angular momenta gives $\omega = \dfrac{v_0}{2\,\ell}$. The kinetic energy goes from $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2$ to $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,v_0^2$, hence the impact dissipates half of the kinetic energy, and it is inelastic because the two bodies carry on joined together.

After the impact the hinge is smooth and the weight is conservative, hence the mechanical energy is conserved. Rotating by $\vartheta$, the center of the rod rises by $\dfrac{\ell}{2}(1 - \cos\vartheta)$ and the point by $\ell\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)$. The maximum angle occurs where the kinetic energy vanishes, and it equals $\cos\vartheta_{\max} = 1 - \dfrac{v_0^2}{10\,g\,\ell}$; the system makes a complete turn only if $v_0^2 \ge 20\,g\,\ell$, that is if the right-hand side drops to $-1$.

The impulse of the reaction of the hinge is obtained from the integrated first cardinal equation. The linear momentum goes from $m\,v_0$ to $\dfrac{5}{4}\,m\,v_0$, hence the hinge provides an impulse of $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,v_0$ **in the same direction** as $v_0$.

The check treats the two bodies separately. For the point one writes the integrated first cardinal equation, for the rod the integrated second cardinal equation with the pole at the hinge. Eliminating the internal impulse $J_B$ between the two relations one recovers $\omega = \dfrac{v_0}{2\,\ell}$, and in addition one obtains $J_B = \dfrac{m\,v_0}{2}$.

</div>
