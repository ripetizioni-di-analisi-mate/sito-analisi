## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at the endpoint $A$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane, with a smooth hinge; the rod is released at rest in the horizontal position, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the angular velocity of the rod and the velocity of the endpoint $B$ when the rod passes through the vertical position.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,-3" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 3,0" punti="0,-1.5,G,baricentro" forze="0 -1.5,0,-0.9,mg" quote="A,B,ℓ,0.7" titolo="The rod starts at rest from the dashed horizontal position and the passage through the vertical is studied"}

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,I_A\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
V = -m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
T + V = \text{constant} = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{3\,g\,\sin\vartheta}{\ell}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{\vartheta}\Big|_{\vartheta = \frac{\pi}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{3\,g}{\ell}} ,
\qquad
v_B = \ell\,\dot{\vartheta} = \sqrt{3\,g\,\ell}
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation, pole at the fixed hinge $A$:
$$
\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\cos\vartheta
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g\,\cos\vartheta}{2\,\ell}
$$
Multiplying by $\dot{\vartheta}$ and integrating with the initial datum $\dot{\vartheta}(0) = 0$ one obtains
$$
\frac{\dot{\vartheta}^2}{2} = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\,\sin\vartheta
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{3\,g\,\sin\vartheta}{\ell}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod has only one degree of freedom and as a Lagrangian coordinate one takes the angle $\vartheta$ by which the rod has descended below the horizontal, with $\vartheta = 0$ in the initial configuration. The state of motion is rotatory about $A$, hence the kinetic energy equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_A\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ with $I_A = \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$.

The hinge is a fixed and ideal constraint, hence its reaction has zero power and the weight is the only force that does work. The weight is conservative with potential $U = -m\,g\,y_G$, and the ordinate of the center of mass equals $y_G = -\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\sin\vartheta$, hence $V = -U = -m\,g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\sin\vartheta$. The hypotheses of the theorem on the conservation of the mechanical energy are therefore satisfied.

The constant is computed in the initial configuration, in which $T = 0$ and $V = 0$, hence $E = 0$. The equality $T + V = 0$ directly gives $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ as a function of $\vartheta$, without integrating the equation of motion, and it is therefore a **first integral**.

In the vertical position we have $\sin\vartheta = 1$, hence $\dot{\vartheta} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3\,g}{\ell}}$ and the endpoint $B$, which is at distance $\ell$ from the hinge, has velocity $\sqrt{3\,g\,\ell}$, directed **horizontally**.

The check follows the dynamical route. The second cardinal equation with the pole at the fixed hinge gives the equation of motion, because the reaction of the hinge has vanishing moment about $A$. Multiplying by $\dot{\vartheta}$, the left-hand side becomes the derivative of $\dfrac{\dot{\vartheta}^2}{2}$ and the right-hand side the derivative of $\dfrac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\,\sin\vartheta$; integrating with the initial datum one recovers the same first integral.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed inclined plane of angle $\alpha$, starting from rest, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the velocity of the center when the latter has descended by a height difference $h$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 5,0 5,3" cerchi="1.84,1.92,0.7" punti="2.2,1.32,C,centro;1.84,1.92,G,baricentro;0.34,1.02,G′,baricentro" linee="-0.9,0 5.6,0" tratteggi="1.84,1.92 -0.3,1.92;0.34,1.02 -0.3,1.02" quote="-0.3 1.02,-0.3 1.92,h,-0.5" etichette="0.95,0.18,α" titolo="The disk rolls without slipping and its center descends by the height difference $h$"}

$$
v_G = R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{v_G^2}{R^2} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2
$$
$$
T + V = \text{constant}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2 = m\,g\,h
$$
$$
v_G = \sqrt{\frac{4\,g\,h}{3}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\,\sqrt{g\,h}
$$

Check with the kinetic energy computed as a rotatory state of motion about the contact point $C$. The Huygens-Steiner theorem gives $I_C = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$, hence
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{v_G^2}{R^2} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pure rolling constraint leaves only one degree of freedom, and the rolling condition relates the velocity of the center to the angular velocity: $v_G = R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$. As the unknown one takes directly $v_G$ in the final configuration.

The external forces are the weight and the reaction of the plane, applied at the contact point $C$. In rolling without slipping the contact point has zero velocity, hence the reaction, including the friction component, has zero power; the internal forces of the rigid disk have zero power as well. The only force that does work is the weight, which is conservative, hence the theorem on the conservation of the mechanical energy applies.

The kinetic energy is written with König's theorem, summing the translation term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2$ and the rotation term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ with $I_G = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$; substituting the rolling condition one obtains $T = \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2$.

The disk starts from rest, hence the final kinetic energy is equal to the potential energy lost, $m\,g\,h$. The velocity turns out to be $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\,\sqrt{g\,h}$, **smaller** than $\sqrt{2\,g\,h}$, which is the velocity of a point descending by the same height difference on a smooth constraint, because in the disk part of the energy goes into the rotation. The result does not depend on $\alpha$, because it depends only on the height difference.

The check computes $T$ in an independent way, as the kinetic energy of a rotatory state of motion about the contact point, and gives the same value.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two bodies of masses $m$ and $2m$, which can be treated as material points, are joined by an inextensible string of negligible mass passing over a homogeneous pulley of mass $2m$ and radius $R$, hinged at its center to a fixed point, as in the diagram in the figure. The string does not slip on the pulley and the system starts from rest. Determine the velocity of the two bodies when the body of mass $2m$ has descended by a distance $h$.

::struttura{nodi="S,0,3.2,-" vincoli="S,incastro,90" cerchi="0,2.6,0.6" punti="0,2.6,,centro" linee="-0.6,2.6 -0.6,1.3;0.6,2.6 0.6,0.7" poligoni="-1,0.7 -0.2,0.7 -0.2,1.3 -1,1.3;0.2,0.1 1,0.1 1,0.7 0.2,0.7" tratteggi="0,2.6 0.6,2.6" etichette="-0.6,1,m;0.6,0.4,2m;-1.3,2.8,2m;0.3,2.8,R" titolo="Pulley of non-negligible mass: the string does not slip in the groove"}

$$
v_1 = v_2 = v ,
\qquad
\dot{\vartheta} = \frac{v}{R}
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,v^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2\,\frac{v^2}{R^2} = 2\,m\,v^2
$$
$$
T + V = \text{constant}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
2\,m\,v^2 = (2m)\,g\,h - m\,g\,h = m\,g\,h
$$
$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{g\,h}{2}}
$$

Check with the acceleration. The motion is uniformly accelerated, because the acceleration is constant, hence $v^2 = 2\,a\,h$ gives $a = \dfrac{v^2}{2\,h} = \dfrac{g}{4}$. Writing instead the equations of the two bodies and of the pulley, with $T_1$ and $T_2$ the tensions of the two portions of string,
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a = T_1 - m\,g \\[4pt]
2m\,a = 2m\,g - T_2 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2\,\dfrac{a}{R} = (T_2 - T_1)\,R
\end{cases}
$$
and summing the three equations one obtains $4\,m\,a = m\,g$, that is $a = \dfrac{g}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, hence the two bodies have velocities of the same magnitude $v$ and the pulley rotates with $\dot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{v}{R}$. The system has only one degree of freedom and as the unknown one takes $v$.

The external forces that do work are the two weights. The reaction of the hinge of the pulley has zero power, because the center is fixed; the tensions of the string are forces internal to the system, and since the string is inextensible and does not slip, their total work vanishes. The theorem on the conservation of the mechanical energy therefore applies.

The kinetic energy sums the three contributions: the two bodies, treated as material points, and the pulley, which rotates about its center with moment of inertia $\dfrac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2 = m\,R^2$. The total equals $2\,m\,v^2$.

In the stage considered the body of mass $2m$ descends by $h$ and the one of mass $m$ rises by $h$, hence the potential energy decreases by $2m\,g\,h - m\,g\,h = m\,g\,h$. Equating it to the kinetic energy one obtains $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{g\,h}{2}}$, **smaller** than the one that would be obtained with a pulley of negligible mass, because part of the energy goes into the rotation of the pulley.

The check follows the dynamical route. The first two equations are Newton's equations of the two bodies, with the two tensions different because the pulley has mass; the third one is the second cardinal equation for the pulley, with the pole at the fixed center. Summing the three equations the tensions cancel and one obtains $a = \dfrac{g}{4}$, the same value that follows from the energy.

</div>
