## Exercise 1

Three material points of masses $m$, $2m$ and $m$ occupy the positions $P_1 = (0, 0)$, $P_2 = (2\ell, 0)$ and $P_3 = (0, 3\ell)$ of the fixed frame $(O; x, y)$, as in the diagram in the figure. At the instant considered the velocities of the three points are $\vec{v}_1 = v\,\hat{\imath}$, $\vec{v}_2 = \vec{0}$ and $\vec{v}_3 = -v\,\hat{\jmath}$. Determine the center of mass $G$, the linear momentum $\vec{Q}$ and the velocity $\vec{v}_G$.

::struttura{punti="0,0,P_1,pieno;2,0,P_2,pieno;0,3,P_3,pieno" frecce="0,0,1,0;0,3,0,2" etichette="0.95,0.25,v;0.3,2.1,v;-0.3,-0.35,m;2.25,-0.35,2m;-0.35,3.3,m" quote="0 0,2 0,2ℓ,-0.5;0 0,0 3,3ℓ,-0.5" titolo="The three points with their velocities at the instant considered"}

$$
M = m + 2m + m = 4m
$$
$$
G - O = \frac{m\,(P_1 - O) + 2m\,(P_2 - O) + m\,(P_3 - O)}{M}
= \frac{2m \cdot 2\ell}{4m}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{m \cdot 3\ell}{4m}\,\hat{\jmath}
= \ell\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{3\,\ell}{4}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{Q} = m\,\vec{v}_1 + 2m\,\vec{v}_2 + m\,\vec{v}_3 = m\,v\,\hat{\imath} - m\,v\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_G = \frac{\vec{Q}}{M} = \frac{v}{4}\,\hat{\imath} - \frac{v}{4}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$

Check by first grouping $P_1$ and $P_3$: their partial center of mass $G_{13}$ has mass $2m$ and position
$$
G_{13} - O = \frac{m \cdot \vec{0} + m \cdot 3\ell\,\hat{\jmath}}{2m} = \frac{3\,\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
$$
G - O = \frac{2m\,\Big( \dfrac{3\,\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big) + 2m\,\big( 2\ell\,\hat{\imath} \big)}{4m} = \ell\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{3\,\ell}{4}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The frame is the fixed one $(O; x, y)$ indicated in the text, because the positions of the three points are already assigned in those coordinates. The unknowns are the position of $G$ and the two vectors $\vec{Q}$ and $\vec{v}_G$, and they are all computed with sums weighted by the masses.

The **center of mass** is defined by $M\,(G - O) = \sum_i m_i\,(P_i - O)$. The point $P_1$ coincides with the origin, therefore it contributes the zero vector; the point $P_2$ contributes only to the $x$ component and the point $P_3$ only to the $y$ component. The center of mass turns out to be interior to the triangle whose vertices are the three points, and it lies shifted toward $P_2$ because $P_2$ has twice the mass.

The **linear momentum** is obtained by summing the vectors $m_i\,\vec{v}_i$, and the point $P_2$ is at rest, therefore it does not contribute, and the two terms $m v\,\hat{\imath}$ and $-m v\,\hat{\jmath}$ remain. From the theorem on the linear momentum and the velocity of the center of mass it follows that $\vec{v}_G = \dfrac{\vec{Q}}{M}$, hence the center of mass moves with a velocity of magnitude $\dfrac{v\,\sqrt{2}}{4}$, inclined by $45^\circ$ below the $x$ axis.

The check uses the associative property of the center of mass: $P_1$ and $P_3$ are replaced by their partial center of mass $G_{13}$, in which the mass $2m$ is concentrated, and the result is combined with $P_2$. The value of $G$ **coincides** with the one found by the direct route.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two homogeneous rods $OA$ and $AB$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, are welded at a right angle at $A$ and form the rigid body in the figure, with $OA$ along the $x$ axis and $AB$ parallel to the $y$ axis. The body rotates about the fixed point $O$ with angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$. Determine the center of mass $G$ and the linear momentum $\vec{Q}$ at the instant in which the body occupies the configuration in the figure.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2,0;B,2,2" aste="O-A;A-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,270" punti="1.5,0.5,G,baricentro" coppie="0.7 0.7,1,ω" quote="O,A,ℓ,0.6;A,B,ℓ,0.6" titolo="The two rods welded at $A$ rotate about the fixed point $O$"}

$$
G - O = \frac{m\,\Big( \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\imath} \Big) + m\,\Big( \ell\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big)}{2m}
= \frac{3\,\ell}{4}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{4}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_G = \vec{\omega} \wedge (G - O) = \omega\,\hat{k} \wedge \Big( \frac{3\,\ell}{4}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{4}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big) = -\frac{\omega\,\ell}{4}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{3\,\omega\,\ell}{4}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{Q} = 2m\,\vec{v}_G = -\frac{m\,\omega\,\ell}{2}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{3\,m\,\omega\,\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
|\vec{Q}| = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\,m\,\omega\,\ell
$$

Check with the pole at $A$: with respect to $A$ the centers of the two rods lie at $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\imath}$ and at $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence
$$
G - A = \frac{m\,\Big( -\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\imath} \Big) + m\,\Big( \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big)}{2m} = -\frac{\ell}{4}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{4}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - O = (G - A) + (A - O) = \frac{3\,\ell}{4}\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{4}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body is rigid and has the point $O$ fixed, therefore it has only one degree of freedom and its state of motion is rotatory about $O$. The position of $G$ is computed in the configuration drawn, with the fixed frame having its origin at $O$.

Each homogeneous rod has its own center of mass at the midpoint, because the density is constant and the midpoint is a center of material symmetry of the segment. The center of the rod $OA$ lies at $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\imath}$ and the center of the rod $AB$ lies at $\ell\,\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\hat{\jmath}$. The two masses are equal, hence the center of mass of the system is the midpoint of the two centers.

The velocity of $G$ is obtained from the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics with the pole at the fixed point $O$, treated in the topic on the kinematics of the rigid body. Since $\vec{v}_O = \vec{0}$, there remains $\vec{v}_G = \vec{\omega} \wedge (G - O)$. The cross product rotates the vector $G - O$ by $90^\circ$ counterclockwise and multiplies its magnitude by $\omega$. From the theorem on the linear momentum and the velocity of the center of mass it follows that $\vec{Q} = 2m\,\vec{v}_G$, with magnitude $\dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\,m\,\omega\,\ell$, **orthogonal** to the segment $OG$.

The check computes $G$ with respect to the pole $A$ and then brings the result back to the pole $O$ by adding $A - O = \ell\,\hat{\imath}$. The two values coincide, as they must, because the position of $G$ is a point of the body and does not depend on the pole with which it is computed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A cart of mass $3m$ slides without friction on a horizontal guide and on its upper surface, which is smooth as well, there lies a material point of mass $m$, as in the diagram in the figure. The system starts from rest. Because of an internal action the point moves a distance $d$ to the right relative to the cart. Determine the displacement of the cart and the displacement of the point relative to the guide.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0.3 4,0.3 4,1.3 0,1.3" cerchi="0.9,0.3,0.3;3.1,0.3,0.3" linee="-1,0 5.6,0" punti="1,1.3,P,pieno" frecce="1,1.75,2.8,1.75" etichette="2.2,0.75,3m;0.55,1.8,m;1.9,2.05,d" titolo="The point $P$ moves by $d$ relative to the cart, on a smooth guide and a smooth surface"}

$$
\vec{R}^{(e)} \cdot \hat{\imath} = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
Q_x = 0 \ \text{ for every } t
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
x_G = \text{constant}
$$

Calling $X$ the displacement of the cart and $X + d$ that of the point, both measured on the fixed guide,
$$
3m\,X + m\,(X + d) = 0
$$
$$
X = -\frac{d}{4} ,
\qquad
X + d = \frac{3\,d}{4}
$$

Check on the linear momentum: calling $u$ the velocity of the cart and $u + \dot{d}$ that of the point, we have
$$
Q_x = 3m\,u + m\,(u + \dot{d}) = 4m\,u + m\,\dot{d} = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
u = -\frac{\dot{d}}{4} ,
$$
and integrating in time one recovers $X = -\dfrac{d}{4}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The external forces acting on the system formed by the cart and the point are the two weights, which are vertical, and the reaction of the smooth guide, which is vertical as well. The horizontal component of the external resultant is therefore zero, hence from the first cardinal equation it follows that $Q_x$ is constant. The system starts from rest, so $Q_x = 0$ at every instant and the abscissa of the center of mass **does not change**.

The actions that the point and the cart exchange, including those that produce the relative displacement, are internal forces, therefore they have zero resultant and do not alter this conclusion.

The relative displacement is $d$ to the right, therefore if the cart moves by $X$ the point moves by $X + d$ relative to the guide. The condition that $x_G$ is constant is written $3m\,X + m\,(X + d) = 0$, whence $X = -\dfrac{d}{4}$, therefore the cart recedes by $\dfrac{d}{4}$, that is it moves **to the left**, whereas the point advances by $\dfrac{3\,d}{4}$ to the right. The two displacements are in the inverse ratio of the masses.

The check starts from the same condition written on the velocities instead of on the displacements. The velocity of the point relative to the guide is $u + \dot{d}$, and imposing $Q_x = 0$ gives $u = -\dfrac{\dot{d}}{4}$. Integrating between the initial instant and the final one one obtains again $X = -\dfrac{d}{4}$.

</div>
