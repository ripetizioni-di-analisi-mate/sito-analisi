## Exercise 1

A homogeneous circular platform of mass $4m$ and radius $R$ rotates without friction about a fixed vertical axis through its center $O$, in a horizontal plane, as in the diagram in the figure, seen from above. On the platform, in contact with the rim, there lies a material point of mass $m$, rigidly connected to the platform; the system rotates with angular velocity $\omega_0$. The point is then moved to the center $O$ and stays there at rest relative to the platform. Determine the final angular velocity and the change of kinetic energy.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" punti="0,0,O,centro;2,0,P,pieno" frecce="1.8,0,0.35,0" coppie="-1.2 1.2,1,ω_0" etichette="0.95,-0.35,R;2.1,0.45,m;-1.3,-1.1,4m" titolo="Seen from above: the point $P$ is brought from the rim to the center of the platform"}

$$
I_i = \frac{1}{2}\,(4m)\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = 3\,m\,R^2 ,
\qquad
I_f = \frac{1}{2}\,(4m)\,R^2 = 2\,m\,R^2
$$
$$
M_z^{(e)} = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
K_z = I_i\,\omega_0 = I_f\,\omega_f
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\omega_f = \frac{3\,m\,R^2}{2\,m\,R^2}\,\omega_0 = \frac{3}{2}\,\omega_0
$$
$$
\Delta T = \frac{1}{2}\,I_f\,\omega_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,I_i\,\omega_0^2
= \frac{1}{2}\,(2\,m\,R^2)\,\frac{9}{4}\,\omega_0^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,(3\,m\,R^2)\,\omega_0^2
= \frac{3}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2
$$

Check by writing the kinetic energy as a function of the angular momentum: from $K_z = I\,\omega$ it follows that $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I\,\omega^2 = \dfrac{K_z^2}{2\,I}$, with $K_z = 3\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0$ constant, hence
$$
T_i = \frac{9\,m^2\,R^4\,\omega_0^2}{2 \cdot 3\,m\,R^2} = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
T_f = \frac{9\,m^2\,R^4\,\omega_0^2}{2 \cdot 2\,m\,R^2} = \frac{9}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
\Delta T = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the system one takes the platform and the point. The external forces are the weights, which are vertical, and the reaction of the axis, whose line of action meets the axis itself. The moment of all these forces about the vertical axis $z$ through $O$ vanishes, hence from the second cardinal equation, projected onto the fixed axis, it follows that the **axial angular momentum** $K_z$ is conserved. The actions that move the point are internal to the system.

The initial moment of inertia sums the contribution of the platform, $\dfrac{1}{2}\,(4m)\,R^2$, obtained in the topic on the geometry of masses, and the contribution $m\,R^2$ of the point on the rim. In the final configuration the point lies on the axis, hence its distance from the axis vanishes and it no longer contributes.

From the conservation $I_i\,\omega_0 = I_f\,\omega_f$ it follows that $\omega_f = \dfrac{3}{2}\,\omega_0$, hence the system rotates **faster** because the moment of inertia has decreased.

The kinetic energy increases by $\dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\omega_0^2$. The increase is the work done by the internal forces that moved the point toward the center, since by the kinetic energy theorem the change of $T$ is equal to the work of all the forces; the internal forces of a non-rigid system do in general a nonzero work.

The check obtains the two kinetic energies from $T = \dfrac{K_z^2}{2\,I}$, an expression that uses only the conserved angular momentum and the two moments of inertia, without going through $\omega_f$. The change turns out to be the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A material point of mass $m$ moves on a smooth horizontal plane, tied to an inextensible string of negligible mass that passes through a hole $O$ of the plane, as in the diagram in the figure. At the beginning the point describes a circle of radius $2R$ with velocity of magnitude $v_0$. The string is pulled slowly from below until the radius becomes $R$. Determine the velocity of the point in the final configuration and the work done on the string.

::struttura{punti="0,0,O,centro;4,0,P,pieno" linee="0,0 4,0" frecce="4,0,4,2.2,v_0" quote="0 0,4 0,2R,0.7" etichette="4.3,-0.35,m" titolo="Seen from above: the point turns about the hole $O$ held by the string"}

$$
\vec{M}_O^{(e)} = (P - O) \wedge \vec{\Phi} = \vec{0}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
K_O = m\,v\,r = \text{constant}
$$
$$
m\,v_0\,(2R) = m\,v_f\,R
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
v_f = 2\,v_0
$$
$$
L = T_f - T_i = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,(2\,v_0)^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,v_0^2
$$

Check with the kinetic energy written as a function of the angular momentum, $T = \dfrac{K_O^2}{2\,m\,r^2}$, with $K_O = 2\,m\,v_0\,R$ constant:
$$
T_i = \frac{4\,m^2\,v_0^2\,R^2}{2\,m\,(2R)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_0^2 ,
\qquad
T_f = \frac{4\,m^2\,v_0^2\,R^2}{2\,m\,R^2} = 2\,m\,v_0^2 ,
\qquad
T_f - T_i = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,v_0^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point is subject to the weight, to the normal reaction of the smooth plane and to the tension of the string. The weight and the normal reaction are vertical and have vanishing moment about the vertical axis through $O$; the tension is directed from $P$ toward $O$, hence its line of action passes through $O$ and its moment about $O$ vanishes as well. From the second cardinal equation it follows that $\vec{K}_O$ is conserved.

As long as the string is pulled slowly the radial velocity is negligible, hence the velocity of the point is orthogonal to the string and the magnitude of the angular momentum equals $m\,v\,r$, with $r$ the distance from the hole.

From the conservation $m\,v_0\,(2R) = m\,v_f\,R$ it follows that $v_f = 2\,v_0$, hence halving the radius the velocity **doubles**.

The work done on the string is equal to the change of the kinetic energy, by the kinetic energy theorem. The weight and the normal reaction have zero power, because they are orthogonal to the velocity, hence the only force that does work is the tension. The work turns out to be positive and equals $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,v_0^2$, since the tension has a component in the same direction as the radial displacement toward the center.

The check computes the two kinetic energies from the conserved angular momentum, with $T = \dfrac{K_O^2}{2\,m\,r^2}$, and recovers the same work.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous circular platform of mass $6m$ and radius $R$ can rotate without friction about a fixed vertical axis through its center $O$, in a horizontal plane, as in the diagram in the figure, seen from above. The system is initially at rest, with a person of mass $m$ standing still on the rim. The person starts walking along the rim with velocity of magnitude $u$ relative to the platform. Determine the angular velocity of the platform.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" punti="0,0,O,centro;0,2,P,pieno" frecce="-0.3,2,-1.7,2,u" etichette="0.5,2.25,m;-1.35,-1.1,6m;1,-0.35,R" tratteggi="0,0 2,0" titolo="Seen from above: the person walks along the rim of the platform, which is initially at rest"}

We denote by $\omega$ the angular velocity of the platform, positive counterclockwise; the velocity of the person relative to the ground, along the rim, is then $\omega\,R + u$.
$$
K_z = 0 \ \text{ at every instant}
$$
$$
\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,(6m)\,R^2\,\omega}_{\text{platform}} + \underbrace{m\,R\,(\omega\,R + u)}_{\text{person}} = 0
$$
$$
3\,m\,R^2\,\omega + m\,R^2\,\omega + m\,R\,u = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\omega = -\frac{u}{4\,R}
$$

Check by computing the two absolute velocities and the angular momentum that follows from them:
$$
\omega\,R = -\frac{u}{4} ,
\qquad
\omega\,R + u = \frac{3\,u}{4} ,
$$
$$
K_z = 3\,m\,R^2\,\Big( -\frac{u}{4\,R} \Big) + m\,R\,\frac{3\,u}{4} = -\frac{3}{4}\,m\,R\,u + \frac{3}{4}\,m\,R\,u = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is formed by the platform and the person. The external forces, the weights and the reaction of the axis, have vanishing moment about the vertical axis through $O$, hence $K_z$ is conserved; the system starts from rest, hence $K_z = 0$ at every instant. The friction forces between the shoes and the platform, which allow the person to walk, are internal to the system.

The velocity of the person relative to the ground is obtained with the theorem of composition of velocities, treated in the topic on the kinematics of the point and relative motions, since to the dragging velocity $\omega\,R$ of the point of the platform on which the person stands one adds the relative velocity $u$.

The axial angular momentum of the platform equals $I_O\,\omega$ with $I_O = \dfrac{1}{2}\,(6m)\,R^2 = 3\,m\,R^2$; that of the person, treated as a material point on the rim, equals $m\,R\,(\omega R + u)$. Imposing that the sum vanishes one obtains a first-degree equation in $\omega$.

The result $\omega = -\dfrac{u}{4\,R}$ says that the platform rotates **in the opposite sense** to the one in which the person walks, with a rim velocity $\dfrac{u}{4}$, whereas the person advances relative to the ground with velocity $\dfrac{3\,u}{4}$. As soon as the person stops relative to the platform, $u = 0$ gives $\omega = 0$ and the whole system returns to rest.

The check computes the two absolute velocities and substitutes them into the angular momentum, and the two contributions have equal magnitudes and opposite signs, hence the sum vanishes as required.

</div>
