## Exercise 1

Two material points of equal mass $m$ are fixed at the endpoints of a rigid rod of negligible mass and length $2\ell$, as in the diagram in the figure. The system moves in a smooth horizontal plane. At the instant considered the midpoint $G$ of the rod has velocity of magnitude $v$ and the rod rotates with angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$. Compute the kinetic energy of the system.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,1.5" aste="A-B" punti="0,0,,pieno;4,1.5,,pieno;2,0.75,G,baricentro" frecce="2,0.75,3.1,2.1,v_G" coppie="1.1 1.4,1,ω" etichette="-0.35,-0.3,m;4.35,1.75,m" quote="A,B,2ℓ,0.7" titolo="Two equal masses at the endpoints of a rigid rod of negligible mass"}

$$
I_G = m\,\ell^2 + m\,\ell^2 = 2\,m\,\ell^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,v_G^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\omega^2 = m\,v^2 + m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2
$$

Check with the direct computation of the two velocities. Calling $\vec{u}$ the unit vector of the rod directed from $G$ toward the endpoint $B$, we have $P_B - G = \ell\,\vec{u}$ and $P_A - G = -\ell\,\vec{u}$, hence
$$
\vec{v}_B = \vec{v}_G + \omega\,\ell\,(\hat{k} \wedge \vec{u}) ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_A = \vec{v}_G - \omega\,\ell\,(\hat{k} \wedge \vec{u}) ,
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( v_A^2 + v_B^2 \big) = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( 2\,v^2 + 2\,\omega^2\,\ell^2 \big) = m\,v^2 + m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is rigid and moves in a plane, hence it has three degrees of freedom; the data of the problem directly assign the velocity of the center of mass and the angular velocity, which are the quantities on which the kinetic energy depends.

The center of mass coincides with the midpoint of the rod, because the two masses are equal. The moment of inertia about the axis through $G$ orthogonal to the plane is computed by summing the contributions $m\,\ell^2$ of the two points, each at distance $\ell$ from $G$; the rod has negligible mass and does not contribute.

**König's theorem** separates the kinetic energy into the two terms $\dfrac{1}{2}\,M\,v_G^2$ and $T'$, and in the frame translating with $G$ the motion is a rotation with angular velocity $\omega$, hence $T' = \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\omega^2$. The two summands are read as the contribution of the translation of the whole mass and as the contribution of the rotation about $G$.

In the check the velocities of the two points are computed with the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics. Expanding the squares of the magnitudes, the two cross terms $\pm\,2\,\omega\,\ell\,\vec{v}_G \cdot (\hat{k} \wedge \vec{u})$ have opposite signs and cancel in the sum, hence $2 v^2 + 2 \omega^2 \ell^2$ remains. The kinetic energy coincides with the one given by König's theorem.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ moves in a vertical plane with the endpoint $A$ constrained to a smooth horizontal guide and the endpoint $B$ constrained to a smooth vertical guide, as in the diagram in the figure. Calling $\vartheta$ the angle that the rod forms with the horizontal guide, compute the kinetic energy of the rod as a function of $\vartheta$ and $\dot{\vartheta}$.

::struttura{nodi="A,3.46,0;B,0,2" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,270;B,carrello,180" linee="-0.6,0 4.6,0;0,-0.6 0,3" punti="1.73,1,G,baricentro;3.46,2,C,centro" tratteggi="3.46,0 3.46,2;0,2 3.46,2" etichette="2.85,0.16,ϑ" titolo="Rod with its endpoints on two orthogonal guides: $C$ is the instantaneous center of rotation"}

$$
A - O = \ell\,\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
B - O = \ell\,\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - O = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{2}\,\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_G = -\frac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
v_G^2 = \frac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\frac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$

Check with the instantaneous center of rotation $C$, the intersection of the vertical through $A$ with the horizontal through $B$. The quadrilateral $OACB$ is a rectangle, hence $|G - C| = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and
$$
I_C = \frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,\frac{\ell^2}{4} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two endpoints slide on fixed guides, hence the rod has only one degree of freedom and as a Lagrangian coordinate one takes the angle $\vartheta$, which identifies the configuration without ambiguity as long as the rod stays in the first quadrant.

The coordinates of $A$ and of $B$ are written at once from the drawing, and the center of mass is the midpoint of the segment $AB$ because the rod is homogeneous. Differentiating with respect to time one obtains $\vec{v}_G$; the square of its magnitude equals $\dfrac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\,(\sin^2\vartheta + \cos^2\vartheta)$, hence it simplifies to $\dfrac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$. The center of mass moves on a circle of center $O$ and radius $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$.

The angular velocity of the rod is $\dot{\vartheta}$, because $\vartheta$ is precisely the angle that the rod forms with a fixed direction. König's theorem then gives $T = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,I_G\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ with $I_G = \dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2$. The kinetic energy turns out to be **independent of $\vartheta$** and proportional to $\dot{\vartheta}^2$.

The check uses the instantaneous center of rotation. The velocity of $A$ is horizontal, hence $C$ lies on the vertical through $A$; the velocity of $B$ is vertical, hence $C$ lies on the horizontal through $B$. The point $C$ is the fourth vertex of the rectangle $OACB$, and its distance from $G$ is half of the diagonal $AB$, that is $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$. Since the state of motion is rotatory about $C$, the kinetic energy equals $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, and the same value is recovered.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide; a material point $P$ of mass $m$ is welded on the rim of the disk, as in the diagram in the figure. The angle $\varphi$ measures the rotation of the disk starting from the configuration in which $P$ occupies the contact point. Compute the kinetic energy of the system as a function of $\varphi$ and $\dot{\varphi}$.

::struttura{cerchi="3,1.5,1.5" linee="0,0 7,0" punti="3,1.5,G,baricentro;3,0,C,centro;1.7,0.75,P,pieno" tratteggi="3,1.5 3,0;3,1.5 1.7,0.75" frecce="3,1.5,4.9,1.5,v_G" etichette="2.5,0.92,φ" titolo="Disk rolling with a point of mass $m$ welded on the rim"}

$$
x_G = R\,\varphi ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_G = R\,\dot{\varphi}\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} = -\dot{\varphi}\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
P - G = -R\,\sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} - R\,\cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = \vec{v}_G + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - G) = R\,\dot{\varphi}\,(1 - \cos\varphi)\,\hat{\imath} + R\,\dot{\varphi}\,\sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
v_P^2 = R^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2\,\big[ (1 - \cos\varphi)^2 + \sin^2\varphi \big] = 2\,R^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2\,(1 - \cos\varphi)
$$
$$
T = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,\Big( \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2 \Big)\,\dot{\varphi}^2}_{\text{disk}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_P^2}_{\text{point}}
= m\,R^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2\,\Big( \frac{5}{2} - \cos\varphi \Big)
$$

Check with the moment of inertia about the contact point $C$. The chord joining two points of the rim separated by the angle $\varphi$ has length $2R\sin\dfrac{\varphi}{2}$, hence $|P - C|^2 = 2\,R^2\,(1 - \cos\varphi)$ and
$$
I_C = \underbrace{m\,R^2 + 2m\,R^2}_{\text{disk}} + \underbrace{2\,m\,R^2\,(1 - \cos\varphi)}_{\text{point}} = m\,R^2\,(5 - 2\cos\varphi) ,
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = m\,R^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2\,\Big( \frac{5}{2} - \cos\varphi \Big)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pure rolling constraint leaves only one degree of freedom, and as a Lagrangian coordinate one takes the rotation angle $\varphi$; the abscissa of the center then equals $x_G = R\,\varphi$, because the arc that unwinds on the guide is equal to the path traveled.

The disk advances to the right as $\varphi$ grows, hence its angular velocity is clockwise and equals $-\dot{\varphi}\,\hat{k}$. The position of $P$ with respect to the center is read on the drawing. For $\varphi = 0$ the point lies at the contact, that is at $-R\,\hat{\jmath}$, and as $\varphi$ grows it rotates clockwise.

The kinetic energy of the disk is computed with König's theorem, using $I_G = \dfrac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2 = m\,R^2$; that of the point is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_P^2$, with $v_P^2$ obtained from the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics. The result depends on $\varphi$ through the term $-\cos\varphi$, hence the kinetic energy is **minimum** in the configuration in which $P$ touches the guide, where the velocity of $P$ vanishes, and **maximum** when $P$ lies at the highest point, where the velocity of $P$ equals $2\,R\,\dot{\varphi}$.

The check computes the kinetic energy as $\dfrac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\dot{\varphi}^2$, which is legitimate because at the instant considered the whole system has a rotatory state of motion about the contact point $C$, which has zero velocity. The moment of inertia of the disk about $C$ is obtained with the Huygens-Steiner theorem and that of the point is $m\,|P - C|^2$. The two results coincide.

</div>
