## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rectangular lamina of mass $m$ and sides $2\ell$ and $\ell$ moves by inertia about its own center of mass $G$, held fixed, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the Euler equations of the motion, identify the permanent rotations and establish their stability, computing the growth coefficient of the perturbation in the unstable case.

::struttura{poligoni="-2,-1 2,-1 2,1 -2,1" nodi="G,0,0,-" frecce="0,0,3,0,ξ;0,0,0,2,η" punti="0,0,G,baricentro" etichette="0.45,-0.3,ζ out of page" quote="-2 -1,2 -1,2ℓ,0.8;2 -1,2 1,ℓ,0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Homogeneous rectangular lamina with a fixed center of mass: the axes $\xi$, $\eta$, $\zeta$ are principal of inertia by symmetry"}

Frame and principal moments. The axes $\xi$ and $\eta$ are axes of material symmetry of the lamina and $\zeta$ is perpendicular to the plane, hence the frame is principal of inertia at $G$. The principal moments of a rectangular lamina of sides $a = 2\ell$ along $\xi$ and $b = \ell$ along $\eta$ are
$$
A = \frac{m\,b^2}{12} = \frac{m\,\ell^2}{12} ,
\qquad
B = \frac{m\,a^2}{12} = \frac{m\,\ell^2}{3} ,
\qquad
C = \frac{m\,\big( a^2 + b^2 \big)}{12} = \frac{5\,m\,\ell^2}{12} ,
$$
hence $A : B : C = 1 : 4 : 5$ and the axis $\eta$ is the one of intermediate moment.

Euler equations. The weight has zero moment with respect to $G$ and the reaction of the constraint is applied at $G$, therefore $\vec{M}_G = \vec{0}$ and
$$
\begin{cases}
A\,\dot{p} + (C-B)\,q\,r = 0 \\[4pt]
B\,\dot{q} + (A-C)\,r\,p = 0 \\[4pt]
C\,\dot{r} + (B-A)\,p\,q = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Substituting the three moments and dividing by $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{12}$:
$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{p} = -q\,r \\[4pt]
4\,\dot{q} = 4\,r\,p \\[4pt]
5\,\dot{r} = -3\,p\,q
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\begin{cases}
\dot{p} = -q\,r \\[4pt]
\dot{q} = r\,p \\[4pt]
\dot{r} = -\dfrac{3}{5}\,p\,q
\end{cases}
$$
Permanent rotations. The three moments are distinct, hence the permanent rotations are those about the three principal axes:
$$
\vec{\omega} = p_0\,\hat{e}_\xi ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} = q_0\,\hat{e}_\eta ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} = r_0\,\hat{e}_\zeta .
$$
Stability. The coefficient that appears in the linearized equation of the perturbation about the axis of moment $A$ is $\dfrac{(C-A)(A-B)}{B\,C}\,p_0^2$:
$$
\frac{(C-A)(A-B)}{B\,C} = \frac{(5-1)(1-4)}{4 \cdot 5} = -\frac{3}{5} < 0 ,
$$
hence the rotation about $\xi$ is stable. About $\zeta$ the roles are exchanged and one obtains
$$
\frac{(B-C)(C-A)}{A\,B} = \frac{(4-5)(5-1)}{1 \cdot 4} = -1 < 0 ,
$$
hence the rotation about $\zeta$ is stable as well. About $\eta$, instead,
$$
\frac{(A-B)(B-C)}{A\,C} = \frac{(1-4)(4-5)}{1 \cdot 5} = \frac{3}{5} > 0 ,
$$
hence the rotation about the intermediate axis is unstable, and the perturbation grows like $e^{\lambda t}$ with
$$
\lambda = q_0\,\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}} \simeq 0.775\,q_0 .
$$

Check with the direct computation of the perturbation and with the first integrals. One sets $q = q_0$ constant to first order and keeps $p$ and $r$ small; the equations of motion become
$$
\dot{p} = -q_0\,r ,
\qquad
\dot{r} = -\frac{3}{5}\,q_0\,p ,
$$
and differentiating the first and substituting the second one obtains $\ddot{p} = \dfrac{3}{5}\,q_0^2\,p$, whose general solution contains $e^{\pm \lambda t}$ with $\lambda = q_0\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{5}}$, as announced. Moreover one checks that the two quantities
$$
A\,p^2 + B\,q^2 + C\,r^2 ,
\qquad
A^2 p^2 + B^2 q^2 + C^2 r^2
$$
are constant: differentiating the first with respect to time and using the Euler equations one obtains $2\big( A p \dot{p} + B q \dot{q} + C r \dot{r} \big) = -2\big[ (C-B) + (A-C) + (B-A) \big] p\,q\,r = 0$, and the same computation with the squares of the moments gives zero again. The two first integrals are therefore verified.

<div class="spiegazione">

The frame $\xi$, $\eta$, $\zeta$ is principal of inertia at $G$ because the first two axes are axes of material symmetry of the lamina: every point of coordinates $(\xi, \eta)$ has a corresponding point at $(-\xi, \eta)$ with the same mass, hence the products of inertia cancel in pairs.

The moment with respect to $G$ of the external forces is zero: the weight is equivalent to its resultant applied at $G$ and the reaction of the constraint is applied at $G$. The motion is therefore **a Poinsot motion**, and the Euler equations are homogeneous.

The permanent rotations are three, one for each principal axis. Their stability depends on the sign of the product of the differences between the principal moments: for the axis of largest moment and for the one of smallest moment the two factors have opposite signs, the perturbation oscillates and the rotation is stable; for the intermediate axis the factors have the same sign and the perturbation grows exponentially.

The number $\lambda \simeq 0.775\,q_0$ says how fast the perturbation grows: the time in which it is multiplied by $e$ is about $\dfrac{1.29}{q_0}$, that is a fraction of a turn. **A lamina launched almost about the intermediate axis changes its orientation after a few turns**, while launched about the other two axes it keeps its axis of rotation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is constrained at its center $G$, held fixed, and moves by inertia, as in the diagram in the figure. At the initial instant the angular velocity is $\vec{\omega}(0) = \omega_0\,\hat{e}_\xi + 2\,\omega_0\,\hat{e}_\zeta$, with $\zeta$ the axis of figure perpendicular to the disk. Integrate the Euler equations, compute the period with which $\vec{\omega}$ rotates in the body-fixed frame and determine the angle that the axis of figure forms with the angular momentum and the precession velocity in space.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,1.6" nodi="G,0,0,-" frecce="0,0,2.6,0,ξ;0,0,0,2.2,η" punti="0,0,G,baricentro" etichette="0.55,-0.5,ζ out of page" quote="0 0,-1.6 0,R,0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Homogeneous disk with a fixed center: $\xi$ and $\eta$ are two orthogonal diameters and the axis of figure $\zeta$ is perpendicular to the disk"}

Principal moments and gyroscopic structure. For a homogeneous disk
$$
A = B = \frac{m\,R^2}{4} ,
\qquad
C = \frac{m\,R^2}{2} = 2\,A ,
$$
hence the disk has gyroscopic structure with respect to $G$ and every diameter is a principal axis.

Euler equations of the torque-free motion, with $A = B$:
$$
\begin{cases}
A\,\dot{p} + (C-A)\,q\,r = 0 \\[4pt]
A\,\dot{q} + (A-C)\,r\,p = 0 \\[4pt]
C\,\dot{r} = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
\begin{cases}
\dot{p} = -r\,q \\[4pt]
\dot{q} = r\,p \\[4pt]
\dot{r} = 0
\end{cases}
$$
because $\dfrac{C-A}{A} = 1$. From the third equation $r = r_0 = 2\,\omega_0$ is constant, and the first two become a linear system with constant coefficients whose solution is
$$
p(t) = \omega_0\cos\big( 2\,\omega_0\,t \big) ,
\qquad
q(t) = \omega_0\sin\big( 2\,\omega_0\,t \big) ,
\qquad
r(t) = 2\,\omega_0 .
$$
The transverse component has constant magnitude $\omega_0$, hence
$$
|\vec{\omega}| = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 + 4\,\omega_0^2} = \sqrt{5}\,\omega_0 ,
$$
and $\vec{\omega}$ describes in the body-fixed frame a cone about the axis of figure with period
$$
\mathcal{T} = \frac{2\pi}{2\,\omega_0} = \frac{\pi}{\omega_0} .
$$
Angular momentum and precession in space. The angular momentum is constant in space and equals
$$
\vec{K}_G = A\,p\,\hat{e}_\xi + A\,q\,\hat{e}_\eta + C\,r\,\hat{e}_\zeta ,
\qquad
|\vec{K}_G| = \sqrt{A^2\,\omega_0^2 + 4\,A^2 \cdot 4\,\omega_0^2} = \sqrt{17}\,A\,\omega_0 .
$$
Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle between the axis of figure and $\vec{K}_G$,
$$
\cos\vartheta = \frac{C\,r_0}{|\vec{K}_G|} = \frac{2A \cdot 2\,\omega_0}{\sqrt{17}\,A\,\omega_0} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{17}} ,
\qquad
\vartheta = 14.0^\circ ,
$$
and the precession velocity of the axis of figure about the fixed direction of $\vec{K}_G$ is
$$
\Omega = \frac{|\vec{K}_G|}{A} = \sqrt{17}\,\omega_0 \simeq 4.12\,\omega_0 .
$$

Check with the first integrals and with the decomposition of the angular velocity. One checks the two first integrals on the solution found:
$$
A\,\big( p^2 + q^2 \big) + C\,r^2 = A\,\omega_0^2 + 2A \cdot 4\,\omega_0^2 = 9\,A\,\omega_0^2 ,
$$
$$
A^2\,\big( p^2 + q^2 \big) + C^2\,r^2 = A^2\,\omega_0^2 + 4A^2 \cdot 4\,\omega_0^2 = 17\,A^2\,\omega_0^2 ,
$$
and neither of the two depends on time, since $p^2 + q^2 = \omega_0^2$ and $r$ is constant. Moreover, in the regular precession $\vec{\omega} = \Omega\,\hat{k} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_\zeta$ holds, with $\hat{k}$ the unit vector of $\vec{K}_G$; the axial component gives
$$
r_0 = \dot{\varphi} + \Omega\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
\dot{\varphi} = 2\,\omega_0 - \sqrt{17}\,\omega_0\cdot\frac{4}{\sqrt{17}} = -2\,\omega_0 ,
$$
and the magnitude of $\dot{\varphi}$ coincides with the angular frequency $2\,\omega_0$ found by integrating the Euler equations: the rotation of $\vec{\omega}$ in the body-fixed frame takes place precisely with the spin velocity, with the sign changed.

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk has two equal principal moments, hence every diameter is a principal axis and the third Euler equation becomes $C\,\dot{r} = 0$: **the component of $\vec{\omega}$ along the axis of figure is constant**. This makes the first two equations linear, and they are integrated exactly.

The system $\dot{p} = -r_0\,q$, $\dot{q} = r_0\,p$ describes the uniform rotation of the vector of components $(p, q)$ in the plane $\xi\eta$ with angular velocity $r_0$. In the body-fixed frame, therefore, $\vec{\omega}$ describes a cone about the axis of figure, called the body cone of Poinsot, with period $\dfrac{2\pi}{r_0}$.

In space the description is different. The angular momentum is constant because the moment of the external forces with respect to $G$ is zero; the axis of figure, the vector $\vec{\omega}$ and the vector $\vec{K}_G$ stay coplanar, and the first two rotate about the third with angular velocity $\Omega = \dfrac{|\vec{K}_G|}{A}$. The motion is therefore a regular precession, which here arises without any applied force.

The angle $\vartheta = 14.0^\circ$ is small because the axial component of the angular velocity is twice the transverse one, and because the axial moment $C$ is twice the transverse one: the angular momentum is therefore almost parallel to the axis of figure. The check on the axial component links the two descriptions, the body-fixed one and the spatial one, and shows that they lead to the same motion.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rigid body with the point $O$ fixed has principal moments of inertia at $O$ equal to $A = m\,\ell^2$, $B = 2\,m\,\ell^2$ and $C = 3\,m\,\ell^2$, as in the diagram in the figure, and moves by inertia starting from the angular velocity $\vec{\omega}(0) = \omega_0\,\big( \hat{e}_\xi + \hat{e}_\eta + \hat{e}_\zeta \big)$. Write the two first integrals and determine the largest and the smallest value that $|\vec{\omega}|$ takes during the motion.

::struttura{poligoni="0.5,0.4 3,1 3.4,2.7 1.2,3" nodi="O,0,0" frecce="0,0,2.8,2.2,ζ;0,0,2.4,-0.8,ξ;0,0,0.2,2.8,η" vincoli="O,cerniera" punti="2,1.8,G,baricentro" riferimento=0 titolo="Rigid body with the fixed point $O$ and body-fixed frame principal of inertia, with distinct moments $A$, $B$, $C$"}

First integrals. The moment of the external forces with respect to $O$ is zero, hence the angular momentum and the kinetic energy are conserved:
$$
A\,p^2 + B\,q^2 + C\,r^2 = 2\,T ,
\qquad
A^2 p^2 + B^2 q^2 + C^2 r^2 = |\vec{K}_O|^2 .
$$
The values of the two constants are computed from the initial data, with $p = q = r = \omega_0$:
$$
2\,T = m\,\ell^2\,\omega_0^2\,\big( 1 + 2 + 3 \big) = 6\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega_0^2 ,
$$
$$
|\vec{K}_O|^2 = m^2\,\ell^4\,\omega_0^2\,\big( 1 + 4 + 9 \big) = 14\,m^2\,\ell^4\,\omega_0^2 .
$$
One sets $S = |\vec{\omega}|^2 = p^2 + q^2 + r^2$ and solves the linear system in the unknowns $p^2$, $q^2$, $r^2$:
$$
\begin{cases}
p^2 + q^2 + r^2 = S \\[4pt]
p^2 + 2\,q^2 + 3\,r^2 = 6\,\omega_0^2 \\[4pt]
p^2 + 4\,q^2 + 9\,r^2 = 14\,\omega_0^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
p^2 = 3\,S - 8\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
q^2 = 10\,\omega_0^2 - 3\,S ,
\qquad
r^2 = S - 2\,\omega_0^2 .
$$
The three quantities are squares, hence they must be nonnegative:
$$
S \ge \frac{8}{3}\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
S \le \frac{10}{3}\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
S \ge 2\,\omega_0^2 .
$$
The most restrictive condition from below is the first, therefore
$$
\frac{8}{3}\,\omega_0^2 \le |\vec{\omega}|^2 \le \frac{10}{3}\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
1.633\,\omega_0 \le |\vec{\omega}| \le 1.826\,\omega_0 .
$$

Check with the initial datum and with the extreme configurations. The initial value $S(0) = 3\,\omega_0^2$ lies between $\dfrac{8}{3}\,\omega_0^2 = 2.667\,\omega_0^2$ and $\dfrac{10}{3}\,\omega_0^2 = 3.333\,\omega_0^2$, hence it is compatible with the bounds found. At the smallest value $S = \dfrac{8}{3}\,\omega_0^2$ one has $p = 0$, and the formulas give
$$
q^2 = 10\,\omega_0^2 - 8\,\omega_0^2 = 2\,\omega_0^2 ,
\qquad
r^2 = \frac{8}{3}\,\omega_0^2 - 2\,\omega_0^2 = \frac{2}{3}\,\omega_0^2 ,
$$
and substituting into the two first integrals one obtains $2\,\big( 2\,\omega_0^2 \big) + 3\left( \dfrac{2}{3}\,\omega_0^2 \right) = 6\,\omega_0^2$ and $4\,\big( 2\,\omega_0^2 \big) + 9\left( \dfrac{2}{3}\,\omega_0^2 \right) = 14\,\omega_0^2$, that is the correct values. At the largest value $S = \dfrac{10}{3}\,\omega_0^2$ one has $q = 0$, with $p^2 = 2\,\omega_0^2$ and $r^2 = \dfrac{4}{3}\,\omega_0^2$, and the two first integrals give $2\,\omega_0^2 + 4\,\omega_0^2 = 6\,\omega_0^2$ and $2\,\omega_0^2 + 12\,\omega_0^2 = 14\,\omega_0^2$: both extreme configurations are compatible.

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is a Poinsot motion, hence the angular momentum is constant as a vector and the kinetic energy is constant; the two constants are computed once and for all from the initial data. There is no need to integrate the Euler equations in order to answer the question.

The two relations fix, in the space of the components $(p, q, r)$, the intersection between an ellipsoid and a sphere; the vector $\vec{\omega}$ travels along that curve. Since the two first integrals are linear combinations of $p^2$, $q^2$ and $r^2$, adding the identity $S = p^2+q^2+r^2$ one obtains a linear system of three equations that expresses the three squares as functions of the single parameter $S$.

The condition that makes the problem determinate is that the three squares be nonnegative: each inequality bounds $S$ on one side, and the admissible interval is the intersection. **The magnitude of the angular velocity oscillates** between $1.633\,\omega_0$ and $1.826\,\omega_0$, that is it varies by about twelve per cent during the motion.

The ends of the interval correspond to the configurations in which one component vanishes: at the minimum $p$ vanishes, that is $\vec{\omega}$ lies in the plane $\eta\zeta$, at the maximum $q$ vanishes. Substituting those configurations into the two first integrals is a direct control of the correctness of the computations.

</div>
