## Exercise 1

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a horizontal guide. An inextensible horizontal cord of negligible mass is attached at its center $G$; the cord passes over a homogeneous pulley of mass $m$ and radius $r$, with a fixed smooth axis, and carries at its other end a body of mass $m$, as in the diagram in the figure. The system starts from rest. Compute the acceleration of the hanging body, the tensions in the two stretches of the cord and the friction force on the disk.

::struttura{linee="0,0 7,0" cerchi="1.6,1,1;8.2,0.4,0.6" nodi="O,8.2,0.4;K,8.8,0.4,-;L,8.8,-1.2,-;G,1.6,1,-;H,8.2,1,-" catene="G-H;K-L" poligoni="8.45,-1.2 9.15,-1.2 9.15,-1.9 8.45,-1.9" vincoli="O,cerniera" punti="1.6,1,G,baricentro;1.6,0,C,centro" etichette="0.5,2.2,m, R;7.3,1.5,m, r;9.5,-1.5,m" titolo="Rolling disk, pulley with a fixed axis and hanging body, connected by an inextensible cord"}

Degrees of freedom and coordinates. The cord is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, hence the displacement $x$ of the center of the disk, the descent $y$ of the body and the rotations $\vartheta$ of the disk and $\varphi$ of the pulley are linked by
$$
y = x ,
\qquad
\dot{x} = R\,\dot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
\dot{x} = r\,\dot{\varphi} ,
$$
and the system has one degree of freedom, described by $x$. Moments of inertia:
$$
I_G^{\text{disk}} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 ,
\qquad
I_O^{\text{pulley}} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,r^2 .
$$
Equations of motion. One denotes by $S_1$ the tension in the horizontal stretch and by $S_2$ the tension in the vertical stretch, and one writes the first cardinal equation for the hanging body, the equation of motion about the fixed axis for the pulley and the second cardinal equation with pole at the contact point $C$ for the disk, where $I_C = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{x} = m\,g - S_2 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,r^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{x}}{r} = \big( S_2 - S_1 \big)\,r \\[6pt]
\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{x}}{R} = S_1\,R
\end{cases}
$$
From the third $S_1 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x}$, from the second $S_2 = S_1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} = 2\,m\,\ddot{x}$, and substituting into the first
$$
m\,\ddot{x} + 2\,m\,\ddot{x} = m\,g ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{g}{3} ,
\qquad
S_1 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,g ,
\qquad
S_2 = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,g .
$$
Friction force on the disk, from the first cardinal equation applied to the disk, with $F_a$ positive in the sense of the motion:
$$
m\,\ddot{x} = S_1 + F_a ,
\qquad
F_a = \frac{m\,g}{3} - \frac{m\,g}{2} = -\frac{1}{6}\,m\,g ,
$$
that is a force of magnitude $\dfrac{m\,g}{6}$ directed opposite to the motion of the disk.

Check with the kinetic energy theorem. The kinetic energy of the system is
$$
T = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,m\,r^2\left(\frac{\dot{x}}{r}\right)^{\!2} + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2
= \left( \frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2} \right) m\,\dot{x}^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 .
$$
The reactions of the constraints do no work, because the contact of the disk has zero velocity, the axis of the pulley is fixed and the cord is inextensible; the only force that does work is the weight of the hanging body. Differentiating $T = m\,g\,x$ with respect to time one obtains
$$
3\,m\,\dot{x}\,\ddot{x} = m\,g\,\dot{x} ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{g}{3} ,
$$
which is the value already found.

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is formed by three bodies connected by ideal constraints, hence it has **a single degree of freedom**: the conditions of inextensibility of the cord and of pure rolling link all the velocities to $\dot{x}$. One writes an equation for each body and introduces the two tensions as auxiliary unknowns.

For the disk one chooses the pole at the contact point, because there the normal reaction and the friction force have zero moment: the equation then contains only the tension $S_1$, with lever arm $R$ with respect to $C$ since the cord is attached at the center and passes at distance $R$ from the contact. The choice is legitimate because the velocity of the geometric contact point is parallel to $\vec{v}_G$ and the distance of $G$ from the contact stays equal to $R$.

The two tensions are different, $S_1 = \dfrac{m g}{2}$ and $S_2 = \dfrac{2\,m\,g}{3}$, and their difference provides the couple that accelerates the pulley. The tension $S_2$ is smaller than the weight $m\,g$ of the hanging body, as it must be for a body descending with acceleration.

The friction force turns out to be negative, that is **directed backwards**: the cord pulls the disk forward applying the force at the center, and the contact must provide the couple that sets the disk in rotation, which requires a force opposite to the motion. The result is consistent with what happens when the force is applied at the center instead of at the top.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a horizontal guide. An inextensible cord of negligible mass is wound on its rim; it comes off horizontally from the highest point $P$, passes over a small pulley of negligible mass and carries a body of mass $m$, as in the diagram in the figure. The system starts from rest. Compute the acceleration of the center of the disk, the acceleration of the hanging body and the tension of the cord.

::struttura{linee="0,0 7,0" cerchi="1.8,1.2,1.2;7.6,1.8,0.6" nodi="O,7.6,1.8;P,1.8,2.4,-;H,7.6,2.4,-;K,8.2,1.8,-;L,8.2,-1.2,-" catene="P-H;K-L" poligoni="7.85,-1.2 8.55,-1.2 8.55,-1.9 7.85,-1.9" vincoli="O,cerniera" punti="1.8,1.2,G,baricentro;1.8,0,C,centro;1.8,2.4,P" etichette="0.5,2.6,m, R;8.9,-1.5,m" titolo="Cord wound on the disk coming off the highest point, pulley of negligible mass and hanging body"}

Degrees of freedom and coordinates. The point $P$ is the point of the disk diametrically opposite to the contact, and in rolling its velocity is $2\,\dot{x}$, that is twice that of the center. The cord is inextensible, hence the descent $y$ of the hanging body satisfies
$$
\dot{y} = 2\,\dot{x} ,
\qquad
\ddot{y} = 2\,\ddot{x} ,
$$
and the system has one degree of freedom, described by $x$. Moment of inertia of the disk with respect to the contact:
$$
I_C = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2 .
$$
Equations of motion, with the first cardinal equation for the hanging body and the second with pole at $C$ for the disk, where the tension $S$ has lever arm $2R$:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\big( 2\,\ddot{x} \big) = m\,g - S \\[6pt]
\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{x}}{R} = 2\,R\,S
\end{cases}
$$
From the second $S = \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,\ddot{x}$, and substituting into the first
$$
2\,m\,\ddot{x} + \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\ddot{x} = m\,g ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{4\,g}{11} ,
\qquad
\ddot{y} = \frac{8\,g}{11} ,
\qquad
S = \frac{3}{11}\,m\,g .
$$

Check with the kinetic energy theorem. The kinetic energy of the system is
$$
T = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( 2\,\dot{x} \big)^2 = \frac{11}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 ,
$$
and the weight of the hanging body does the work $m\,g\,y = 2\,m\,g\,x$, while the other forces do no work. Differentiating $T = 2\,m\,g\,x$ with respect to time one obtains
$$
\frac{11}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}\,\ddot{x} = 2\,m\,g\,\dot{x} ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{4\,g}{11} ,
$$
which coincides with the value found. Finally one checks the first cardinal equation of the hanging body: $m\,\ddot{y} = \dfrac{8}{11}\,m\,g$ and $m\,g - S = m\,g - \dfrac{3}{11}\,m\,g = \dfrac{8}{11}\,m\,g$, and the two sides coincide.

<div class="spiegazione">

The cord comes off the highest point of the disk, which in rolling has twice the velocity of the center: this is the key point of the problem, and it follows at once from the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics applied with pole at the contact point, which is the instantaneous center of rotation. The hanging body therefore descends with twice the acceleration of the center of the disk.

For the disk one chooses again the pole at the contact point, where the reactions of the contact have zero moment. The lever arm of the tension with respect to $C$ is $2R$, because the cord is horizontal and passes through the highest point.

The hanging body descends with acceleration $\dfrac{8\,g}{11} \simeq 0.73\,g$, that is almost three quarters of the acceleration of free fall. The reason is that each descent of the body corresponds to half as much advance of the disk, therefore **the kinetic energy of translation to be supplied to the disk is one quarter** of the energy that would be needed with the cord attached at the center.

The tension turns out to be small, $\dfrac{3}{11}\,m\,g \simeq 0.27\,m\,g$, precisely because the hanging body descends almost freely.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a plane inclined by $\alpha$ to the horizontal. An inextensible cord is wound on its rim; it comes off the point $P$ farthest from the plane, is parallel to the inclined plane, passes over a small pulley of negligible mass placed at the top of the plane and carries a body of mass $m$ hanging vertically, as in the diagram in the figure. The system starts from rest. Compute the acceleration of the center of the disk, the tension of the cord and the friction force, and give the values for $\alpha = 30^\circ$.

::struttura{linee="0,0 6,3.46 6,0 0,0" cerchi="2.15,2.28,0.9;6.71,5.38,0.5" nodi="O,6.71,5.38;P,1.7,3.06,-;H,6.46,5.81,-;K,7.21,5.38,-;L,7.21,2,-" catene="P-H;K-L" poligoni="6.86,2 7.56,2 7.56,1.3 6.86,1.3" vincoli="O,cerniera" punti="2.15,2.28,G,baricentro;2.6,1.5,C,centro;1.7,3.06,P" etichette="1.05,0.3,α;1.05,3.55,2m;7.9,1.7,m" titolo="Disk on an inclined plane towed by a cord that comes off the point farthest from the plane"}

Degrees of freedom and coordinates. One denotes by $x$ the displacement of the center of the disk along the plane, positive upwards. The point $P$ is diametrically opposite to the contact, hence its velocity is $2\,\dot{x}$; the cord is inextensible, therefore the descent $y$ of the hanging body satisfies $\dot{y} = 2\,\dot{x}$. The system has one degree of freedom. Moment of inertia of the disk with respect to the contact:
$$
I_C = \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2 + (2m)\,R^2 = 3\,m\,R^2 .
$$
Equations of motion, with the first cardinal equation for the hanging body and the second with pole at $C$ for the disk, where the tension $S$ acts with lever arm $2R$ and the component of the weight along the plane $2\,m\,g\sin\alpha$ with lever arm $R$:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\big( 2\,\ddot{x} \big) = m\,g - S \\[6pt]
3\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{x}}{R} = 2\,R\,S - 2\,m\,g\,R\sin\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
From the first $S = m\,g - 2\,m\,\ddot{x}$, and substituting into the second
$$
3\,m\,\ddot{x} = 2\,m\,g - 4\,m\,\ddot{x} - 2\,m\,g\sin\alpha ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{2\,g\,\big( 1 - \sin\alpha \big)}{7} ,
\qquad
S = \frac{m\,g\,\big( 3 + 4\sin\alpha \big)}{7} .
$$
Friction force, from the first cardinal equation applied to the disk and projected along the plane, with $F_a$ positive upwards:
$$
2\,m\,\ddot{x} = S + F_a - 2\,m\,g\sin\alpha ,
\qquad
F_a = 2\,m\,\ddot{x} - S + 2\,m\,g\sin\alpha .
$$
With $\alpha = 30^\circ$:
$$
\ddot{x} = \frac{g}{7} ,
\qquad
S = \frac{5}{7}\,m\,g ,
\qquad
F_a = \frac{2}{7}\,m\,g - \frac{5}{7}\,m\,g + m\,g = \frac{4}{7}\,m\,g ,
$$
hence the disk climbs with acceleration $\dfrac{g}{7}$ and the friction force is directed up along the plane.

Check with the kinetic energy theorem and with the second cardinal equation with respect to $G$. The kinetic energy is
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot m\,R^2\left(\frac{\dot{x}}{R}\right)^{\!2} + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( 2\,\dot{x} \big)^2 = \frac{7}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 ,
$$
and the potential of the weights, with the disk climbing by $x\sin\alpha$ and the body descending by $2x$, is $U = -2\,m\,g\,x\sin\alpha + 2\,m\,g\,x$. Differentiating $T - U = \text{constant}$ one obtains
$$
7\,m\,\dot{x}\,\ddot{x} = 2\,m\,g\,\big( 1 - \sin\alpha \big)\,\dot{x} ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{2\,g\,\big( 1 - \sin\alpha \big)}{7} ,
$$
which is the value found. Moreover the second cardinal equation with pole at $G$, where the tension and the friction force both have lever arm $R$ and opposite senses of rotation, gives with $\alpha = 30^\circ$
$$
m\,R^2\,\frac{\ddot{x}}{R} = R\,\big( S - F_a \big) ,
\qquad
m\,\frac{g}{7} = \frac{5}{7}\,m\,g - \frac{4}{7}\,m\,g = \frac{1}{7}\,m\,g ,
$$
and the two sides coincide.

<div class="spiegazione">

The system climbs, because the weight of the hanging body acts with the lever arm doubled by the cord wound on the rim, while the component of the weight of the disk along the plane acts with lever arm $R$. The condition for climbing is $\ddot{x} > 0$, that is $\sin\alpha < 1$, which is always satisfied: with this arrangement the disk climbs for every inclination, and it stops only in the limit case of the vertical plane.

The choice of the pole at the contact point removes both the normal reaction and the friction force from the equation of rotation, and leaves as unknowns the tension and the acceleration alone. The friction force is then computed from the first cardinal equation applied to the disk.

With $\alpha = 30^\circ$ the friction force equals $\dfrac{4}{7}\,m\,g$ **upwards**: the contact must oppose the tendency of the disk to rotate faster than rolling allows. Pure rolling requires $F_a \le f_s\,N$, with $N = 2\,m\,g\cos\alpha$, hence a coefficient of static friction at least equal to $\dfrac{4}{7\sqrt{3}} \simeq 0.33$.

The two checks are independent of the procedure followed: the first uses the kinetic energy theorem with the overall potential, the second uses the second cardinal equation written with a different pole.

</div>
