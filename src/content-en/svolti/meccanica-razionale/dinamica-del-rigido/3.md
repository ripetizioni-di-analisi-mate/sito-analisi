## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at the end $A$ to a fixed smooth horizontal axis, perpendicular to the vertical plane of the motion, as in the diagram in the figure. The rod is released from rest in the horizontal position. Compute the initial angular acceleration, the angular velocity when the rod passes through the vertical position and the reaction of the hinge at that instant.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3.2,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" punti="1.6,0,G,baricentro" forze="1.6 0,0,-1,mg" quote="A,B,ℓ,0.9" coppie="0.7 0,-1,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$, released from rest in the horizontal position"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.4,-2.1" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" punti="1.2,-1.05,G,baricentro" forze="1.2 -1.05,0,-1,mg" reazioni="A,1.1,0,Φ_Ax;A,0,1.1,Φ_Ay" tratteggi="0,0 3.1,0" etichette="0.85,-0.4,ϑ" titolo="Free-body diagram in a generic position: the weight and the two components of the reaction at $A$"}

Degree of freedom and coordinate: the angle $\vartheta$ that the rod forms with the horizontal, positive in the sense of the fall. Moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $A$, from the Huygens-Steiner theorem:
$$
I_A = \frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\left(\frac{\ell}{2}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 .
$$
Equation of motion about the fixed axis, with the lever arm of the weight with respect to $A$ equal to $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$:
$$
\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\,\cos\vartheta .
$$
Initial angular acceleration, for $\vartheta = 0$:
$$
\ddot{\vartheta}_0 = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell} .
$$
Angular velocity in the vertical position, from the first integral of the energy with $V = m\,g\,y_G$:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2 = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2} ,
\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{3\,g}{\ell}} .
$$
Reaction of the hinge in the vertical position. At that instant $\cos\vartheta = 0$, hence $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$ and the acceleration of $G$ is purely centripetal, directed from $G$ towards $A$ and of magnitude
$$
a_G = \omega^2\,\frac{\ell}{2} = \frac{3\,g}{\ell}\cdot\frac{\ell}{2} = \frac{3}{2}\,g .
$$
The first cardinal equation projected onto the vertical gives
$$
\Phi_A - m\,g = m\,\frac{3}{2}\,g ,
\qquad
\Phi_A = \frac{5}{2}\,m\,g ,
$$
directed upwards; the horizontal component is zero.

Check of the first integral by differentiation and of the reaction in the horizontal position. One multiplies both sides of the equation of motion by $\dot{\vartheta}$:
$$
\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta ,
$$
and one recognizes on the left the derivative of $\dfrac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and on the right the derivative of $m\,g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$. Integrating from $\vartheta = 0$ with $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ one obtains $\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \dfrac{3g}{\ell}\sin\vartheta$, which for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ gives again $\omega^2 = \dfrac{3g}{\ell}$. At the initial instant, instead, one has $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ and $\ddot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{3g}{2\ell}$, hence the acceleration of $G$ is purely tangential and equals $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\ddot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{3}{4}\,g$ downwards; the first cardinal equation then gives
$$
\Phi_A - m\,g = -\frac{3}{4}\,m\,g ,
\qquad
\Phi_A = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,g ,
$$
that is, at the instant of release the hinge supports only a quarter of the weight of the rod.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod has a fixed smooth axis, therefore one uses the equation of motion about a fixed axis, in which the reaction of the hinge does not appear: its line of action meets the axis, hence its axial moment is zero.

The lever arm of the weight with respect to the axis is the horizontal distance between $A$ and $G$, that is $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$. The equation is **nonlinear**, because it contains $\cos\vartheta$; in order to obtain the angular velocity in a given position it is therefore convenient to use the first integral of the energy instead of integrating the differential equation.

The constraint is ideal and fixed, hence its reaction does no work, and the weight is conservative with potential energy $V = m\,g\,y_G$: the sum $T + V$ is conserved. The lowering of the center of mass from the horizontal to the vertical position equals $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, and from this follows the angular velocity.

The reaction is always computed last, with the first cardinal equation, because it requires the acceleration of the center of mass and hence the motion already known. In the vertical position the moment of the weight is zero, therefore $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$ and only the centripetal acceleration remains: the hinge must support the weight and in addition provide the centripetal force, and **the reaction equals two and a half times the weight**. In the horizontal position the opposite happens: the angular velocity is zero, the acceleration is purely tangential and directed downwards, hence the reaction is smaller than the weight.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ oscillates in a vertical plane about a fixed smooth horizontal axis, perpendicular to the rod and passing through a point $A$ at distance $a$ from the center of mass $G$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the reduced length and the angular frequency of the small oscillations as functions of $a$, the value of $a$ that makes the period smallest and the corresponding period for $\ell = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;E,0,0.6,-;D,0,-3.4,-" aste="E-D" vincoli="A,cerniera,0" punti="0,-1.4,G,baricentro;0,-2.35,O*,centro" quote="A,0 -1.4,a,0.9;A,0 -2.35,ℓ_r,-1.3" titolo="Rod suspended at distance $a$ from the center of mass: the center of oscillation $O^*$ lies at distance $\ell_r$ from the axis"}

Quantities needed. Radius of gyration of the rod about the center of mass in the direction of the axis and moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $A$, from the Huygens-Steiner theorem:
$$
\rho^2 = \frac{\ell^2}{12} ,
\qquad
I_A = m\left(\frac{\ell^2}{12} + a^2\right) .
$$
Reduced length and angular frequency of the small oscillations:
$$
\ell_r = \frac{I_A}{m\,a} = a + \frac{\ell^2}{12\,a} ,
\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{\ell_r}} = \sqrt{\frac{12\,a\,g}{12\,a^2 + \ell^2}} .
$$
The period $\mathcal{T} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\ell_r}{g}}$ is smallest when $\ell_r$ is smallest. One sets the derivative equal to zero:
$$
\frac{d\ell_r}{da} = 1 - \frac{\ell^2}{12\,a^2} = 0 ,
\qquad
a^* = \frac{\ell}{2\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\,\ell \simeq 0.289\,\ell ,
$$
$$
\ell_r^* = a^* + \frac{\ell^2}{12\,a^*} = 2\,a^* = \frac{\ell}{\sqrt{3}} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T}^* = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g\sqrt{3}}} .
$$
The second derivative $\dfrac{d^2\ell_r}{da^2} = \dfrac{\ell^2}{6\,a^3}$ is positive, hence $a^*$ is a minimum point. With $\ell = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}$:
$$
a^* = 0.35\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\ell_r^* = 0.69\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T}^* = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.69}{9.81}} = 1.67\,\mathrm{s} .
$$

Check with the reversibility and with a known case. One suspends the rod at the center of oscillation, that is at distance $a' = \ell_r - a = \dfrac{\ell^2}{12\,a}$ from the center of mass, on the other side. The new reduced length is
$$
\ell_r' = a' + \frac{\ell^2}{12\,a'} = \frac{\ell^2}{12\,a} + a = \ell_r ,
$$
hence the period does not change, as the reversibility of the compound pendulum predicts. Moreover, for $a = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$, that is with the axis at one end, the formulas give
$$
\ell_r = \frac{\ell}{2} + \frac{\ell}{6} = \frac{2\,\ell}{3} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{2\,\ell}{3\,g}} = 1.79\,\mathrm{s} ,
$$
which is the known value for the rod suspended at one end and is larger than the minimum period, as it must be.

<div class="spiegazione">

The moment of inertia with respect to the axis is obtained from the Huygens-Steiner theorem, which links $I_A$ to the moment about the center of mass through the distance $a$. The ratio $\dfrac{I_A}{m\,a}$ is the length of the simple pendulum that oscillates with the same period, that is the **reduced length**.

The expression $\ell_r = a + \dfrac{\rho^2}{a}$ shows two effects that oppose each other. Bringing the axis closer to the center of mass makes the distance $a$ decrease, which would tend to shorten the period, but the term $\dfrac{\rho^2}{a}$ grows without bound, because the moment of the weight becomes small while the inertia stays finite; for $a \to 0$ the period diverges and for $a \to \infty$ it grows like $\sqrt{a}$. The minimum is where the two terms are equal, that is for $a = \rho$.

The value $a^* = \dfrac{\ell}{2\sqrt{3}}$ does not coincide with any notable point of the rod: it lies between the center of mass and the end, at about $29\%$ of the length from the center. The corresponding period is $1.67\,\mathrm{s}$, against $1.79\,\mathrm{s}$ for the rod suspended at the end.

The reversibility is an independent check of the result: exchanging the axis with the center of oscillation the reduced length stays the same, and this holds for every $a$, not only for the optimal value. The case $a = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$ brings one back to the known result of the rod hinged at one end, already obtained in the first exercise.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rigid system is formed by a homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ and by a homogeneous disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R = \dfrac{\ell}{4}$, welded to the rod with its center at $B$ and lying in the same vertical plane, as in the diagram in the figure. The system rotates about a fixed smooth horizontal axis through $A$ and perpendicular to the plane. Compute the moment of inertia with respect to the axis, the reduced length, the angular frequency of the small oscillations and the magnitude of the reaction of the axis when the rod passes through the vertical, starting from rest in the horizontal position.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" cerchi="4,0,1" vincoli="A,cerniera" punti="3.33,0,G,baricentro" quote="A,B,ℓ,1.6;4 0,5 0,R,-1.3" etichette="5.35,1.15,2m;1.5,0.75,m" coppie="0.9 0,-1,ϑ" titolo="Rod with a disk welded at the end: the data of the problem"}

Quantities needed. Total mass and position of the center of mass, measured from $A$ along the rod:
$$
M = 3\,m ,
\qquad
d = \frac{m\cdot\dfrac{\ell}{2} + 2m\cdot\ell}{3m} = \frac{5}{6}\,\ell .
$$
Moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $A$, the sum of the contribution of the rod and that of the disk transferred with Huygens-Steiner:
$$
I_A = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 + \left[\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2m\left(\frac{\ell}{4}\right)^{\!2} + 2m\,\ell^2\right] = m\,\ell^2\left(\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{16} + 2\right) = \frac{115}{48}\,m\,\ell^2 .
$$
Reduced length and angular frequency of the small oscillations:
$$
\ell_r = \frac{I_A}{M\,d} = \frac{\dfrac{115}{48}\,m\,\ell^2}{3m\cdot\dfrac{5}{6}\,\ell} = \frac{23}{24}\,\ell ,
\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{\ell_r}} = \sqrt{\frac{24\,g}{23\,\ell}} .
$$
Angular velocity in the vertical position, starting from rest in the horizontal position, from the first integral of the energy:
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,I_A\,\omega_v^2 = M\,g\,d ,
\qquad
\omega_v^2 = \frac{2\cdot 3m\,g\cdot\dfrac{5}{6}\,\ell}{\dfrac{115}{48}\,m\,\ell^2} = \frac{48\,g}{23\,\ell} .
$$
Reaction of the axis in the vertical position. At that instant the moment of the weight with respect to the axis is zero, hence $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$ and the acceleration of the center of mass is purely centripetal:
$$
a_G = \omega_v^2\,d = \frac{48\,g}{23\,\ell}\cdot\frac{5}{6}\,\ell = \frac{40}{23}\,g ,
$$
$$
\Phi_A - M\,g = M\,a_G ,
\qquad
\Phi_A = 3m\,g\left(1 + \frac{40}{23}\right) = \frac{189}{23}\,m\,g \simeq 8.22\,m\,g ,
$$
directed upwards.

Check with the limit case and with the comparison between the two results. If the disk is reduced to a particle of mass $2m$ placed at $B$, its own moment of inertia vanishes and one obtains
$$
I_A = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 + 2m\,\ell^2 = \frac{7}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
\ell_r = \frac{\dfrac{7}{3}\,m\,\ell^2}{\dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,\ell} = \frac{14}{15}\,\ell ,
$$
that is a value slightly smaller than $\dfrac{23}{24}\,\ell = 0.958\,\ell$: the real disk, which has mass distributed around $B$, lengthens the reduced length, as it must. Moreover the angular velocity in the vertical position satisfies
$$
\omega_v^2 = \frac{2\,g}{\ell_r} = \frac{2\,g}{\dfrac{23}{24}\,\ell} = \frac{48\,g}{23\,\ell} ,
$$
and the relation $\omega_v^2 = \dfrac{2g}{\ell_r}$ is exactly that of a simple pendulum of length $\ell_r$ that starts from rest in the horizontal position: the two computations coincide.

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is a single rigid body, because the disk is welded to the rod; the center of mass is found with the weighted average of the positions of the centers of mass of the two parts, and the moment of inertia is the sum of the two contributions, each transferred to the axis through $A$ with the **Huygens-Steiner theorem**.

The contribution of the disk is dominated by the transfer term $2m\,\ell^2$, while its own moment $\dfrac{1}{16}\,m\,\ell^2$ is small: a disk of radius $\dfrac{\ell}{4}$ welded at distance $\ell$ from the axis behaves almost like a concentrated mass, and the comparison with the limit case confirms it.

The reduced length sums up the behavior of the pendulum in a single number: the system oscillates like a simple pendulum of length $\dfrac{23}{24}\,\ell$, that is slightly less than the rod. All the subsequent questions can then be answered by reasoning about that simple pendulum, and this is the control used in the check.

The reaction of the axis in the vertical position equals $8.22\,m\,g$, that is **almost three times the total weight** $3\,m\,g$: the part due to the centripetal acceleration exceeds the weight itself, because the center of mass is far from the axis and the angular velocity gained in the fall is high.

</div>
