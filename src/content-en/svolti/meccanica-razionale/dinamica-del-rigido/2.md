## Exercise 1

A homogeneous pulley, modeled as a disk of mass $M$ and radius $R$, rotates about a fixed smooth horizontal axis through its center $O$. An inextensible cord of negligible mass is wound on it, and a body of mass $m$ hangs from its end, as in the diagram in the figure. The system starts from rest. Compute the acceleration of the hanging body, the tension of the cord and the reaction of the axis.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,1.4" nodi="O,0,0;P,1.4,0,-;Q,1.4,-2.6,-" poligoni="1.05,-2.6 1.75,-2.6 1.75,-3.2 1.05,-3.2" catene="P-Q" forze="1.4 -3.2,0,-0.9,mg" vincoli="O,cerniera" etichette="-1.4,1.1,M, R;2.1,-2.9,m" titolo="Pulley with a wound cord and a hanging body of mass $m$"}

Degrees of freedom and coordinates: the system has one degree of freedom. One denotes by $y$ the descent of the body, measured downwards from rest, and by $\vartheta$ the rotation of the pulley; the cord is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, hence
$$
\dot{y} = R\,\dot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
a = \ddot{y} = R\,\ddot{\vartheta} .
$$
Moment of inertia of the pulley with respect to its axis:
$$
I_O = \frac{1}{2}\,M\,R^2 .
$$
Equations of motion, with the first cardinal equation for the hanging body and the equation of motion about the fixed axis for the pulley, denoting by $S$ the tension of the cord:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a = m\,g - S \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,M\,R^2\,\dfrac{a}{R} = S\,R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
S = \frac{1}{2}\,M\,a ,
\qquad
m\,a + \frac{1}{2}\,M\,a = m\,g ,
\qquad
a = \frac{2\,m\,g}{2m + M} ,
\qquad
S = \frac{M\,m\,g}{2m + M} .
$$
Reaction of the axis: the center of the pulley is fixed, hence $\vec{a}_O = \vec{0}$ and the first cardinal equation applied to the pulley alone gives
$$
\Phi_O - M\,g - S = 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_O = M\,g + \frac{M\,m\,g}{2m+M} = \frac{M\,g\,(M + 3m)}{2m + M} ,
$$
directed upwards.

Check with the kinetic energy theorem. The kinetic energy of the system is
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,M\,R^2\left(\frac{\dot{y}}{R}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(m + \frac{M}{2}\right)\dot{y}^2 ,
$$
the cord and the axis are ideal and fixed constraints, hence their reactions do no work, and the weight of the body does the work $m\,g\,y$. Differentiating the equality $T = m\,g\,y$ with respect to time one obtains
$$
\left(m + \frac{M}{2}\right)\dot{y}\,\ddot{y} = m\,g\,\dot{y} ,
\qquad
\ddot{y} = \frac{m\,g}{m + \dfrac{M}{2}} = \frac{2\,m\,g}{2m+M} ,
$$
which is the value already found.

<div class="spiegazione">

The cord is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, therefore the velocity of the hanging body equals the velocity of the points of the rim of the pulley, that is $R\,\dot{\vartheta}$: the system has a single degree of freedom and the tension $S$ is the only further unknown.

The two equations are written by separating the two bodies. For the hanging body the first cardinal equation projected onto the vertical holds, with the weight downwards and the tension upwards. For the pulley the equation of motion about a fixed axis holds: the axis is smooth, hence its reaction has zero axial moment, the weight of the pulley has a line of action that meets the axis and it too has zero moment, therefore only the moment of the tension remains, with lever arm $R$.

The result $a = \dfrac{2mg}{2m+M}$ is **smaller than $g$** and tends to $g$ when the mass of the pulley becomes negligible: with $M = 0$ one recovers free fall, and the tension vanishes. This is the immediate consistency check of the formula.

The reaction of the axis is computed last, when the motion is known. The center of the pulley is fixed, hence the resultant of the forces on it is zero; the forces are the weight $Mg$, the tension $S$ applied at the point where the cord leaves the rim and the reaction $\Phi_O$. The reaction is **larger than the weight of the pulley** but smaller than $M g + m g$, because the hanging body is descending with acceleration and pulls the cord with a force smaller than its own weight.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two bodies of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$, with $m_1 > m_2$, hang from the two ends of an inextensible cord of negligible mass that passes without slipping over a homogeneous pulley of mass $M$ and radius $R$, with a fixed smooth horizontal axis, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the acceleration of the bodies and the two tensions of the cord. Then apply the result to the case $m_1 = 2m$, $m_2 = m$, $M = 2m$.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,1.4" nodi="O,0,0;A,-1.4,0,-;B,1.4,0,-;C,-1.4,-2.4,-;D,1.4,-1.5,-" poligoni="-1.75,-2.4 -1.05,-2.4 -1.05,-3 -1.75,-3;1.05,-1.5 1.75,-1.5 1.75,-2.1 1.05,-2.1" catene="A-C;B-D" forze="-1.4 -3,0,-0.9,m_1 g;1.4 -2.1,0,-0.9,m_2 g" vincoli="O,cerniera" titolo="Heavy pulley with two bodies hanging from the ends of the cord"}

Degrees of freedom and coordinates: one degree of freedom. One denotes by $y$ the descent of the body $m_1$; the cord is inextensible, hence $m_2$ rises by the same amount and
$$
a = \ddot{y} = R\,\ddot{\vartheta} .
$$
The cord does not slip on the pulley, therefore the tensions in the two branches, $S_1$ and $S_2$, are different. Moment of inertia of the pulley:
$$
I_O = \frac{1}{2}\,M\,R^2 .
$$
Equations of motion, with the first cardinal equation for each body and the equation of motion about the fixed axis for the pulley:
$$
\begin{cases}
m_1\,a = m_1\,g - S_1 \\[6pt]
m_2\,a = S_2 - m_2\,g \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,M\,R^2\,\dfrac{a}{R} = (S_1 - S_2)\,R
\end{cases}
$$
From the first two one obtains $S_1 = m_1(g-a)$ and $S_2 = m_2(g+a)$; substituting into the third,
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,M\,a = m_1\,g - m_1\,a - m_2\,g - m_2\,a ,
$$
$$
a = \frac{2\,(m_1 - m_2)\,g}{2m_1 + 2m_2 + M} ,
$$
$$
S_1 = \frac{m_1\,g\,(M + 4m_2)}{2m_1+2m_2+M} ,
\qquad
S_2 = \frac{m_2\,g\,(M + 4m_1)}{2m_1+2m_2+M} .
$$
With $m_1 = 2m$, $m_2 = m$ and $M = 2m$ one obtains
$$
a = \frac{2\,m\,g}{4m+2m+2m} = \frac{g}{4} ,
\qquad
S_1 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,g ,
\qquad
S_2 = \frac{5}{4}\,m\,g .
$$

Check with the limit case of the Atwood machine and with the third equation. Setting $M = 0$ one obtains
$$
a = \frac{(m_1-m_2)\,g}{m_1+m_2} ,
\qquad
S_1 = S_2 = \frac{2\,m_1\,m_2\,g}{m_1+m_2} ,
$$
that is the known values for a pulley of negligible mass, with the two tensions equal. With the numerical values, the third equation gives
$$
\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2m\,R^2\cdot\frac{g}{4R} = \frac{m\,g\,R}{4} ,
\qquad
(S_1 - S_2)\,R = \left(\frac{3}{2} - \frac{5}{4}\right) m\,g\,R = \frac{m\,g\,R}{4} ,
$$
and the two sides coincide.

<div class="spiegazione">

The cord does not slip on the pulley, therefore the pulley is set in rotation and the tensions in the two branches are different: their difference provides the couple that accelerates the pulley. With a pulley of negligible mass the couple needed is zero and the two tensions coincide, which is the case studied in the dynamics of the particle.

Three equations are written, one for each body and one for the pulley, and the unknowns are $a$, $S_1$ and $S_2$. The first two equations are projections of the first cardinal equation onto the vertical, with the senses chosen consistently: $m_1$ descends, hence its acceleration agrees with the weight, while $m_2$ rises.

The result shows that the mass of the pulley appears in the sum in the denominator through $\dfrac{M}{2}$, that is through the moment of inertia divided by $R^2$: the pulley behaves like **an additional mass equal to half its own**, translating with the bodies. The radius $R$ does not appear in the result, because the pulley is a homogeneous disk and its moment of inertia is proportional to $R^2$.

The limit $M \to 0$ returns the classical Atwood machine, and the direct check of the third equation with the numerical values confirms that the two tensions found are consistent with the angular acceleration of the pulley.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ carries a circular groove of radius $r < R$, cut around the center, of negligible mass compared with the disk. An inextensible string is wound on this groove, fastened above to the ceiling and vertical, as in the diagram in the figure. The disk is released from rest and descends unwinding the string, with the axis always horizontal. Compute the acceleration of the center and the tension of the string, and apply the result to the cases $r = R$ and $r = \dfrac{R}{2}$.

::struttura{linee="-1.7,3.4 1.7,3.4" cerchi="0,0,1.5" nodi="K,-0.72,0;B,-0.72,3.4,-" catene="K-B" punti="0,0,G,baricentro" forze="0 0,0,-1.1,mg" quote="0 0,-0.72 0,r,-0.5;0 0,1.5 0,R,0.5" titolo="Disk with the string wound on the groove of radius $r$: the data of the problem"}

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,1.5" punti="0,0,G,baricentro;-0.72,0,K" forze="0 0,0,-1.1,mg" reazioni="-0.72 0,0,1.4,S" coppie="0.75 0,-1,ϑ" titolo="Free-body diagram: the weight at $G$ and the tension $S$ applied at the point $K$ where the string leaves the groove"}

Degrees of freedom and coordinates: one degree of freedom. The string is inextensible and vertical, hence the point $K$ where the string leaves the groove has zero vertical velocity; by the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics the descent $y$ of the center and the rotation $\vartheta$ are linked by
$$
\dot{y} = r\,\dot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
a = \ddot{y} = r\,\ddot{\vartheta} .
$$
Moment of inertia about the center of mass:
$$
I_G = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 .
$$
Equations of motion, with the first cardinal equation onto the vertical and the second with pole at $G$:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a = m\,g - S \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{a}{r} = S\,r
\end{cases}
$$
$$
S = \frac{m\,R^2\,a}{2\,r^2} ,
\qquad
m\,a\left(1 + \frac{R^2}{2\,r^2}\right) = m\,g ,
$$
$$
a = \frac{2\,g\,r^2}{R^2 + 2\,r^2} ,
\qquad
S = \frac{m\,g\,R^2}{R^2 + 2\,r^2} .
$$
In the two cases required:
$$
r = R : \quad a = \frac{2}{3}\,g , \quad S = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,g ;
\qquad
r = \frac{R}{2} : \quad a = \frac{1}{3}\,g , \quad S = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,g .
$$

Check with the kinetic energy theorem and with the limit case. The kinetic energy is
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\left(\frac{\dot{y}}{r}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\left(1 + \frac{R^2}{2\,r^2}\right)\dot{y}^2 ,
$$
the string is inextensible and the point $K$ has zero velocity, hence the tension does no work. Differentiating $T = m\,g\,y$ with respect to time one obtains
$$
m\left(1 + \frac{R^2}{2r^2}\right)\ddot{y} = m\,g ,
\qquad
\ddot{y} = \frac{2\,g\,r^2}{R^2 + 2r^2} ,
$$
which coincides with the value found. Moreover, for $r \to 0$ one has $a \to 0$ and $S \to m\,g$: with a groove of very small radius the disk stays practically hanging from the string, and the result is consistent.

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible and vertical, hence the point $K$ where it leaves the groove does not move in the vertical direction; since the motion of the disk is an instantaneous rotation about $K$, the velocity of the center equals $r\,\dot{\vartheta}$ and not $R\,\dot{\vartheta}$. The radius that enters the kinematic condition is that of the groove, while the radius that enters the moment of inertia is that of the disk.

The second cardinal equation is written with pole at $G$, where the weight has zero moment and the tension has lever arm $r$. One could also choose the pole at $K$, with $I_K = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,r^2$; the moment of the tension would be zero and the same acceleration would be obtained directly.

The comparison between the two cases shows the role of the groove: with $r = R$ the acceleration equals $\dfrac{2}{3}\,g$, with $r = \dfrac{R}{2}$ it drops to $\dfrac{g}{3}$, and the tension doubles. **The narrower the groove, the more slowly the disk descends**, because for the same descent the rotation is larger and absorbs more kinetic energy.

The two controls are independent of the cardinal equations: the first uses the kinetic energy theorem, the second examines the behavior of the formula when the radius of the groove tends to zero.

</div>
