## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rests with its end $A$ on a smooth horizontal plane and moves in a vertical plane, as in the diagram in the figure. At the initial instant the rod forms the angle $\vartheta_0 = 60^\circ$ with the plane and is at rest. Compute the angular velocity at the instant when the rod reaches the plane and the reaction of the plane at the initial instant and at that final instant.

::struttura{linee="0,0 6,0" nodi="A,1.2,0;B,3,3.12" aste="A-B" punti="2.1,1.56,G,baricentro" tratteggi="1.2,0 3.9,0" etichette="2,0.33,ϑ" titolo="Rod resting with one end on a smooth horizontal plane: $\vartheta$ is the angle with the plane"}

::struttura{linee="0,0 6,0" nodi="A,1.2,0;B,3,3.12" aste="A-B" punti="2.1,1.56,G,baricentro" forze="2.1 1.56,0,-1,mg" reazioni="A,0,1.2,N" tratteggi="2.1,1.56 2.1,0" titolo="Free-body diagram: the weight at $G$ and the normal reaction $N$ at $A$; the center of mass descends along the dashed vertical line"}

Degrees of freedom and coordinates. The plane is smooth and the weight is vertical, hence the horizontal resultant of the external forces is zero; the rod starts from rest, therefore
$$
\ddot{x}_G = 0 ,
\qquad
x_G = \text{constant} ,
$$
and the center of mass descends along a vertical line. The system then has one degree of freedom, described by the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the plane, and
$$
y_G = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\sin\vartheta ,
\qquad
\dot{y}_G = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
I_G = \frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2 .
$$
Kinetic energy and first integral of the energy, the reaction of the smooth plane doing no work because it is vertical while $A$ moves horizontally:
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\frac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\cos^2\vartheta + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
= \frac{m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2}{24}\,\big( 4 - 3\sin^2\vartheta \big) ,
$$
$$
\frac{m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2}{24}\,\big( 4 - 3\sin^2\vartheta \big) = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \sin\vartheta_0 - \sin\vartheta \big) ,
\qquad
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{12\,g\,\big( \sin\vartheta_0 - \sin\vartheta \big)}{\ell\,\big( 4 - 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)} .
$$
Angular velocity for $\vartheta = 0$, with $\sin\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$:
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{12\,g\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{4\,\ell} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}\,g}{2\,\ell} ,
\qquad
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{3\sqrt{3}\,g}{2\,\ell}} \simeq 1.61\,\sqrt{\frac{g}{\ell}} .
$$
Reaction of the plane. The cardinal equations, with the second written with pole at $G$ and the lever arm of $N$ equal to $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, are
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \ddot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta - \dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta \big) = N - m\,g \\[8pt]
\dfrac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = -N\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\cos\vartheta
\end{cases}
$$
One obtains $\ddot{\vartheta} = -\dfrac{6\,N\cos\vartheta}{m\,\ell}$ from the second and substitutes it into the first:
$$
N = \frac{m\,\big( 2\,g - \ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta \big)}{2\,\big( 4 - 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)} .
$$
At the initial instant $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ and $\sin\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, hence
$$
N_0 = \frac{2\,m\,g}{2\left(4 - \dfrac{9}{4}\right)} = \frac{4}{7}\,m\,g \simeq 0.57\,m\,g ,
$$
while for $\vartheta = 0$ the term $\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta$ vanishes and
$$
N_1 = \frac{2\,m\,g}{2\cdot 4} = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,g .
$$

Check with the case of the vertical rod and with the control of the contact. Setting $\vartheta_0 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and evaluating the formula at the initial instant one obtains
$$
N = \frac{2\,m\,g}{2\,(4-3)} = m\,g ,
$$
that is the static value of a vertical rod at rest resting on the plane: the result is consistent. Moreover the numerator $2g - \ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta$ stays positive throughout the fall, since the term $\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta$ has maximum about $0.68\,g$ in the interval $0 \le \vartheta \le 60^\circ$ and is therefore always smaller than $2g$; therefore $N > 0$ and the rod stays in contact with the plane until the end, as was assumed.

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane is smooth and the weight is vertical, hence no horizontal force acts on the rod: the first cardinal equation projected onto the horizontal gives $\ddot{x}_G = 0$, and since the rod starts from rest the center of mass descends along a vertical line. This reduces the system to **a single degree of freedom** and makes it possible to write the kinetic energy directly as a function of $\vartheta$ and $\dot{\vartheta}$.

The energy is conserved because the reaction of the plane is perpendicular to the displacement of the supporting point, which moves horizontally, and because the weight is conservative. The first integral gives $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ as a function of $\vartheta$ without integrating the differential equation of motion.

For the reaction, instead, the cardinal equations are needed. The second one, written with pole at $G$, contains only the moment of $N$, because the weight has a line of action through $G$; the minus sign arises from the fact that $N$ is applied at $A$, which lies to the left of $G$, hence it tends to rotate the rod clockwise, that is in the sense that decreases $\vartheta$.

The result $N_1 = \dfrac{m g}{4}$ does not depend on the initial angle, because for $\vartheta = 0$ the term with $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ vanishes whatever the angular velocity: **at the instant when the rod reaches the plane the reaction is always one quarter of the weight**. At the initial instant, instead, the reaction depends on $\vartheta_0$ and equals $\dfrac{4}{7}\,m\,g$, that is more than half the weight but less than the static value $m\,g$ of the vertical rod.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ has the end $A$ resting on a smooth horizontal floor and the end $B$ resting on a smooth vertical wall, as in the diagram in the figure. The rod is initially against the wall, that is $\vartheta_0 = 0$, and is set in motion with negligible initial angular velocity. Denoting by $\vartheta$ the angle between the rod and the wall, compute the angular acceleration, the angular velocity and the two reactions as functions of $\vartheta$.

::struttura{linee="0,4.2 0,0 5,0" nodi="A,2.6,0;B,0,3" aste="A-B" punti="1.3,1.5,G,baricentro" etichette="0.33,2.3,ϑ" quote="B,A,ℓ,-0.75" titolo="Rod with one end on the floor and the other on the wall, both smooth"}

::struttura{linee="0,4.2 0,0 5,0" nodi="A,2.6,0;B,0,3" aste="A-B" punti="1.3,1.5,G,baricentro" forze="1.3 1.5,0,-1,mg" reazioni="A,0,1.2,N_A;B,1.2,0,N_B" titolo="Free-body diagram: the two reactions are perpendicular to the smooth guides"}

Degree of freedom and coordinates. The two ends lie on the two guides, hence
$$
A - O = \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
B - O = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - O = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big) ,
$$
and the system has one degree of freedom. The distance of $G$ from the corner $O$ equals $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and stays constant, therefore $G$ travels along an arc of a circle of center $O$.
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\frac{\ell^2}{4}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{m\,\ell^2}{6}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
V = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\cos\vartheta .
$$
The two reactions are perpendicular to the respective guides, which are fixed and smooth, hence they do no work and the energy is conserved. Differentiating $T + V = \text{constant}$ with respect to time one obtains
$$
\frac{m\,\ell^2}{3}\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta ,
\qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\,\sin\vartheta ,
$$
and integrating from $\vartheta_0 = 0$ with zero angular velocity
$$
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{3\,g}{\ell}\,\big( 1 - \cos\vartheta \big) .
$$
Reactions. From the first cardinal equation, with $\vec{a}_G$ obtained by differentiating $G - O$ twice:
$$
\begin{cases}
N_B = m\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \ddot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta - \dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta \big) \\[8pt]
N_A - m\,g = -m\,\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \ddot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta + \dot{\vartheta}^2\cos\vartheta \big)
\end{cases}
$$
Substituting $\ddot{\vartheta}$ and $\dot{\vartheta}^2$:
$$
N_B = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,g\,\sin\vartheta\,\big( 3\cos\vartheta - 2 \big) ,
\qquad
N_A = m\,g\left( \frac{5}{2} - \frac{3}{2}\,\cos\vartheta - \frac{9}{4}\,\sin^2\vartheta \right) .
$$

Check in the initial configuration. For $\vartheta = 0$ the formulas give
$$
N_B = 0 ,
\qquad
N_A = m\,g\left( \frac{5}{2} - \frac{3}{2} \right) = m\,g ,
$$
that is the static values of a vertical rod at rest: the wall is not pressed and the floor supports the whole weight. Moreover, for small $\vartheta$, $N_B \simeq \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,g\,\vartheta$ is positive, hence the rod does press against the wall in the first phase of the motion.

<div class="spiegazione">

The two ends are constrained to the two guides, therefore the configuration depends on the angle $\vartheta$ alone. The most useful property is that the distance of $G$ from the corner $O$ stays equal to $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$: the center of mass describes an arc of a circle, and its velocity has magnitude $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\dot{\vartheta}$. From this the kinetic energy takes the particularly simple form $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{6}\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$, in which the coefficient does not depend on $\vartheta$.

Since the coefficient is constant, the derivative of the first integral gives directly the equation of motion $\ddot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{3g}{2\ell}\sin\vartheta$, without the quadratic terms that would appear with a kinetic energy with a variable coefficient.

The reactions are obtained from the first cardinal equation projected onto the two axes, because the only external forces are the weight and the two reactions, both perpendicular to the smooth guides. The acceleration of $G$ is obtained by differentiating twice the vector $G - O$, of constant magnitude: the term with $\ddot{\vartheta}$ is tangential, the one with $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ is centripetal and directed towards $O$.

The sign of $N_B$ depends on the factor $3\cos\vartheta - 2$: **the reaction of the wall vanishes for $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{2}{3}$** and would become negative beyond that value, which is impossible for a support. Beyond that angle the rod separates from the wall, and the subsequent motion is the one studied in the first exercise.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The rod of the previous exercise starts from rest with the end $B$ on the wall and the initial angle $\vartheta_0$ between the rod and the wall, with $0 \le \vartheta_0 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the angle $\vartheta^*$ at which the end $B$ separates from the wall, the angular velocity and the reaction of the floor at that instant, and discuss the cases $\vartheta_0 = 0$ and $\vartheta_0 = 60^\circ$.

::struttura{linee="0,4.2 0,0 5,0" nodi="A,2.6,0;B,0,3" aste="A-B" punti="1.3,1.5,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,3.6 1.68,0" etichette="0.33,2.25,ϑ" quote="B,A,ℓ,-0.75" titolo="The initial configuration, dashed, forms the angle $\vartheta_0$ with the wall; the generic configuration forms the angle $\vartheta$"}

Quantities needed. The same expressions as in the previous exercise hold, with the constant of the first integral fixed by the initial configuration $\vartheta = \vartheta_0$, $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$:
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\,\sin\vartheta ,
\qquad
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{3\,g}{\ell}\,\big( \cos\vartheta_0 - \cos\vartheta \big) .
$$
Reaction of the wall as a function of $\vartheta$, from the first cardinal equation projected onto the horizontal:
$$
N_B = m\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \ddot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta - \dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta \big)
= \frac{3}{4}\,m\,g\,\sin\vartheta\,\big( 3\cos\vartheta - 2\cos\vartheta_0 \big) .
$$
Condition of separation $N_B = 0$ with $\sin\vartheta \neq 0$:
$$
3\cos\vartheta^* - 2\cos\vartheta_0 = 0 ,
\qquad
\cos\vartheta^* = \frac{2}{3}\,\cos\vartheta_0 .
$$
Angular velocity at the separation:
$$
\dot{\vartheta}^{*2} = \frac{3\,g}{\ell}\left( \cos\vartheta_0 - \frac{2}{3}\cos\vartheta_0 \right) = \frac{g\,\cos\vartheta_0}{\ell} .
$$
Reaction of the floor at the separation, from the formula of the previous exercise written with generic $\vartheta_0$:
$$
N_A = m\,g\left( \frac{5}{2} - \frac{3}{2}\,\cos\vartheta_0\cos\vartheta - \frac{9}{4}\,\sin^2\vartheta \right) ,
$$
and substituting $\cos\vartheta^* = \dfrac{2}{3}\cos\vartheta_0$, that is $\sin^2\vartheta^* = 1 - \dfrac{4}{9}\cos^2\vartheta_0$:
$$
N_A^* = m\,g\left( \frac{5}{2} - \cos^2\vartheta_0 - \frac{9}{4} + \cos^2\vartheta_0 \right) = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,g .
$$
Cases required:
$$
\vartheta_0 = 0 : \quad \cos\vartheta^* = \frac{2}{3} , \quad \vartheta^* = 48.2^\circ , \quad \dot{\vartheta}^* = \sqrt{\frac{g}{\ell}} ;
$$
$$
\vartheta_0 = 60^\circ : \quad \cos\vartheta^* = \frac{1}{3} , \quad \vartheta^* = 70.5^\circ , \quad \dot{\vartheta}^* = \sqrt{\frac{g}{2\,\ell}} .
$$

Check with the formula of the first exercise. At the instant of separation the wall no longer exerts any force, hence the rod has only the end $A$ resting on the smooth floor and the formula of the first exercise holds, in which the angle is measured from the floor, that is $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \vartheta$. At the separation one has
$$
\sin\theta = \cos\vartheta^* = \frac{2}{3}\,\cos\vartheta_0 ,
\qquad
\dot{\theta}^2 = \dot{\vartheta}^{*2} = \frac{g\,\cos\vartheta_0}{\ell} ,
$$
and substituting into the formula
$$
N = \frac{m\,\big( 2\,g - \ell\,\dot{\theta}^2\sin\theta \big)}{2\,\big( 4 - 3\sin^2\theta \big)}
$$
the numerator becomes $\dfrac{2}{3}\,m\,g\,\big( 3 - \cos^2\vartheta_0 \big)$ and the denominator $\dfrac{8}{3}\,\big( 3 - \cos^2\vartheta_0 \big)$, therefore $N = \dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,g$, which is the value found above for every $\vartheta_0$. For $\vartheta_0 \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, finally, one has $\cos\vartheta_0 \to 0$, hence $\cos\vartheta^* \to 0$ and $\dot{\vartheta}^* \to 0$: the rod is already lying on the floor and does not move, as it must be.

<div class="spiegazione">

The constraint of the wall is a one-sided support: it can push the rod to the right, not hold it back. The condition for the contact to persist is therefore $N_B \ge 0$, and the separation occurs at the first instant at which $N_B$ vanishes.

The factor $\sin\vartheta$ in $N_B$ vanishes only for $\vartheta = 0$, which is the initial configuration in the case $\vartheta_0 = 0$; the actual separation therefore corresponds to the vanishing of the second factor, that is to $\cos\vartheta^* = \dfrac{2}{3}\cos\vartheta_0$. Since $\dfrac{2}{3}\cos\vartheta_0 < \cos\vartheta_0$, the angle $\vartheta^*$ is always larger than $\vartheta_0$ and smaller than $90^\circ$: **the separation always occurs, before the rod reaches the floor**.

The angular velocity at the separation, $\dot{\vartheta}^* = \sqrt{\dfrac{g\cos\vartheta_0}{\ell}}$, is the larger the closer to the wall the rod starts, because the descent of the center of mass up to the separation is larger.

The value of the reaction of the floor at the separation is $\dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,g$ for every $\vartheta_0$. The check shows why: at the instant of separation the rod has only the support on the floor, and substituting the angle and the angular velocity of the separation into the general formula of the reaction the factors depending on $\vartheta_0$ cancel. After the separation the motion continues with the support on the floor alone and with the center of mass descending along a vertical line.

</div>
