## Exercise 1

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is keyed on a spindle of negligible mass, pivoted at the point $O$, and rotates about its own axis of figure with constant spin velocity $\dot{\varphi}$. The axis of figure is kept horizontal and the center of the disk lies at distance $d$ from $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the precession velocity of the axis about the vertical through $O$, and compute its value and its period for $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$, $R = 15\,\mathrm{cm}$, $d = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\dot{\varphi} = 200\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;B,3.4,0,-" aste="O-B" cerchi="3.4,0,1.1" vincoli="O,cerniera" frecce="0,-1.2,0,3,asse di precessione;0,0,4.9,0,ζ" punti="3.4,0,G,baricentro" forze="3.4 0,0,-1.1,mg" coppie="0 2.5,1,Ω;4.6 0,1,φ̇" quote="O,3.4 0,d,1.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Gyroscope with a horizontal axis of figure pivoted at $O$"}

Quantities needed. The disk has gyroscopic structure with respect to $O$, with axis of figure $\zeta$; the axial moment and the angle of nutation are
$$
C = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 ,
\qquad
\vartheta = \frac{\pi}{2} .
$$
Condition of regular precession. The general relation $C\,\Omega\,r - A\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta = M\,g\,d$, with $\cos\vartheta = 0$ and $r = \dot{\varphi} + \Omega\cos\vartheta = \dot{\varphi}$, reduces to
$$
C\,\Omega\,\dot{\varphi} = m\,g\,d ,
\qquad
\Omega = \frac{m\,g\,d}{C\,\dot{\varphi}} = \frac{2\,g\,d}{R^2\,\dot{\varphi}} .
$$
Numerical values:
$$
C = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 0.15^2 = 0.045\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2} ,
$$
$$
\Omega = \frac{4 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.20}{0.045 \cdot 200} = 0.87\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = \frac{2\pi}{\Omega} = 7.21\,\mathrm{s} .
$$
The sense of the precession is the one for which $\vec{K}_O$ rotates towards the moment of the weight, that is the axis of figure turns about the vertical in the sense that carries the tip of the axis to the side towards which the weight would tend to make it descend.

Check with the derivative of the angular momentum. The spin velocity is much larger than the precession velocity, since $\dfrac{\Omega}{\dot{\varphi}} = 0.0044$, hence the angular momentum is almost parallel to the axis of figure:
$$
|\vec{K}_O| \simeq C\,\dot{\varphi} = 0.045 \cdot 200 = 9.0\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s} .
$$
This vector has constant magnitude and rotates about the vertical with angular velocity $\Omega$, hence its derivative is horizontal, perpendicular to the axis, of magnitude $C\,\dot{\varphi}\,\Omega$. The moment of the weight with respect to $O$ is horizontal as well and perpendicular to the axis, of magnitude $m\,g\,d$. Equating the two magnitudes:
$$
C\,\dot{\varphi}\,\Omega = m\,g\,d ,
\qquad
9.0 \cdot 0.872 = 7.85\,\mathrm{N\,m} ,
\qquad
m\,g\,d = 4 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.20 = 7.85\,\mathrm{N\,m} ,
$$
and the two values coincide. The computation with the approximation of the fast gyroscope therefore confirms the result obtained from the exact condition, as it must for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, where the term in $\Omega^2$ vanishes.

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is a heavy gyroscope with a fixed point: the disk has two equal principal moments with respect to every diameter through its center, and the gyroscopic structure is preserved with respect to $O$ as well, because $O$ lies on the axis of figure.

With the axis horizontal the condition of regular precession loses the term $A\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta$, hence the transverse moment of inertia $A$ does not enter the result: the precession velocity depends only on the axial moment, on the weight and on the distance $d$.

The result $\Omega = \dfrac{m\,g\,d}{C\,\dot{\varphi}}$ contains the spin in the denominator, therefore **the faster the disk turns, the slower the precession**. With the given data the disk makes about thirty-two turns per second about its own axis, while the axis makes one complete turn about the vertical in a little more than seven seconds.

The check uses the vector interpretation: the external moment changes the direction of the angular momentum without changing its magnitude, because it is perpendicular to it, and a vector of constant magnitude that rotates with angular velocity $\Omega$ has a derivative of magnitude $|\vec{K}_O|\,\Omega$. The equality between this derivative and the moment of the weight gives the same formula.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is keyed on a spindle of negligible mass pivoted at the point $O$, with the center of the disk at distance $d = R$ from $O$ on the axis of figure, as in the diagram in the figure. The disk rotates with constant spin velocity $\dot{\varphi}$ and the axis of figure forms the constant angle $\vartheta = 60^\circ$ with the upward vertical. Determine the possible precession velocities and the condition on $\dot{\varphi}$ for the regular precession to exist, and give the values for $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\dot{\varphi} = 150\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;B,2.6,1.5,-" aste="O-B" cerchi="2.6,1.5,0.95" vincoli="O,cerniera" frecce="0,0,0,3.6,asse di precessione;0,0,4.2,2.42,ζ" punti="2.6,1.5,G,baricentro" forze="2.6 1.5,0,-1,mg" coppie="0 3,1,Ω;4 2.3,1,φ̇" quote="O,2.6 1.5,d = R,1.0" etichette="0.42,1.0,ϑ" riferimento=0 titolo="Disk inclined by $\vartheta = 60^\circ$ to the vertical, pivoted at $O$ at distance $R$ from the center"}

Quantities needed. The disk has gyroscopic structure with respect to $O$ because $O$ lies on the axis of figure. The axial moment and the transverse moment with respect to $O$, the latter obtained with the Huygens-Steiner theorem, are
$$
C = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 ,
\qquad
A = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = \frac{5}{4}\,m\,R^2 .
$$
Condition of regular precession. One substitutes $r = \dot{\varphi} + \Omega\cos\vartheta$ into the relation $C\,\Omega\,r - A\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta = m\,g\,d$ and obtains the second-degree equation in $\Omega$
$$
\big( C - A \big)\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta + C\,\Omega\,\dot{\varphi} - m\,g\,d = 0 .
$$
With $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $d = R$ and the two moments written above:
$$
-\frac{3}{4}\,m\,R^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,\Omega^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\Omega\,\dot{\varphi} - m\,g\,R = 0 ,
$$
$$
3\,\Omega^2 - 4\,\dot{\varphi}\,\Omega + \frac{8\,g}{R} = 0 ,
\qquad
\Omega = \frac{2\,\dot{\varphi} \pm \sqrt{4\,\dot{\varphi}^2 - \dfrac{24\,g}{R}}}{3} .
$$
The two roots are real if and only if the discriminant is nonnegative, that is if
$$
\dot{\varphi}^2 \ge \frac{6\,g}{R} ,
\qquad
\dot{\varphi} \ge \sqrt{\frac{6\,g}{R}} .
$$
Numerical values, with $R = 0.10\,\mathrm{m}$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$:
$$
\sqrt{\frac{6 \cdot 9.81}{0.10}} = 24.3\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
$$
hence with $\dot{\varphi} = 150\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ the condition is satisfied and the equation $3\,\Omega^2 - 600\,\Omega + 784.8 = 0$ gives
$$
\Omega_1 = 1.32\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\Omega_2 = 198.7\,\mathrm{rad/s} .
$$

Check with the approximation of the fast gyroscope and with the relations between the roots. For large $\dot{\varphi}$ the term in $\Omega^2$ is negligible in the small root, and the equation reduces to $C\,\Omega\,\dot{\varphi} = m\,g\,d$, that is
$$
\Omega \simeq \frac{2\,g}{R\,\dot{\varphi}} = \frac{2 \cdot 9.81}{0.10 \cdot 150} = 1.31\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
$$
which differs by one per cent from the exact value $1.32\,\mathrm{rad/s}$. Moreover the two roots of the equation satisfy
$$
\Omega_1 + \Omega_2 = \frac{4\,\dot{\varphi}}{3} = 200.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} ,
\qquad
\Omega_1\,\Omega_2 = \frac{8\,g}{3\,R} = 261.6\,\mathrm{rad^2/s^2} ,
$$
and the values found give $1.32 + 198.7 = 200.0$ and $1.32 \cdot 198.7 = 262.3$, in agreement within the rounding errors.

<div class="spiegazione">

The condition of regular precession is a second-degree equation in $\Omega$, because the precession velocity appears both in the term $C\,\Omega\,r$, through $r = \dot{\varphi} + \Omega\cos\vartheta$, and in the term $A\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta$ that accounts for the centrifugal force due to the precession itself.

Once the angle of nutation and the spin are fixed, the possible precessions are two: a slow one and a fast one. In the **slow precession** the gyroscopic effect supports the weight almost by itself, and the precession velocity is inversely proportional to $\dot{\varphi}$; in the **fast precession** it is the centrifugal term that balances the weight, and the precession velocity is of the same order as $\dot{\varphi}$. In real tops the slow precession is observed, because it is the one compatible with the usual initial conditions.

The condition $\dot{\varphi} \ge \sqrt{\dfrac{6\,g}{R}}$ says that below a certain spin no regular precession with that angle is possible: the top cannot keep the inclination and its axis begins to oscillate in nutation. With $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ the threshold value is about $24\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, that is less than four turns per second.

The two checks are independent: the first compares the small root with the approximate formula of the fast gyroscope, the second uses the relations between the sum and the product of the roots and the coefficients of the equation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rotor, modeled as a homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$, is keyed on a horizontal shaft supported by two bearings $A$ and $B$ at distance $b$, symmetric with respect to the rotor. The whole support is mounted on a platform that rotates about a vertical axis with constant angular velocity $\Omega$, as in the diagram in the figure, while the rotor turns about its own axis with constant angular velocity $\dot{\varphi}$. Compute the moment that the bearings have to exert and the corresponding forces, for $m = 20\,\mathrm{kg}$, $R = 30\,\mathrm{cm}$, $\dot{\varphi} = 100\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, $\Omega = 2\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ and $b = 50\,\mathrm{cm}$.

::struttura{nodi="A,-1.9,0;B,1.9,0" aste="A-B" cerchi="0,0,0.85" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" frecce="-2.9,0,3.1,0,ζ;0,-1.9,0,2.6,asse di precessione" reazioni="A,0,1.1,Φ_A;B,0,-1.1,Φ_B" coppie="2.5 0,1,φ̇;0 2.1,1,Ω" quote="A,B,b,-3" riferimento=0 titolo="Rotor on two bearings forced to precess about a vertical axis"}

Quantities needed. The rotor has gyroscopic structure and its axial moment is
$$
C = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 20 \cdot 0.30^2 = 0.90\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2} .
$$
The axis of figure is horizontal and the axis of precession is vertical, hence $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the axial component of the angular velocity is $r = \dot{\varphi}$.

Gyroscopic moment. The angular momentum with respect to the center of mass of the rotor is almost parallel to the axis of figure, with magnitude
$$
|\vec{K}_G| \simeq C\,\dot{\varphi} = 0.90 \cdot 100 = 90\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s} ,
$$
and it rotates about the vertical axis with angular velocity $\Omega$ without changing magnitude. Its derivative is therefore horizontal, perpendicular to the axis of figure, and the moment that the bearings have to exert is
$$
|\vec{M}| = C\,\dot{\varphi}\,\Omega = 0.90 \cdot 100 \cdot 2 = 180\,\mathrm{N\,m} .
$$
Forces on the bearings. The moment is a couple, because the center of mass lies on the axis of precession and the resultant of the forces due to the precession is zero; the two forces are therefore equal and opposite, vertical, applied at the two bearings at distance $b$:
$$
|\vec{\Phi}_A| = |\vec{\Phi}_B| = \frac{C\,\dot{\varphi}\,\Omega}{b} = \frac{180}{0.50} = 360\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
To these there are added the static reactions, which support the weight $m\,g = 196\,\mathrm{N}$ shared between the two bearings.

Check with the Euler equations. One takes the frame fixed to the rotor with $\hat{e}_\zeta$ along the axis of figure. The angular velocity is $\vec{\omega} = \Omega\,\hat{k} + \dot{\varphi}\,\hat{e}_\zeta$, with $\hat{k}$ vertical and perpendicular to the axis; its axial component is $r = \dot{\varphi}$, constant, while the transverse component has constant magnitude $\Omega$ and rotates in the body-fixed frame with velocity $-\dot{\varphi}$, because the frame turns about $\hat{e}_\zeta$ with velocity $\dot{\varphi}$ relative to the support. One therefore has
$$
\dot{p} = \dot{\varphi}\,q ,
\qquad
\dot{q} = -\dot{\varphi}\,p ,
\qquad
p^2 + q^2 = \Omega^2 ,
$$
and substituting into the first two Euler equations, with $B = A$:
$$
M_\xi = A\,\dot{p} + (C-A)\,q\,r = A\,\dot{\varphi}\,q + (C-A)\,q\,\dot{\varphi} = C\,\dot{\varphi}\,q ,
$$
$$
M_\eta = A\,\dot{q} + (A-C)\,r\,p = -A\,\dot{\varphi}\,p + (A-C)\,\dot{\varphi}\,p = -C\,\dot{\varphi}\,p .
$$
The transverse moment then has magnitude $C\,\dot{\varphi}\sqrt{p^2+q^2} = C\,\dot{\varphi}\,\Omega = 180\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, equal to the value found, and the computation is exact, without the approximation of the fast gyroscope. The third equation gives $M_\zeta = C\,\dot{r} = 0$, consistently with the fact that the spin is uniform.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rotor is forced to precess: an external constraint requires the axis of figure to rotate about the vertical with velocity $\Omega$. The angular momentum then changes direction, and by the second cardinal equation this requires an external moment, which the bearings have to provide.

The moment is perpendicular both to the axis of figure and to the axis of precession, hence it is horizontal and perpendicular to the shaft: the two forces on the bearings are vertical and opposite. It is the reason why the shaft of a turbine on board a ship that is turning loads its own supports with forces that do not exist when the ship goes straight ahead.

The check with the Euler equations confirms the result without approximations. In the computation there appears the sum $A\,\dot{\varphi}\,q + (C-A)\,q\,\dot{\varphi}$, in which the transverse moment $A$ cancels: the moment required equals exactly $C\,\dot{\varphi}\,\Omega$ whatever $A$ is. This happens because the axis of figure is perpendicular to the axis of precession, that is because $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ makes the term $A\,\Omega^2\cos\vartheta$ of the condition of regular precession vanish.

The forces on the bearings due to the precession are $360\,\mathrm{N}$ each, while the weight of the rotor is $196\,\mathrm{N}$ and in statics it is shared as $98\,\mathrm{N}$ per bearing: a precession of only two radians per second brings the load on a support to **almost four times** the static value.

</div>
