## Exercise 1

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a plane inclined by $\alpha$ to the horizontal, as the diagram in the figure shows. The motion takes place in a vertical plane, the disk starts from rest and the friction is sufficient to prevent slipping. Compute the acceleration of the center, the friction force and the velocity of the center after the disk has traveled the distance $\ell$ along the plane.

::struttura{linee="0,0 6,3.46 6,0 0,0" cerchi="2.57,2.48,0.86" punti="2.57,2.48,G,baricentro;3,1.73,C,centro" etichette="1.05,0.26,α;1.75,3.3,m, R" titolo="Homogeneous disk rolling without slipping on the inclined plane"}

::struttura{linee="0,0 6,3.46 6,0 0,0" cerchi="2.57,2.48,0.86" punti="2.57,2.48,G,baricentro;3,1.73,C,centro" forze="2.57 2.48,0,-1,mg" reazioni="3 1.73,-0.6,1.04,N;3 1.73,0.95,0.55,F_a" coppie="2.57 2.48,1,ϑ" etichette="1.05,0.26,α" titolo="Free-body diagram: the weight at $G$, the normal reaction $N$ and the friction force $F_a$ at the contact $C$"}

Degree of freedom and coordinate: the abscissa $x$ of $G$ along the plane, oriented downwards, and the rotation $\vartheta$ measured counterclockwise, with the condition of pure rolling
$$
\dot{x} = R\,\dot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = R\,\ddot{\vartheta} = a_G .
$$
Moment of inertia of the disk about the center of mass:
$$
I_G = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 .
$$
Equations of motion, with the first cardinal equation along the plane and the second with pole at $G$:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a_G = m\,g\sin\alpha - F_a \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{a_G}{R} = F_a\,R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
F_a = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,a_G ,
\qquad
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,a_G = m\,g\sin\alpha ,
\qquad
a_G = \frac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha ,
\qquad
F_a = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,g\sin\alpha .
$$
The acceleration is constant and the motion starts from rest, therefore
$$
v_G = \sqrt{2\,a_G\,\ell} = 2\sqrt{\frac{g\,\ell\,\sin\alpha}{3}} .
$$

Check with the kinetic energy theorem. The kinetic energy of the disk in rolling is
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\left(\frac{v_G}{R}\right)^{\!2} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2 ,
$$
the normal reaction and the friction force are applied at the contact point, which has zero velocity, hence they do no work, and the weight does the work $m\,g\,\ell\sin\alpha$. From the equality
$$
\frac{3}{4}\,m\,v_G^2 = m\,g\,\ell\sin\alpha
$$
it follows that $v_G^2 = \dfrac{4}{3}\,g\,\ell\sin\alpha$, which is the square of the value found above.

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk rolls without slipping on a fixed guide, therefore the system has **one degree of freedom**: the rotation $\vartheta$ and the advance $x$ are linked by the condition of pure rolling $\dot{x} = R\,\dot{\vartheta}$, which is obtained by setting the velocity of the contact point equal to zero. One chooses $x$ because the result required is the acceleration of the center.

The external forces are three: the weight applied at $G$, the normal reaction $N$ and the friction force $F_a$, both applied at the contact point $C$. The first cardinal equation projected along the plane contains the weight and the friction, since $N$ is perpendicular to the plane. The second cardinal equation is written with pole at $G$, where $\vec{K}_G = I_G\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k}$; with respect to $G$ the weight and the normal reaction have zero moment, because their lines of action pass through $G$, hence only the moment of $F_a$ remains, with lever arm $R$.

From the second equation it follows that $F_a = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,a_G$, and substituting into the first one obtains $a_G = \dfrac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha$. The acceleration is therefore **smaller than that of a body sliding without friction**, $g\sin\alpha$, because part of the work of the weight goes into the kinetic energy of rotation. The friction force is directed up along the plane and equals one third of the component of the weight along the plane.

The static friction does no work, since the point at which it is applied has zero velocity at every instant, therefore the check with the kinetic energy theorem is a control independent of the cardinal equations and gives the same value of the velocity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A cord is wound on a homogeneous cylinder of mass $m$ and radius $R$ resting on a horizontal guide; the cord comes off horizontally from the highest point $P$ and is pulled with a constant force $F$, as in the diagram in the figure. The cylinder rolls without slipping. Compute the acceleration of the center and the friction force, and determine the largest value of $F$ compatible with pure rolling if the coefficient of static friction is $f_s$.

::struttura{linee="0,0 7,0" cerchi="2.6,1.2,1.2" punti="2.6,1.2,G,baricentro;2.6,0,C,centro" nodi="P,2.6,2.4,-" forze="4.2 2.4,1.6,0,F" etichette="1.95,2.9,P" titolo="Cylinder pulled by a cord that comes off the highest point"}

::struttura{linee="0,0 7,0" cerchi="2.6,1.2,1.2" punti="2.6,1.2,G,baricentro;2.6,0,C,centro" forze="4.2 2.4,1.6,0,F;2.6 1.2,0,-0.8,mg" reazioni="2.6 0,0,1.2,N;2.6 0,1.2,0,F_a" coppie="2.6 1.2,-1,ϑ" titolo="Free-body diagram: the force $F$ at the top, the weight, the normal reaction and the friction"}

Degree of freedom and coordinate: the abscissa $x$ of $G$, oriented in the sense of $F$, and the rotation $\vartheta$ measured clockwise, with
$$
\dot{x} = R\,\dot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
a_G = R\,\ddot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
I_G = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 .
$$
Equations of motion, with the first cardinal equation along the guide and the second with pole at $G$, taking $F_a$ positive in the sense of $F$:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a_G = F + F_a \\[6pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{a_G}{R} = (F - F_a)\,R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
F_a = F - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,a_G ,
\qquad
m\,a_G = 2F - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,a_G ,
\qquad
a_G = \frac{4F}{3m} ,
\qquad
F_a = \frac{F}{3} .
$$
The normal reaction is $N = m\,g$, because the motion is horizontal, hence the condition of pure rolling $F_a \le f_s\,N$ becomes
$$
\frac{F}{3} \le f_s\,m\,g ,
\qquad
F \le 3\,f_s\,m\,g .
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation written with pole at the contact point $C$. The moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $C$ is $I_C = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$; with respect to $C$ the weight, the normal reaction and the friction force have zero moment, while $F$ has lever arm $2R$. Therefore
$$
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{a_G}{R} = 2\,R\,F ,
\qquad
a_G = \frac{4F}{3m} ,
$$
which is the value already found.

<div class="spiegazione">

The force is applied at the highest point of the cylinder, which in rolling has velocity $2\,\dot{x}$, that is twice that of the center; the lever arm of $F$ with respect to the contact point is therefore $2R$ and the force is more effective than it would be if applied at the center.

In the second cardinal equation with pole at $G$ there appear the moments of $F$, with lever arm $R$, and of the friction force, also with lever arm $R$ but with the opposite sense of rotation, whence the difference $F - F_a$. The weight and the normal reaction have lines of action that pass through $G$, hence zero moment.

The result $F_a = \dfrac{F}{3}$ is positive, therefore the friction force is **directed in the sense of $F$**: the contact pushes the cylinder forward. If the same force were applied at the center, the two equations would give $a_G = \dfrac{2F}{3m}$ and $F_a = -\dfrac{F}{3}$, that is friction directed backwards and half the acceleration.

The largest value of $F$ is obtained by requiring that the static friction force needed does not exceed the limit value $f_s\,N$. The normal reaction equals $m\,g$ because the vertical acceleration is zero and the cord is horizontal. Beyond the value $3\,f_s\,m\,g$ the contact slips and the condition $\dot{x} = R\,\dot{\vartheta}$ no longer holds.

The check with the pole at the contact point is legitimate because the velocity of the geometric contact point is parallel to $\vec{v}_G$ and the distance of $G$ from the contact stays equal to $R$; at that pole the reactions of the contact have zero moment and the equation contains the single unknown $a_G$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A solid disk, a thin ring and a solid sphere, all homogeneous, of mass $m$ and radius $R$, are released from rest at the same instant and from the same height on a plane inclined by $\alpha = 30^\circ$, as the diagram in the figure shows. The coefficient of static friction between the bodies and the plane is $f_s = 0.20$. Establish which bodies roll without slipping and in which order they arrive at the bottom, and compute the velocity of the center after a descent of height $h$.

::struttura{linee="0,0 8,4.62 8,0 0,0" cerchi="2.15,2.05,0.7;3.95,3.09,0.7;5.75,4.13,0.7" punti="2.15,2.05,G,baricentro;3.95,3.09,G,baricentro;5.75,4.13,G,baricentro" etichette="1.1,0.3,α;1.35,3.05,disk;3.1,4.1,ring;5.05,5.15,sphere" titolo="Three homogeneous bodies of equal mass and equal radius on the same inclined plane"}

One sets $I_G = \beta\,m\,R^2$, with $\beta$ depending on the shape of the body:
$$
\beta_{\text{disk}} = \frac{1}{2} ,
\qquad
\beta_{\text{ring}} = 1 ,
\qquad
\beta_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{2}{5} .
$$
Equations of motion for the generic body, with the first cardinal equation along the plane and the second with pole at $G$:
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,a_G = m\,g\sin\alpha - F_a \\[6pt]
\beta\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{a_G}{R} = F_a\,R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a_G = \frac{g\sin\alpha}{1+\beta} ,
\qquad
F_a = \frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\,m\,g\sin\alpha .
$$
Condition of pure rolling, with $N = m\,g\cos\alpha$:
$$
\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\,m\,g\sin\alpha \le f_s\,m\,g\cos\alpha ,
\qquad
\tan\alpha \le \frac{1+\beta}{\beta}\,f_s .
$$
With $\alpha = 30^\circ$ one has $\tan\alpha = 0.577$ and the three right-hand sides are
$$
\text{disk: } 3\,f_s = 0.60 ,
\qquad
\text{ring: } 2\,f_s = 0.40 ,
\qquad
\text{sphere: } \frac{7}{2}\,f_s = 0.70 .
$$
The disk and the sphere roll without slipping, the ring slips. For the two bodies that roll,
$$
a_{G,\text{disk}} = \frac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha = \frac{g}{3} ,
\qquad
a_{G,\text{sphere}} = \frac{5}{7}\,g\sin\alpha = \frac{5\,g}{14} ,
$$
hence the sphere has the larger acceleration and arrives first. The velocity of the center after a descent of height $h$ is obtained from the kinetic energy theorem,
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_G^2\,(1+\beta) = m\,g\,h ,
\qquad
v_G = \sqrt{\frac{2\,g\,h}{1+\beta}} ,
$$
that is $v_G = \sqrt{\dfrac{4\,g\,h}{3}}$ for the disk and $v_G = \sqrt{\dfrac{10\,g\,h}{7}}$ for the sphere.

Check with the limit case and with the kinematic comparison. For $\beta \to 0$, that is for a body whose mass is all concentrated on the axis, the formulas give $a_G = g\sin\alpha$, $F_a = 0$ and $v_G = \sqrt{2gh}$, which are the values of a particle sliding without friction: the result is consistent. Moreover the motion is uniformly accelerated from rest, hence $v_G^2 = 2\,a_G\,s$ with $s = \dfrac{h}{\sin\alpha}$; for the disk
$$
2\cdot\frac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha\cdot\frac{h}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{4\,g\,h}{3} ,
$$
which coincides with the square of the velocity obtained from the energy.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equations of motion are the same for the three bodies and change only through the number $\beta$, which measures how much mass is far from the axis. The result $a_G = \dfrac{g\sin\alpha}{1+\beta}$ contains neither the mass nor the radius, therefore **the order of arrival depends only on the shape**: the sphere, which has the smallest $\beta$, arrives before the disk, and the ring would be the last.

The friction force needed grows with $\beta$, since a body with mass farther from the axis requires a larger couple to be set in rotation. The ring has $\beta = 1$ and requires $F_a = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,g\sin\alpha$, which with $f_s = 0.20$ exceeds the largest value available: the ring *slips*, its equations of motion are different and the rolling formula does not apply to it.

The final velocity is obtained more quickly from the kinetic energy theorem than from the integration of the equations of motion, because the static friction does no work and the normal reaction is perpendicular to the displacement. The factor $1+\beta$ distributes the energy between translation and rotation: for the sphere $\dfrac{2}{7}$ of the kinetic energy goes into rotation, for the disk $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

The limit case $\beta \to 0$ and the comparison with the formula of uniformly accelerated motion are two independent controls: the first checks the structure of the formula, the second checks that the value of the velocity is consistent with the acceleration found from the cardinal equations.

</div>
