## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $A$ to a fixed smooth horizontal axis, perpendicular to the vertical plane of the motion, and is released from rest in the horizontal position, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the two components of the reaction of the hinge as functions of the angle $\vartheta$ that the rod forms with the horizontal, and determine the value of $\vartheta$ for which the horizontal component is largest in magnitude.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.6,-2.3" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" punti="1.3,-1.15,G,baricentro" forze="1.3 -1.15,0,-1,mg" reazioni="A,1.1,0,Φ_Ax;A,0,1.1,Φ_Ay" tratteggi="0,0 2.1,0" etichette="0.95,-0.42,ϑ" titolo="Free-body diagram of the rod in the generic position $\vartheta$"}

Degree of freedom and coordinate: the angle $\vartheta$, measured from the horizontal downwards. Position of the center of mass and moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $A$:
$$
G - A = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big) ,
\qquad
I_A = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 .
$$
Equation of motion about the fixed axis and first integral of the energy, with $\vartheta = 0$ and $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ at the initial instant:
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}\,\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{3\,g}{\ell}\,\sin\vartheta .
$$
Acceleration of the center of mass, differentiating $G - A$ twice:
$$
\vec{a}_G = -\frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \ddot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta + \dot{\vartheta}^2\cos\vartheta \big)\,\hat{\imath}
- \frac{\ell}{2}\,\big( \ddot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta - \dot{\vartheta}^2\sin\vartheta \big)\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
Substituting $\ddot{\vartheta}$ and $\dot{\vartheta}^2$:
$$
a_{Gx} = -\frac{9}{8}\,g\,\sin 2\vartheta ,
\qquad
a_{Gy} = \frac{3}{4}\,g\,\big( 3\sin^2\vartheta - 1 \big) .
$$
First cardinal equation, the only external forces being the weight and the reaction:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} = m\,a_{Gx} \\[6pt]
\Phi_{Ay} - m\,g = m\,a_{Gy}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_{Ax} = -\frac{9}{8}\,m\,g\,\sin 2\vartheta ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,g\,\big( 1 + 9\sin^2\vartheta \big) .
$$
The horizontal component is largest in magnitude for $\sin 2\vartheta = 1$, that is for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, where
$$
\Phi_{Ax} = -\frac{9}{8}\,m\,g ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = \frac{11}{8}\,m\,g ,
\qquad
|\vec{\Phi}_A| = \frac{\sqrt{202}}{8}\,m\,g \simeq 1.78\,m\,g .
$$

Check at the two ends of the interval. For $\vartheta = 0$ the formulas give $\Phi_{Ax} = 0$ and $\Phi_{Ay} = \dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,g$; at that instant the angular velocity is zero, the acceleration of the center of mass is purely tangential and equals $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\ddot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{3}{4}\,g$ downwards, hence $\Phi_{Ay} = m\,g - \dfrac{3}{4}\,m\,g = \dfrac{1}{4}\,m\,g$, as was found. For $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the formulas give $\Phi_{Ax} = 0$ and $\Phi_{Ay} = \dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,g$; at that instant the moment of the weight is zero, the acceleration is purely centripetal and equals $\dot{\vartheta}^2\,\dfrac{\ell}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}\,g$ upwards, hence $\Phi_{Ay} = m\,g + \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,g = \dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,g$. The two controls, carried out without using the general formulas, give the same values.

<div class="spiegazione">

The motion is determined first, with the equation of motion about a fixed axis, in which the reaction of the hinge does not appear. The first integral of the energy provides $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ as a function of $\vartheta$, and the two quantities $\ddot{\vartheta}$ and $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ are all that is needed to compute the acceleration of the center of mass.

The acceleration of $G$ is obtained by differentiating twice the vector $G - A$, of constant magnitude $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$: the first term is the tangential acceleration, proportional to $\ddot{\vartheta}$, the second is the centripetal acceleration, proportional to $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ and directed from $G$ towards $A$.

The two components of the reaction are obtained from the first cardinal equation applied to the rod, because the only external forces are the weight and the reaction of the hinge. The vertical component **grows from $\dfrac{m g}{4}$ to $\dfrac{5}{2}\,m\,g$** during the fall, while the horizontal component is zero at the ends and largest halfway.

The negative sign of $\Phi_{Ax}$ for $0 < \vartheta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ indicates that the hinge pulls the rod to the left, that is towards the side opposite to the one where the center of mass lies: it is the force that keeps $G$ on the circle of center $A$. The largest reaction, about $1.78$ times the weight, is reached at $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ for the horizontal component, while the vertical reaction keeps growing up to the vertical position.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is welded by its midpoint $G$ to a vertical shaft, with which it forms the constant angle $\alpha$; the point $G$ lies on the axis of rotation, halfway between the two bearings $O$ and $B$, placed at distance $h$ from each other, as in the diagram in the figure. The shaft rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ and its mass is negligible. Compute the reactions of the bearings due to the rotation, and their value for $m = 3\,\mathrm{kg}$, $\ell = 0.80\,\mathrm{m}$, $\alpha = 30^\circ$, $h = 0.60\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\omega = 50\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;B,0,4;D,1.7,3.15,-;E,-1.7,0.85,-" aste="O-B;D-E" vincoli="O,cerniera;B,cerniera,90" punti="0,2,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,2 0,3.7" reazioni="O,1.1,0,Φ_O;B,-1.1,0,Φ_B" etichette="0.38,2.9,α" quote="O,B,h,-0.95" titolo="Rod welded by its midpoint to a vertical shaft: the transverse reactions of the two bearings"}

Frame and quantities needed. One takes the body-fixed frame with origin at $G$, the $\zeta$ axis along the shaft and the $\xi$ axis in the plane of the rod, perpendicular to $\zeta$. The point of the rod of curvilinear abscissa $s$, measured from $G$, has coordinates $\xi = s\sin\alpha$ and $\zeta = s\cos\alpha$, hence the products of inertia are
$$
I_{\xi\zeta} = \int \xi\,\zeta\,dm = \frac{m}{\ell}\int_{-\ell/2}^{\ell/2} s^2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,ds = \frac{m\,\ell^2}{24}\,\sin 2\alpha ,
\qquad
I_{\eta\zeta} = 0 ,
$$
because every point of the rod has $\eta = 0$. The center of mass lies on the axis, hence $\vec{a}_G = \vec{0}$ and the first cardinal equation gives
$$
\vec{\Phi}_O + \vec{\Phi}_B - m\,g\,\hat{k} = \vec{0} .
$$
Second cardinal equation with pole at $G$. The angular velocity is constant, hence $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$ and
$$
\frac{d\vec{K}_G}{dt} = I_{\eta\zeta}\,\omega^2\,\hat{e}_\xi - I_{\xi\zeta}\,\omega^2\,\hat{e}_\eta = -\frac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{24}\,\sin 2\alpha\;\hat{e}_\eta .
$$
The weight has zero moment with respect to $G$, and the two bearings lie at distance $\dfrac{h}{2}$ from $G$; denoting by $\Phi_{O\xi}$ and $\Phi_{B\xi}$ the transverse components, the moment of the reactions with respect to $G$ has component along $\hat{e}_\eta$ equal to $\dfrac{h}{2}\,\big( \Phi_{B\xi} - \Phi_{O\xi} \big)$. The components along $\hat{e}_\eta$ of the two reactions are zero, since the moment to be balanced has no component along $\hat{e}_\xi$ and their sum is zero by the first cardinal equation. One therefore obtains the system
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{O\xi} + \Phi_{B\xi} = 0 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{h}{2}\,\big( \Phi_{B\xi} - \Phi_{O\xi} \big) = -\dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{24}\,\sin 2\alpha
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_{B\xi} = -\frac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2\,\sin 2\alpha}{24\,h} ,
\qquad
\Phi_{O\xi} = +\frac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2\,\sin 2\alpha}{24\,h} .
$$
The two transverse reactions are therefore opposite, lie in the plane of the rod and rotate with the shaft. With the given data:
$$
\frac{3 \cdot 0.80^2 \cdot 50^2 \cdot \sin 60^\circ}{24 \cdot 0.60} = 288.7\,\mathrm{N} .
$$

Check with the direct computation of the centrifugal forces. In the frame fixed to the shaft every element of mass $dm = \dfrac{m}{\ell}\,ds$ is subject to the centrifugal force $\omega^2\,\xi\,dm$ directed along $\hat{e}_\xi$. The resultant is
$$
\int_{-\ell/2}^{\ell/2} \frac{m}{\ell}\,\omega^2\,s\sin\alpha\,ds = 0 ,
$$
consistently with the fact that $G$ lies on the axis, while the moment with respect to $G$ is
$$
\int_{-\ell/2}^{\ell/2} \frac{m}{\ell}\,\omega^2\,s^2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,ds\;\hat{e}_\eta = \frac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{24}\,\sin 2\alpha\;\hat{e}_\eta .
$$
The reactions must balance this couple, hence their moment is $-\dfrac{m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2}{24}\sin 2\alpha\;\hat{e}_\eta$, which is the right-hand side found above.

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is statically balanced, because the center of mass lies on the axis of rotation and its acceleration is zero: the resultant of the reactions balances the weight alone, as in statics. It is not dynamically balanced, however, since the product of inertia $I_{\xi\zeta}$ is different from zero and the axis of the shaft is not a principal axis of inertia at $G$.

The product of inertia is computed by integration along the rod, using the coordinates of the generic point in the body-fixed frame. The result $\dfrac{m\,\ell^2}{24}\sin 2\alpha$ vanishes for $\alpha = 0$, when the rod is aligned with the shaft, and for $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, when the rod is perpendicular to the shaft and passes through $G$: in both cases the axis of rotation is principal of inertia and the dynamic reactions are zero.

The transverse reactions form a **couple**, because their sum is zero and their moment balances the derivative of the angular momentum. The magnitude grows with the square of the angular velocity: doubling the rotation speed the forces on the bearings quadruple, and it is for this reason that fast rotors are balanced with care.

The direct computation of the centrifugal forces in the body-fixed frame is a route independent of the cardinal equations and gives the same moment, because the two procedures describe the same effect: the distribution of mass away from the axis generates a couple that the bearings have to absorb.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A point mass $m$ is keyed at distance $e$ from the axis, at the height $\zeta = c$, on a vertical shaft that rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ and is supported by the bearings $A$ at the height $\zeta = 0$ and $B$ at the height $\zeta = h$, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the dynamic reactions of the two bearings and determine the products $m_1 e_1$ and $m_2 e_2$ of the two balancing masses to be placed, diametrically opposite to the first mass, in the planes $\zeta = 0$ and $\zeta = h$. Give the numerical values for $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $e = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$, $c = 20\,\mathrm{cm}$, $h = 60\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\omega = 100\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, with the balancing masses placed at $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;P,1.3,1.33,-" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera,90" cerchi="1.3,1.33,0.3" punti="0,1.33,C" reazioni="A,-1.1,0,Φ_A;B,-1.1,0,Φ_B" quote="A,0 1.33,c,-1.1;A,B,h,1.75;0 1.33,1.3 1.33,e,-0.75" etichette="2.1,1.85,m" titolo="Shaft with an unbalancing mass at the height $c$: the dynamic reactions of the bearings"}

Frame and quantities needed. One takes the body-fixed frame with origin at $A$, the $\zeta$ axis along the shaft and the $\xi$ axis through the mass, which therefore has coordinates $(e, 0, c)$. The acceleration of the center of mass of the system is centripetal,
$$
\vec{a}_G = -\omega^2\,e\,\hat{e}_\xi ,
$$
and the products of inertia with respect to $A$ are
$$
I_{\xi\zeta} = m\,e\,c ,
\qquad
I_{\eta\zeta} = 0 .
$$
Cardinal equations, neglecting the weight and the mass of the shaft. The first one projected onto $\hat{e}_\xi$ and the second with pole at $A$ projected onto $\hat{e}_\eta$ give
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{A\xi} + \Phi_{B\xi} = -m\,\omega^2\,e \\[6pt]
h\,\Phi_{B\xi} = -I_{\xi\zeta}\,\omega^2 = -m\,e\,c\,\omega^2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_{B\xi} = -\frac{m\,e\,c\,\omega^2}{h} ,
\qquad
\Phi_{A\xi} = -\frac{m\,e\,(h - c)\,\omega^2}{h} .
$$
Both are directed towards the axis and rotate with the shaft; their magnitudes with the given data are
$$
|\Phi_A| = \frac{2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.40 \cdot 100^2}{0.60} = 666.7\,\mathrm{N} ,
\qquad
|\Phi_B| = \frac{2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.20 \cdot 100^2}{0.60} = 333.3\,\mathrm{N} .
$$
Balancing. One adds two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ at the heights $\zeta = 0$ and $\zeta = h$, diametrically opposite to the first mass, that is with $\xi_1 = -e_1$ and $\xi_2 = -e_2$. The conditions of static and dynamic balancing are
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,e - m_1\,e_1 - m_2\,e_2 = 0 \\[6pt]
m\,e\,c - m_2\,e_2\,h = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
m_2\,e_2 = \frac{m\,e\,c}{h} ,
\qquad
m_1\,e_1 = \frac{m\,e\,(h-c)}{h} .
$$
With the given data:
$$
m_2\,e_2 = \frac{2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.20}{0.60} = 0.0333\,\mathrm{kg\,m} ,
\qquad
m_1\,e_1 = \frac{2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.40}{0.60} = 0.0667\,\mathrm{kg\,m} ,
$$
and at $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the axis one obtains $m_2 = 0.33\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $m_1 = 0.67\,\mathrm{kg}$.

Check of the balancing conditions on the complete system. After the addition of the two masses, the static moment with respect to the axis is
$$
m\,e - m_1\,e_1 - m_2\,e_2 = 0.100 - 0.0667 - 0.0333 = 0 ,
$$
hence the center of mass of the system lies on the axis. The overall product of inertia is
$$
m\,e\,c - m_1\,e_1 \cdot 0 - m_2\,e_2\,h = 2 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.20 - 0.0333 \cdot 0.60 = 0.020 - 0.020 = 0 ,
$$
hence the axis is principal of inertia. The two conditions together make both right-hand sides of the system of the reactions vanish, therefore $\Phi_{A\xi} = \Phi_{B\xi} = 0$ and the bearings carry the weight alone.

<div class="spiegazione">

The unbalancing mass produces two distinct effects. The first is a static imbalance, because the center of mass does not lie on the axis and its centripetal acceleration requires a resultant force $m\,e\,\omega^2$ directed towards the axis. The second is a dynamic imbalance, because the product of inertia $I_{\xi\zeta} = m\,e\,c$ is different from zero and the derivative of the angular momentum has a transverse component.

The second cardinal equation is written with pole at $A$, which is a fixed point: the reaction at $A$ has zero moment with respect to itself, hence only $\Phi_{B\xi}$ appears in the equation, multiplied by the lever arm $h$. Once solved for $\Phi_{B\xi}$, the value is substituted into the first equation and one obtains $\Phi_{A\xi}$.

The negative sign of both reactions indicates that the bearings **push the shaft towards the axis of rotation**: it is they that provide the centripetal force keeping the mass on its circle. The reaction at $A$ is the larger one because the mass is closer to $A$ than to $B$, according to the same distribution as for two supports carrying a load.

Balancing requires two masses and not one alone, because the conditions to be satisfied are two: making the static moment vanish and making the product of inertia vanish. A single mass, placed at the same level as the unbalancing mass, would be enough, but in a real machine the correction planes are fixed by the construction and in general do not coincide with the plane of the imbalance. The two conditions distribute the correction between the two planes in inverse proportion to the distance from the plane of the imbalance, as one sees from the values $\dfrac{h-c}{h}$ and $\dfrac{c}{h}$.

</div>
