## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $OA$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $O$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane, and can rotate freely about it; the hinge is smooth. A couple of moment $\mathcal{M}$ is applied to the rod, counterclockwise, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations, say for which values of $\mathcal{M}$ they exist and compute the reaction of the hinge.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.5,-2.6" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 0,-3.4" coppie="1.1 -1.9,1,ℳ" forze="0.75 -1.3,0,-1,mg" etichette="0.62,-2.5,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with a couple $\mathcal{M}$ applied"}

As Lagrangian coordinate one takes the angle $\vartheta$ that $OA$ makes with the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise. The center of mass $G$ is the midpoint of the rod, hence
$$
G - O = \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The rod is a rigid body with one degree of freedom and with two unknown reaction components at $O$; one therefore writes first the moment equation with respect to $O$, in which the reaction of the hinge does not appear, since its line of action passes through the pole:
$$
M_O^{(e)} = -\,\frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta + \mathcal{M} = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\sin\vartheta = \frac{2\,\mathcal{M}}{m g\,\ell} .
$$
The equation admits solutions if and only if
$$
\mathcal{M} \le \frac{m g\,\ell}{2} ,
$$
and in that case the equilibrium configurations are two,
$$
\vartheta_1 = \arcsin\frac{2\,\mathcal{M}}{m g\,\ell} \in \left[0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right] ,
\qquad
\vartheta_2 = \pi - \vartheta_1 .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives the reaction of the hinge, given that a couple has zero resultant:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ox} = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Oy} - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\vec{\Phi}_O = m g\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $A$, written with the reaction found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_A^{(e)} &= (x_G - x_A)\,(-\,m g) + (x_O - x_A)\,\Phi_{Oy} + \mathcal{M} \\[6pt]
&= \left(-\,\frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\right)(-\,m g) + (-\,\ell\sin\vartheta)\,m g + \mathcal{M} \\[6pt]
&= -\,\frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta + \mathcal{M} ,
\end{aligned}
$$
an expression that vanishes in the configurations found.

With $\mathcal{M} = \dfrac{m g\,\ell}{4}$ one finds $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, hence $\vartheta_1 = 30^\circ$ and $\vartheta_2 = 150^\circ$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is a rigid body, therefore the cardinal equations are necessary and sufficient for the equilibrium and the three scalar equations of the plane suffice to solve the problem. The unknowns are three: the angle $\vartheta$ and the two components of the reaction at $O$.

The pole is taken at $O$ because the reaction of the hinge has its line of action passing through $O$, hence zero moment with respect to that pole. The moment equation then contains the single unknown $\vartheta$ and is the configuration equation of the system.

The weight has moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$ with respect to $O$ and tends to rotate the rod clockwise, therefore it enters with the minus sign; the couple contributes with its own moment $\mathcal{M}$ with respect to any pole. Equilibrium requires that the two moments balance each other.

The condition $\mathcal{M} \le \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$ says that a couple stronger than the maximum moment that the weight can oppose makes the rod rotate without any equilibrium configuration existing; the maximum value occurs for $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, that is, with the rod horizontal, where the moment arm of the weight is greatest.

The reaction of the hinge equals $m g$ and is directed **upward**, in every configuration, since the couple does not alter the resultant and the hinge supports the weight of the rod alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $A$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane, with a smooth hinge. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects the end $B$ to the fixed point $D$, placed on the horizontal through $A$ at distance $2\ell$ from $A$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configuration in which the rod lies below the horizontal and the reaction of the hinge.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.72,-1.27;D,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 6.4,0" linee="2.72,-1.27 3.31,-1.04 3.74,-1.11 3.93,-0.57 4.44,-0.84 4.63,-0.29 5.14,-0.57 5.41,-0.23 6.00,0.00" forze="1.36 -0.63,0,-1,mg" quote="A,D,2ℓ,1.9" etichette="0.75,-0.42,ϑ;4.6,-0.22,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$ with the spring between the end $B$ and the fixed point $D$"}

One places the origin at $A$ and denotes by $\vartheta$ the angle that $AB$ makes with the horizontal, measured below it, with $\vartheta \in \left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. Then
$$
B - A = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - A = \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
D - A = 2\ell\,\hat{\imath} .
$$
The spring has zero natural length, therefore it exerts on $B$ the force
$$
\vec{F}_B = -\,k\,(B - D) = k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta)\,\hat{\imath} + k\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The moment equation with respect to $A$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge. The moment of the elastic force equals
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_B\,F_{By} - y_B\,F_{Bx} &= \ell\cos\vartheta \cdot k\ell\sin\vartheta - (-\,\ell\sin\vartheta) \cdot k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta) \\[6pt]
&= k\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta + 2k\ell^2\sin\vartheta - k\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta \\[6pt]
&= 2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta ,
\end{aligned}
$$
while the moment of the weight equals $x_G\,(-\,m g) = -\,\dfrac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$. The equilibrium equation is therefore
$$
2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta - \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\tan\vartheta = \frac{m g}{4\,k\,\ell} .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives the reaction of the hinge:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} + k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta) = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} + k\ell\sin\vartheta - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_{Ax} = -\,k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta) ,
\quad
\Phi_{Ay} = m g - k\ell\sin\vartheta .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $B$, written with the reaction found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_B^{(e)} &= (x_G - x_B)(-\,m g) + (x_A - x_B)\,\Phi_{Ay} - (y_A - y_B)\,\Phi_{Ax} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \ell\cos\vartheta\,(m g - k\ell\sin\vartheta) + \ell\sin\vartheta\,k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta) \\[6pt]
&= -\,\frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + 2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta ,
\end{aligned}
$$
which vanishes in the configuration found.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod has one degree of freedom and the hinge exerts two reaction components, hence the unknowns are three, as many as the scalar cardinal equations of the plane. The angle $\vartheta$ is measured below the horizontal because the equilibrium is sought with the rod lowered, given that the weight pulls $B$ downward and the spring pulls it toward $D$.

In the moment of the elastic force the terms in $\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta$ cancel, and there remains $2k\ell^2\sin\vartheta$. Only the horizontal component $2k\ell$ of the elastic force, the one directed toward $D$, produces a moment with respect to $A$, with moment arm $\ell\sin\vartheta$. This simplification occurs every time the spring has zero natural length and one fixed end.

The equilibrium equation says that the rod descends the more, the greater the weight is compared with the elastic force. For $k \to \infty$ one has $\vartheta \to 0$ and the rod tends to the horizontal, because the spring becomes inextensible and brings $B$ onto the line $AD$; for $k \to 0$ one has $\vartheta \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the rod hangs vertically.

The component $\Phi_{Ax}$ is negative, that is, the hinge pulls the rod **to the left**, in opposition to the spring, which pulls it toward $D$. The vertical component is positive when $k\ell\sin\vartheta < m g$, that is, when the spring is not stretched enough to support the weight of the rod by itself.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $A$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane, and makes the angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal. The rod is held in this position by an ideal vertical string that connects the point $C$ of the rod, with $AC = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\ell$, to the ceiling; at the end $B$ a particle of mass $m_1$ is hung with a second vertical string. The system is the one in the figure. Determine the tension of the string at $C$ and the reaction of the hinge, and say for which values of $m_1$ the hinge pulls the rod downward.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4.33,2.5;C,2.89,1.67;S,2.89,4.3,-;Q,4.33,0.7,-" aste="A-B" catene="C-S;B-Q" vincoli="A,cerniera" linee="1.9,4.3 3.9,4.3" punti="4.33,0.7" forze="2.17 1.25,0,-1,mg;Q,0,-1,m_1g" reazioni="C,0,1,T" etichette="0.95,0.3,α" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$, with $AC = \dfrac{2}{3}\,\ell$: the vertical string at $C$ supports it and at $B$ the weight $m_1 g$ is hung"}

One places the origin at $A$. The abscissas of the relevant points are
$$
x_C = \frac{2}{3}\,\ell\cos\alpha ,
\qquad
x_G = \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\alpha ,
\qquad
x_B = \ell\cos\alpha .
$$
The string at $C$ pulls the rod upward with the force $T\,\hat{\jmath}$; the string hung at $B$ pulls the rod downward with the force $-\,m_1 g\,\hat{\jmath}$, because its tension balances the hanging weight. The moment equation with respect to $A$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge:
$$
\frac{2}{3}\,\ell\cos\alpha\;T - \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\alpha\;m g - \ell\cos\alpha\;m_1 g = 0 .
$$
The factor $\ell\cos\alpha$ is collected and cancels, since it differs from zero for $\alpha \neq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, therefore
$$
\frac{2}{3}\,T = \frac{m g}{2} + m_1 g ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
T = \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{4} .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives the reaction of the hinge:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} + T - m g - m_1 g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = (m + m_1)\,g - \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{4} = \frac{g\,(m - 2\,m_1)}{4} .
$$
The reaction is directed downward when $\Phi_{Ay} < 0$, that is, when
$$
m_1 > \frac{m}{2} .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $B$, written with the values found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_B^{(e)} &= (x_A - x_B)\,\Phi_{Ay} + (x_C - x_B)\,T + (x_G - x_B)\,(-\,m g) \\[6pt]
&= \ell\cos\alpha\left[ -\,\frac{g\,(m - 2m_1)}{4} - \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{3\,g\,(m + 2m_1)}{4} + \frac{m g}{2} \right] \\[6pt]
&= \ell\cos\alpha\left[ -\,\frac{m g}{4} + \frac{m_1 g}{2} - \frac{m g}{4} - \frac{m_1 g}{2} + \frac{m g}{2} \right] = 0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is a rigid body in an assigned configuration, therefore the residual degrees of freedom are zero and the three scalar cardinal equations determine the three unknowns: the tension $T$ and the two components of the reaction at $A$.

The string hung at $B$ can be replaced with the force that it exerts on the rod. The hanging particle is in equilibrium under its own weight and the tension of the second string, hence that tension equals $m_1 g$, and by the principle of action and reaction the string pulls the rod downward with the same magnitude.

All the active forces and the tension are vertical, and the moment arms with respect to $A$ are proportional to $\cos\alpha$; therefore the angle cancels and **the result does not depend on the inclination of the rod**. The only case excluded is $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, in which the rod is vertical, all the lines of action pass through $A$ and the moment equation is identically satisfied.

The moment arm of the string at $C$, equal to $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\ell\cos\alpha$, is smaller than the moment arm of the load hung at $B$, therefore the contribution of the load to the tension equals $\dfrac{3}{2}\,m_1 g$, that is, one and a half times its weight. Consequently the hinge must hold the rod back when the load exceeds $\dfrac{m}{2}$. For $m_1 > \dfrac{m}{2}$ one indeed finds $\Phi_{Ay} < 0$, and the hinge **pulls the rod downward**. For $m_1 = \dfrac{m}{2}$ the reaction vanishes and the rod would remain in equilibrium even with the hinge removed.

</div>
