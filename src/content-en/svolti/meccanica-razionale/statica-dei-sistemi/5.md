## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ rests in a vertical plane with the end $A$ on a rough horizontal floor, with coefficient of static friction $f$, and with the end $B$ on a smooth vertical wall; the rod makes the angle $\vartheta$ with the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the reactions of the two supports and the minimum angle for which the equilibrium is possible, then compute it for $f = 0.4$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.87,4.1" aste="A-B" linee="-0.9,0 3.9,0;2.87,0 2.87,4.8" forze="1.43 2.05,0,-1.2,mg" reazioni="A,0,1.2,N_A;A,1.2,0,T_A;B,-1.2,0,N_B" etichette="0.85,0.33,ϑ" titolo="Rod resting on the rough floor at $A$ and on the smooth wall at $B$: free-body diagram"}

One places the origin at $A$, with the $x$ axis horizontal toward the right, so that
$$
B - A = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - A = \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The wall is smooth, therefore at $B$ the reaction is horizontal and directed toward the left, $\vec{\Phi}_B = -\,N_B\,\hat{\imath}$ with $N_B \ge 0$. The floor is rough, therefore at $A$ the reaction has a normal component $N_A\,\hat{\jmath}$ with $N_A \ge 0$ and a tangential component $T_A\,\hat{\imath}$. The cardinal equations, with the pole at $A$, give
$$
\begin{cases}
T_A - N_B = 0 \\[4pt]
N_A - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
-\,\dfrac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + N_B\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation $N_A = m g$; from the third
$$
N_B = \frac{m g\cos\vartheta}{2\sin\vartheta} = \frac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta} ,
$$
and from the first $T_A = N_B$. The Coulomb-Morin law requires $|T_A| \le f\,N_A$, that is,
$$
\frac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta} \le f\,m g ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\tan\vartheta \ge \frac{1}{2 f} .
$$
The minimum equilibrium angle is consequently
$$
\vartheta_{\min} = \arctan\frac{1}{2 f} .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $B$, written with the reactions found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_B^{(e)} &= (x_A - x_B)\,N_A - (y_A - y_B)\,T_A + (x_G - x_B)(-\,m g) \\[6pt]
&= -\,\ell\cos\vartheta \cdot m g + \ell\sin\vartheta \cdot \frac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta} + \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta \\[6pt]
&= -\,m g\,\ell\cos\vartheta + \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta = 0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

With $f = 0.4$ one finds $\tan\vartheta \ge 1.25$, hence $\vartheta_{\min} = 51.34^\circ$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is a rigid body in an assigned configuration, with three unknown reaction components, $N_A$, $T_A$ and $N_B$, and three scalar cardinal equations. Friction leaves the number of unknowns unchanged and adds the inequality that the tangential component must satisfy.

The pole is taken at $A$ because two of the three unknown components are applied there, and consequently have zero moment; in the moment equation there remains $N_B$ alone, with moment arm $\ell\sin\vartheta$, against the weight, with moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$.

The normal reaction of the floor equals the whole weight, because the wall is smooth and has no vertical component. The push of the wall and the friction at the ground are equal, as the equilibrium of horizontal translation requires, since the wall pushes the rod toward the left and the friction holds it back toward the right.

The condition $\tan\vartheta \ge \dfrac{1}{2f}$ says that the rod is in equilibrium only if it is sufficiently **steep**: as $\vartheta$ decreases the push of the wall grows and the available friction, bounded by $f\,m g$, at a certain point is no longer enough. For $f \to 0$ the minimum angle tends to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, that is, without friction only the vertical rod stays at rest, in agreement with the fact that all the reactions would be vertical or horizontal and could not balance the moment of the weight.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rests in a vertical plane on a plane inclined at $\alpha$ to the horizontal, rough with coefficient of static friction $f$. At the point $D$ of the rim diametrically opposite to the contact point $C$ a force $\vec{F}$ is applied, parallel to the inclined plane and directed upward, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the magnitude of $\vec{F}$ that keeps the disk in equilibrium, the reaction of the contact and the no-slipping condition, then discuss it for $\alpha = 20^\circ$ and $f = 0.3$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 7,2.55 7,0 0,0" cerchi="3.09,2.4,1.2" punti="3.09,2.4,O,baricentro;3.5,1.27,C,centro;2.68,3.53,D" forze="2.68 3.53,1.05,0.38,F;3.09 2.4,0,-1.2,mg" reazioni="3.5 1.27,-0.41,1.13,N;3.5 1.27,1.13,0.41,T" etichette="1,0.2,α" titolo="Disk resting on a rough inclined plane, with the force $\vec{F}$ parallel to the plane applied at $D$"}

One introduces the unit vector $\hat{u}$ parallel to the plane, directed upward, and the unit vector $\hat{n}$ normal to the plane, outward from the plane itself:
$$
\hat{u} = \cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{n} = -\sin\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\alpha\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
With respect to this basis the weight is written as $-\,m g\,\hat{\jmath} = -\,m g\sin\alpha\,\hat{u} - m g\cos\alpha\,\hat{n}$. The contact exerts at $C$ the reaction $N\,\hat{n} + T\,\hat{u}$, and one has $C - O = -\,R\,\hat{n}$ and $D - O = R\,\hat{n}$. The cardinal equations, projected onto $\hat{u}$ and onto $\hat{n}$ and with the pole at the center $O$, give
$$
\begin{cases}
F + T - m g\sin\alpha = 0 \\[4pt]
N - m g\cos\alpha = 0 \\[4pt]
R\,T - R\,F = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The third equation gives $T = F$; substituting into the first one obtains
$$
2\,F = m g\sin\alpha ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
F = \frac{m g\sin\alpha}{2} ,
\qquad
T = \frac{m g\sin\alpha}{2} ,
\qquad
N = m g\cos\alpha .
$$
The Coulomb-Morin law requires $|T| \le f\,N$, that is,
$$
\frac{m g\sin\alpha}{2} \le f\,m g\cos\alpha ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\tan\alpha \le 2 f .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to the contact point $C$, in which the reaction of the contact does not appear. The weight is applied at $O = C + R\,\hat{n}$ and the force at $D = C + 2R\,\hat{n}$, therefore
$$
M_C^{(e)} = R\,\hat{n} \wedge (-\,m g\,\hat{\jmath}) + 2R\,\hat{n} \wedge (F\,\hat{u}) = \big( m g R\sin\alpha - 2 R F \big)\,\hat{k} ,
$$
where $\hat{n} \wedge \hat{\jmath} = -\sin\alpha\,\hat{k}$ and $\hat{n} \wedge \hat{u} = -\,\hat{k}$ have been used. The vanishing of this expression gives again $F = \dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{2}$.

With $\alpha = 20^\circ$ and $f = 0.3$ one has $\tan 20^\circ = 0.36$ and $2f = 0.6$, hence the condition is satisfied and the disk remains in equilibrium with $F = 0.17\,m g$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk is a rigid body in an assigned configuration, with three unknowns: the two components of the reaction of the contact and the magnitude of the applied force. It is convenient to project the equations onto the directions $\hat{u}$ and $\hat{n}$ of the inclined plane, because along them the weight is resolved once and for all and the reactions have separate components.

The moment equation with respect to $O$ is the one that relates $T$ and $F$. The normal reaction has its line of action along $OC$, which passes through $O$, hence zero moment; there remain the friction, with moment arm $R$, and the applied force, with moment arm $R$, which tend to rotate the disk in opposite directions. From this $T = F$.

The result $F = \dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{2}$ says that the force applied at the upper rim bears **half** of the component of the weight along the plane, and the other half is borne by the friction. The reason is read off from the check. With respect to the contact point the force $\vec{F}$ has moment arm $2R$, twice the moment arm $R$ of the weight, therefore half of its magnitude suffices.

The condition $\tan\alpha \le 2 f$ is less restrictive than the condition $\tan\alpha \le f$ of a block resting on the same plane, which cannot rotate, precisely because the force at $D$ cooperates with the friction. With the force applied at the center, on the other hand, the moment equation with respect to $O$ would give $T = 0$ and the equilibrium would require $F = m g\sin\alpha$, that is, the whole component of the weight along the plane, without the friction intervening.

</div>
