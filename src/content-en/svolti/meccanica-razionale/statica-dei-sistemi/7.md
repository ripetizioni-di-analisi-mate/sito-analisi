## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $3b$ is placed horizontally in a vertical plane, hinged at $A$ to a fixed point. At the point $C$ with $AC = b$ a vertical ideal spring of constant $k$ and natural length $\ell_0$ is attached, fixed to the ceiling; at the end $B$ a particle of mass $m_1$ is hung with a vertical string. The system is the one in the figure. Determine the length of the spring at equilibrium and the reaction of the hinge.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,2,0;Q,6,-1.4,-" aste="A-B" catene="B-Q" vincoli="A,cerniera" linee="1.1,2.6 2.9,2.6;2.00,0.00 2.00,0.47 2.22,0.80 1.78,1.13 2.22,1.47 1.78,1.80 2.00,2.13 2.00,2.60" punti="6,-1.4" forze="3 0,0,-1,mg;Q,0,-1,m_1g" reazioni="C,0,1,T" quote="A,C,b,1.1;C,B,2b,1.1" etichette="2.45,1.45,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$, held up by the vertical spring at $C$ and loaded at $B$ by the hanging weight"}

One places the origin at $A$, with the $x$ axis along the rod. The abscissas of the points are
$$
x_C = b ,
\qquad
x_G = \frac{3b}{2} ,
\qquad
x_B = 3b .
$$
The spring is vertical and stretched between the ceiling and the point $C$, hence it exerts on $C$ the force $T\,\hat{\jmath}$, with
$$
T = k\,(\lambda - \ell_0) ,
$$
where $\lambda$ is the length of the spring at equilibrium. The string hung at $B$ pulls the rod downward with the force $-\,m_1 g\,\hat{\jmath}$. The moment equation with respect to $A$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge:
$$
b\,T - \frac{3b}{2}\,m g - 3b\,m_1 g = 0 ,
$$
and dividing by $b$ one obtains
$$
T = \frac{3\,m g}{2} + 3\,m_1 g = \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{2} .
$$
The elongation and the length of the spring are therefore
$$
\lambda - \ell_0 = \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{2\,k} ,
\qquad
\lambda = \ell_0 + \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{2\,k} .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives the reaction of the hinge:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} + T - m g - m_1 g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = (m + m_1)\,g - \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{2} = -\,\frac{g\,(m + 4\,m_1)}{2} .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $C$, written with the values found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_C^{(e)} &= (x_A - x_C)\,\Phi_{Ay} + (x_G - x_C)(-\,m g) + (x_B - x_C)(-\,m_1 g) \\[6pt]
&= (-\,b)\left(-\,\frac{g\,(m + 4 m_1)}{2}\right) + \frac{b}{2}\,(-\,m g) + 2b\,(-\,m_1 g) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{m g b}{2} + 2\,m_1 g b - \frac{m g b}{2} - 2\,m_1 g b = 0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is a rigid body in an assigned configuration; the unknowns are the tension of the spring and the two components of the reaction at $A$, and the scalar cardinal equations of the plane are three.

The particle hung at $B$ is in equilibrium under its own weight and the tension of the string, hence that tension equals $m_1 g$; by the principle of action and reaction the string pulls the rod downward with the same magnitude.

The moment equation with respect to $A$ is solved for $T$ at once, because the reaction of the hinge has zero moment. The moment arm of the spring is $b$, that of the own weight is $\dfrac{3b}{2}$ and that of the hanging load is $3b$. The spring, attached near the hinge, must therefore exert a force **much greater** than the total weight.

From the tension one goes back to the elongation with the law of the ideal spring, since the magnitude of the elastic force is $k$ times the change in length. The length at equilibrium exceeds the natural one, hence the spring works in tension, as it must, given that it pulls the rod upward.

The reaction of the hinge is negative, that is, the hinge **pulls the rod downward**, and its magnitude grows with the hanging load. The rod behaves as a lever with its fulcrum at $A$, in which the spring lifts a point close to the fulcrum and the opposite end tends to rise, therefore the hinge must hold it back.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$ is hinged at its midpoint $O$ to a fixed point, in a vertical plane, with a smooth hinge. At the end $A$ a particle of mass $m_1$ is hung with a vertical string; at the end $B$ an ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length is attached, whose second end is the fixed point $D$ placed on the horizontal through $O$ at distance $2\ell$ from $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations and the reaction of the hinge.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,-2.17,-1.25;B,2.17,1.25;D,5,0;Q,-2.17,-2.6,-" aste="A-B" catene="A-Q" vincoli="O,cerniera" tratteggi="0,0 5,0" linee="2.17,1.25 2.68,1.02 2.96,0.68 3.48,0.89 3.69,0.36 4.21,0.57 4.49,0.23 5.00,0.00" punti="-2.17,-2.6" forze="O,0,-1,mg;Q,0,-1,m_1g" etichette="0.95,0.25,ϑ;3.4,1.15,k" titolo="Rod hinged at its midpoint $O$, with the weight hung at $A$ and the spring between $B$ and the fixed point $D$"}

One places the origin at $O$ and denotes by $\vartheta$ the angle that the half-axis $OB$ makes with the positive $x$ half-axis, measured counterclockwise. Then
$$
B - O = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
A - O = -\,\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
D - O = 2\ell\,\hat{\imath} .
$$
The center of mass of the rod coincides with $O$, hence the own weight has zero moment with respect to $O$. The string hung at $A$ pulls the rod downward with the force $-\,m_1 g\,\hat{\jmath}$, and the spring exerts on $B$ the force
$$
\vec{F}_B = -\,k\,(B - D) = k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta)\,\hat{\imath} - k\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The moment equation with respect to $O$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge. The moment of the hanging load equals
$$
x_A\,(-\,m_1 g) = m_1 g\,\ell\cos\vartheta ,
$$
and the moment of the elastic force equals
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_B\,F_{By} - y_B\,F_{Bx} &= \ell\cos\vartheta\,(-\,k\ell\sin\vartheta) - \ell\sin\vartheta\,k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta) \\[6pt]
&= -\,k\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta - 2k\ell^2\sin\vartheta + k\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta \\[6pt]
&= -\,2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta .
\end{aligned}
$$
The equilibrium equation is therefore
$$
m_1 g\,\ell\cos\vartheta - 2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\tan\vartheta = \frac{m_1 g}{2\,k\,\ell} .
$$
The equilibrium configurations are two, $\vartheta_1 = \arctan\dfrac{m_1 g}{2 k \ell} \in \left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ and $\vartheta_2 = \vartheta_1 + \pi$. The first cardinal equation gives the reaction of the hinge:
$$
\Phi_{Ox} = -\,k\ell\,(2 - \cos\vartheta) ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Oy} = (m + m_1)\,g + k\ell\sin\vartheta .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $A$, written with the reaction found. In it the hanging load has zero moment, while the own weight and the reaction appear, both applied at $O$, and the elastic force applied at $B$:
$$
M_A^{(e)} = m_1 g\,\ell\cos\vartheta - 2\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta ,
$$
an expression that vanishes in the configurations found. Two limiting cases are checked as well. For $m_1 \to 0$ one finds $\vartheta_1 \to 0$, that is, the rod sets itself horizontal with $B$ on the side of $D$; for $k \to 0$ one finds $\vartheta_1 \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, that is, the rod sets itself vertical with the hanging load at the bottom.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is hinged at its midpoint, therefore it has one degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ locates it. The unknowns are three, the angle and the two components of the reaction, as many as the scalar cardinal equations of the plane.

The own weight is applied at $O$, which is the center of mass of a homogeneous rod, and the hinge is at the same point, hence the own weight has **zero moment** with respect to $O$ and does not enter the configuration equation. It does enter the first cardinal equation, because it contributes to the resultant.

In the moment of the elastic force the terms in $\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta$ cancel, and there remains the contribution of the horizontal component $2k\ell$ of the force alone, with moment arm $\ell\sin\vartheta$. The equilibrium balances this moment, which tends to bring $B$ back onto the horizontal, with that of the load hung at $A$, which tends to rotate the rod counterclockwise.

The equilibrium configuration depends on the ratio between the hanging weight and the stiffness of the spring. With a very stiff spring the rod stays almost horizontal; with a compliant spring the rod rotates almost to the vertical. The second solution $\vartheta_2 = \vartheta_1 + \pi$ corresponds to the rod turned over, with $A$ and $B$ exchanged.

The horizontal component of the reaction is negative in both configurations, since $\cos\vartheta \le 1 < 2$, and the hinge always pulls the rod **to the left**, in opposition to the spring, which pulls $B$ toward $D$.

</div>
