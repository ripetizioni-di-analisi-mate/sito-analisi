## Exercise 1

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, in a vertical plane. A particle of mass $m_1$ is welded to the rim of the disk. At the center $O$ of the disk a horizontal force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$ is applied, with $F > 0$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations, the reaction of the contact and the conditions for the equilibrium to be possible; the coefficient of static friction at the contact equals $f$.

::struttura{linee="-0.6,0 8.2,0" cerchi="4,1.5,1.5" tratteggi="4,1.5 3.14,0.27;4,1.5 4,0" punti="4,1.5,O,baricentro;4,0,C,centro;3.14,0.27,P" forze="4 1.5,1.2,0,F;4 1.5,0,-1.9,mg;3.14 0.27,0,-0.8,m_1g" reazioni="4 0,0,1.3,N;4 0,-1.3,0,T" etichette="3.85,0.72,φ" titolo="Disk rolling without slipping with the point $P$ of mass $m_1$ welded to the rim"}

The rigid disk in the plane has three degrees of freedom and rolling without slipping on a fixed guide removes two of them, the contact and the absence of slipping, hence the system has one degree of freedom. As Lagrangian coordinate one chooses the angle $\varphi$, measured clockwise, by which the disk has rotated starting from the configuration in which $P$ touches the guide, and rolling without slipping gives $x_O = R\,\varphi$. It follows that
$$
O = (R\varphi,\; R) ,
\qquad
C = (R\varphi,\; 0) ,
\qquad
P = \big( R\varphi - R\sin\varphi,\; R - R\cos\varphi \big) ,
$$
where $C$ is the contact point. The disk with the welded particle is a single rigid body, and the unknowns are $\varphi$ and the two components $N$ and $T$ of the reaction of the contact.

The moment equation with respect to $C$ does not contain the reaction of the contact, which is applied at $C$:
$$
(y_O - y_C)\,(-\,F) + (x_P - x_C)\,(-\,m_1 g) = -\,R\,F + m_1 g\,R\sin\varphi = 0 ,
$$
where the weight of the disk, applied at $O$, has zero moment arm because $O$ lies on the vertical through $C$. Dividing by $R$ one obtains
$$
\sin\varphi = \frac{F}{m_1 g} .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives the reaction of the contact:
$$
\begin{cases}
F + T = 0 \\[4pt]
N - (m + m_1)\,g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
T = -\,F ,
\quad
N = (m + m_1)\,g .
$$
The conditions for the equilibrium to be possible are two:
$$
\begin{cases}
F \le m_1 g \\[4pt]
F \le f\,(m + m_1)\,g
\end{cases}
$$
and when the first is satisfied with strict inequality the equilibrium configurations are two,
$$
\varphi_1 = \arcsin\frac{F}{m_1 g} ,
\qquad
\varphi_2 = \pi - \varphi_1 .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to the center $O$, written with the reaction found. The force $\vec{F}$ and the weight of the disk are applied at $O$ and have zero moment, the normal reaction has zero moment arm, hence there remain the friction and the weight of the welded particle:
$$
M_O^{(e)} = R\,T + m_1 g\,R\sin\varphi = -\,R\,F + m_1 g\,R\sin\varphi ,
$$
an expression that vanishes in the configurations found.

<div class="spiegazione">

The constraint of rolling without slipping is a link between the rotation and the translation, therefore the disk has a single degree of freedom. As coordinate one takes the angle of rotation, positive clockwise because the disk advances to the right rotating in that direction, and the position of the welded particle turns out to be that of a point of the cycloid described by the rim.

The pole is taken at the contact point because the reaction, which has two unknown components, is applied there, and the moment equation with respect to $C$ then contains the single unknown $\varphi$ and is the configuration equation. The weight of the disk does not appear in it, since the center lies on the vertical through $C$ and its moment arm is zero.

Equilibrium is realized where the moment of the weight of the welded particle, with moment arm $R\sin\varphi$, balances the moment of the applied force, with moment arm $R$. The moment arm of the weight is greatest for $\varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and equals $R$, and from this follows the condition $F \le m_1 g$, which says that a force stronger than the weight of the welded particle makes the disk roll without any equilibrium configuration existing.

The second condition concerns the friction. The friction must balance the applied force entirely, given that the normal reaction is vertical, hence it equals $F$ in magnitude; by the Coulomb-Morin law one must have $F \le f\,N$, with $N$ equal to the total weight. If the coefficient of friction is too small the disk **slips** instead of staying at rest.

The two equilibrium configurations correspond to the welded particle on the left side of the disk, at the bottom or at the top. In both the horizontal component of the reaction is $-\,F$, that is, the friction pushes the disk to the left, in opposition to the applied force.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is placed in a vertical plane with the end $A$ constrained by a sleeve that slides on a smooth horizontal guide, the $x$ axis, and the end $B$ constrained by a sleeve that slides on a smooth vertical guide, the $y$ axis. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $A$ to the origin $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine all the equilibrium configurations with $\vartheta \in \left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$, where $\vartheta$ is the angle that the rod makes with the horizontal, and the reactions of the two sleeves.

::struttura{nodi="A,3.06,0;B,0,2.57" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello;B,carrello,180" linee="-0.8,0 4.4,0;0,-0.8 0,3.6;0.00,0.00 0.55,0.00 0.94,-0.26 1.33,0.26 1.73,-0.26 2.12,0.26 2.51,0.00 3.06,0.00" forze="1.53 1.29,0,-1.1,mg" etichette="2.45,0.42,ϑ;1.5,0.55,k" titolo="Rod with its ends on two smooth orthogonal guides and spring between $A$ and the origin"}

One has
$$
A - O = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
B - O = \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - O = \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The sleeve at $A$ slides on a horizontal guide, therefore its reaction is vertical, $\vec{\Phi}_A = \Phi_A\,\hat{\jmath}$; the one at $B$ slides on a vertical guide, therefore its reaction is horizontal, $\vec{\Phi}_B = \Phi_B\,\hat{\imath}$. The spring, of zero natural length, exerts on $A$ the force
$$
\vec{F}_A = -\,k\,(A - O) = -\,k\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives the two reactions at once:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_B - k\ell\cos\vartheta = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_A - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_B = k\ell\cos\vartheta ,
\quad
\Phi_A = m g .
$$
The moment equation with respect to $O$ contains four contributions: the elastic force, applied at $A$ on the $x$ axis, has zero moment arm; there remain the weight, $\vec{\Phi}_A$ and $\vec{\Phi}_B$:
$$
-\,\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\;m g + \ell\cos\vartheta\;\Phi_A - \ell\sin\vartheta\;\Phi_B = 0 .
$$
Substituting the reactions found one obtains
$$
-\,\frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + m g\,\ell\cos\vartheta - k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta = 0 ,
$$
that is,
$$
\ell\cos\vartheta\left( \frac{m g}{2} - k\,\ell\sin\vartheta \right) = 0 .
$$
The equilibrium configurations are therefore
$$
\vartheta = \frac{\pi}{2} ,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\sin\vartheta = \frac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell} ,
$$
and the second exists only if $m g \le 2\,k\,\ell$; for $m g = 2\,k\,\ell$ the two solutions coincide.

Check with the moment equation with respect to $A$, written with the reactions found. With respect to $A$ the elastic force and $\vec{\Phi}_A$ have zero moment, both being applied at $A$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_A^{(e)} &= (x_G - x_A)(-\,m g) - (y_B - y_A)\,\Phi_B \\[6pt]
&= \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \ell\sin\vartheta\,k\ell\cos\vartheta = \ell\cos\vartheta\left( \frac{m g}{2} - k\,\ell\sin\vartheta \right) ,
\end{aligned}
$$
which vanishes in the same configurations. With $m g = k\,\ell$ the second solution is $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, $\vartheta = 30^\circ$, and in it $\Phi_A = m g$ and $\Phi_B = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,k\,\ell$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each sleeve removes one degree of freedom, therefore one degree of freedom remains for the rod, located by the angle $\vartheta$. The unknowns are three, the angle and the two reactions, as many as the scalar cardinal equations of the plane.

One starts from the first cardinal equation because each component contains a single reaction, given that the horizontal guide exerts only vertical forces and the vertical guide only horizontal forces. The spring, being directed along the $x$ axis, appears only in the horizontal component.

The moment equation is taken with respect to $O$, where the two guides meet, because there the elastic force has zero moment arm, given that its line of action is the $x$ axis, which passes through $O$. There remain the weight and the two reactions, and the equation factorizes.

The factorization shows two families of equilibrium. The first, $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, is the vertical rod with $A$ at the origin. In it the spring is at its natural length, the weight has zero moment arm with respect to $O$ and all the equations are satisfied. The second requires $\sin\vartheta = \dfrac{m g}{2 k \ell}$ and exists only if the spring is sufficiently stiff, that is, if $2 k \ell \ge m g$; with a spring that is too compliant only the vertical configuration remains.

The value $\Phi_A = m g$ says that the horizontal guide supports the whole weight of the rod, because the vertical guide exerts only horizontal forces. The reaction $\Phi_B$ balances the force of the spring, and both equal $k\ell\cos\vartheta$, that is, $k$ times the abscissa of $A$.

</div>
