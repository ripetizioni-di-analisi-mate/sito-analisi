## Exercise 1

Two homogeneous rods $AB$ and $BC$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, are hinged to each other at the common end $B$ and hinged to the ground at $A$ and at $C$, in a vertical plane; the points $A$ and $C$ lie at the same height and each rod makes the angle $\vartheta$ with the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure. All the hinges are smooth. Determine the reactions of the two hinges to the ground and the internal reaction at $B$, and compute them for $\vartheta = 45^\circ$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,3;C,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,cerniera" cerniere="B" forze="1.5 1.5,0,-1,mg;4.5 1.5,0,-1,mg" quote="A,C,2ℓcosϑ,-3.7" etichette="0.85,0.3,ϑ" titolo="Two rods hinged to each other at $B$ and hinged to the ground at $A$ and at $C$"}

One places the origin at $A$, with the $x$ axis horizontal toward the right. Then
$$
B - A = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
C - A = 2\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} ,
$$
and the centers of mass of the two rods have abscissas $x_{G_1} = \dfrac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}$ and $x_{G_2} = \dfrac{3\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}$.

The unknowns are six: the four components of the external reactions at $A$ and at $C$ and the two components $X_B$, $Y_B$ of the force that the rod $AB$ exerts on the rod $BC$. One writes first the cardinal equations of the whole system, in which the internal reaction does not appear, with the pole at $A$:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} + \Phi_{Cx} = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} + \Phi_{Cy} - 2\,m g = 0 \\[4pt]
2\ell\cos\vartheta\,\Phi_{Cy} - \dfrac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g - \dfrac{3\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the third equation, dividing by $\ell\cos\vartheta$, one obtains $2\,\Phi_{Cy} = 2\,m g$, hence
$$
\Phi_{Cy} = m g ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = m g .
$$
One now takes the system apart and writes the cardinal equations of the rod $BC$ alone, on which the weight at $G_2$, the reaction at $C$ and the force $X_B\,\hat{\imath} + Y_B\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $B$ act; the pole is taken at $B$:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Cx} + X_B = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Cy} + Y_B - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
\ell\cos\vartheta\,\Phi_{Cy} + \ell\sin\vartheta\,\Phi_{Cx} - \dfrac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation, with $\Phi_{Cy} = m g$, gives $Y_B = 0$. The third, after division by $\ell$, gives
$$
\sin\vartheta\,\Phi_{Cx} = -\,\frac{m g\cos\vartheta}{2} ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_{Cx} = -\,\frac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta} ,
$$
and from the first $X_B = \dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$. Finally $\Phi_{Ax} = -\,\Phi_{Cx} = \dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$.

::struttura{nodi="B,3,3;C,6,0" aste="B-C" vincoli="C,cerniera" forze="4.5 1.5,0,-1,mg" reazioni="B,0.9,0,X_B;B,0,0.9,Y_B;C,-0.9,0,Φ_Cx;C,0,0.9,Φ_Cy" titolo="Free-body diagram of the rod $BC$: at $B$ the action of the rod $AB$ appears"}

Check with the cardinal equations of the rod $AB$, which have not been used. On it the weight at $G_1$, the reaction at $A$ and the force $-\,X_B\,\hat{\imath} - Y_B\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $B$ act:
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_x &= \Phi_{Ax} - X_B = \frac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta} - \frac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta} = 0 , \\[6pt]
R_y &= \Phi_{Ay} - m g - Y_B = m g - m g - 0 = 0 , \\[6pt]
M_A &= -\,\frac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g + \ell\sin\vartheta\,X_B = -\,\frac{m g\,\ell\cos\vartheta}{2} + \frac{m g\,\ell\cos\vartheta}{2} = 0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

For $\vartheta = 45^\circ$ one has $\tan\vartheta = 1$, hence
$$
\vec{\Phi}_A = \frac{m g}{2}\,\hat{\imath} + m g\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{\Phi}_C = -\,\frac{m g}{2}\,\hat{\imath} + m g\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
X_B = \frac{m g}{2} ,
\quad
Y_B = 0 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is made of two rigid bodies, hence the scalar cardinal equations available are six; the unknowns are the four components of the external reactions and the two of the internal reaction, and the problem is well posed. The configuration is assigned, therefore there are no residual degrees of freedom.

One starts from the whole system because in it the internal reaction is an internal force, and by the theorem on the internal forces it appears neither in the resultant nor in the resultant moment. The moment equation with respect to $A$ then contains $\Phi_{Cy}$ alone, given that $\Phi_{Cx}$ has a horizontal line of action passing through $A$.

The result $\Phi_{Ay} = \Phi_{Cy} = m g$ could be foreseen from the symmetry, since the two rods are equal and placed symmetrically with respect to the vertical through $B$, and each hinge therefore supports the weight of one rod.

Taking the system apart serves to make the internal reaction appear. Taking the rod $BC$ alone as subsystem, the force that $AB$ transmits to it at $B$ becomes external and appears in the equations. The pole is taken at $B$ because in this way the internal reaction has zero moment and the equation contains the components of the reaction at $C$ alone.

The horizontal components of the two hinges are **equal and opposite** and equal $\dfrac{m g}{2\tan\vartheta}$. The hinges therefore push the rods inward and prevent the arch from opening. The push grows as $\vartheta$ decreases, that is, when the arch is flatter, and tends to zero when the rods approach the vertical.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two homogeneous rods $AB$ and $BC$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, are hinged to each other at $B$; the end $A$ is hinged to the ground, the end $C$ is constrained by a roller support that slides on a smooth horizontal guide passing through $A$. At $C$ the horizontal force $\vec{F} = -\,F\,\hat{\imath}$ acts, with $F > 0$, directed toward $A$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configuration, the reaction of the roller support and the internal reaction at $B$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.6,1.5;C,5.2,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello" cerniere="B" linee="-0.7,0 6,0" forze="1.3 0.75,0,-1,mg;3.9 0.75,0,-1,mg;C,-1.1,0,F" etichette="0.85,0.27,ϑ" titolo="Two rods hinged at $B$, with a hinge at $A$ and a roller support at $C$, pushed by the force $\vec{F}$"}

Let $\vartheta$ be the angle that each rod makes with the horizontal. The system has one degree of freedom, because the two rigid bodies have six degrees of freedom, the internal hinge removes two of them and the constraints to the ground three. The unknowns are $\vartheta$, the two components of the reaction at $A$, the reaction $\Phi_C$ of the roller support and the two components $X_B$, $Y_B$ of the force that $AB$ exerts on $BC$, six in all, as many as the equations.

One writes the cardinal equations of the whole system with the pole at $A$:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} - F = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} + \Phi_C - 2\,m g = 0 \\[4pt]
2\ell\cos\vartheta\,\Phi_C - \dfrac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g - \dfrac{3\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The force $\vec{F}$ is applied at $C$, which lies on the line through $A$ and $C$, hence it has zero moment with respect to $A$. From the third equation one obtains $\Phi_C = m g$, from the first $\Phi_{Ax} = F$ and from the second $\Phi_{Ay} = m g$.

One takes the system apart and writes the cardinal equations of the rod $BC$ alone, with the pole at $B$:
$$
\begin{cases}
X_B - F = 0 \\[4pt]
Y_B + \Phi_C - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
\ell\cos\vartheta\,\Phi_C + \ell\sin\vartheta\,(-\,F) - \dfrac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the first $X_B = F$, from the second $Y_B = 0$. The third, with $\Phi_C = m g$ and after division by $\ell$, gives the configuration equation:
$$
m g\cos\vartheta - F\sin\vartheta - \frac{m g\cos\vartheta}{2} = 0 ,
\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
\frac{m g}{2}\cos\vartheta - F\sin\vartheta = 0 ,
$$
therefore
$$
\tan\vartheta = \frac{m g}{2\,F} .
$$

Check with the moment equation of the rod $AB$ with respect to $A$, which has not been used. On $AB$ the weight at $G_1$ and the force $-\,X_B\,\hat{\imath} - Y_B\,\hat{\jmath}$ applied at $B$ act:
$$
M_A = -\,\frac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}\,m g + \ell\sin\vartheta\,X_B = -\,\frac{m g\,\ell\cos\vartheta}{2} + F\,\ell\sin\vartheta ,
$$
an expression that vanishes exactly in the configuration found. Moreover, for $F \to \infty$ one has $\vartheta \to 0$ and the two rods tend to stretch out on the guide, while for $F \to 0$ one has $\vartheta \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the rods tend to overlap along the vertical.

<div class="spiegazione">

Compared with the previous exercise the roller support replaces a hinge, therefore one constraint fewer leaves one degree of freedom, and the angle $\vartheta$ is not assigned but is one of the unknowns, determined by the equilibrium.

The equations of the whole system give the vertical reactions and the horizontal component at $A$, but not the angle. In the moment equation with respect to $A$ the factor $\ell\cos\vartheta$ cancels, because all the forces that appear in it are vertical or else have their line of action through $A$. In order to find $\vartheta$ it is therefore necessary to take the system apart.

In the moment equation of the rod $BC$ with respect to $B$ three contributions appear: the reaction of the roller support, with moment arm $\ell\cos\vartheta$; the force $\vec{F}$, with moment arm $\ell\sin\vartheta$; the weight of the rod, with moment arm $\dfrac{\ell\cos\vartheta}{2}$. The equilibrium balances them and gives the configuration equation.

The internal reaction turns out to be **horizontal**, of magnitude $F$. The rod $AB$ pushes the rod $BC$ toward the right with the same force with which $\vec{F}$ pushes it toward the left, while the vertical component is zero because the roller support bears the whole weight of the rod $BC$.

The check with the rod $AB$ reproduces the same configuration equation even though it starts from a different free-body diagram, and this confirms that the internal reactions found are consistent with both subsystems.

</div>
