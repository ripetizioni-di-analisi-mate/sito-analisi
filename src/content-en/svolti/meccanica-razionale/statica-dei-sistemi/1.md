## Exercise 1

A particle $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to a smooth fixed straight guide, placed in a vertical plane and inclined at the angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal. An ideal spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $P$ to the upper end $O$ of the guide. The system in the figure is subject to the weight alone besides the elastic force. Determine the equilibrium position of $P$ on the guide and the constraint reaction, then compute them for $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and $\alpha = 30^\circ$.

::struttura{linee="0,3 5.2,0;0.00,3.00 0.47,2.73 0.63,2.38 1.13,2.60 1.19,2.06 1.69,2.28 1.74,1.74 2.13,1.77 2.60,1.50" tratteggi="3.8,0 5.2,0" punti="0,3,O;2.6,1.5,P" forze="2.6 1.5,0,-1.1,mg" reazioni="2.6 1.5,0.55,0.95,Φ" quote="0 3,2.6 1.5,s,-0.9" etichette="1.35,2.75,k;4.6,0.17,α" titolo="Particle on a smooth straight guide inclined at $\alpha$, pulled back toward $O$ by the spring of constant $k$"}

As coordinate one takes the abscissa $s$ of $P$ on the guide, measured from $O$ and positive downward. The unit tangent vector to the guide, oriented as $s$, and the unit normal vector are
$$
\hat{t} = \cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} - \sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{n} = \sin\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\alpha\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
Since the natural length is zero, the elastic force on $P$ equals $-\,k\,(P - O) = -\,k\,s\,\hat{t}$. The resultant of the active forces is therefore
$$
\vec{F} = -\,m g\,\hat{\jmath} - k\,s\,\hat{t} .
$$
The equilibrium condition on the smooth guide is $\vec{F} \cdot \hat{t} = 0$:
$$
m g \sin\alpha - k\,s = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
s = \frac{m g \sin\alpha}{k} .
$$
The reaction is obtained from $\vec{\Phi} = -\,\vec{F}$, which in the equilibrium configuration has the normal component alone:
$$
\Phi = -\,\vec{F} \cdot \hat{n} = m g \cos\alpha .
$$

Check with the limiting cases:
$$
\alpha \to 0 : \quad s \to 0 ,\; \Phi \to m g ,
\qquad
\alpha \to \frac{\pi}{2} : \quad s \to \frac{m g}{k} ,\; \Phi \to 0 .
$$

Numerical values with $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $k = 100\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $\alpha = 30^\circ$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$:
$$
s = \frac{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 0.5}{100}\,\mathrm{m} = 0.098\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\Phi = 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,\mathrm{N} = 16.99\,\mathrm{N} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The particle has a single degree of freedom, because it is forced to remain on the guide, therefore a single coordinate suffices to locate its position; one chooses $s$ measured along the guide, so that the elastic force takes the simplest expression.

The guide is smooth, hence the reaction is orthogonal to $\hat{t}$ and disappears from the projection of the equilibrium equation onto $\hat{t}$. There remains an equation in the single unknown $s$, and this is the reason why one projects onto the tangent instead of writing the two Cartesian components.

The component of the weight along $\hat{t}$ equals $-mg\,\hat{\jmath} \cdot \hat{t} = mg\sin\alpha$ and is positive, that is, directed downward along the guide; the elastic force equals $-ks$ and opposes the movement away from $O$. Equilibrium holds where the two balance each other, therefore the elongation of the spring is **proportional to the component of the weight along the guide**.

In the limiting cases the result reproduces two known situations. With a horizontal guide the spring stays at its natural length and the constraint supports the whole weight. With a vertical guide the spring supports the whole weight, stretches by $\dfrac{mg}{k}$ and the reaction vanishes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle $P$ of mass $m$ is suspended in a vertical plane from two ideal strings $PA$ and $PB$, fixed at the points $A$ and $B$ placed at the same height, as in the diagram in the figure. The string $PA$ makes the angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal and the string $PB$ the angle $\beta$, with $\alpha, \beta \in \left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. Determine the tensions of the two strings and verify that they are both positive. Then compute them for $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $\alpha = 60^\circ$ and $\beta = 30^\circ$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3;B,5,3;P,2,0" catene="A-P;B-P" linee="-0.6,3 5.6,3" tratteggi="0.4,0 3.6,0" forze="P,0,-1.1,mg" etichette="1.45,0.25,α;2.6,0.3,β" titolo="Particle suspended from two ideal strings: the tensions are directed from $P$ toward the suspension points"}

The forces are projected onto the axes. The unit vectors directed from $P$ toward $A$ and from $P$ toward $B$ are
$$
\hat{u}_A = -\cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{u}_B = \cos\beta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\beta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
and the strings exert on $P$ the forces $T_A\,\hat{u}_A$ and $T_B\,\hat{u}_B$. The equilibrium of the particle requires that the resultant vanish:
$$
\begin{cases}
-\,T_A \cos\alpha + T_B \cos\beta = 0 \\[4pt]
T_A \sin\alpha + T_B \sin\beta - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the first equation one obtains $T_A = T_B\,\dfrac{\cos\beta}{\cos\alpha}$. Substituting into the second one gets
$$
T_B\,\frac{\cos\beta\sin\alpha + \cos\alpha\sin\beta}{\cos\alpha} = m g ,
$$
and since $\cos\beta\sin\alpha + \cos\alpha\sin\beta = \sin(\alpha + \beta)$ it follows that
$$
T_B = \frac{m g \cos\alpha}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} ,
\qquad
T_A = \frac{m g \cos\beta}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} .
$$

Check with the projection onto the direction orthogonal to $PA$, that is, onto $\hat{w} = \sin\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\alpha\,\hat{\jmath}$, which has not been used in the computation. One finds $\hat{u}_A \cdot \hat{w} = 0$ and $\hat{u}_B \cdot \hat{w} = \sin(\alpha + \beta)$, therefore the equilibrium equation projected onto $\hat{w}$ becomes
$$
T_B \sin(\alpha + \beta) - m g \cos\alpha = 0 ,
$$
which is satisfied by the value found for $T_B$.

Numerical values with $m = 2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $\alpha = 60^\circ$, $\beta = 30^\circ$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Since $\sin 90^\circ = 1$, one finds
$$
T_A = 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \cos 30^\circ\,\mathrm{N} = 16.99\,\mathrm{N} ,
\qquad
T_B = 2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot \cos 60^\circ\,\mathrm{N} = 9.81\,\mathrm{N} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The particle is free to move in the plane and the two strings are unilateral constraints. As long as the tensions turn out to be positive the strings are taut and behave as bilateral constraints, therefore one simply writes that the resultant of the three forces vanishes.

The tensions are directed from $P$ toward the suspension points, since a string pulls the body to which it is attached. In the two equations the cosines therefore appear for the horizontal components and the sines for the vertical ones, with the minus sign on the horizontal component of $T_A$ because $A$ lies to the left of $P$.

Since $\alpha + \beta < \pi$, one has $\sin(\alpha + \beta) > 0$, and the cosines are positive because the two angles are acute; therefore **both tensions are positive** and the strings are indeed taut.

The result says that the string closer to the vertical carries the greater tension. With $\alpha = 60^\circ$ and $\beta = 30^\circ$ the string $PA$, which is more vertical, bears $16.99\,\mathrm{N}$ against the $9.81\,\mathrm{N}$ of the other. In the symmetric case $\alpha = \beta$ one obtains $T_A = T_B = \dfrac{m g}{2 \sin\alpha}$, which for vertical strings equals $\dfrac{m g}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A ring $P$ of mass $m$, which can be treated as a particle, is threaded on a smooth fixed circular guide of radius $R$ and center $O$, placed in a vertical plane. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $P$ to the point $B$ of the guide, the end of the right horizontal radius, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations and the reaction of the guide in each of them.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" linee="1.53,-1.29 1.61,-1.06 1.82,-0.90 1.63,-0.60 1.97,-0.49 1.92,-0.23 2.00,0.00" segmenti="0,0,1.53,-1.29;0,0,0,-2" vettori="1.53,-1.29,1.53,-2.49,mg;1.53,-1.29,0.69,-0.58,Φ" punti="1.53,-1.29,pieno,P;2,0,pieno,B;0,0,pieno,O" etichette="0.42,-1.1,ϑ;2.35,-0.7,k" xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.2 ymin=-2.9 ymax=2.5 titolo="Ring on a smooth circular guide, connected by a spring to the point $B$ of the guide"}

The position of $P$ is located by the angle $\vartheta$ that the radius $OP$ makes with the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise, so that
$$
P - O = R\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - R\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
B - O = R\,\hat{\imath} .
$$
The unit tangent vector and the outward unit normal vector at the position $P$ are
$$
\hat{t} = \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{n} = \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The elastic force on $P$ equals $-\,k\,(P - B)$, hence the resultant of the active forces is
$$
\vec{F} = -\,k\,R\,(\sin\vartheta - 1)\,\hat{\imath} + \big( k\,R\cos\vartheta - m g \big)\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The equilibrium condition $\vec{F} \cdot \hat{t} = 0$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{F} \cdot \hat{t} &= -\,k R (\sin\vartheta - 1)\cos\vartheta + (k R\cos\vartheta - m g)\sin\vartheta \\[6pt]
&= -\,k R \sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta + k R \cos\vartheta + k R \sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta - m g \sin\vartheta \\[6pt]
&= k R \cos\vartheta - m g \sin\vartheta = 0 ,
\end{aligned}
$$
therefore
$$
\tan\vartheta = \frac{k R}{m g} .
$$
The equilibrium configurations are consequently two,
$$
\vartheta_1 = \arctan\frac{k R}{m g} \in \left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right) ,
\qquad
\vartheta_2 = \vartheta_1 + \pi .
$$
The reaction is $\vec{\Phi} = -\,\vec{F}$, directed along $\hat{n}$. The normal component of the active force equals
$$
\vec{F} \cdot \hat{n} = m g \cos\vartheta - k R\,(1 - \sin\vartheta) ,
$$
and setting $W = \sqrt{m^2 g^2 + k^2 R^2}$ one has, in the two configurations,
$$
\cos\vartheta_1 = \frac{m g}{W} ,\; \sin\vartheta_1 = \frac{k R}{W} ,
\qquad
\cos\vartheta_2 = -\,\frac{m g}{W} ,\; \sin\vartheta_2 = -\,\frac{k R}{W} .
$$
Substituting one obtains
$$
\vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\Big|_{\vartheta_1} = W - k R ,
\qquad
\vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,\Big|_{\vartheta_2} = -\,(W + k R) ,
$$
therefore $\vec{\Phi} = -\,(W - k R)\,\hat{n}$ in the first configuration, directed toward the center, and $\vec{\Phi} = (W + k R)\,\hat{n}$ in the second, directed outward.

Check with two limiting cases:
$$
k \to 0 : \quad \vartheta_1 \to 0 ,\; |\vec{\Phi}| \to m g ,
\qquad
g \to 0 : \quad \vartheta_1 \to \frac{\pi}{2} ,\; |\vec{\Phi}| \to 0 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ring is constrained to the circle, therefore it has a single degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ locates it. The angle $\vartheta$ is measured from the downward vertical and counterclockwise, as established by the convention on positive moments.

In the computation of the tangential component the two terms that contain $\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta$ cancel. There remains $k R \cos\vartheta - m g \sin\vartheta$, that is, the tangential component of the elastic force behaves like that of a constant horizontal force of magnitude $k R$. A spring with zero natural length, stretched between two points of the same circle, therefore produces on the tangent the same effect as a constant force.

The two solutions are diametrically opposite, as happens every time the equilibrium equation on a circle has the form $\tan\vartheta = c$. In the first the ring lies in the lower right quadrant, between the lowest point and $B$, and the guide pushes it **toward the center**; in the second it lies on the opposite side and the guide pushes it **outward**, that is, it holds it back.

The limiting cases confirm the result. Without the spring the ring comes to rest at the lowest point and the guide must support the whole weight. Without weight the ring comes to rest at $B$, where the spring has zero length and exerts no force, and the reaction vanishes.

</div>
