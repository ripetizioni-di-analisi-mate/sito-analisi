## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is hinged at $A$ to a vertical wall, in a vertical plane, and makes the angle $\alpha$ with the horizontal, with $\alpha \in \left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. The end $B$ is connected to the wall by an ideal horizontal string, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the tension of the string and the reaction of the hinge, then compute them for $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $\alpha = 30^\circ$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4.33,2.5;C,0,2.5" aste="A-B" catene="B-C" vincoli="A,cerniera,180" linee="0,-0.7 0,3.4" tratteggi="0,0 1.7,0" forze="2.17 1.25,0,-1.1,mg" etichette="0.78,0.24,α" titolo="Rod hinged to the wall at $A$ and held back by the horizontal string $BC$"}

One places the origin at $A$, with the $x$ axis horizontal toward the right. Then
$$
B - A = \ell\cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - A = \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The string pulls $B$ toward the wall, hence it exerts on $B$ the force $-\,T\,\hat{\imath}$ with $T \ge 0$. The unknowns are three, $T$, $\Phi_{Ax}$ and $\Phi_{Ay}$, and the configuration is assigned. One writes the moment equation with respect to $A$, which does not contain the reaction of the hinge:
$$
x_G\,(-\,m g) - y_B\,(-\,T) = -\,\frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\alpha + T\,\ell\sin\alpha = 0 ,
$$
therefore
$$
T = \frac{m g\cos\alpha}{2\sin\alpha} = \frac{m g}{2\tan\alpha} .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives the two components of the reaction:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} - T = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_{Ax} = \frac{m g}{2\tan\alpha} ,
\quad
\Phi_{Ay} = m g .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $B$, written with the values found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_B^{(e)} &= (x_G - x_B)(-\,m g) + (x_A - x_B)\,\Phi_{Ay} - (y_A - y_B)\,\Phi_{Ax} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\alpha - \ell\cos\alpha \cdot m g + \ell\sin\alpha \cdot \frac{m g\cos\alpha}{2\sin\alpha} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\alpha - m g\,\ell\cos\alpha + \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\alpha = 0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Numerical values with $m = 4\,\mathrm{kg}$, $\alpha = 30^\circ$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$:
$$
T = \Phi_{Ax} = \frac{4 \cdot 9.81}{2\tan 30^\circ}\,\mathrm{N} = 33.98\,\mathrm{N} ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = 39.24\,\mathrm{N} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is a rigid body in an assigned configuration, hence the residual degrees of freedom are zero and the three scalar cardinal equations determine the three unknowns. The string is replaced with the force that it exerts on the rod, directed from $B$ toward the wall, since a string pulls.

The pole is taken at $A$ because the reaction of the hinge has zero moment with respect to that point. In the moment equation the weight has moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\alpha$ and clockwise moment, while the tension has moment arm $\ell\sin\alpha$ and counterclockwise moment, therefore the equilibrium requires that the two balance each other.

The tension grows as $\alpha$ decreases, because the moment arm of the weight increases and that of the tension decreases. For $\alpha \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the tension tends to zero, and indeed a vertical rod is in equilibrium on the hinge alone; for $\alpha \to 0$ the tension grows beyond every bound.

The reaction of the hinge has vertical component equal to the whole weight, because the string is horizontal and supports no part of it, and horizontal component **equal to the tension**, in the opposite direction. With $\alpha = 30^\circ$ the tension equals $33.98\,\mathrm{N}$, that is, almost as much as the weight of the rod.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is placed horizontally in a vertical plane. At $A$ there is a fixed hinge, at $B$ a roller support that slides on a smooth guide inclined at the angle $\beta$ to the horizontal, as in the diagram in the figure; moreover at $B$ a horizontal force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$ acts. Determine the constraint reactions, then compute them for $m = 6\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $\beta = 30^\circ$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello,300" tratteggi="6,0 7.6,0" forze="3 0,0,-1.1,mg;B,1.2,0,F" quote="A,B,ℓ,1.4" etichette="6.8,-0.45,β" titolo="Horizontal rod with a hinge at $A$ and a roller support on a guide inclined at $\beta$ at $B$"}

The guide has direction $\hat{u} = \cos\beta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\beta\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence the reaction of the roller support, orthogonal to the guide, is written as
$$
\vec{\Phi}_B = \Phi_B\,\big(-\sin\beta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\beta\,\hat{\jmath}\big) .
$$
The unknowns are $\Phi_{Ax}$, $\Phi_{Ay}$ and $\Phi_B$. The moment equation with respect to $A$ does not contain the reaction of the hinge, and the force $\vec{F}$ has its line of action along the axis of the rod, which passes through $A$, hence it does not appear in it:
$$
\ell\,\Phi_B\cos\beta - \frac{\ell}{2}\,m g = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_B = \frac{m g}{2\cos\beta} .
$$
The first cardinal equation gives
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{Ax} - \Phi_B\sin\beta + F = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Ay} + \Phi_B\cos\beta - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
and substituting the value of $\Phi_B$ one obtains
$$
\Phi_{Ax} = \frac{m g\tan\beta}{2} - F ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = m g - \frac{m g}{2} = \frac{m g}{2} .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $B$, written with the values found:
$$
M_B^{(e)} = (x_A - x_B)\,\Phi_{Ay} + (x_G - x_B)(-\,m g) = -\,\ell\,\frac{m g}{2} + \left(-\,\frac{\ell}{2}\right)(-\,m g) = 0 .
$$

Numerical values with $m = 6\,\mathrm{kg}$, $\beta = 30^\circ$ and $g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$:
$$
\Phi_B = \frac{6 \cdot 9.81}{2\cos 30^\circ}\,\mathrm{N} = 33.98\,\mathrm{N} ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Ax} = (16.99 - F)\,\mathrm{N} ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Ay} = 29.43\,\mathrm{N} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The roller support on a smooth guide exerts a reaction orthogonal to the guide, therefore its direction is known and only the magnitude $\Phi_B$ remains unknown, with its sign. The rigid body is thus constrained by three reaction components and has no residual degrees of freedom.

The pole is taken at $A$ in order to make the reaction of the hinge disappear. The force $\vec{F}$ is horizontal and applied at $B$, which lies on the axis of the rod, hence its line of action passes through $A$, its moment arm is zero and **the force $\vec{F}$ does not appear in the moment equation**. It follows that $\Phi_B$ and $\Phi_{Ay}$ do not depend on $F$.

The value $\Phi_{Ay} = \dfrac{m g}{2}$ says that the two constraints each support half the weight, as happens for a homogeneous rod resting symmetrically. The horizontal component of the reaction at $A$, on the other hand, depends on $F$ and changes sign for $F = \dfrac{m g\tan\beta}{2}$, that is, for $F = 16.99\,\mathrm{N}$ with the assigned data; for larger values the hinge pulls the rod **to the left**.

For $\beta \to 0$ the guide becomes horizontal, the reaction of the roller support returns to the vertical and equals $\dfrac{m g}{2}$, and $\Phi_{Ax} = -\,F$, hence the whole horizontal force is balanced by the hinge.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $\ell$ is placed in a vertical plane with the end $A$ constrained by a roller support that slides on a smooth vertical guide and the end $B$ by a roller support that slides on a smooth horizontal guide; the two guides meet at the origin $O$. At $B$ the horizontal force $\vec{F} = -\,F\,\hat{\imath}$ acts, with $F > 0$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configuration and the reactions of the two roller supports.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,3.21;B,3.83,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,180;B,carrello,270" linee="0,-0.5 0,4.2;-0.6,0 4.8,0" forze="1.92 1.61,0,-1.1,mg;B,-1.2,0,F" etichette="3.1,0.3,ϑ" titolo="Rod with its ends on two smooth orthogonal guides, pushed at $B$ by the horizontal force $\vec{F}$"}

Let $\vartheta$ be the angle that the rod makes with the horizontal, with $\vartheta \in \left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. Then
$$
A - O = \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
B - O = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
G - O = \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The roller support at $A$ slides on a vertical guide, therefore its reaction is horizontal, $\vec{\Phi}_A = \Phi_A\,\hat{\imath}$; the roller support at $B$ slides on a horizontal guide, therefore its reaction is vertical, $\vec{\Phi}_B = \Phi_B\,\hat{\jmath}$. The unknowns are $\vartheta$, $\Phi_A$ and $\Phi_B$.

The first cardinal equation gives the two reactions at once:
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_A - F = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_B - m g = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi_A = F ,
\quad
\Phi_B = m g .
$$
The moment equation with respect to $B$ then contains the single unknown $\vartheta$, given that $\vec{\Phi}_B$ and $\vec{F}$ are applied at $B$:
$$
(x_G - x_B)(-\,m g) - (y_A - y_B)\,\Phi_A = \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \ell\sin\vartheta\,F = 0 ,
$$
therefore
$$
\tan\vartheta = \frac{m g}{2\,F} .
$$

Check with the moment equation with respect to $A$, written with the values found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_A^{(e)} &= (x_G - x_A)(-\,m g) + (x_B - x_A)\,\Phi_B - (y_B - y_A)\,(-\,F) \\[6pt]
&= -\,\frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + \ell\cos\vartheta\,m g - \ell\sin\vartheta\,F \\[6pt]
&= \frac{m g\,\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - F\,\ell\sin\vartheta ,
\end{aligned}
$$
which vanishes in the configuration found.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each roller support removes one degree of freedom, therefore one degree of freedom remains for the rod, located by $\vartheta$; the unknown reaction components are two, and in all the unknowns are three, as many as the equations.

Here it is convenient to start from the first cardinal equation, because each of the two components contains a single reaction, given that the vertical guide exerts only horizontal forces and the horizontal guide only vertical forces. The two reactions therefore turn out to be independent of the configuration.

The pole is taken at $B$ because both $\vec{\Phi}_B$ and $\vec{F}$ are applied there, and consequently have zero moment; in the equation there remain the weight, with moment arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, and the reaction $\Phi_A$, with moment arm $\ell\sin\vartheta$.

The result says that the rod lowers when $F$ grows. For $F \to \infty$ one has $\vartheta \to 0$ and the rod tends to lie down on the horizontal guide, while for $F \to 0$ one has $\vartheta \to \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the rod tends to straighten up against the vertical guide. Without the force $\vec{F}$ the only equilibrium configuration is the **vertical** one.

</div>
