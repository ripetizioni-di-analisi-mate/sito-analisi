## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of mass $m$ and length $3a$ is placed horizontally in a vertical plane and rests on two smooth unilateral supports, one at $A$ and one at the point $C$ with $AC = 2a$. At the end $B$ a particle of mass $m_1$ is hung with a vertical string, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the reactions of the two supports and the value of $m_1$ beyond which the rod lifts at $A$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello;C,carrello" forze="3 0,0,-1.1,mg;B,0,-1.3,m_1g" quote="A,C,2a,1.3;C,B,a,1.3" titolo="Rod resting at $A$ and at $C$ on unilateral supports, with the weight $m_1 g$ hung at $B$"}

One places the origin at $A$, with the $x$ axis along the rod. The abscissas of the points are
$$
x_A = 0 ,
\qquad
x_C = 2a ,
\qquad
x_G = \frac{3a}{2} ,
\qquad
x_B = 3a .
$$
The supports are unilateral and smooth, hence they exert vertical reactions directed upward, $\vec{\Phi}_A = \Phi_A\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\vec{\Phi}_C = \Phi_C\,\hat{\jmath}$, with
$$
\Phi_A \ge 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_C \ge 0 .
$$
The string hung at $B$ pulls the rod downward with the force $-\,m_1 g\,\hat{\jmath}$, because the hanging particle is in equilibrium under its own weight and the tension of the string. The cardinal equations of statics, with the pole at $A$, give
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_A + \Phi_C - m g - m_1 g = 0 \\[4pt]
2a\,\Phi_C - \dfrac{3a}{2}\,m g - 3a\,m_1 g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation, dividing by $2a$,
$$
\Phi_C = \frac{3\,m g}{4} + \frac{3\,m_1 g}{2} = \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{4} ,
$$
and substituting into the first
$$
\Phi_A = (m + m_1)\,g - \frac{3\,g\,(m + 2\,m_1)}{4} = \frac{g\,(m - 2\,m_1)}{4} .
$$
The condition $\Phi_A \ge 0$ is satisfied if and only if
$$
m_1 \le \frac{m}{2} ,
$$
therefore for $m_1 > \dfrac{m}{2}$ the rod lifts at $A$ and the configuration considered is not one of equilibrium.

Check with the moment equation with respect to $C$, written with the values found:
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_C^{(e)} &= (x_A - x_C)\,\Phi_A + (x_G - x_C)(-\,m g) + (x_B - x_C)(-\,m_1 g) \\[6pt]
&= -\,2a\,\frac{g\,(m - 2m_1)}{4} + \left(-\,\frac{a}{2}\right)(-\,m g) + a\,(-\,m_1 g) \\[6pt]
&= -\,\frac{m g a}{2} + m_1 g a + \frac{m g a}{2} - m_1 g a = 0 .
\end{aligned}
$$
In the limiting case $m_1 = \dfrac{m}{2}$ one finds $\Phi_A = 0$ and $\Phi_C = \dfrac{3\,m g}{2}$, which is the total weight $m g + m_1 g$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is a rigid body in an assigned configuration; the unknowns are the two reactions, and of the three scalar cardinal equations the first, the one for horizontal translations, is identically satisfied because all the forces are vertical.

The pole is taken at $A$ because the reaction $\Phi_A$ has its line of action passing through $A$ and hence zero moment, and the moment equation then contains the single unknown $\Phi_C$. The weight of the rod has moment arm $\dfrac{3a}{2}$ and the hanging load has moment arm $3a$, and both tend to rotate the rod clockwise about $A$, therefore they enter with the minus sign.

The support at $C$ lies between the center of mass and the load, therefore the contribution of the load hung at $B$ to the reaction $\Phi_C$ equals $\dfrac{3\,m_1 g}{2}$, that is, one and a half times its weight. The sum of the two reactions is the total weight, hence what the support at $C$ bears in addition is subtracted from the support at $A$, whose reaction decreases as $m_1$ grows.

The detachment condition is read off from the sign of $\Phi_A$. A unilateral support can push and not pull, hence a negative value of $\Phi_A$ is devoid of physical meaning and indicates that the rod **lifts at $A$** and rotates about $C$. With $m_1 = \dfrac{m}{2}$ the reaction at $A$ vanishes and this is the detachment value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A particle $P$ of mass $m$ rests on the concave side of a smooth circular guide of radius $R$ and center $O$, placed in a vertical plane and limited to the arc $|\vartheta| \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, where $\vartheta$ is the angle measured from the downward vertical through $O$ counterclockwise. The constraint is unilateral and can only push $P$ toward the center. A horizontal force $\vec{F}_1 = F_1\,\hat{\imath}$ and a vertical force $\vec{F}_2 = F_2\,\hat{\jmath}$ are applied to the particle, both of assigned magnitude, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configuration, the reaction of the guide and the conditions on $F_1$ and $F_2$ for the equilibrium to be possible.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 curve="2*sin(t),-2*cos(t),-1.047,1.047" segmenti="0,0,0,-2;0,0,1.15,-1.64;0,0,1.73,-1" vettori="1.15,-1.64,1.15,-2.74,mg;1.15,-1.64,2.35,-1.64,F₁;1.15,-1.64,1.15,-0.54,F₂" punti="1.15,-1.64,pieno,P;0,0,pieno,O" etichette="0.3,-1.25,ϑ;1.05,-0.25,60°" xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.1 ymin=-3.1 ymax=0.9 titolo="Particle resting on the concave side of a smooth circular guide, limited to the arc $|\vartheta| \le \dfrac{\pi}{3}$"}

The position of $P$ is
$$
P - O = R\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - R\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
and the unit tangent vector and the inward unit normal vector, the latter directed toward $O$, are
$$
\hat{t} = \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\hat{\nu} = -\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The resultant of the active forces is
$$
\vec{F} = F_1\,\hat{\imath} + (F_2 - m g)\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
The equilibrium condition on the smooth guide is $\vec{F} \cdot \hat{t} = 0$:
$$
F_1\cos\vartheta + (F_2 - m g)\sin\vartheta = 0 ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\tan\vartheta = \frac{F_1}{m g - F_2} .
$$
The guide can only push toward the center, therefore the reaction is $\vec{\Phi} = \Phi\,\hat{\nu}$ with $\Phi \ge 0$, and from the equilibrium equation $\vec{F} + \vec{\Phi} = \vec{0}$ projected onto $\hat{\nu}$ it follows that
$$
\Phi = -\,\vec{F} \cdot \hat{\nu} = F_1\sin\vartheta + (m g - F_2)\cos\vartheta .
$$
Setting $W = \sqrt{F_1^2 + (m g - F_2)^2}$, in the equilibrium configuration with $\cos\vartheta > 0$ one has
$$
\sin\vartheta = \frac{F_1}{W} ,
\qquad
\cos\vartheta = \frac{m g - F_2}{W} ,
\qquad\text{therefore}\qquad
\Phi = \frac{F_1^2 + (m g - F_2)^2}{W} = W .
$$
The conditions for the equilibrium to be possible are two:
$$
\begin{cases}
F_2 < m g \\[4pt]
F_1 \le \sqrt{3}\,(m g - F_2)
\end{cases}
$$

Check with a limiting case. For $F_1 = 0$ and $F_2 < m g$ one obtains $\vartheta = 0$ and $\Phi = m g - F_2$, that is, the particle stays at the bottom of the bowl and the guide pushes with the excess of the weight over the vertical force; for $F_2 = 0$ one recovers $\Phi = \sqrt{F_1^2 + m^2 g^2}$, the magnitude of the resultant of the active forces.

<div class="spiegazione">

The particle has a single degree of freedom on the guide and the angle $\vartheta$ locates it. The equilibrium condition is obtained by projecting onto the tangent, the direction along which the smooth constraint exerts no force.

The two vertical forces, the weight and $\vec{F}_2$, add up to a single vertical force of component $F_2 - m g$. The problem is therefore that of a particle in a bowl subject to a horizontal force and to a reduced weight of magnitude $m g - F_2$, and the equilibrium configuration is the one in which the resultant of the active forces is **normal to the guide**.

The value $\Phi = W$ confirms this reading, since the reaction equals in magnitude the resultant of the active forces, as it must be when the resultant is normal to the guide. In particular $\Phi > 0$ every time $F_2 < m g$, hence the particle always presses on the guide and does not detach from it.

The two conditions have different meanings. The first, $F_2 < m g$, requires that the vertical force remain smaller than the weight. If it exceeded it, the resultant of the active forces would be directed upward and the guide would have to pull the particle outward, whereas a unilateral constraint can only push. The second says that the solution $\vartheta$ must fall inside the arc actually present, since $\tan\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \sqrt{3}$; if $F_1$ is larger, the particle leaves the guide at its edge.

</div>
