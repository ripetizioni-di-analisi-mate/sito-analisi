## Exercise 1

The body in the figure, of mass $m$, rests on a plane inclined by the angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal, with static friction coefficient $f_s$ between body and plane. The only active forces are the weight and the reaction of the plane. Determine the equilibrium condition, say whether the body stays still for $\alpha = 25^\circ$ and $f_s = 0.40$ and compute the minimum friction coefficient that guarantees equilibrium for $\alpha = 25^\circ$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 4,1.87 4,0;1.45,0.68 2.54,1.18 2.24,1.82 1.15,1.31" linee="-0.5,0 4.6,0" forze="1.85 1.25,0,-1,mg" reazioni="1.99 0.93,-0.34,0.72,Φ_n;1.99 0.93,0.72,0.34,Φ_t" etichette="0.8,0.12,α" titolo="Body on a rough inclined plane: the reaction has normal component $\Phi_n$ and friction component $\Phi_t$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_t - m g \sin\alpha = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_n - m g \cos\alpha = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_t = m g \sin\alpha,
\qquad
\Phi_n = m g \cos\alpha
$$
$$
|\Phi_t| \leq f_s\,\Phi_n
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
m g \sin\alpha \leq f_s\,m g \cos\alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tan\alpha \leq f_s
$$
$$
\alpha = 25^\circ:
\qquad
\tan 25^\circ = 0.47 > 0.40
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the body slides}
$$
$$
f_{s,\min} = \tan 25^\circ = 0.47
$$

Check with the friction angle:
$$
\rho = \arctan 0.40 = 21.80^\circ < 25^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One projects the equilibrium of the body on the direction of the plane and on its normal. Along the plane there act the component of the weight $m g \sin\alpha$, directed downward, and the friction component $\Phi_t$, which balances it; along the normal there act the component of the weight $m g \cos\alpha$ and the normal component $\Phi_n$, which balances it. The two equations give the components of the reaction at once.

The **Coulomb-Morin law** requires $|\Phi_t| \leq f_s\,\Phi_n$. Substituting the two components and dividing by $m g \cos\alpha$, which is positive for $0 < \alpha < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, one obtains the condition $\tan\alpha \leq f_s$, which does not contain the mass; the equilibrium therefore depends only on the inclination of the plane and on the pair of materials in contact.

With $\alpha = 25^\circ$ one has $\tan\alpha = 0.47$, which exceeds $f_s = 0.40$, hence the condition is not satisfied and the body **slides** along the plane. In order for it to remain in equilibrium a friction coefficient at least equal to $\tan 25^\circ = 0.47$ is needed.

The check reads the same result on the friction cone. The friction angle of the contact equals $\rho = \arctan 0.40 = 21.80^\circ$, whereas the reaction, having to balance the weight, is vertical and forms the angle $\alpha = 25^\circ$ with the normal to the plane; since $25^\circ$ exceeds $21.80^\circ$, the reaction falls outside the cone and equilibrium is not possible.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On the same body as in the previous exercise, resting on a plane inclined by the angle $\alpha$ with static friction coefficient $f_s$, there also acts a horizontal force $F$ directed toward the plane, as in the figure. Determine the interval of the values of $F$ for which the body stays in equilibrium, write it with the friction angle $\rho = \arctan f_s$ and compute it for $\alpha = 30^\circ$ and $f_s = \tan 15^\circ = 2 - \sqrt{3}$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 4,2.31 4,0;1.39,0.80 2.43,1.40 2.08,2.01 1.04,1.41" linee="-0.5,0 4.6,0" forze="1.73 1.4,0,-1,mg;1.73 1.4,1,0,F" reazioni="1.91 1.1,-0.35,0.6,Φ_n;1.91 1.1,0.6,0.35,Φ_t" etichette="0.8,0.15,α" titolo="Body on a rough inclined plane with a horizontal force $F$"}

$$
\hat{t} = \cos\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\alpha\,\hat{\jmath},
\qquad
\hat{n} = -\sin\alpha\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\alpha\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
F\cos\alpha - m g \sin\alpha + \Phi_t = 0 \\[4pt]
-F\sin\alpha - m g \cos\alpha + \Phi_n = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_t = m g \sin\alpha - F\cos\alpha,
\qquad
\Phi_n = m g \cos\alpha + F\sin\alpha
$$
$$
|\,m g \sin\alpha - F\cos\alpha\,| \leq f_s\,\big( m g \cos\alpha + F\sin\alpha \big)
$$
$$
m g\,\frac{\sin\alpha - f_s\cos\alpha}{\cos\alpha + f_s\sin\alpha} \leq F \leq m g\,\frac{\sin\alpha + f_s\cos\alpha}{\cos\alpha - f_s\sin\alpha}
$$
$$
m g \tan(\alpha - \rho) \leq F \leq m g \tan(\alpha + \rho),
\qquad
\rho = \arctan f_s,
\qquad
\alpha + \rho < \frac{\pi}{2}
$$
$$
\alpha = 30^\circ,
\quad
\rho = 15^\circ:
\qquad
m g \tan 15^\circ \leq F \leq m g \tan 45^\circ,
\qquad
\big( 2 - \sqrt{3} \big)\,m g \leq F \leq m g
$$

Check with the case of a smooth plane:
$$
f_s = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\rho = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F = m g \tan\alpha
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One writes the equilibrium in the basis formed by the unit vector $\hat{t}$, directed along the plane upward, and by the unit vector $\hat{n}$, normal to the plane and pointing out of it. The force $F$ has component $F\cos\alpha$ along $\hat{t}$ and component $-F\sin\alpha$ along $\hat{n}$, since it is horizontal and points against the plane; the weight has components $-m g \sin\alpha$ and $-m g \cos\alpha$. The two equilibrium equations give the components of the reaction.

The force $F$ has two opposite effects: its component along the plane pushes the body upward, whereas its normal component increases $\Phi_n$ and hence the available friction force. For small $F$ the descent of the body prevails, for large $F$ the ascent prevails, and between the two cases lies the interval of equilibrium.

The **Coulomb-Morin law** is written as an inequality between absolute values, which is equivalent to two inequalities. From $m g \sin\alpha - F\cos\alpha \leq f_s\,\Phi_n$ one obtains the lower bound; from $F\cos\alpha - m g \sin\alpha \leq f_s\,\Phi_n$ one obtains the upper bound, provided that $\cos\alpha - f_s\sin\alpha$ is positive, that is, that $\alpha + \rho$ does not reach $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Dividing numerator and denominator by $\cos\alpha\cos\rho$ and using $f_s = \tan\rho$, the two fractions become $\tan(\alpha - \rho)$ and $\tan(\alpha + \rho)$, by the addition formula for the tangent.

With $\alpha = 30^\circ$ and $\rho = 15^\circ$ the interval is $\big( 2 - \sqrt{3} \big)\,m g \leq F \leq m g$, that is, about $0.27\,m g \leq F \leq m g$. The upper bound **equals exactly** $m g$, since $\alpha + \rho = 45^\circ$.

The check considers the smooth plane. With $f_s = 0$ the friction angle is zero, the two endpoints coincide and equilibrium is possible for the single value $F = m g \tan\alpha$, which is the horizontal component needed to balance the weight on a frictionless plane.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The homogeneous rod $AB$ in the figure, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, rests with the end $A$ on the rough horizontal floor, of static friction coefficient $f_s$, and with the end $B$ on a smooth vertical wall; the rod forms the angle $\alpha$ with the floor, in a vertical plane. Compute the reactions of the two supports and the equilibrium condition, and determine the minimum inclination for $f_s = 0.50$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2.29,3.28" aste="A-B" linee="-0.6,0 3.1,0;2.29,-0.1 2.29,3.9" forze="1.15 1.64,0,-1.1,mg" reazioni="A,0,1.1,Φ_An;A,1.1,0,Φ_At;B,-1.1,0,Φ_Bn" etichette="0.75,0.2,α" punti="1.15,1.64,G,baricentro" titolo="Rod resting between a rough floor and a smooth wall"}

$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{At} - \Phi_{Bn} = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{An} - m g = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_{Bn}\,\ell\sin\alpha - m g\,\dfrac{\ell\cos\alpha}{2} = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_{An} = m g,
\qquad
\Phi_{Bn} = \frac{m g}{2\tan\alpha},
\qquad
\Phi_{At} = \frac{m g}{2\tan\alpha}
$$
$$
|\Phi_{At}| \leq f_s\,\Phi_{An}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{2\tan\alpha} \leq f_s
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tan\alpha \geq \frac{1}{2 f_s}
$$
$$
f_s = 0.50:
\qquad
\tan\alpha \geq 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha \geq 45^\circ
$$

Check with the moment about $B$:
$$
-\Phi_{An}\,\ell\cos\alpha + \Phi_{At}\,\ell\sin\alpha + m g\,\frac{\ell\cos\alpha}{2} = -m g\,\ell\cos\alpha + \frac{m g\,\ell\cos\alpha}{2} + \frac{m g\,\ell\cos\alpha}{2} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wall is smooth, therefore at $B$ it exerts the normal reaction $\Phi_{Bn}$ alone, horizontal and directed toward the rod; the floor is rough, therefore at $A$ it exerts the normal reaction $\Phi_{An}$, vertical, and the friction component $\Phi_{At}$, horizontal. The unknowns are three.

The first two equations are the equilibrium for horizontal and vertical translation; the third is the equilibrium for rotation about $A$, in which the reaction $\Phi_{Bn}$ has arm $\ell\sin\alpha$, which is the height of $B$ above the floor, and the weight has arm $\dfrac{\ell\cos\alpha}{2}$, which is the horizontal distance of the center of mass from $A$. The reactions of the support are treated in full in the topic on the statics of the point and of systems.

The floor supports the whole weight, since the wall exerts a horizontal thrust; the thrust of the wall, and with it the friction force required of the floor, grows as $\alpha$ decreases, since $\tan\alpha$ appears in the denominator. For $\alpha$ close to zero the required friction coefficient grows beyond every bound.

The **Coulomb-Morin law** gives the condition $\tan\alpha \geq \dfrac{1}{2 f_s}$. With $f_s = 0.50$ one obtains $\tan\alpha \geq 1$, that is, $\alpha \geq 45^\circ$, hence the rod **stays in equilibrium** only if it is inclined by at least $45^\circ$ to the floor.

The check writes the equilibrium for rotation about $B$, which is an equation independent of the one used. The moments of $\Phi_{An}$, of $\Phi_{At}$ and of the weight cancel one another, hence the values found are correct.

</div>
