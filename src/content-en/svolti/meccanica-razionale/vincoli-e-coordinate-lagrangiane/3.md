## Exercise 1

The material point $P$ in the figure, of mass $m$, is constrained to the smooth and fixed circular guide of center $O$ and radius $R$, lying in a vertical plane. The constraint is bilateral. Determine the number of degrees of freedom, choose a Lagrangian coordinate, write the position, the velocity and the virtual displacement of $P$, establish the direction of the constraint reaction and verify that its virtual work is zero.

::struttura{linee="2.00,0.00 1.97,0.35 1.88,0.68 1.73,1.00 1.53,1.29 1.29,1.53 1.00,1.73 0.68,1.88 0.35,1.97 0.00,2.00 -0.35,1.97 -0.68,1.88 -1.00,1.73 -1.29,1.53 -1.53,1.29 -1.73,1.00 -1.88,0.68 -1.97,0.35 -2.00,0.00 -1.97,-0.35 -1.88,-0.68 -1.73,-1.00 -1.53,-1.29 -1.29,-1.53 -1.00,-1.73 -0.68,-1.88 -0.35,-1.97 -0.00,-2.00 0.35,-1.97 0.68,-1.88 1.00,-1.73 1.29,-1.53 1.53,-1.29 1.73,-1.00 1.88,-0.68 1.97,-0.35 2.00,-0.00" nodi="O,0,0,-;P,1.15,1.64,-" punti="0,0,O,pieno;1.15,1.64,P,pieno" tratteggi="0,0 1.15,1.64;0,0 2.3,0" reazioni="1.15 1.64,-0.52,-0.74,Φ" frecce="1.15,1.64,0.48,2.11,δP" forze="1.15 1.64,0,-0.8,mg" etichette="0.62,0.22,ϑ;0.35,1,R" titolo="Point on a smooth circular guide: $\delta P$ is tangent to the guide, $\vec{\Phi}$ is radial"}

$$
n = 2 - 1 = 1,
\qquad
\vartheta = \text{angle of } P - O \text{ with the } x \text{ axis}
$$
$$
P - O = R\,\big( \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = R\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\big( -\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big),
\qquad
\delta P = R\,\delta\vartheta\,\big( -\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
f(P) = x^2 + y^2 - R^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{grad} f = 2\,(x\,\hat{\imath} + y\,\hat{\jmath}) = 2\,(P - O)
$$
$$
\vec{\Phi} = \Phi\,\big( \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$

Check of the virtual work of the reaction:
$$
\vec{\Phi} \cdot \delta P = \Phi\,R\,\delta\vartheta\,\big( -\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta + \sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta \big) = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point has two coordinates in the plane and the constraint imposes the single equation $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$, therefore there remains $2 - 1 = 1$ degree of freedom. As Lagrangian coordinate one takes the angle $\vartheta$ that the vector $P - O$ forms with the $x$ axis, since with it the position is written with the trigonometric functions of the circle.

The velocity is obtained by differentiating the position with respect to time, and the virtual displacement is obtained from the same derivative with respect to the Lagrangian coordinate, multiplied by the arbitrary $\delta\vartheta$; the two vectors therefore have the same direction, that of the tangent unit vector $-\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$. The constraint is fixed, hence virtual displacements and possible displacements coincide up to the factor $dt$.

The guide is smooth and bilateral, hence by the **theorem on the reaction of a smooth constraint** the reaction is parallel to $\operatorname{grad} f$, that is, to the vector $P - O$, hence it is **radial** and has a single scalar unknown $\Phi$, positive if the reaction is directed toward the outside of the guide.

The check computes the scalar product between the reaction and the virtual displacement, which turns out to be zero for every $\vartheta$ and for every $\delta\vartheta$, therefore the constraint is ideal and the virtual work of the reaction will not appear in the equilibrium conditions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The material point $P$ in the figure is constrained to the smooth and fixed guide of equation $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$, with $a > 0$, lying in the vertical plane $Oxy$ with $y$ directed upward. Choose a Lagrangian coordinate, write the velocity and the virtual displacement of $P$, determine the direction of the constraint reaction and establish at which point of the guide the reaction is vertical.

::struttura{linee="-3.5,3.06 -3,2.25 -2.5,1.56 -2,1 -1.5,0.56 -1,0.25 -0.5,0.06 0,0 0.5,0.06 1,0.25 1.5,0.56 2,1 2.5,1.56 3,2.25 3.5,3.06" punti="2,1,P,pieno;0,0,V,pieno" reazioni="2 1,-0.8,0.8,Φ" frecce="2,1,2.9,1.9,δP" titolo="Point on the guide $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2a}$: the reaction is orthogonal to the tangent, and at the vertex $V$ it turns out to be vertical"}

$$
q = x,
\qquad
P - O = x\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{x^2}{2a}\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = \dot{x}\,\Big( \hat{\imath} + \frac{x}{a}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big),
\qquad
\delta P = \delta x\,\Big( \hat{\imath} + \frac{x}{a}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big)
$$
$$
f(P) = \frac{x^2}{2a} - y,
\qquad
\operatorname{grad} f = \frac{x}{a}\,\hat{\imath} - \hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{\Phi} = \frac{\Phi}{\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{x^2}{a^2}}}\,\Big( -\frac{x}{a}\,\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath} \Big)
$$
$$
\vec{\Phi} \parallel \hat{\jmath}
\iff
\frac{x}{a} = 0
\iff
x = 0
$$

Check of the orthogonality between reaction and virtual displacement:
$$
\Big( -\frac{x}{a}\,\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath} \Big) \cdot \Big( \hat{\imath} + \frac{x}{a}\,\hat{\jmath} \Big)\,\delta x = \Big( -\frac{x}{a} + \frac{x}{a} \Big)\,\delta x = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide is the graph of a function, therefore the abscissa $x$ identifies the point without ambiguity and is taken as Lagrangian coordinate; the system has one degree of freedom. The velocity is obtained by differentiating the two coordinates of $P$ with respect to time, and the derivative of $\dfrac{x^2}{2a}$ equals $\dfrac{x}{a}\,\dot{x}$. The virtual displacement has the same direction, because the constraint is fixed.

The vector $\hat{\imath} + \dfrac{x}{a}\,\hat{\jmath}$ is tangent to the guide, since its slope $\dfrac{x}{a}$ is the derivative of the function that describes the guide. The guide is smooth, hence the reaction is orthogonal to the tangent; the vector $-\dfrac{x}{a}\,\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}$ is orthogonal to the tangent, because the scalar product of the two vectors is zero, and the unit vector of the reaction is obtained by dividing by the magnitude $\sqrt{1 + \dfrac{x^2}{a^2}}$.

The reaction is vertical if and only if its horizontal component vanishes, that is, if and only if $x = 0$, hence the reaction **is vertical only at the vertex** $V = (0, 0)$, where the tangent to the guide is horizontal. At every other point the guide also exerts a horizontal thrust.

The check verifies directly the orthogonality between the direction of the reaction and the virtual displacement, which turns out to be true for every $x$; the virtual work of the reaction is therefore zero and the constraint is ideal.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The material point $P$ in the figure is constrained to the smooth and fixed spherical surface of center $O$ and radius $R$, with a bilateral constraint. Determine the number of degrees of freedom, choose the Lagrangian coordinates, compute the magnitude of the velocity of $P$ and establish the direction of the constraint reaction.

::struttura{linee="2.00,0.00 1.97,0.35 1.88,0.68 1.73,1.00 1.53,1.29 1.29,1.53 1.00,1.73 0.68,1.88 0.35,1.97 0.00,2.00 -0.35,1.97 -0.68,1.88 -1.00,1.73 -1.29,1.53 -1.53,1.29 -1.73,1.00 -1.88,0.68 -1.97,0.35 -2.00,0.00 -1.97,-0.35 -1.88,-0.68 -1.73,-1.00 -1.53,-1.29 -1.29,-1.53 -1.00,-1.73 -0.68,-1.88 -0.35,-1.97 -0.00,-2.00 0.35,-1.97 0.68,-1.88 1.00,-1.73 1.29,-1.53 1.53,-1.29 1.73,-1.00 1.88,-0.68 1.97,-0.35 2.00,-0.00" nodi="O,0,0,-;P,0.68,1.88,-" punti="0,0,O,pieno;0.68,1.88,P,pieno" tratteggi="0,0 0.68,1.88;0,0 0,2.3" reazioni="0.68 1.88,0.31,0.85,Φ" frecce="0.68,1.88,1.65,1.53,δP" etichette="0.2,1.05,R;0.22,1.5,ϑ" titolo="Point on a smooth spherical surface: the reaction is radial; the figure shows the plane of the meridian"}

$$
n = 3 - 1 = 2,
\qquad
\vartheta = \text{colatitude},
\qquad
\varphi = \text{longitude}
$$
$$
P - O = R\,\big( \sin\vartheta\cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\vartheta\sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{k} \big)
$$
$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial \vartheta} = R\,\big( \cos\vartheta\cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} - \sin\vartheta\,\hat{k} \big),
\qquad
\frac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi} = R\sin\vartheta\,\big( -\sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = \frac{\partial P}{\partial \vartheta}\,\dot{\vartheta} + \frac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi}\,\dot{\varphi},
\qquad
|\vec{v}_P| = R\,\sqrt{\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \dot{\varphi}^2\sin^2\vartheta}
$$
$$
\vec{\Phi} = \frac{\Phi}{R}\,(P - O)
$$

Check of the orthogonality between the radial direction and the virtual displacements:
$$
(P - O) \cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial \vartheta} = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial \vartheta}\,|P - O|^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial R^2}{\partial \vartheta} = 0,
\qquad
(P - O) \cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi} = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial R^2}{\partial \varphi} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point has three coordinates in space and the constraint imposes the single equation $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = R^2$, therefore there remain $3 - 1 = 2$ degrees of freedom. As Lagrangian coordinates one takes the colatitude $\vartheta$, that is, the angle between $P - O$ and the $z$ axis, and the longitude $\varphi$, that is, the angle between the projection of $P - O$ on the plane $Oxy$ and the $x$ axis; they are the spherical coordinates with fixed radius.

The velocity is a combination of the two partial derivatives, with the generalized velocities as coefficients. The two vectors $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial \vartheta}$ and $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi}$ are orthogonal to each other, since their scalar product equals $-R^2\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta\cos\varphi\sin\varphi + R^2\cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta\sin\varphi\cos\varphi = 0$, and they have magnitudes $R$ and $R\sin\vartheta$; the square of the magnitude of the velocity is therefore the sum of the squares of the two components, whence the expression $|\vec{v}_P| = R\sqrt{\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \dot{\varphi}^2\sin^2\vartheta}$.

The constraint is smooth and bilateral, hence the reaction is parallel to $\operatorname{grad}(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - R^2) = 2\,(P - O)$, hence **it is radial** and has a single scalar unknown.

The check shows that the radial direction is orthogonal to both vectors $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial \vartheta}$ and $\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial \varphi}$, hence to every virtual displacement, which is a linear combination of them. It is obtained by differentiating the identity $|P - O|^2 = R^2$ with respect to each coordinate, whose right-hand side is constant.

</div>
