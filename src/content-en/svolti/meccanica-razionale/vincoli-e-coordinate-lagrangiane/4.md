## Exercise 1

The homogeneous disk in the figure, of radius $R$, rolls without slipping on the fixed horizontal guide $y = 0$ of the vertical plane $Oxy$. At the initial instant the center $C$ lies on the $y$ axis and the point $P$ of the rim is in contact with the guide. Write the pure rolling constraint, choose the Lagrangian coordinate, determine the trajectory of $P$ and compute the velocity of $P$ and that of the highest point of the disk.

::struttura{cerchi="2,1,1" linee="-0.4,0 7,0" tratteggi="0.00,0.00 0.00,0.03 0.02,0.12 0.07,0.27 0.16,0.46 0.30,0.68 0.50,0.93 0.77,1.18 1.09,1.42 1.47,1.63 1.90,1.80 2.37,1.92 2.86,1.99 3.36,1.99 3.85,1.94 4.32,1.82 4.76,1.65 5.14,1.45 5.48,1.21 5.75,0.96 5.96,0.72 6.11,0.49 6.21,0.29 6.26,0.14 6.28,0.04 6.28,0.00" punti="2,1,C,baricentro;2,0,H,centro;1.09,1.42,P,pieno;2,2,T,pieno" coppie="2 1,1,ϑ" quote="0 0,2 0,x_C,0.9" riferimento=0 etichette="-0.35,0.3,O" titolo="Disk rolling without slipping: the point $P$ of the rim travels along a cycloid"}

$$
\dot{x}_C + R\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x_C + R\,\vartheta = 0
$$
$$
q = \vartheta,
\qquad
C - O = -R\,\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + R\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
P - C = R\,\Big[ \cos\Big( \vartheta - \frac{\pi}{2} \Big)\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\Big( \vartheta - \frac{\pi}{2} \Big)\,\hat{\jmath} \Big] = R\,\big( \sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
P - O = R\,\big( -\vartheta + \sin\vartheta \big)\,\hat{\imath} + R\,\big( 1 - \cos\vartheta \big)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = R\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\big( \cos\vartheta - 1 \big)\,\hat{\imath} + R\,\dot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath},
\qquad
|\vec{v}_P| = 2R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|\,\Big| \sin\frac{\vartheta}{2} \Big|
$$
$$
\vec{v}_T = \vec{v}_C + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge \big( R\,\hat{\jmath} \big) = -R\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{\imath} - R\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{\imath} = 2\,\vec{v}_C
$$

Check in the notable configurations:
$$
\vec{v}_P(0) = \vec{0},
\qquad
\vec{v}_P(\pi) = -2R\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{\imath} = \vec{v}_T
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk stays in contact with the guide, hence its center keeps the height $R$ and the contact constraint imposes $y_C = R$. The **theorem on pure rolling** gives the second condition, $\dot{x}_C + R\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0$, which integrates to $x_C + R\,\vartheta = \text{constant}$; the constant is zero, since at the initial instant $x_C = 0$ and $\vartheta$ is measured starting from that instant. One degree of freedom remains and $\vartheta$ is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

The vector $P - C$ rotates with the disk. At the initial instant it equals $-R\,\hat{\jmath}$, that is, it forms the angle $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ with the $x$ axis, and after a rotation by $\vartheta$ it forms the angle $\vartheta - \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Adding $P - C$ to the position of the center one obtains the position of $P$, which describes a **cycloid**, that is, the curve traced by a point of the rim of a rolling disk.

The velocity of $P$ is obtained by differentiating, and its magnitude simplifies with the half-angle formulas, since $(\cos\vartheta - 1)^2 + \sin^2\vartheta = 2 - 2\cos\vartheta = 4\sin^2\dfrac{\vartheta}{2}$. The velocity of the highest point $T$ is obtained from the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics applied to the points $C$ and $T$, and it turns out to be twice the velocity of the center, because $T$ is twice as far from the contact point as $C$ is.

The check verifies two known configurations. For $\vartheta = 0$ the point $P$ is in contact with the guide and its velocity **is zero**, in agreement with the pure rolling constraint; for $\vartheta = \pi$ the point $P$ is the highest point of the disk and its velocity coincides with $\vec{v}_T$, that is, with twice the velocity of the center.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The homogeneous disk in the figure, of radius $r$, rolls without slipping inside a fixed circular guide of center $O$ and radius $R > r$, lying in a vertical plane. Let $\varphi$ be the angle that the vector $C - O$ forms with the downward vertical, where $C$ is the center of the disk, and let $\vartheta$ be the rotation angle of the disk, positive counterclockwise. Determine the number of degrees of freedom and the relation between $\vartheta$ and $\varphi$, and write it in the case $R = 3r$.

::struttura{linee="3.00,0.00 2.96,0.47 2.85,0.93 2.67,1.36 2.43,1.76 2.12,2.12 1.76,2.43 1.36,2.67 0.93,2.85 0.47,2.96 0.00,3.00 -0.47,2.96 -0.93,2.85 -1.36,2.67 -1.76,2.43 -2.12,2.12 -2.43,1.76 -2.67,1.36 -2.85,0.93 -2.96,0.47 -3.00,0.00 -2.96,-0.47 -2.85,-0.93 -2.67,-1.36 -2.43,-1.76 -2.12,-2.12 -1.76,-2.43 -1.36,-2.67 -0.93,-2.85 -0.47,-2.96 -0.00,-3.00 0.47,-2.96 0.93,-2.85 1.36,-2.67 1.76,-2.43 2.12,-2.12 2.43,-1.76 2.67,-1.36 2.85,-0.93 2.96,-0.47 3.00,-0.00" cerchi="1.15,-1.64,1" nodi="O,0,0,-" punti="0,0,O,pieno;1.15,-1.64,C,baricentro;1.72,-2.46,A,centro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-3.2;0,0 1.72,-2.46" coppie="1.15 -1.64,1,ϑ" etichette="0.28,-2.2,φ;-1.6,1.4,R;1.1,-1.1,r" titolo="Disk of radius $r$ rolling inside a circular guide of radius $R$: the contact is at $A$"}

$$
n = 3 - 2 = 1
$$
$$
C - O = (R - r)\,\big( \sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} \big),
\qquad
A - O = R\,\big( \sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_C = (R - r)\,\dot{\varphi}\,\big( \cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_A = \vec{v}_C + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge (A - C) = \big[ (R - r)\,\dot{\varphi} + r\,\dot{\vartheta} \big]\,\big( \cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} \big)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_A = \vec{0}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\dot{\vartheta} = -\frac{R - r}{r}\,\dot{\varphi}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\vartheta = -\frac{R - r}{r}\,\varphi + \text{constant}
$$
$$
R = 3r
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\dot{\vartheta} = -2\,\dot{\varphi},
\qquad
\vartheta = -2\varphi + \text{constant}
$$

Check with the limit case $r \to R$:
$$
\lim_{r \to R} \frac{R - r}{r} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\dot{\vartheta} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk is a plane rigid body, hence it starts from three parameters; the contact with the circular guide imposes that the center be at distance $R - r$ from $O$, and the absence of slipping imposes the second condition. There remains $3 - 2 = 1$ degree of freedom, therefore $\vartheta$ and $\varphi$ are not independent.

The center of the disk travels along the circle of center $O$ and radius $R - r$, since the disk stays in contact with the guide; its velocity is obtained by differentiating the position and is tangent to that circle. The contact point $A$ lies on the guide, hence it is at distance $R$ from $O$, and the vector $A - C$ equals $r\,(\sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath})$.

The velocity of the point of the disk that is located at $A$ is obtained from the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics, with $\hat{k} \wedge (\sin\varphi\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\varphi\,\hat{\jmath}) = \cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + \sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath}$. The two contributions have the same direction, that tangent to the guide, therefore the pure rolling condition is the single equation $(R - r)\,\dot{\varphi} + r\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0$.

The left-hand side is the derivative of the function $(R - r)\,\varphi + r\,\vartheta$, hence the condition integrates and the constraint **is holonomic**. With $R = 3r$ one obtains $\dot{\vartheta} = -2\,\dot{\varphi}$, hence when the center travels through an angle $\varphi$ counterclockwise the disk rotates twice as much clockwise.

The check uses the limit case in which the radius of the disk tends to that of the guide. Then the disk fills the guide, its center does not move and the rotation tends to vanish, as the formula gives.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The disk of radius $R$ in the figure keeps vertical and rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal plane. Its configuration is identified by the coordinates $x$ and $y$ of the contact point, by the angle $\psi$ that the plane of the disk forms with the $x$ axis and by the angle of proper rotation $\varphi$. Write the constraint equations, say whether the constraint is holonomic and determine the number of configuration parameters and the number of kinematic degrees of freedom.

::struttura{nodi="A,-0.77,-0.64,-;B,0.77,0.64,-" aste="A-B" punti="0,0,H,pieno" tratteggi="0,0 2.2,0" frecce="0,0,1.38,1.15,v_C" etichette="0.9,0.22,ψ" titolo="Top view of the disk: $\psi$ is the angle of the plane of the disk with the $x$ axis, and the center advances along that direction"}

$$
\begin{cases}
\dot{x} + R\,\dot{\varphi}\cos\psi = 0 \\[4pt]
\dot{y} + R\,\dot{\varphi}\sin\psi = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\text{configuration parameters} = 4,
\qquad
\text{kinematic degrees of freedom} = 4 - 2 = 2
$$
$$
\text{maneuver:}
\qquad
\Delta x = 0,
\qquad
\Delta y = 0,
\qquad
\Delta \psi = 0,
\qquad
\Delta \varphi = -\frac{2s}{R}
$$

Check with the plane case, $\psi = 0$ constant:
$$
\dot{x} + R\,\dot{\varphi} = 0,
\qquad
\dot{y} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The contact point has zero velocity, hence the center of the disk advances in the direction of the plane of the disk with velocity of magnitude $R\,|\dot{\varphi}|$; the components of that velocity along the axes are $-R\,\dot{\varphi}\cos\psi$ and $-R\,\dot{\varphi}\sin\psi$, and the contact point moves on the plane with the same velocity as the center. One obtains in this way the two constraint equations, which contain the velocities and do not appear as derivatives of relations between the coordinates alone.

In order to establish whether the constraint is holonomic one looks for a relation between $x$, $y$, $\psi$ and $\varphi$ conserved along the admissible motions. Consider the following maneuver: the disk rolls forward through a length $s$ with $\psi = 0$, therefore $\varphi$ decreases by $\dfrac{s}{R}$; then it rotates half a turn about the vertical through the contact point, which is allowed because the two constraint equations are satisfied by $\dot{x} = \dot{y} = \dot{\varphi} = 0$ with $\dot{\psi}$ arbitrary; then it rolls again through a length $s$, returning to the starting position, and $\varphi$ decreases by another $\dfrac{s}{R}$; finally it rotates half a turn backward about the vertical, bringing $\psi$ back to the initial value.

At the end of the maneuver the coordinates $x$, $y$ and $\psi$ have resumed the initial values, whereas $\varphi$ has decreased by $\dfrac{2s}{R}$. Since $s$ is arbitrary, from the same initial configuration every value of $\varphi$ is reached with the other three coordinates unchanged; no relation between the four parameters is conserved along the admissible motions, therefore the constraint **is nonholonomic**.

The disk therefore has four configuration parameters, all free, and two kinematic degrees of freedom, since in every configuration the admissible velocities form a subspace of dimension $4 - 2 = 2$ of the space of the quadruples $(\dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{\psi}, \dot{\varphi})$.

The check recovers the plane case. With $\psi = 0$ constant the disk advances along the $x$ axis and the equations reduce to $\dot{x} + R\,\dot{\varphi} = 0$ and $\dot{y} = 0$, that is, to the holonomic constraint of the **theorem on pure rolling**. The nonholonomy arises from the possibility of changing the orientation $\psi$.

</div>
