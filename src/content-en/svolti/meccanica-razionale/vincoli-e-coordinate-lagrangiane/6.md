## Exercise 1

The rod $AB$ in the figure, of length $\ell$, has the end $A$ on a smooth vertical guide and the end $B$ on a smooth horizontal guide. Write the reactions exerted by the two guides, compute the virtual work of the reactions and establish whether the system of constraints is ideal.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,2.4;B,3.2,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,180;B,carrello,270" linee="0,-0.4 0,3.6;-0.4,0 4.2,0" reazioni="A,1.1,0,Φ_A;B,0,1.1,Φ_B" frecce="0,2.4,0,3.2,δA;3.2,0,2.35,0,δB" etichette="2.75,0.25,ϑ" titolo="Reactions of the two smooth guides and virtual displacements of the ends of the rod"}

$$
\vec{\Phi}_A = \Phi_A\,\hat{\imath},
\qquad
\vec{\Phi}_B = \Phi_B\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\delta A = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath},
\qquad
\delta B = -\ell\sin\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta\,\hat{\imath}
$$
$$
\delta L^{(v)} = \vec{\Phi}_A \cdot \delta A + \vec{\Phi}_B \cdot \delta B = \Phi_A\,\ell\cos\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta\,\big( \hat{\imath} \cdot \hat{\jmath} \big) - \Phi_B\,\ell\sin\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta\,\big( \hat{\jmath} \cdot \hat{\imath} \big) = 0
$$

Check with the numerical value $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\delta\vartheta = 1$:
$$
\delta A = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\ell\,\hat{\jmath},
\qquad
\delta B = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\ell\,\hat{\imath},
\qquad
\delta L^{(v)} = \Phi_A \cdot 0 + \Phi_B \cdot 0 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two guides are smooth and bilateral, therefore by the **theorem on the reaction of a smooth constraint** each reaction is orthogonal to its own guide, hence the vertical guide exerts a horizontal reaction at $A$, the horizontal guide exerts a vertical reaction at $B$. The two scalar unknowns $\Phi_A$ and $\Phi_B$ remain undetermined until the equations of equilibrium or of motion are written.

The virtual displacements of the ends are directed along the respective guides, since the two points must stay on the guides; they are therefore orthogonal to the respective reactions. In the scalar product $\vec{\Phi}_A \cdot \delta A$ the factor $\hat{\imath} \cdot \hat{\jmath} = 0$ appears, and the same happens in the second product.

The virtual work of the reactions **is zero for every virtual displacement**, hence the system of constraints is ideal. The principle of virtual work and the Lagrange equations, in which the reactions do not appear, can therefore be applied.

The check computes the same products in a particular configuration, with the rod inclined by $45^\circ$. The virtual displacements have magnitude $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\,\ell$ and remain orthogonal to the reactions, hence the virtual work is zero in that configuration as well.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The system in the figure is formed by the rods $AB$ and $BC$, both of length $\ell$, hinged to each other at $B$, with a fixed hinge at $A$ and a roller support at $C$ on the horizontal guide through $A$. All the constraints are smooth. List the constraint reactions, including those of the internal hinge, and compute their total virtual work.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,2.6;C,4,0" aste="A-B;B-C" cerniere="B" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello,270" linee="-0.6,0 5.2,0" reazioni="A,1,0,Φ_Ax;A,0,1,Φ_Ay;C,0,1,Φ_C;B,0.9,0.6,Φ_B;2 2.6,-0.9,-0.6,−Φ_B" frecce="4,0,3.2,0,δC" etichette="0.75,0.33,ϑ" titolo="External reactions and pair of internal reactions in the hinge $B$"}

$$
\delta A = \vec{0},
\qquad
\delta C = -2\ell\sin\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta\,\hat{\imath},
\qquad
\vec{\Phi}_C = \Phi_C\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\delta L^{(v)} = \vec{\Phi}_A \cdot \delta A + \vec{\Phi}_C \cdot \delta C + \vec{\Phi}_B \cdot \delta B + \big( -\vec{\Phi}_B \big) \cdot \delta B
$$
$$
\vec{\Phi}_A \cdot \delta A = 0,
\qquad
\vec{\Phi}_C \cdot \delta C = -2\Phi_C\,\ell\sin\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta\,\big( \hat{\jmath} \cdot \hat{\imath} \big) = 0,
\qquad
\vec{\Phi}_B \cdot \delta B - \vec{\Phi}_B \cdot \delta B = 0
$$
$$
\delta L^{(v)} = 0
$$

Check by counting the unknowns and the equations:
$$
\text{unknowns} = 2 + 1 + 2 = 5,
\qquad
\text{cardinal equations for the two bodies} = 3 + 3 = 6,
\qquad
6 - 5 = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fixed hinge at $A$ exerts the two components $\Phi_{Ax}$ and $\Phi_{Ay}$; the roller support at $C$ exerts the vertical component $\Phi_C$ alone, normal to the guide; the internal hinge at $B$ exerts two opposite forces, $\vec{\Phi}_B$ on the second rod and $-\vec{\Phi}_B$ on the first, by the principle of action and reaction.

The virtual work is computed term by term. The point $A$ is fixed, hence its virtual displacement is zero and the reaction of the hinge does no work. The point $C$ slides on the horizontal guide, hence its virtual displacement is horizontal while the reaction is vertical, therefore their scalar product is zero. In the internal hinge the two forces are opposite and are applied at the same geometric point $B$, which belongs to both rods and has a single virtual displacement, since the two rods stay connected; the two contributions **cancel each other**.

The total virtual work of the reactions is zero, hence the system of constraints is ideal. This is the reason why in the Lagrange equations of a linkage neither the external reactions nor the internal reactions of the hinges appear.

The check counts unknowns and equations. The unknown reactions are five and the cardinal equations written for each of the two rods are six, therefore there remains one equation more than the unknowns, and that equation is the equilibrium condition of the system, that is, the relation that identifies the equilibrium configurations in the single Lagrangian coordinate $\vartheta$. The count confirms that the system has one degree of freedom.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The homogeneous disk in the figure, of radius $R$, rolls without slipping on the fixed horizontal guide $y = 0$, and the contact is rough. Write the reaction that the guide exerts at the contact point, compute its virtual work and compare the result with the one obtained if the disk slides on the guide instead of rolling.

::struttura{cerchi="3,1.2,1.2" linee="-0.3,0 6.3,0" punti="3,1.2,C,baricentro;3,0,H,centro" reazioni="3 0,0,1.3,Φ_n;3 0,1.2,0,Φ_t" coppie="3 1.2,1,ϑ" frecce="3,1.2,4.2,1.2,δx_C" titolo="Reaction at the contact point: normal component $\Phi_n$ and friction component $\Phi_t$"}

$$
\vec{\Phi}_H = \Phi_t\,\hat{\imath} + \Phi_n\,\hat{\jmath},
\qquad
\Phi_n \geq 0
$$
$$
\vec{v}_H = \vec{0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta H = \vec{0}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta L^{(v)} = \vec{\Phi}_H \cdot \delta H = 0
$$
$$
\text{with slipping:}
\qquad
\delta H = \delta x_C\,\hat{\imath},
\qquad
\delta L^{(v)} = \Phi_t\,\delta x_C \neq 0
$$

Check with the velocity of the contact point:
$$
\vec{v}_H = \vec{v}_C + \dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{k} \wedge \big( -R\,\hat{\jmath} \big) = \big( \dot{x}_C + R\,\dot{\vartheta} \big)\,\hat{\imath} = \vec{0}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The contact is rough, hence the guide exerts on the disk a force with normal component $\Phi_n$, non-negative because the contact is unilateral, and tangential component $\Phi_t$, which is the friction force.

The pure rolling constraint imposes that the point of the disk in contact with the guide have zero velocity in every admissible state of motion. The constraint is fixed, therefore the virtual displacements coincide with the possible displacements up to the factor $dt$, and the virtual displacement of the contact point **is zero**. The virtual work of the reaction is then zero and the constraint is ideal, notwithstanding the presence of friction, as the **theorem on ideal pure rolling** establishes.

If instead the disk slides on the guide, the point of the disk in contact has nonzero velocity and its virtual displacement is horizontal, of component $\delta x_C$. The virtual work of the friction component is then $\Phi_t\,\delta x_C$, which does not vanish; the constraint is not ideal and friction is treated as an assigned active force.

The difference between the two cases lies in the point of application of the friction force. In rolling that point has zero velocity, hence the force does no work, whereas in sliding that point moves with respect to the guide.

The check obtains the velocity of the contact point from the fundamental formula of rigid kinematics and sets it to zero, recovering the constraint $\dot{x}_C + R\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ on which the reasoning is based.

</div>
