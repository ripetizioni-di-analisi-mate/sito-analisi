## Exercise 1

The pulley in the figure is a homogeneous disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R$, hinged at the center $C$ to a smooth fixed horizontal axis. An inextensible string of negligible mass is wound on the pulley, does not slip on it and holds at its other endpoint the material point $P$ of mass $m$. Compute the acceleration of $P$.

::struttura{nodi="C,0,0,-;Q,1.2,0,-;P,1.2,-2.6,-" cerchi="0,0,1.2" vincoli="C,cerniera,90" catene="Q-P" punti="0,0,C,centro;1.2,-2.6,P" etichette="-0.95,-0.75,2m;1.45,-2.25,m" quote="Q,P,y,-0.75" titolo="Pulley with a fixed axis and material point hanging from the string"}

The string is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, hence the system has one degree of freedom; the descent $y$ of $P$, measured downwards from a reference configuration, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
v_P = |\dot{y}| ,
\qquad
|\dot{\varphi}| = \frac{|\dot{y}|}{R} ,
\qquad
I_C = \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2 = m\,R^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\frac{\dot{y}^2}{R^2} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 = m\,\dot{y}^2
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_P = m\,g\,y
$$
$$
2\,m\,\ddot{y} = m\,g
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{y} = \frac{g}{2}
$$

Check with the cardinal equations. With $\mathcal{T}$ the magnitude of the tension of the string, the first cardinal equation for $P$ projected on the downward vertical and the second one for the pulley with pole at $C$ give
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{y} = m\,g - \mathcal{T} \\[4pt]
m\,R^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{y}}{R} = \mathcal{T}\,R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\mathcal{T} = m\,\ddot{y} ,
\qquad
m\,\ddot{y} = m\,g - m\,\ddot{y}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{y} = \frac{g}{2} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = \frac{m\,g}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, therefore the portion of string that unwinds is equal to the arc travelled by the point of the pulley in contact with it. It follows that $v_P = R\,|\dot{\varphi}|$ and the system has **one degree of freedom**.

The kinetic energy is the sum of that of the material point and that of the pulley, which rotates about the fixed axis through $C$ with moment of inertia $I_C = \dfrac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2$. Substituting $\dot{\varphi} = \dfrac{\dot{y}}{R}$ the radius cancels: the result does not depend on $R$.

The weight of the pulley has zero moment with respect to the fixed axis, because the center of mass coincides with $C$, and the reaction of the hinge does no virtual work; only the weight of $P$ therefore remains in the potential. With $y$ measured downwards the height of $P$ is $-y$, therefore $U = m\,g\,y$.

The acceleration is $\dfrac{g}{2}$, that is **half the acceleration of free fall**: the pulley absorbs kinetic energy in the rotation, hence the point descends more slowly than a free falling body. The result does not contain $R$, because increasing the radius makes the moment of inertia and the arm of the tension grow in the same way.

In the check the equation of the point and that of the pulley are written separately, with the tension as unknown. Eliminating $\mathcal{T}$ gives back the same result; in addition the cardinal equations give the tension, $\mathcal{T} = \dfrac{m\,g}{2}$, which Lagrange's method does not provide because the inextensibility constraint is ideal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The pulley in the figure is a homogeneous disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R$ hinged at the center $K$ to a smooth fixed horizontal axis. An inextensible string of negligible mass, which does not slip on the pulley, connects the material point $P_1$ of mass $m$, resting on a smooth horizontal plane, to the material point $P_2$ of mass $3m$, which hangs vertically. Compute the acceleration of the two points.

::struttura{nodi="K,0,0,-;T,0,1,-;B,-3.5,1,-;Q,1,0,-;P,1,-2.6,-" cerchi="0,0,1" vincoli="K,cerniera,270" catene="B-T;Q-P" linee="-5.4,0.9 -0.1,0.9" punti="-3.5,1,P_1;1,-2.6,P_2;0,0,K,centro" etichette="-3.5,1.45,m;1.5,-2.95,3m;-0.85,-0.8,2m" titolo="Two material points connected by the string wound on the pulley of mass $2m$"}

The string is inextensible and does not slip on the pulley, hence the system has one degree of freedom; the descent $y$ of $P_2$ is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
v_1 = v_2 = |\dot{y}| ,
\qquad
|\dot{\varphi}| = \frac{|\dot{y}|}{R} ,
\qquad
I_K = \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,R^2 = m\,R^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,(3m)\,\dot{y}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{\dot{y}^2}{R^2} = \frac{5}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2
$$
$$
U = 3\,m\,g\,y
$$
$$
5\,m\,\ddot{y} = 3\,m\,g
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{y} = \frac{3}{5}\,g
$$

Check with the cardinal equations. With $\mathcal{T}_1$ and $\mathcal{T}_2$ the tensions in the two portions of string,
$$
\begin{cases}
3\,m\,\ddot{y} = 3\,m\,g - \mathcal{T}_2 \\[4pt]
m\,\ddot{y} = \mathcal{T}_1 \\[4pt]
m\,R^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{y}}{R} = \big( \mathcal{T}_2 - \mathcal{T}_1 \big)\,R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
5\,m\,\ddot{y} = 3\,m\,g ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T}_1 = \frac{3}{5}\,m\,g ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T}_2 = \frac{6}{5}\,m\,g
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two portions of string are inextensible and the string does not slip on the pulley, therefore the two points have velocities of equal magnitude and the angular velocity of the pulley is $\dfrac{|\dot{y}|}{R}$: the system has **one degree of freedom**.

Three summands appear in the kinetic energy, one for each body. The plane is horizontal and smooth, hence the weight of $P_1$ is balanced by the reaction of the plane and does not appear in the potential; the weight of $P_2$ remains, which descends by the height $y$.

The acceleration $\dfrac{3}{5}\,g$ is smaller than the one that would be obtained with a pulley of negligible mass, which is $\dfrac{3}{4}\,g$: the mass of the pulley adds the term $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2$ to the kinetic energy and **slows down** the motion.

In the check two distinct tensions appear, because the pulley has mass and its equation of rotational motion requires a non-zero resultant moment. The pole of the second cardinal equation is chosen at $K$, where the reaction of the hinge and the weight of the pulley are applied, which have zero moment with respect to that point. Adding the three equations eliminates the tensions and gives back Lagrange's equation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The homogeneous disk of center $C$, mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide. An inextensible string of negligible mass is wound on the disk, unwinds from the highest point, stays horizontal up to an ideal pulley of negligible mass and holds the material point $P$ of mass $m$, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the acceleration of the center of the disk.

::struttura{nodi="S,2.5,2,-;N,6.2,2,-;Q,6.5,1.7,-;P,6.5,-1,-" cerchi="2.5,1,1;6.2,1.7,0.3,foro" linee="-0.5,0 5.2,0" catene="S-N;Q-P" punti="2.5,1,C,baricentro;2.5,0,H,centro;6.5,-1,P;6.2,1.7,K" frecce="2.5,1,3.75,1,x" etichette="1.4,1.5,m;6.95,-1.3,m" titolo="Rolling disk, with the string unwinding from the top and the point $P$ hanging from the pulley"}

The pure rolling constraint and the inextensibility of the string leave one degree of freedom; the abscissa $x$ of the center $C$, positive towards the pulley, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
|\dot{\varphi}| = \frac{|\dot{x}|}{R} ,
\qquad
v_S = 2\,|\dot{x}| ,
\qquad
v_P = v_S = 2\,|\dot{x}|
$$
$$
T = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{\dot{x}^2}{R^2}}_{\text{disk}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,m\,(2\dot{x})^2}_{\text{point}}
= \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + 2\,m\,\dot{x}^2 = \frac{11}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2
$$
$$
U = 2\,m\,g\,x
$$
$$
\frac{11}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} = 2\,m\,g
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{4}{11}\,g
$$

Check with the cardinal equations. With $\mathcal{T}$ the magnitude of the tension of the string, the second cardinal equation for the disk with pole at the contact point $H$ and the first one for the point $P$ give
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{x}}{R} = \mathcal{T}\,(2R) \\[8pt]
m\,(2\ddot{x}) = m\,g - \mathcal{T}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\mathcal{T} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\ddot{x} ,
\qquad
2\,m\,\ddot{x} = m\,g - \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\ddot{x}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{11}{4}\,m\,\ddot{x} = m\,g ,
\qquad
\ddot{x} = \frac{4}{11}\,g
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $S$ from which the string unwinds is the highest point of the disk. In the act of motion the contact point $H$ has zero velocity, hence $H$ is the instantaneous center of rotation and the velocity of $S$ has magnitude equal to the angular velocity times the distance $HS = 2R$, that is $v_S = 2\,|\dot{x}|$. The string is inextensible, therefore $P$ descends with the same velocity: $v_P = 2\,|\dot{x}|$.

This factor $2$ is the delicate point of the exercise. The square of the velocity of $P$, that is $4\,\dot{x}^2$, enters the kinetic energy, and the descent of $P$ in the potential is $2x$: in both cases **twice** the displacement of the center.

The kinetic energy of the disk is written with König's theorem, as in the exercises on pure rolling; the pulley and the string do not contribute, given that their mass is negligible.

The acceleration $\dfrac{4}{11}\,g$ is smaller than $\dfrac{2}{5}\,g$, the value that would be obtained if the string were attached to the center of the disk: unwinding from the top, the string makes $P$ descend by twice the displacement of the center, therefore for the same weight the mass in motion is larger.

In the check the pole of the second cardinal equation is the contact point, where the reaction of the guide has zero moment; the moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $H$ is $I_H = \dfrac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2$ by the Huygens-Steiner theorem, and the tension has arm $2R$. Eliminating $\mathcal{T}$ between the two equations gives back Lagrange's equation.

</div>
