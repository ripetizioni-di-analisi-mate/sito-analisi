## Exercise 1

The homogeneous disk of center $C$, mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, in the vertical plane. A spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length connects the center $C$ to the point $O$ of the line described by $C$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Write the equation of motion and the period of the oscillations.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,1;C,3.5,1,-" cerchi="3.5,1,1" linee="-0.5,0 6.5,0;0,1 0.4,1.25 0.95,0.75 1.5,1.25 2.05,0.75 2.45,1 2.5,1" punti="3.5,1,C,baricentro;3.5,0,H,centro" etichette="1.2,1.55,k" quote="0 0,3.5 0,x,0.75" titolo="Disk rolling without slipping, pulled back by the spring of constant $k$"}

The pure rolling constraint on a fixed guide leaves one degree of freedom and the abscissa $x$ of the center $C$, measured from $O$, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
|\dot{\varphi}| = \frac{|\dot{x}|}{R} ,
\qquad
I_C = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I_C\,\dot{\varphi}^2
= \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{x}^2 + \frac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,\frac{\dot{x}^2}{R^2}
= \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{x}^2
$$
$$
U = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2
$$
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{x}} = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial T}{\partial x} = 0 ,
\qquad
Q_x = \frac{\partial U}{\partial x} = -k\,x
$$
$$
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{x} = -\frac{2\,k}{3\,m}\,x
$$
$$
\omega^2 = \frac{2\,k}{3\,m} ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\,\sqrt{\frac{3\,m}{2\,k}}
$$

Check with the cardinal equations. With $\Phi_T$ the tangential component of the reaction of the guide, the first cardinal equation projected on the horizontal and the second one with pole at $C$ give
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x + \Phi_T \\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dfrac{\ddot{x}}{R} = -\Phi_T\,R
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\Phi_T = -\frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} ,
\qquad
m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x}
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{x} = -k\,x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk has three configuration parameters in the plane, but the pure rolling constraint requires the contact point to have zero velocity, hence two conditions remain and the system has **one degree of freedom**. The abscissa of the center is chosen as coordinate because the potential of the spring is written directly as a function of it.

Pure rolling links the magnitude of the angular velocity to that of the velocity of the center by $|\dot{\varphi}| = \dfrac{|\dot{x}|}{R}$, as obtained in the topic on the kinematics of the rigid body. The square of $\dot{\varphi}$ appears in the kinetic energy, therefore the sign of the rotation has no effect on the computation.

The kinetic energy is written with König's theorem: the first summand is that of the center of mass, the second is that of the relative rotational motion, with $I_C = \dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2$ the moment of inertia of the disk with respect to the axis through the center orthogonal to the plane of the motion.

The weight is orthogonal to the displacement of the center and does zero work, hence only the spring appears in the potential; with zero rest length its length coincides with $x$ and one has $U = -\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,x^2$.

The equation obtained is that of the **harmonic oscillator**, therefore the motion is harmonic with angular frequency $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k}{3\,m}}$ and period $2\pi\,\sqrt{\dfrac{3\,m}{2\,k}}$. The period is larger than that of the material point of mass $m$ with the same spring, since part of the kinetic energy is due to the rotation of the disk.

In the check two cardinal equations appear together with the unknown $\Phi_T$, which is the tangential component of the reaction of the guide. The pole of the second equation is chosen at $C$ because the normal reaction and the weight have zero moment with respect to that point. Eliminating $\Phi_T$ between the two equations gives back Lagrange's equation; the component $\Phi_T$ is different from zero, and nevertheless it does not appear in Lagrange's method, since the contact point has zero velocity and the constraint is therefore ideal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The homogeneous disk of center $C$, mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed plane inclined at an angle $\alpha$, in the vertical plane. A spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length connects $C$ to the fixed point $O$ of the line parallel to the inclined plane described by $C$, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the equation of motion and find the configuration in which the system can remain at rest.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 6,0 0,3.464" cerchi="3.048,2.743,0.9" linee="0.45,4.243 0.7915,4.3345 0.9745,3.6515 1.6575,3.8345 1.8405,3.1515 2.5235,3.3345 2.7065,2.6515 3.048,2.743" punti="0.45,4.243,O;3.048,2.743,C,baricentro;2.598,1.964,H,centro" frecce="3.048,2.743,4.0,2.193,s" etichette="4.9,0.25,α;1.9,4.15,k" titolo="Disk rolling on an inclined plane, connected to the fixed point $O$ by a spring of constant $k$"}

The system has one degree of freedom and the curvilinear abscissa $s$ of the center $C$ along the line parallel to the inclined plane, measured from $O$ downwards, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{s}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 \cdot \frac{\dot{s}^2}{R^2} = \frac{3}{4}\,m\,\dot{s}^2
$$
$$
y_C = -s\sin\alpha + \text{const.} ,
\qquad
U = -m\,g\,y_C - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2 = m\,g\,s\sin\alpha - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2
$$
$$
Q_s = \frac{\partial U}{\partial s} = m\,g\sin\alpha - k\,s
$$
$$
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\sin\alpha - k\,s
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{s} = \frac{2}{3}\,g\sin\alpha - \frac{2\,k}{3\,m}\,s
$$
$$
Q_s = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
s_e = \frac{m\,g\sin\alpha}{k}
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation, pole at the contact point $H$, which is the instantaneous center of rotation:
$$
I_H = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2 + m\,R^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,R^2 ,
\qquad
I_H\,\frac{\ddot{s}}{R} = \big( m\,g\sin\alpha - k\,s \big)\,R
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\sin\alpha - k\,s
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pure rolling constraint leaves one degree of freedom, hence the coordinate $s$ is enough. The abscissa $s$ is measured along the line described by the center, which is parallel to the inclined plane and at distance $R$ from it; both $O$ and $C$ lie on that line, therefore the length of the spring coincides with $s$.

The kinetic energy is the same as in the previous exercise, since the pure rolling constraint links the velocity of the center to the angular velocity in the same way on any fixed guide, whatever its inclination.

The center descends by the height $s\sin\alpha$ when $s$ grows, therefore the potential of the weight is $m\,g\,s\sin\alpha$; the potential of the spring, $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2$, adds to it. The additive constant in the height has no effect, since only the derivative of $U$ appears in the equations.

The equation of motion is that of a harmonic oscillator with a constant term: setting $\sigma = s - s_e$ gives $\ddot{\sigma} = -\dfrac{2\,k}{3\,m}\,\sigma$, hence the center oscillates about the configuration $s_e$ with angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,k}{3\,m}}$.

The configuration of rest is found by making the Lagrangian component of the load vanish, since in it the right-hand side of the equation is zero and the system, initially at rest, remains at rest. The result is $s_e = \dfrac{m\,g\sin\alpha}{k}$: the spring **stretches** until its force balances the component of the weight along the plane.

In the check the pole of the second cardinal equation is the contact point $H$. At that instant $H$ is the instantaneous center of rotation and the moment of inertia with respect to the axis through $H$ is obtained from the Huygens-Steiner theorem, $I_H = I_C + m\,R^2$. With respect to $H$ the reaction of the guide has zero moment, whereas the component of the weight along the plane and the elastic force have arm $R$; the resulting equation coincides with Lagrange's one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The homogeneous disk of center $C$, mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal guide, in the vertical plane; a material point of mass $m$ is welded at the point $P$ of its rim, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the equation of motion.

::struttura{cerchi="3,1.3,1.3" linee="-0.5,0 7,0" tratteggi="3,1.3 3,0;3,1.3 3.996,0.465" punti="3,1.3,C,baricentro;3.996,0.465,P;3,0,H,centro" forze="3.996 0.465,0,-0.65,mg" etichette="3.22,0.66,ϑ" titolo="Disk rolling with the material point $P$ welded on the rim"}

The system has one degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ by which the disk is rotated, positive counterclockwise, starting from the configuration in which $P$ touches the guide, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
C - O = -R\,\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + R\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
P - O = R\,(\sin\vartheta - \vartheta)\,\hat{\imath} + R\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = R\,(\cos\vartheta - 1)\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{\imath} + R\sin\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
v_P^2 = 2\,R^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
T = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2}_{\text{disk}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\,m\,v_P^2}_{\text{point}}
= \frac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,(7 - 4\cos\vartheta)\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_P = -m\,g\,R\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)
$$
$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,(7 - 4\cos\vartheta)\,\dot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,(7 - 4\cos\vartheta)\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\,m\,R^2\sin\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta} = m\,R^2\sin\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
Q_\vartheta = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = -m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,(7 - 4\cos\vartheta)\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,R^2\sin\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta = 0
$$

Check with the energy integral. The constraints are fixed and the load is conservative, hence
$$
E = \frac{1}{4}\,m\,R^2\,(7 - 4\cos\vartheta)\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + m\,g\,R\,(1 - \cos\vartheta) = \text{const.}
$$
$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,(7 - 4\cos\vartheta)\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\ddot{\vartheta} + m\,R^2\sin\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^3 + m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0 ,
$$
which, divided by $\dot{\vartheta}$, gives back the equation of motion.

<div class="spiegazione">

The pure rolling constraint leaves one degree of freedom and the angle of rotation is chosen as coordinate, because both the position of the center and that of the welded point depend on it. The pure rolling condition gives $x_C = -R\,\vartheta$: for $\vartheta > 0$, that is for a counterclockwise rotation, the disk advances towards the left.

The position of $P$ is obtained by adding to the position of the center the vector $P - C$, which rotates with the disk; in the initial configuration $P$ lies below the center, hence $P - C = R\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - R\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}$. Differentiating with respect to time gives the velocity, and the square of its magnitude is simplified with the identity $\sin^2\vartheta + \cos^2\vartheta = 1$.

The kinetic energy of the disk is the sum of the term of the center of mass, with $|\dot{x}_C| = R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$, and of the rotational term; the kinetic energy of the material point adds to it. The coefficient depends on $\vartheta$, hence the **kinetic matrix is not constant** and the equation contains the term quadratic in the velocities.

The weight of the disk does not appear in the potential, because the height of its center of mass remains constant; the weight of the point $P$ remains, whose height is $R\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)$.

Here the derivative $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta}$ is different from zero, therefore Lagrange's equation contains all three terms. In the left-hand side the general structure is recognized: the coefficient of the second derivative is the only entry of the kinetic matrix, and the term in $\dot{\vartheta}^2$ comes from its dependence on the configuration.

In the check the mechanical energy is differentiated with respect to time. The result is zero by virtue of the equation of motion, and conversely from the derivative of the energy the equation is recovered by dividing by $\dot{\vartheta}$: the check is independent, because the energy integral is written directly from $T$ and from $V$ without going through the derivatives of the Lagrangian.

</div>
