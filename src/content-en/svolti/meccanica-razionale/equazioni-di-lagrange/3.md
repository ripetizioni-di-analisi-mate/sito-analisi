## Exercise 1

The material point $P$ of mass $m$ slides on a smooth vertical straight guide and is connected to the fixed point $O$ of the guide by a spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the equation of motion and the height at which the point can remain at rest.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,0,-2" linee="0,1.4 0,-3.6;0,0 0.38,-0.3 -0.38,-0.72 0.38,-1.14 -0.38,-1.56 0,-1.82 0,-2" etichette="0.62,-0.9,k;0.35,-2.15,m" quote="O,P,y,1.1" titolo="Material point on a smooth vertical guide, pulled back by the spring of constant $k$"}

The point has one degree of freedom and the ordinate $y$ of $P$ is taken as Lagrangian coordinate, with the origin at $O$ and the $y$ axis vertical and upwards.

$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{y}^2
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,y^2
$$
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{y}} = m\,\ddot{y} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial T}{\partial y} = 0 ,
\qquad
Q_y = \frac{\partial U}{\partial y} = -m\,g - k\,y
$$
$$
m\,\ddot{y} = -m\,g - k\,y
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{y} = -\frac{k}{m}\left( y + \frac{m\,g}{k} \right)
$$
$$
Q_y = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
y_e = -\frac{m\,g}{k}
$$

Check with the first cardinal equation projected on the vertical. The active forces are the weight $-m\,g\,\hat{\jmath}$ and the elastic force $-k\,y\,\hat{\jmath}$, whereas the reaction of the smooth guide is horizontal:
$$
m\,\ddot{y} = -m\,g - k\,y
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide is straight, therefore the point has one degree of freedom and the ordinate, which determines the position on the guide, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

The active forces are the weight and the elastic force, both conservative. The potential of the weight is $-m\,g\,y$, that of the spring is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2$ with $\ell$ the length of the spring; the rest length is zero and the spring has one endpoint at $O$, hence $\ell = |y|$ and the elastic potential is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,y^2$.

The equation obtained is rewritten by setting $\eta = y - y_e$, with $y_e = -\dfrac{m\,g}{k}$: one finds $\ddot{\eta} = -\dfrac{k}{m}\,\eta$, hence the point oscillates with **harmonic motion** about $y_e$ with angular frequency $\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$. The weight does not change the angular frequency and only shifts the center of the oscillations.

The height $y_e$ is negative, that is $P$ lies below $O$: in that configuration the spring is stretched by $\dfrac{m\,g}{k}$ and its force balances the weight.

In the check the first cardinal equation is written for the point. The reaction of the smooth guide is orthogonal to the guide, hence it is horizontal and does not appear in the vertical projection; the weight and the elastic force remain, and Lagrange's equation is recovered. The comparison also shows why the reaction does not appear in Lagrange's method: its virtual work is zero, since the virtual displacements are vertical.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The material point $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to a smooth fixed circular guide, of center $O$ and radius $R$, placed in a vertical plane. A spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length connects $P$ to the highest point $A$ of the guide, as the diagram in the figure shows. Write the equation of motion and discuss it as $k$ varies.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" linee="0,2 0.3583,1.8531 0.1877,1.0979 0.9043,0.8043 0.7337,0.0491 1.4503,-0.2447 1.2798,-0.9999 1.638,-1.147" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2;0,0 1.638,-1.147" punti="0,0,O;0,2,A;1.638,-1.147,P" etichette="0.42,-1.5,ϑ;1.45,0.75,k;1.78,-1.42,m" titolo="Point constrained to a smooth circular guide, connected to the point $A$ by the spring of constant $k$"}

The point has one degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ between the radius $OP$ and the downward vertical is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
P - O = R\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - R\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
A - O = R\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
v_P^2 = R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
|P - A|^2 = R^2\sin^2\vartheta + R^2\,(\cos\vartheta + 1)^2 = 2\,R^2\,(1 + \cos\vartheta)
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_P - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,|P - A|^2 = m\,g\,R\cos\vartheta - k\,R^2\,(1 + \cos\vartheta)
$$
$$
Q_\vartheta = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = \big( k\,R^2 - m\,g\,R \big) \sin\vartheta
$$
$$
m\,R^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = \big( k\,R^2 - m\,g\,R \big) \sin\vartheta
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} = \left( \frac{k}{m} - \frac{g}{R} \right) \sin\vartheta
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation, pole at $O$. The elastic force is $\vec{F} = -k\,(P - A)$, with $P - A = R\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - R\,(\cos\vartheta + 1)\,\hat{\jmath}$, therefore
$$
M_O^{(F)} = (P - O) \wedge \vec{F} \cdot \hat{k} = -k\,R^2 \big[ -\sin\vartheta\,(\cos\vartheta + 1) + \cos\vartheta\sin\vartheta \big] = k\,R^2\sin\vartheta ,
$$
$$
m\,R^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = k\,R^2\sin\vartheta - m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide is a fixed circle, hence the point has one degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ determines the configuration. The velocity of $P$ is tangent to the circle and has magnitude $R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$, therefore the kinetic energy is $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$.

The distance between $P$ and $A$ is computed as in the case of the rod with the spring: the square is expanded and $\sin^2\vartheta + \cos^2\vartheta = 1$ is used, which gives $|P - A|^2 = 2\,R^2\,(1 + \cos\vartheta)$. The spring has maximum length $2R$ at $\vartheta = 0$, when $P$ lies at the lowest point of the guide, and zero length at $\vartheta = \pi$.

The potential is the sum of that of the weight, $-m\,g\,y_P = m\,g\,R\cos\vartheta$, and of the elastic one. Both contributions to the Lagrangian component are proportional to $\sin\vartheta$, but they have opposite sign, because the weight pulls $P$ back downwards whereas the spring pulls it upwards.

The coefficient $\dfrac{k}{m} - \dfrac{g}{R}$ changes sign when $k\,R$ passes through the value $m\,g$, therefore three cases are distinguished.

- For $k\,R < m\,g$ the right-hand side is negative for $0 < \vartheta < \pi$, hence the equation is that of the simple pendulum of length $\dfrac{m\,g\,R}{m\,g - k\,R}$.
- For $k\,R = m\,g$ the right-hand side is zero for every $\vartheta$, hence $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$ and the point travels along the guide with constant angular velocity.
- For $k\,R > m\,g$ the right-hand side is positive for $0 < \vartheta < \pi$, therefore the spring **prevails** over the weight and takes the point away from the configuration $\vartheta = 0$.

In the check the moment of the elastic force with respect to the center is computed with the cross product. The moment of the weight is $-m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta$ and the moment of the reaction of the guide is zero, because that reaction is directed along the radius and its line of action passes through $O$. The same equation of motion is obtained.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The material point $P_1$, of mass $m$, slides on a smooth plane inclined at an angle $\alpha$ and is connected by an inextensible string, of negligible mass, to the material point $P_2$ of mass $2m$, which hangs vertically; the string passes over an ideal pulley placed at the top of the plane, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the equation of motion and compute the velocity of the system after $P_2$ has descended by the length $d$, starting from rest.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,3.464" cerchi="6.35,3.75,0.33,foro" linee="3.402,1.964 6.1,3.52;6.68,3.75 6.68,1.7" punti="3.402,1.964,P_1;6.68,1.7,P_2;6.35,3.75,K" etichette="0.6,0.22,α;3.5,1.55,m;7.05,2.15,2m" quote="3.402 1.964,6 3.464,s,-0.75" titolo="Two material points connected by an inextensible string passing over the pulley $K$"}

The string is inextensible and the pulley is ideal, hence the system has one degree of freedom; the distance $s$ of $P_1$ from the pulley, measured along the inclined plane, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
v_1 = |\dot{s}| ,
\qquad
v_2 = |\dot{s}| ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{s}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,\dot{s}^2 = \frac{3}{2}\,m\,\dot{s}^2
$$
$$
y_1 = -s\sin\alpha + \text{const.} ,
\qquad
y_2 = s + \text{const.}
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_1 - 2\,m\,g\,y_2 = m\,g\,s\sin\alpha - 2\,m\,g\,s
$$
$$
Q_s = \frac{\partial U}{\partial s} = m\,g\,(\sin\alpha - 2)
$$
$$
3\,m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\,(\sin\alpha - 2)
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{s} = \frac{g\,(\sin\alpha - 2)}{3} < 0
$$
$$
\frac{3}{2}\,m\,v^2 = 2\,m\,g\,d - m\,g\,d\sin\alpha
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
v = \sqrt{\frac{2\,g\,d\,(2 - \sin\alpha)}{3}}
$$

Check with the cardinal equations. With $\mathcal{T}$ the magnitude of the tension of the string, projecting the first cardinal equation along the plane for $P_1$ and along the vertical for $P_2$ gives
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\sin\alpha - \mathcal{T} \\[4pt]
2\,m\,\ddot{s} = -2\,m\,g + \mathcal{T}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
3\,m\,\ddot{s} = m\,g\sin\alpha - 2\,m\,g ,
\qquad
\mathcal{T} = \frac{2\,m\,g\,(1 + \sin\alpha)}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The string is inextensible and its total length is constant, therefore if $P_1$ moves away from the pulley by the length $\delta s$, the vertical portion shortens by the same length and $P_2$ rises by $\delta s$. The two points therefore have velocities of equal magnitude, and the system has **one degree of freedom**.

The two terms $\dfrac{1}{2}\,m\,v_1^2$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}\,(2m)\,v_2^2$ appear in the kinetic energy, and they add up because both velocities have magnitude $|\dot{s}|$. The string and the pulley are ideal and do not appear in the kinetic energy, given that their mass is negligible.

The heights are written as functions of $s$: when $s$ grows, $P_1$ descends along the plane by $s\sin\alpha$ and $P_2$ rises by $s$. The potential of the weight of the two points gives the Lagrangian component $m\,g\,(\sin\alpha - 2)$, which is constant.

The value $\sin\alpha$ is smaller than or equal to $1$, therefore $\sin\alpha - 2$ is negative for every $\alpha$: the acceleration $\ddot{s}$ is negative and $s$ decreases. The motion is **uniformly accelerated**, $P_1$ moves up along the plane and $P_2$ descends, because the weight of $P_2$ exceeds the component of the weight of $P_1$ along the plane.

The velocity after the descent by the length $d$ is obtained from the energy integral, without integrating the equation of motion: the constraints are fixed and the load is conservative, hence $T - U$ is constant. In the portion considered $P_2$ descends by $d$ and $P_1$ rises by $d\sin\alpha$ along the vertical, hence the work of the weight is $2\,m\,g\,d - m\,g\,d\sin\alpha$.

In the check Newton's equations are written for the two points, with the tension of the string as unknown. The positive direction is that of increasing $s$ for $P_1$ and, consequently, the upward one for $P_2$. Adding the two equations eliminates the tension and gives back Lagrange's equation; Lagrange's method gives that equation directly, whereas the tension, which is the reaction of the inextensibility constraint, is obtained only from the cardinal equations.

</div>
