## Exercise 1

The material point $P$ of mass $m$ slides on a smooth horizontal straight guide, which rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ about a fixed vertical axis through its point $O$. A spring of elastic constant $k$ and rest length $\ell_0$ connects $P$ to $O$, as the diagram in the figure, seen from above, shows. Write the equation of motion, find the distance at which $P$ can remain at rest relative to the guide and write a first integral.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,2.6,0" cerchi="0,0,0.13" linee="-3.2,0 3.8,0;0,0 0.38,0.26 0.9,-0.26 1.42,0.26 1.94,-0.26 2.3,0 2.6,0" coppie="-2.2 0,1,ω" etichette="1.2,-0.5,k" quote="O,P,r,-0.62" titolo="Rotating guide seen from above: the point $P$ is pulled back by the spring of constant $k$"}

The point has one degree of freedom, because the rotation of the guide is prescribed; the distance $r$ of $P$ from $O$ is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
\vec{v}_P = \dot{r}\,\hat{u} + \omega\,r\,\hat{w} ,
\qquad
v_P^2 = \dot{r}^2 + \omega^2\,r^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,r^2 ,
\qquad
T_2 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 ,
\qquad
T_1 = 0 ,
\qquad
T_0 = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,r^2
$$
$$
U = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,(r - \ell_0)^2 ,
\qquad
Q_r = \frac{\partial U}{\partial r} = -k\,(r - \ell_0)
$$
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{r}} = m\,\ddot{r} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial T}{\partial r} = m\,\omega^2\,r
$$
$$
m\,\ddot{r} - m\,\omega^2\,r = -k\,(r - \ell_0)
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
m\,\ddot{r} = \big( m\,\omega^2 - k \big)\,r + k\,\ell_0
$$
$$
\ddot{r} = 0 \;\text{ and }\; \dot{r} = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
r_e = \frac{k\,\ell_0}{k - m\,\omega^2}
\qquad \text{if } k > m\,\omega^2
$$
$$
\mathcal{H} = T_2 - T_0 + V = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,r^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(r - \ell_0)^2 = \text{const.}
$$

Check that $\mathcal{H}$ is constant:
$$
\frac{d\mathcal{H}}{dt} = m\,\dot{r}\,\ddot{r} - m\,\omega^2\,r\,\dot{r} + k\,(r - \ell_0)\,\dot{r}
= \dot{r}\,\Big[ m\,\ddot{r} - m\,\omega^2\,r + k\,(r - \ell_0) \Big] = 0
$$
by the equation of motion.

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide rotates with a prescribed law, hence its position depends on time and the constraint is **moving**. The position of $P$ is determined by the distance $r$ from $O$ alone, because the angle of the guide is $\omega t$ and is known: the system has one degree of freedom.

The velocity of $P$ splits into the component $\dot{r}$ along the guide and the component $\omega\,r$ orthogonal to it, due to the rotation. The two components are orthogonal, hence the square of the magnitude is the sum of the squares.

The quadratic term $T_2$ and the term $T_0$, which does not contain the generalized velocity, appear in the kinetic energy; the term $T_1$ is zero because the two components of the velocity are orthogonal. The presence of $T_0$ is the consequence of the moving constraint.

The derivative $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial r} = m\,\omega^2\,r$ is the term which in the rotating frame is interpreted as the **centrifugal force**. In the equation of motion it appears with the sign opposite to that of the elastic force, therefore the motion depends on the comparison between $k$ and $m\,\omega^2$.

The configuration of rest relative to the guide is obtained by making the right-hand side of the equation vanish. It exists only if $k > m\,\omega^2$, that is if the spring is stiff enough with respect to the rotation; otherwise the centrifugal force exceeds the elastic force at every distance and the point moves away.

The Lagrangian does not depend explicitly on time, therefore the **Jacobi function** is a first integral. It is not the mechanical energy: the minus sign appears in the term $-T_0$, and the mechanical energy $T + V$ changes in time, because the moving constraint does work on the point.

In the check $\mathcal{H}$ is differentiated with respect to time and $\dot{r}$ is factored out: the expression in square brackets is the left-hand side of the equation of motion, hence it is zero. The check is independent of the computation of the derivatives of the Lagrangian, because $\mathcal{H}$ is written directly from $T_2$, $T_0$ and $V$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The material point $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to a smooth circular guide of center $O$ and radius $R$, which rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$ about its own vertical diameter, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the equation of motion and find the configurations in which $P$ stays at rest relative to the guide.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2,foro" tratteggi="0,2.9 0,-2.9;0,0 1.638,-1.147" coppie="0 2.5,1,ω" punti="0,0,O;1.638,-1.147,P" etichette="0.42,-1.5,ϑ;1.95,-1.4,m" titolo="Circular guide rotating about its own vertical diameter"}

The point has one degree of freedom, because the rotation of the guide is prescribed; the angle $\vartheta$ between the radius $OP$ and the downward vertical is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
d = R\sin\vartheta ,
\qquad
y_P = -R\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
v_P^2 = R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \omega^2\,d^2 = R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \omega^2\,R^2\sin^2\vartheta
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,R^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\omega^2\,R^2\sin^2\vartheta
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_P = m\,g\,R\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
Q_\vartheta = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = -m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = m\,R^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta} = m\,\omega^2\,R^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
m\,R^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,\omega^2\,R^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta - m\,g\,R\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = \left( \omega^2\cos\vartheta - \frac{g}{R} \right) \sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = 0 \;\text{ and }\; \dot{\vartheta} = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin\vartheta = 0
\quad \text{or} \quad
\cos\vartheta = \frac{g}{\omega^2\,R}
$$

Check in the case $\omega = 0$:
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = -\frac{g}{R}\sin\vartheta ,
$$
which is the equation of the simple pendulum of length $R$, as must be the case when the guide is at rest.

<div class="spiegazione">

The guide rotates with a prescribed law, therefore the constraint is moving and the point has one degree of freedom, determined by the angle $\vartheta$ measured in the plane of the guide.

The velocity of $P$ has two orthogonal components: the one in the plane of the guide, of magnitude $R\,|\dot{\vartheta}|$, and the one orthogonal to the plane, due to the rotation, of magnitude $\omega\,d$ with $d = R\sin\vartheta$ the distance of $P$ from the axis of rotation. The square of the magnitude is the sum of the squares, and the second summand is the term $T_0$.

The height of $P$ does not depend on the rotation, because the axis is vertical; therefore the potential of the weight is the same as in the case with the guide at rest.

Here $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta}$ is different from zero, given that $T_0$ depends on $\vartheta$, and it produces the term $m\,\omega^2\,R^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta$, which in the rotating frame is the moment of the centrifugal force with respect to $O$.

The configurations in which $P$ stays at rest relative to the guide are $\vartheta = 0$, $\vartheta = \pi$ and, if $\omega^2\,R \ge g$, the two configurations with $\cos\vartheta = \dfrac{g}{\omega^2\,R}$. These last ones exist only when the rotation is fast enough: it is the behavior of the **centrifugal governor**, in which the masses rise as the speed of rotation grows.

In the check $\omega = 0$ is set and the equation of the simple pendulum of length $R$ is recovered. The limiting case checks the term due to the moving constraint, which must disappear when the guide is fixed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The uniform rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the endpoint $O$ to a fixed vertical axis, about which the vertical plane containing the rod rotates with constant angular velocity $\omega$, as in the diagram in the figure. The hinge is smooth and the rod can rotate in that plane. Write the equation of motion.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.543,-1.839" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,1.5 0,-2.6" coppie="0 1.1,1,ω" punti="0.771,-0.919,G,baricentro" forze="0.771 -0.919,0,-0.65,mg" etichette="0.3,-1.0,ϑ" quote="O,A,ℓ,-0.6" titolo="Rod hinged to a vertical axis rotating with constant angular velocity $\omega$"}

The system has one degree of freedom, because the rotation of the plane is prescribed; the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

Let $Q$ be the point of the rod at distance $s$ from $O$. Its distance from the axis of rotation is $s\sin\vartheta$, therefore
$$
v_Q^2 = s^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \omega^2\,s^2\sin^2\vartheta ,
\qquad
dm = \frac{m}{\ell}\,ds
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\ell} \frac{m}{\ell}\,s^2\,\big( \dot{\vartheta}^2 + \omega^2\sin^2\vartheta \big)\,ds
= \frac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\big( \dot{\vartheta}^2 + \omega^2\sin^2\vartheta \big)
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_G = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
Q_\vartheta = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = -\frac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = \left( \omega^2\cos\vartheta - \frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell} \right) \sin\vartheta
$$

Check of the value of $T$ with the moment of inertia. The motion of the rod is the composition of the rotation $\dot{\vartheta}$ in the moving plane and of the rotation $\omega$ about the vertical axis; the moments of inertia with respect to the axis through $O$ orthogonal to the rod and with respect to the vertical axis through $O$ are
$$
I_\perp = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_v = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\sin^2\vartheta ,
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,I_\perp\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,I_v\,\omega^2 = \frac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{6}\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega^2\sin^2\vartheta ,
$$
which coincides with the result of the integral.

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane containing the rod rotates with a prescribed law, therefore the constraint is moving and the system has one degree of freedom, given by the angle that the rod forms with the vertical in the moving plane.

The kinetic energy is computed by integration over the length of the rod, because the points of the rod have different velocities. The point at distance $s$ from $O$ has two orthogonal components of velocity: $s\,\dot{\vartheta}$ in the moving plane and $\omega\,s\sin\vartheta$ orthogonal to it, given that its distance from the vertical axis is $s\sin\vartheta$.

The integral $\displaystyle\int_0^{\ell} s^2\,ds = \dfrac{\ell^3}{3}$ gives the factor $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$, which is the moment of inertia of the rod with respect to an orthogonal axis through one endpoint.

The equation has the same form as the one of the point on the rotating circular guide, with $\dfrac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}$ in place of $\dfrac{g}{R}$: the **reduced length** of the rod, $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\ell$, appears, as in the compound pendulum.

In the check the kinetic energy is recomputed with the moments of inertia instead of with the integral: the motion is the composition of two rotations about orthogonal axes through $O$, and since the rod is thin its moment of inertia with respect to a generic axis through $O$ depends only on the angle that the axis forms with the rod. The vertical axis forms the angle $\vartheta$ with the rod, therefore $I_v = \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\sin^2\vartheta$. Every point of the rod has zero coordinate along the first axis, hence the product of inertia between the two axes is zero and the mixed term does not appear in the kinetic energy; the two computations coincide.

</div>
