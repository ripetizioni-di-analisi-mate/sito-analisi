## Exercise 1

The uniform rod $AB$ in the figure, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, has the endpoint $A$ constrained to a smooth horizontal guide and the endpoint $B$ constrained to a smooth vertical guide; the two guides are fixed and intersect at the point $O$, and the system moves in the vertical plane. Write the equation of motion and compute the angular velocity of the rod when it passes through the horizontal configuration, knowing that it starts from rest from the vertical configuration.

::struttura{nodi="A,2.057,0;B,0,2.451" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,270;B,carrello,180" linee="-0.7,0 3.1,0;0,-0.7 0,3.3" punti="1.028,1.226,G,baricentro" forze="1.028 1.226,0,-0.7,mg" etichette="0.3,1.95,ϑ" quote="A,B,2ℓ,-0.65" titolo="Rod with the endpoints on two smooth orthogonal guides"}

The two guides impose two conditions on the three configuration parameters of the rod in the plane, hence the system has one degree of freedom; the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the vertical guide, with $0 < \vartheta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, is taken as Lagrangian coordinate.

$$
A - O = 2\,\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
B - O = 2\,\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
G - O = \ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_G = \ell\dot{\vartheta}\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\dot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
v_G^2 = \ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
I_G = \frac{1}{12}\,m\,(2\ell)^2 = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_G = -m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
Q_\vartheta = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g}{4\,\ell}\sin\vartheta
$$

The mechanical energy is constant, hence from the configuration $\vartheta = 0$, with $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$, to the configuration $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ one obtains
$$
\frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + m\,g\,\ell\cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 0 + m\,g\,\ell\cos 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
|\dot{\vartheta}| = \sqrt{\frac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}}
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation, pole at the instantaneous center of rotation $C$, which is the intersection point of the perpendiculars to the velocities of $A$ and of $B$:
$$
I_C = I_G + m\,\ell^2 = \frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_C\,\ddot{\vartheta} = m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod in the plane has three configuration parameters and the two guides impose two conditions, hence **one degree of freedom** remains. The angle $\vartheta$ determines the configuration, because the positions of the two endpoints depend on it.

The center of mass is the midpoint of the rod, hence its coordinates are half those of the endpoints. Differentiating gives the velocity, and the square of its magnitude simplifies to $\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$: the center of mass describes a circle of center $O$ and radius $\ell$, since the distance $OG$ is half the hypotenuse of a right triangle and therefore remains constant.

The kinetic energy is written with König's theorem. The angular velocity of the rod is $\dot{\vartheta}$ in magnitude, because the rod forms the angle $\vartheta$ with the fixed vertical guide.

The potential contains the weight alone, given that the reactions of the smooth guides do no virtual work. The height of the center of mass is $\ell\cos\vartheta$, therefore $U = -m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta$.

The equation of motion has **positive** right-hand side for $0 < \vartheta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$: the rod moves away from the vertical configuration, because in that configuration the center of mass lies at the highest point of the circle it describes.

For the second question the energy integral is used: the constraints are fixed and the weight is conservative, therefore $E = T - U$ is constant and gives $|\dot{\vartheta}| = \sqrt{\dfrac{3\,g}{2\,\ell}}$ in the horizontal configuration.

In the check the pole is the instantaneous center of rotation $C$, which is constructed by intersecting the perpendicular to the horizontal guide through $A$ with the perpendicular to the vertical guide through $B$; the point $C$ is at distance $\ell$ from $G$, hence $I_C = \dfrac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$ by the Huygens-Steiner theorem. The reactions of the guides pass through $C$ and have zero moment, therefore the moment of the weight remains, $m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta$, and the equation coincides with Lagrange's one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The system of the previous exercise is modified by adding a spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length between the endpoint $A$ and the point $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the equation of motion and find the configurations in which the system can remain at rest.

::struttura{nodi="A,2.057,0;B,0,2.451" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,270;B,carrello,180" linee="-0.7,0 3.1,0;0,-0.7 0,3.3;0,0 0.3,0.25 0.75,-0.25 1.2,0.25 1.65,-0.25 1.9,0 2.057,0" punti="1.028,1.226,G,baricentro" etichette="0.3,1.95,ϑ;1.3,0.45,k" quote="A,B,2ℓ,-0.65" titolo="Rod with the endpoints on two orthogonal guides and spring between $A$ and $O$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate remains the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the vertical guide and the kinetic energy is that of the previous exercise.

$$
T = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
|A - O| = 2\,\ell\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,(2\,\ell\sin\vartheta)^2 = -m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta - 2\,k\,\ell^2\sin^2\vartheta
$$
$$
Q_\vartheta = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta - 4\,k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\ddot{\vartheta} = \big( m\,g\,\ell - 4\,k\,\ell^2\cos\vartheta \big)\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,\big( m\,g - 4\,k\,\ell\cos\vartheta \big)}{4\,m\,\ell}\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
Q_\vartheta = 0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\sin\vartheta = 0
\quad \text{or} \quad
\cos\vartheta = \frac{m\,g}{4\,k\,\ell}
$$

Check in the limiting case $k \to 0$:
$$
\ddot{\vartheta} = \frac{3\,g}{4\,\ell}\sin\vartheta ,
$$
which is the equation of the previous exercise.

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring does not change the constraints, hence the system still has one degree of freedom and the kinetic energy is the same. The potential changes, and the elastic term is added to it.

The length of the spring is the distance $OA$, that is $2\,\ell\sin\vartheta$; the rest length is zero, therefore the elastic potential is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,(2\,\ell\sin\vartheta)^2$. Differentiating uses $\dfrac{d}{d\vartheta}\sin^2\vartheta = 2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta$.

In the right-hand side $\sin\vartheta$ is factored out: the sign of the acceleration depends on the sign of $m\,g - 4\,k\,\ell\cos\vartheta$, hence on the value of $\vartheta$ and on the data. The spring pulls $A$ towards $O$ and tends to straighten the rod, whereas the weight tends to lay it down.

The configurations of rest are found by making the Lagrangian component of the load vanish, since in them the right-hand side of the equation of motion is zero. The first is $\vartheta = 0$, with the rod vertical; the second exists when $\dfrac{m\,g}{4\,k\,\ell} \le 1$, that is when the spring is stiff enough, and gives $\vartheta = \arccos\dfrac{m\,g}{4\,k\,\ell}$.

In the check $k = 0$ is set: the spring disappears, the elastic potential vanishes and the equation becomes again the one of the previous exercise. The limiting case checks the added term and its coefficients.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A material point of mass $m$ is welded at the endpoint $B$ of the rod of exercise 1, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$ with the endpoints on the two smooth orthogonal guides, as in the diagram in the figure. Write the equation of motion.

::struttura{nodi="A,2.057,0;B,0,2.451" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,270;B,carrello,180" linee="-0.7,0 3.1,0;0,-0.7 0,3.3" punti="1.028,1.226,G,baricentro" forze="1.028 1.226,0,-0.7,mg;0 2.451,0,-0.7,mg" etichette="0.3,1.75,ϑ;-0.55,2.75,m" quote="A,B,2ℓ,-0.65" titolo="Rod with the endpoints on two guides and material point welded at the endpoint $B$"}

The Lagrangian coordinate remains the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the vertical guide.

$$
B - O = 2\,\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_B = -2\,\ell\,\dot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
v_B^2 = 4\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\sin^2\vartheta
$$
$$
T = \underbrace{\frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\vartheta}^2}_{\text{rod}} + \underbrace{2\,m\,\ell^2\sin^2\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2}_{\text{point}}
= \frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\big( 1 + 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
U = -m\,g\,y_G - m\,g\,y_B = -m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta - 2\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta = -3\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = \frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\big( 1 + 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)\,\dot{\vartheta}
$$
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}} = \frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\big( 1 + 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 8\,m\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2
$$
$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta} = 4\,m\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 ,
\qquad
Q_\vartheta = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = 3\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta
$$
$$
\frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\big( 1 + 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 4\,m\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 = 3\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta
$$

Check with the energy integral:
$$
E = \frac{2}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\big( 1 + 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)\,\dot{\vartheta}^2 + 3\,m\,g\,\ell\cos\vartheta = \text{const.}
$$
$$
\frac{dE}{dt} = \frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\big( 1 + 3\sin^2\vartheta \big)\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 4\,m\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^3 - 3\,m\,g\,\ell\sin\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta} = 0 ,
$$
which, divided by $\dot{\vartheta}$, gives the equation of motion.

<div class="spiegazione">

The material point is welded to the rod, hence it does not add degrees of freedom: the system still has a single degree of freedom and the coordinate remains $\vartheta$.

The velocity of $B$ is obtained by differentiating its position, which depends on $\vartheta$ through $\cos\vartheta$; its magnitude is $2\,\ell\,|\dot{\vartheta}\sin\vartheta|$ and it vanishes at $\vartheta = 0$, the configuration in which $B$ is at the highest point of the vertical guide and reverses the motion.

The kinetic energy of the point contains $\sin^2\vartheta$, therefore the **kinetic matrix depends on the configuration**: the coefficient of the second derivative is not constant and the term quadratic in the velocities appears in the equation, coming partly from $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\vartheta}}$ and partly from $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta}$.

The two quadratic terms have coefficients $8\,m\,\ell^2$ and $4\,m\,\ell^2$ and opposite sign, hence their difference is $4\,m\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta\,\dot{\vartheta}^2$: it is the example of a situation in which forgetting the derivative $\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \vartheta}$ doubles that term.

The potential is the sum of the contributions of the two weights, with $y_G = \ell\cos\vartheta$ and $y_B = 2\,\ell\cos\vartheta$.

In the check the mechanical energy is differentiated with respect to time. The result vanishes only if the equation of motion holds, therefore the check confirms the coefficients of the two terms; the energy integral is written from $T$ and from $V$ without using the derivatives of the Lagrangian, hence it is an independent route.

</div>
