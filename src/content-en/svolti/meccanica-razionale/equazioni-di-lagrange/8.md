## Exercise 1

The material point $P$ of mass $m$ moves on a smooth horizontal plane and is connected to the fixed point $O$ of the plane by a spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero rest length, as in the diagram in the figure, seen from above. Write the equations of motion in polar coordinates, the two first integrals and the condition for the trajectory to be a circle of center $O$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,2.2,1.3" cerchi="0,0,0.1" linee="0,0 0.0713,0.1932 0.4787,0.1319 0.6213,0.5182 1.0287,0.4569 1.1713,0.8432 1.5787,0.7819 1.7213,1.1682 2.1287,1.1069 2.2,1.3" tratteggi="0,0 3.4,0" etichette="0.95,0.17,φ;0.72,0.98,k" quote="O,P,r,0.62" titolo="Point on a smooth horizontal plane pulled back by the spring: polar coordinates $r$ and $\varphi$"}

The point moves on a plane, hence it has two degrees of freedom; the polar coordinates $r$ and $\varphi$ with center $O$ are taken as Lagrangian coordinates.

$$
P - O = r\cos\varphi\,\hat{\imath} + r\sin\varphi\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
v_P^2 = \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) ,
\qquad
U = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2
$$
$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{r}} = m\,\dot{r} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial T}{\partial r} = m\,r\,\dot{\varphi}^2 ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial U}{\partial r} = -k\,r
$$
$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{\varphi}} = m\,r^2\,\dot{\varphi} ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial T}{\partial \varphi} = 0 ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial U}{\partial \varphi} = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{r} - m\,r\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = -k\,r \\[6pt]
\dfrac{d}{dt}\big( m\,r^2\,\dot{\varphi} \big) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
p_\varphi = m\,r^2\,\dot{\varphi} = c = \text{const.} ,
\qquad
E = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2 = \text{const.}
$$
$$
m\,\ddot{r} = \frac{c^2}{m\,r^3} - k\,r
$$
$$
r \equiv r_0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
m\,r_0\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = k\,r_0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{\varphi} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
$$

Check with the second cardinal equation, pole at $O$. The elastic force has line of action through $O$, hence its moment with respect to $O$ is zero and
$$
\frac{d K_O}{dt} = 0 ,
\qquad
K_O = (P - O) \wedge m\,\vec{v}_P \cdot \hat{k} = m\,r^2\,\dot{\varphi} ,
$$
which is the kinetic momentum $p_\varphi$ found with Lagrange's method.

<div class="spiegazione">

The point is free to move in the plane, therefore it has **two degrees of freedom**. Polar coordinates are convenient because the length of the spring coincides with $r$, hence the potential does not depend on $\varphi$.

The square of the velocity in polar coordinates is $\dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2$, with the radial component and the transverse one orthogonal to each other; see the topic on the kinematics of the point.

The coordinate $\varphi$ is **cyclic**, because neither $T$ nor $U$ depends on it: $T$ contains $r$ and the generalized velocities, and $U$ contains only $r$. The second equation therefore reduces to $\dfrac{d p_\varphi}{dt} = 0$.

The first integral $p_\varphi = c$ is used to eliminate $\dot{\varphi}$ from the first equation: one obtains $\dot{\varphi} = \dfrac{c}{m\,r^2}$ and substitutes it, which gives a differential equation in $r$ alone. This is the advantage of a cyclic coordinate: the problem with two degrees of freedom reduces to one with a single degree of freedom.

The second first integral is the mechanical energy, constant because the constraints are fixed and the spring is conservative; the plane is horizontal, hence the weight does not appear.

The trajectory is a circle of center $O$ when $r$ remains constant, that is when $\ddot{r} = 0$ with $\dot{r} = 0$. The condition gives $\dot{\varphi} = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$ **independently of the radius**: with a spring of zero rest length all the circular orbits have the same period.

In the check $p_\varphi$ is recognized as the angular momentum of the point with respect to $O$. The elastic force is directed towards $O$, hence it has zero moment with respect to that pole, and the second cardinal equation gives the conservation of $K_O$: the result coincides with the one obtained from the cyclic coordinate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The uniform rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the endpoint $O$ by a smooth fixed hinge and can rotate freely in a smooth horizontal plane; a ring of mass $m$, which can be treated as the material point $P$, slides on the rod without friction, as in the diagram in the figure, seen from above. At the initial instant the ring is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $O$ with zero radial velocity and the rod rotates with angular velocity $\omega_0$. Compute the angular velocity of the rod and the radial velocity of the ring at the instant in which the ring reaches the endpoint $A$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2.771,1.6" aste="O-A" cerchi="0,0,0.1" tratteggi="0,0 3.5,0" punti="1.472,0.85,P" etichette="0.95,0.18,φ;2.3,1.78,rod m and ℓ;1.6,0.5,m" quote="O,1.472 0.85,r,0.62" titolo="Ring sliding on a rod free to rotate in a horizontal plane"}

The system has two degrees of freedom and the angle $\varphi$ of rotation of the rod and the distance $r$ of the ring from $O$ are taken as Lagrangian coordinates.

$$
I_O = \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 ,
\qquad
v_P^2 = \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{r}^2 + r^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) ,
\qquad
U = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{r} - m\,r\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = 0 \\[6pt]
\dfrac{d}{dt}\left[ \left( \dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,r^2 \right) \dot{\varphi} \right] = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
p_\varphi = \left( \frac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2 + m\,r^2 \right) \dot{\varphi} = \text{const.} ,
\qquad
E = T = \text{const.}
$$
At the initial instant $r = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and $\dot{\varphi} = \omega_0$; at the final instant $r = \ell$ and the values sought are denoted by $\dot{\varphi}_1$ and $\dot{r}_1$.
$$
\left( \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} \right) m\,\ell^2\,\omega_0 = \left( \frac{1}{3} + 1 \right) m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\varphi}_1
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\frac{7}{12}\,\omega_0 = \frac{4}{3}\,\dot{\varphi}_1
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{\varphi}_1 = \frac{7}{16}\,\omega_0
$$
$$
\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{7}{12}\,m\,\ell^2\,\omega_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{3}\,m\,\ell^2\,\dot{\varphi}_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\dot{r}_1^2
$$
$$
\frac{7}{12}\,\ell^2\,\omega_0^2 - \frac{4}{3}\,\ell^2\,\frac{49}{256}\,\omega_0^2 = \dot{r}_1^2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{r}_1^2 = \frac{21}{64}\,\ell^2\,\omega_0^2
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{r}_1 = \frac{\sqrt{21}}{8}\,\ell\,\omega_0
$$

Check of the sign of the radial motion. At the initial instant the first equation gives
$$
\ddot{r} = r\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\omega_0^2 > 0 ,
$$
hence $r$ grows and the ring does reach the endpoint $A$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates and the ring slides on it, therefore the system has **two degrees of freedom**. The ring is a point of the rod only instantaneously: its distance from $O$ changes in time, hence the second coordinate $r$ is needed.

The kinetic energy is the sum of that of the rod, which rotates about the fixed axis through $O$ with moment of inertia $\dfrac{1}{3}\,m\,\ell^2$, and that of the ring, written in polar coordinates. The plane is horizontal and the constraints are smooth, hence the potential is zero.

The coordinate $\varphi$ is **cyclic**, because the kinetic energy depends on $r$ and on the generalized velocities but not on $\varphi$. The conjugate kinetic momentum is the angular momentum of the system with respect to the axis of rotation and it is conserved, whereas the moment of inertia grows with $r$; when the ring moves away from the axis, therefore, the angular velocity decreases.

The two first integrals are enough to answer without integrating the equations. The first one links $\dot{\varphi}_1$ to $\omega_0$ and gives $\dot{\varphi}_1 = \dfrac{7}{16}\,\omega_0$; the second one, the energy, provides $\dot{r}_1$ once $\dot{\varphi}_1$ is known.

The kinetic energy remains constant, but it is redistributed: the rotational part decreases, because $\dot{\varphi}$ drops more rapidly than the moment of inertia grows, and the difference is found in the radial part.

In the check it is verified that the motion really goes in the supposed direction. The first equation, at the initial instant, gives $\ddot{r} > 0$: the term $m\,r\,\dot{\varphi}^2$ is the effect which in the frame attached to the rod is interpreted as the centrifugal force, and it pushes the ring towards the endpoint.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The material point $P$ of mass $m$ is constrained to the inner surface of a smooth fixed right circular cone, of half-angle $\alpha$, with the vertex $O$ at the bottom and vertical axis, as the diagram in the figure shows. Write the equations of motion, the two first integrals and the condition for the trajectory to be a horizontal circle.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 linee="-2,3 0,0 2,3" curve="2*cos(t),3+0.6*sin(t),0,6.29;1.2*cos(t),1.8+0.36*sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="0,0,0,3.9" punti="0,0,pieno,O;1.2,1.8,pieno,P" etichette="0.2,0.55,α;0.82,0.82,s;0.2,3.75,axis" xmin=-2.7 xmax=2.7 ymin=-0.6 ymax=4.3 titolo="Point constrained to the inner surface of a smooth cone of half-angle $\alpha$"}

The point moves on a surface, hence it has two degrees of freedom; the distance $s$ of $P$ from the vertex, measured along the generating line, and the angle $\varphi$ that determines the generating line on which $P$ lies are taken as Lagrangian coordinates.

$$
d = s\sin\alpha ,
\qquad
z_P = s\cos\alpha
$$
$$
v_P^2 = \dot{s}^2 + d^2\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = \dot{s}^2 + s^2\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi}^2
$$
$$
T = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{s}^2 + s^2\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) ,
\qquad
U = -m\,g\,z_P = -m\,g\,s\cos\alpha
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
m\,\ddot{s} - m\,s\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = -m\,g\cos\alpha \\[6pt]
\dfrac{d}{dt}\big( m\,s^2\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi} \big) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
p_\varphi = m\,s^2\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi} = \text{const.} ,
\qquad
E = \frac{1}{2}\,m\,\big( \dot{s}^2 + s^2\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi}^2 \big) + m\,g\,s\cos\alpha = \text{const.}
$$
$$
s \equiv s_0
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
m\,s_0\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = m\,g\cos\alpha
\qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad
\dot{\varphi} = \frac{1}{\sin\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{g\cos\alpha}{s_0}}
$$

Check with the angular momentum with respect to the vertical axis. The reaction of the smooth surface is orthogonal to the cone and its line of action meets the axis, hence its axial moment is zero; the weight has zero axial moment too, because it is parallel to the axis. Therefore
$$
K_z = m\,d^2\,\dot{\varphi} = m\,s^2\sin^2\alpha\,\dot{\varphi} = \text{const.} ,
$$
which is the kinetic momentum $p_\varphi$ found with Lagrange's method.

<div class="spiegazione">

The point is constrained to a fixed surface, therefore it has **two degrees of freedom**. The coordinates chosen are the natural ones on the cone: the distance from the vertex along the generating line and the angle about the axis.

The distance of $P$ from the axis is $s\sin\alpha$ and its height is $s\cos\alpha$, because the generating line forms the angle $\alpha$ with the axis. The velocity has the component $\dot{s}$ along the generating line and the component $s\sin\alpha\,\dot{\varphi}$ orthogonal to the meridian plane; the two are orthogonal, hence the square of the magnitude is the sum of the squares.

The potential of the weight is $-m\,g\,z_P$ and depends on $s$ alone, therefore the coordinate $\varphi$ is **cyclic** and the conjugate kinetic momentum is conserved. The second first integral is the mechanical energy, since the constraints are fixed and the weight is conservative.

The trajectory is a horizontal circle when $s$ remains constant. The first equation with $\ddot{s} = 0$ expresses the balance, along the generating line, between the component of the weight and the centrifugal term; solving it gives the angular velocity required, which grows as $s_0$ decreases and as $\alpha$ decreases.

In the check the angular momentum with respect to the vertical axis is used. The two forces acting on the point, the weight and the normal reaction, both have zero axial moment with respect to that axis, hence $K_z$ is constant; its value coincides with $p_\varphi$. The check confirms that the cyclic coordinate corresponds to the rotational symmetry of the problem about the axis of the cone.

</div>
