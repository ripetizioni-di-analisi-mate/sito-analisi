## Exercise 1

A material point $P$ moves on the $x$ axis with the equation
$$
\ddot{x} = -\,\omega^2 x ,
$$
and at the initial instant it is at $x(0) = x_0 > 0$ with velocity $\dot{x}(0) = v_0 > 0$. Determine the law of motion, the amplitude, the period, the maximum magnitude of the velocity and the first instant at which the point passes through the origin. The graph of the law of motion is in the figure.

::grafico{f="1.2*cos(1.5*x-0.9)" segmenti="0,1.2,9,1.2;0,-1.2,9,-1.2" punti="1.65,0,pieno,t₁" etichette="8.2,1.42,A;8,-1.6,−A" xmin=0 xmax=9 ymin=-1.9 ymax=1.9 nomi="t,x" legenda=0 titolo="Law of the harmonic motion: the point starts from $x_0 > 0$ with positive velocity, reaches the amplitude $A$ and passes through the origin at the instant $t_1$"}

The motion is rectilinear, hence the only unknown is the function $x(t)$, the solution of the linear second order differential equation with the given initial data.

$$
x(t) = x_0\cos(\omega t) + \frac{v_0}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega t)
$$
$$
x(t) = A\cos(\omega t - \varphi) ,
\qquad
\begin{cases}
A\cos\varphi = x_0 \\[4pt]
A\sin\varphi = \dfrac{v_0}{\omega}
\end{cases}
$$
$$
A = \sqrt{x_0^2 + \frac{v_0^2}{\omega^2}} ,
\qquad
\varphi = \arctan\frac{v_0}{\omega\,x_0} \in \left( 0, \frac{\pi}{2} \right)
$$
$$
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} ,
\qquad
|\dot{x}|_{\max} = A\,\omega = \sqrt{\omega^2 x_0^2 + v_0^2}
$$

First passage through the origin:
$$
\cos(\omega t_1 - \varphi) = 0 ,
\qquad
\omega t_1 - \varphi = \frac{\pi}{2} ,
\qquad
t_1 = \frac{1}{\omega}\left( \frac{\pi}{2} + \varphi \right) .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The general solution of $\ddot{x} = -\omega^2x$ is a linear combination of $\cos(\omega t)$ and $\sin(\omega t)$. Imposing $x(0) = x_0$ one determines the coefficient of the cosine and imposing $\dot{x}(0) = v_0$ one determines the coefficient of the sine, which equals $\dfrac{v_0}{\omega}$ because the derivative of $\sin(\omega t)$ at $t = 0$ equals $\omega$.

The same function is written in the form $A\cos(\omega t - \varphi)$ by expanding the cosine of the difference; comparing the coefficients of $\cos(\omega t)$ and of $\sin(\omega t)$ gives the system written above. Adding the squares of the two equations one gets the **amplitude**, and dividing them side by side one gets the phase $\varphi$. Since $x_0 > 0$ and $v_0 > 0$, both right-hand sides are positive, hence $\varphi$ belongs to the first quadrant.

The period depends only on $\omega$ and not on the initial data. The magnitude of the velocity is $A\omega\,|\sin(\omega t - \varphi)|$, therefore its maximum value is $A\omega$ and it is reached at the passages through the origin.

The point passes through the origin when the cosine vanishes; the first positive value of time is obtained from the first solution $\omega t_1 - \varphi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, which is positive because $\varphi < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

</div>

Check. Verify that the function found satisfies the initial data and the equation. One has $x(0) = x_0$ and
$$
\dot{x}(t) = -\,\omega\,x_0\sin(\omega t) + v_0\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
\dot{x}(0) = v_0 ,
\qquad
\ddot{x}(t) = -\,\omega^2 x_0\cos(\omega t) - \omega\,v_0\sin(\omega t) = -\,\omega^2 x(t) .
$$
The amplitude is also checked with the first integral $\dot{x}^2 + \omega^2x^2$, which is constant because its derivative equals $2\dot{x}(\ddot{x} + \omega^2x) = 0$. Its initial value is $v_0^2 + \omega^2x_0^2$ and at the turning point, where $\dot{x} = 0$ and $|x| = A$, it equals $\omega^2A^2$; equating the two one recovers $A = \sqrt{x_0^2 + \dfrac{v_0^2}{\omega^2}}$.

## Exercise 2

A material point $P$ travels along the circular guide with center $O$ and radius $R$ in the figure with angular velocity
$$
\dot{\vartheta}(t) = \omega_0 - \alpha t ,
$$
where $\omega_0 > 0$ and $\alpha > 0$ are constants and $\vartheta$ is the central angle, measured counterclockwise. Compute the intrinsic components of the acceleration, the magnitude of the acceleration at the initial instant, the instant at which the point stops and the number of turns completed up to that instant.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,0.71,0.71" punti="0,0;0.71,0.71" linee="1.0,0.0 0.99,0.16 0.95,0.31 0.89,0.45 0.81,0.59 0.71,0.71 0.59,0.81 0.45,0.89 0.31,0.95 0.16,0.99 0.0,1.0 -0.16,0.99 -0.31,0.95 -0.45,0.89 -0.59,0.81 -0.71,0.71 -0.81,0.59 -0.89,0.45 -0.95,0.31 -0.99,0.16 -1.0,0.0 -0.99,-0.16 -0.95,-0.31 -0.89,-0.45 -0.81,-0.59 -0.71,-0.71 -0.59,-0.81 -0.45,-0.89 -0.31,-0.95 -0.16,-0.99 0.0,-1.0 0.16,-0.99 0.31,-0.95 0.45,-0.89 0.59,-0.81 0.71,-0.71 0.81,-0.59 0.89,-0.45 0.95,-0.31 0.99,-0.16 1.0,0.0;0,0 0.71,0.71;0.32,0.0 0.32,0.05 0.31,0.09 0.3,0.12 0.28,0.16 0.26,0.19 0.24,0.21 0.23,0.22" frecce="0.71,0.71,0.21,1.21,v_P;0,0,1.6,0,x;0,0,0,1.6,y" etichette="0.4,0.32,ϑ;0.3,0.45,R" riferimento=0 titolo="Point on a circular guide of radius $R$: the velocity is tangent and the angular velocity decreases linearly in time"}

$$
s(t) = R\,\vartheta(t) ,
\qquad
\dot{s} = R\,\dot{\vartheta} = R\,(\omega_0 - \alpha t) ,
\qquad
\ddot{s} = -R\,\alpha
$$
$$
a_t = -R\,\alpha ,
\qquad
a_n = \frac{\dot{s}^2}{R} = R\,(\omega_0 - \alpha t)^2
$$
$$
|\vec{a}_P(0)| = \sqrt{a_t^2 + a_n^2}\Big|_{t = 0} = R\,\sqrt{\alpha^2 + \omega_0^4}
$$

Stopping and angle described:
$$
\dot{\vartheta}(t^*) = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
t^* = \frac{\omega_0}{\alpha} ,
$$
$$
\vartheta(t^*) - \vartheta(0) = \int_0^{t^*} (\omega_0 - \alpha t)\,dt = \omega_0 t^* - \frac{1}{2}\,\alpha\,t^{*2} = \frac{\omega_0^2}{2\alpha} ,
\qquad
n = \frac{\omega_0^2}{4\pi\,\alpha} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a circle $s = R\vartheta$ holds, therefore the scalar velocity is $R\dot{\vartheta}$ and the tangential acceleration is $R\ddot{\vartheta} = -R\alpha$, **constant and negative**. The point therefore slows down uniformly.

The normal acceleration is $\dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{R}$, with radius of curvature equal to $R$; it decreases until it vanishes when the point stops.

At the initial instant one has $\dot{\vartheta} = \omega_0$, hence $a_t = -R\alpha$ and $a_n = R\omega_0^2$; the magnitude of the acceleration is the square root of the sum of the squares, because the two components are orthogonal.

The point stops when the angular velocity vanishes, that is at the instant $t^* = \dfrac{\omega_0}{\alpha}$. The angle described is the integral of the angular velocity between $0$ and $t^*$ and equals $\dfrac{\omega_0^2}{2\alpha}$; dividing it by $2\pi$ one obtains the number of turns $n = \dfrac{\omega_0^2}{4\pi\alpha}$, which in general is not an integer.

</div>

Check. The angle described is obtained in a different way, using the relation between angular velocity and angle valid for constant deceleration. From $\ddot{\vartheta} = -\alpha$ there follows $\dot{\vartheta}^2 = \omega_0^2 - 2\alpha\,(\vartheta - \vartheta_0)$, as one verifies by differentiating both sides with respect to time. Imposing $\dot{\vartheta} = 0$ one obtains
$$
\vartheta - \vartheta_0 = \frac{\omega_0^2}{2\alpha} ,
$$
which coincides with the result of the integration. The acceleration is also checked with the formulas in polar coordinates, with $r = R$ constant: $\vec{a}_P = -R\dot{\vartheta}^2\,\hat{u}_r + R\ddot{\vartheta}\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = -R(\omega_0 - \alpha t)^2\,\hat{u}_r - R\alpha\,\hat{u}_\vartheta$, whose components coincide with $-a_n$ and $a_t$.

## Exercise 3

A material point $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ combining two orthogonal harmonic motions of equal angular frequency and out of phase by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$:
$$
x(t) = A\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = B\sin(\omega t) ,
$$
with $A > B > 0$. The trajectory is in the figure. Determine the trajectory, prove that the acceleration is directed toward $O$ and compute the areal velocity with respect to $O$.

::grafico{curve="1.6*cos(t),0.9*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="0,0,pieno;0.86,0.76,pieno,P" vettori="0.86,0.76,0.41,0.36;0.86,0.76,0.2,1.0" etichette="0.62,0.3,aₚ;0.12,1.1,vₚ" uguale=1 xmin=-2.1 xmax=2.4 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.6 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Elliptical trajectory with semiaxes $A$ and $B$: the acceleration is directed toward the center at every instant"}

$$
\frac{x^2}{A^2} + \frac{y^2}{B^2} = \cos^2(\omega t) + \sin^2(\omega t) = 1
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = -A\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + B\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_P = -A\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} - B\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} = -\,\omega^2\,(P - O)
$$
$$
(P - O) \wedge \vec{v}_P = \big( A\cos(\omega t)\,B\omega\cos(\omega t) + B\sin(\omega t)\,A\omega\sin(\omega t) \big)\,\hat{k} = A\,B\,\omega\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\frac{dA_s}{dt} = \frac{1}{2}\,\big| (P - O) \wedge \vec{v}_P \big| = \frac{1}{2}\,A\,B\,\omega ,
$$

where $A_s$ denotes the area swept by the segment $OP$, called so in order not to confuse it with the amplitude $A$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Dividing the first equation by $A$ and the second one by $B$, then adding the squares, time is eliminated and one obtains the equation of the **ellipse** with semiaxes $A$ and $B$ and center at $O$.

The acceleration is obtained by differentiating twice: each component is multiplied by $-\omega^2$, therefore $\vec{a}_P = -\omega^2\,(P - O)$. The acceleration vector is thus parallel to the position vector and of opposite orientation, that is it is **always directed toward the center** $O$; the motion is central with pole at $O$.

The component along $\hat{k}$ of the cross product $(P - O) \wedge \vec{v}_P$ equals $x\dot{y} - y\dot{x}$, that is $AB\omega\cos^2(\omega t) + AB\omega\sin^2(\omega t) = AB\omega$, constant. The areal velocity with respect to $O$ is therefore constant and equals $\dfrac{1}{2}AB\omega$, as it must be in a motion with central acceleration, where the transverse component of the acceleration is zero.

The point takes the time $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$ to travel along the ellipse, and the area swept in one period is $\dfrac{1}{2}AB\omega\,T = \pi AB$, that is the area of the ellipse.

</div>

Check. Verify that the transverse acceleration is zero, as the constancy of the areal velocity requires. Writing $P - O = r\,\hat{u}_r$ one has, from the identity $r\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\vartheta} = \dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{d}{dt}(r^2\dot{\vartheta})$ and from $r^2\dot{\vartheta} = x\dot{y} - y\dot{x} = AB\omega$,
$$
r\,\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\,\dot{r}\,\dot{\vartheta} = \frac{1}{r}\,\frac{d}{dt}\big( A\,B\,\omega \big) = 0 ,
$$
hence $\vec{a}_P$ has only a radial component, in agreement with $\vec{a}_P = -\omega^2 r\,\hat{u}_r$. In the particular case $B = A$ the ellipse becomes the circle of radius $A$ and the formulas give $|\vec{v}_P| = A\omega$ constant and $|\vec{a}_P| = A\omega^2$, that is uniform circular motion.
