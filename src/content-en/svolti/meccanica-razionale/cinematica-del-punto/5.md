## Exercise 1

A material point $P$ is constrained to the parabolic guide of equation
$$
y = \frac{x^2}{2\ell} ,
$$
drawn in the figure, and travels along it with constant horizontal component of the velocity, $\dot{x} = u > 0$; at the initial instant it is at the vertex. Compute the magnitude of the velocity, the intrinsic components of the acceleration and the radius of curvature as functions of $x$, and give their values at $x = 0$ and at $x = \ell$.

::grafico{curve="t,0.5*t*t,-1.7,2.0" punti="0,0,pieno;1,0.5,pieno,P" vettori="1,0.5,1.57,1.07" etichette="1.3,0.68,vₚ" uguale=1 xmin=-2.1 xmax=2.5 ymin=-0.4 ymax=2.3 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Parabolic guide $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2\ell}$ traveled with $\dot{x} = u$ constant"}

The coordinate $x$ is taken as the one that identifies the position on the guide, because the curve is the graph of a function of $x$ and the horizontal component of the velocity is the datum of the problem.

$$
y = \frac{x^2}{2\ell} ,
\qquad
\dot{y} = \frac{x\,\dot{x}}{\ell} = \frac{u\,x}{\ell} ,
\qquad
\ddot{y} = \frac{u\,\dot{x}}{\ell} = \frac{u^2}{\ell}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = u\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{u x}{\ell}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_P = \frac{u^2}{\ell}\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_P| = \frac{u}{\ell}\,\sqrt{\ell^2 + x^2}
$$
$$
a_t = \frac{\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{a}_P}{|\vec{v}_P|} = \frac{\dfrac{u^3 x}{\ell^2}}{\dfrac{u}{\ell}\sqrt{\ell^2 + x^2}} = \frac{u^2\,x}{\ell\,\sqrt{\ell^2 + x^2}}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P = \frac{u^3}{\ell}\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
a_n = \frac{u^2}{\sqrt{\ell^2 + x^2}} ,
\qquad
\rho = \frac{\big( \ell^2 + x^2 \big)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\ell^2}
$$

Values at the two points requested:
$$
x = 0 : \quad |\vec{v}_P| = u , \quad a_t = 0 , \quad a_n = \frac{u^2}{\ell} , \quad \rho = \ell ;
$$
$$
x = \ell : \quad |\vec{v}_P| = u\sqrt{2} , \quad a_t = \frac{u^2}{\sqrt{2}\,\ell} , \quad a_n = \frac{u^2}{\sqrt{2}\,\ell} , \quad \rho = 2\sqrt{2}\,\ell .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coordinate $y$ depends on time through $x$, hence its derivative is computed with the chain rule and equals $\dfrac{x\dot{x}}{\ell}$. Differentiating once more, and recalling that $\dot{x} = u$ is constant, one obtains $\ddot{y} = \dfrac{u^2}{\ell}$, therefore the acceleration is vertical and constant.

The magnitude of the velocity grows with $|x|$, since the guide becomes steeper and steeper while the horizontal component stays the same.

The tangential acceleration is obtained from the dot product of velocity and acceleration; it is zero at the vertex, where the velocity is horizontal and therefore orthogonal to the acceleration, and it is positive for $x > 0$, where the point accelerates.

At the vertex the whole acceleration is normal and the radius of curvature equals $\ell$; at $x = \ell$ the two intrinsic components are equal and the radius of curvature is almost three times as large, because the parabola straightens out as one moves away from the vertex.

</div>

Check. Compare the sum of the squares of the intrinsic components with the magnitude of the acceleration, which equals $\dfrac{u^2}{\ell}$:
$$
a_t^2 + a_n^2 = \frac{u^4x^2}{\ell^2\,(\ell^2 + x^2)} + \frac{u^4}{\ell^2 + x^2} = \frac{u^4\,(x^2 + \ell^2)}{\ell^2\,(\ell^2 + x^2)} = \frac{u^4}{\ell^2} .
$$
The radius of curvature is also checked with the formula of differential calculus for the graph of a function, $\rho = \dfrac{\big( 1 + y'^2 \big)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{|y''|}$, treated in the topic on derivatives in Analysis 1. With $y' = \dfrac{x}{\ell}$ and $y'' = \dfrac{1}{\ell}$ one obtains
$$
\rho = \frac{\left( 1 + \dfrac{x^2}{\ell^2} \right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\dfrac{1}{\ell}} = \frac{\big( \ell^2 + x^2 \big)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\ell^2} ,
$$
which coincides with the result.

## Exercise 2

A material point $P$ describes the cycloid in the figure with
$$
x(t) = R\,(\omega t - \sin\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = R\,(1 - \cos\omega t) ,
\qquad 0 \le \omega t \le 2\pi ,
$$
with $R > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ constants. Compute the magnitude of the velocity, the time law $s(t)$ with the origin of the arcs at the initial position, the intrinsic components of the acceleration, the radius of curvature and the length of one complete arch.

::grafico{curve="t-sin(t),1-cos(t),0,6.29" linee="-0.3,0 6.6,0" punti="0,0,pieno;3.14,2,pieno,P" uguale=1 xmin=-0.6 xmax=7.0 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.6 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="One arch of a cycloid, described by a point of the circle of radius $R$ that rolls without slipping on the $x$ axis"}

$$
\vec{v}_P = R\omega\,(1 - \cos\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\omega\sin\omega t\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_P|^2 = R^2\omega^2\big( 1 - 2\cos\omega t + \cos^2\omega t + \sin^2\omega t \big) = 2R^2\omega^2\,(1 - \cos\omega t)
$$
$$
1 - \cos\omega t = 2\sin^2\frac{\omega t}{2}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
|\vec{v}_P| = 2R\omega\,\sin\frac{\omega t}{2}
$$
$$
s(t) = \int_0^t 2R\omega\sin\frac{\omega \tau}{2}\,d\tau = 4R\left( 1 - \cos\frac{\omega t}{2} \right) ,
\qquad
s\!\left( \frac{2\pi}{\omega} \right) = 8R
$$
$$
a_t = \frac{d}{dt}|\vec{v}_P| = R\omega^2\cos\frac{\omega t}{2} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_P = R\omega^2\sin\omega t\,\hat{\imath} + R\omega^2\cos\omega t\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
|\vec{a}_P| = R\omega^2
$$
$$
a_n = \sqrt{|\vec{a}_P|^2 - a_t^2} = R\omega^2\sin\frac{\omega t}{2} ,
\qquad
\rho = \frac{\dot{s}^2}{a_n} = \frac{4R^2\omega^2\sin^2\dfrac{\omega t}{2}}{R\omega^2\sin\dfrac{\omega t}{2}} = 4R\,\sin\frac{\omega t}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Differentiating the two equations of motion one obtains the components of the velocity; adding their squares and using $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$ one arrives at $2R^2\omega^2(1 - \cos\omega t)$.

The half-angle formula $1 - \cos\varphi = 2\sin^2\dfrac{\varphi}{2}$ allows the square root to be extracted without an absolute value, since for $0 \le \omega t \le 2\pi$ one has $\sin\dfrac{\omega t}{2} \ge 0$. The magnitude of the velocity vanishes at the endpoints of the interval, that is at the points where the cycloid touches the $x$ axis, and it equals $2R\omega$ at the highest point.

The arc length coordinate is obtained by integrating the magnitude of the velocity; over one complete arch it equals $8R$, that is **eight times the radius** of the circle that generates the curve.

The acceleration has constant magnitude $R\omega^2$. The tangential acceleration is the derivative of the magnitude of the velocity, hence it equals $R\omega^2\cos\dfrac{\omega t}{2}$; the normal acceleration is obtained from the relation $a_t^2 + a_n^2 = |\vec{a}_P|^2$, since $\sin\dfrac{\omega t}{2} \ge 0$ in the interval considered.

The radius of curvature equals $4R\sin\dfrac{\omega t}{2}$, hence it vanishes at the points of contact with the $x$ axis, where the curve has a cusp, and it equals $4R$ at the highest point.

</div>

Check. Compute the radius of curvature again with the cross product formula. One has
$$
\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P = \Big[ R\omega(1 - \cos\omega t)\,R\omega^2\cos\omega t - R\omega\sin\omega t\,R\omega^2\sin\omega t \Big]\,\hat{k} = R^2\omega^3\,(\cos\omega t - 1)\,\hat{k} ,
$$
hence $|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P| = 2R^2\omega^3\sin^2\dfrac{\omega t}{2}$ and
$$
\rho = \frac{|\vec{v}_P|^3}{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|} = \frac{8R^3\omega^3\sin^3\dfrac{\omega t}{2}}{2R^2\omega^3\sin^2\dfrac{\omega t}{2}} = 4R\,\sin\frac{\omega t}{2} ,
$$
in agreement with the value found.

## Exercise 3

A material point $P$ moves on the circular guide with center $O$ and radius $R$ in the figure; the angle $\vartheta$, measured at the center starting from the downward vertical, varies with the law
$$
\vartheta(t) = \vartheta_0\,\sin(\Omega t) ,
$$
with $0 < \vartheta_0 < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $\Omega > 0$ constants. Compute the scalar velocity, the intrinsic components of the acceleration and their values at the instants $t = 0$ and $\Omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,0.47,-0.88" punti="0,0;0.47,-0.88" linee="-0.87,-0.5 -0.82,-0.57 -0.77,-0.64 -0.71,-0.71 -0.64,-0.77 -0.57,-0.82 -0.5,-0.87 -0.42,-0.91 -0.34,-0.94 -0.26,-0.97 -0.17,-0.98 -0.09,-1.0 0.0,-1.0 0.09,-1.0 0.17,-0.98 0.26,-0.97 0.34,-0.94 0.42,-0.91 0.5,-0.87 0.57,-0.82 0.64,-0.77 0.71,-0.71 0.77,-0.64 0.82,-0.57 0.87,-0.5;0,0 0.47,-0.88;0.0,-0.33 0.02,-0.33 0.05,-0.33 0.07,-0.32 0.09,-0.32 0.11,-0.31 0.13,-0.3 0.15,-0.29" tratteggi="0,0 0,-1.15" frecce="0.47,-0.88,0.91,-0.64,v_P;0,0,1.5,0,x;0,0,0,0.9,y" etichette="0.02,-0.52,ϑ;0.4,-0.38,R" riferimento=0 titolo="Point on a circular guide of radius $R$: the angle $\vartheta$ is measured from the downward vertical counterclockwise"}

$$
s = R\,\vartheta = R\,\vartheta_0\sin(\Omega t)
$$
$$
\dot{s} = R\,\vartheta_0\,\Omega\cos(\Omega t) ,
\qquad
\ddot{s} = -\,R\,\vartheta_0\,\Omega^2\sin(\Omega t)
$$
$$
a_t = \ddot{s} = -\,R\,\vartheta_0\,\Omega^2\sin(\Omega t) ,
\qquad
a_n = \frac{\dot{s}^2}{R} = R\,\vartheta_0^2\,\Omega^2\cos^2(\Omega t)
$$

Values requested:
$$
t = 0 : \quad a_t = 0 , \quad a_n = R\,\vartheta_0^2\,\Omega^2 ;
\qquad
\Omega t = \frac{\pi}{2} : \quad a_t = -\,R\,\vartheta_0\,\Omega^2 , \quad a_n = 0 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the circular guide the arc and the angle are linked by $s = R\vartheta$, therefore the time law is obtained by multiplying by $R$ the law given for the angle. The motion is harmonic in the arc, with amplitude $R\vartheta_0$ and angular frequency $\Omega$.

The scalar velocity is the derivative of the arc and its magnitude is maximum at the passages through the lowest point, where $\cos(\Omega t) = \pm 1$; it vanishes instead at the extreme positions, where $\vartheta = \pm\vartheta_0$.

The tangential acceleration, equal to $\ddot{s}$, is proportional to $-\sin(\Omega t)$, hence it is opposite to the angular displacement and it vanishes at the passage through the vertical.

The normal acceleration is $\dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{R}$ and it vanishes at the extreme positions, where the point is at rest. In the two positions requested the acceleration is therefore **entirely normal** at the passage through the vertical and **entirely tangential** at the position of maximum displacement.

</div>

Check. Repeat the computation with the formulas in polar coordinates with pole $O$, with $r = R$ constant and with the angle $\vartheta$ measured from the downward vertical, so that the transverse unit vector is tangent to the guide:
$$
\vec{a}_P = -R\,\dot{\vartheta}^2\,\hat{u}_r + R\,\ddot{\vartheta}\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = -R\,\vartheta_0^2\Omega^2\cos^2(\Omega t)\,\hat{u}_r - R\,\vartheta_0\Omega^2\sin(\Omega t)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta .
$$
The component along $\hat{u}_\vartheta$ coincides with $a_t$ and the one along $\hat{u}_r$ with $-a_n$, because $\hat{n} = -\hat{u}_r$. As a second check, for small $\vartheta_0$ the arc performs a harmonic motion of amplitude $R\vartheta_0$ and angular frequency $\Omega$, whose maximum magnitude of the tangential acceleration equals $R\vartheta_0\Omega^2$, in agreement with the value found at $\Omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.
