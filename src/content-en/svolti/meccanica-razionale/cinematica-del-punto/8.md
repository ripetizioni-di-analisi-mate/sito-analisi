## Exercise 1

A point $Q$ is at rest in the fixed frame, at the position $Q - O = d\,\hat{\imath}$ with $d > 0$. A moving frame has origin at $O$ and rotates about the $z$ axis with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$, with $\omega > 0$; at the instant $t = 0$ the moving unit vectors coincide with the fixed ones. The situation is in the figure. Determine the relative trajectory of $Q$, its relative velocity and its relative acceleration.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;Q,1.4,0" punti="0,0;1.4,0" tratteggi="1.4,0.0 1.37,0.27 1.29,0.54 1.16,0.78 0.99,0.99 0.78,1.16 0.54,1.29 0.27,1.37 0.0,1.4 -0.27,1.37 -0.54,1.29 -0.78,1.16 -0.99,0.99 -1.16,0.78 -1.29,0.54 -1.37,0.27 -1.4,0.0 -1.37,-0.27 -1.29,-0.54 -1.16,-0.78 -0.99,-0.99 -0.78,-1.16 -0.54,-1.29 -0.27,-1.37 0.0,-1.4 0.27,-1.37 0.54,-1.29 0.78,-1.16 0.99,-0.99 1.16,-0.78 1.29,-0.54 1.37,-0.27 1.4,0.0" frecce="0,0,2.1,0,x;0,0,0,2.0,y;0,0,1.53,1.29,ξ;0,0,-1.29,1.53,η" coppie="-1.1 -1.0,1,ω" quote="O,Q,d,-0.4" titolo="Point $Q$ at rest in the fixed frame: for the observer attached to the rotating triad $(\xi, \eta)$ it describes the dashed circle"}

The coordinates of $Q$ in the moving frame are obtained by projecting $Q - O$ onto the moving unit vectors $\hat{e}_1 = \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{e}_2 = -\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$.

$$
\xi = (Q - O) \cdot \hat{e}_1 = d\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
\eta = (Q - O) \cdot \hat{e}_2 = -\,d\sin(\omega t)
$$
$$
\xi^2 + \eta^2 = d^2
$$
$$
\vec{v}_a = \vec{0} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_a = \vec{0}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_r = \vec{v}_a - \vec{v}_\tau = -\,\vec{\omega} \wedge (Q - O) ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_r| = \omega\,d
$$
$$
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r = -\,2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \big( \vec{\omega} \wedge (Q - O) \big) = 2\,\omega^2\,(Q - O)
$$
$$
\vec{a}_r = \vec{a}_a - \vec{a}_\tau - \vec{a}_c = \omega^2\,(Q - O) - 2\,\omega^2\,(Q - O) = -\,\omega^2\,(Q - O)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relative coordinates are obtained by projecting the vector $Q - O$, which is constant, onto the two moving unit vectors, which rotate with angular velocity $\omega$. The sum of their squares equals $d^2$ at every instant, therefore the **relative trajectory is the circle** with center $O$ and radius $d$.

The angle that identifies the point in the moving frame is $-\omega t$, hence for the moving observer the point travels along that circle clockwise with angular velocity of magnitude $\omega$. This is the motion that an observer in rotation attributes to an object at rest.

Since $Q$ is at rest in the fixed frame, the absolute velocity and the absolute acceleration are zero; the composition theorems are then read backwards and give the relative velocity and acceleration starting from the transport and Coriolis terms.

The transport acceleration is the centripetal term $-\omega^2\,(Q - O)$ alone, because the moving origin is fixed and the angular velocity is constant. The Coriolis acceleration, computed with $\vec{v}_r = -\vec{\omega} \wedge (Q - O)$, equals $2\omega^2\,(Q - O)$. Their sum with the sign changed gives $\vec{a}_r = -\omega^2\,(Q - O)$, that is a centripetal acceleration of magnitude $\omega^2 d$.

</div>

Check. The relative velocity and acceleration are computed again by differentiating the relative coordinates directly, as the moving observer would do:
$$
\dot{\xi} = -d\,\omega\sin(\omega t) ,
\quad
\dot{\eta} = -d\,\omega\cos(\omega t)
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
|\vec{v}_r| = d\,\omega ,
$$
$$
\ddot{\xi} = -d\,\omega^2\cos(\omega t) = -\omega^2\xi ,
\quad
\ddot{\eta} = d\,\omega^2\sin(\omega t) = -\omega^2\eta
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\vec{a}_r = -\,\omega^2\,(Q - O) ,
$$
in agreement with the results obtained from the composition theorems.

## Exercise 2

A point $P$ moves in the fixed frame with uniform rectilinear motion along the $x$ axis, with $P - O = u t\,\hat{\imath}$ and $u > 0$. A moving frame has origin at $O$ and rotates about the $z$ axis with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$; at the instant $t = 0$ the moving unit vectors coincide with the fixed ones. The relative trajectory is dashed in the figure. Determine the relative trajectory, the magnitude of the relative velocity and the magnitude of the relative acceleration.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0" punti="0,0" linee="0,0 2.4,0" tratteggi="0.0,0.0 0.06,-0.01 0.11,-0.05 0.15,-0.11 0.16,-0.19 0.14,-0.27 0.1,-0.35 0.02,-0.43 -0.08,-0.48 -0.21,-0.51 -0.35,-0.5 -0.49,-0.45 -0.63,-0.37 -0.76,-0.24 -0.85,-0.08 -0.91,0.12 -0.92,0.33 -0.88,0.55 -0.78,0.77 -0.63,0.98 -0.42,1.15 -0.17,1.27 0.11,1.34 0.42,1.34 0.73,1.27 1.02,1.13 1.29,0.92 1.52,0.64 1.68,0.31 1.77,-0.07 1.77,-0.46 1.68,-0.86 1.5,-1.24 1.24,-1.59 0.89,-1.87 0.48,-2.08 0.02,-2.2 -0.47,-2.21 -0.96,-2.11 -1.43,-1.9" frecce="0,0,2.7,0,x;0,0,0,1.8,y" coppie="-1.5 0.9,1,ω" etichette="1.9,0.18,absolute trajectory" titolo="Rectilinear absolute trajectory and spiral relative trajectory, seen from the frame that rotates with angular velocity $\omega$"}

In the moving frame the polar coordinates $r_{\text{rel}}$ and $\vartheta_{\text{rel}}$ are used, because the distance from the origin is the same in the two frames and the relative angle is obtained by subtracting the rotation of the moving triad.

$$
r_{\text{rel}} = |P - O| = u t ,
\qquad
\vartheta_{\text{rel}} = 0 - \omega t = -\,\omega t
$$
$$
r_{\text{rel}} = \frac{u}{\omega}\,\big| \vartheta_{\text{rel}} \big|
$$
$$
\dot{r}_{\text{rel}} = u ,
\qquad
\ddot{r}_{\text{rel}} = 0 ,
\qquad
\dot{\vartheta}_{\text{rel}} = -\,\omega ,
\qquad
\ddot{\vartheta}_{\text{rel}} = 0
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_r| = \sqrt{\dot{r}_{\text{rel}}^2 + r_{\text{rel}}^2\,\dot{\vartheta}_{\text{rel}}^2} = u\,\sqrt{1 + \omega^2t^2}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_r = \big( \ddot{r}_{\text{rel}} - r_{\text{rel}}\dot{\vartheta}_{\text{rel}}^2 \big)\,\hat{u}_r + \big( r_{\text{rel}}\ddot{\vartheta}_{\text{rel}} + 2\dot{r}_{\text{rel}}\dot{\vartheta}_{\text{rel}} \big)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = -\,u\,\omega^2 t\,\hat{u}_r - 2\,u\,\omega\,\hat{u}_\vartheta
$$
$$
|\vec{a}_r| = u\,\omega\,\sqrt{\omega^2 t^2 + 4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance of $P$ from the origin does not depend on the frame, because the moving origin coincides with the fixed one, hence $r_{\text{rel}} = ut$. The angle measured by the moving observer is instead the difference between the absolute angle, which is zero because the point stays on the $x$ axis, and the angle $\omega t$ by which the moving triad has rotated.

Eliminating time between the two relative coordinates one obtains $r_{\text{rel}} = \dfrac{u}{\omega}|\vartheta_{\text{rel}}|$, hence the relative trajectory is an **Archimedean spiral**, traveled clockwise.

The formulas in polar coordinates apply in the moving frame because the moving observer sees his own unit vectors as constant, hence for him the same differentiation rules as in the fixed frame hold. The relative velocity has radial component $u$ and transverse component $-\omega ut$, and its magnitude grows with time.

The relative acceleration is different from zero even though the absolute motion is uniform rectilinear. The radial term $-u\omega^2t$ is centripetal and the transverse term $-2u\omega$ is constant. The magnitude is obtained from the square root of the sum of the squares, since $\big( \omega^2 t \big)^2 + \big( 2\omega \big)^2 = \omega^2\big( \omega^2t^2 + 4 \big)$.

</div>

Check. The relative acceleration is obtained from the theorem on the composition of accelerations, knowing that the absolute one is zero because the absolute motion is uniform rectilinear. One has $\vec{a}_\tau = -\omega^2\,(P-O)$, of magnitude $\omega^2 ut$ and directed toward $O$, while
$$
\vec{v}_r = \vec{v}_a - \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) = u\,\hat{\imath} - \omega\,ut\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r = 2\,\omega\,u\,\hat{\jmath} + 2\,\omega^2 u t\,\hat{\imath} ,
$$
computed at the instant $t$ in the fixed basis, which at a generic $t$ is read on the moving basis as radial component $2\omega^2ut$ and transverse component $2\omega u$. Then
$$
\vec{a}_r = -\,\vec{a}_\tau - \vec{a}_c
$$
has radial component $\omega^2ut - 2\omega^2ut = -\omega^2 ut$ and transverse component $-2\omega u$, in agreement with the result.

## Exercise 3

A moving frame has origin $\Omega$ coinciding with $O$ and rotates with angular velocity constant in the moving basis
$$
\vec{\omega} = \frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}\,\big( \hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_3 \big) ,
\qquad \omega > 0 .
$$
A point $P$ moves along the $\zeta$ axis of the moving frame with $P - \Omega = u t\,\hat{e}_3$ and $u > 0$; the diagram is in the figure. Compute the absolute velocity, the three accelerations of the composition theorem and the absolute acceleration, all expressed in the moving basis.

::struttura{nodi="Ω,0,0;P,0,1.1" punti="0,0;0,1.1" frecce="0,0,-1.28,-0.6,ξ;0,0,1.6,0,η;0,0,0,2.25,ζ;0,0,-0.96,0.68,ω;0,1.1,0,1.7,v_r;0,1.1,-0.85,1.1,a_c" riferimento=0 titolo="Moving frame rotating about the bisector of the axes $\xi$ and $\zeta$, drawn in projection, with the point $P$ running along the $\zeta$ axis"}

The moving origin is fixed and the angular velocity is constant in the moving basis, hence $\vec{a}_\Omega = \vec{0}$ and $\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \dfrac{d^{*}\vec{\omega}}{dt} + \vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$.

$$
\vec{v}_r = u\,\hat{e}_3 ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_r = \vec{0}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_\tau = \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega) = \frac{\omega\,u t}{\sqrt{2}}\,\big( \hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_3 \big) = -\,\frac{\omega\,u t}{\sqrt{2}}\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{v}_a = u\,\hat{e}_3 - \frac{\omega\,u t}{\sqrt{2}}\,\hat{e}_2 ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_a| = u\,\sqrt{1 + \frac{\omega^2t^2}{2}}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r = \frac{2\,\omega\,u}{\sqrt{2}}\,\big( \hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_3 \big) = -\,\sqrt{2}\,\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{a}_\tau = \vec{\omega} \wedge \big( \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - \Omega) \big) = \frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}\,\big( \hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_3 \big) \wedge \left( -\frac{\omega u t}{\sqrt{2}}\,\hat{e}_2 \right) = \frac{\omega^2 u t}{2}\,\big( \hat{e}_1 - \hat{e}_3 \big)
$$
$$
\vec{a}_a = \frac{\omega^2 u t}{2}\,\hat{e}_1 - \sqrt{2}\,\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_2 - \frac{\omega^2 u t}{2}\,\hat{e}_3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relative motion is rectilinear and uniform along the $\zeta$ axis, hence the relative velocity is $u\,\hat{e}_3$ and the relative acceleration is zero.

In the cross products the relations of the right-handed orthonormal basis are used: $\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_3 = -\hat{e}_2$, $\hat{e}_1 \wedge \hat{e}_2 = \hat{e}_3$ and $\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_2 = -\hat{e}_1$. The term $\hat{e}_3 \wedge \hat{e}_3$ is zero, therefore in the transport velocity only the contribution of $\hat{e}_1$ remains.

The Coriolis acceleration is different from zero because the relative velocity, directed as $\hat{e}_3$, forms with $\vec{\omega}$ an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and is therefore not parallel to the axis of rotation. Its magnitude equals $2\,|\vec{\omega}|\,|\vec{v}_r|\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} = \sqrt{2}\,\omega u$.

The transport acceleration is computed by taking the cross product with $\vec{\omega}$ twice and has opposite components along $\hat{e}_1$ and $\hat{e}_3$; it is orthogonal to $\vec{\omega}$, as it must be for a centripetal term with respect to the axis of rotation.

</div>

Check. Verify that the transport acceleration is orthogonal to the angular velocity and directed toward the axis of rotation:
$$
\vec{a}_\tau \cdot \vec{\omega} = \frac{\omega^2ut}{2}\,\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}}\,\big( \hat{e}_1 - \hat{e}_3 \big) \cdot \big( \hat{e}_1 + \hat{e}_3 \big) = \frac{\omega^3ut}{2\sqrt{2}}\,(1 - 1) = 0 .
$$
The magnitude of the transport acceleration is also checked with the formula $|\vec{a}_\tau| = \omega^2 r_\perp$, where $r_\perp$ is the distance of $P$ from the axis of rotation. Since $P - \Omega = ut\,\hat{e}_3$ forms with $\vec{\omega}$ the angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, one has $r_\perp = ut\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{ut}{\sqrt{2}}$, hence
$$
|\vec{a}_\tau| = \omega^2\,\frac{ut}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\omega^2ut}{\sqrt{2}} ,
$$
which coincides with the magnitude of $\dfrac{\omega^2ut}{2}\big( \hat{e}_1 - \hat{e}_3 \big)$, equal to $\dfrac{\omega^2ut}{2}\sqrt{2}$.
