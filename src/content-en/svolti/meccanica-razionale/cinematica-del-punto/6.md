## Exercise 1

A straight rod rotates in the plane $Oxy$ about its fixed end $O$ with constant angular velocity $\omega$, so that the angle it forms with the $x$ axis equals $\vartheta = \omega t$. A slider $P$ runs along the rod with $|P - O| = s_0 + u t$, where $s_0 > 0$ and $u > 0$ are constants; the system is in the figure. Compute the relative velocity, the transport velocity and the absolute velocity of $P$, the magnitude of the absolute velocity and the instant at which it forms an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ with the rod, under the assumption $s_0 < \dfrac{u}{\omega}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;B,2.3,1.93;P,1.38,1.16" aste="O-B" punti="0,0;1.38,1.16" linee="0.5,0.0 0.5,0.04 0.49,0.09 0.48,0.13 0.47,0.17 0.45,0.21 0.43,0.25 0.41,0.29 0.38,0.32" frecce="1.38,1.16,1.92,1.61,v_r;1.38,1.16,0.8,1.85,v_τ;0,0,2.9,0,x;0,0,0,2.4,y" coppie="0.35 0.75,1,ω" etichette="0.63,0.08,ϑ;0.85,0.78,r" riferimento=0 titolo="Slider $P$ on a rod rotating about $O$: the relative velocity is directed along the rod, the transport velocity is orthogonal to it"}

The moving frame attached to the rod is chosen, with origin at $O$ and first unit vector $\hat{e}_1$ directed from $O$ toward the slider; the second unit vector $\hat{e}_2$ is obtained by rotating $\hat{e}_1$ by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ counterclockwise. With this choice the angular velocity of the moving frame is $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$ and the relative motion of the slider is uniform rectilinear.

$$
P - O = (s_0 + ut)\,\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_O = \vec{0} ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_r = \frac{d^{*}}{dt}(P - O) = u\,\hat{e}_1
$$
$$
\vec{v}_\tau = \vec{v}_O + \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) = \omega\,(s_0 + ut)\,\big( \hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_1 \big) = \omega\,(s_0 + ut)\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\tau + \vec{v}_r = u\,\hat{e}_1 + \omega\,(s_0 + ut)\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_a| = \sqrt{u^2 + \omega^2\,(s_0 + ut)^2}
$$

Angle $\beta$ between $\vec{v}_a$ and the rod:
$$
\tan\beta = \frac{\omega\,(s_0 + ut)}{u} = 1
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
s_0 + u t^* = \frac{u}{\omega}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
t^* = \frac{1}{\omega} - \frac{s_0}{u} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moving frame is chosen attached to the rod because in it the constraint is fixed, since the slider runs along a line that, for the moving observer, does not move. The relative motion is then rectilinear and uniform, with velocity $u$ directed along $\hat{e}_1$.

The transport velocity is the one that a point rigidly attached to the rod at the position occupied by $P$ would have; since the origin $O$ is fixed, it reduces to $\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O)$. The product $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_1$ equals $\hat{e}_2$, therefore the transport velocity is orthogonal to the rod and has magnitude $\omega\,|P - O|$.

The absolute velocity is the sum of the two, by the theorem on the composition of velocities, and its two components are orthogonal: the magnitude is therefore the square root of the sum of the squares.

The angle between the absolute velocity and the rod is obtained from the ratio of the component orthogonal to the rod to the one parallel to it. Requiring that it equal $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ one obtains the equality of the two components, that is $\omega(s_0 + ut) = u$. The assumption $s_0 < \dfrac{u}{\omega}$ guarantees that the instant $t^*$ is **positive**, hence that the required configuration is reached.

</div>

Check. The absolute velocity is computed again with the formulas in polar coordinates with pole $O$, with $r = s_0 + ut$ and $\vartheta = \omega t$. Since $\hat{e}_1 = \hat{u}_r$ and $\hat{e}_2 = \hat{u}_\vartheta$, one has
$$
\vec{v}_P = \dot{r}\,\hat{u}_r + r\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = u\,\hat{u}_r + \omega\,(s_0 + ut)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta ,
$$
which coincides with the result obtained with the composition theorem. A second check is made in the limiting case $u = 0$: the slider stays still on the rod at distance $s_0$, the relative velocity vanishes and $\vec{v}_a = \omega s_0\,\hat{e}_2$ remains, that is the velocity of the uniform circular motion of radius $s_0$.

## Exercise 2

A circular platform of radius $R$ rotates in its own plane about the fixed center $O$ with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$, with $\omega > 0$. A point $P$ moves along the edge of the platform, as in the figure, with relative velocity of magnitude $u > 0$ directed clockwise. Compute the absolute velocity of $P$ and determine for which value of $u$ the point stays still with respect to the fixed frame.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,1.5" nodi="O,0,0;P,0.86,1.23" nomi=0 punti="0,0,O,centro;0.86,1.23,P" frecce="0.86,1.23,1.52,0.77,v_r" coppie="-0.8 -0.7,1,ω" titolo="Point moving on the edge of a rotating platform: the relative velocity is tangent to the edge and directed clockwise"}

The moving frame attached to the platform is taken, with origin at the fixed center $O$. In the moving frame the point travels along the circle of radius $R$ with constant scalar velocity, hence the relative velocity is tangent to the edge.

$$
\vec{v}_r = -u\,\hat{u}_\vartheta ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_\tau = \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) = \omega\,R\,\hat{u}_\vartheta
$$
$$
\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\tau + \vec{v}_r = \big( \omega R - u \big)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta
$$
$$
\vec{v}_a = \vec{0}
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
u = \omega\,R
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The transverse unit vector $\hat{u}_\vartheta$ is tangent to the edge and points counterclockwise, hence a relative velocity directed clockwise is written $-u\,\hat{u}_\vartheta$.

The transport velocity is that of the point of the platform which at the instant considered is at $P$: it equals $\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O)$, and since $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{u}_r = \hat{u}_\vartheta$ it turns out to be $\omega R\,\hat{u}_\vartheta$, tangent to the edge and directed counterclockwise.

The two velocities are parallel, therefore the absolute velocity is tangent to the edge and has component $\omega R - u$. The fixed observer thus sees the point turn counterclockwise if $u < \omega R$ and clockwise if $u > \omega R$.

The point is still with respect to the fixed frame when the two components cancel each other, that is for $u = \omega R$. In that case the relative motion compensates the transport, therefore the point always occupies the **same position** in the fixed frame.

</div>

Check. The result is checked by writing the motion directly in the fixed frame. The angle that identifies $P$ with respect to the $x$ axis is the sum of the angle of rotation of the platform and of the angle described on the edge, that is $\vartheta(t) = \omega t - \dfrac{u}{R}\,t$. From the formulas in polar coordinates with $r = R$ constant one obtains
$$
\vec{v}_P = R\,\dot{\vartheta}\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = \left( \omega R - u \right)\hat{u}_\vartheta ,
$$
which coincides with the result, and vanishes for $u = \omega R$.

## Exercise 3

A cart translates along the $x$ axis with the law $x_\Omega(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\,a_0 t^2$, with $a_0 > 0$ constant. At the point $\Omega$ of the cart a rod of length $\ell$ is hinged, which rotates with respect to the cart with constant angular velocity $\omega$, so that the angle it forms with the $x$ axis equals $\vartheta = \omega t$; the system is in the figure. Compute the absolute velocity and the absolute acceleration of the free end $A$ of the rod.

::struttura{nodi="Ω,1.6,0;A,2.63,1.23" aste="Ω-A" vincoli="Ω,carrello,270" linee="-0.3,0 4.6,0;2.15,0.0 2.15,0.06 2.14,0.12 2.12,0.18 2.1,0.23 2.07,0.29 2.04,0.33 2.0,0.38 1.95,0.42" punti="2.63,1.23" frecce="1.6,-0.55,2.6,-0.55,v_Ω;0,0,0,1.5,y" coppie="0.9 1.0,1,ω" etichette="2.3,0.12,ϑ;2.0,0.75,ℓ" titolo="Cart translating along the $x$ axis with a hinged rod that rotates with respect to the cart"}

The moving frame attached to the cart is taken, with origin at $\Omega$ and unit vectors parallel to the fixed ones. The cart translates, hence the angular velocity of the moving frame is zero and the composition theorem reduces to the sum of the velocities and to that of the accelerations, without a Coriolis term.

$$
\vec{\omega}_{\text{frame}} = \vec{0} ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_\Omega = a_0 t\,\hat{\imath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_\Omega = a_0\,\hat{\imath}
$$
$$
A - \Omega = \ell\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_r = -\ell\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_r = -\ell\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_a = \vec{v}_\Omega + \vec{v}_r = \big( a_0 t - \ell\omega\sin(\omega t) \big)\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_a = \vec{a}_\Omega + \vec{a}_r = \big( a_0 - \ell\omega^2\cos(\omega t) \big)\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unit vectors of the frame attached to the cart stay parallel to $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$, because the cart translates without rotating; its angular velocity is therefore zero and Poisson's formulas give zero derivatives for the moving unit vectors.

In the frame of the cart the end $A$ describes the circle with center $\Omega$ and radius $\ell$ with angular velocity $\omega$, therefore the relative velocity is tangent to that circle and the relative acceleration is centripetal, directed from $A$ toward $\Omega$.

The transport velocity coincides with $\vec{v}_\Omega$, since $\vec{\omega}_{\text{frame}} \wedge (A - \Omega) = \vec{0}$; for the same reason the transport acceleration coincides with $\vec{a}_\Omega$ and the Coriolis acceleration is **zero**.

The absolute velocity is therefore the vector sum of the velocity of the cart and of the velocity with which the end turns about the hinge, and the same holds for the accelerations.

</div>

Check. Differentiate directly in the fixed frame the position of $A$, which equals
$$
A - O = \left( \frac{1}{2}\,a_0t^2 + \ell\cos(\omega t) \right)\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
Differentiating once one obtains $\big( a_0t - \ell\omega\sin(\omega t) \big)\hat{\imath} + \ell\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$ and differentiating twice $\big( a_0 - \ell\omega^2\cos(\omega t) \big)\hat{\imath} - \ell\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$: the two results coincide with those obtained with the composition theorems. In the limiting case $\omega = 0$ the rod stays still with respect to the cart and the formulas give $\vec{v}_a = a_0t\,\hat{\imath}$ and $\vec{a}_a = a_0\,\hat{\imath}$, that is the motion of the cart.
