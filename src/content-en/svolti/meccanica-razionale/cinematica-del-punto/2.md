## Exercise 1

A material point $P$ starts from the origin with horizontal velocity $v_0\,\hat{\imath}$ and moves in the vertical plane $Oxy$ with constant acceleration $-g\,\hat{\jmath}$, so that
$$
x(t) = v_0 t ,
\qquad
y(t) = -\frac{1}{2}\,g t^2 .
$$
The trajectory is drawn in the figure. Compute the tangential and normal components of the acceleration and the radius of curvature as functions of time, and the radius of curvature at the initial instant.

::grafico{curve="t,-0.5*t*t,0,2.2;cos(t),-1+sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="0,0,1,0;0,0,0,-1" segmenti="0,-1,-1,-1" punti="0,0,pieno;0,-1,pieno,C" etichette="0.55,0.18,vₚ;0.12,-0.62,aₚ;-0.65,-0.88,ρ" uguale=1 xmin=-1.4 xmax=2.5 ymin=-2.6 ymax=0.5 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Parabolic trajectory and osculating circle at the origin, with center $C$ and radius $\rho = \dfrac{v_0^2}{g}$"}

The frame is the given one; the unknowns are $a_t$, $a_n$ and $\rho$, which are obtained from the Cartesian components of the velocity and of the acceleration.

$$
\vec{v}_P = v_0\,\hat{\imath} - g t\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_P = -g\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_P| = \sqrt{v_0^2 + g^2t^2}
$$
$$
a_t = \frac{\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{a}_P}{|\vec{v}_P|} = \frac{g^2 t}{\sqrt{v_0^2 + g^2t^2}}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P = -v_0\,g\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
a_n = \frac{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|}{|\vec{v}_P|} = \frac{v_0\,g}{\sqrt{v_0^2 + g^2t^2}}
$$
$$
\rho = \frac{|\vec{v}_P|^3}{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|} = \frac{\big( v_0^2 + g^2t^2 \big)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{v_0\,g} ,
\qquad
\rho(0) = \frac{v_0^2}{g}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity is obtained by differentiating the two equations of motion and the acceleration is the datum of the problem, constant and directed downward.

The dot product $\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{a}_P$ equals $(v_0)(0) + (-gt)(-g) = g^2t$; dividing it by the magnitude of the velocity one obtains the tangential acceleration. It is positive for $t > 0$, therefore the magnitude of the velocity grows while the point descends.

The cross product has the component along $\hat{k}$ alone, equal to $v_0 \cdot (-g) - (-gt)\cdot 0 = -v_0 g$. Its magnitude divided by $|\vec{v}_P|$ gives the normal acceleration, which decreases in time, since the numerator stays constant while the magnitude of the velocity grows.

At the initial instant the velocity is horizontal and the acceleration is vertical, hence the acceleration is entirely normal and the radius of curvature equals $\dfrac{v_0^2}{g}$. This is also the radius of the **osculating circle** at the origin, whose center lies on the vertical below $O$, on the concave side.

</div>

Check. The two intrinsic components must combine into the magnitude of the acceleration, which equals $g$ at every instant:
$$
a_t^2 + a_n^2 = \frac{g^4t^2 + v_0^2g^2}{v_0^2 + g^2t^2} = g^2\,\frac{g^2t^2 + v_0^2}{v_0^2 + g^2t^2} = g^2 .
$$
One also checks the radius of curvature with the relation $a_n = \dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}$: from $\dot{s} = |\vec{v}_P|$ there follows
$$
\rho = \frac{|\vec{v}_P|^2}{a_n} = \frac{v_0^2 + g^2t^2}{\dfrac{v_0 g}{\sqrt{v_0^2 + g^2t^2}}} = \frac{\big( v_0^2 + g^2t^2 \big)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{v_0 g} ,
$$
which coincides with the result found.

## Exercise 2

A material point $P$ travels along the ellipse in the figure with the equations
$$
x(t) = 2\ell\cos(\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = \ell\sin(\omega t) ,
$$
with $\ell > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ constants. Compute the radius of curvature at the vertex $A = O + 2\ell\,\hat{\imath}$ and at the vertex $B = O + \ell\,\hat{\jmath}$, and say at which of the two the trajectory is more curved.

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" punti="2,0,pieno,A;0,1,pieno,B;0,0,pieno" vettori="2,0,2,0.8;0,1,-0.8,1" etichette="2.1,0.5,vₚ;-0.95,1.2,vₚ" uguale=1 xmin=-2.6 xmax=2.9 ymin=-1.6 ymax=1.7 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Ellipse with semiaxes $2\ell$ and $\ell$, traveled counterclockwise: $A$ is the vertex on the major semiaxis, $B$ the one on the minor semiaxis"}

$$
\vec{v}_P = -2\ell\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_P = -2\ell\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P = \big( 2\ell^2\omega^3\sin^2(\omega t) + 2\ell^2\omega^3\cos^2(\omega t) \big)\,\hat{k} = 2\ell^2\omega^3\,\hat{k}
$$

At the vertex $A$, reached for $\omega t = 0$:
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = \ell\omega ,
\qquad
\rho_A = \frac{\ell^3\omega^3}{2\ell^2\omega^3} = \frac{\ell}{2} .
$$

At the vertex $B$, reached for $\omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$:
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = 2\ell\omega ,
\qquad
\rho_B = \frac{8\ell^3\omega^3}{2\ell^2\omega^3} = 4\ell .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross product of velocity and acceleration has the component along $\hat{k}$ alone, equal to $(-2\ell\omega\sin)(-\ell\omega^2\sin) - (\ell\omega\cos)(-2\ell\omega^2\cos)$, that is $2\ell^2\omega^3(\sin^2 + \cos^2) = 2\ell^2\omega^3$, which is constant throughout the motion.

At the vertex $A$ one has $\omega t = 0$, hence the velocity is $\ell\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$ and its magnitude is $\ell\omega$; at the vertex $B$ one has $\omega t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, hence the velocity is $-2\ell\omega\,\hat{\imath}$ and its magnitude is $2\ell\omega$. Since the cross product is the same at the two points, the radius of curvature depends only on the cube of the magnitude of the velocity.

It turns out that $\rho_A = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$ and $\rho_B = 4\ell$, therefore the trajectory is **more curved at the vertex on the major semiaxis**, where the radius of curvature is eight times smaller. At the two vertices the acceleration is entirely normal, since the velocity is parallel to one axis and the acceleration to the other.

</div>

Check. For an ellipse with semiaxes $a$ and $b$ the radius of curvature at the vertex of the semiaxis $a$ equals $\dfrac{b^2}{a}$ and at the vertex of the semiaxis $b$ it equals $\dfrac{a^2}{b}$. With $a = 2\ell$ and $b = \ell$ one obtains
$$
\frac{b^2}{a} = \frac{\ell^2}{2\ell} = \frac{\ell}{2} = \rho_A ,
\qquad
\frac{a^2}{b} = \frac{4\ell^2}{\ell} = 4\ell = \rho_B ,
$$
in agreement with the computed values. A second check is made at the vertex $A$, where one has $|\vec{a}_P| = 2\ell\omega^2$ and $a_t = 0$, hence $\rho_A = \dfrac{|\vec{v}_P|^2}{|\vec{a}_P|} = \dfrac{\ell^2\omega^2}{2\ell\omega^2} = \dfrac{\ell}{2}$.

## Exercise 3

A material point $P$ travels along the circle with center $O$ and radius $R$ in the figure, starting from rest at the point $A = O + R\,\hat{\imath}$ with the law
$$
\vartheta(t) = \frac{1}{2}\,\alpha t^2 ,
$$
where $\vartheta$ is the central angle measured counterclockwise starting from $A$ and $\alpha > 0$ is constant. Compute the intrinsic components of the acceleration, the instant at which they have the same magnitude and the angle described up to that instant.

::grafico{curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29;0.3*cos(t),0.3*sin(t),0,1.047" punti="0,0,pieno;1,0,pieno,A;0.5,0.87,pieno,P" vettori="0.5,0.87,-0.19,1.27;0.5,0.87,0.1,0.17" segmenti="0,0,0.5,0.87" etichette="-0.55,1.22,aₜ;0.62,0.45,aₙ;0.3,0.1,ϑ" uguale=1 xmin=-1.5 xmax=1.7 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.6 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Point on a circular guide of radius $R$: the tangential acceleration is constant, the normal one grows with the square of the angular velocity"}

$$
s(t) = R\,\vartheta(t) = \frac{1}{2}\,R\,\alpha t^2 ,
\qquad
\dot{s} = R\,\alpha t ,
\qquad
\ddot{s} = R\,\alpha
$$
$$
a_t = \ddot{s} = R\,\alpha ,
\qquad
a_n = \frac{\dot{s}^2}{R} = \frac{R^2\alpha^2t^2}{R} = R\,\alpha^2 t^2
$$

Equality of the magnitudes:
$$
R\,\alpha = R\,\alpha^2 t^2
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
t^* = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}} ,
\qquad
\vartheta(t^*) = \frac{1}{2}\,\alpha\,\frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{1}{2}\ \mathrm{rad} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On a circle the arc and the central angle are linked by $s = R\vartheta$, hence the time law is obtained by multiplying by $R$ the law given for the angle. Differentiating one gets the scalar velocity $\dot{s} = R\alpha t$ and the tangential acceleration $\ddot{s} = R\alpha$, constant.

The radius of curvature of a circle is the radius itself, therefore the normal acceleration equals $\dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{R}$ and grows with the square of time. At the initial instant the point is at rest, hence the acceleration is entirely tangential.

The equality $R\alpha = R\alpha^2t^2$ is simplified by dividing by $R\alpha > 0$ and gives $\alpha t^2 = 1$, from which the instant $t^* = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}$ follows. Substituting into the law of the angle one obtains $\vartheta(t^*) = \dfrac{1}{2}\ \mathrm{rad}$, that is a little less than $29^\circ$, hence the two components have the same magnitude **well before a complete turn**.

At that instant the acceleration forms $45^\circ$ with the radius, since its two orthogonal components have the same magnitude, and it equals $|\vec{a}_P| = \sqrt{2}\,R\alpha$.

</div>

Check. Repeat the computation in polar coordinates, with $r = R$ constant and $\vartheta = \dfrac{1}{2}\alpha t^2$. The polar formulas give
$$
\vec{a}_P = \big( \ddot{r} - r\dot{\vartheta}^2 \big)\,\hat{u}_r + \big( r\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\vartheta} \big)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = -R\,\alpha^2t^2\,\hat{u}_r + R\,\alpha\,\hat{u}_\vartheta .
$$
The component along $\hat{u}_\vartheta$ equals $R\alpha$ and coincides with $a_t$, because for $\dot{\vartheta} > 0$ one has $\hat{u}_\vartheta = \hat{t}$; the component along $\hat{u}_r$ equals $-R\alpha^2t^2$ and coincides with $-a_n$, because $\hat{n} = -\hat{u}_r$. The two computations give the same result.
