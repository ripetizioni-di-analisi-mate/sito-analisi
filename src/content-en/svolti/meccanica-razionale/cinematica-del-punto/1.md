## Exercise 1

A material point $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ with the equations
$$
x(t) = \ell\,\omega t ,
\qquad
y(t) = \frac{1}{2}\,\ell\,\omega^2 t^2 ,
\qquad t \ge 0 ,
$$
where $\ell > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ are constants. Determine the trajectory, drawn in the figure, the velocity, the acceleration and the magnitude of the velocity.

::grafico{curve="t,t*t/2,0,2.4" vettori="1.2,0.72,2.0,1.68;1.2,0.72,1.2,1.72" punti="0,0,pieno;1.2,0.72,pieno,P" etichette="1.75,1.0,vₚ;1.3,1.45,aₚ" uguale=1 xmin=-0.4 xmax=2.9 ymin=-0.3 ymax=3.1 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Trajectory of the motion: the parabola $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2\ell}$, traveled starting from the origin"}

The frame is the given one and the unknowns are the vector functions $\vec{v}_P(t)$ and $\vec{a}_P(t)$, which are obtained by differentiating the position vector.

$$
P - O = \ell\omega t\,\hat{\imath} + \frac{1}{2}\,\ell\omega^2 t^2\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = \ell\omega\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\omega^2 t\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_P = \ell\omega^2\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = \sqrt{\ell^2\omega^2 + \ell^2\omega^4t^2} = \ell\omega\,\sqrt{1 + \omega^2t^2}
$$

Trajectory: from the first equation $\omega t = \dfrac{x}{\ell}$, and substituting into the second one
$$
y = \frac{1}{2}\,\ell\,\frac{x^2}{\ell^2} = \frac{x^2}{2\ell} ,
\qquad x \ge 0 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The position vector has the two given functions as components, hence the velocity is obtained by differentiating them with respect to time, since the unit vectors $\hat{\imath}$ and $\hat{\jmath}$ of the fixed frame are constant. The derivative of $\ell\omega t$ equals $\ell\omega$ and the derivative of $\dfrac{1}{2}\ell\omega^2t^2$ equals $\ell\omega^2 t$.

Differentiating a second time one obtains the acceleration: the first component has zero derivative, the second one has derivative $\ell\omega^2$. The acceleration is therefore **constant**, directed as $\hat{\jmath}$, and the motion is uniformly accelerated in the direction of the $y$ axis.

For the trajectory one eliminates time between the two equations of motion. Since $t \ge 0$ and $\ell\omega > 0$, the variable $x$ takes all nonnegative values, therefore the trajectory is the branch with $x \ge 0$ of the parabola $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2\ell}$, with vertex at the origin and concavity upward.

The magnitude of the velocity grows with time, since the component along $\hat{\jmath}$ grows in magnitude while the one along $\hat{\imath}$ stays constant; at the initial instant it equals $\ell\omega$.

</div>

Check. Compute the intrinsic components of the acceleration and verify that the sum of their squares gives $|\vec{a}_P|^2 = \ell^2\omega^4$. One has
$$
a_t = \frac{d}{dt}|\vec{v}_P| = \frac{\ell\omega^3 t}{\sqrt{1 + \omega^2t^2}} ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P = \ell^2\omega^3\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
a_n = \frac{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|}{|\vec{v}_P|} = \frac{\ell\omega^2}{\sqrt{1 + \omega^2t^2}} ,
$$
hence
$$
a_t^2 + a_n^2 = \frac{\ell^2\omega^6t^2 + \ell^2\omega^4}{1 + \omega^2t^2} = \ell^2\omega^4 = |\vec{a}_P|^2 ,
$$
as it must be, since $\hat{t}$ and $\hat{n}$ are orthogonal.

## Exercise 2

A material point $P$ moves in the plane $Oxy$ with the equations
$$
x(t) = \ell\,(1 - \cos\omega t) ,
\qquad
y(t) = \ell\,\sin\omega t ,
$$
with $\ell > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ constants. Recognize the trajectory, shown in the figure, establish the direction of travel, write the time law $s(t)$ with the origin of the arcs at $O$ and compute the radius of curvature.

::grafico{curve="1-cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29" vettori="0.5,0.87,1.37,1.37" punti="0,0,pieno;1,0,pieno,C;0.5,0.87,pieno,P" etichette="1.15,1.3,vₚ;0.62,0.3,ℓ" segmenti="1,0,0.5,0.87" uguale=1 xmin=-0.5 xmax=2.6 ymin=-1.4 ymax=1.5 legenda=0 nomi="x,y" titolo="Trajectory: the circle with center $C$ and radius $\ell$, which passes through the origin"}

$$
P - C = -\ell\cos\omega t\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\omega t\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad C = O + \ell\,\hat{\imath}
$$
$$
|P - C|^2 = \ell^2\cos^2\omega t + \ell^2\sin^2\omega t = \ell^2
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P = \ell\omega\sin\omega t\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\omega\cos\omega t\,\hat{\jmath} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_P = \ell\omega^2\cos\omega t\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\omega^2\sin\omega t\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = \ell\omega ,
\qquad
s(t) = \int_0^t |\vec{v}_P|\,d\tau = \ell\omega t
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P = -\ell^2\omega^3\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\rho = \frac{|\vec{v}_P|^3}{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|} = \frac{\ell^3\omega^3}{\ell^2\omega^3} = \ell
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In order to recognize the trajectory one looks for a fixed point whose distance from $P$ does not depend on time. Writing $x - \ell = -\ell\cos\omega t$ and $y = \ell\sin\omega t$ and adding the squares one obtains $(x - \ell)^2 + y^2 = \ell^2$, therefore the trajectory is the **circle** with center $C = O + \ell\,\hat{\imath}$ and radius $\ell$, which passes through the origin.

The magnitude of the velocity is constant and equals $\ell\omega$, hence the motion is uniform circular motion and the time law with the origin of the arcs at $O$ is $s(t) = \ell\omega t$, where the positive direction of the arcs is the one in which the point moves.

The direction of travel is read on the initial velocity: at the instant $t = 0$ the point is at $O$, which is the leftmost point of the circle, and $\vec{v}_P(0) = \ell\omega\,\hat{\jmath}$ points upward. A point that rises starting from the leftmost end of a circle travels along it **clockwise**.

The radius of curvature computed with the formula equals $\ell$ at every instant, in agreement with the property that for a circle the radius of curvature coincides with the radius.

</div>

Check. For uniform circular motion the acceleration must be centripetal, that is $\vec{a}_P = -\omega^2\,(P - C)$. One computes
$$
-\omega^2\,(P - C) = -\omega^2\big( -\ell\cos\omega t\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\sin\omega t\,\hat{\jmath} \big) = \ell\omega^2\cos\omega t\,\hat{\imath} - \ell\omega^2\sin\omega t\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
which coincides with the acceleration found by differentiating. Moreover $\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{a}_P = \ell^2\omega^3\sin\omega t\cos\omega t - \ell^2\omega^3\cos\omega t\sin\omega t = 0$, hence the acceleration is orthogonal to the velocity, as it must be when $|\vec{v}_P|$ is constant.

## Exercise 3

A material point $P$ moves in space with
$$
P - O = R\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} + h\,\omega t\,\hat{k} ,
$$
with $R > 0$, $h > 0$ and $\omega > 0$ constants. The trajectory, drawn in the figure, is a circular helix. Compute the velocity, the acceleration, the magnitude of the velocity, the time law $s(t)$ and the radius of curvature.

::grafico{curve="cos(t),0.25*t+0.45*sin(t),0,12.57;cos(t),0.45*sin(t),0,6.29" linee="-1,0 -1,3.3;1,0 1,3.3" segmenti="0,0,0,3.6" riferimento="-2.1,0.2,0,0.8,x,z" assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-2.4 xmax=1.8 ymin=-0.7 ymax=3.8 legenda=0 titolo="Circular helix of radius $R$ and pitch $2\pi h$, drawn in projection: the point turns about the $z$ axis and rises with constant velocity"}

$$
\vec{v}_P = -R\omega\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + R\omega\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} + h\omega\,\hat{k}
$$
$$
\vec{a}_P = -R\omega^2\cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} - R\omega^2\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}
$$
$$
|\vec{v}_P| = \sqrt{R^2\omega^2\sin^2\omega t + R^2\omega^2\cos^2\omega t + h^2\omega^2} = \omega\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}
$$
$$
s(t) = \int_0^t \omega\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}\,d\tau = \omega t\,\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P = R h \omega^3 \sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} - R h \omega^3 \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath} + R^2\omega^3\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P| = R\omega^3\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}
$$
$$
\rho = \frac{|\vec{v}_P|^3}{|\vec{v}_P \wedge \vec{a}_P|} = \frac{\omega^3 (R^2 + h^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{R\omega^3\sqrt{R^2 + h^2}} = \frac{R^2 + h^2}{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two components of the position vector describe a uniform circular motion of radius $R$ in the plane $Oxy$, the third one a uniform rise along $\hat{k}$; the trajectory is therefore the circular helix traced on the cylinder with axis $z$ and radius $R$. At every complete turn, which takes the time $\dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$, the height increases by $2\pi h$, which is the **pitch** of the helix.

The magnitude of the velocity turns out to be constant, since the sum $\sin^2\omega t + \cos^2\omega t$ equals $1$; the arc length coordinate is then obtained by integrating a constant and grows linearly in time.

The acceleration has zero component along $\hat{k}$ and is directed toward the axis of the cylinder, with magnitude $R\omega^2$. The radius of curvature equals $\dfrac{R^2 + h^2}{R}$, hence it is greater than the radius of the cylinder, therefore the helix is **less curved** than the base circle, and as $h \to 0$ the radius of curvature tends to $R$.

</div>

Check. Since $|\vec{v}_P|$ is constant, the tangential acceleration is zero and the acceleration is entirely normal, hence $|\vec{a}_P| = a_n = \dfrac{\dot{s}^2}{\rho}$. From the direct computation $|\vec{a}_P| = R\omega^2$ and $\dot{s} = \omega\sqrt{R^2+h^2}$, therefore
$$
\rho = \frac{\dot{s}^2}{|\vec{a}_P|} = \frac{\omega^2(R^2 + h^2)}{R\omega^2} = \frac{R^2 + h^2}{R} ,
$$
in agreement with the value obtained from the formula with the cross product. One also checks $\vec{v}_P \cdot \vec{a}_P = R^2\omega^3\sin\omega t\cos\omega t - R^2\omega^3\cos\omega t \sin\omega t = 0$, consistent with $a_t = 0$.
