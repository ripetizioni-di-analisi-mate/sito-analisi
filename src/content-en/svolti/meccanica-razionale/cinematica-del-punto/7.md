## Exercise 1

A platform rotates in the plane $Oxy$ about the fixed center $O$ with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$. A slider $P$ runs on a radial guide attached to the platform, as in the figure, with
$$
|P - O| = \ell\,\sin(\Omega t) ,
$$
where $\ell > 0$ and $\Omega > 0$ are constants. Compute the three accelerations of the composition theorem and the absolute acceleration, and say at which instants the Coriolis acceleration is zero and at which it is maximum in magnitude.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" nodi="O,0,0;P,0.98,0.69" nomi=0 punti="0,0,O,centro;0.98,0.69,P" linee="0,0 1.64,1.15" frecce="0.98,0.69,1.55,1.09,v_r;0.98,0.69,0.7,1.09,a_c" coppie="-1.0 -0.9,1,ω" titolo="Slider on a radial guide of a rotating platform: the relative velocity is directed along the guide"}

The moving frame attached to the platform is taken, with origin at $O$ and first unit vector $\hat{e}_1$ along the guide; in it the guide is fixed and the slider performs a rectilinear harmonic motion.

$$
P - O = \ell\sin(\Omega t)\,\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_O = \vec{0} ,
\qquad
\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \vec{0}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_r = \ell\,\Omega\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_r = -\,\ell\,\Omega^2\sin(\Omega t)\,\hat{e}_1
$$
$$
\vec{a}_\tau = \vec{\omega} \wedge \big( \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) \big) = -\,\omega^2\,\ell\sin(\Omega t)\,\hat{e}_1
$$
$$
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r = 2\,\omega\,\ell\,\Omega\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{a}_a = \vec{a}_\tau + \vec{a}_r + \vec{a}_c = -\,\ell\,\big( \Omega^2 + \omega^2 \big)\sin(\Omega t)\,\hat{e}_1 + 2\,\omega\,\ell\,\Omega\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{e}_2
$$

Notable instants:
$$
\vec{a}_c = \vec{0}
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\cos(\Omega t) = 0
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\Omega t = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi ,
$$
$$
|\vec{a}_c|_{\max} = 2\,\omega\,\ell\,\Omega
\quad \text{for} \quad
\Omega t = k\pi .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the frame attached to the platform the slider moves on a fixed line with a harmonic law, therefore the relative velocity and the relative acceleration are both directed along $\hat{e}_1$ and are obtained by differentiating $\ell\sin(\Omega t)$ once and twice.

The transport acceleration contains three terms, two of which are zero: $\vec{a}_O$ because the moving origin is the fixed center and $\dot{\vec{\omega}} \wedge (P - O)$ because the angular velocity is constant. The centripetal term $\vec{\omega} \wedge (\vec{\omega} \wedge (P-O))$ remains, which for $\vec{\omega}$ orthogonal to the plane equals $-\omega^2\,(P - O)$.

The Coriolis acceleration is $2\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r$; since $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_1 = \hat{e}_2$, it is **orthogonal to the guide** and proportional to the relative velocity.

Adding the three contributions, the components along $\hat{e}_1$ add up to each other and give the term $-\ell(\Omega^2 + \omega^2)\sin(\Omega t)$, while the component along $\hat{e}_2$ is the Coriolis acceleration alone.

The Coriolis acceleration vanishes when the relative velocity vanishes, that is at the turning points of the harmonic motion, and it is maximum in magnitude at the passages through the center, where the relative velocity is maximum.

</div>

Check. The absolute acceleration is computed again with the formulas in polar coordinates, setting $r = \ell\sin(\Omega t)$ and $\vartheta = \omega t$, so that $\hat{u}_r = \hat{e}_1$ and $\hat{u}_\vartheta = \hat{e}_2$. One has $\dot{r} = \ell\Omega\cos(\Omega t)$, $\ddot{r} = -\ell\Omega^2\sin(\Omega t)$, $\dot{\vartheta} = \omega$ and $\ddot{\vartheta} = 0$, hence
$$
\vec{a}_P = \big( \ddot{r} - r\dot{\vartheta}^2 \big)\,\hat{u}_r + \big( r\ddot{\vartheta} + 2\dot{r}\dot{\vartheta} \big)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = -\ell\big( \Omega^2 + \omega^2 \big)\sin(\Omega t)\,\hat{u}_r + 2\,\omega\,\ell\,\Omega\cos(\Omega t)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta ,
$$
which coincides with the result obtained with the theorem on the composition of accelerations.

## Exercise 2

A platform rotates about the fixed center $O$ with variable angular velocity $\vec{\omega}(t) = \alpha t\,\hat{k}$, with $\alpha > 0$ constant, and starts from rest. A slider $P$ runs on a radial guide attached to the platform, as in the figure, starting from $O$ at the instant $t = 0$ with constant relative velocity of magnitude $u$. Compute the absolute velocity and the three accelerations of the composition theorem, and the ratio of the transverse component of the absolute acceleration to the Coriolis acceleration.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" nodi="O,0,0;P,1.22,0.44" nomi=0 punti="0,0,O,centro;1.22,0.44,P" linee="0,0 1.88,0.68" frecce="1.22,0.44,1.78,0.64,v_r;1.22,0.44,1.0,1.05,a_c" coppie="-1.0 -0.9,1,ω(t)" titolo="Platform rotating with increasing angular velocity and slider running on the radial guide with constant relative velocity"}

The moving frame is attached to the platform, with origin at $O$ and first unit vector $\hat{e}_1$ along the guide.

$$
P - O = u t\,\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\vec{\omega} = \alpha t\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\dot{\vec{\omega}} = \alpha\,\hat{k} ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_O = \vec{0}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_r = u\,\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_\tau = \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) = \alpha\,u\,t^2\,\hat{e}_2 ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_a = u\,\hat{e}_1 + \alpha\,u\,t^2\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{a}_r = \vec{0} ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\vec{\omega} \wedge \vec{v}_r = 2\,\alpha\,u\,t\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{a}_\tau = \dot{\vec{\omega}} \wedge (P - O) + \vec{\omega} \wedge \big( \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) \big) = \alpha\,u\,t\,\hat{e}_2 - \alpha^2 u\,t^3\,\hat{e}_1
$$
$$
\vec{a}_a = -\,\alpha^2 u\,t^3\,\hat{e}_1 + 3\,\alpha\,u\,t\,\hat{e}_2 ,
\qquad
\frac{a_{a,\,\hat{e}_2}}{|\vec{a}_c|} = \frac{3\,\alpha u t}{2\,\alpha u t} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The relative motion is rectilinear and uniform, hence the relative velocity is constant in magnitude and direction with respect to the platform and the relative acceleration is zero.

The transport acceleration has two nonzero terms. The first one, $\dot{\vec{\omega}} \wedge (P - O)$, is due to the change of the angular velocity and is transverse, equal to $\alpha u t\,\hat{e}_2$. The second one is the centripetal term $-\omega^2\,(P-O) = -\alpha^2t^2\,ut\,\hat{e}_1$, directed toward the center.

The Coriolis acceleration is transverse and equals $2\alpha u t\,\hat{e}_2$, hence it is **twice** the transport term due to $\dot{\vec{\omega}}$.

Adding the three contributions, the transverse component of the absolute acceleration equals $3\alpha u t$, that is one and a half times the Coriolis acceleration, since one part comes from the accelerated rotation of the platform and one part, twice as large, from the motion of the slider along the guide.

</div>

Check. The absolute acceleration is computed again with the formulas in polar coordinates, with $r = ut$ and $\dot{\vartheta} = \alpha t$, hence $\ddot{\vartheta} = \alpha$, $\dot{r} = u$ and $\ddot{r} = 0$:
$$
\vec{a}_P = \big( 0 - u t\,\alpha^2t^2 \big)\,\hat{u}_r + \big( ut\,\alpha + 2u\,\alpha t \big)\,\hat{u}_\vartheta = -\,\alpha^2 u t^3\,\hat{u}_r + 3\,\alpha u t\,\hat{u}_\vartheta ,
$$
in agreement with the result. As a second check, in the case $\alpha = 0$ the platform does not rotate, all three accelerations vanish and the uniform rectilinear motion with $\vec{v}_a = u\,\hat{e}_1$ remains.

## Exercise 3

A platform rotates about the fixed center $O$ with constant angular velocity $\vec{\omega} = \omega\,\hat{k}$. A straight guide whose distance from $O$ equals $d > 0$ is fixed on it, as in the figure, and a slider $P$ runs on the guide with constant relative velocity of magnitude $u$; at the instant $t = 0$ the slider is at the foot $H$ of the perpendicular drawn from $O$ to the guide. Compute the absolute velocity and the absolute acceleration, and determine the value of $u$ for which the absolute acceleration at $t = 0$ is directed toward $O$.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" nodi="O,0,0;H,0,0.9;P,0.8,0.9" nomi=0 punti="0,0,O,centro;0,0.9,H;0.8,0.9,P" linee="-1.78,0.9 1.78,0.9" tratteggi="0,0 0,0.9" frecce="0.8,0.9,1.45,0.9,v_r;0.8,0.9,0.8,1.6,a_c" coppie="-1.0 -1.0,1,ω" quote="O,H,d,-0.45" titolo="Straight guide at distance $d$ from the center of a rotating platform, with the slider $P$ running on it with constant relative velocity"}

The moving frame attached to the platform is taken, with origin at $O$, first unit vector $\hat{e}_1$ parallel to the guide and in the direction of the relative motion, second unit vector $\hat{e}_2$ directed from $O$ toward $H$.

$$
P - O = u t\,\hat{e}_1 + d\,\hat{e}_2 ,
\qquad
\vec{v}_r = u\,\hat{e}_1 ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_r = \vec{0}
$$
$$
\vec{v}_\tau = \vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O) = \omega\,u t\,\hat{e}_2 - \omega\,d\,\hat{e}_1
$$
$$
\vec{v}_a = \big( u - \omega d \big)\,\hat{e}_1 + \omega\,u t\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{a}_\tau = -\,\omega^2\,(P - O) = -\,\omega^2 u t\,\hat{e}_1 - \omega^2 d\,\hat{e}_2 ,
\qquad
\vec{a}_c = 2\,\omega\,u\,\hat{e}_2
$$
$$
\vec{a}_a = -\,\omega^2 u t\,\hat{e}_1 + \big( 2\,\omega u - \omega^2 d \big)\,\hat{e}_2
$$

At the instant $t = 0$ the slider is at $H$ and the direction of $O$ is that of $-\hat{e}_2$:
$$
\vec{a}_a(0) = \big( 2\,\omega u - \omega^2 d \big)\,\hat{e}_2 ,
$$
which is directed toward $O$ when $2\omega u - \omega^2 d < 0$, that is for $u < \dfrac{\omega d}{2}$; it is zero for $u = \dfrac{\omega d}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

In the frame attached to the platform the guide is fixed and the slider runs on it with uniform rectilinear motion, therefore the relative velocity is constant and the relative acceleration is zero. The relative position is written with the two coordinates $\xi = ut$ along the guide and $\eta = d$ constant.

The transport velocity is computed with the cross product $\vec{\omega} \wedge (P - O)$, recalling that $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_1 = \hat{e}_2$ and $\hat{k} \wedge \hat{e}_2 = -\hat{e}_1$. The component along the guide equals $-\omega d$ and does not depend on time, because the distance of the guide from the center stays $d$.

The transport acceleration reduces to the centripetal term, since the moving origin is fixed and the angular velocity is constant; it therefore equals $-\omega^2\,(P - O)$.

The Coriolis acceleration is orthogonal to the guide and constant, because the relative velocity is constant in magnitude and direction with respect to the platform.

At the initial instant the component of the absolute acceleration along the guide is zero and only the orthogonal component remains, the sum of the centripetal term $-\omega^2 d$ and of the Coriolis acceleration $2\omega u$. For $u < \dfrac{\omega d}{2}$ the centripetal term prevails and the absolute acceleration is **directed toward the center**.

</div>

Check. Write the motion in the fixed frame and differentiate twice. With $\hat{e}_1 = \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$ and $\hat{e}_2 = -\sin(\omega t)\,\hat{\imath} + \cos(\omega t)\,\hat{\jmath}$ one has
$$
P - O = \big( u t\cos\omega t - d\sin\omega t \big)\,\hat{\imath} + \big( u t\sin\omega t + d\cos\omega t \big)\,\hat{\jmath} .
$$
Differentiating twice and collecting the terms one obtains
$$
\vec{a}_P = \big( -\omega^2 u t\cos\omega t - 2\omega u\sin\omega t + \omega^2 d\sin\omega t \big)\,\hat{\imath} + \big( -\omega^2 u t\sin\omega t + 2\omega u\cos\omega t - \omega^2 d\cos\omega t \big)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
that is $-\omega^2ut\,\hat{e}_1 + (2\omega u - \omega^2 d)\,\hat{e}_2$, in agreement with the result. At $t = 0$ and with $u = \dfrac{\omega d}{2}$ the absolute acceleration vanishes, as found.
