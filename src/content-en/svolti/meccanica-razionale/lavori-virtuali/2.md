## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the origin $O$ and moves in the vertical plane $Oxy$. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects the end $A$ to the fixed point $C = (\ell, 0)$, as in the diagram in the figure. The constraints are smooth and bilateral. Write the potential of the system as a function of the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.9,-2.1;C,2.8,0" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0.95,-1.05,G,baricentro" linee="2.80,0.00 2.68,-0.28 2.82,-0.47 2.36,-0.53 2.63,-0.91 2.17,-0.97 2.44,-1.35 1.98,-1.41 2.25,-1.79 2.02,-1.82 1.90,-2.10" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.6;0,0 2.8,0" forze="0.95 -2.05,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="0.35,-1,ϑ;2.75,-1.1,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with a spring between $A$ and the fixed point $C = (\ell, 0)$"}

The system has one degree of freedom and $\vartheta$ is taken as the generalized coordinate. The positions of the end and of the center of mass are
$$
A - O = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}) ,
\qquad
y_G = -\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta .
$$
The potential of the weight is therefore
$$
U_{\text{weight}} = -m g\,y_G = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta .
$$
The length of the spring is obtained from the square of the distance between $A$ and $C$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ell_{AC}^{\,2} &= (\ell\sin\vartheta - \ell)^2 + \ell^2\cos^2\vartheta \\[6pt]
&= \ell^2\,(\sin^2\vartheta - 2\sin\vartheta + 1 + \cos^2\vartheta) = \ell^2\,(2 - 2\sin\vartheta) ,
\end{aligned}
$$
hence, with zero natural length,
$$
U_{\text{spring}} = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell_{AC}^{\,2} = -k\,\ell^2\,(1 - \sin\vartheta) .
$$
Adding the two contributions and discarding the additive constant gives
$$
U(\vartheta) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is hinged at a fixed point, hence it can only rotate and the system has one degree of freedom; the angle $\vartheta$ identifies the configuration, therefore it is a generalized coordinate.

The potential of the weight requires only the height of the center of mass, which for a homogeneous rod is the midpoint. The height is negative when the rod lies below the hinge, and the expression $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$ gives it correctly for every value of $\vartheta$.

For the spring one needs the square of its length, which is computed as the sum of the squares of the differences of the coordinates of $A$ and of $C$; the identity $\sin^2\vartheta + \cos^2\vartheta = 1$ simplifies the expression. The natural length is zero, hence the potential of the spring reduces to $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell_{AC}^{\,2}$ and contains no square roots.

The potentials of the two force systems **are added**, since the elementary work of the system of forces is the sum of the elementary works. The constant term $-k\,\ell^2$ is discarded, given that the potential is defined up to a constant.

</div>

The result is checked on the value of the Lagrangian component $Q_\vartheta$, which for a conservative force system is $\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta}$ and must coincide with the moment with respect to $O$ of the active forces. In the configuration $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the rod is horizontal and the end $A$ coincides with $C$, therefore the spring has zero length and exerts no force; the moment with respect to $O$ is then that of the weight alone, $-\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$. The derivative of the potential gives
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta + k\,\ell^2\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
U'\!\left( \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2} ,
$$
in agreement with the direct computation of the moment.

## Exercise 2

Two homogeneous rods $OA$ and $AB$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, are hinged to each other at $A$ and to the ground at $O$, and move in the vertical plane, as in the diagram in the figure. A constant couple of moment $\mathcal{M}$ in the counterclockwise direction acts on the second rod. The constraints are smooth and bilateral. Write the potential of the system as a function of the angles $\vartheta$ and $\varphi$ that the two rods form with the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.1,-2.2;B,2.9,-3.1" aste="O-A;A-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" cerniere="A" punti="0.55,-1.1,G_1,baricentro;2,-2.65,G_2,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.6;1.1,-2.2 1.1,-4" coppie="2.6 -2,1,ℳ" etichette="0.3,-1.1,ϑ;1.5,-3.3,φ" titolo="Double pendulum with a couple $\mathcal{M}$ applied to the second rod"}

The system has two degrees of freedom and $\vartheta$ and $\varphi$ are taken as generalized coordinates. The heights of the two centers of mass are
$$
y_{G_1} = -\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
y_{G_2} = -\ell\cos\vartheta - \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\varphi ,
$$
therefore the potential of the weight of the two rods is
$$
U_{\text{weight}} = -m g\,(y_{G_1} + y_{G_2}) = \frac{3}{2}\,m g \ell\cos\vartheta + \frac{1}{2}\,m g \ell\cos\varphi .
$$
The constant couple applied to the second rod does the elementary work $\mathcal{M}\,d\varphi$, hence it has potential $U_{\text{couple}} = \mathcal{M}\,\varphi$. The potential of the system is thus
$$
U(\vartheta, \varphi) = \frac{3}{2}\,m g \ell\cos\vartheta + \frac{1}{2}\,m g \ell\cos\varphi + \mathcal{M}\,\varphi .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two rods are connected by an internal hinge, therefore each one can rotate about its own end and the system has two degrees of freedom; the angles $\vartheta$ and $\varphi$ are independent and identify the configuration.

The center of mass of the first rod lies at $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $O$, hence its height is $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$. The center of mass of the second rod lies at $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $A$, and the height of $A$ is $-\ell\cos\vartheta$; adding the two contributions gives $y_{G_2}$. The coefficient $\dfrac{3}{2}$ in front of $\cos\vartheta$ arises from the fact that the angle $\vartheta$ raises or lowers both the center of mass of the first rod and that of the second.

The couple has zero resultant and moment $\mathcal{M}$ with respect to every pole, hence its elementary work on a rigid body that rotates by the angle $d\varphi$ is $\mathcal{M}\,d\varphi$. The function $\mathcal{M}\,\varphi$ has this differential, hence it is the **potential of the couple**. The rotation that appears is that of the second rod, since the couple acts on it.

</div>

The potential is checked on the particular case in which the two rods remain aligned, that is, $\varphi = \vartheta$. In this condition the system behaves as a single rod of mass $2m$ and length $2\ell$, whose center of mass lies at distance $\ell$ from $O$, with the couple $\mathcal{M}$ applied. The potential of such a rod is
$$
2 m g\,\ell\cos\vartheta + \mathcal{M}\,\vartheta ,
$$
and it coincides with the expression found when $\varphi = \vartheta$ is set, since $\dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 2$.

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, is hinged at the origin $O$ and moves in the vertical plane. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and natural length $\ell_0 = \ell$ connects the end $A$ to the fixed point $C = (0, 2\ell)$, as in the diagram in the figure. The constraints are smooth and bilateral. Write the potential of the system as a function of the angle $\vartheta \in [0, \pi]$ between the rod and the upward vertical.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2.1,2.1;C,0,3" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,270" punti="1.05,1.05,G,baricentro" linee="0.00,3.00 0.28,2.88 0.47,3.02 0.53,2.56 0.91,2.83 0.97,2.37 1.35,2.64 1.41,2.18 1.79,2.45 1.82,2.22 2.10,2.10" tratteggi="0,0 0,3" forze="1.05 0.1,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="0.35,1.2,ϑ;1.1,3.1,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with a spring of natural length $\ell_0 = \ell$ between $A$ and the fixed point $C = (0, 2\ell)$"}

The system has one degree of freedom and $\vartheta$ is taken as the generalized coordinate. The positions of the end and of the center of mass are
$$
A - O = 2\ell\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}) ,
\qquad
y_G = \ell\cos\vartheta ,
$$
therefore the potential of the weight is $U_{\text{weight}} = -m g \ell\cos\vartheta$. The square of the distance between $A$ and $C$ is
$$
\ell_{AC}^{\,2} = 4\ell^2\sin^2\vartheta + (2\ell\cos\vartheta - 2\ell)^2 = 8\ell^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta) ,
$$
and from the half-angle formula $1 - \cos\vartheta = 2\sin^2\dfrac{\vartheta}{2}$ it follows that
$$
\ell_{AC} = 4\,\ell\,\sin\frac{\vartheta}{2} ,
\qquad \vartheta \in [0, \pi] .
$$
The spring has natural length $\ell_0 = \ell$, hence its potential is
$$
U_{\text{spring}} = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,\left( 4\,\ell\,\sin\frac{\vartheta}{2} - \ell \right)^{2} .
$$
The potential of the system is thus
$$
U(\vartheta) = -m g \ell\cos\vartheta - \frac{k\,\ell^2}{2}\,\left( 4\sin\frac{\vartheta}{2} - 1 \right)^{2} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the angle is measured from the upward vertical, because the attachment point $C$ of the spring lies above the hinge; with this choice the height of the center of mass is $+\ell\cos\vartheta$ and that of $A$ is $2\ell\cos\vartheta$.

The natural length is not zero, hence the difference $\ell_{AC} - \ell_0$ appears in the potential of the spring and the length is needed, not its square. The square root is removed with the half-angle formula: for $\vartheta \in [0, \pi]$ one has $\sin\dfrac{\vartheta}{2} \geq 0$, therefore $\sqrt{8\ell^2\,(1 - \cos\vartheta)} = 4\,\ell\,\sin\dfrac{\vartheta}{2}$.

The point $A$ describes a circle of center $O$ and radius $2\ell$, and $C$ is the highest point of this circle; the length of the spring is hence the chord joining $A$ with the highest point, and the formula $4\,\ell\,\sin\dfrac{\vartheta}{2}$ is the expression of the chord subtended by the central angle $\vartheta$.

</div>

The expression is checked on two known configurations. For $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the rod is horizontal, the distance between $A = (2\ell, 0)$ and $C = (0, 2\ell)$ is $2\sqrt{2}\,\ell$, and the formula gives $4\,\ell\,\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} = 2\sqrt{2}\,\ell$. For $\vartheta = \pi$ the rod is vertical and pointing downwards, the distance between $A = (0, -2\ell)$ and $C$ is $4\ell$, and the formula gives $4\,\ell\,\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 4\ell$. In both cases the values coincide.
