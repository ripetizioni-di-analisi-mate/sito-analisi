## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, is placed horizontally in the vertical plane, with a smooth hinge at $A$ and a smooth roller support on a horizontal plane at $B$. At the point $D$, at distance $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$ from $A$, the vertical force $F$ directed downwards acts, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the reaction of the roller support.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" punti="2,0,G,baricentro" forze="3 0,0,-1,F;2 -1.4,0,-0.6,mg" quote="A,3 0,1.5 ℓ,1.75;3 0,B,0.5 ℓ,1.75" titolo="Supported rod with the force $F$ applied at $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$ from the hinge"}

The system, with all the constraints active, has zero degrees of freedom. The roller support at $B$ is removed and the unknown reaction $\vec{\Phi}_B = \Phi_B\,\hat{\jmath}$ is applied at $B$ as an active force. The released system has one degree of freedom, since the rod can rotate about the hinge $A$; the angle $\vartheta$ of rotation, measured counterclockwise from the horizontal configuration, is taken as the coordinate. The virtual displacements of the points of application, starting from $\vartheta = 0$, have vertical component
$$
\delta y_B = 2\ell\,\delta\vartheta ,
\qquad
\delta y_G = \ell\,\delta\vartheta ,
\qquad
\delta y_D = \frac{3\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta .
$$
The remaining constraints are ideal and bilateral, therefore the principle of virtual work gives
$$
\delta L = \Phi_B\,(2\ell\,\delta\vartheta) - m g\,(\ell\,\delta\vartheta) - F\,\left( \frac{3\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta \right) = 0
$$
for every $\delta\vartheta$, hence
$$
2\ell\,\Phi_B - m g\,\ell - \frac{3}{2}\,F\,\ell = 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_B = \frac{m g}{2} + \frac{3}{4}\,F .
$$
The reaction of the roller support is therefore directed **upwards**.

<div class="spiegazione">

With all the constraints active the system is statically determinate and admits no virtual displacement other than the zero one, therefore the principle of virtual work in its original form gives no information. Removing the roller support gives a system with one degree of freedom, and its reaction, put back as an active force, appears in the virtual work.

In the rotation about $A$ every point of the rod moves perpendicularly to the rod itself; in the horizontal configuration this displacement is vertical and its magnitude is proportional to the distance from $A$. Hence the three expressions of $\delta y$, with the arms $2\ell$, $\ell$ and $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$.

The reaction of the hinge does not appear in the virtual work, because it is applied at a fixed point, whose virtual displacement is zero. This is the advantage of the method: the reaction of the roller support is obtained from a single equation, without computing the two components of the hinge.

The coefficient $\dfrac{3}{4}$ in front of $F$ is the ratio between the arm of the force, $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$, and the span $2\ell$: the support closer to the load carries the greater part of it.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equation of statics for the rotation about $A$, in which the two components of the hinge have zero moment:
$$
2\ell\,\Phi_B - m g\,\ell - \frac{3\ell}{2}\,F = 0 ,
$$
which is the same equation obtained with virtual work. A second check is made on the particular case $F = 0$, in which one obtains $\Phi_B = \dfrac{m g}{2}$: with the weight alone, applied at the midspan, the two constraints carry half of the load each.

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at $O$ by a smooth hinge and is in the horizontal configuration, held in equilibrium by the vertical force $F$ directed upwards and applied at $A$, as in the diagram in the figure. The plane is vertical. Determine the value of $F$ and the components of the reaction of the hinge.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,3.4,0" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera" punti="1.7,0,G,baricentro" forze="A,0,0.9,F;1.7 -1.3,0,-0.6,mg" quote="O,1.7 0,0.5 ℓ,1.75;1.7 0,A,0.5 ℓ,1.75" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$, held horizontal by the force $F$ applied at $A$"}

With the hinge active the system has one degree of freedom, the angle $\vartheta$ of rotation about $O$. The vertical virtual displacements starting from the horizontal configuration are $\delta y_A = \ell\,\delta\vartheta$ and $\delta y_G = \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta$, hence the principle of virtual work gives
$$
F\,\ell\,\delta\vartheta - m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta = 0 ,
\qquad
F = \frac{m g}{2} .
$$
For the reaction the hinge is removed and the unknown force $\vec{\Phi}_O = \Phi_{Ox}\,\hat{\imath} + \Phi_{Oy}\,\hat{\jmath}$ is applied at $O$. The rod, free in the plane, has three degrees of freedom, and the two virtual displacements of translation are considered, for which every point moves by $\delta O = \delta x\,\hat{\imath} + \delta y\,\hat{\jmath}$. The virtual work of all the active forces is then
$$
\delta L = \Phi_{Ox}\,\delta x + \big( \Phi_{Oy} + F - m g \big)\,\delta y = 0 ,
$$
and since $\delta x$ and $\delta y$ are arbitrary and independent one obtains
$$
\Phi_{Ox} = 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Oy} = m g - F = \frac{m g}{2} .
$$
The reaction of the hinge is therefore vertical, directed **upwards**, of magnitude $\dfrac{m g}{2}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation is written with the hinge in place, because the reaction of the hinge does no virtual work and the equation contains the active forces alone. The vanishing of the virtual work for every $\delta\vartheta$ gives the value of $F$.

To compute the reaction, on the other hand, a virtual displacement that moves the point $O$ is needed, and such a displacement exists only after the hinge has been removed. The force $\vec{\Phi}_O$ is put back as an active force, so that the released system is in equilibrium in the same configuration.

In the translation all the points move by the same vector, therefore the resultant of the active forces appears in the virtual work. The two components $\delta x$ and $\delta y$ are independent, hence the two coefficients vanish separately, and the two translation equations are obtained.

The sum $F + \Phi_{Oy}$ has to equal $m g$, hence the hinge carries the half of the weight that the force $F$ does not carry. The two vertical forces are equal, since the weight is applied at the midpoint and their arms with respect to $O$ are in the ratio $1$ to $2$.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equations of statics applied to the rod. The horizontal translation gives $\Phi_{Ox} = 0$, since no horizontal forces act. The vertical translation gives $\Phi_{Oy} - m g + F = 0$ and the rotation about $O$ gives $F\,\ell - m g\,\dfrac{\ell}{2} = 0$; the system of the three equations returns $F = \dfrac{m g}{2}$, $\Phi_{Ox} = 0$ and $\Phi_{Oy} = \dfrac{m g}{2}$.

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at $O$ by a smooth hinge and moves in the vertical plane; an ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $A$ to the fixed point $B = (0, \ell)$. In the equilibrium configuration $\vartheta = 0$, with the rod vertical and pointing downwards, shown in the diagram in the figure, compute the reaction of the hinge. It is assumed that $k \neq \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,0,-2.6;B,0,2.6" aste="O-A" punti="0,-1.3,G,baricentro" linee="-0.9,2.6 -0.9,2.2 -0.65,1.93 -1.15,1.38 -0.65,0.83 -1.15,0.28 -0.65,-0.28 -1.15,-0.83 -0.65,-1.38 -1.15,-1.93 -0.9,-2.2 -0.9,-2.6" tratteggi="0,2.6 -0.9,2.6;0,-2.6 -0.9,-2.6;0,0 0.5,0" forze="0 -1.3,0,-0.5,mg;A,0,0.8,2kℓ" reazioni="0.5 0,0,0.65,Φ_Oy" etichette="-1.6,0,k" titolo="Free-body diagram of the rod in the configuration $\vartheta = 0$: the spring, drawn at the side, goes from $B$ to $A$ along the vertical"}

In the configuration $\vartheta = 0$ one has $A = (0, -\ell)$ and $B = (0, \ell)$, therefore the spring has length $2\ell$ and exerts on $A$ the force
$$
-k\,(A - B) = -k\,(0,\; -2\ell) = 2\,k\,\ell\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
directed upwards. The hinge is removed and the unknown reaction $\vec{\Phi}_O$ is applied at $O$; the rod, free in the plane, has three degrees of freedom. For the two virtual displacements of translation $\delta O = \delta x\,\hat{\imath} + \delta y\,\hat{\jmath}$ the virtual work of the active forces and of $\vec{\Phi}_O$ is
$$
\delta L = \Phi_{Ox}\,\delta x + \big( \Phi_{Oy} + 2\,k\,\ell - m g \big)\,\delta y = 0 ,
$$
whence, by the arbitrariness and the independence of $\delta x$ and $\delta y$,
$$
\Phi_{Ox} = 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_{Oy} = m g - 2\,k\,\ell .
$$
The reaction is vertical; it is directed upwards if $k < \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$ and **downwards** if $k > \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The active forces have to be evaluated in the configuration in which the reaction is computed, therefore one starts from the value of the elastic force at $\vartheta = 0$. The spring has zero natural length, hence the force it exerts on $A$ is $-k\,(A - B)$, that is, $k$ times the vector going from $A$ to $B$; in this configuration that vector is vertical and of magnitude $2\ell$.

In the virtual translation all the points of the rod move in the same way, including $O$, $G$ and $A$, whereas the point $B$ stays fixed because it belongs to the frame. The three forces applied to the rod therefore appear in the virtual work, and their sum has to vanish component by component.

The reaction changes direction as the stiffness of the spring varies. If the spring is not very stiff, the hinge has to support the part of the weight that the spring does not hold, and the reaction is upwards. If the spring is very stiff, the elastic force exceeds the weight and the hinge has to **hold** the rod down.

In the limiting case $k = \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$ the reaction is zero: it is the same value for which the potential is constant and every configuration is an equilibrium configuration.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equations of statics applied to the rod. The horizontal translation gives $\Phi_{Ox} = 0$, since the weight and the elastic force are vertical; the vertical translation gives $\Phi_{Oy} + 2\,k\,\ell - m g = 0$. The rotation about $O$ is identically satisfied, because the weight and the elastic force have lines of action coinciding with the vertical through $O$ and therefore zero moment; this confirms that $\vartheta = 0$ is an equilibrium configuration.
