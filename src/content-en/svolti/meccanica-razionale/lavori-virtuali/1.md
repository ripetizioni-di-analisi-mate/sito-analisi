## Exercise 1

A particle is acted upon by the constant force $\vec{F} = F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath})$, with $F > 0$. The particle moves from the position $P_1 = (0, 0)$ to the position $P_2 = (2\ell, \ell)$, first along the segment $P_1 P_2$ and then along the polygonal path that goes from $P_1$ to $(2\ell, 0)$ and from there to $P_2$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Compute the work of the force in the two cases.

::struttura{nodi="P_1,0,0;P_2,4,2" linee="0,0 4,2" tratteggi="0,0 4,0;4,0 4,2" forze="2 1,0.8,0.8,F" punti="4,0,Q" titolo="The particle goes from $P_1$ to $P_2$ along the segment or along the polygonal path $P_1 Q P_2$"}

The force is constant, therefore its work along a path with endpoints $P_1$ and $P_2$ is
$$
L = \vec{F} \cdot (P_2 - P_1) .
$$
Along the segment one has $P_2 - P_1 = 2\ell\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\,\hat{\jmath}$, hence
$$
L = F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}) \cdot (2\ell\,\hat{\imath} + \ell\,\hat{\jmath}) = F\,(2\ell + \ell) = 3\,F\,\ell .
$$
Along the polygonal path the works on the two parts are added,
$$
\begin{aligned}
L &= F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}) \cdot (2\ell\,\hat{\imath}) + F\,(\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}) \cdot (\ell\,\hat{\jmath}) \\[6pt]
&= 2\,F\,\ell + F\,\ell = 3\,F\,\ell .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force is the same at every point of the plane, therefore in the line integral $\vec{F}$ is factored out in front of the integral and $\displaystyle\int_\gamma dP = P_2 - P_1$ remains. The work of a **constant force** therefore depends only on the endpoints of the path.

Along the polygonal path the first part is horizontal, with displacement $2\ell\,\hat{\imath}$, and the second is vertical, with displacement $\ell\,\hat{\jmath}$. On the first part only the horizontal component of the force does work, on the second only the vertical component, since the scalar product annihilates the components orthogonal to the displacement. The sum of the two contributions is $3\,F\,\ell$, as along the segment.

</div>

The constant force is conservative, with potential $U = F\,(x + y)$: the gradient of this function is indeed $F\,\hat{\imath} + F\,\hat{\jmath}$. The work is then computed again as a difference of potential,
$$
L = U(P_2) - U(P_1) = F\,(2\ell + \ell) - F\,(0 + 0) = 3\,F\,\ell ,
$$
and the value coincides with the one found along the two paths, as the theorem on the work of a conservative force requires.

## Exercise 2

A particle $P$ is connected to the origin $O$ by an ideal spring of elastic constant $k$ and zero natural length. The particle moves from the position $P_1 = (\ell, 0)$ to the position $P_2 = (0, 2\ell)$ along an arbitrary path, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the work of the elastic force.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P_1,2,0;P_2,0,4" linee="2,0 1.9,0.6 1.5,1.5 0.9,2.6 0.4,3.4 0,4;0,0 0.25,0 0.4,0.18 0.7,-0.18 1,0.18 1.3,-0.18 1.6,0.18 1.75,0 2,0" tratteggi="0,0 0,4" punti="2,0;0,4" titolo="The particle moves from $P_1 = (\ell, 0)$ to $P_2 = (0, 2\ell)$ with the spring attached at $O$"}

The elastic force pulling back towards the fixed point $O$ is conservative and has potential
$$
U = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2 ,
\qquad
r = |P - O| .
$$
The distances in the two positions are $r_1 = \ell$ and $r_2 = 2\ell$, hence
$$
L = U(P_2) - U(P_1) = -\frac{1}{2}\,k\,(2\ell)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2 = -\frac{3}{2}\,k\,\ell^2 .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The spring has zero natural length, therefore its length coincides with the distance $r$ of the particle from the origin and the potential of the spring reduces to $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2$. The end $O$ is fixed, hence the force applied at $O$ has zero displacement and does no work; only the force applied at $P$ remains.

The particle moves away from the origin, since $r_2 = 2\ell$ is greater than $r_1 = \ell$, and the elastic force is directed towards $O$. The force and the displacement therefore form an obtuse angle along the whole path, hence the work is **negative**. The result does not depend on the path followed, given that the force is conservative.

</div>

The value is checked with the line integral along the segment $P_1 P_2$, parametrized by $P(u) = \big( \ell\,(1-u),\; 2\ell\,u \big)$ with $u \in [0, 1]$. The force is $\vec{F} = -k\,P$ and the elementary displacement is $\dfrac{dP}{du} = (-\ell,\; 2\ell)$, therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
L &= \int_0^1 \Big( -k\,\ell\,(1-u) \cdot (-\ell) - k\,2\ell\,u \cdot 2\ell \Big)\,du \\[6pt]
&= k\,\ell^2 \int_0^1 (1 - u)\,du - 4\,k\,\ell^2 \int_0^1 u\,du \\[6pt]
&= \frac{k\,\ell^2}{2} - 2\,k\,\ell^2 = -\frac{3}{2}\,k\,\ell^2 ,
\end{aligned}
$$
in agreement with the computation carried out with the potential.

## Exercise 3

A particle is acted upon by the position-dependent force $\vec{F} = c\,y\,\hat{\imath}$, with $c > 0$ constant. Compute the work along the two paths in the figure, both from $A = (0, 0)$ to $B = (\ell, \ell)$: the path $A C B$ with $C = (\ell, 0)$ and the path $A D B$ with $D = (0, \ell)$. Determine whether the force is conservative.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,3;C,3,0;D,0,3" linee="0,0 3,0 3,3" tratteggi="0,0 0,3;0,3 3,3" forze="1.5 3,0.9,0,F;1.5 1.5,0.45,0,F" punti="0,0;3,0;0,3;3,3" titolo="The two paths $ACB$ and $ADB$ between the same endpoints; the force $\vec{F} = c\,y\,\hat{\imath}$ grows with the height"}

Along $AC$ the height is $y = 0$, therefore the force is zero and the work is zero. Along $CB$ the displacement is vertical, $dP = dy\,\hat{\jmath}$, and the force is horizontal, hence the scalar product is zero. The work on the first path is thus
$$
L_{ACB} = 0 + 0 = 0 .
$$
Along $AD$ the displacement is vertical and the force horizontal, therefore the contribution is zero. Along $DB$ the height is constant, $y = \ell$, hence $\vec{F} = c\,\ell\,\hat{\imath}$ and the displacement is $dP = dx\,\hat{\imath}$, whence
$$
L_{ADB} = 0 + \int_0^{\ell} c\,\ell\,dx = c\,\ell^2 .
$$
The two works are different, therefore the force **is not conservative**.

<div class="spiegazione">

On each part one computes the scalar product $\vec{F} \cdot dP$ and integrates. On the vertical parts the force, which is directed as $\hat{\imath}$, is orthogonal to the displacement, therefore the work is zero; on the part $AC$ the force itself vanishes, since its magnitude is proportional to the height and the height is zero.

The path $ADB$ rises immediately to the height $\ell$ and then travels the horizontal part with the force at its maximum value $c\,\ell$, in the same direction as the displacement; the work is therefore $c\,\ell^2$. The path $ACB$ travels the horizontal part at zero height, where the force is zero.

The work therefore depends on the path, and by the theorem on the work of a conservative force this rules out the existence of a potential.

</div>

The same result is reached with the closedness condition of the differential form $\vec{F} \cdot dP$, which for a conservative force in the plane requires $\dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial x}$. Here $F_x = c\,y$ and $F_y = 0$, hence
$$
\frac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = c \neq 0 = \frac{\partial F_y}{\partial x} ,
$$
and the condition, which is necessary, is not satisfied.

## Exercise 4

A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the origin $O$ and rotates in the vertical plane, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the work of the weight when the rod passes from the downward vertical configuration, $\vartheta = 0$, to the horizontal configuration, $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, where $\vartheta$ is the angle between the rod and the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2.6,0" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.6" punti="1.3,0,G,baricentro;0,-1.3,G_0,baricentro" coppie="0.9 -0.9,1,ϑ" forze="1.3 0,0,-0.8,mg" titolo="The rod rotates from the downward vertical configuration, dashed, to the horizontal configuration"}

The weight is conservative and its potential depends only on the height of the center of mass, which is the midpoint of the rod,
$$
y_G = -\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
U(\vartheta) = -m g\,y_G = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta .
$$
The work is the difference of the values of the potential in the two configurations,
$$
L = U\!\left( \frac{\pi}{2} \right) - U(0) = 0 - \frac{m g \ell}{2} = -\frac{m g \ell}{2} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is homogeneous, therefore its center of mass coincides with the midpoint and is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from the hinge. The height of this point is $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, because the downward vertical corresponds to $\vartheta = 0$ and the angle is measured starting from it.

The potential of the weight of an extended body is written with the total mass and the height of the center of mass, hence for the rod it is $U = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$. Passing from $\vartheta = 0$ to $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ the center of mass **rises** from $-\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ to $0$, hence the work of the weight is negative.

</div>

The computation is repeated with the moment of the weight with respect to the hinge. The rod rotates about $O$, therefore the elementary work of the weight is $dL = M_O\,d\vartheta$, where $M_O$ is the component along $\hat{k}$ of the moment of the weight with respect to $O$. With $G - O = \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath})$ and weight $-m g\,\hat{\jmath}$ one obtains $M_O = -\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$, hence
$$
L = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \left( -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta \right) d\vartheta = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\,\big[ -\cos\vartheta \big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = -\frac{m g \ell}{2} ,
$$
in agreement with the value found with the potential.
