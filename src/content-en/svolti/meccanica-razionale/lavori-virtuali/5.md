## Exercise 1

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a plane inclined by the angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal. The center $C$ is connected by an ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length to a fixed anchor placed uphill, at the same distance $R$ from the plane, as in the diagram in the figure. The plane of the motion is vertical. Determine the equilibrium configuration.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 4.6,2.66 4.6,0" cerchi="2.3,2.02,0.6" punti="2.3,2.02,C,baricentro;2.6,1.5,H,centro;4,3,D" linee="4.00,3.00 3.78,2.88 3.77,2.61 3.29,2.85 3.26,2.32 2.79,2.56 2.75,2.03 2.52,2.15 2.30,2.02" forze="2.3 1.45,0,-0.5," quote="4 3,2.3 2.02,s,-1.1" etichette="0.75,0.2,α;3.4,2.9,k;1.95,1.75,mg" titolo="Disk rolling on an inclined plane, pulled back by a spring anchored uphill"}

The system has one degree of freedom, because pure rolling ties the rotation of the disk to the translation of the center. The distance $s$ of the center $C$ from the anchor $D$, measured along the plane downhill, is taken as the generalized coordinate, so that the spring has length $s$ and the height of the center is
$$
y_C = y_D - s\sin\alpha .
$$
The constraints are fixed and ideal, since the reaction of the contact is applied at a point of zero velocity, and the active force system is conservative. The potential of the system, up to additive constants, is
$$
U(s) = -m g\,y_C - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2 = m g\,s\sin\alpha - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2 .
$$
The equilibrium condition $U'(s) = 0$ gives
$$
m g\sin\alpha - k\,s = 0 ,
\qquad
s_{\text{eq}} = \frac{m g\sin\alpha}{k} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Pure rolling is a constraint that, for a disk on a fixed straight guide, integrates and reduces to the relation between the angle of rotation and the displacement of the center; the system therefore remains holonomic with a single degree of freedom. The reaction of the contact is applied at the point of contact, whose velocity is zero, hence its virtual work is zero and the constraint is **ideal**.

The height of the center decreases when $s$ grows, since $s$ is measured downhill; hence the sign of the potential of the weight, which turns out to be increasing in $s$. The potential of the spring is instead decreasing, quadratically in absolute value.

The derivative of the potential is the Lagrangian component of the force system relative to $s$, and it has the dimensions of a force. The equilibrium equation then expresses the balance between the component of the weight along the plane, $m g\sin\alpha$, and the pull of the spring, $k\,s$.

The equilibrium configuration exists for every value of the data and is **unique**, because the equation is of the first degree in $s$. As the stiffness $k$ grows the center gets closer to the anchor, as is to be expected from a stiffer spring.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equations of statics applied to the disk. The forces acting on the disk are the weight and the force of the spring, both applied at the center, and the reaction of the contact, which splits into the normal component $N$ and the tangential one $T$. The rotation about the center $C$ gives
$$
T\,R = 0 ,
$$
because the weight and the elastic force have lines of action passing through $C$; therefore $T = 0$. The translation along the plane, with the axis directed uphill, then gives
$$
k\,s - m g\sin\alpha + T = 0 ,
$$
whence $s = \dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{k}$, in agreement with the computation carried out with the potential.

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a plane inclined by the angle $\alpha$. An inextensible string is wound on the rim of the disk and leaves it at the point $E$ diametrically opposite the contact, parallel to the inclined plane; the string passes over an ideal pulley placed uphill and holds a hanging body of mass $M$, as in the diagram in the figure. The plane of the motion is vertical. Determine the value of $M$ for which the system is in equilibrium.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 5.2,3.0 5.2,0;5.41,3.2 5.91,3.2 5.91,2.7 5.41,2.7" cerchi="2.5,2.14,0.6;5.66,4.66,0.22" punti="2.5,2.14,C,baricentro;2.8,1.62,H,centro;2.2,2.66,E" nodi="E,2.2,2.66,-;P,5.66,4.66,-;Q,5.66,3.2,-" catene="E-P;P-Q" etichette="0.7,0.2,α;5.66,2.95,M;3.8,3.9,string" titolo="Disk on an inclined plane with a string wound on the rim, pulley and hanging body"}

The system has one degree of freedom: pure rolling ties the rotation of the disk to the displacement of the center, and the inextensible string ties the displacement of the hanging body to that of the point $E$. The displacement $s$ of the center $C$ along the plane, positive uphill, is taken as the generalized coordinate. The state of motion of the disk is a rotation about the point of contact $H$, which has zero velocity, therefore the velocity of $E$ is proportional to its distance from $H$,
$$
\frac{|\vec{v}_E|}{|\vec{v}_C|} = \frac{|E - H|}{|C - H|} = \frac{2R}{R} = 2 ,
\qquad
\delta E = 2\,\delta s \ \text{along the plane} .
$$
The string is inextensible, therefore the part between $E$ and the pulley shortens by $2\,\delta s$ and the hanging body descends by $2\,\delta s$. The virtual work of the active forces is hence
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = -m g\sin\alpha\,\delta s + M g\,(2\,\delta s) = \big( 2 M g - m g\sin\alpha \big)\,\delta s .
$$
The constraints are ideal and bilateral, hence equilibrium requires $\delta L^{(a)} = 0$ for every $\delta s$, that is,
$$
2 M g - m g\sin\alpha = 0 ,
\qquad
M = \frac{m\sin\alpha}{2} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $E$ lies at the end of the diameter passing through the contact, hence its distance from the instantaneous center of rotation is $2R$, twice that of the center $C$. In a rotational state of motion the velocities are proportional to the distances from the instantaneous center of rotation, therefore $E$ moves with twice the velocity of $C$.

The string is inextensible, hence the sum of the lengths of the two parts remains constant: if the part between $E$ and the pulley shortens by $2\,\delta s$, the vertical part lengthens by the same amount and the hanging body **descends** by $2\,\delta s$. The weight of the body then does positive work.

The weight of the disk does work only through the change of height of the center, which for a displacement $\delta s$ uphill is $\delta s\sin\alpha$; hence the term $-m g\sin\alpha\,\delta s$.

The pulley is ideal and the string is inextensible, therefore these constraints are ideal and do not appear in the virtual work; the same holds for the reaction of the contact in pure rolling. The factor $2$ in the result comes entirely from the point at which the string is attached: if the string left the center of the disk the condition would be $M = m\sin\alpha$.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equations of statics applied to the two bodies separately. The hanging body is in equilibrium under its own weight and the tension of the string, hence the tension is $T = M g$. For the disk one writes the rotation about the point of contact $H$, with respect to which the reaction of the contact has zero moment:
$$
T \cdot 2R - m g\sin\alpha \cdot R = 0 ,
$$
since the arm of the tension with respect to $H$ is $2R$ and that of the component of the weight along the plane is $R$. One obtains $T = \dfrac{m g\sin\alpha}{2}$, and equating it to $M g$ gives back $M = \dfrac{m\sin\alpha}{2}$.

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping inside a fixed circular guide of radius $3R$, in a vertical plane, as in the diagram in the figure. A constant couple of moment $\mathcal{M} > 0$ in the clockwise direction is applied to the disk. Determine the equilibrium configurations and the condition on the data for them to exist.

::struttura{linee="-2.82,-1.03 -2.76,-1.17 -2.70,-1.32 -2.62,-1.45 -2.54,-1.59 -2.46,-1.72 -2.36,-1.85 -2.26,-1.97 -2.16,-2.08 -2.05,-2.19 -1.93,-2.30 -1.81,-2.40 -1.68,-2.49 -1.55,-2.57 -1.41,-2.65 -1.27,-2.72 -1.12,-2.78 -0.98,-2.84 -0.83,-2.88 -0.67,-2.92 -0.52,-2.95 -0.37,-2.98 -0.21,-2.99 -0.05,-3.00 0.10,-3.00 0.26,-2.99 0.42,-2.97 0.57,-2.94 0.73,-2.91 0.88,-2.87 1.03,-2.82 1.17,-2.76 1.32,-2.70 1.45,-2.62 1.59,-2.54 1.72,-2.46 1.85,-2.36 1.97,-2.26 2.08,-2.16 2.19,-2.05 2.30,-1.93" cerchi="1.15,-1.64,1" punti="0,0,O;1.15,-1.64,C,baricentro;1.72,-2.46,H,centro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-3.2;0,0 1.72,-2.46" coppie="0.6 -1.05,-1,ℳ" forze="1.15 -2.6,0,-0.55,mg" etichette="0.35,-1.5,ϑ" titolo="Disk rolling inside a fixed circular guide of radius $3R$, with a clockwise couple applied"}

The system has one degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ that the radius $OC$ forms with the downward vertical is taken as the generalized coordinate. The center of the disk describes a circle of radius $2R$, hence
$$
C - O = 2R\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta) ,
\qquad
y_C = -2R\cos\vartheta .
$$
The angular velocity $\omega$ of the disk is obtained from the condition of zero velocity of the point of contact $H$, with $H - C = R\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta)$:
$$
\vec{v}_H = \vec{v}_C + \omega\,\hat{k} \wedge (H - C) = (2R\dot{\vartheta} + \omega R)\,(\cos\vartheta,\; \sin\vartheta) = \vec{0} ,
$$
whence $\omega = -2\,\dot{\vartheta}$, therefore the angle of absolute rotation of the disk is $\varphi = -2\,\vartheta$. The couple has moment $-\mathcal{M}\,\hat{k}$, hence its elementary work is
$$
dL_{\mathcal{M}} = -\mathcal{M}\,d\varphi = 2\,\mathcal{M}\,d\vartheta ,
$$
and the potential of the system is
$$
U(\vartheta) = -m g\,y_C + 2\,\mathcal{M}\,\vartheta = 2\,m g R\cos\vartheta + 2\,\mathcal{M}\,\vartheta .
$$
The equilibrium condition is
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -2\,m g R\sin\vartheta + 2\,\mathcal{M} = 0 ,
\qquad
\sin\vartheta = \frac{\mathcal{M}}{m g R} .
$$
The equation has a solution if and only if
$$
\mathcal{M} \leq m g R ,
$$
and in that case the equilibrium configurations are two in $[0, 2\pi)$, symmetric with respect to the vertical through $O$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The center of the disk stays at distance $3R - R = 2R$ from the center of the guide, since the disk touches the guide from the inside; the angle $\vartheta$ therefore identifies the configuration of the center, and pure rolling ties the rotation of the disk to $\vartheta$.

The relation between $\omega$ and $\dot{\vartheta}$ is obtained by requiring that the point of contact have zero velocity, as pure rolling requires. The negative sign says that the disk rotates in the **opposite direction** to the one in which its center moves along the guide, and the factor $2$ comes from the ratio between the distance $2R$ traveled by the center and the radius $R$ of the disk.

The couple is clockwise, therefore its moment is $-\mathcal{M}\,\hat{k}$ with $\mathcal{M} > 0$; multiplying by $d\varphi = -2\,d\vartheta$ gives a positive elementary work when $\vartheta$ grows. The couple therefore tends to make the disk rise along the guide in the direction of increasing $\vartheta$.

The equilibrium equation compares the moment of the couple with that of the weight. If $\mathcal{M} > m g R$ the couple prevails in every configuration and the system **has no equilibrium**; if $\mathcal{M} = m g R$ the two configurations reduce to the single one $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equation of statics for the rotation about the point of contact $H$, with respect to which the reaction of the contact has zero moment. With $C - H = -R\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta)$ the moment of the weight is
$$
(C - H) \wedge (-m g\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot \hat{k} = m g R\sin\vartheta ,
$$
and the couple contributes $-\mathcal{M}$, whatever the pole. The equation $m g R\sin\vartheta - \mathcal{M} = 0$ coincides with the one obtained by setting the derivative of the potential equal to zero.
