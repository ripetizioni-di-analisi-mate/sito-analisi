## Exercise 1

A particle $P$ of mass $m$ rests on a smooth horizontal plane that can only push it upwards. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and natural length $\ell_0$ connects $P$ to the fixed point $C$, placed on the vertical through $P$ at the height $h > \ell_0$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the condition for $P$ to remain in equilibrium on the plane.

::struttura{nodi="C,0,2.6;P,0,0" linee="-1.4,0 1.4,0;0.00,2.60 0.00,2.30 0.25,2.13 -0.25,1.80 0.25,1.47 -0.25,1.13 0.25,0.80 -0.25,0.47 0.00,0.30 0.00,0.00" forze="0.45 0,0,-0.6,mg" quote="P,C,h,-1.6" etichette="1.1,1.6,k" frecce="-0.65,0,-0.65,0.8,δy" titolo="Particle resting on a horizontal plane and pulled upwards by a spring"}

The configuration with $P$ on the plane is a boundary configuration, because the unilateral constraint is satisfied with equality. The virtual displacements of $P$ starting from this configuration are the horizontal ones, which are reversible, and the vertical ones directed upwards, for which
$$
\delta y \geq 0 .
$$
The active forces are the weight $-m g\,\hat{\jmath}$ and the force of the spring, which is vertical and directed towards $C$, of magnitude $k\,(h - \ell_0)$ since the spring is elongated by $h - \ell_0$. The virtual work is hence
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = \big( k\,(h - \ell_0) - m g \big)\,\delta y ,
$$
and it does not contain $\delta x$, given that both forces are vertical. The equilibrium condition $\delta L^{(a)} \leq 0$ for every $\delta y \geq 0$ is equivalent to
$$
k\,(h - \ell_0) \leq m g .
$$
If the inequality is satisfied strictly the particle remains resting on the plane; if equality holds the particle is on the point of **detaching**, because the reaction of the plane vanishes.

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane is a unilateral constraint: it prevents the particle from descending and does not prevent it from rising. The virtual displacements that take $P$ away from the plane are therefore allowed, whereas the opposite ones are not; for these displacements the sign cannot be reversed, hence the inequality remains and no equality is obtained.

The spring is elongated, given that $h > \ell_0$, therefore it pulls $P$ towards $C$, that is, upwards, with the force of magnitude $k\,(h - \ell_0)$. The weight acts in the opposite direction. The sum of the two forces is vertical, and its work for a displacement $\delta y$ upwards is positive when the spring prevails.

The condition $\delta L^{(a)} \leq 0$ then says that the weight has to prevail over the elastic force, or else equal it. Otherwise the particle rises, and equilibrium on the plane is impossible.

The horizontal displacements are reversible and give no conditions, because no active force has a horizontal component; if there were a horizontal force, it would have to be zero at equilibrium.

</div>

The result is checked with the equilibrium of the particle written with the forces. The reaction of the smooth plane is $N\,\hat{\jmath}$ with $N \geq 0$, since the constraint is unilateral and can only push; the vertical equilibrium gives
$$
N + k\,(h - \ell_0) - m g = 0 ,
\qquad
N = m g - k\,(h - \ell_0) .
$$
The condition $N \geq 0$ coincides with the one found with virtual work, and the equality $N = 0$ identifies the detachment.

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, is placed horizontally in the vertical plane, with a smooth hinge at $A$ and the end $B$ resting on a horizontal plane that can only push it upwards. At the point $D$, at distance $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$ from $A$, the vertical force $F$ directed upwards acts, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the condition on $F$ for the rod to remain resting at $B$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" linee="3.4,-0.18 4.6,-0.18" punti="2,0,G,baricentro" forze="3 0,0,0.95,F;2 -1.4,0,-0.6,mg" frecce="4,0,4,0.7,δy_B" quote="A,3 0,1.5 ℓ,1.75;3 0,B,0.5 ℓ,1.75" titolo="Rod hinged at $A$, resting at $B$, with an upward force applied at $D$"}

The system has one degree of freedom, the angle $\vartheta$ of rotation about $A$. The support at $B$ prevents the end from descending, therefore the virtual displacements starting from the horizontal configuration are those with
$$
\delta\vartheta \geq 0 ,
$$
that is, the counterclockwise rotations, which lift $B$. The vertical displacements of the points of application are $\delta y_G = \ell\,\delta\vartheta$ and $\delta y_D = \dfrac{3\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta$, hence
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = -m g\,\ell\,\delta\vartheta + F\,\frac{3\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta = \ell\,\left( \frac{3}{2}\,F - m g \right)\,\delta\vartheta .
$$
The equilibrium condition $\delta L^{(a)} \leq 0$ for every $\delta\vartheta \geq 0$ is equivalent to
$$
\frac{3}{2}\,F - m g \leq 0 ,
\qquad
F \leq \frac{2}{3}\,m g .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The hinge at $A$ is bilateral and ideal, therefore its reaction does no virtual work; the support at $B$ is unilateral, hence it admits only the displacements that lift the end.

In the counterclockwise rotation by $\delta\vartheta$ every point of the rod rises by an amount proportional to its distance from $A$. The center of mass, which is at distance $\ell$ from $A$, rises by $\ell\,\delta\vartheta$, and the point $D$, which is at distance $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$, rises by $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta$.

The weight does negative work in this rotation, since the center of mass rises, whereas the force $F$ does positive work, given that it is directed upwards like the displacement of $D$. Equilibrium requires the contribution of the weight to prevail.

The coefficient $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is the ratio between the arm of the force and that of the weight with respect to $A$. If $F$ exceeds $\dfrac{2}{3}\,m g$ the rod **rotates** about the hinge and the end $B$ is lifted.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equation of statics for the rotation about $A$, in which the reaction $\Phi_B\,\hat{\jmath}$ of the support has arm $2\ell$:
$$
2\ell\,\Phi_B - m g\,\ell + F\,\frac{3\ell}{2} = 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_B = \frac{m g}{2} - \frac{3}{4}\,F .
$$
The support is unilateral and can only push upwards, hence the condition $\Phi_B \geq 0$ is equivalent to $F \leq \dfrac{2}{3}\,m g$, as found with virtual work.

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, rests on two unilateral supports that can only push it upwards, placed at the end $A$ and at the point $D$ at distance $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$ from $A$. At the end $B$ the vertical force $F$ directed downwards acts, as in the diagram in the figure. The plane is vertical. Determine the condition on $F$ for the rod to remain resting at $A$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,3,0" aste="A-B" linee="-0.6,-0.18 0.6,-0.18;2.4,-0.18 3.6,-0.18" punti="2,0,G,baricentro" forze="B,0,-0.95,F;2 -1.4,0,-0.6,mg" frecce="0,0,0,0.75,δy_A" quote="A,3 0,1.5 ℓ,1.75;3 0,B,0.5 ℓ,1.75" titolo="Rod on two unilateral supports with a vertical load at the overhanging end"}

With both supports active the system admits no virtual displacement other than the zero one. The virtual displacement that leaves the support at $A$ is then considered, that is, the clockwise rotation about $D$, which lifts $A$; its amplitude is denoted by $\delta\vartheta \geq 0$. In this rotation the points to the left of $D$ rise and those to the right descend, hence
$$
\delta y_A = \frac{3\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta ,
\qquad
\delta y_G = \frac{\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta ,
\qquad
\delta y_B = -\frac{\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta ,
$$
since $G$ is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $D$ on the side of $A$ and $B$ is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $D$ on the opposite side. The virtual work of the active forces is
$$
\delta L^{(a)} = -m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta + F\,\frac{\ell}{2}\,\delta\vartheta = \frac{\ell}{2}\,(F - m g)\,\delta\vartheta ,
$$
and the condition $\delta L^{(a)} \leq 0$ for every $\delta\vartheta \geq 0$ is equivalent to
$$
F \leq m g .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The support at $D$ remains active during the rotation considered, because the rod keeps touching it; the support at $A$ is instead left, and this is the non-reversible virtual displacement that provides the condition.

In the rotation about $D$ the direction of the vertical displacement depends on the position of the point with respect to $D$: the center of mass, which lies to the left of $D$, rises, whereas the end $B$, which lies to the right, descends. Hence the negative sign of $\delta y_B$.

The weight therefore does negative work and the force $F$, directed downwards and applied at a point that descends, does positive work. The equilibrium condition requires the contribution of the weight to prevail, and it is the expression of the fact that the overhanging load tends to make the rod **overturn** about $D$.

The two arms with respect to $D$ are equal, both of them $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$, because the center of mass is at distance $\ell$ from $A$ and the support $D$ is at distance $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$ from it; this makes the comparison between $F$ and $m g$ direct.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equation of statics for the rotation about $D$, in which the reaction $\Phi_A\,\hat{\jmath}$ has arm $\dfrac{3\ell}{2}$, the weight has arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ on the same side and the force $F$ has arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ on the opposite side:
$$
-\frac{3\ell}{2}\,\Phi_A + m g\,\frac{\ell}{2} - F\,\frac{\ell}{2} = 0 ,
\qquad
\Phi_A = \frac{m g - F}{3} .
$$
The support is unilateral, hence the condition $\Phi_A \geq 0$ is equivalent to $F \leq m g$, in agreement with the computation carried out with virtual work.
