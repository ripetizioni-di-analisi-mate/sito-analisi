## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $2\ell$, has the end $A$ constrained by a smooth sleeve to slide on a fixed horizontal guide and can rotate about $A$ in the vertical plane, below the guide. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $A$ to the fixed point $O$ of the guide, and the constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$ with $F > 0$ is applied at the end $B$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations.

::struttura{nodi="A,1.8,0;B,4.4,-1.5" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,90" linee="-0.4,0 5,0;0.00,0.00 0.25,0.00 0.38,0.25 0.64,-0.25 0.90,0.25 1.16,-0.25 1.42,0.25 1.55,0.00 1.80,0.00" punti="0,0,O;3.1,-0.75,G,baricentro" forze="B,0.75,0,F;3.1 -2.3,0,-0.5,mg" quote="0 0,A,s,0.75" etichette="0.9,0.45,k;2.6,-0.35,ϑ" titolo="Rod with one end on a horizontal guide, restoring spring and constant force at $B$"}

The system has two degrees of freedom and the abscissa $s$ of $A$ on the guide, measured from $O$, and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the guide, measured below the guide, are taken as generalized coordinates. The positions of the points needed are
$$
A = (s,\; 0) ,
\qquad
G = (s + \ell\cos\vartheta,\; -\ell\sin\vartheta) ,
\qquad
B = (s + 2\ell\cos\vartheta,\; -2\ell\sin\vartheta) .
$$
The constraints are smooth, fixed and bilateral, therefore ideal, and the three force systems are conservative. The potential is the sum of the three contributions,
$$
U(s, \vartheta) = -m g\,y_G - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2 + F\,x_B = m g \ell\sin\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,s^2 + F\,(s + 2\ell\cos\vartheta) .
$$
The equilibrium configurations are the stationary points of $U$, therefore
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial s} = -k\,s + F = 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = m g \ell\cos\vartheta - 2\,F\,\ell\sin\vartheta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The first equation gives
$$
s_{\text{eq}} = \frac{F}{k} ,
$$
and the second, divided by $\ell\cos\vartheta$, gives
$$
\tan\vartheta = \frac{m g}{2\,F} ,
\qquad
\vartheta_{\text{eq}} = \arctan\frac{m g}{2\,F} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sleeve leaves $A$ free to slide along the guide and leaves the rod free to rotate, therefore two coordinates are needed: the abscissa $s$ and the angle $\vartheta$. The two coordinates are independent and the system is holonomic with two degrees of freedom.

The potential is written by adding the contributions of the weight, of the spring and of the constant force. The force $F\,\hat{\imath}$ is constant, therefore its potential is $F\,x_B$, that is, the force times the abscissa of the point of application.

The two equilibrium equations **uncouple**: only $s$ appears in the first, because the potential depends on $s$ through the spring and through $F\,s$, whereas only $\vartheta$ appears in the second. The translation along the guide indeed carries the whole rod with it, without rotating it, therefore in it the weight does no work.

The first equation expresses the balance between the applied force and the pull of the spring on the sleeve. The second expresses the balance of the moments with respect to $A$ between the weight, with arm $\ell\cos\vartheta$, and the force $F$, with arm $2\ell\sin\vartheta$. The factor $\cos\vartheta$, which cancels, does not vanish for $\vartheta \in \left( 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$, the interval in which the solution falls.

</div>

The result is checked on the virtual work computed directly. For the virtual displacement of translation alone, with $\delta\vartheta = 0$, all the points move by $\delta s\,\hat{\imath}$ and the virtual work is $(F - k\,s)\,\delta s$, since the weight is vertical; the vanishing of this work gives $s = \dfrac{F}{k}$. For the virtual displacement of rotation alone about $A$, with $\delta s = 0$, one has
$$
\delta G = \ell\,(-\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta)\,\delta\vartheta ,
\qquad
\delta B = 2\ell\,(-\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta)\,\delta\vartheta ,
$$
therefore the virtual work is $\big( m g \ell\cos\vartheta - 2 F \ell\sin\vartheta \big)\,\delta\vartheta$, in agreement with the second equation.

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $M$ and length $4\ell$, is hinged at its midpoint $O$ by a smooth hinge and moves in the vertical plane. A ring $P$ of mass $m$, which can be treated as a particle, slides without friction along the rod and is connected to $O$ by an ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,-1.85,1.6;B,1.85,-1.6" aste="A-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="1.5,-1.3,P" linee="0.00,0.00 0.17,-0.14 0.43,-0.08 0.37,-0.61 0.89,-0.48 0.84,-1.02 1.36,-0.89 1.33,-1.16 1.50,-1.30" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.2" forze="1.5 -2.3,0,-0.5,mg" quote="O,1.5 -1.3,r,0.65" etichette="0.3,-1.75,ϑ;1.15,-0.3,k" titolo="Rod hinged at the midpoint with a sliding ring pulled back by a spring"}

The system has two degrees of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical and the abscissa $r$ of $P$ along the rod, measured from $O$ and positive towards $B$, with $r \in [-2\ell,\, 2\ell]$, are taken as generalized coordinates. The positions needed are
$$
P - O = r\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta) ,
\qquad
y_P = -r\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
y_G = 0 ,
$$
where $G$ is the center of mass of the rod, which coincides with $O$ and is fixed. The potential is therefore
$$
U(r, \vartheta) = -m g\,y_P - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2 = m g\,r\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2 ,
$$
and the equilibrium configurations satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial r} = m g\cos\vartheta - k\,r = 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = -m g\,r\sin\vartheta = 0
\end{cases}
$$
The second equation is satisfied when $r = 0$ or when $\sin\vartheta = 0$, and the two cases are discussed separately.

If $r = 0$, the first equation becomes $m g\cos\vartheta = 0$, therefore $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ or $\vartheta = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$: the rod is **horizontal** and the ring is at the hinge.

If $\sin\vartheta = 0$ with $\vartheta = 0$, the first equation gives $r = \dfrac{m g}{k}$; with $\vartheta = \pi$ it gives $r = -\dfrac{m g}{k}$. In both cases the rod is **vertical** and the ring is at distance $\dfrac{m g}{k}$ below the hinge. These configurations are admissible if and only if
$$
\frac{m g}{k} \leq 2\ell .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates about the midpoint and the ring slides along the rod, therefore two independent coordinates are needed. The abscissa $r$ is taken with its sign, so that a single number identifies the position of the ring on the whole rod.

The center of mass of the homogeneous rod coincides with the midpoint, which is the fixed hinge; its height does not change, hence the weight of the rod has constant potential and does not enter the equilibrium equations.

The spring has length $|r|$, therefore its potential is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,r^2$ and holds for negative $r$ as well. The potential of the weight of the ring depends on the product $r\cos\vartheta$, hence the two coordinates are coupled and the two equations are not solved separately.

The second equation vanishes in two distinct ways, and each one has to be substituted into the first. The configurations with $\vartheta = 0$ and $\vartheta = \pi$ describe the same physical position of the ring, because the rod is symmetric with respect to $O$ and the two orientations differ by the exchange of the ends $A$ and $B$.

The condition $\dfrac{m g}{k} \leq 2\ell$ requires the spring to be stiff enough to hold the ring on the rod: if it is not satisfied, the ring reaches the end and equilibrium has to be sought on the boundary of the interval of the configurations.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equations of statics applied to the ring and to the rod. The ring is tied to the rod by a smooth constraint, therefore the reaction of the rod on $P$ is perpendicular to the rod; the equilibrium of the ring in the direction of the rod hence requires that the component of the weight along the rod and the force of the spring balance each other,
$$
-m g\cos\vartheta + k\,r = 0 ,
$$
which is the first equation. For the rod one writes the rotation about $O$: the weight of the rod has zero moment, since it is applied at $O$, and the reaction of the ring on the rod, perpendicular to it and of magnitude $m g\,|\sin\vartheta|$, has arm $|r|$; equilibrium then requires $m g\,r\sin\vartheta = 0$, which is the second equation.

## Exercise 3

Two rings $P_1$ and $P_2$, which can be treated as particles of mass $m$ and $2m$, slide without friction along a fixed vertical guide. An ideal spring of constant $k$ connects $P_1$ to the fixed point $O$ of the guide, and an ideal spring of constant $2k$ connects $P_1$ to $P_2$; both have zero natural length, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configuration.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P_1,0,-1.6;P_2,0,-3.6" linee="-0.45,0 0.45,0;0.00,0.00 0.00,-0.25 0.25,-0.36 -0.25,-0.58 0.25,-0.80 -0.25,-1.02 0.25,-1.24 0.00,-1.35 0.00,-1.60;0.00,-1.60 0.00,-1.90 0.25,-2.02 -0.25,-2.26 0.25,-2.50 -0.25,-2.74 0.25,-2.98 -0.25,-3.22 0.00,-3.34 0.00,-3.60" tratteggi="0,0.3 0,-4.3" punti="0,-1.6;0,-3.6" etichette="0.75,-0.8,k;0.85,-2.8,2k;-0.75,-1.6,y_1;-0.75,-3.6,y_2" titolo="Two rings on a vertical guide, connected by springs of constant $k$ and $2k$"}

The system has two degrees of freedom and the heights $y_1$ and $y_2$ of the two rings, measured from $O$ upwards, are taken as generalized coordinates. The two springs have length $|y_1|$ and $|y_2 - y_1|$, hence the potential is
$$
U(y_1, y_2) = -m g\,y_1 - 2 m g\,y_2 - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,y_1^2 - \frac{1}{2}\,(2k)\,(y_2 - y_1)^2 .
$$
The equilibrium configurations satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial y_1} = -m g - k\,y_1 + 2k\,(y_2 - y_1) = 0 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial y_2} = -2 m g - 2k\,(y_2 - y_1) = 0
\end{cases}
$$
From the second equation one obtains
$$
y_2 - y_1 = -\frac{m g}{k} ,
$$
and substituting into the first gives $-m g - k\,y_1 - 2 m g = 0$, therefore
$$
y_1 = -\frac{3\,m g}{k} ,
\qquad
y_2 = y_1 - \frac{m g}{k} = -\frac{4\,m g}{k} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rings slide on a vertical guide, therefore each one is identified by its own height and the system has two degrees of freedom. The guide is smooth and fixed, hence the constraints are ideal and bilateral.

The first spring connects $P_1$ to a fixed point, hence its potential depends on $y_1$ alone. The second connects the two rings, therefore its potential depends on the difference $y_2 - y_1$ and couples the two coordinates.

Both springs appear in the derivative with respect to $y_1$, because $P_1$ is an end of both; only the upper spring appears in the derivative with respect to $y_2$. The system is therefore solved starting from the second equation.

The negative sign of the results says that at equilibrium both rings lie **below** the point $O$, as is to be expected with gravity directed downwards. The elongation of the spring $2k$ is $\dfrac{m g}{k}$ and that of the spring $k$ is $\dfrac{3\,m g}{k}$.

</div>

The result is checked with the equilibrium of the single rings. The ring $P_2$ is subject to its own weight $2 m g$ and to the spring of constant $2k$ alone, hence this spring has to be elongated by $\dfrac{2 m g}{2k} = \dfrac{m g}{k}$, in agreement with the value found for $y_2 - y_1$. The spring of constant $k$ instead holds the total weight of the two rings, $3 m g$, therefore its elongation is $\dfrac{3 m g}{k}$, which is the absolute value of $y_1$.
