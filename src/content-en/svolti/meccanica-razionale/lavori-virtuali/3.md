## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the origin $O$ by a smooth hinge and moves in the vertical plane. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects the end $A$ to the fixed point $B = (0, \ell)$, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.3,-1.8;B,0,2.2" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0.65,-0.9,G,baricentro" linee="0.00,2.20 0.12,1.82 0.40,1.69 0.11,1.14 0.66,0.88 0.37,0.33 0.93,0.07 0.64,-0.48 1.19,-0.74 0.90,-1.29 1.18,-1.42 1.30,-1.80" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.2" forze="0.65 -2.35,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="0.3,-0.85,ϑ;1.25,0.8,k" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with the spring between $A$ and the fixed point $B = (0, \ell)$"}

The system has one degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise, is taken as the generalized coordinate. The constraints are fixed, smooth and bilateral, hence ideal, and the active force system is conservative, therefore the equilibrium configurations are the stationary points of the potential. The quantities needed are
$$
A - O = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} - \cos\vartheta\,\hat{\jmath}) ,
\qquad
y_G = -\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta ,
$$
$$
\ell_{AB}^{\,2} = \ell^2\sin^2\vartheta + (-\ell\cos\vartheta - \ell)^2 = \ell^2\,(2 + 2\cos\vartheta) .
$$
The potential of the system is hence
$$
U(\vartheta) = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2\,(2 + 2\cos\vartheta) = \left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2 \right)\cos\vartheta - k\,\ell^2 ,
$$
and the equilibrium condition $U'(\vartheta) = 0$ is written
$$
-\left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2 \right)\sin\vartheta = 0 .
$$
If $k \neq \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$ the coefficient is different from zero, therefore the equation is equivalent to $\sin\vartheta = 0$ and the equilibrium configurations are
$$
\vartheta_1 = 0 ,
\qquad
\vartheta_2 = \pi .
$$
If instead $k = \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$ the potential is constant, hence **every configuration is an equilibrium configuration**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod rotates about the fixed hinge, hence a single angle identifies the configuration and the system has one degree of freedom. The hinge is smooth and its reaction is applied at a fixed point, whose virtual displacement is zero, therefore the constraint is ideal and the principle of virtual work applies.

The potential of the weight is written with the height of the center of mass, which is the midpoint of the rod. For the spring one needs the square of the distance between $A$ and $B$; expanding the square and using the Pythagorean identity gives $\ell^2\,(2 + 2\cos\vartheta)$, and the zero natural length makes it possible to use this square directly in the potential.

Both contributions contain $\cos\vartheta$, therefore it is factored out. The sign of the coefficient depends on the comparison between $\dfrac{m g \ell}{2}$ and $k\,\ell^2$, but the equilibrium equation remains $\sin\vartheta = 0$ as long as the coefficient does not vanish: the two equilibrium configurations are the one with the rod **vertical and pointing downwards** and the one with the rod **vertical and pointing upwards**.

In the case $k = \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$ the potential does not depend on $\vartheta$, therefore its derivative is zero for every $\vartheta$ and every configuration is an equilibrium configuration. In that condition the moment of the elastic force with respect to $O$ balances the moment of the weight in every position of the rod.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equation of statics for the rotation about $O$, which for a rigid body with a fixed point is enough to give equilibrium. The elastic force applied at $A$ is $-k\,(A - B)$, that is,
$$
\vec{F}_{\text{el}} = -k\,\ell\sin\vartheta\,\hat{\imath} + k\,\ell\,(\cos\vartheta + 1)\,\hat{\jmath} ,
$$
and the moment with respect to $O$ of the two active forces, in the component along $\hat{k}$, is
$$
\begin{aligned}
M_O &= (A - O) \wedge \vec{F}_{\text{el}} \cdot \hat{k} + (G - O) \wedge (-m g\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot \hat{k} \\[6pt]
&= k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta .
\end{aligned}
$$
The expression coincides with $U'(\vartheta)$, therefore the vanishing of the moment gives the same equilibrium configurations.

## Exercise 2

A constant couple of moment $\mathcal{M}$ in the counterclockwise direction also acts on the rod of the previous exercise, as in the diagram in the figure. Determine the equilibrium configurations and the condition on the data for them to exist, under the assumption $k \neq \dfrac{m g}{2 \ell}$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.3,-1.8;B,0,2.2" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0.65,-0.9,G,baricentro" linee="0.00,2.20 0.12,1.82 0.40,1.69 0.11,1.14 0.66,0.88 0.37,0.33 0.93,0.07 0.64,-0.48 1.19,-0.74 0.90,-1.29 1.18,-1.42 1.30,-1.80" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.2" forze="0.65 -2.35,0,-0.6,mg" coppie="2.1 -1.3,1,ℳ" etichette="0.3,-0.85,ϑ;1.25,0.8,k" titolo="The same rod with a constant couple $\mathcal{M}$ in the counterclockwise direction"}

The couple has elementary work $\mathcal{M}\,d\vartheta$, hence it has potential $\mathcal{M}\,\vartheta$ and the potential of the system becomes
$$
U(\vartheta) = \left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2 \right)\cos\vartheta + \mathcal{M}\,\vartheta - k\,\ell^2 .
$$
The equilibrium condition is
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\left( \frac{m g \ell}{2} - k\,\ell^2 \right)\sin\vartheta + \mathcal{M} = 0 ,
$$
that is,
$$
\sin\vartheta = \frac{2\,\mathcal{M}}{\ell\,(m g - 2\,k\,\ell)} .
$$
The equation has a solution if and only if the right-hand side has absolute value not greater than $1$, that is,
$$
2\,|\mathcal{M}| \leq \ell\,|m g - 2\,k\,\ell| .
$$
When the inequality is satisfied strictly the equilibrium configurations are **two** in $[0, 2\pi)$, since two angles correspond to an admissible value of $\sin\vartheta$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The couple is constant, therefore its elementary work on a rigid body that rotates by the angle $d\vartheta$ is $\mathcal{M}\,d\vartheta$ and the function $\mathcal{M}\,\vartheta$ is its potential. The potential of the couple is added to that of the weight and to that of the spring.

The derivative of the potential now contains the constant term $\mathcal{M}$, therefore the equilibrium equation no longer has the form $\sin\vartheta = 0$ and the vertical configurations are in general not equilibrium configurations. Dividing by the coefficient, which is different from zero by assumption, gives the value of $\sin\vartheta$.

The sine takes values in the interval $[-1, 1]$, hence the equation has a solution only if the couple is not too large compared with the moment that the weight and the spring can exert. If $2\,|\mathcal{M}| > \ell\,|m g - 2\,k\,\ell|$ the system **has no equilibrium configurations**, because the couple makes the rod rotate in every position.

</div>

The condition is checked on the limiting case $\mathcal{M} = 0$, in which the equation becomes $\sin\vartheta = 0$ and the two vertical configurations of the previous exercise are recovered, and on the case $2\,\mathcal{M} = \ell\,(m g - 2\,k\,\ell)$ with $m g > 2\,k\,\ell$, in which one obtains $\sin\vartheta = 1$ and the two configurations reduce to the single one $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, with the rod horizontal.

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $AB$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, has the end $A$ constrained to a smooth vertical guide and the end $B$ to a smooth horizontal guide, meeting at the origin $O$, as in the diagram in the figure. An ideal spring of constant $k$ and zero natural length connects $B$ to the origin. The plane is vertical. Determine the equilibrium configurations with $\vartheta \in \left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, where $\vartheta$ is the angle between the rod and the horizontal guide.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,2.4;B,3.2,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello,180;B,carrello" linee="-0.3,0 4.2,0;0,-0.3 0,3.2;0.00,0.00 0.35,0.00 0.56,0.30 0.97,-0.30 1.39,0.30 1.81,-0.30 2.23,0.30 2.64,-0.30 2.85,0.00 3.20,0.00" punti="1.6,1.2,G,baricentro" forze="1.6 0.15,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="1.6,-0.62,k;2.72,0.22,ϑ" titolo="Rod with its ends on two orthogonal guides and spring between $B$ and the origin"}

The system has one degree of freedom and $\vartheta$ is taken as the generalized coordinate. The positions of the points needed are
$$
A = (0,\; \ell\sin\vartheta) ,
\qquad
B = (\ell\cos\vartheta,\; 0) ,
\qquad
G = \left( \frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta,\; \frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta \right) .
$$
The spring has length $|B - O| = \ell\cos\vartheta$, hence the potential of the system is
$$
U(\vartheta) = -m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta - \frac{1}{2}\,k\,\ell^2\cos^2\vartheta .
$$
The equilibrium condition is
$$
U'(\vartheta) = -\frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta + k\,\ell^2\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta = \ell\cos\vartheta\,\left( k\,\ell\sin\vartheta - \frac{m g}{2} \right) = 0 ,
$$
and the product vanishes when one of the two factors vanishes:
$$
\cos\vartheta = 0
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
\sin\vartheta = \frac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell} .
$$
The first equation gives $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, with the rod vertical. The second has a solution in the interval considered if and only if $m g \leq 2\,k\,\ell$, and in that case it gives
$$
\vartheta^* = \arcsin\frac{m g}{2\,k\,\ell} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two guides are smooth and fixed, therefore their constraints are ideal; the rod is rigid, hence its length ties the positions of the two ends and a single degree of freedom remains. The angle $\vartheta$ identifies the configuration, and from it one obtains the coordinates of $A$, of $B$ and of the center of mass, which for a homogeneous rod is the midpoint.

The spring connects $B$ to the origin and has zero natural length, hence its potential is $-\dfrac{1}{2}\,k\,|B - O|^2$, with $|B - O| = \ell\cos\vartheta$. The potential of the weight depends only on the ordinate of the center of mass, which is $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$.

The derivative of the potential factors by taking out $\ell\cos\vartheta$, therefore the equilibrium equation splits into two simpler equations. The configuration $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is an equilibrium configuration for every value of the data: in it $B$ coincides with $O$, the spring has zero length and the weight has its line of action along the vertical guide, on which $A$ is constrained.

The second configuration exists only if the spring is stiff enough, that is, if $2\,k\,\ell \geq m g$, because the sine of an angle does not exceed $1$. The spring must indeed pull $B$ back towards $O$ with a force capable of balancing the moment of the weight.

</div>

The result is checked with the cardinal equations of statics applied to the rod in the configuration $\vartheta^*$. The reactions of the two smooth guides are $\vec{\Phi}_A = \Phi_A\,\hat{\imath}$ at $A$ and $\vec{\Phi}_B = \Phi_B\,\hat{\jmath}$ at $B$, because each guide exerts a force normal to its own direction, and the spring exerts at $B$ the force $-k\,\ell\cos\vartheta\,\hat{\imath}$. The horizontal translation and the vertical one give
$$
\begin{cases}
\Phi_A - k\,\ell\cos\vartheta = 0 \\[4pt]
\Phi_B - m g = 0
\end{cases}
$$
and the rotation about $B$, with $G - B = \dfrac{\ell}{2}\,(-\cos\vartheta,\; \sin\vartheta)$ and $A - B = \ell\,(-\cos\vartheta,\; \sin\vartheta)$, gives
$$
-\Phi_A\,\ell\sin\vartheta + m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\cos\vartheta = 0 .
$$
Substituting $\Phi_A = k\,\ell\cos\vartheta$ gives $\cos\vartheta\,\left( \dfrac{m g}{2} - k\,\ell\sin\vartheta \right) = 0$, which is the same equation found with the potential.
