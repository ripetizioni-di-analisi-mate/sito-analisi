## Exercise 1

A homogeneous rod $OA$, of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, is hinged at the origin by a smooth hinge and moves in the vertical plane. The constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, is applied at the end $A$, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the Lagrangian component of the force system and determine the equilibrium configurations.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.3,-1.8" aste="O-A" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" punti="0.65,-0.9,G,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.2" forze="A,0.9,0,F;0.65 -2.3,0,-0.6,mg" etichette="0.3,-0.85,ϑ" titolo="Rod hinged at $O$ with a constant horizontal force applied at $A$"}

The system has one degree of freedom and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the downward vertical, measured counterclockwise, is taken as the generalized coordinate. The positions of the points of application are
$$
A = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta) ,
\qquad
G = \frac{\ell}{2}\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta) ,
$$
and the derivatives with respect to the generalized coordinate are
$$
\frac{\partial A}{\partial \vartheta} = \ell\,(\cos\vartheta,\; \sin\vartheta) ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial G}{\partial \vartheta} = \frac{\ell}{2}\,(\cos\vartheta,\; \sin\vartheta) .
$$
The Lagrangian component of the force system is the sum of the scalar products of each force with the derivative of its own point of application,
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_\vartheta &= \vec{F} \cdot \frac{\partial A}{\partial \vartheta} + (-m g\,\hat{\jmath}) \cdot \frac{\partial G}{\partial \vartheta} \\[6pt]
&= F\,\ell\cos\vartheta - m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta .
\end{aligned}
$$
The constraints are ideal and bilateral, therefore equilibrium requires $Q_\vartheta = 0$, that is,
$$
F\,\ell\cos\vartheta = \frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta ,
\qquad
\tan\vartheta = \frac{2\,F}{m g} .
$$
The equilibrium configurations are therefore two in $[0, 2\pi)$,
$$
\vartheta_1 = \arctan\frac{2\,F}{m g} ,
\qquad
\vartheta_2 = \vartheta_1 + \pi .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Lagrangian component is obtained from the definition, without going through the potential: one writes the positions of the points of application as functions of $\vartheta$, computes their partial derivatives and carries out the scalar products with the forces.

The force $\vec{F}$ is horizontal, therefore in the scalar product with $\dfrac{\partial A}{\partial \vartheta}$ only the component $\ell\cos\vartheta$ survives; the weight is vertical, hence in its scalar product $\dfrac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta$ survives, with the minus sign coming from the direction of the weight.

The coordinate $\vartheta$ is an angle, hence $Q_\vartheta$ has the dimensions of a moment, and indeed it coincides with the moment of the active forces with respect to the hinge.

The equation $\tan\vartheta = \dfrac{2 F}{m g}$ has two solutions in the full turn, which correspond to the two positions of the rod on the same line: one with $A$ **below** the hinge and one with $A$ above. For $F \to 0$ the angle tends to zero and the downward vertical rod is recovered.

</div>

The result is checked with the potential, since both forces are conservative. The constant force $F\,\hat{\imath}$ has potential $F\,x_A = F\,\ell\sin\vartheta$, and the weight has potential $-m g\,y_G = \dfrac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta$, hence
$$
U(\vartheta) = F\,\ell\sin\vartheta + \frac{m g \ell}{2}\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
U'(\vartheta) = F\,\ell\cos\vartheta - \frac{m g \ell}{2}\sin\vartheta ,
$$
and the derivative coincides with the $Q_\vartheta$ computed directly, as the theorem on the Lagrangian components of a conservative force system requires.

## Exercise 2

Two homogeneous rods $OA$ and $AB$, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, are hinged to each other at $A$ and to the ground at $O$ by smooth hinges, and move in the vertical plane. The constant force $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{\imath}$, with $F > 0$, is applied at the end $B$, as in the diagram in the figure. Compute the Lagrangian components of the force system and determine the equilibrium configurations.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.1,-2.2;B,2.9,-3.1" aste="O-A;A-B" vincoli="O,cerniera,90" cerniere="A" punti="0.55,-1.1,G_1,baricentro;2,-2.65,G_2,baricentro" tratteggi="0,0 0,-2.6;1.1,-2.2 1.1,-4" forze="B,0.9,0,F" etichette="0.3,-1.1,ϑ;1.5,-3.4,φ" titolo="Double pendulum with a constant horizontal force applied at $B$"}

The system has two degrees of freedom and the angles $\vartheta$ and $\varphi$ that the two rods form with the downward vertical are taken as generalized coordinates. The positions of the points of application are
$$
G_1 = \frac{\ell}{2}\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta) ,
$$
$$
G_2 = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta) + \frac{\ell}{2}\,(\sin\varphi,\; -\cos\varphi) ,
$$
$$
B = \ell\,(\sin\vartheta,\; -\cos\vartheta) + \ell\,(\sin\varphi,\; -\cos\varphi) .
$$
Differentiating with respect to $\vartheta$ and carrying out the scalar products with the forces gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_\vartheta &= -m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\sin\vartheta - m g\,\ell\sin\vartheta + F\,\ell\cos\vartheta \\[6pt]
&= F\,\ell\cos\vartheta - \frac{3}{2}\,m g\,\ell\sin\vartheta ,
\end{aligned}
$$
and differentiating with respect to $\varphi$
$$
Q_\varphi = -m g\,\frac{\ell}{2}\sin\varphi + F\,\ell\cos\varphi .
$$
The constraints are ideal and bilateral, therefore the equilibrium configurations satisfy
$$
\begin{cases}
Q_\vartheta = 0 \\[4pt]
Q_\varphi = 0
\end{cases}
$$
that is,
$$
\tan\vartheta = \frac{2\,F}{3\,m g} ,
\qquad
\tan\varphi = \frac{2\,F}{m g} .
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The position of $G_2$ and that of $B$ depend on both angles, because the second rod is attached to the end of the first; the position of $G_1$ depends instead on $\vartheta$ alone.

In the derivative with respect to $\vartheta$ the contributions of all three forces appear, since a rotation of the first rod drags the second one as well. The weight of the second rod enters with the arm $\ell$, which is the distance of $A$ from $O$, whereas the weight of the first enters with the arm $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$; the sum of the two coefficients gives $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

In the derivative with respect to $\varphi$ only the forces applied to the second rod appear, that is, its weight and the force $\vec{F}$, since $G_1$ does not depend on $\varphi$. The equilibrium system therefore splits into **two independent equations**, one for each angle.

The comparison between the two results shows that $\tan\varphi$ is three times $\tan\vartheta$: the second rod leans more, because the same horizontal force acts on it but a smaller weight.

</div>

The two equations are checked with the potential. The weight of the two rods and the constant force are conservative, with
$$
U(\vartheta, \varphi) = \frac{3}{2}\,m g \ell\cos\vartheta + \frac{1}{2}\,m g \ell\cos\varphi + F\,\ell\,(\sin\vartheta + \sin\varphi) ,
$$
where the term in $F$ is $F\,x_B$. The partial derivatives
$$
\frac{\partial U}{\partial \vartheta} = -\frac{3}{2}\,m g \ell\sin\vartheta + F\,\ell\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial U}{\partial \varphi} = -\frac{1}{2}\,m g \ell\sin\varphi + F\,\ell\cos\varphi
$$
coincide with $Q_\vartheta$ and $Q_\varphi$ computed directly.

## Exercise 3

A homogeneous rod $AB$ of length $\ell$ has the end $A$ constrained by a smooth sleeve to slide on a fixed horizontal guide, and can rotate about $A$ in the horizontal plane, as in the diagram in the figure. A force of constant magnitude $F$, always perpendicular to the rod and directed as in the figure, is applied at the end $B$. The weight is orthogonal to the plane of the motion and is balanced by the supporting plane, therefore it does no work. Compute the Lagrangian components of the force system and determine whether it is conservative.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,1.2,0;B,3.33,1.49" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,carrello" linee="-0.2,0 4.6,0" forze="B,-0.57,0.82,F" etichette="1.95,0.22,ϑ" quote="O,A,s,0.8" titolo="Rod with the end $A$ on a horizontal guide and a force always perpendicular to the rod applied at $B$"}

The system has two degrees of freedom and the abscissa $s$ of the sleeve and the angle $\vartheta$ between the rod and the guide are taken as generalized coordinates. The position of the point of application and the unit vector perpendicular to the rod are
$$
B = (s + \ell\cos\vartheta,\; \ell\sin\vartheta) ,
\qquad
\hat{n} = (-\sin\vartheta,\; \cos\vartheta) ,
$$
and the force is $\vec{F} = F\,\hat{n}$. The derivatives of the position of $B$ are
$$
\frac{\partial B}{\partial s} = (1,\; 0) ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial B}{\partial \vartheta} = (-\ell\sin\vartheta,\; \ell\cos\vartheta) ,
$$
therefore
$$
Q_s = F\,(-\sin\vartheta) \cdot 1 + F\cos\vartheta \cdot 0 = -F\sin\vartheta ,
$$
$$
Q_\vartheta = F\,(-\sin\vartheta)(-\ell\sin\vartheta) + F\cos\vartheta \cdot \ell\cos\vartheta = F\,\ell\,(\sin^2\vartheta + \cos^2\vartheta) = F\,\ell .
$$
If the force system admitted a potential $U(s, \vartheta)$ of class $C^2$, by Schwarz's theorem the mixed derivatives would be equal, that is, $\dfrac{\partial Q_s}{\partial \vartheta} = \dfrac{\partial Q_\vartheta}{\partial s}$. Here instead
$$
\frac{\partial Q_s}{\partial \vartheta} = -F\cos\vartheta ,
\qquad
\frac{\partial Q_\vartheta}{\partial s} = 0 ,
$$
and the two expressions are different for every $\vartheta$ with $\cos\vartheta \neq 0$, hence the force system **is not conservative**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The force has constant magnitude but variable direction, since it remains perpendicular to the rod: its expression therefore contains the coordinate $\vartheta$ through the unit vector $\hat{n}$.

The component $Q_s$ is obtained from the virtual displacement that translates the rod along the guide without rotating it: in this displacement the point $B$ moves by $\delta s\,\hat{\imath}$, and only the horizontal component of the force does work, which is $-F\sin\vartheta$.

The component $Q_\vartheta$ is obtained from the virtual displacement that rotates the rod keeping $A$ fixed: in this displacement $B$ moves perpendicularly to the rod, by $\ell\,\delta\vartheta$, in the same direction as the force. The scalar product then gives $F\,\ell$ directly, and the Pythagorean identity removes every dependence on $\vartheta$.

The condition $\dfrac{\partial Q_s}{\partial \vartheta} = \dfrac{\partial Q_\vartheta}{\partial s}$ is *necessary* for a potential to exist, given that $Q_s$ and $Q_\vartheta$ would be its first partial derivatives. The two mixed derivatives are different, therefore no function $U$ can have these partial derivatives.

</div>

The conclusion is checked by computing the work along a closed cycle in the space of the configurations, which for a conservative force system would be zero. One travels the rectangle with $s$ from $0$ to $d$ at $\vartheta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ fixed, then $\vartheta$ from $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to $0$ with $s = d$, then $s$ from $d$ to $0$ with $\vartheta = 0$, then $\vartheta$ from $0$ to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ with $s = 0$. The four contributions are
$$
\int_0^{d} (-F\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2})\,ds = -F d ,
\qquad
\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{0} F\,\ell\,d\vartheta = -\frac{\pi}{2}\,F\,\ell ,
$$
$$
\int_{d}^{0} (-F\sin 0)\,ds = 0 ,
\qquad
\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} F\,\ell\,d\vartheta = \frac{\pi}{2}\,F\,\ell ,
$$
and their sum is $-F d$, different from zero for $d \neq 0$.
