## Exercise 1

The homogeneous rectangular plate in the figure has mass $m$ and sides $a$ and $b$, with the center of mass $G$ at the origin and the sides parallel to the axes. Compute the moment of inertia about the diagonal and the corresponding radius of gyration, and write the numerical value in the case $a = 2\ell$, $b = \ell$.

::struttura{poligoni="-1.5,-0.75 1.5,-0.75 1.5,0.75 -1.5,0.75" punti="0,0,G,baricentro" tratteggi="-2.1,-1.05 2.1,1.05" frecce="-2.6,0,2.9,0,x;0,-1.9,0,2,y" quote="-1.5 -0.75,1.5 -0.75,a,1.2;1.5 -0.75,1.5 0.75,b,0.55" etichette="1.35,0.42,d" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangular plate with the dashed line $d$ that contains the diagonal and passes through the center of mass"}

$$
I_{xx} = \frac{mb^2}{12} ,
\qquad
I_{yy} = \frac{ma^2}{12} ,
\qquad
I_{xy} = 0
$$
$$
\vec{u} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}\,(a,\ b,\ 0)
$$
$$
I_d = I_{xx}u_x^2 + I_{yy}u_y^2
= \frac{mb^2}{12} \cdot \frac{a^2}{a^2 + b^2} + \frac{ma^2}{12} \cdot \frac{b^2}{a^2 + b^2}
= \frac{m\,a^2b^2}{6\,(a^2 + b^2)}
$$
$$
\rho_d = \sqrt{\frac{I_d}{m}} = \frac{ab}{\sqrt{6\,(a^2 + b^2)}}
$$
$$
a = 2\ell ,\ b = \ell
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
I_d = \frac{m \cdot 4\ell^2 \cdot \ell^2}{6 \cdot 5\ell^2} = \frac{2\,m\ell^2}{15} ,
\qquad
\rho_d = \ell\sqrt{\frac{2}{15}} = \frac{\sqrt{30}}{15}\,\ell \approx 0.365\,\ell
$$

Check in the case of the square, that is with $a = b = \ell$:
$$
I_d = \frac{m\,\ell^4}{6 \cdot 2\ell^2} = \frac{m\ell^2}{12}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axes $x$ and $y$ are central and parallel to the sides, hence the two planes orthogonal to the plate that contain them are planes of material symmetry and the product of inertia $I_{xy}$ vanishes. The matrix is therefore diagonal, with $I_{xx} = \dfrac{mb^2}{12}$ and $I_{yy} = \dfrac{ma^2}{12}$, the central values of the rectangular plate.

The diagonal issuing from the vertex $\left(-\dfrac{a}{2}, -\dfrac{b}{2}\right)$ passes through $G$ and has the direction of the vector $(a, b, 0)$, hence its unit vector is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}(a, b, 0)$.

Applying the formula $I_d = \vec{u} \cdot \mathbb{I}_G\,\vec{u}$, and observing that the term with $I_{xy}$ vanishes, the two summands have the same denominator and add up to $\dfrac{m\,a^2b^2}{6(a^2 + b^2)}$. Dividing by $m$ and taking the square root one obtains the radius of gyration $\rho_d = \dfrac{ab}{\sqrt{6(a^2 + b^2)}}$.

With $a = 2\ell$ and $b = \ell$ one gets $I_d = \dfrac{2m\ell^2}{15}$ and $\rho_d \approx 0.365\,\ell$, that is a length smaller than half the short side: with respect to the diagonal the mass of the plate **lies on average very close to the axis**.

The check verifies the formula in the case of the square, in which the diagonal is an axis of material symmetry and is therefore a principal axis. The value $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$ coincides with the smallest principal moment computed for the square plate with respect to the pole at a vertex, as it must be, since for the square the diagonal through the vertex and the diagonal through the center are the same line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A homogeneous rigid cylinder has mass $m$, radius $R$ and height $h = 2R$. Let $\zeta$ be the axis of the cylinder and let $\xi$ be a central axis orthogonal to $\zeta$, as the cross section in the figure shows. Compute the moment of inertia about the central axis $u$ that makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the axis of the cylinder, and the corresponding radius of gyration.

::struttura{poligoni="-1.5,-1.5 1.5,-1.5 1.5,1.5 -1.5,1.5" punti="0,0,,baricentro" frecce="0,-2.4,0,2.6,ζ;-2.6,0,2.7,0,ξ;0,0,1.84,1.84,u" quote="-1.5 -1.5,1.5 -1.5,2R,0.6;1.5 -1.5,1.5 1.5,h = 2R,0.6" etichette="0.95,0.3,45°;-0.4,-0.45,G" riferimento=0 titolo="Cross section of the cylinder with the axis $\zeta$, the orthogonal central axis $\xi$ and the axis $u$ inclined at $45^\circ$"}

$$
I_{\zeta} = \frac{mR^2}{2} ,
\qquad
I_{\xi} = \frac{m\,(3R^2 + h^2)}{12} = \frac{m\,(3R^2 + 4R^2)}{12} = \frac{7\,mR^2}{12}
$$
$$
\vec{u} = \sin 45^\circ\,\hat{\imath} + \cos 45^\circ\,\hat{k} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1,\ 0,\ 1)
$$
$$
I_u = I_{\xi}\,\frac{1}{2} + I_{\zeta}\,\frac{1}{2} = \frac{7\,mR^2}{24} + \frac{mR^2}{4} = \frac{13\,mR^2}{24}
$$
$$
\rho_u = \sqrt{\frac{I_u}{m}} = R\sqrt{\frac{13}{24}} = \frac{\sqrt{78}}{12}\,R \approx 0.736\,R
$$

Check with the two limiting cases of the formula $I_u(\alpha) = I_{\xi}\sin^2\alpha + I_{\zeta}\cos^2\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle between $u$ and the axis of the cylinder:
$$
I_u(0) = I_{\zeta} = \frac{mR^2}{2} = \frac{12\,mR^2}{24} ,
\qquad
I_u(90^\circ) = I_{\xi} = \frac{7\,mR^2}{12} = \frac{14\,mR^2}{24} ,
\qquad
\frac{12}{24} < \frac{13}{24} < \frac{14}{24}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis of the cylinder is an axis of material symmetry, hence every plane containing it is a plane of material symmetry and the triple formed by the axis $\zeta$ and by two central axes orthogonal to it is a principal frame. In this frame the tensor is diagonal and the products of inertia vanish.

The principal moments are read off the standard values: $I_{\zeta} = \dfrac{mR^2}{2}$ about the axis of the cylinder and $I_{\xi} = \dfrac{m(3R^2 + h^2)}{12}$ about a central axis orthogonal to it. With $h = 2R$ the second one equals $\dfrac{7mR^2}{12}$.

The unit vector of the axis $u$ has component $\sin 45^\circ$ along $\xi$ and component $\cos 45^\circ$ along $\zeta$, hence both squares equal $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the formula $I_u = \vec{u} \cdot \mathbb{I}_G\,\vec{u}$ reduces to the arithmetic mean of the two principal moments. One gets $I_u = \dfrac{13mR^2}{24}$ and $\rho_u \approx 0.736\,R$.

The check verifies the general formula in the two cases in which the result is known. For $\alpha = 0$ the axis $u$ coincides with the axis of the cylinder and the formula returns $I_{\zeta}$; for $\alpha = 90^\circ$ it coincides with $\xi$ and returns $I_{\xi}$. The value found for $45^\circ$ **lies between the two**, as it must be, because the moment of inertia about an axis through $G$ lies between the smallest and the largest of the principal moments.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The system in the figure consists of three material points: a point of mass $2m$ at $(\ell, \ell, 0)$, a point of mass $m$ at $(0, 0, 2\ell)$ and a point of mass $m$ at $(\ell, 0, 0)$. Compute the moment of inertia about the line through $O$ with unit vector $\vec{u} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)$ and the corresponding radius of gyration.

::struttura{punti="0.8,-0.8,P_1;0,4,P_2;-1.2,-0.8,P_3;0,0,O" tratteggi="0,0 0.8,-0.8;0,0 0,4;0,0 -1.2,-0.8" frecce="0,0,-2,-1.33,x;0,0,3,0,y;0,0,0,4.6,z;0,0,1.2,1.8,u" etichette="1.1,-0.95,2m;0.3,3.9,m;-1.75,-0.95,m" riferimento=0 titolo="The three material points and the line through $O$ with unit vector $\vec{u} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)$"}

$$
I_{xx} = 2m\ell^2 + m\,(2\ell)^2 + 0 = 6\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{yy} = 2m\ell^2 + m\,(2\ell)^2 + m\ell^2 = 7\,m\ell^2
$$
$$
I_{zz} = 2m\,(2\ell^2) + 0 + m\ell^2 = 5\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{xy} = 2m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{xz} = I_{yz} = 0
$$
$$
\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2
\begin{pmatrix}
6 & -2 & 0 \\
-2 & 7 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 5
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
I_u = \frac{1}{3}\,\big( 6 + 7 + 5 \big)\,m\ell^2 - 2 \cdot 2m\ell^2 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = 6\,m\ell^2 - \frac{4\,m\ell^2}{3} = \frac{14\,m\ell^2}{3}
$$
$$
M = 4m ,
\qquad
\rho_u = \sqrt{\frac{I_u}{M}} = \ell\sqrt{\frac{14}{12}} = \frac{\sqrt{42}}{6}\,\ell \approx 1.080\,\ell
$$

Check with the direct computation of the distances from the line, using $d^2 = |P - O|^2 - \big( (P - O) \cdot \vec{u} \big)^2$:
$$
2m \left( 2\ell^2 - \frac{4\ell^2}{3} \right) + m \left( 4\ell^2 - \frac{4\ell^2}{3} \right) + m \left( \ell^2 - \frac{\ell^2}{3} \right)
= \frac{4\,m\ell^2}{3} + \frac{8\,m\ell^2}{3} + \frac{2\,m\ell^2}{3}
= \frac{14\,m\ell^2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$ is built first, because the formula $I_u = \vec{u} \cdot \mathbb{I}_O\,\vec{u}$ requires the six independent components.

In the computation of $I_{xx}$ one adds $m_i(y_i^2 + z_i^2)$: the first point brings $2m\ell^2$, the second $4m\ell^2$ and the third nothing, because it lies on the $x$ axis. The computations of $I_{yy}$ and $I_{zz}$ follow the same rule with the corresponding pairs of coordinates. The products $I_{xz}$ and $I_{yz}$ vanish, because the only point with $z \ne 0$ has $x = 0$ and $y = 0$; the product $I_{xy}$ receives the contribution of the point of mass $2m$ alone and equals $2m\ell^2$.

In the formula for the moment of inertia about the axis with unit vector $\vec{u}$ every component of $\vec{u}$ equals $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, hence every product $u_hu_k$ equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$. The diagonal term gives one third of the trace, that is $6m\ell^2$, and the term with the product of inertia subtracts $\dfrac{4m\ell^2}{3}$; one gets $I_u = \dfrac{14m\ell^2}{3}$.

The radius of gyration is computed with the total mass $M = 4m$ and equals about $1.080\,\ell$, that is a little more than $\ell$: the value is reasonable, because the point farthest from the axis is the one of mass $m$ placed at $(0, 0, 2\ell)$, which is at distance $\ell\sqrt{\dfrac{8}{3}} \approx 1.63\,\ell$ from it.

The check recomputes $I_u$ from the definition. For each point one uses $d^2 = |P - O|^2 - \big((P - O) \cdot \vec{u}\big)^2$, with $(P - O) \cdot \vec{u}$ equal to the sum of the coordinates divided by $\sqrt{3}$: the first point gives $\dfrac{2\ell}{\sqrt{3}}$, the second $\dfrac{2\ell}{\sqrt{3}}$ and the third $\dfrac{\ell}{\sqrt{3}}$. Adding the three contributions one recovers $\dfrac{14m\ell^2}{3}$.

</div>
