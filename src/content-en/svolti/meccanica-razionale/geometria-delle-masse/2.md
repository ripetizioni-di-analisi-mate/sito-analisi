## Exercise 1

The homogeneous plate in the figure is obtained from the square with vertices $(0,0)$ and $(2\ell, 2\ell)$ by removing the square of side $\ell$ that has a vertex at the point $(2\ell, 2\ell)$. Determine the center of mass of the plate.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,1 1,1 1,2 0,2" fori="1,1 2,1 2,2 1,2" punti="0.833,0.833,G,baricentro" quote="0 0,2 0,2ℓ,0.45;1 2,2 2,ℓ,-0.4" tratteggi="0,0 2,2" frecce="-0.5,0,2.7,0,x;0,-0.5,0,2.7,y" riferimento=0 titolo="L-shaped plate obtained from the square of side $2\ell$ by removing the square of side $\ell$, with the dashed diagonal that is an axis of material symmetry"}

$$
A_1 = (2\ell)^2 = 4\ell^2 ,
\qquad
A_2 = \ell^2 ,
\qquad
A = A_1 - A_2 = 3\ell^2
$$
$$
G_1 = (\ell,\ \ell) ,
\qquad
G_2 = \left( \frac{3\ell}{2},\ \frac{3\ell}{2} \right)
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{A_1\,\ell - A_2\,\dfrac{3\ell}{2}}{A} = \frac{4\ell^3 - \dfrac{3}{2}\,\ell^3}{3\ell^2} = \frac{5}{6}\,\ell ,
\qquad
y_G = \frac{5}{6}\,\ell
$$

Check with the material symmetry with respect to the diagonal $y = x$:
$$
x_G = y_G = \frac{5}{6}\,\ell
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The plate is homogeneous, hence the surface density $\sigma$ is constant and the masses of the parts are proportional to the areas: in the weighted average the factor $\sigma$ cancels and the areas can be used directly in place of the masses.

The body is treated as the **difference** of two solid plates, the large square of area $4\ell^2$ and the missing square of area $\ell^2$. The additivity of the center of mass holds in this form too: since the large square is the union of the given plate and of the missing square, one has $A\,(G - O) = A_1\,(G_1 - O) - A_2\,(G_2 - O)$, and the missing square enters with the minus sign.

The large square has its center of mass at its own center $G_1 = (\ell, \ell)$ and the missing square at its own center $G_2 = \left(\dfrac{3\ell}{2}, \dfrac{3\ell}{2}\right)$, both by material symmetry. Substituting in the formula one gets $G = \left(\dfrac{5}{6}\,\ell,\ \dfrac{5}{6}\,\ell\right)$, which **lies outside the missing square** and closer to the origin than the center of the solid square, because the part removed lay on the opposite side.

The check uses symmetry. The reflection with respect to the line $y = x$ interchanges the coordinates and maps the plate onto itself, hence the plane orthogonal to the drawing that contains that line is a plane of material symmetry and the center of mass belongs to it, that is $x_G = y_G$. The computation, carried out separately on the two coordinates, gives two equal values.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The homogeneous plate in the figure is the half-disk with center $O$ and radius $R$ contained in the half-plane $y \ge 0$, with the concentric half-disk of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ removed. Determine the center of mass of the plate.

::struttura{poligoni="2.400,0.000 2.385,0.269 2.340,0.534 2.265,0.793 2.162,1.041 2.032,1.277 1.876,1.496 1.697,1.697 1.496,1.876 1.277,2.032 1.041,2.162 0.793,2.265 0.534,2.340 0.269,2.385 0.000,2.400 -0.269,2.385 -0.534,2.340 -0.793,2.265 -1.041,2.162 -1.277,2.032 -1.496,1.876 -1.697,1.697 -1.876,1.496 -2.032,1.277 -2.162,1.041 -2.265,0.793 -2.340,0.534 -2.385,0.269 -2.400,0.000" fori="1.200,0.000 1.185,0.188 1.141,0.371 1.069,0.545 0.971,0.705 0.849,0.849 0.705,0.971 0.545,1.069 0.371,1.141 0.188,1.185 0.000,1.200 -0.188,1.185 -0.371,1.141 -0.545,1.069 -0.705,0.971 -0.849,0.849 -0.971,0.705 -1.069,0.545 -1.141,0.371 -1.185,0.188 -1.200,0.000" punti="0,1.188,G,baricentro;0,0,O" quote="0 0,2.4 0,R,0.6" tratteggi="0,-0.4 0,2.9" frecce="-2.9,0,2.9,0,x" riferimento=0 titolo="Half annulus obtained from the half-disk of radius $R$ by removing the half-disk of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$"}

$$
A_1 = \frac{\pi R^2}{2} ,
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi}{2} \left( \frac{R}{2} \right)^2 = \frac{\pi R^2}{8} ,
\qquad
A = A_1 - A_2 = \frac{3\pi R^2}{8}
$$
$$
y_{G_1} = \frac{4R}{3\pi} ,
\qquad
y_{G_2} = \frac{4}{3\pi} \cdot \frac{R}{2} = \frac{2R}{3\pi}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{A_1\,y_{G_1} - A_2\,y_{G_2}}{A}
= \frac{\dfrac{\pi R^2}{2} \cdot \dfrac{4R}{3\pi} - \dfrac{\pi R^2}{8} \cdot \dfrac{2R}{3\pi}}{\dfrac{3\pi R^2}{8}}
= \frac{\dfrac{2R^3}{3} - \dfrac{R^3}{12}}{\dfrac{3\pi R^2}{8}}
= \frac{14\,R}{9\pi}
$$

Check with the comparison between the two half-disks:
$$
\frac{4R}{3\pi} \approx 0.42\,R ,
\qquad
\frac{14R}{9\pi} \approx 0.50\,R ,
\qquad
\frac{4R}{3\pi} < \frac{14R}{9\pi} < R
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $y$ axis is an axis of material symmetry both of the large half-disk and of the one removed, hence it is an axis of material symmetry of the plate and therefore $x_G = 0$: only the ordinate remains to be computed.

As in the previous exercise the plate is treated as the difference of two solid half-disks, and the masses are replaced by the areas because the plate is homogeneous. The center of mass of a homogeneous half-disk of radius $r$ is at distance $\dfrac{4r}{3\pi}$ from the center of the diameter, a result obtained in the theory by integrating in polar coordinates; applying it with $r = R$ and with $r = \dfrac{R}{2}$ one obtains the two partial ordinates.

The numerator equals $\dfrac{2R^3}{3} - \dfrac{R^3}{12} = \dfrac{7R^3}{12}$ and the denominator equals $\dfrac{3\pi R^2}{8}$, hence $y_G = \dfrac{7R^3}{12} \cdot \dfrac{8}{3\pi R^2} = \dfrac{14R}{9\pi}$.

The check compares the result with that of the solid half-disk. By removing matter near the center of the diameter the center of mass **moves away from the diameter**, therefore the value found must exceed $\dfrac{4R}{3\pi} \approx 0.42\,R$, and indeed it equals about $0.50\,R$. The value moreover remains smaller than $R$, as it must be for a point interior to the region occupied by the half annulus.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The homogeneous plate in the figure is the circular sector with center $O$, radius $R$ and half-angle $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, with the bisector on the $x$ axis. Determine the center of mass of the plate.

::struttura{poligoni="2.121,-2.121 2.319,-1.903 2.494,-1.667 2.646,-1.414 2.772,-1.148 2.871,-0.871 2.942,-0.585 2.986,-0.294 3.000,0.000 2.986,0.294 2.942,0.585 2.871,0.871 2.772,1.148 2.646,1.414 2.494,1.667 2.319,1.903 2.121,2.121 0.000,0.000" punti="1.801,0,G,baricentro;0,0," tratteggi="0,0 2.121,2.121;0,0 2.121,-2.121" etichette="1.1,0.42,α;1.1,-0.52,α;-0.3,-0.35,O" frecce="-0.6,0,3.7,0,x;0,-2.7,0,2.7,y" riferimento=0 titolo="Circular sector of radius $R$ and half-angle $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, with the bisector on the $x$ axis"}

$$
A = \alpha R^2 = \frac{\pi R^2}{4}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{1}{A} \int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha} \int_0^R (r\cos\vartheta)\,r\,dr\,d\vartheta
= \frac{1}{A} \cdot \frac{R^3}{3} \int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha} \cos\vartheta\,d\vartheta
= \frac{1}{A} \cdot \frac{R^3}{3} \cdot 2\sin\alpha
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{2R\sin\alpha}{3\alpha}
= \frac{2R \cdot \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{3 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{4}}
= \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\pi}\,R \approx 0.60\,R ,
\qquad
y_G = 0
$$

Check with the case of the half-disk, that is with $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$:
$$
\frac{2R\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}}{3 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{4R}{3\pi}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Polar coordinates $r$ and $\vartheta$ with the pole at $O$ are used, because the sector is described by the inequalities $0 \le r \le R$ and $-\alpha \le \vartheta \le \alpha$. The area element is $dA = r\,dr\,d\vartheta$ and the abscissa of a point is $x = r\cos\vartheta$.

The ordinate of the center of mass vanishes, because the $x$ axis is an axis of material symmetry of the sector.

In the double integral the variables separate, since the integrand is the product of $r^2$ by $\cos\vartheta$ and the domain is a rectangle in the coordinates $r$ and $\vartheta$. The integral in $r$ of $r^2$ between $0$ and $R$ equals $\dfrac{R^3}{3}$ and the integral in $\vartheta$ of $\cos\vartheta$ between $-\alpha$ and $\alpha$ equals $2\sin\alpha$. Dividing by the area $\alpha R^2$ one obtains the general formula $x_G = \dfrac{2R\sin\alpha}{3\alpha}$, and substituting $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ one gets $x_G = \dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{3\pi}\,R$, about $0.60\,R$.

The check verifies the general formula in the known case of the half-disk, which corresponds to $\alpha = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$: one recovers $\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}$, the value proved in the theory. The result is consistent also with the limiting case $\alpha \to 0$, in which the sector reduces to a radius and the formula gives $\dfrac{2R}{3}$, that is the center of mass of a rod whose density is proportional to the distance from $O$.

</div>
