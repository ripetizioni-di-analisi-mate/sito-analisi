## Exercise 1

The homogeneous rigid rod $AB$ in the figure has mass $m$ and length $\ell$, and lies in the plane $Oxy$. Compute the moment of inertia of the rod about the axis orthogonal to the plane through the end $A$ and about the axis orthogonal to the plane through the point $Q$ which is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{4}$ from the center $G$, and determine the corresponding radius of gyration.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" punti="2,0,G,baricentro;3,0,Q" quote="A,B,ℓ,0.55;2 0,3 0,d,-0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Homogeneous rod with the center of mass $G$ at the midpoint and the point $Q$ at distance $d = \dfrac{\ell}{4}$ from $G$"}

$$
I_G = \frac{m\ell^2}{12}
$$
$$
I_A = I_G + m \left( \frac{\ell}{2} \right)^2 = \frac{m\ell^2}{12} + \frac{m\ell^2}{4} = \frac{m\ell^2}{3}
$$
$$
I_Q = I_G + m \left( \frac{\ell}{4} \right)^2 = \frac{m\ell^2}{12} + \frac{m\ell^2}{16} = \frac{7\,m\ell^2}{48}
$$
$$
\rho_Q = \sqrt{\frac{I_Q}{m}} = \ell\,\sqrt{\frac{7}{48}} \approx 0.382\,\ell
$$

Check with the direct computation of the integral, measuring the abscissa $s$ from $A$:
$$
\int_0^{\ell} \frac{m}{\ell} \left( s - \frac{3\ell}{4} \right)^2 ds
= \frac{m}{\ell} \left[ \frac{1}{3} \left( s - \frac{3\ell}{4} \right)^3 \right]_0^{\ell}
= \frac{m}{3\ell} \left( \frac{\ell^3}{64} + \frac{27\ell^3}{64} \right)
= \frac{7\,m\ell^2}{48}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three axes considered are parallel to one another, because they are all orthogonal to the plane of the rod, hence the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** applies starting from the central axis.

The starting value is $I_G = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$, the moment of inertia of a homogeneous rod about the axis orthogonal to it through the midpoint.

The end $A$ is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{2}$ from $G$, therefore the transport term is $m\dfrac{\ell^2}{4}$ and one gets $I_A = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$. The point $Q$ is at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{4}$ from $G$, hence the transport term is $m\dfrac{\ell^2}{16}$ and one gets $I_Q = \dfrac{7m\ell^2}{48}$. The radius of gyration relative to this last axis equals $\rho_Q = \ell\sqrt{\dfrac{7}{48}} \approx 0.382\,\ell$, that is, the whole mass of the rod, concentrated at that distance from the axis through $Q$, would give the same moment of inertia.

The check recomputes $I_Q$ from the definition. Measuring the abscissa from $A$, the point $Q$ has abscissa $\dfrac{\ell}{2} + \dfrac{\ell}{4} = \dfrac{3\ell}{4}$ and the distance of a point of the rod from the axis through $Q$ equals $\left| s - \dfrac{3\ell}{4} \right|$; the integral of the density $\dfrac{m}{\ell}$ times the square of that distance gives $\dfrac{7m\ell^2}{48}$, the same value. One notes finally that $I_Q < I_A$, as it must be, because among the axes of one and the same direction the one closer to the center of mass gives the smaller moment of inertia.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The homogeneous rigid disk in the figure has mass $m$ and radius $R$ and lies in the plane $Oxy$ with its center at $O$. Let $P$ be a point of the boundary. Compute the moment of inertia of the disk about the axis through $P$ orthogonal to the plane of the disk and about the line tangent to the boundary at $P$.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2" punti="0,0,O,baricentro;2,0,P" linee="2,-2.6 2,2.6" tratteggi="0,0 2,0" etichette="2.35,2.3,t;0.85,0.3,R" riferimento=0 titolo="Homogeneous disk with the point $P$ on the boundary and the line $t$ tangent to the boundary at $P$"}

$$
I_{zz}^{(O)} = \frac{mR^2}{2} ,
\qquad
I_{xx}^{(O)} = I_{yy}^{(O)} = \frac{mR^2}{4}
$$
$$
I_{zz}^{(P)} = \frac{mR^2}{2} + mR^2 = \frac{3\,mR^2}{2}
$$
$$
I_t = \frac{mR^2}{4} + mR^2 = \frac{5\,mR^2}{4}
$$

Check with the relation between the moments of inertia of a plane system, applied to the pole $P$:
$$
I_t + I_{d} = \frac{5\,mR^2}{4} + \frac{mR^2}{4} = \frac{3\,mR^2}{2} = I_{zz}^{(P)}
$$
where $d$ is the line through $P$ containing the diameter.

<div class="spiegazione">

The frame is chosen with the origin at the center $O$, the $x$ axis along the diameter through $P$ and the $z$ axis orthogonal to the disk, so that the tangent $t$ at $P$ is parallel to the $y$ axis.

The starting values are the central ones of the homogeneous disk: $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$ about the axis orthogonal to the plane and $\dfrac{mR^2}{4}$ about any diameter.

The axis orthogonal to the disk through $P$ is parallel to the central orthogonal axis and is at distance $R$ from it, hence the **Huygens-Steiner theorem** gives $I_{zz}^{(P)} = \dfrac{mR^2}{2} + mR^2 = \dfrac{3mR^2}{2}$.

The tangent $t$ is parallel to the diameter orthogonal to $OP$ and is at distance $R$ from it, because $O$ lies on the diameter and the tangent passes through $P$. The transport therefore gives $I_t = \dfrac{mR^2}{4} + mR^2 = \dfrac{5mR^2}{4}$.

The check uses the relation $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$, valid for every pole when the whole mass lies in the plane $Oxy$. With respect to the pole $P$, the two lines of the plane are the tangent $t$ and the line $d$ containing the diameter; for this last one the moment of inertia is $\dfrac{mR^2}{4}$, because $d$ coincides with a central diameter and the transport vanishes. The sum of the two equals $\dfrac{3mR^2}{2}$, that is the value computed for the orthogonal axis at $P$, and the two results are therefore **consistent with each other**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The system in the figure consists of a homogeneous rigid rod $OA$ of mass $m$ and length $2R$ and of a homogeneous rigid disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R$, welded to the rod so that the center $C$ of the disk lies on the extension of $OA$ at distance $R$ from $A$. Compute the moment of inertia of the system about the axis through $O$ orthogonal to the plane.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2,0" aste="O-A" cerchi="3,0,1" punti="3,0,C;1,0,G_1" quote="O,A,2R,0.8;A,3 0,R,1.4" etichette="0.85,0.35,m;3.3,0.75,2m" riferimento=0 titolo="Rod of mass $m$ and length $2R$ with a disk of mass $2m$ and radius $R$ welded at the end"}

$$
I_{\text{rod}}^{(O)} = \frac{m\,(2R)^2}{3} = \frac{4\,mR^2}{3}
$$
$$
I_{\text{disk}}^{(O)} = \frac{2m\,R^2}{2} + 2m\,(3R)^2 = mR^2 + 18\,mR^2 = 19\,mR^2
$$
$$
I_O = \frac{4\,mR^2}{3} + 19\,mR^2 = \frac{61\,mR^2}{3}
$$

Check by passing through the center of mass of the system, whose abscissa is
$$
x_G = \frac{m \cdot R + 2m \cdot 3R}{3m} = \frac{7R}{3}
$$
$$
I_G = \left( \frac{m\,(2R)^2}{12} + m \left( \frac{4R}{3} \right)^2 \right) + \left( mR^2 + 2m \left( \frac{2R}{3} \right)^2 \right) = \frac{19\,mR^2}{9} + \frac{17\,mR^2}{9} = 4\,mR^2
$$
$$
I_O = I_G + 3m\,x_G^2 = 4\,mR^2 + 3m\,\frac{49R^2}{9} = \frac{61\,mR^2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moment of inertia is additive, hence the moment of inertia of the system about the axis through $O$ is the sum of the moments of inertia of the rod and of the disk about the same axis.

For the rod the axis through $O$ passes through one of its ends and is orthogonal to the rod, therefore it equals $\dfrac{m(2R)^2}{3}$, with the length $2R$ in place of $\ell$.

For the disk one starts from the central value $\dfrac{2m R^2}{2} = mR^2$ and transports from the axis through $C$ to the axis through $O$. Since $C$ lies on the extension of $OA$ at distance $R$ from $A$, one gets $OC = 2R + R = 3R$, hence the transport term is $2m(3R)^2 = 18mR^2$. Adding the two contributions one obtains $I_O = \dfrac{61mR^2}{3}$.

The check follows a different route, passing through the center of mass of the system. First one computes $x_G = \dfrac{7R}{3}$ with the weighted average of the abscissas of the two partial centers of mass, then one computes $I_G$ by transporting to the axis through $G$ both the contribution of the rod and that of the disk, and finally one returns to $O$ with a single transport of the total mass $3m$. The two routes give the same value $\dfrac{61mR^2}{3}$, and the value $I_G = 4mR^2$ **is smaller than $I_O$**, as it must be, because among the parallel axes of one and the same direction the smallest moment belongs to the central one.

</div>
