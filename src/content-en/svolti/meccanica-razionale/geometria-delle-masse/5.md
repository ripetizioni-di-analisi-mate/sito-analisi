## Exercise 1

Three material points are placed, in the frame $(O; x, y, z)$, as the diagram in the figure shows: the point $P_1$ of mass $m$ at $(\ell, 0, 0)$, the point $P_2$ of mass $m$ at $(0, \ell, 0)$ and the point $P_3$ of mass $2m$ at $(0, 0, \ell)$. Write the inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$.

::struttura{punti="-1.2,-0.8,P_1;2,0,P_2;0,2,P_3;0,0,O" tratteggi="0,0 -1.2,-0.8;0,0 2,0;0,0 0,2" frecce="0,0,-1.8,-1.2,x;0,0,3,0,y;0,0,0,3,z" etichette="-0.8,-1.1,m;2.15,0.4,m;0.55,2.05,2m" riferimento=0 titolo="The three points lie on the coordinate axes, each at distance $\ell$ from the origin"}

$$
I_{xx} = m\ell^2 + 2m\ell^2 = 3\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{yy} = m\ell^2 + 2m\ell^2 = 3\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{zz} = m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 = 2\,m\ell^2
$$
$$
I_{xy} = I_{xz} = I_{yz} = 0
$$
$$
\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 3 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 2
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Check with the moment of inertia about the point $O$:
$$
I_O = m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 + 2m\ell^2 = 4\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{xx} + I_{yy} + I_{zz} = 8\,m\ell^2 = 2\,I_O
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moment of inertia about the $x$ axis is computed by adding, for each point, the mass times the square of the distance from the $x$ axis, that is times $y^2 + z^2$. The point $P_1$ lies on the $x$ axis, hence its distance vanishes and it does not contribute; the point $P_2$ is at distance $\ell$ and the point $P_3$ is at distance $\ell$, therefore $I_{xx} = m\ell^2 + 2m\ell^2 = 3m\ell^2$. The computation of $I_{yy}$ is identical up to the interchange of the roles, whereas in the computation of $I_{zz}$ the two points of mass $m$ contribute and one gets $2m\ell^2$.

Each point has two vanishing coordinates, because it lies on a coordinate axis, hence every product $xy$, $xz$ and $yz$ is zero and the three products of inertia vanish. The matrix of the tensor is then **diagonal**, and by the definition of principal axis the three coordinate axes are principal axes of inertia relative to $O$, with principal moments $3m\ell^2$, $3m\ell^2$ and $2m\ell^2$.

The check uses the relation between the trace of the matrix and the moment of inertia about the point $O$. This last one is computed by adding the mass times the square of the distance from $O$, which equals $\ell$ for each point, and one gets $I_O = 4m\ell^2$. The sum of the three diagonal entries equals $8m\ell^2$, that is twice $I_O$, as the relation $I_{xx} + I_{yy} + I_{zz} = 2\,I_O$ requires.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four material points of mass $m$ occupy the vertices of the square of side $\ell$ in the figure, in the plane $Oxy$: the origin $O$, the point $(\ell, 0, 0)$, the point $(0, \ell, 0)$ and the point $(\ell, \ell, 0)$. Write the inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$ and compute the moment of inertia about the diagonal issuing from $O$.

::struttura{punti="0,0,;2,0,A;0,2,B;2,2," tratteggi="0,0 2,0;2,0 2,2;2,2 0,2;0,2 0,0" frecce="-0.7,0,3.2,0,x;0,-0.7,0,3.2,y;0,0,2.8,2.8,u" quote="0 0,2 0,ℓ,0.6" etichette="1.45,0.75,45°;-0.3,-0.35,O;2.4,1.72,C" riferimento=0 titolo="Four points of mass $m$ at the vertices of a square of side $\ell$; the line with unit vector $\vec{u}$ is the diagonal issuing from $O$"}

$$
I_{xx} = m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 = 2\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{yy} = m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 = 2\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{zz} = m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 + 2m\ell^2 = 4\,m\ell^2
$$
$$
I_{xy} = m\,\ell \cdot \ell = m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{xz} = I_{yz} = 0
$$
$$
\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & -1 & 0 \\
-1 & 2 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 4
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\vec{u} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 1, 0) ,
\qquad
I_u = \vec{u} \cdot \mathbb{I}_O\,\vec{u} = 2\,m\ell^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + 2\,m\ell^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} - 2\,m\ell^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = m\ell^2
$$

Check with the direct computation of the distances from the diagonal:
$$
I_u = m \cdot 0 + m \left( \frac{\ell}{\sqrt{2}} \right)^2 + m \left( \frac{\ell}{\sqrt{2}} \right)^2 + m \cdot 0 = m\ell^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All the points lie in the plane $Oxy$, hence $z = 0$ for each of them and the products $I_{xz}$ and $I_{yz}$ vanish. The system is therefore plane and the $z$ axis is automatically principal.

In the computation of $I_{xx}$ only the points with $y \ne 0$ contribute, that is $(0, \ell, 0)$ and $(\ell, \ell, 0)$, each with $m\ell^2$; by symmetry $I_{yy}$ has the same value. In the computation of $I_{zz}$ the distance from $O$ equals $0$ for the origin, $\ell$ for the two points on the axes and $\ell\sqrt{2}$ for the opposite vertex, hence $I_{zz} = 4m\ell^2$; the value coincides with $I_{xx} + I_{yy}$, as must happen for a plane system.

The product of inertia $I_{xy}$ receives a contribution only from the point that has both coordinates non-zero, that is $(\ell, \ell, 0)$, and equals $m\ell^2$. In the matrix it appears with its sign changed, according to the convention adopted.

For the moment of inertia about the diagonal one applies the formula $I_u = \vec{u} \cdot \mathbb{I}_O\,\vec{u}$ with the unit vector $\vec{u} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 1, 0)$, which has $u_x = u_y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $u_z = 0$. The three terms equal $I_{xx}u_x^2 = m\ell^2$, $I_{yy}u_y^2 = m\ell^2$ and $-2I_{xy}u_xu_y = -m\ell^2$, hence $I_u = m\ell^2$.

The check computes the same moment of inertia from the definition. The points $O$ and $(\ell, \ell, 0)$ belong to the diagonal, therefore their distance from it vanishes; the points $(\ell, 0, 0)$ and $(0, \ell, 0)$ are symmetric with respect to the diagonal and are at distance $\dfrac{\ell}{\sqrt{2}}$ from it, that is half the diagonal of the square. The sum of the two contributions equals $m\ell^2$, the same value obtained with the tensor.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Three material points of mass $m$ are placed, in the frame $(O; x, y, z)$, at $(\ell, \ell, 0)$, $(0, \ell, \ell)$ and $(\ell, 0, \ell)$, as the diagram in the figure shows. Write the inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$, compute the moment of inertia about the line through $O$ with unit vector $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)$ and determine the principal moments of inertia.

::struttura{punti="0.8,-0.8,P_1;2,2,P_2;-1.2,1.2,P_3;0,0,O" tratteggi="0,0 0.8,-0.8;0,0 2,2;0,0 -1.2,1.2" frecce="0,0,-1.8,-1.2,x;0,0,3,0,y;0,0,0,3,z;0,0,1.2,1.8,u" riferimento=0 titolo="The three points of mass $m$ and the line with unit vector $\vec{u} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)$"}

$$
I_{xx} = m\ell^2 + 2\,m\ell^2 + m\ell^2 = 4\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{yy} = 4\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{zz} = 4\,m\ell^2
$$
$$
I_{xy} = m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{xz} = m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_{yz} = m\ell^2
$$
$$
\mathbb{I}_O = m\ell^2
\begin{pmatrix}
4 & -1 & -1 \\
-1 & 4 & -1 \\
-1 & -1 & 4
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
I_u = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} \big( 4 + 4 + 4 - 2 - 2 - 2 \big) = 2\,m\ell^2
$$
$$
\det\big( \mathbb{I}_O - I\,\mathbb{1} \big) = 0
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
I_1 = 2\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_2 = I_3 = 5\,m\ell^2
$$

Check with the direct computation of the distances from the line with unit vector $\vec{u}$ and with the trace:
$$
d_i^2 = |P_i - O|^2 - \big( (P_i - O) \cdot \vec{u} \big)^2 = 2\ell^2 - \frac{4\ell^2}{3} = \frac{2\ell^2}{3}
\quad \text{for each point}
$$
$$
I_u = 3m \cdot \frac{2\ell^2}{3} = 2\,m\ell^2 ,
\qquad
I_1 + I_2 + I_3 = 12\,m\ell^2 = I_{xx} + I_{yy} + I_{zz}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each point has one vanishing coordinate and two coordinates equal to $\ell$. In the computation of $I_{xx}$, which adds $m(y^2 + z^2)$, the first point brings $m\ell^2$, the second $2m\ell^2$ and the third $m\ell^2$, hence $I_{xx} = 4m\ell^2$; the computations of $I_{yy}$ and $I_{zz}$ give the same value, because the configuration is invariant under cyclic permutation of the three coordinates.

In the product $I_{xy} = \sum_i m\,x_iy_i$ only the point with both $x$ and $y$ non-zero contributes, that is $(\ell, \ell, 0)$, and one gets $m\ell^2$; for the same reason $I_{xz}$ and $I_{yz}$ equal $m\ell^2$. In the matrix the three products appear with their sign changed.

The moment of inertia about the line with unit vector $\vec{u}$ is obtained from the formula $I_u = \vec{u} \cdot \mathbb{I}_O\,\vec{u}$. Since every component of $\vec{u}$ equals $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, each product $u_hu_k$ equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$, hence $I_u$ is one third of the sum of all nine entries of the matrix, that is $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}(12 - 6) = 2m\ell^2$.

The principal moments are the eigenvalues of the matrix. The matrix has the form $5\,m\ell^2\,\mathbb{1} - \mathbb{J}$, where $\mathbb{J}$ is the matrix with all entries equal to $m\ell^2$; since $\mathbb{J}$ has eigenvalue $3m\ell^2$ in the direction $(1,1,1)$ and zero eigenvalue on the orthogonal plane, one obtains $I_1 = 2m\ell^2$ in the direction $(1,1,1)$ and $I_2 = I_3 = 5m\ell^2$ on every direction orthogonal to it. The smallest principal moment coincides with $I_u$, as it must be, because $\vec{u}$ is precisely the normalized direction $(1,1,1)$.

The check recomputes $I_u$ from the definition. The distance of a point from the line through $O$ with unit vector $\vec{u}$ satisfies $d^2 = |P - O|^2 - \big((P - O) \cdot \vec{u}\big)^2$; for each of the three points one gets $|P - O|^2 = 2\ell^2$ and $(P - O) \cdot \vec{u} = \dfrac{2\ell}{\sqrt{3}}$, hence $d^2 = \dfrac{2\ell^2}{3}$ and the sum gives $2m\ell^2$. The second check compares the sum of the eigenvalues with the trace of the matrix, and the two values coincide.

</div>
