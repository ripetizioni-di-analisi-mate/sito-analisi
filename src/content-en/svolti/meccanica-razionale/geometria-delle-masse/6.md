## Exercise 1

The homogeneous rigid rod in the figure has mass $m$ and length $\ell$, has one end at the origin $O$ and makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the $x$ axis, in the plane $Oxy$. Write the inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$ and compute the moment of inertia about the line containing the rod.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2.121,2.121" aste="O-A" frecce="-0.7,0,3.4,0,x;0,-0.7,0,3.4,y" etichette="0.85,0.35,45°;1.3,0.85,m" quote="O,A,ℓ,-0.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Homogeneous rod with one end at $O$, inclined at $45^\circ$ to the $x$ axis"}

$$
\lambda = \frac{m}{\ell} ,
\qquad
x(s) = s\cos 45^\circ = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\qquad
y(s) = s\sin 45^\circ = \frac{s}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\qquad
0 \le s \le \ell
$$
$$
I_{xx} = \int_0^{\ell} \lambda\,y^2\,ds = \frac{m}{\ell} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\ell^3}{3} = \frac{m\ell^2}{6} ,
\qquad
I_{yy} = \frac{m\ell^2}{6} ,
\qquad
I_{xy} = \frac{m}{\ell} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\ell^3}{3} = \frac{m\ell^2}{6}
$$
$$
\mathbb{I}_O = \frac{m\ell^2}{6}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & -1 & 0 \\
-1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 2
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\vec{u} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,(1, 1, 0) ,
\qquad
I_u = \frac{m\ell^2}{6} \left( \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \right) = 0
$$

Check with the known value of the moment of inertia of the rod about the orthogonal axis through one end:
$$
I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy} = \frac{m\ell^2}{6} + \frac{m\ell^2}{6} = \frac{m\ell^2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The rod is described by the arc length $s$ measured from $O$, so that the current point has coordinates $x = s\cos 45^\circ$ and $y = s\sin 45^\circ$, both equal to $\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{2}}$. The linear density is constant and equals $\lambda = \dfrac{m}{\ell}$.

The rod lies in the plane $Oxy$, hence $z = 0$ and the products $I_{xz}$ and $I_{yz}$ vanish. The moment $I_{xx}$ is the integral of $\lambda y^2$, that is of $\dfrac{m}{\ell} \cdot \dfrac{s^2}{2}$ between $0$ and $\ell$, and equals $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{6}$; the computation of $I_{yy}$ is identical because the two coordinates coincide along the rod, and the product $I_{xy}$, which integrates $\lambda\,xy = \dfrac{m}{\ell} \cdot \dfrac{s^2}{2}$, gives the same value.

The moment of inertia about the line containing the rod is obtained with the unit vector $\vec{u} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,1,0)$, which is directed along the rod. The three terms cancel and one gets $I_u = 0$, in agreement with the definition: all the points of the rod have zero distance from that line, hence the moment of inertia **vanishes** and the line of the rod is a principal axis with zero principal moment.

The check verifies the result against a known value. Since the system is plane, one has $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy} = \dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$, which is the moment of inertia of a homogeneous rod about the orthogonal axis through one end, independent of the inclination of the rod in the plane. The two values coincide.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The homogeneous triangular plate in the figure has mass $m$ and occupies the right triangle with vertices $O = (0,0)$, $A = (a, 0)$ and $B = (0, b)$ of the plane $Oxy$. Write the inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$ and compute the product of inertia with respect to the central axes parallel to $x$ and $y$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 3,0 0,2" punti="1,0.667,G,baricentro" quote="0 0,3 0,a,0.5;0 2,0 0,b,0.5" frecce="-0.7,0,3.8,0,x;0,-0.7,0,2.9,y" riferimento=0 titolo="Homogeneous triangular plate with the legs on the axes, of lengths $a$ and $b$"}

$$
\sigma = \frac{2m}{ab} ,
\qquad
0 \le x \le a ,
\qquad
0 \le y \le b\left( 1 - \frac{x}{a} \right)
$$
$$
I_{xx} = \sigma \int_0^a \int_0^{b\left(1 - \frac{x}{a}\right)} y^2\,dy\,dx
= \sigma\,\frac{b^3}{3} \int_0^a \left( 1 - \frac{x}{a} \right)^3 dx
= \frac{2m}{ab} \cdot \frac{b^3}{3} \cdot \frac{a}{4}
= \frac{mb^2}{6}
$$
$$
I_{yy} = \frac{ma^2}{6} ,
\qquad
I_{xy} = \sigma \int_0^a x\,\frac{b^2}{2} \left( 1 - \frac{x}{a} \right)^2 dx = \frac{2m}{ab} \cdot \frac{b^2}{2} \cdot \frac{a^2}{12} = \frac{mab}{12}
$$
$$
\mathbb{I}_O = \frac{m}{6}
\begin{pmatrix}
b^2 & -\dfrac{ab}{2} & 0 \\[6pt]
-\dfrac{ab}{2} & a^2 & 0 \\[6pt]
0 & 0 & a^2 + b^2
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
G = \left( \frac{a}{3},\ \frac{b}{3} \right) ,
\qquad
I_{xy}^{(G)} = I_{xy} - m\,x_G\,y_G = \frac{mab}{12} - \frac{mab}{9} = -\frac{mab}{36}
$$

Check with the computation of $I_{xx}$ by integrating over horizontal strips:
$$
I_{xx} = \sigma \int_0^b y^2\,a\left( 1 - \frac{y}{b} \right) dy = \frac{2m}{ab} \cdot a \left( \frac{b^3}{3} - \frac{b^3}{4} \right) = \frac{2m}{b} \cdot \frac{b^3}{12} = \frac{mb^2}{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of the triangle is $\dfrac{ab}{2}$, hence the surface density equals $\sigma = \dfrac{2m}{ab}$. The domain is described by $0 \le x \le a$ and by $y$ between $0$ and the ordinate of the hypotenuse, which is $b\left(1 - \dfrac{x}{a}\right)$.

In the computation of $I_{xx}$ one integrates first in $y$, obtaining $\dfrac{b^3}{3}\left(1 - \dfrac{x}{a}\right)^3$, and then in $x$; the integral of $\left(1 - \dfrac{x}{a}\right)^3$ between $0$ and $a$ equals $\dfrac{a}{4}$, hence $I_{xx} = \dfrac{mb^2}{6}$. Interchanging the roles of the two axes gives $I_{yy} = \dfrac{ma^2}{6}$, and since the plate is plane one gets $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$.

In the computation of $I_{xy}$ the integration in $y$ gives $\dfrac{b^2}{2}\left(1 - \dfrac{x}{a}\right)^2$, and the integral of $x\left(1 - \dfrac{x}{a}\right)^2$ between $0$ and $a$ equals $\dfrac{a^2}{12}$; it follows that $I_{xy} = \dfrac{mab}{12}$. The product of inertia is positive, because the plate lies in the first quadrant.

The center of mass of the triangle is $\left(\dfrac{a}{3}, \dfrac{b}{3}\right)$, as proved in the theory. The transport of the products of inertia, read backwards, gives $I_{xy}^{(G)} = I_{xy} - m\,x_G y_G = -\dfrac{mab}{36}$. The value **is negative**, and this can be read off the drawing: with respect to the central axes most of the plate lies in the second and in the fourth quadrant, where the product $xy$ is negative.

The check recomputes $I_{xx}$ by integrating over horizontal strips rather than vertical ones. The strip at height $y$ has width $a\left(1 - \dfrac{y}{b}\right)$, hence the integral of $y^2$ times that width between $0$ and $b$ gives $a\left(\dfrac{b^3}{3} - \dfrac{b^3}{4}\right) = \dfrac{ab^3}{12}$, and multiplying by $\sigma$ one recovers $\dfrac{mb^2}{6}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The system in the figure consists of two equal homogeneous rigid rods, each of mass $m$ and length $\ell$, welded at a right angle: the rod $OA$ lies on the $x$ axis with $A = (\ell, 0)$, the rod $AB$ is vertical with $B = (\ell, \ell)$. Write the inertia tensor relative to the pole $O$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,2,0;B,2,2" aste="O-A;A-B" punti="1,0,G_1;2,1,G_2" quote="O,A,ℓ,0.6;A,B,ℓ,0.6" frecce="-0.7,0,3.1,0,x;0,-0.7,0,3.1,y" riferimento=0 titolo="Two equal rods welded at $A$, with the partial centers of mass $G_1$ and $G_2$"}

$$
I_{xx} = 0 + \int_0^{\ell} \frac{m}{\ell}\,y^2\,dy = \frac{m\ell^2}{3}
$$
$$
I_{yy} = \int_0^{\ell} \frac{m}{\ell}\,x^2\,dx + m\ell^2 = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} + m\ell^2 = \frac{4\,m\ell^2}{3}
$$
$$
I_{xy} = 0 + \int_0^{\ell} \frac{m}{\ell}\,\ell\,y\,dy = m\,\frac{\ell^2}{2} = \frac{m\ell^2}{2}
$$
$$
\mathbb{I}_O = \frac{m\ell^2}{6}
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & -3 & 0 \\
-3 & 8 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 10
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Check of $I_{zz}$ with the Huygens-Steiner theorem applied to the two rods:
$$
I_{zz} = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} + \left( \frac{m\ell^2}{12} + m \left( \ell^2 + \frac{\ell^2}{4} \right) \right) = \frac{m\ell^2}{3} + \frac{4\,m\ell^2}{3} = \frac{5\,m\ell^2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The contributions of the two rods add up, because the moment of inertia and the products of inertia are additive; the pole and the frame are the same for both.

The rod $OA$ has all its points with $y = 0$, hence it contributes neither to $I_{xx}$ nor to $I_{xy}$; its contribution to $I_{yy}$ is the integral of $\dfrac{m}{\ell}x^2$ between $0$ and $\ell$, that is $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$.

The rod $AB$ has all its points with $x = \ell$ and with $y$ varying between $0$ and $\ell$. Its contribution to $I_{xx}$ is the integral of $\dfrac{m}{\ell}y^2$, that is $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$; the contribution to $I_{yy}$ is $\dfrac{m}{\ell}\ell^2 \cdot \ell = m\ell^2$, because the distance from the $y$ axis is constant and equal to $\ell$; the contribution to $I_{xy}$ is the integral of $\dfrac{m}{\ell}\,\ell\,y$, that is $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{2}$.

Collecting the values with common denominator $6$ one obtains the matrix of the statement, in which the products appear with their sign changed. The entry $I_{zz}$ equals $\dfrac{5m\ell^2}{3}$, as the relation $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$ valid for plane systems requires.

The check recomputes $I_{zz}$ by another route, with the Huygens-Steiner theorem applied separately to the two rods. For the rod $OA$ the axis through $O$ passes through one end, hence the contribution is $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{3}$. For the rod $AB$ one starts from the central value $\dfrac{m\ell^2}{12}$ and transports from its center of mass $G_2 = \left(\ell, \dfrac{\ell}{2}\right)$ to the point $O$, which is at distance $\sqrt{\ell^2 + \dfrac{\ell^2}{4}}$ from it; the transport term therefore equals $m\left(\ell^2 + \dfrac{\ell^2}{4}\right)$ and the overall contribution is $\dfrac{4m\ell^2}{3}$. The sum gives $\dfrac{5m\ell^2}{3}$, the same value.

</div>
