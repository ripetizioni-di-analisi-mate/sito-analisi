## Exercise 1

The homogeneous plate in the figure is the disk with center $O$ and radius $R$ with the disk of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ removed, whose center $C$ lies on the $x$ axis at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from $O$. The mass of the plate is $m$. Compute the moment of inertia about the axis through $O$ orthogonal to the plane, the position of the center of mass and the moment of inertia about the central axis parallel to the first one.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,2.4;1.2,0,1.2,foro" punti="0,0,O;1.2,0,C;-0.4,0,G,baricentro" quote="0 0,2.4 0,R,-2.3" frecce="-3.1,0,3.2,0,x;0,-3,0,3.1,y" riferimento=0 titolo="Disk of radius $R$ with the disk of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ removed, whose center $C$ is at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from $O$"}

$$
\sigma = \frac{m}{\pi R^2 - \pi \dfrac{R^2}{4}} = \frac{4m}{3\pi R^2} ,
\qquad
m_1 = \sigma\,\pi R^2 = \frac{4m}{3} ,
\qquad
m_2 = \sigma\,\pi \frac{R^2}{4} = \frac{m}{3}
$$
$$
I_O = \frac{1}{2}\,m_1 R^2 - \left( \frac{1}{2}\,m_2 \frac{R^2}{4} + m_2 \frac{R^2}{4} \right)
= \frac{2\,mR^2}{3} - \frac{mR^2}{8}
= \frac{13\,mR^2}{24}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{m_1 \cdot 0 - m_2 \cdot \dfrac{R}{2}}{m} = -\frac{R}{6} ,
\qquad
y_G = 0
$$
$$
I_G = I_O - m\,x_G^2 = \frac{13\,mR^2}{24} - \frac{mR^2}{36} = \frac{37\,mR^2}{72}
$$

Check with the relation $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$ with respect to the pole $O$:
$$
I_{xx} = \frac{m_1 R^2}{4} - \frac{m_2 R^2}{16} = \frac{mR^2}{3} - \frac{mR^2}{48} = \frac{5\,mR^2}{16}
$$
$$
I_{yy} = \frac{m_1 R^2}{4} - \left( \frac{m_2 R^2}{16} + m_2 \frac{R^2}{4} \right) = \frac{mR^2}{3} - \frac{5\,mR^2}{48} = \frac{11\,mR^2}{48}
$$
$$
I_{xx} + I_{yy} = \frac{15\,mR^2}{48} + \frac{11\,mR^2}{48} = \frac{13\,mR^2}{24}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The body is the difference between the solid disk of radius $R$ and the disk of radius $\dfrac{R}{2}$ that occupies the hole, both with the density $\sigma$ of the given plate. The first step is therefore the computation of $\sigma$ from the mass $m$ and from the area actually occupied, which is $\pi R^2 - \pi \dfrac{R^2}{4} = \dfrac{3\pi R^2}{4}$. From $\sigma$ one obtains the fictitious masses $m_1 = \dfrac{4m}{3}$ of the solid disk and $m_2 = \dfrac{m}{3}$ of the missing disk, whose difference is $m$.

The solid disk contributes $\dfrac{1}{2}m_1R^2$, because the axis passes through its center. The missing disk must instead be transported from its own center $C$ to the point $O$, which is at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$, hence its contribution is $\dfrac{1}{2}m_2\dfrac{R^2}{4} + m_2\dfrac{R^2}{4}$ and it is subtracted. One gets $I_O = \dfrac{13mR^2}{24}$.

The center of mass is found with the same difference applied to the moments of the masses. Since the hole lies on the side of the positive $x$, the center of mass shifts to the opposite side and one gets $x_G = -\dfrac{R}{6}$; the ordinate vanishes because the $x$ axis is an axis of material symmetry.

The central moment of inertia is obtained from the Huygens-Steiner theorem read backwards: since $I_O = I_G + m\,x_G^2$, one gets $I_G = I_O - m\dfrac{R^2}{36} = \dfrac{37mR^2}{72}$, which **is smaller than $I_O$**, as the theorem requires.

The check computes separately the moments of inertia about the two axes $x$ and $y$ of the plane, again as the difference between solid disk and missing disk. In the computation of $I_{xx}$ the hole requires no transport, because its center lies on the $x$ axis; in the computation of $I_{yy}$ the transport equals $m_2\dfrac{R^2}{4}$. The sum of the two values coincides with $I_O$, as the relation valid for plane systems requires.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The homogeneous plate in the figure is the half-disk of mass $m$, center $O$ and radius $R$, contained in the half-plane $y \ge 0$. Compute the moment of inertia about the $x$ axis, which contains the diameter, about the axis through $O$ orthogonal to the plate and about the central axis parallel to the diameter.

::struttura{poligoni="2.400,0.000 2.385,0.269 2.340,0.534 2.265,0.793 2.162,1.041 2.032,1.277 1.876,1.496 1.697,1.697 1.496,1.876 1.277,2.032 1.041,2.162 0.793,2.265 0.534,2.340 0.269,2.385 0.000,2.400 -0.269,2.385 -0.534,2.340 -0.793,2.265 -1.041,2.162 -1.277,2.032 -1.496,1.876 -1.697,1.697 -1.876,1.496 -2.032,1.277 -2.162,1.041 -2.265,0.793 -2.340,0.534 -2.385,0.269 -2.400,0.000" punti="0,1.019,G,baricentro;0,0,O" tratteggi="-2.9,1.019 2.9,1.019" quote="0 0,2.4 0,R,0.55" frecce="-3.1,0,3.2,0,x;0,-0.7,0,3,y" riferimento=0 titolo="Homogeneous half-disk with the center of mass at distance $\dfrac{4R}{3\pi}$ from the diameter"}

$$
\sigma = \frac{2m}{\pi R^2}
$$
$$
I_{xx} = \int_0^{\pi} \int_0^R \sigma\,(r\sin\vartheta)^2\,r\,dr\,d\vartheta
= \sigma\,\frac{R^4}{4} \int_0^{\pi} \sin^2\vartheta\,d\vartheta
= \frac{2m}{\pi R^2} \cdot \frac{R^4}{4} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2}
= \frac{mR^2}{4}
$$
$$
I_{zz}^{(O)} = \int_0^{\pi} \int_0^R \sigma\,r^2 \cdot r\,dr\,d\vartheta = \frac{2m}{\pi R^2} \cdot \frac{R^4}{4} \cdot \pi = \frac{mR^2}{2}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{4R}{3\pi} ,
\qquad
I_{G} = I_{xx} - m\,y_G^2 = \frac{mR^2}{4} - \frac{16\,mR^2}{9\pi^2} = \frac{(9\pi^2 - 64)\,mR^2}{36\pi^2} \approx 0.0699\,mR^2
$$

Check with the relation $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$ with respect to the pole $O$:
$$
I_{yy} = \frac{mR^2}{4} ,
\qquad
I_{xx} + I_{yy} = \frac{mR^2}{4} + \frac{mR^2}{4} = \frac{mR^2}{2} = I_{zz}^{(O)}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Polar coordinates with the pole at $O$ are used, because the half-disk is described by $0 \le r \le R$ and $0 \le \vartheta \le \pi$. The surface density is obtained from the area $\dfrac{\pi R^2}{2}$ and equals $\sigma = \dfrac{2m}{\pi R^2}$.

In the moment of inertia about the $x$ axis the distance of a point from the axis is $|y| = r\sin\vartheta$. The integral separates into the product of the integral of $r^3$, which equals $\dfrac{R^4}{4}$, and the integral of $\sin^2\vartheta$ between $0$ and $\pi$, which equals $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$; it follows that $I_{xx} = \dfrac{mR^2}{4}$, the same value as for the whole disk of equal mass and radius.

For the axis orthogonal to the plate the distance of a point from $O$ is $r$, hence the angular integral equals $\pi$ and one gets $I_{zz}^{(O)} = \dfrac{mR^2}{2}$.

The central moment is obtained from the Huygens-Steiner theorem applied backwards, because the $x$ axis and the parallel central axis are at distance $y_G = \dfrac{4R}{3\pi}$. Subtracting the transport term $m\,y_G^2 = \dfrac{16mR^2}{9\pi^2}$ one gets $\dfrac{(9\pi^2 - 64)\,mR^2}{36\pi^2}$, about $0.0699\,mR^2$, a value **much smaller** than $\dfrac{mR^2}{4}$: with respect to the diameter the half-disk has almost all its mass far from the axis, whereas with respect to the central axis the mass is distributed around the axis itself.

The check uses the relation between the three moments of a plane system. The $y$ axis is an axis of material symmetry of the half-disk, and a computation identical to that of $I_{xx}$, with $\cos^2\vartheta$ in place of $\sin^2\vartheta$, gives $I_{yy} = \dfrac{mR^2}{4}$. The sum of the two equals $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$, that is the value already found for $I_{zz}^{(O)}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The homogeneous plate in the figure is the disk with center $O$ and radius $R$ with two equal disks of radius $\dfrac{R}{3}$ removed, whose centers $C_1$ and $C_2$ lie on the $x$ axis at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$ from $O$, one on each side. The mass of the plate is $m$. Compute the moment of inertia about the axis through $O$ orthogonal to the plane.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,3;1.5,0,1,foro;-1.5,0,1,foro" punti="0,0,O;1.5,0,C_1;-1.5,0,C_2" quote="0 0,3 0,R,-2.9;0 0,1.5 0,d,1.4" frecce="-3.9,0,4,0,x;0,-3.7,0,3.8,y" riferimento=0 titolo="Disk with two symmetric holes of radius $\dfrac{R}{3}$, whose centers are at distance $d = \dfrac{R}{2}$ from $O$"}

$$
\sigma = \frac{m}{\pi R^2 - 2\pi \dfrac{R^2}{9}} = \frac{9m}{7\pi R^2} ,
\qquad
m_1 = \sigma\,\pi R^2 = \frac{9m}{7} ,
\qquad
m_2 = \sigma\,\pi \frac{R^2}{9} = \frac{m}{7}
$$
$$
I_O = \frac{1}{2}\,m_1 R^2 - 2 \left( \frac{1}{2}\,m_2 \frac{R^2}{9} + m_2 \frac{R^2}{4} \right)
= \frac{9\,mR^2}{14} - \frac{11\,mR^2}{126}
= \frac{5\,mR^2}{9}
$$

Check with the relation $I_{zz} = I_{xx} + I_{yy}$ with respect to the pole $O$:
$$
I_{xx} = \frac{m_1R^2}{4} - 2\,\frac{m_2R^2}{36} = \frac{9\,mR^2}{28} - \frac{mR^2}{126} = \frac{79\,mR^2}{252}
$$
$$
I_{yy} = \frac{m_1R^2}{4} - 2 \left( \frac{m_2R^2}{36} + m_2\frac{R^2}{4} \right) = \frac{9\,mR^2}{28} - \frac{5\,mR^2}{63} = \frac{61\,mR^2}{252}
$$
$$
I_{xx} + I_{yy} = \frac{140\,mR^2}{252} = \frac{5\,mR^2}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area occupied by the plate is $\pi R^2 - 2\pi\dfrac{R^2}{9} = \dfrac{7\pi R^2}{9}$, hence the density equals $\sigma = \dfrac{9m}{7\pi R^2}$ and the fictitious masses are $m_1 = \dfrac{9m}{7}$ for the solid disk and $m_2 = \dfrac{m}{7}$ for each hole. The difference $m_1 - 2m_2$ gives back $m$, as it must be.

The solid disk contributes $\dfrac{1}{2}m_1R^2$. Each hole must be transported from its own center to $O$, which is at distance $\dfrac{R}{2}$, and therefore contributes $\dfrac{1}{2}m_2\dfrac{R^2}{9} + m_2\dfrac{R^2}{4}$; since the two holes are equal and lie at the same distance from $O$, their overall contribution is twice this and it is subtracted. Carrying out the computations with common denominator $126$ one gets $\dfrac{81\,mR^2}{126} - \dfrac{11\,mR^2}{126} = \dfrac{5mR^2}{9}$.

The result exceeds $\dfrac{mR^2}{2}$, that is the moment of inertia of a solid disk of equal mass and radius. The plate with the holes has indeed the same mass $m$ distributed farther from the axis, because the matter removed lay in the region near the center.

The check computes $I_{xx}$ and $I_{yy}$ with the same difference. The centers of the holes lie on the $x$ axis, hence in the computation of $I_{xx}$ there is no transport, whereas in that of $I_{yy}$ each hole brings the term $m_2\dfrac{R^2}{4}$. The sum of the two moments equals $\dfrac{5mR^2}{9}$, that is the value already found for the orthogonal axis.

</div>
