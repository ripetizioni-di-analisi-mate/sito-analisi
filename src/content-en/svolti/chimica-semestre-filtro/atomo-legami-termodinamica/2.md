## Exercise 1

Neutral nitrogen and oxygen have respectively $Z=7$ and $Z=8$. Write their ground-state configurations and compare the number of unpaired electrons in the $2p$ subshell.

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm N:&\quad 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^3,\\[4pt]\mathrm O:&\quad 1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4.\end{aligned}$$

![Three 2p orbitals: three separate electrons in nitrogen and one pair plus two unpaired electrons in oxygen](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/atomo-legami-termodinamica/orbitali-en.svg)

N has **three unpaired electrons** and O has **two**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The 1s and 2s orbitals contain four electrons in total. Three electrons therefore remain for nitrogen’s 2p subshell and four for oxygen’s. The three p orbitals have equal energy in the isolated atom: Hund’s rule requires separate occupancy before pairing. Oxygen’s fourth electron pairs with one of the others with opposite spin, as required by Pauli. Adding an electron therefore reduces the unpaired count in this case. These counts concern isolated atoms; molecular electronic structure also requires a bonding model.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The ions $\mathrm{Na^+}$ and $\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$ have atomic numbers 11 and 12 respectively. Derive their configurations and predict the smaller ionic radius, comparing ions under similar structural conditions.

$$N_e(\mathrm{Na^+})=11-1=10,\qquad N_e(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}})=12-2=10.$$

Both have configuration $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6=[\mathrm{Ne}]$. In this isoelectronic series, **Mg²⁺ has the smaller radius**.

<div class="spiegazione">

The charges specify the electrons lost, so the ions have equal electron counts and the same occupied subshells. Magnesium’s nucleus nevertheless has one more proton. With equal electron numbers, greater nuclear attraction contracts the electron cloud more strongly. The comparison cannot be obtained simply by reading neutral-atom radii from the periodic table: the species being compared are ions. Assuming similar structural conditions avoids treating radius as a universal constant, since crystal ionic radii also depend on coordination.

</div>
