## Exercise 1

Build calcium phosphate and ammonium sulfate formulas from $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$, $\mathrm{PO_4^{3-}}$, $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$ and $\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$. Use the smallest whole-number ratios and check total charge.

For calcium and phosphate, the least common multiple of 2 and 3 is 6:

$$3(+2)+2(-3)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathrm{Ca_3(PO_4)_2}.$$

For ammonium and sulfate, two cations balance one anion:

$$2(+1)+1(-2)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathrm{(NH_4)_2SO_4}.$$

Parentheses preserve the identities of the **polyatomic ions**.

<div class="spiegazione">

A neutral salt formula must balance its constituents’ charges. In calcium phosphate, one calcium ion and one phosphate ion would leave a negative charge, so a one-to-one ratio is unsuitable. Three calcium ions and two phosphate ions instead give equal positive and negative charge magnitudes. In the second salt, each sulfate requires two ammonium ions. Subscripts multiply the entire bracketed group without changing its internal subscripts: phosphate retains four oxygen atoms per group and ammonium four hydrogen atoms. Zero total charge provides the final check for both formulas.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Assign oxygen’s oxidation state in $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ and iron’s in $\mathrm{Fe_2O_3}$, and name both compounds. Use $+1$ for H; O is $-2$ in iron oxide.

In the peroxide, let $x$ be oxygen’s oxidation state:

$$2(+1)+2x=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=-1.$$

In the oxide, let $y$ be iron’s oxidation state:

$$2y+3(-2)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad y=+3.$$

The names are **hydrogen peroxide** and **iron(III) oxide**.

![H-O-O-H structure with two lone pairs per oxygen, compared with the ammonium ion](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/atomo-legami-termodinamica/lewis-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The usual oxygen oxidation state of minus two has an exception in peroxides, identifiable by their O-O group. In H-O-O-H, each oxygen is assigned the electrons of its bond to H, whereas O-O electrons are divided between identical atoms, giving minus one. In iron oxide, the three oxygens contribute minus six in total, balanced by two iron atoms at plus three. The Roman numeral in the Stock name specifies that oxidation state; the formula’s subscript 2 instead counts iron atoms. Oxidation state and stoichiometric subscript therefore have different roles.

</div>
