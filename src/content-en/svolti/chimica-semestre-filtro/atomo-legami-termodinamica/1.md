## Exercise 1

Determine protons, neutrons and electrons in ${}^{24}_{12}\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}$. Compare it with neutral ${}^{25}_{12}\mathrm{Mg}$ and identify the differences involving the nucleus.

![Constituent counts for two magnesium isotopes and the positive ion](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/atomo-legami-termodinamica/isotopi-en.svg)

$$\begin{aligned}{}^{24}\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}:&\quad N_p=12,\quad N_n=24-12=12,\quad N_e=12-2=10;\\[6pt]{}^{25}\mathrm{Mg}:&\quad N_p=12,\quad N_n=25-12=13,\quad N_e=12.\end{aligned}$$

The second nucleus has **one extra neutron**. The two-electron difference instead reflects charge.

<div class="spiegazione">

Atomic number identifies the element through its protons and is unchanged between the cases. Mass number counts only nucleons, so neutrons are obtained by subtracting protons. The ion’s positive charge indicates fewer electrons than in the neutral atom: removing electrons does not change mass number. As a check, the ion’s twelve positive nuclear charges and ten negative electron charges give a net charge of two positive elementary charges. The figure separates isotope change from ionisation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A hypothetical element has only two isotopes, of mass $20.0\,\mathrm u$ and $22.0\,\mathrm u$. The sample’s mean mass is $21.2\,\mathrm u$. Determine the fraction and percentage of the heavier isotope.

Let $x$ be the heavier fraction; the lighter fraction is $1-x$.

$$\begin{aligned}20.0(1-x)+22.0x&=21.2,\\[4pt]20.0+2.0x&=21.2,\\[4pt]x&=0.60.\end{aligned}$$

The heavier isotope accounts for **60%** of atoms and the lighter isotope for 40%.

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean is weighted because each mass contributes in proportion to the relative number of atoms of that isotope. The fractions sum to one, leaving only one unknown. The result lies between zero and one, as an abundance fraction must. The mean mass is also closer to 22 than to 20, consistent with the heavier isotope predominating. Numerical checking gives 8.0 atomic mass units from the lighter contribution and 13.2 from the heavier one, totalling 21.2. The mean is not the mass number of an individual nucleus.

</div>
