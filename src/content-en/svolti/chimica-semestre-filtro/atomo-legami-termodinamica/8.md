## Exercise 1

For ideal nitrogen at $300\,\mathrm K$, calculate $v_{\mathrm{rms}}$ using $M=28.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ and $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$. Then determine how it changes at $600\,\mathrm K$ for the same gas.

Molar mass in SI units is $0.0280\,\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$.

$$v_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3\cdot8.314\cdot300}{0.0280}}\simeq517\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.$$

At twice the temperature,

$$\dfrac{v_{\mathrm{rms},2}}{v_{\mathrm{rms},1}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{600}{300}}=\sqrt2,$$

so the value is approximately **731 m/s**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Conversion to kilograms is required because the joule contains the kilogram in its definition. Leaving 28 in the denominator would incorrectly reduce speed by the square root of one thousand. After conversion, the expression under the square root has units of speed squared. Temperature enters through a square root: doubling T doubles mean translational energy but increases root-mean-square speed only by the square root of two. This is a statistical speed and does not mean that every molecule moves at the same speed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The curves show speed distributions of the same ideal gas at $300\,\mathrm K$ and $600\,\mathrm K$. Explain why the hotter curve has a lower peak yet indicates a larger molecular fraction above $800\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

![Normalised Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions of nitrogen at 300 K and 600 K](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/atomo-legami-termodinamica/maxwell-en.svg)

Each curve has total area one. At higher temperature, the distribution is broader and shifted towards higher characteristic speeds. **The required fraction is the area to the right of 800 m/s**, which is larger at 600 K.

<div class="spiegazione">

Height at one point represents a probability density, whereas a molecular fraction requires area over an interval. The lower peak does not indicate fewer molecules overall: both distributions are normalised to the same total. Heating increases the probability of high speeds while retaining a range of possible speeds. The curves therefore overlap, and one cannot conclude that every molecule in the hotter sample is faster than every molecule in the colder sample. The conclusion about the 800 m/s threshold concerns the model distributions shown.

</div>
