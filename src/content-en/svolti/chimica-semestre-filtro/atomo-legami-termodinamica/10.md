## Exercise 1

A closed system absorbs $550\,\mathrm J$ and expands by $2.00\,\mathrm L$ against a constant external pressure of $1.00\,\mathrm{bar}$. Expansion is the only work. Neglect macroscopic kinetic and potential energies and calculate $w$ and $\Delta U$, taking received work as positive.

Use $1\,\mathrm{L\,bar}=100\,\mathrm J$.

$$w=-p_{\mathrm{ext}}\Delta V=-1.00\cdot2.00\cdot100=-200\,\mathrm J.$$

The first law gives

$$\Delta U=q+w=550-200=+350\,\mathrm J.$$

The system **increases its internal energy**.

<div class="spiegazione">

Heat enters the system and has positive sign. During expansion, the system performs work on its surroundings, so received work is negative. Of the 550 joules absorbed, 200 are transferred as work and 350 remain as an internal-energy increase. The litre-bar conversion requires both factors: one bar is one hundred thousand pascals and one litre is one thousandth of a cubic metre, giving one hundred joules. This is a closed-system balance; matter flow would require additional energy terms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At the composition considered, process A has $\Delta H_A=20.0\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ and $\Delta S_A=100\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$. At $300\,\mathrm K$, it is mechanistically coupled 1:1 to process B, with $\Delta G_B=+6.00\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Calculate $\Delta G_A$ and the coupled balance, explaining why the link between processes matters.

Converting entropy to kilojoules,

$$\Delta G_A=20.0-300\cdot0.100=-10.0\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.$$

For coupled progress at the specified ratio,

$$\Delta G_{\mathrm{tot}}=-10.0+6.00=-4.0\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.$$

The overall transformation is **exergonic**. The following curve assumes that $\Delta H_A$ and $\Delta S_A$ remain constant across the plotted temperature range.

![Temperature dependence of Gibbs energy for process A under the stated assumptions](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/atomo-legami-termodinamica/gibbs-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Entropy must be converted before subtraction because enthalpy is in kilojoules. Process A is endothermic, but at 300 K its entropy term makes it exergonic. Adding Gibbs changes is appropriate because the mechanism couples each progression of A to one of B. Simply placing both processes in the same vessel would not guarantee this transfer of Gibbs energy. The negative balance indicates a thermodynamically favoured direction at the specified composition; rate requires kinetic information. In an open biological system, inflows and outflows can maintain compositions away from equilibrium.

</div>
