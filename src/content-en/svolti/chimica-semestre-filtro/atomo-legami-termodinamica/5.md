## Exercise 1

Compare ethene, $\mathrm{H_2C=CH_2}$, with ethyne, $\mathrm{HC\equiv CH}$. Count sigma and pi bonds in each entire molecule and assign carbon hybridisation in the localised model.

In ethene, four C-H bonds plus the sigma component of C=C give **five σ and one π**. Each carbon has three domains and is described as $sp^2$.

In ethyne, two C-H bonds plus the sigma component of C≡C give **three σ and two π**. Each carbon has two domains and is described as $sp$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each directly bonded atom pair contributes one sigma component. Additional components of a multiple bond are pi bonds: one in a double and two in a triple bond. The count includes bonds to hydrogen, which would be missed by considering only carbon–carbon bonding. For hybridisation, however, a multiple bond counts as one electron domain; a triple bond therefore does not imply three bond directions around carbon. The different sideways overlap also explains the barrier to rotation around a double bond.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the gas-phase reaction $\mathrm{H_2+Cl_2\rightarrow2HCl}$, use mean bond energies $D_{\mathrm{H-H}}=436$, $D_{\mathrm{Cl-Cl}}=243$ and $D_{\mathrm{H-Cl}}=431$, all in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Estimate enthalpy per mole of reaction as written.

One H-H and one Cl-Cl bond are broken; two H-Cl bonds are formed.

$$\begin{aligned}\Delta H&\simeq\sum D_b-\sum D_f,\\[5pt]&=(436+243)-2\cdot431,\\[5pt]&=-183\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.\end{aligned}$$

The estimate is **exothermic**. Here $b$ and $f$ indicate broken and formed bonds, respectively.

![Bond-energy minimum and positive cost of separating the atoms](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/atomo-legami-termodinamica/energia-legame-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Breaking the initial bonds requires 679 kJ per mole of reaction. Forming two H-Cl bonds releases 862 kJ, so the system’s net change is negative. The coefficient 2 before HCl also multiplies the formed-bond energy. The figure shows why dissociation requires energy: atoms move from the bonded minimum to the separated reference. Mean bond energies give an estimate and concern gaseous species; phase changes would require additional contributions. The enthalpy sign alone determines neither reaction rate nor the Gibbs energy sign.

</div>
