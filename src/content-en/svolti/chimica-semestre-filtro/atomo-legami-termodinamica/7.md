## Exercise 1

A fixed amount of ideal gas changes from $p_1=100\,\mathrm{kPa}$, $V_1=2.00\,\mathrm L$ and $T_1=300\,\mathrm K$ to $V_2=3.00\,\mathrm L$ and $T_2=330\,\mathrm K$. Derive the final absolute pressure and compare with an isothermal transformation between the same volumes.

Because $nR$ is constant,

$$\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1}=\dfrac{p_2V_2}{T_2}.$$

$$p_2=100\cdot\dfrac{2.00}{3.00}\cdot\dfrac{330}{300}\simeq73.3\,\mathrm{kPa}.$$

The isothermal comparison gives $100\cdot\dfrac{2.00}{3.00}\simeq66.7\,\mathrm{kPa}$. **Heating reduces the pressure drop.**

<div class="spiegazione">

Greater volume reduces wall-collision frequency with other conditions unchanged, while greater temperature increases mean kinetic energy. The combined equation includes both effects. Litres and kilopascals can be used in these ratios because the same unit appears in the comparison’s numerator and denominator; no numerical gas constant is needed. Temperatures are absolute. Final pressure remains below its initial value because volume increases by a factor of 1.5 while temperature increases by only 1.1.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an inspiration model, $0.500\,\mathrm L$ of dry ideal gas is added at $310\,\mathrm K$ and $1.00\,\mathrm{atm}$. Pressure and temperature remain constant, and added volume is measured under these same conditions. Use $R=0.082057\,\mathrm{L\,atm\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$ to calculate incoming moles and explain why $p_1V_1=p_2V_2$ does not hold throughout entry.

The added amount satisfies

$$\Delta n=\dfrac{p\Delta V}{RT}=\dfrac{1.00\cdot0.500}{0.082057\cdot310}\simeq0.0197\,\mathrm{mol}.$$

In this model, $pV$ increases because $n$ increases. **Boyle’s law requires a fixed amount of gas.**

<div class="spiegazione">

The quoted volume refers to the gas’s final conditions, so it can be converted directly into amount of substance at 310 K. This is an open-system exercise: molecules cross the boundary and change the total number present. The entire inspiration therefore cannot be treated as one constant-amount isotherm, even though temperature is maintained. Actual breathing also involves humidification, gas exchange and pressure variations; these are excluded here to isolate incoming matter.

</div>
