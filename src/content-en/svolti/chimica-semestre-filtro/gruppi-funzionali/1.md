## Exercise 1

Compare ethanol, propan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol and benzyl alcohol, $\mathrm{C_6H_5CH_2OH}$. Classify each alcohol and determine whether the ring in the last compound makes it a phenol. Also explain why solubility cannot be inferred solely by counting OH groups.

| Compound | Carbons bonded to the hydroxyl-bearing carbon | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ethanol | 1 | Primary |
| Propan-2-ol | 2 | Secondary |
| 2-Methylpropan-2-ol | 3 | Tertiary |
| Benzyl alcohol | 1 | Primary |

![The hydroxyl-bearing carbon is bonded to one, two or three carbons in the three alcohol classes](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/alcoli-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The observation point is the carbon directly bonded to OH. In ethanol it is CH2 with one neighboring carbon; in propan-2-ol it is CH with two methyls; in the tertiary alcohol it has three methyls and no H. Counting every carbon in the molecule would therefore give incorrect classifications.

Benzyl alcohol retains a CH2 between ring and OH. Oxygen is not directly conjugated with the ring as in phenol: proximity to an aromatic group is insufficient to change the functional group. A phenol would have structure Ar–OH.

All these alcohols can donate and accept hydrogen bonds, but their hydrocarbon portions differ. The size and shape of the nonpolar portion affect overall interactions with water. OH count is therefore useful information, but it does not alone determine miscibility or boiling point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three solutes are studied separately in buffers at $pH=10$ and 25 °C. Use the supplied values: phenol $pK_a=10$, a thiol $pK_a=9$, ethanol $pK_a=16$. Under the dilute-solution approximation, find base/acid ratios and deprotonated fractions, ignoring other reactions.

$$
r=10^{pH-pK_a},\qquad f_{\mathrm{base}}=\dfrac r{1+r}.
$$

The results are: phenol $r=1$, fraction $\dfrac12$; thiol $r=10$, fraction $\dfrac{10}{11}\approx0.909$; ethanol $r=10^{-6}$, fraction approximately $10^{-6}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The buffer fixes pH: it should not be calculated from each solute's starting concentration. Activities are approximated by concentrations, as stated. Ratio r compares two forms, whereas the deprotonated fraction uses their sum in the denominator. For the thiol, ten parts thiolate and one part thiol make eleven total parts.

The supplied values show why an SH group can provide a substantial nucleophilic fraction where an alcohol remains almost entirely neutral. They do not establish that every protein cysteine has precisely pKa 9: its environment and interactions can modify the value. Comparison with phenol connects the result to conjugate-base stabilization; for ethanol, the large pKa–pH difference strongly disfavors alkoxide in water under the stated conditions.

</div>
