## Exercise 1

Follow reduction of butan-2-one with a suitable hydride donor and subsequent protonation. Identify where the new C–H bond forms, where the intermediate charge appears, and which stereoisomers are expected assuming entirely achiral reagents, catalysts and environment and no subsequent selection.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_3}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3CH(O^-)CH_2CH_3}
\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_3}.
$$

The product is butan-2-ol; the achiral model predicts a racemic mixture of its two enantiomers.

![A planar carbonyl becomes a tetrahedral center after attack, followed by oxygen protonation](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/carbonile-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The hydride equivalent brings a pair to the carbonyl carbon. Simultaneously, the C=O pi-bond pair moves to oxygen, so carbon does not exceed its octet. The intermediate's negative charge is on O and is neutralized by final protonation.

Product carbon 2 bears H, OH, methyl and ethyl: four different groups, making it stereogenic. The planar reactant offers two enantiotopic faces. Under the achiral assumptions neither is favored, and equal amounts of both enantiomers form. The condensed structure does not show which is R and which S and cannot justify declaring only one.

An enzyme or chiral catalyst could instead discriminate between the faces. Predicting a racemate therefore depends on the stated conditions rather than the word “reduction” alone.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pure sample contains $5.80\,\mathrm g$ of propanone. Quantitative reduction gives propan-2-ol without side reactions. Use the supplied molar masses: $58.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$ and $60.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. Calculate the theoretical product mass and explain the increase without adding carbons.

$$
n=\dfrac{5.80}{58.0}=0.100\,\mathrm{mol},\qquad
m=0.100\cdot60.0=6.00\,\mathrm g.
$$

The theoretical alcohol mass is 6.00 g; the 0.20 g increase corresponds to net addition of two H per molecule within the supplied molar-mass model.

<div class="spiegazione">

Substrate/product stoichiometry is one to one because reduction changes C=O into CH–OH without fragmenting or joining carbon skeletons. Ketone moles are therefore also theoretical alcohol moles. Keeping mass unchanged merely because the three carbons are conserved would be incorrect.

One H enters the new C–H bond through reducing equivalents, and another appears on O during protonation. The added matter comes from reagents and medium: it is not created. A balance restricted to the organic substance does not include the mass of all reducing-agent byproducts.

The result is theoretical and uses the stated quantitative conversion and selectivity. A lower isolated yield would change the collected mass but not the calculated stoichiometric ratio.

</div>
