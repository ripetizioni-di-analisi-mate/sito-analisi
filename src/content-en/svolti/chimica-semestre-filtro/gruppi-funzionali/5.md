## Exercise 1

Benzaldehyde reacts with excess methanol under acid catalysis while water is removed. Write the balance for its dimethyl acetal and compare its behavior in two subsequent tests: acidic water and mild aqueous base without other reactive reagents.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5CHO}+2\,\mathrm{CH_3OH}
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{C_6H_5CH(OCH_3)_2}+\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$

Water and acid favor hydrolysis to benzaldehyde and methanol. In mild base, the acetal is relatively stable and the same rapid deprotection is not assumed.

![A hemiacetal center with OH and OR becomes an acetal center when it bears two OR groups](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/acetali-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The first alcohol addition forms a hemiacetal. Replacing OH as well requires making it a suitable leaving group by protonation; water loss and attack by the second alcohol give the center bearing two OR groups. Carbon remains bonded to H and phenyl, preserving the aldehyde skeleton.

Acid is catalytic and does not by itself determine net direction. During preparation, water removal and abundant methanol favor product; in the hydrolysis test, activities change by making water abundant. The constant at the same temperature should not be invented as a function of catalyst amount.

Under basic conditions, alkoxide departure from the acetal center is unfavorable. This different sensitivity explains using acetals as temporary protecting groups without claiming absolute stability under every possible condition.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Propanone and methylamine react under conditions suitable for imine formation. Write the neutral product, describe the intermediate before water loss, and explain why excessive acidity can be counterproductive. No reducing agent is present.

$$
\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CO}+\mathrm{CH_3NH_2}
\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{(CH_3)_2C{=}NCH_3}+\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$

Before dehydration, a carbinolamine-type intermediate forms, with OH and NHCH3 on the same carbon after appropriate proton transfers.

<div class="spiegazione">

The N pair attacks electrophilic carbon. The carbonyl temporarily becomes tetrahedral, and proton transfers allow representation of a neutral intermediate. Subsequent OH activation facilitates water elimination; C=N replaces the carbonyl bond in the product.

Methylamine is primary and its methyl group remains on N. Writing a saturated amine with a single C–N bond would require an imine reduction not included in the data. With a secondary amine and an enolizable carbonyl compound, an enamine would instead be a possible neutral product: amine substitution is therefore essential.

Moderate acid catalysis assists dehydration, but excessively low pH converts methylamine mainly into methylammonium. Its pair is no longer available for initial attack. Water must also be controlled because condensation is reversible; simply adding more acid does not replace this management of equilibrium.

</div>
