## Exercise 1

4-Hydroxybutanoic acid, $\mathrm{HOCH_2CH_2CH_2COOH}$, forms a lactone under acid catalysis and conditions favoring water loss. Count ring members and predict the organic product after complete lactone opening with aqueous base, without subsequent acidification.

The lactone has five members: three CH2 carbons, one carbonyl carbon and one oxygen. Basic opening gives $\mathrm{HOCH_2CH_2CH_2COO^-}$ with the base's counterion.

![The lactone's five-membered ring and the external position of carbonyl oxygen](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/lattone-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Count the ring by following the closed bond path, rather than every atom in the formula. Alcohol oxygen attacks the carbonyl and becomes incorporated into the ring. The oxygen of the C=O double bond instead remains outside. The hydroxy acid's name contains four carbons, but the ring also includes a heteroatom.

During basic opening, the lactone behaves as an ester. OH− attack, a tetrahedral intermediate and acyl cleavage restore an open chain with terminal hydroxyl and carboxylate. The balance preserves all four carbons; no decarboxylation is required.

The neutral acid is not the stated final form because acid work-up is absent. Reverse cyclization is an equilibrium influenced by medium: assuming that newly formed carboxylate closes quantitatively in base would be incorrect. A favorable five-membered ring does not remove dependence on conditions.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Heat $5.10\,\mathrm g$ of pure acetoacetic acid, assuming quantitative decarboxylation and complete tautomerization of the enol product. Use $M(\text{acid})=102.0$, $M(\text{propanone})=58.0$ and $M(\mathrm{CO_2})=44.0$, in $\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. Calculate final masses and compare with biological pyruvate.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2COOH}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3}+\mathrm{CO_2},
$$
$$
n=\dfrac{5.10}{102.0}=0.0500\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

The products are $2.90\,\mathrm g$ of propanone and $2.20\,\mathrm g$ of CO2.

<div class="spiegazione">

The carboxyl group loses its carbon as carbon dioxide. The remaining portion retains three carbons and initially has an enol form, which reaches the ketone through tautomerization. The ratio of acid consumed to ketone and CO2 is one to one to one: it is not divided by two merely because two products form.

Calculated total mass is 5.10 g. In an open vessel, gas can escape and condensed-phase mass can fall, but this does not represent lost matter in the complete balance. The beta-keto acid's structure permits the thermal transformation under suitable conditions.

Pyruvate is instead an alpha-keto acid. In the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, decarboxylation, oxidation and transfer to acetyl-CoA are coupled using specific cofactors. The propanone-plus-CO2 outcome cannot be applied to every compound containing carbonyl and carboxyl groups while ignoring their separation and the reacting system.

</div>
