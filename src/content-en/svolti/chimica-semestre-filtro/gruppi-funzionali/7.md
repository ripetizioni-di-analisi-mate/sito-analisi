## Exercise 1

A model system completely reduces $5.00\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of quinone Q to QH2. Write the half-reaction and calculate electron and proton equivalents. If a donor supplies two electrons per molecule, how many millimoles of donor are theoretically required? Consider only this ideal balance.

$$
\mathrm Q+2\,e^-+2\,\mathrm{H^+}\longrightarrow\mathrm{QH_2}.
$$

The requirements are $10.0\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of electrons, $10.0\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of protons in the half-reaction, and $5.00\,\mathrm{mmol}$ of the two-electron donor.

![Para-benzoquinone and hydroquinone show bond changes and net addition of two hydrogens](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/chinoni-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient two applies to each Q molecule, so it multiplies Q moles without confusing electrons with donor molecules. Two negative and two positive charges cancel in the formal balance; Q and QH2 are represented here as neutral species.

The half-reaction alone does not describe total net proton consumption by the complete system: the donor half-reaction must also be added and common terms canceled where appropriate. Nor does it establish the number of protons translocated across a membrane.

The process can pass through one-electron semiquinone states. The calculated total remains unchanged even if transfers occur in separate steps. This is reduction, not tautomerization: hydrogen content and oxidation state change along with ring-bond arrangement.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A teaching model assigns two consecutive steps, under the same transformed standard conditions, $\Delta G_1^{\circ\prime}=+5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ and $\Delta G_2^{\circ\prime}=-35\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. The first releases an enol form from a phosphorylated derivative and the second converts it to the keto form. These are hypothetical data, not tabulated PEP values. Calculate and interpret the balance.

$$
\Delta G_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\circ\prime}=+5-35=-30\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

The overall transformation is favored under the model's transformed standard conditions even though the isolated first step has a positive value.

<div class="spiegazione">

The enol intermediate appears as product of the first step and reactant of the second: adding the reactions removes it from the overall balance. Free-energy changes add because they depend on initial and final states. Values referring to different temperatures or conventions cannot instead be added arbitrarily.

The conceptual connection to phosphoenolpyruvate is product stabilization following phosphoryl-group removal or transfer. The cost of breaking one bond does not describe the complete transformation, which includes new interactions and tautomerization.

A negative standard value determines neither rate nor the sign of ΔG at every concentration. The actual state also requires the reaction quotient. Enzymes can facilitate the pathway but cannot create a different equilibrium value under unchanged conditions.

</div>
