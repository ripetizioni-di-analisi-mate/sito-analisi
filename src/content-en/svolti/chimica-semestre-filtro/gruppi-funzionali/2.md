## Exercise 1

Butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol are to be converted separately into carbonyl compounds while preserving C–C bonds. Anhydrous PCC conditions that stop a primary alcohol at its aldehyde are available. Predict the products and explain why 2-methylpropan-2-ol does not undergo the same type of transformation.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CH_2OH}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CH_2CHO},
$$
$$
\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_3}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_3}.
$$

The products are butanal and butan-2-one. No carbonyl from this mild oxidation with an intact skeleton is assigned to the tertiary alcohol.

<div class="spiegazione">

The carbon originally bonded to OH remains the carbonyl carbon. In the primary alcohol it is terminal and retains an H in the product, identifying an aldehyde. In the secondary alcohol it retains two bonds to carbons, identifying a ketone. Moving C=O along the chain would invent a different transformation.

Oxidation also requires removal of the H bonded to the hydroxyl-bearing carbon. In 2-methylpropan-2-ol that carbon already has three methyl groups and no H. A carbonyl retaining the same three carbon substituents would violate carbon valence: skeletal changes would be needed.

Finally, the aldehyde must not automatically be replaced with a carboxylic acid. The stated conditions are part of the data. In aqueous medium with an oxidant allowing further reaction, butanal could be oxidized further, but that is not the selected case.

</div>

## Exercise 2

1,2-Epoxypropane is treated with methoxide in methanol and the alkoxide product is protonated. Draw the constitutional product and identify which oxygen becomes OH. Do not assign an absolute configuration because the substrate's configuration is unspecified.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3OCH_2CH(OH)CH_3}
$$

Methoxy bonds to the terminal carbon; the original ring oxygen becomes OH on the middle carbon.

![Basic epoxide opening by methoxide with retention of the ring oxygen in the OH group](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/epossido-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Distinguish the ring carbons: one is CH2, while the other also bears CH3. The strong nucleophile attacks the less hindered site through an SN2 pathway and breaks only the bond between that carbon and oxygen. The other ring C–O bond survives, so the initial product contains an alkoxide on the middle carbon.

Protonation does not move oxygen; it converts O− to OH. The product contains two oxygens: one introduced with methoxide and one from the epoxide. Proposing a simple ether containing only one oxygen would lose an atom without a step accounting for its departure.

Attack is from the back. If the attacked carbon were stereogenic, local inversion would have to be tracked; here the attacked site is CH2. Without a specified starting configuration, a single product enantiomer cannot be invented.

</div>
