## Exercise 1

An esterification model A + B ⇌ E + W treats acid, alcohol, ester and water as ideal solutes in an inert solvent at constant volume and temperature. Initially there are 1.00 mol A and 1.00 mol B, with no E or W. The dimensionless constant, with activities proportional to concentrations and standard factors canceling, is $K=4$. Calculate equilibrium extent.

Let x be the numerical value of converted moles:

$$
K=\dfrac{x^2}{(1-x)^2}=4,\qquad
\dfrac{x}{1-x}=2,\qquad x=\dfrac23.
$$

At equilibrium: 0.667 mol each of ester and water, and 0.333 mol of each reactant.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each converted mole consumes one mole of acid and one of alcohol, generating one mole of each product. All concentrations share the same volume; since total coefficients are equal on both sides, the volume factor cancels from the ratio. Water is a variable solute here, not an abundant solvent with unit activity.

Choose the positive root compatible with $0\le x\le1$. Conversion is not complete: both directions remain possible. With one initial mole of water instead, $K=\dfrac{x(1+x)}{(1-x)^2}$ and the physical root would be approximately 0.543 mol, a smaller value.

Catalytic acid speeds attainment of equilibrium without changing the supplied K. The case illustrates water's effect without treating an ideal model as a universal quantitative prediction for real concentrated mixtures.

</div>

## Exercise 2

$0.200\,\mathrm{mol}$ of ethyl ethanoate ideally undergoes Claisen self-condensation using sufficient ethoxide and subsequent acid work-up. Identify the beta-keto ester and maximum theoretical amount, explaining base's role and the difference from an aldol reaction.

$$
2\,\mathrm{CH_3COOCH_2CH_3}
\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2COOCH_2CH_3}+\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}.
$$

The neutral product is ethyl 3-oxobutanoate, also called ethyl acetoacetate. The maximum amount is $0.100\,\mathrm{mol}$.

![Beta-keto ester formation using ethoxide followed by acid work-up](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/claisen-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

One molecule's enolate uses its alpha carbon to attack another molecule's carbonyl. The tetrahedral intermediate can expel ethoxide and restore the carbonyl, forming a beta-keto ester rather than the beta-hydroxy carbonyl of aldol addition alone. Two esters are required per product, explaining the halving of moles.

Base then removes a proton between the two carbonyls, forming a particularly stabilized anion and shifting equilibrium. This classical pathway requires at least two alpha H on the donor, and base is not merely catalytic. Final acid work-up protonates the product and allows writing the neutral form shown.

Ethoxide matches the ester's ethoxy group, so any exchange leaves its identity unchanged. A different alkoxide could instead introduce transesterification products. The displayed balance summarizes organic products after final treatment and does not list salts from work-up reagents.

</div>
