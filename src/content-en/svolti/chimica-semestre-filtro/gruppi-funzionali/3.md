## Exercise 1

A buffer at 25 °C maintains $pH=8.0$. For an amine B, $pK_a(\mathrm{BH^+})=9.5$ and $[\mathrm B]+[\mathrm{BH^+}]=0.0200\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Approximate activities by concentrations and calculate both concentrations, assuming no other forms and no change in buffered pH upon adding the solute.

$$
r=10^{8.0-9.5}=0.03162,
$$
$$
[\mathrm B]=0.0200\dfrac r{1+r}\approx0.000613\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}},
$$
$$
[\mathrm{BH^+}]\approx0.01939\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}.
$$

The protonated fraction is approximately 96.9%.

<div class="spiegazione">

The stated pKa belongs to conjugate acid BH+, rather than deprotonation of an arbitrary H bonded to the base. The B/BH+ ratio is small because pH is below pKa. Converting the ratio into concentrations requires imposing the total balance rather than simply multiplying total concentration by r.

The results sum to 0.0200 mol/L within rounding. This check verifies that molecules have not been counted twice and that neither form has been omitted. The neutral species remains present despite being a minority and can contribute to nucleophilic reactivity.

Lowering pH further would reduce its fraction. However, an absolute reaction rate cannot be inferred from equilibrium alone: electrophile concentration and kinetic constants would be required. The solution keeps acid-base distribution separate from a complete rate comparison.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compare ethylamine, aniline and ethanamide in terms of N-pair availability for protonation. Then explain why choline cannot be neutralized simply by removing a proton from nitrogen despite containing an OH group.

Ethylamine: a pair not directly conjugated with a pi system. Aniline: interaction with the aromatic ring. Ethanamide: strong delocalization with the carbonyl. Under usual aqueous conditions, the pair is far less available in the amide than in the aliphatic amine.

![An amine lone pair and amide resonance contributors with explicit formal charges](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/gruppi-funzionali/azoto-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

A delocalized pair still exists but contributes to stabilizing the starting species. Binding it to a proton changes that balance, so the formal presence of a pair does not ensure identical basicity. An amide also has partial C–N double-bond character. Lower basicity at N does not mean that the amide cannot be protonated: carbonyl O is generally favored in acid. The qualitative comparison must refer to the same solvent: it does not replace thermodynamic data for every possible substituent.

Choline has N bonded to three methyls and a hydroxyethyl group. All four bonds are to carbons, and its quaternary-ammonium positive charge does not arise from adding an H to N. There is therefore no N–H to deprotonate into an ordinary neutral amine. Any OH deprotonation concerns another site and does not turn N into a trivalent nitrogen.

</div>
