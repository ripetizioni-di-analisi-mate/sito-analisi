## Exercise 1

Compare benzene, pyrimidine and cyclooctatetraene in their ordinary geometries. For each, determine whether pi-electron counting and geometry support aromaticity. Clarify the role of pyrimidine nitrogen lone pairs.

Benzene and pyrimidine have planar continuously conjugated rings with six pi electrons and are aromatic. Pyrimidine’s pyridine-like lone pairs do not add another two pairs to the pi system. Cyclooctatetraene has eight pi electrons but adopts nonplanar geometry and is **nonaromatic** in its ordinary structure.

![Benzene and pyrimidine with three pi-electron pairs in their rings](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/carbonio-idrocarburi-stereochimica/aromatici-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Checking proceeds from structure to count: a circuit of suitable orbitals and the model’s geometry conditions are required. Each benzene carbon contributes one p electron; pyrimidine nitrogens modify electron distribution while preserving a six-electron circuit. Their lone pairs occupy orbitals different from those counted in the pi system. Using only the number eight for cyclooctatetraene would confuse its real geometry with a hypothetical planar antiaromatic ring. Out-of-plane deformation instead avoids that case. The comparison explains why aromatic, antiaromatic and nonaromatic classification requires both electronic and geometric information.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For toluene, nitrobenzene and chlorobenzene, qualitatively predict activation or deactivation relative to benzene and predominant orientation in ordinary electrophilic substitution. Explain the apparent halogen exception and why exact product percentages cannot be deduced.

Toluene’s methyl is generally **activating and ortho/para-directing**. Nitro is **deactivating and meta-directing**. Chlorine is **deactivating and ortho/para-directing**. Comparing overall rate with benzene and comparing positions within the same ring are distinct assessments.

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituents alter the stability of cationic electrophilic-attack intermediates. Methyl contributes donating effects, while nitro withdraws density and relatively disfavors certain pathways. For chlorine, inductive withdrawal lowers overall reactivity, but resonance donation relatively favours ortho and para intermediates. This is consistent because the two comparisons use different references. Ortho-versus-para selection also depends on steric effects, electrophile and conditions, and further substituents may alter distribution. Assigning percentages would require data or an explicit quantitative model; the present reasoning supplies justified qualitative trends.

</div>
