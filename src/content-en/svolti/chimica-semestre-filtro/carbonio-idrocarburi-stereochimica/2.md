## Exercise 1

Assign the IUPAC name and molecular formula of $\mathrm{CH_3CH(CH_3)CH_2CH_2CH_3}$. Justify the chain and numbering direction.

The longest continuous chain contains five carbons. The two directions assign methyl locant 2 or 4, so 2 is selected.

**2-Methylpentane**, with formula $\mathrm{C_6H_{14}}$.

Direct hydrogen counting confirms $3+1+3+2+2+3=14$. The branch carbon contributes to molecular formula without making the parent chain six carbons long.

![Five numbered parent-chain vertices in 2-methylpentane](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/carbonio-idrocarburi-stereochimica/nomenclatura-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

A parent chain is a continuous path that does not revisit a carbon. At a branch point, all directions cannot be added together to manufacture a six-atom chain. The path is identified first, then vertices are numbered and the substituent located. Selecting the lower locant describes the same connectivity from the preferred direction rather than creating a different isomer. The formula provides an independent check: six carbons in a saturated acyclic alkane require fourteen hydrogens. A result of twelve would call for a ring or unsaturation, neither of which is present.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CHCH(CH_3)CH_3}$, a student proposes 2-methylbut-3-ene. Correct the name and derive the degree of unsaturation from the molecular formula.

The chain containing the double bond has four carbons. Numbering from the unsaturated end puts the double bond at C1 and methyl at C3: **3-methylbut-1-ene**.

The formula is $\mathrm{C_5H_{10}}$, so
$$I=\dfrac{2\cdot5+2-10}{2}=1.$$

The displayed double bond accounts for the single unsaturation unit.

<div class="spiegazione">

The proposed name retains the correct parent-chain carbon count but incorrectly prioritizes the branch locant. In this example, numbering must give the double bond the lower locant. The CH at the branch point bonds to three carbons and one hydrogen; overall counting prevents treating the parenthesized methyl as a separate molecule. Unsaturation checks ring and multiple-bond counts rather than methyl position. Two structures with the same formula and I value can therefore have different names and connectivity.

</div>
