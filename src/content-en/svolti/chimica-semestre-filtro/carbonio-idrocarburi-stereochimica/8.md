## Exercise 1

The Newman projection shows 2-bromobutane along C2–C3, with C2 at the front. Consider E2 elimination of the highlighted Br/Hβ pair in this conformation without changing to another rotamer. Identify the new double bond and the product’s E/Z configuration.

Front Br and rear Hβ are antiperiplanar. HBr loss creates C2=C3, giving but-2-ene. The two remaining methyls are on the same side of the plane containing the C2–C3 axis and eliminated pair, so they remain on the same side of the product double bond. Because each outranks H, this conformation gives **(Z)-but-2-ene**.

![Newman projection with antiperiplanar Br and beta H and both methyls on the same side](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/carbonio-idrocarburi-stereochimica/e2-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The drawing distinguishes the front carbon, represented at the centre, from the rear carbon, represented by the circle. The eliminated bonds are opposite when viewed along the axis, satisfying the 180-degree dihedral requirement. Tracking stereochemistry requires retaining the positions of surviving groups rather than redrawing them arbitrarily: both methyls are on the right and stay on the same side when the carbons become trigonal planar. This result concerns the specified reacting conformer. A real mixture can access other rotamers and beta hydrogens, giving a different distribution; the question isolates one pathway for a checkable stereochemical assignment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Ordinary E2 is proposed to obtain an alkene from neopentyl bromide, $\mathrm{(CH_3)_3CCH_2Br}$. Identify alpha and beta carbons, check available hydrogens and assess whether a stronger base solves the problem.

The alpha carbon is CH₂Br. Its only carbon neighbour, beta, is bonded to three methyls and the alpha carbon: it is quaternary and bears no H. A beta hydrogen to remove with bromide is therefore absent. A stronger base does not enable this ordinary E2 pathway on the given connectivity.

<div class="spiegazione">

Hydrogens on the three methyls are separated from the alpha carbon by the quaternary carbon and are not at the required beta position. Alpha hydrogens cannot replace those needed on the adjacent carbon: the E2 under consideration creates the double bond precisely between alpha and beta. Structural checking therefore precedes comparing base strength or bulk. The conclusion concerns the ordinary pathway without rearrangements or additional transformations; it does not claim that the substance cannot undergo any reaction. Distinguishing an unavailable pathway from a slow available one avoids assigning every negative result to an insufficiently strong reagent.

</div>
