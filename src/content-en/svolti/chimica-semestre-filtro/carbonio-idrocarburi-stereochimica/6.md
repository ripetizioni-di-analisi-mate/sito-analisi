## Exercise 1

Reconstruct radical methane chlorination with initiation, two propagation steps and an ethane-forming termination. Sum only the propagation steps and check radical balance.

Initiation is $\mathrm{Cl_2}\xrightarrow{h\nu}2\mathrm{Cl}^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}$. Propagation consists of
$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{Cl}^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}+\mathrm{CH_4}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{HCl}+\mathrm{CH_3}^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}},\\\mathrm{CH_3}^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}+\mathrm{Cl_2}&\longrightarrow\mathrm{CH_3Cl}+\mathrm{Cl}^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}.\end{aligned}$$
Cancelling intermediate radicals gives $\mathrm{CH_4+Cl_2\longrightarrow CH_3Cl+HCl}$. One termination is $2\mathrm{CH_3}^{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{C_2H_6}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Each propagation consumes one radical and produces one: the radical centre changes species but the chain can continue. Initiation produces two radicals from chlorine, whereas termination consumes two without regeneration. When propagation steps are summed, chlorine and methyl radicals appear on both sides and cancel; net balance preserves one carbon, four hydrogens and two chlorines. The selected termination explains possible ethane formation without inserting it arbitrarily into the monosubstitution net equation. A real mixture can undergo other terminations and further chlorination, so this one balance does not describe the entire product distribution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the illustrated allylic carbocation contributors, number carbons from the left as C1, C2 and C3. Explain what changes between contributors. If a nucleophile attacks C1 or C3 of the unsubstituted cation, must two different constitutional products form?

Charge and double-bond placement change in the electronic representation; nuclei retain the same connectivity. In the unsubstituted allylic cation the terminal positions are symmetry-equivalent. Product drawings read from opposite ends therefore represent the **same substituted allylic connectivity**.

![Identical nuclear positions in the two allyl contributors](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/carbonio-idrocarburi-stereochimica/risonanza-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Contributors are not two molecules periodically converting into one another. The actual intermediate has delocalized electron density, and nucleophilic attack can be represented at the system’s termini. Product counting nevertheless requires a symmetry check after bonding: drawing the entering group on the left or right does not prove different products. In unsubstituted allyl, reversing the chain identifies them. A substituent making the termini different can remove that equivalence and create a separate regioselectivity problem. The reasoning thus distinguishes contributor count, represented attack sites and genuinely distinguishable products.

</div>
