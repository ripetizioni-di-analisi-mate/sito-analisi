## Exercise 1

Determine carbon hybridization, local geometry and total sigma and pi bond counts in ethene, $\mathrm{CH_2{=}CH_2}$. Explain why counting five sigma bonds does not imply free rotation around C=C.

Each carbon forms three sigma bonds, two to H and one to the other C. Local geometry is trigonal planar and the model is $sp^2$.

$$N_\sigma=4+1=5,\qquad N_\pi=1.$$

C=C contains both contributions: rotating one end changes pi overlap.

![Explicit bonds in ethene and ethyne](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/carbonio-idrocarburi-stereochimica/legami-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Counting must include all molecular bonds rather than only carbon–carbon ones. Each connection between two nuclei contains a sigma contribution; multiplicity adds pi contributions. Each ethene carbon retains an unhybridized p orbital for sideways overlap. The sp² model therefore describes local angles and the alignment required by the pi system. Ordinary single-bond rotation preserves sigma overlap, but rotation here also disrupts pi overlap and has a high energetic cost. Comparison with ethyne in the figure shows that a triple bond adds a second pi contribution without adding a second sigma bond between the same carbons.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The figure shows a central carbon bonded to OH, COOH, CH₃ and H. Derive the molecular formula and decide whether that carbon is stereogenic. Interpret both wedges without using optical-rotation sign.

There are three carbons: the centre, methyl and carboxyl carbon. Hydrogens comprise three in methyl, one at the centre and one on each OH. There are three oxygens.

$$\mathrm{C_3H_6O_3}.$$

The centre is tetrahedral with four different groups. CH₃ on the solid wedge points forwards; H on the hashed wedge points backwards.

![Four different groups at the central carbon and wedge orientation](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/carbonio-idrocarburi-stereochimica/cunei-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

The condensed COOH group contains one carbon, two oxygens and one hydrogen and must be expanded during counting. The central carbon gains no additional hydrogens: its H is already shown and its four bonds are complete. Methyl and carboxyl both begin with carbon, but they are different substituents because their subsequent connections differ. Stereogenicity depends on that distinction and tetrahedral geometry. Wedges describe bond orientation, whereas optical rotation requires measurement under specified conditions. Reading the drawing therefore establishes composition and stereogenicity without assigning an optical sign.

</div>
