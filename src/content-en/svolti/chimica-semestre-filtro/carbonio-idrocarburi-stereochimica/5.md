## Exercise 1

In a specified solvent at fixed temperature and wavelength, a solution has $\alpha=+3.6^\circ$, $\ell=1.5\,\mathrm{dm}$ and $c=0.020\,\mathrm{g/mL}$. Calculate specific rotation. Predict observed rotation with a $0.75\,\mathrm{dm}$ cell containing the same solution, using the linear model.

Apply the stated convention:
$$[\alpha]=\dfrac{3.6}{1.5\cdot0.020}=+120\,\mathrm{{}^\circ mL\,g^{-1}\,dm^{-1}}.$$
With the new path,
$$\alpha'=120\cdot0.75\cdot0.020=+1.8^\circ.$$
Observed rotation halves, while specific rotation is unchanged in the stated model and conditions.

<div class="spiegazione">

The first division removes geometric and concentration factors using the stated units. There is no need to convert grams per millilitre into grams per hundred millilitres: doing so without changing the formula introduces a wrong factor of one hundred. The second step uses the same specific rotation because substance, solvent, temperature, wavelength and concentration are unchanged. The new path is half the old one, providing a ratio check. Positive sign characterizes the measurement without by itself assigning R or S. The linear model must hold over the experimental range to justify proportionality.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A mixture contains only R and S. Under identical achiral conditions with additive response, $[\alpha]_R=+40$ and $[\alpha]_{\mathrm{mix}}=+12$ in the same units. Determine enantiomeric excess and mole fractions. Specific rotations are normalized to the correct total concentration.

Pure S has value $-40$. Therefore
$$\begin{aligned}12&=40x_R-40x_S,\\x_R+x_S&=1,\\x_R-x_S&=0.30.\end{aligned}$$
This gives
$$x_R=0.65,\qquad x_S=0.35,\qquad ee=30\%.$$
R is the majority because the mixture’s sign matches its experimental value.

<div class="spiegazione">

The rotation ratio gives the difference between fractions in the additive model, not the majority fraction itself. The second equation, requiring a unit sum, reconstructs both amounts. The check is sixty-five minus thirty-five equals thirty percent, with a total of one hundred percent. R was not conventionally equated with a positive sign: the pure-reference measurement establishes that correspondence for this substance. An optically active impurity or incorrect concentration normalization would invalidate direct calculation, so those possibilities are excluded by the question.

</div>
