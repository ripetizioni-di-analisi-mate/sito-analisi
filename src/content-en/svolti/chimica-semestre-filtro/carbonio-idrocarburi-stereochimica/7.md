## Exercise 1

At constant temperature and solvent, a substitution gives these data: A, $[\mathrm{RX}]=0.10\,\mathrm{mol/L}$ and $[\mathrm{Nu^-}]=0.10\,\mathrm{mol/L}$, $v=1.0\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$; B, only RX doubles and rate doubles; C, only Nu⁻ doubles and rate doubles. Derive orders and k in a power-law model.

Writing $v=k[\mathrm{RX}]^a[\mathrm{Nu^-}]^b$, separate comparisons give $2^a=2$ and $2^b=2$. Thus $a=b=1$ and
$$k=\dfrac{1.0\cdot10^{-4}}{0.10\cdot0.10}=1.0\cdot10^{-2}\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}\,s^{-1}}.$$
The law is compatible with the SN2 model.

![One maximum in the concerted model and an intermediate in the two-step model](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/carbonio-idrocarburi-stereochimica/meccanismi-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Each comparison changes just one concentration, allowing the rate change to determine its exponent. Orders are inferred from data rather than copied from stoichiometric coefficients. The unit of k follows by dividing concentration per time by two concentration factors. Products are specified as substitution products, but rate law alone does not establish every microscopic detail: inversion at the reacting centre, absence of rearrangements and steric dependence would provide further checks. The SN2 profile illustrates a possible concerted step consistent with the data, whereas elementary E2 can share bimolecular kinetics but yield different products.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solvolysis of an enantiopure halide with a single stereogenic centre at the halogen-bearing carbon produces the two substitution-product enantiomers in a 40:60 ratio. No additional stereogenic centres form. Rate depends on substrate and does not change appreciably when nucleophile is increased under the examined conditions. Interpret the data using an SN1 model and explain why the ratio need not be 50:50.

The kinetics are consistent with rate-limiting ionization followed by carbocation attack. The intermediate’s planar geometry makes two faces accessible, so both configurations can occur. Ion pairing or solvent organization can affect the two attacks differently and produce the observed ratio.

<div class="spiegazione">

Lack of nucleophile rate dependence does not mean absence from the mechanism: it acts after the step controlling substrate consumption. The enantiomeric ratio provides additional information without requiring a carbocation completely separated from the leaving group. Perfect racemization would require equivalent attack probabilities at both faces under suitable conditions. A 40:60 result is therefore compatible with SN1 and does not automatically prove contamination or a different pathway. Stronger evidence could come from solvent effects, intermediate stability and possible rearranged products. The data support a model without making every microscopic detail directly observed.

</div>
