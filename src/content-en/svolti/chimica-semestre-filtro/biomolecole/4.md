## Exercise 1

Calculate the molar mass of linear glycylalanine. Data: glycine $75.07$, alanine $89.09$ and water $18.02$, all in $\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. Identify the N-terminus and C-terminus, and compare the result with Ala-Gly without terminal modifications.

![Planar peptide unit with an amide C–N bond.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/peptide-en.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(\mathrm{Gly{-}Ala})
&=75.07+89.09-18.02\\[4pt]
&=146.14\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
M(\mathrm{Ala{-}Gly})=M(\mathrm{Gly{-}Ala}).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two residues in a linear chain require only one peptide bond. The balance from free-amino-acid composition therefore removes one water molecule. The initial sum is $164.16\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$; subtracting $18.02$ gives **$146.14\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$**. This subtraction describes product composition and does not require a real synthesis to proceed by direct condensation in water.

Gly-Ala is written from N to C: glycine retains the amino end and alanine the carboxyl end. In Ala-Gly the terminal roles are reversed. Both peptides have the same residues and number of bonds, so their molar masses match despite different sequence connectivity. As a mass check, adding one mole of water to the dipeptide during complete hydrolysis restores $146.14+18.02=164.16\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, the sum of both amino acids. Mass alone does not identify residue order.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A glycine methyl ester reacts with the alanine amino group. Assume suitable protection of other sites, omitted from the balance, and a one-to-one ratio. $0.020\,\mathrm{mol}$ of ester is converted into the desired amide only. Identify the dipeptide sequence and coproduct, and calculate their amounts.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{H_2NCH_2COOCH_3}
+\mathrm{H_2NCH(CH_3)COOH}\\[4pt]
&\quad\longrightarrow
\mathrm{H_2NCH_2CO{-}NHCH(CH_3)COOH}\\[4pt]
&\qquad+\mathrm{CH_3OH}.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
n_{\mathrm{Gly{-}Ala}}=n_{\mathrm{CH_3OH}}=0.020\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The attacked carbonyl belongs to esterified glycine, while the incoming nitrogen belongs to alanine. The resulting bond therefore runs Gly–CO–NH–Ala, corresponding to **Gly-Ala** after appropriate deprotection. The amine attacks the carbonyl, a tetrahedral intermediate forms, and elimination of the methoxy group with proton transfers produces methanol. This sequence distinguishes aminolysis from simply bringing two free amino acids together.

Each converted ester gives one dipeptide molecule and one CH₃OH, so both amounts equal $0.020\,\mathrm{mol}$. The methyl group provides an independent atom-balance check: since it does not enter the dipeptide, it must appear in methanol. Writing water as the only coproduct would lose that carbon. Protection and selective conversion are model assumptions; without selectivity, other groups could react and stoichiometry alone would not identify one peptide.

</div>
