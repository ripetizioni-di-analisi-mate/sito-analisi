## Exercise 1

Identify $R_1=\mathrm{CH_2COO^-}$ and $R_2=\mathrm{CH_2CONH_2}$. At pH 7, assume the shown charges predominate. The first residue is replaced by the second in a protein without changing other groups. Calculate the side-chain charge change and identify which interaction with a positive lysine may weaken.

![Structures of side chains R₁ and R₂ to identify.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/sidechains-data-en.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_1&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Asp}\ (D),\qquad q_1=-1,\\[4pt]
R_2&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Asn}\ (N),\qquad q_2=0,\\[4pt]
\Delta q&=q_2-q_1=0-(-1)=+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first terminal group is a carboxylate and identifies **aspartate**. The second is an amide and identifies asparagine; its nitrogen is not treated as an ordinary protonated side-chain amine. Only side chains are compared because the other contributions are stated to be unchanged. The change $+1$ means loss of a negative charge while the new chain remains neutral: it does not mean asparagine acquires positive charge.

The Asp–Lys pair can benefit from attraction between opposite charges. Converting Asp to Asn removes this local ionic contribution. The amide nevertheless retains polarity and hydrogen-bonding possibilities, so the substitution is not equivalent to introducing a completely hydrocarbon chain. The information predicts a particular interaction change, whereas its overall stability effect requires the site geometry. As a check, including lysine with charge $+1$ changes the pair from $-1+1=0$ to $0+1=+1$, giving the same calculated difference.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A protein contains two residues with $R_A=\mathrm{CH_2SH}$ and two with $R_B=\mathrm{CH_2CH_2SCH_3}$. Identify them. A reagent selectively oxidises thiol pairs to disulfides while leaving thioethers unchanged. Determine the maximum number of bridges and the number of thiols remaining after complete conversion.

$$
R_A\longrightarrow\mathrm{Cys}\ (C),\qquad
R_B\longrightarrow\mathrm{Met}\ (M).
$$
$$
N_{\mathrm{S{-}S}}=\dfrac{2}{2}=1,
\qquad N_{\mathrm{SH,final}}=2-2(1)=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Cysteine contains an S–H thiol; methionine contains a thioether with sulfur bonded to two carbons. The specified reagent recognises thiols, so only the two cysteines enter the balance. One cysteine pair can form **one disulfide bridge**, provided the geometry allows contact; the requested value is the stoichiometric maximum. No thiols from this pair remain after complete conversion.

Counting four sulfur atoms in the protein does not justify two bridges: methionine sulfur belongs to a different functional group. Other protein bonds lie outside the selective transformation. To check conservation, the product has two sulfur atoms in the bridge and two in the thioethers, still four overall. The thiol half-reaction releases two protons and two electrons per bridge; a real oxidant must accept the electrons. The mere presence of a chemical element therefore does not determine residue reactivity.

</div>
