## Exercise 1

Two open-chain aldohexoses have their C2, C3, C4 and C5 OH groups respectively right-left-right-right and left-left-right-right in Fischer projections. Identify the D/L series and stereochemical relationship. State whether simple rotation around a single bond can convert one into the other.

| Centre | First sugar | Second sugar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C2 | OH on right | OH on left |
| C3 | OH on left | OH on left |
| C4 | OH on right | OH on right |
| C5 | OH on right | OH on right |

Both D; C2 epimers: D-glucose and D-mannose.

<div class="spiegazione">

![Opposite C2 OH positions in D-glucose and D-mannose.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/fischer-sugar-en.svg)

The stereogenic centre furthest from the carbonyl assigns D/L, here C5. Its OH is on the right in both Fischer projections, so both sugars belong to the **D series**. Comparing rows shows only one inversion, at C2. Connectivity is identical and the other three centres match, so the molecules are C2 epimers and belong to the diastereomer class.

Enantiomerism would require inversion of all four centres, a condition not met. Conformational rotation around a single bond changes spatial orientation without inverting a tetrahedral carbon’s configuration, so it does not perform the required epimerisation. As a check, the anomeric centre is not yet present: open-chain C1 is carbonyl and planar. The C2 difference therefore cannot be classified as anomerism. The figure shows the only differing centre while retaining the Fischer horizontal-bonds-forward convention.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the Haworth projection of D-glucopyranose, CH₂OH is above the ring. Assign α/β to forms with C1 OH below and above. A mixture containing only these forms has specific rotation $56.2$, whereas pure α and β give $112$ and $19$ under the same units and conditions. Assuming additivity and neglecting the open form, calculate the fractions.

![Anomeric OH in the two D-glucopyranose anomers.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/haworth-data-en.svg)

$$
x_\alpha+x_\beta=1,
\qquad112x_\alpha+19x_\beta=56.2.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_\alpha&=\dfrac{56.2-19}{112-19}=0.40,\\[4pt]
x_\beta&=1-0.40=0.60.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For this D sugar, anomeric OH below and CH₂OH above are on opposite sides and therefore identify **α**. With both above, the form is β. This is a relation between the two groups in the stated conventional projection. C5 retains its configuration during interconversion, whereas C1 can change through the planar open-chain form.

Additivity allows mixture rotation to be written as a weighted mean of the two pure rotations. Substituting $x_\beta=1-x_\alpha$ isolates the α fraction; β follows from the population balance. The fractions are $40\%$ and $60\%$. Substitution checks $112(0.40)+19(0.60)=44.8+11.4=56.2$. Mixture rotation also lies between the pure values, as a positive-weight average must. The two-form model is a stated approximation; actual mutarotation passes through a small open-chain population.

</div>
