## Exercise 1

For glycine in dilute solution, assume $pK_{a,1}=2.30$ and $pK_{a,2}=9.60$. Calculate $pI$ and determine the mean-charge sign and migration direction at pH 8, neglecting solvent flow. Distinguish mean charge from predominant form.

$$
pI=\dfrac{2.30+9.60}{2}=5.95.
$$
$$
\dfrac{[\mathrm{Gly^-}]}{[\mathrm{Gly^0}]}=10^{8-9.60}\simeq0.0251,
\qquad
\dfrac{[\mathrm{Gly^+}]}{[\mathrm{Gly^0}]}=10^{2.30-8}\simeq0.0000020.
$$
$$
\bar q\simeq\dfrac{0.0000020-0.0251}{1+0.0251+0.0000020}
\simeq-0.0245.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

![Mean glycine charge curve with pKa 2.30 and 9.60.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/pi-en.svg)

The zero-charge zwitterion lies between the two dissociations, so the mean of the two $pK_a$ values applies. At $pI$, the small positive and negative populations balance. At pH 8, above $5.95$, the negative population exceeds the positive one and the **mean charge is negative**. The electric field therefore produces migration towards the positive electrode.

The calculated ratios also show why “negative charge” does not mean nearly all molecules have charge −1. Setting the zwitterion population to one gives a negative population of about $0.0251$ and a positive population of about two millionths. The zwitterion remains most abundant, while the imbalance of minority species gives a slightly negative mean. The mean formula weights each population by its charge and divides by the total. The calculated sign independently checks the prediction based on comparing pH with $pI$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An acidic amino acid has successive $pK_a$ values $2.00$, $3.80$, $9.70$ and successive charges $+1$, $0$, $-1$, $-2$. Approximate $pI$ using the two equilibria adjacent to the neutral form and identify the predominant species at pH 7. Assume a dilute solution and constants valid under the stated conditions.

$$
pI\simeq\dfrac{2.00+3.80}{2}=2.90.
$$
$$
q_{\mathrm{pred}}=(-1)+(-1)+(+1)=-1.
$$
$$
\dfrac{[A^-]}{[HA^0]}=10^{7-3.80}\simeq1585,
\qquad
\dfrac{[A^{2-}]}{[A^-]}=10^{7-9.70}\simeq0.0020.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The neutral species appears at the first dissociation and is consumed by the second. Its bracketing values are therefore $2.00$ and $3.80$. An average including $9.70$ would describe different equilibria rather than charge compensation around the neutral form. The result **$pI\simeq2.90$** neglects the extremely small population of the −2 form in this region.

At pH 7 both carboxyl groups have mainly lost their protons, while ammonium remains protonated because its $pK_a$ exceeds pH. The dominant form therefore has charge −1. The ratios provide a quantitative check: the −1 form is about 1585 times more abundant than the neutral form, whereas the −2 form is only about $0.20\%$ of the −1 form. Identifying ionisable groups and the charge sequence precedes calculation; indiscriminately averaging all three $pK_a$ values has no such physical meaning.

</div>
