## Exercise 1

Compare maltose, with a glucose α$(1\to4)$ glucose linkage, and sucrose, linking glucose C1 to fructose C2. Determine free anomeric centres and reducing character before and after complete hydrolysis. Refer to the common alkaline reducing-sugar test.

| Sample | Free anomeric centres per molecule | Before hydrolysis | Products |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maltose | 1 | Reducing | Two glucoses |
| Sucrose | 0 | Nonreducing | Glucose and fructose |

After hydrolysis, both samples give reducing products in the stated test.

<div class="spiegazione">

In maltose, the first glucose uses C1 in the bond, whereas the second uses a C4 hydroxyl and retains its own hemiacetal C1. That centre can open to an aldehyde and makes the disaccharide **reducing**. In sucrose both linked atoms are anomeric: C1 for aldose glucose and C2 for ketose fructose. Both are locked, so sucrose is nonreducing before hydrolysis.

Hydrolysis restores a free hemiacetal or hemiketal to the affected residues. Maltose gives two glucoses, whereas sucrose gives glucose and fructose. Fructose responds to the alkaline test because it can interconvert with aldoses through an enediol, making the hydrolysed sample reducing. As a structural check, total OH count is insufficient to classify behaviour: both disaccharides have many OH groups but differ in anomeric-carbon state. Checking products also confirms two monosaccharides per cleaved disaccharide.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Start with $0.50\,\mathrm{mol}$ of lactose and hydrolyse $40\%$, with excess water and no side reactions. Calculate remaining lactose, glucose, galactose, water consumed and the total amount of carbohydrate molecules. Do not assume complete hydrolysis.

$$
\xi=0.40(0.50)=0.20\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{Lac}}&=0.50-0.20=0.30\,\mathrm{mol},\\[4pt]
n_{\mathrm{Glc}}&=n_{\mathrm{Gal}}=n_{\mathrm{H_2O,cons}}=0.20\,\mathrm{mol},\\[4pt]
n_{\mathrm{tot}}&=0.30+0.20+0.20=0.70\,\mathrm{mol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The reaction is lactose plus water giving glucose and galactose, with all coefficients equal to one. Reaction extent $\xi$ equals the moles of lactose actually cleaved, namely $40\%$ of the initial amount. This quantity is subtracted from the reactant and added to each product. Total carbohydrate molecules increase because every converted disaccharide becomes two monosaccharides.

To check sugar-residue conservation, the initial sample contains $2(0.50)=1.00\,\mathrm{mol}$ of monosaccharide units. At the end it contains $2(0.30)+0.20+0.20=1.00\,\mathrm{mol}$ of the same units. Moles of molecules and moles of units are therefore different quantities. Water consumption is $0.20\,\mathrm{mol}$ even with excess solvent; excess guarantees availability but does not change the stoichiometric coefficient. No concentration is requested, so no final volume assumption is needed.

</div>
