## Exercise 1

For DNA 5′-AGTCC-3′, write the aligned complement and then express it 5′→3′. Count phosphodiester links in the linear duplex and hydrogen bonds between canonical paired bases. Exclude nicks and terminal modifications.

![One phosphate connects the C3′ oxygen to the next residue’s C5′ oxygen.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/nucleotide-en.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&5'\text{-AGTCC-}3'\\
&3'\text{-TCAGG-}5'
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
5'\text{-GGACT-}3'.
$$
$$
N_{\mathrm{PDE}}=2(5-1)=8,
\qquad N_{\mathrm H}=2(2)+3(3)=13.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply A–T and G–C pairing at every position to obtain TCAGG below the given strand. The strands are antiparallel, so the aligned complement runs 3′ to 5′. Reversing its order gives **5′-GGACT-3′**. The aligned complement and reverse complement represent the same molecule written in different directions; end labels prevent confusion.

Each strand has five nucleotides and four links between neighbours. Both linear strands therefore contain eight phosphodiester bridges overall. The two A–T pairs contribute four hydrogen bonds, and the three G–C pairs contribute nine, totalling thirteen. An independent check uses the given strand’s composition: A and T occupy two positions, G and C three, returning five total pairs. Hydrogen bonds link bases between strands, whereas phosphodiesters connect sugars along each backbone. The figure shows the second linkage type, with one phosphate shared by two ester bonds.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In one balance, $0.030\,\mathrm{mol}$ NADH and $0.020\,\mathrm{mol}$ FADH₂ are completely oxidised. Calculate total electrons donated. In a separate experiment, $0.015\,\mathrm{mol}$ ROOH is reduced according to $2\mathrm{GSH}+\mathrm{ROOH}\to\mathrm{GSSG}+\mathrm{ROH}+\mathrm{H_2O}$. Calculate GSH consumed and GSSG formed; do not assume coupling between the experiments.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n(e^-)&=2(0.030)+2(0.020)\\[4pt]
&=0.100\,\mathrm{mol},\\[4pt]
n(\mathrm{GSH})&=2(0.015)=0.030\,\mathrm{mol},\\[4pt]
n(\mathrm{GSSG})&=0.015\,\mathrm{mol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One mole of NADH donates two moles of electrons when it returns to NAD⁺; one mole of FADH₂ donates two when it returns to FAD. Their contributions are $0.060$ and $0.040\,\mathrm{mol}$ and add together. Different proton counts in their half-reactions do not change the fact that both coenzymes transfer two electrons overall per oxidised molecule.

In the second experiment, two GSH form one GSSG for every hydroperoxide reduced. This ratio determines consumption and products without arbitrarily connecting the system to the first experiment’s coenzymes. As a glutathione-unit check, the initial $0.030\,\mathrm{mol}$ reappears as $2(0.015)=0.030\,\mathrm{mol}$ of units in the disulfide. Each GSSG formed corresponds to two electrons donated by thiols; these are used by peroxide reduction in the overall balance. Biological peroxidases can catalyse the reaction, whereas the stated stoichiometry remains a property of the transformation and does not by itself determine rate.

</div>
