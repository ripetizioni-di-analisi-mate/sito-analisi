## Exercise 1

A fatty acid has 18 carbons and two cis double bonds at Δ9 and Δ12. Determine its ω series and the amount of H₂ needed to saturate $0.15\,\mathrm{mol}$ of the acid, reducing only C=C bonds. Qualitatively compare its packing with that of the saturated derivative of the same length.

![Local cis and trans geometries around a chain double bond.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/cis-trans-en.svg)

$$
18{:}2\,\Delta^{9,12},\qquad\omega=18-12=6.
$$
$$
n(\mathrm{H_2})=2(0.15)=0.30\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Δ positions are counted from the carboxyl group. From the methyl end, Δ12 is encountered first because it is closest to that end; subtracting it from 18 gives **ω-6**. Δ9 describes the other double bond and does not determine the first unsaturation from methyl. The carboxyl group remains reference C1 in the original count.

Each double bond adds one H₂ molecule. Two unsaturations therefore require two moles of H₂ per mole of acid, giving total consumption of $0.30\,\mathrm{mol}$. As an atom check, each molecule gains four H and changes from two unsaturations to zero without changing carbon count. Cis double bonds introduce bends that hinder tail alignment; at equal length, the saturated derivative tends to pack more regularly and melt higher. This qualitative prediction compares otherwise similar structures. The figure shows local cis/trans geometry of one C=C, not the count of two bonds in the question.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pure triacylglycerol has two palmitoyl residues and one oleoyl residue. $0.080\,\mathrm{mol}$ is completely saponified. Calculate NaOH consumed and glycerol, palmitate and oleate produced. Identify the functional group distinguishing this hydrolysis from that of a ceramide.

![Three triacylglycerol esters and the ceramide amide.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/lipids-en.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
n(\mathrm{NaOH})&=3(0.080)=0.240\,\mathrm{mol},\\[4pt]
n(\mathrm{glycerol})&=0.080\,\mathrm{mol},\\[4pt]
n(\mathrm{palmitate})&=2(0.080)=0.160\,\mathrm{mol},\\[4pt]
n(\mathrm{oleate})&=0.080\,\mathrm{mol}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Glycerol has three esterified functions, so complete saponification consumes three hydroxide equivalents per mole of triacylglycerol. The product retains one glycerol backbone per starting molecule. Tails are released as carboxylates and retain their identities: two palmitates and one oleate per triacylglycerol.

The tail check gives $0.160+0.080=0.240\,\mathrm{mol}$ of carboxylates, matching the moles of cleaved esters and consumed NaOH. The oleate double bond is not hydrogenated by simple hydrolysis, so oleate does not become palmitate or stearate. A ceramide instead has a fatty acid **amide-linked** to sphingosine nitrogen, with different reactivity and a different backbone; the triester’s three-to-one ratio cannot automatically be assigned to it. The figure compares condensed lipid formulas and makes the acyl group’s O or N partner explicit.

</div>
