## Exercise 1

A $120\,\mathrm{kDa}$ protein complex contains four $30\,\mathrm{kDa}$ chains. A treatment separates them without amide hydrolysis; each chain retains most of its α-helices. Identify the level certainly lost, the level retained by assumption and the additional evidence needed to assess complete folding.

| Evidence | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Four separated chains | Quaternary association lost |
| Amides not hydrolysed | Primary sequences retained |
| Most α-helices retained | Secondary structure partly retained |
| No overall arrangement data | Tertiary preservation not established |

<div class="spiegazione">

Quaternary structure concerns association between chains, so complete separation removes the original organisation. Total mass is conserved because $4(30)=120\,\mathrm{kDa}$, and the treatment does not cut the peptide backbone. Consequently, each chain’s residue order, its **primary sequence**, remains unchanged.

α-Helices are local secondary structures stabilised mainly by backbone hydrogen bonds. Their partial retention shows that complex dissociation does not necessarily remove all ordered segments. Tertiary folding also includes the mutual arrangement of regions distant in sequence, however. Establishing its preservation would require measurements of overall shape or distances between regions, in addition to helix data. The exercise therefore separates a conclusion required by the evidence from an undetermined property. As a check, four intact chains remain four sequences: simple dissociation requires no new peptide ends.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One chain has six cysteines, all involved in intramolecular disulfides. Only these bonds are reduced. Calculate bridges reduced, thiols formed and electrons required. State what can be inferred about tertiary structure without knowing the new conformation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{S{-}S}}&=\dfrac{6}{2}=3,\\[4pt]
N_{\mathrm{SH}}&=2(3)=6,\\[4pt]
N_{e^-}&=2(3)=6.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
3\,\mathrm{RSSR}+6\,\mathrm{H^+}+6e^-
\longrightarrow6\,\mathrm{RSH}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One bridge requires two cysteines, so six paired residues give three disulfides. Reduction restores two SH groups per bridge and consumes two electrons and two protons: three bridges yield **six thiols**, requiring six electrons. These numbers refer to one protein molecule; for one mole of proteins, the same coefficients are expressed in moles.

Removing the bridges eliminates three covalent constraints between chain regions. This can alter tertiary folding but does not show that every local structure disappears or predict a unique conformation. Since peptide bonds are conserved, primary sequence stays unchanged and the chain count remains one. Checking the half-reaction gives six sulfur atoms on each side and six hydrogens added to thiols. The proton charge $+6$ and electron charge $-6$ also cancel. A disulfide is a covalent bond and must be distinguished from the hydrogen bonds stabilising helices.

</div>
