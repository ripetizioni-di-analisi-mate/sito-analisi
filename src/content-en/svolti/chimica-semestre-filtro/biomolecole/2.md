## Exercise 1

In an alanine Fischer projection, COOH is at the top, CH₃ at the bottom, NH₂ on the left and H on the right. Assign D/L and R/S, explaining priorities and the effect of H position. Determine whether the optical-rotation sign follows from these data.

![Alanine and cysteine Fischer projections with NH₂ on the left.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/biomolecole/fischer-aa-data-en.svg)

$$
\mathrm{NH_2}>\mathrm{COOH}>\mathrm{CH_3}>\mathrm H.
$$
$$
\mathrm{L\!{-}Ala},\qquad C_\alpha:S.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Fischer projection has the conventional amino-acid orientation, so NH₂ on the left assigns the **L series**. For R/S, first compare atoms directly attached to the centre: N outranks C, and H is last. The two carbon-starting groups are compared at the next level. The carboxyl carbon presents oxygens, whereas methyl presents hydrogens, so carboxyl outranks methyl.

In the figure, priorities one, two and three appear clockwise. The lowest-priority group H is horizontal, however, and therefore points towards the observer: the apparent direction is reversed to give **S**. A check is to view the same arrangement from the opposite side: H then points away and the sequence one, two, three appears anticlockwise. This changes the viewpoint without exchanging groups; swapping two substituents would instead invert the configuration. L and S describe configuration in two distinct systems. Optical sign requires measurement under specified conditions and does not follow automatically from either L or S.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Replace CH₃ in the preceding Fischer projection with CH₂SH while retaining every other group. Determine cysteine configuration. Then compare its number of α stereocentres with that of glycine.

$$
\mathrm{NH_2}>\mathrm{CH_2SH}>\mathrm{COOH}>\mathrm H.
$$
$$
\mathrm{L\!{-}Cys}:C_\alpha R,
\qquad N_{\mathrm{stereo},\mathrm{Cys}}=1,
\qquad N_{\mathrm{stereo},\mathrm{Gly}}=0.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

NH₂ remains on the left with carboxyl at the top, so cysteine remains **L**. In the CIP priorities, amino and H remain first and last respectively. Comparison of the two carbon-starting groups changes: CH₂SH presents sulfur at the next level, while carboxyl presents oxygen. Since sulfur has a higher atomic number, CH₂SH outranks COOH. Swapping these two priorities relative to alanine reverses the absolute assignment to **R**, still accounting for H pointing forwards.

Cysteine retains four different substituents at Cα and therefore has one stereogenic centre. Glycine instead has two H atoms on the same carbon: exchanging them produces no distinguishable structure, so that carbon is achiral. As a conceptual check, changing priority order alone can change the R/S symbol without changing the geometric arrangement in the Fischer projection. This is why L = S cannot be used as a general rule.

</div>
