## Exercise 1

A reaction follows Arrhenius with $E_a=50.0\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Calculate the ratio of constants at $310$ and $300\,\mathrm{K}$, assuming constant pre-exponential factor and activation energy. Use $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$.

$$
\ln\dfrac{k_{310}}{k_{300}}=-\dfrac{50000}{8.314}\left(\dfrac{1}{310}-\dfrac{1}{300}\right)=0.6467.
$$
$$
\boxed{\dfrac{k_{310}}{k_{300}}=e^{0.6467}\simeq1.91}.
$$
The constant increases by approximately $91\%$; the ratio is dimensionless.

<div class="spiegazione">

Converting energy from kilojoules to joules makes units consistent with the gas constant. Reciprocal temperature decreases on going to 310 K; the minus sign therefore produces a positive logarithm. The result concerns the constant, not necessarily the final rate of a mixture whose concentrations may also change. There is no universal rule that every ten-degree increase doubles rate: the ratio depends on activation energy and starting temperatures. For a denaturing enzyme, the model assumptions themselves could fail.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a schematic free-energy profile, reactants and products have levels $0$ and $-20\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. The maxima of uncatalysed and catalysed pathways are respectively $80$ and $45\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. Calculate both directions' barriers and establish whether the catalyst changes the product–reactant difference.

$$
\Delta G^\ddagger_{+,1}=80-0=80,\qquad
\Delta G^\ddagger_{-,1}=80-(-20)=100,
$$
$$
\Delta G^\ddagger_{+,2}=45-0=45,\qquad
\Delta G^\ddagger_{-,2}=45-(-20)=65.
$$
All values are in $\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$. In both pathways,
$$
\boxed{G_{\mathrm{products}}-G_{\mathrm{reactants}}=-20\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$

![The catalysed pathway lowers the maximum and preserves reactant and product levels.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/reazioni-cinetica-equilibrio/barriere-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Each barrier is measured from its own starting point. The reverse direction starts at products, twenty units lower, so its barrier is twenty units greater. Catalysis changes access to the pathway while preserving the energy difference between states. At unchanged temperature, the same reaction's equilibrium constant therefore remains unchanged. The diagram describes free-energy barriers; numerically identifying them with Arrhenius activation energy would require further assumptions. The horizontal axis is a reaction coordinate, not elapsed time.

</div>
