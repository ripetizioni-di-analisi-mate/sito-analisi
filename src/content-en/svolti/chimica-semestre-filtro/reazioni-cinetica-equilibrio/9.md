## Exercise 1

For the salt $\mathrm{MX_2(s)}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{M^{2+}}+2\mathrm{X^-}$ at fixed temperature, $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=4.00\cdot10^{-12}$. Calculate molar solubility in pure water, assuming an ideal solution, no side reactions and excess solid. Concentrations are normalised to $c^\circ=1\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.

Writing $s$ for the solubility value in $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$,
$$
[M^{2+}]=s,\qquad[X^-]=2s,
$$
$$
4s^3=4.00\cdot10^{-12}\Rightarrow
\boxed{s=1.00\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}}.
$$
X concentration is $2.00\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Solubility counts moles of dissolved formula units per litre. Each unit produces two X ions, whose activity is squared; both factors of two therefore enter the product, giving four. Using $s^2$ would apply the stoichiometry of a one-to-one salt. Excess solid ensures a phase is present with which saturation equilibrium can be established. If insufficient mass were available, all salt could dissolve without reaching the calculated concentrations, requiring a balance based on the amount actually introduced.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Mix $20.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ of solution containing free calcium at $2.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ with $30.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ containing free oxalate at $1.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. The fixed-temperature model assigns $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=2.00\cdot10^{-9}$ to calcium oxalate. Assume additive volumes, ideality and no protonation or complexes. Assess the tendency before appreciable precipitation.

$$
[\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}]_0=2.00\cdot10^{-3}\dfrac{20.0}{50.0}=8.00\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}},
$$
$$
[\mathrm{C_2O_4^{2-}}]_0=1.00\cdot10^{-3}\dfrac{30.0}{50.0}=6.00\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}.
$$
$$
Q_{\mathrm{ps}}=(8.00\cdot10^{-4})(6.00\cdot10^{-4})=4.80\cdot10^{-7},\qquad
\boxed{\dfrac{Q_{\mathrm{ps}}}{K_{\mathrm{ps}}}=240>1}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each species is diluted into the final fifty-millilitre volume; directly multiplying starting-solution concentrations would overestimate the ion product. The ratio above one indicates supersaturation relative to the selected phase and thermodynamically favoured precipitation. It does not establish when visible crystals will appear; nucleation may require time. These data are assigned to a teaching model and do not alone describe a biological fluid, where protonated species, complexes and activity coefficients modify free-ion activities.

</div>
