## Exercise 1

For $\mathrm A\rightleftharpoons\mathrm B$ at $298\,\mathrm{K}$, $K=10$. An ideal mixture has $[A]=0.20$ and $[B]=0.40\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Calculate $Q$ and $\Delta_rG$ and identify the initially favoured direction. Use $R=8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$.

$$
Q=\dfrac{[B]}{[A]}=\dfrac{0.40}{0.20}=2,
$$
$$
\Delta_rG=RT\ln\dfrac{Q}{K}=8.314\cdot298\ln\dfrac{2}{10}\simeq-3987\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}.
$$
$$
\boxed{\Delta_rG\simeq-3.99\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}}.
$$
Initial conversion from A to B is favoured.

<div class="spiegazione">

Normalising both concentrations to the same standard state cancels in the ratio. Product is already more concentrated than reactant, but the current ratio two is smaller than the equilibrium ratio ten: forward conversion remains favoured. The logarithm of a ratio below one is negative. Using only $-RT\ln K$ would calculate the standard change and ignore sample composition. The result is per mole of reaction extent and indicates the initial tendency; it does not specify how rapidly the system approaches equilibrium.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a vessel at constant volume and temperature, prepare $[A]_0=0.50\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $[B]_0=0$ for $\mathrm A\rightleftharpoons2\mathrm B$. In the ideal model, $K=1.00$ with concentrations normalised to $c^\circ=1\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Calculate equilibrium concentrations.

If $x$ is the numerical value of A concentration consumed in $\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$,
$$
[A]=0.50-x,\qquad[B]=2x,
$$
$$
\dfrac{(2x)^2}{0.50-x}=1\Rightarrow4x^2+x-0.50=0.
$$
The roots are $x=0.25$ and $x=-0.50$; only the first lies in the physical interval $0\le x\le0.50$.
$$
\boxed{[A]_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.25,\quad[B]_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.50\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each mole of A consumed produces two moles of B, so the product change is twice as large. Using $[B]=x$ would alter the reaction balance. The quadratic also gives a mathematical root that would make B concentration negative; the physical constraint excludes it. Substitution checks the results and gives $\dfrac{0.50^2}{0.25}=1$. The sum of concentrations is not conserved in this transformation; the conserved combination is instead $[A]+\dfrac{[B]}{2}=0.50\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, consistent with stoichiometry.

</div>
