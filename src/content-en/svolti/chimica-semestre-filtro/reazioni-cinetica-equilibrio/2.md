## Exercise 1

Completely react $13.5\,\mathrm{g}$ of aluminium with $24.0\,\mathrm{g}$ of oxygen according to $4\mathrm{Al}+3\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{Al_2O_3}$. Assume no side reactions and use molar masses of $27.0$, $32.0$ and $102.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$, respectively. Determine the limiting reactant, theoretical product mass and remaining oxygen.

$$
n_{\mathrm{Al}}=\dfrac{13.5}{27.0}=0.500\,\mathrm{mol},\qquad
n_{\mathrm{O_2}}=\dfrac{24.0}{32.0}=0.750\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$
$$
\xi_{\max}=\min\left\{\dfrac{0.500}{4},\dfrac{0.750}{3}\right\}=0.125\,\mathrm{mol}.
$$
Aluminium is limiting. Hence
$$
n_{\mathrm{Al_2O_3}}=2\xi=0.250\,\mathrm{mol},\qquad
m_{\mathrm{Al_2O_3}}=25.5\,\mathrm{g},
$$
$$
n_{\mathrm{O_2,res}}=0.750-3\cdot0.125=0.375\,\mathrm{mol},\qquad m_{\mathrm{O_2,res}}=12.0\,\mathrm{g}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Available moles are divided by coefficients because a given extent consumes different amounts of the reactants. The minimum identifies aluminium. Product mass is not the sum of all initial masses: some oxygen remains available. The mass check therefore combines product and remaining reactant: $25.5+12.0=37.5\,\mathrm{g}$, equal to the initial mass. The calculation describes the specified complete conversion; in an actual experiment, a surface layer could impede reaction and require a separate kinetic analysis.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A pure $10.0\,\mathrm{g}$ calcium carbonate sample is completely decomposed with gas removal: $\mathrm{CaCO_3(s)}\longrightarrow\mathrm{CaO(s)}+\mathrm{CO_2(g)}$. The collected carbon dioxide weighs $3.52\,\mathrm{g}$. Use $M_{\mathrm{CaCO_3}}=100.0$ and $M_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=44.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}$. Calculate collection yield and interpret the difference.

$$
n_{\mathrm{CaCO_3}}=\dfrac{10.0}{100.0}=0.100\,\mathrm{mol},\qquad
m_{\mathrm{CO_2,theor}}=0.100\cdot44.0=4.40\,\mathrm{g}.
$$
$$
\boxed{\eta=\dfrac{3.52}{4.40}\cdot100\%=80.0\%}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The carbonate-to-carbon-dioxide stoichiometric ratio is one to one. The theoretical mass indicates the gas formed under the stated assumptions, whereas collected mass concerns recovery efficiency. Since the question specifies complete decomposition, eighty percent does not measure chemical conversion: some gas was not collected. If conversion were unknown, the same final observation could result from incomplete reaction, collection losses or both. A yield definition must therefore identify which experimental amount is compared with the theoretical amount.

</div>
