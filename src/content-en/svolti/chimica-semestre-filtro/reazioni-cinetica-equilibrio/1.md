## Exercise 1

Balance complete ethanol combustion to carbon dioxide and water. Use the smallest integer coefficients and verify every element.

Assign ethanol a coefficient of one. Two carbon atoms require two carbon dioxide molecules, while six hydrogen atoms require three water molecules:
$$
\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}+a\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{CO_2}+3\mathrm{H_2O}.
$$
The oxygen balance gives
$$
1+2a=2\cdot2+3=7,\qquad a=3.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathrm{C_2H_5OH}+3\mathrm{O_2}\longrightarrow2\mathrm{CO_2}+3\mathrm{H_2O}}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Products contain seven oxygen atoms, but one already comes from ethanol. Molecular oxygen therefore supplies only the remaining six. The final check counts two carbons, six hydrogens and seven oxygens on each side; every species is neutral. Coefficients also describe mole ratios, so one mole of ethanol requires three moles of oxygen for complete combustion. Other products, such as carbon monoxide, would change the equation to be balanced. Changing formula subscripts to obtain the correct count would instead change the substances in the problem.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Mixing calcium chloride and sodium phosphate solutions forms solid calcium phosphate. Write the net ionic equation, assuming the reacting species are $\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}$ and $\mathrm{PO_4^{3-}}$, and check charge.

The solid formula is $\mathrm{Ca_3(PO_4)_2}$. Three calcium ions and two phosphate ions are therefore required:
$$
\boxed{3\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}+2\mathrm{PO_4^{3-}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ca_3(PO_4)_2(s)}}.
$$
$$
q_{\mathrm{reactants}}=3\cdot(+2)+2\cdot(-3)=0.
$$
The solid is neutral; calcium, phosphorus and oxygen counts are respectively $3$, $2$ and $8$ on each side.

<div class="spiegazione">

Sodium and chloride ions are spectators in the proposed description and cancel from the complete ionic equation. Charge is balanced by selecting the numbers of ions entering the solid, rather than altering an individual ion's charge. Counting phosphate groups conserves phosphorus and oxygen together. The equation represents the observed net process; the precipitated amount instead requires concentrations, solubility product and, in a real system, phosphate protonation equilibria. Balancing and quantitatively predicting precipitation are separate steps.

</div>
