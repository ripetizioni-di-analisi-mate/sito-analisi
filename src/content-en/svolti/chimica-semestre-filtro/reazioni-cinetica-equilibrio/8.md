## Exercise 1

At the same temperature, for matching species and consistent standard states, $\mathrm A\rightleftharpoons\mathrm B$ has $K_1=2$ and $\mathrm B\rightleftharpoons\mathrm C$ has $K_2=3$. Find constants for $\mathrm A\rightleftharpoons\mathrm C$ and $\dfrac12\mathrm C\rightleftharpoons\dfrac12\mathrm A$. Explain why constants do not add.

$$
\Delta_rG^\circ_{A\to C}=\Delta_rG^\circ_1+\Delta_rG^\circ_2=-RT\ln(K_1K_2),
$$
$$
\boxed{K_{A\to C}=2\cdot3=6}.
$$
Reversing and halving coefficients gives
$$
\boxed{K_{\dfrac12C\to\dfrac12A}=\left(\dfrac16\right)^{\dfrac{1}{2}}\simeq0.408}.
$$

![Combining successive equilibria by multiplying their constants.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/reazioni-cinetica-equilibrio/costanti-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Adding equations cancels B because it appears once as product and once as reactant. Standard free energies are additive; because they depend on the logarithm of the constant, adding logarithms corresponds to multiplying constants. Reversing direction changes the energy's sign and makes the constant reciprocal. Halving coefficients also halves energy and produces the square root. The operation changes reaction notation, not a physical composition already at equilibrium; activities satisfy all equivalent forms when exponents are transformed consistently.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In an open model at constant volume, metabolite M enters at $J=12\,\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}\,min^{-1}}$ and is consumed at $k[M]$, where $k=0.20\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}$. Reverse reaction is negligible in the model. Calculate steady concentration and its new value after $k$ increases to $0.30\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}$ at unchanged input. Is the first state chemical equilibrium?

$$
\dfrac{d[M]}{dt}=J-k[M],\qquad
[M]_{\mathrm{st}}=\dfrac{J}{k}.
$$
$$
[M]_{\mathrm{st},1}=\dfrac{12}{0.20}=60\,\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}},\qquad
[M]_{\mathrm{st},2}=\dfrac{12}{0.30}=40\,\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}}.
$$
In both cases, steady consumption flux is $12\,\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}\,min^{-1}}$ and remains externally supplied.

<div class="spiegazione">

The balance vanishes when input and consumption match. This equality concerns flows through an open system and a net conversion, so it does not establish equilibrium between a reaction and its reverse. After $k$ increases, consumption at the old concentration would be $0.30\cdot60=18$ in the same units; it exceeds input and M decreases. The decrease stops at the new steady concentration of forty, where consumption returns to twelve. The calculated ratio is set by input and kinetics; it is not a thermodynamic equilibrium constant.

</div>
