## Exercise 1

A salt MX has $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=4.00\cdot10^{-10}$ at the working temperature. Compare solubility in pure water with that in a solution already containing X at $c_0=0.0200\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$. Assume ideality, no other equilibria and excess solid. Check the common-ion approximation.

$$
s_{\mathrm{water}}=\sqrt{4.00\cdot10^{-10}}=2.00\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}.
$$
In the second solution, using normalised numerical values,
$$
s(c_0+s)=K_{\mathrm{ps}},\qquad
s\simeq\dfrac{4.00\cdot10^{-10}}{0.0200}=2.00\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}.
$$
$$
\dfrac{s}{c_0}=1.00\cdot10^{-6}\ll1,\qquad
\boxed{\dfrac{s_{\mathrm{water}}}{s}\simeq1000}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Existing X ions supply almost all their final concentration. A very small M concentration, and thus little dissolved salt, therefore suffices to reach the same solubility product. The ratio of newly supplied to initial X is one millionth, justifying neglect of $s$ beside $c_0$. The positive solution of the complete equation is approximately $1.999998\cdot10^{-8}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, confirming the rounded result. The constant has not decreased a thousandfold; the decrease concerns solubility in a solution of different composition.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A dissolution model for a defined monosodium-urate phase uses $\mathrm{NaU(s)}\rightleftharpoons\mathrm{Na^+}+\mathrm{U^-}$ and $K_{\mathrm{ps}}=4.0\cdot10^{-5}$ at the assigned temperature. Two samples have equal total urate but free activities $a_{U^-,A}=6.0\cdot10^{-4}$ and $a_{U^-,B}=2.0\cdot10^{-4}$. Both have $a_{Na^+}=0.10$. Compare saturation relative to this phase. Values are model data, not clinical thresholds.

$$
Q_A=0.10\cdot6.0\cdot10^{-4}=6.0\cdot10^{-5},\qquad
Q_B=0.10\cdot2.0\cdot10^{-4}=2.0\cdot10^{-5}.
$$
$$
\boxed{\dfrac{Q_A}{K_{\mathrm{ps}}}=1.5;\qquad\dfrac{Q_B}{K_{\mathrm{ps}}}=0.5}.
$$
A is supersaturated; B is undersaturated relative to the specified solid.

<div class="spiegazione">

Total concentration includes different forms of the substance, whereas the product requires free-anion activity. Equal totals can therefore accompany different precipitation tendencies. Sample A has a thermodynamic tendency towards precipitation, but nucleation time cannot be obtained from these numbers. The comparison does not automatically apply to solid uric acid: the neutral species belongs to a different equilibrium influenced by protonation. Specifying phase and species permits interpretation of the model without turning a saturation result into an individual clinical prediction.

</div>
