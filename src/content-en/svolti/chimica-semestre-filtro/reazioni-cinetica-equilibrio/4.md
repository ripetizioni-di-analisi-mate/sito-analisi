## Exercise 1

In a sample at constant volume and temperature, a species degrades according to $-\dfrac{d[A]}{dt}=k[A]$, with no appreciable reverse reaction. Its half-life is $30.0\,\mathrm{min}$. Determine $k$ and the fraction remaining after $90.0\,\mathrm{min}$; establish whether doubling initial concentration changes that fraction.

$$
k=\dfrac{\ln2}{30.0}=0.0231\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}.
$$
$$
\dfrac{[A](90)}{[A]_0}=e^{-k\cdot90}=\left(\dfrac12\right)^3=\dfrac18=12.5\%.
$$
The remaining fraction is independent of $[A]_0$; doubling the initial value doubles final concentration while preserving the percentage.

<div class="spiegazione">

Ninety minutes corresponds to three successive halvings. Each interval removes half the amount present at its start, so the fractions multiply. Subtracting fifty percent of the initial amount three times would produce a negative value and use a different model. Independence of half-life from initial concentration follows from the first-order law. The prediction requires unchanged temperature, volume and rate constant; it does not describe degradation with saturation, a mechanism change or continuous addition of new material.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A species decreases from $30$ to $20$ and then $10\,\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}}$ at times $0$, $5$ and $10\,\mathrm{min}$. At constant volume and temperature, compare zero- and first-order models. Calculate the constant and first half-life in the model consistent with these ideal data.

Both intervals show decreases of $10\,\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}}$. The ratios instead are $\dfrac{20}{30}=\dfrac23$ and $\dfrac{10}{20}=\dfrac12$, so they differ.
$$
k_0=\dfrac{30-20}{5}=2\,\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}\,min^{-1}},
$$
$$
[A](t)=30-2t,\qquad
\boxed{t_{1/2}=\dfrac{30}{2\cdot2}=7.5\,\mathrm{min}}.
$$
In the numerical expression, $[A]$ is in $\mathrm{\mu mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $t$ in minutes.

![Different-order curves have different time courses even when they start at the same concentration and slope.](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/reazioni-cinetica-equilibrio/cinetiche-en.svg)

<div class="spiegazione">

Equal losses over equal intervals identify a constant slope in the ideal data and support the zero-order model. First order would instead require equal ratios. Halving from thirty to fifteen requires losing fifteen concentration units, which takes seven and a half minutes at rate two. The model reaches zero at fifteen minutes and cannot extend to negative values; the law must cease to apply near exhaustion. Actual experimental data would also require comparing uncertainty and regression residuals.

</div>
