## Exercise 1

A buffer contains $0.0200\,\mathrm{mol}$ acetic acid and $0.0300\,\mathrm{mol}$ acetate in $1.00\,\mathrm L$. Calculate pH before and after adding $0.00500\,\mathrm{mol}$ HCl. Assume $pK_a=4.76$, ideal behaviour and negligible volume change.

The first calculation uses the initial ratio; the second must use updated amounts. Balance neutralisation: strong acid consumes conjugate base and produces the acid form. Check that neither component is exhausted, then apply the buffer relation again using the final ratio.

![Mole balance after HCl addition](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/acidi-basi-redox/tampone-en.svg)

$$
\mathrm{pH}_{i}=4.76+\log_{10}\dfrac{0.0300}{0.0200}=4.936
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COO^-+H^+\rightarrow CH_3COOH}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\mathrm{A^-}}&=0.0300-0.00500=0.0250\,\mathrm{mol},\\
n_{\mathrm{HA}}&=0.0200+0.00500=0.0250\,\mathrm{mol},\\
\mathrm{pH}_{f}&=4.76+\log_{10}\dfrac{0.0250}{0.0250}=4.76.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Strong acid reacts with the conjugate base, decreasing acetate moles and increasing acetic-acid moles by the same amount. Both components remain appreciable, so Henderson–Hasselbalch applies to their final ratio. pH changes from approximately $4.94$ to **$4.76$**. Equal component amounts make the logarithm zero.

pH falls, as expected on adding acid, but cannot be calculated from HCl alone as if it had been added to pure water. The strong reagent has been consumed by the buffer pair. Keeping the initial ratio would ignore that reaction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the bicarbonate buffer model at $37\,^\circ\mathrm C$, use $pK'=6.10$, $\alpha=0.030\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}\,mmHg^{-1}}$, $[\mathrm{HCO_3^-}]=24.0\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=40.0\,\mathrm{mmHg}$. Calculate pH and the dissolved $\mathrm{CO_2}$ component concentration used in the model.

The denominator must be a concentration, not a pressure alone. Use the Henry coefficient in the stated units: pressure cancels in the product, leaving a concentration comparable with bicarbonate. Only then is the ratio ready for the logarithm.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha p_{\mathrm{CO_2}}&=0.030\cdot40.0=1.20\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}},\\
\mathrm{pH}&=6.10+\log_{10}\dfrac{24.0}{1.20}\\
&=6.10+\log_{10}20=7.401\simeq7.40.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Henry's relation converts the $\mathrm{CO_2}$ partial pressure to dissolved concentration using the supplied coefficient. Numerator and denominator use the same units, giving a ratio of $20$. The apparent $pK'$ incorporates equilibrium with $\mathrm{CO_2}$; the data refer to $37\,^\circ\mathrm C$. The **model pH is $7.40$**. The relation describes a chemical equilibrium of the open system.

Do not substitute a constant for a different carbonic equilibrium or temperature. This calculation illustrates the model and its units; alone it does not describe every exchange and response in an organism.

</div>
