## Exercise 1

Balance $\mathrm{MnO_4^-+Fe^{2+}\rightarrow Mn^{2+}+Fe^{3+}}$ in acidic medium. Identify oxidising and reducing agents.

Manganese changes oxidation state while iron undergoes the opposite change. Write separate half-reactions, balancing oxygen and hydrogen with water and protons because the medium is acidic. Balance charge with electrons, then equalise electrons lost and gained before adding.

![Electron balance in the permanganate–iron system](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/acidi-basi-redox/redox-en.svg)

$$
\mathrm{MnO_4^-+8H^++5e^-\rightarrow Mn^{2+}+4H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{5Fe^{2+}\rightarrow5Fe^{3+}+5e^-}
$$

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{MnO_4^-+8H^++5Fe^{2+}\rightarrow Mn^{2+}+4H_2O+5Fe^{3+}}}
$$

$$
q_{L}=-1+8+10=17,\qquad q_{R}=2+15=17.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Manganese goes from oxidation number $+7$ to $+2$, gaining five electrons; it is part of the oxidising agent, permanganate. Each iron(II) loses one electron, making it the reducing agent and requiring its half-reaction to be multiplied by five. Eight protons and four waters balance hydrogen and oxygen. Check atoms and charge after addition: total charge is $+17$ on both sides.

In the charge checks, subscripts L and R denote left and right sides. Balancing atoms alone is insufficient: charge conservation is an independent check. No electron should remain in this overall solution reaction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Balance $\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}+I^-\rightarrow Cr^{3+}+I_2}$ in acidic medium.

Dichromate contains two chromium atoms, while iodine product is diatomic. Account for both counts before matching electrons. Each half-reaction must independently conserve atoms and charge; only after appropriate multiplication can electrons cancel and the equations be added.

$$
\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}+14H^++6e^-\rightarrow2Cr^{3+}+7H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{6I^-\rightarrow3I_2+6e^-}
$$

$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}+14H^++6I^-\rightarrow2Cr^{3+}+7H_2O+3I_2}}
$$

$$
q_{L}=-2+14-6=6,\qquad q_{R}=2\cdot3=6.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both chromium atoms go from $+6$ to $+3$, gaining six electrons in total. Six iodide ions lose those same six electrons, forming three iodine molecules. Oxygen balancing requires seven water molecules, and hydrogen balancing fourteen protons. Total charge is $+6$ on both sides, satisfying charge conservation as well.

Using three iodide ions because chromium changes by three units would forget the second chromium atom. Writing six iodine molecules would instead forget that each molecule contains two atoms. The final iodine-atom check catches that second error.

</div>
