## Exercise 1

For the cell $\mathrm{Zn+Cu^{2+}\rightarrow Zn^{2+}+Cu}$ at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$, use $E^\circ_{\mathrm{Cu^{2+}/Cu}}=0.34\,\mathrm V$ and $E^\circ_{\mathrm{Zn^{2+}/Zn}}=-0.76\,\mathrm V$. Calculate $E^\circ$, $\Delta_rG^\circ$ and potential when $a_{\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}}=0.10$ and $a_{\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}}=0.0010$. Assume pure metals and $F=96485\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}$.

Identify cathode and anode from the reaction direction. Both supplied values are reduction potentials, so subtract the anode value rather than simply adding them. Count electrons before finding Gibbs energy. For actual conditions, build the quotient from activities and apply Nernst.

![Electron circuit and salt bridge of the cell](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/acidi-basi-redox/pila-en.svg)

$$
\begin{aligned}
E^\circ&=0.34-(-0.76)=1.10\,\mathrm V,\\
\Delta_rG^\circ&=-2\cdot96485\cdot1.10=-212267\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}\\
&\simeq-212.3\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}},\\
Q&=\dfrac{0.10}{0.0010}=100,\\
E&=1.10-\dfrac{0.05916}{2}\log_{10}100\\
&=1.04084\,\mathrm V\simeq1.041\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Copper(II) is reduced at the cathode and zinc oxidised at the anode, so subtract reduction potentials in cathode-minus-anode order. The reaction transfers two electrons, giving $n=2$ in both the Gibbs relation and Nernst. Pure metals have unit activity and do not appear in $Q$. Because $Q>1$, potential is lower than standard; it remains positive, so the written reaction is spontaneous under the assigned conditions.

Gibbs energy is expressed per mole of reaction as written. Its negative sign agrees with positive potential. Changing pure-metal amounts without exhausting them does not change their activities; changing the ionic activity ratio does change potential.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $\mathrm{O_2+4H^++4e^-\rightarrow2H_2O}$, use $E^\circ=1.229\,\mathrm V$ at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$. Calculate potential at pH $7.00$, with standard oxygen pressure and unit water activity. Compare it with pH $0$.

pH gives proton activity. Protons are reactants with coefficient four, so their fourth power appears in the quotient denominator. The other assigned factors are unity. Separate constructing this quotient from numerical substitution into Nernst.

$$
Q=\dfrac{1}{a_{\mathrm{H^+}}^4},\qquad a_{\mathrm{H^+}}=10^{-7}
$$

$$
\log_{10}Q=28
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E&=1.229-\dfrac{0.05916}{4}\cdot28\\
&=1.229-0.41412=0.81488\,\mathrm V\simeq0.815\,\mathrm V.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
E(\mathrm{pH}=0)=1.229\,\mathrm V.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The half-reaction consumes four protons per four electrons, placing the fourth power of proton activity in the quotient denominator. At pH $7$, the quotient logarithm is $28$ and the Nernst correction lowers potential by $0.41412\,\mathrm V$. **Potential is approximately $0.815\,\mathrm V$**. This comparison explains why a chemical standard potential at unit proton activity differs from a value evaluated at fixed pH.

The correction does not arise from changing electron count: the half-reaction is unchanged. System composition changes. If oxygen pressure also differed from standard, its contribution would have to be included in the quotient rather than ignored.

</div>
