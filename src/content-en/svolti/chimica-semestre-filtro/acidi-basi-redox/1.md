## Exercise 1

Identify the acid, base and conjugate pairs in the reaction between ammonia and water.

First compare reactants and products while following one proton. Ammonia goes from three hydrogens to four, while water goes from two to one. Separating these changes conceptually identifies donor and acceptor; it does not imply that a proton remains free in water.

$$
\mathrm{NH_3+H_2O\rightleftharpoons NH_4^++OH^-}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{H_2O}&\mathrm{\longrightarrow OH^-+H^+},\\
\mathrm{NH_3+H^+}&\mathrm{\longrightarrow NH_4^+}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Water donates a proton to ammonia, so it is the **Brønsted–Lowry acid** and $\mathrm{OH^-}$ is its conjugate base. Ammonia accepts that proton, so it is the base and $\mathrm{NH_4^+}$ is its conjugate acid. The pairs are therefore $\mathrm{H_2O/OH^-}$ and $\mathrm{NH_4^+/NH_3}$. Accepting a proton raises the charge by one unit.

Check charge as well: the reactants are neutral and the products have opposite charges that cancel. Pairing ammonia with hydroxide would be wrong because they do not differ by just one proton.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the reactions in which dihydrogen phosphate $\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-}$ acts first as an acid and then as a base towards water.

Construct two separate conceptual experiments. In the first, remove a proton from phosphate and transfer it to water; in the second, take a proton from water and add it to phosphate. Conserve atoms and check charge before assigning acid and base roles.

![Conjugate phosphate forms](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/acidi-basi-redox/coppie-en.svg)

$$
\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-+H_2O\rightleftharpoons HPO_4^{2-}+H_3O^+}
$$

$$
\mathrm{H_2PO_4^-+H_2O\rightleftharpoons H_3PO_4+OH^-}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first reaction the ion loses a proton and forms $\mathrm{HPO_4^{2-}}$, so it acts as an acid. In the second it accepts a proton from water and forms $\mathrm{H_3PO_4}$, so it acts as a base. Its ability to do both justifies the term **amphiprotic**. The reactions have different constants: being able to write both does not mean that they proceed to the same extent.

Total charge remains minus one in each equation. Predicting which behaviour dominates in an actual solution requires constants and conditions; merely noticing hydrogen atoms in the formula is insufficient.

</div>
