## Exercise 1

Identify the Lewis acid and base when $\mathrm{BF_3}$ and $\mathrm{NH_3}$ form an adduct.

No proton is transferred here. Follow an electron pair: nitrogen has a lone pair and boron can accept it. Draw the arrow from donor to acceptor, then assign the roles using the Lewis definition.

$$
\mathrm{F_3B+{:}NH_3\longrightarrow F_3B\!\leftarrow\!NH_3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The nitrogen atom in ammonia has a lone pair. Boron in $\mathrm{BF_3}$ can accept it to form a new bond, making $\mathrm{BF_3}$ the **Lewis acid** and $\mathrm{NH_3}$ the base. The coordinate-bond arrow points from the electron-pair donor to the acceptor.

Donating the pair is not the net electron loss of a redox half-reaction: the pair participates in the bond formed. A species is not a Lewis acid merely because it contains hydrogen, nor excluded because it lacks hydrogen.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify the species in $\mathrm{H^++OH^-\rightarrow H_2O}$ using both Brønsted–Lowry and Lewis descriptions.

The two definitions describe the same event from different viewpoints. The first follows the proton; the second follows the electron pair that makes the new bond. Use the abbreviated proton formula without confusing it with its actual solvation.

$$
\mathrm{H^++{:}OH^-\longrightarrow H_2O}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Hydroxide accepts a proton, so it is a Brønsted–Lowry base. The new O–H bond uses an oxygen lone pair, so hydroxide is also a Lewis base. The proton is bound by accepting that pair and is the acid in both descriptions. In water the proton is solvated; $\mathrm{H^+}$ is an abbreviation.

Initial charges sum to zero, consistent with neutral water as product. Both descriptions applying here does not mean that every Lewis reaction transfers protons: the preceding adduct is a counterexample.

</div>
