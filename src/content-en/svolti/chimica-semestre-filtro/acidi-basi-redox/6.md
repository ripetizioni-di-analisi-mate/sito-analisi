## Exercise 1

Calculate pH for $\mathrm{NH_4Cl}$ at $0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$, given $K_b(\mathrm{NH_3})=1.80\cdot10^{-5}$. Assume $K_w=10^{-14}$, an ideal solution and negligible chloride hydrolysis.

Dissolving separates the salt ions but does not by itself determine pH. Identify the ion reacting with water: ammonium is ammonia's conjugate acid. The given constant belongs to the base, so first convert it to the relevant acid constant, then set up dissociation.

$$
\mathrm{NH_4^++H_2O\rightleftharpoons NH_3+H_3O^+}
$$

$$
K_a(\mathrm{NH_4^+})=\dfrac{10^{-14}}{1.80\cdot10^{-5}}=5.556\cdot10^{-10}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]&\simeq\sqrt{K_aC}=7.454\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}},\\
\mathrm{pH}&\simeq5.13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Ammonium is the conjugate acid of ammonia, so its acid constant is $\dfrac{K_w}{K_b}$. The dissociated concentration is about $0.00745\%$ of the initial concentration, consistent with $C-x\simeq C$. Acidity comes from ammonium; chloride remains a spectator in the stated model. The quadratic calculation gives $\mathrm{pH}=5.12765$, agreeing with the approximation.

A salt can therefore produce an acidic solution even though its simple dissolution equation does not directly release protons. Hydronium arises from the subsequent reaction with water. Pure water's contribution is small compared with the hydronium obtained.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate pH for $0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ sodium acetate at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$, with $K_a(\mathrm{CH_3COOH})=1.80\cdot10^{-5}$. Assume $K_w=10^{-14}$, an ideal solution and negligible sodium hydrolysis.

Here the active ion is a conjugate base. Write its reaction with water to identify the hydroxide produced and derive its basic constant from the acetic-acid value. After calculating hydroxide, convert pOH to pH, keeping temperature explicit.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COO^-+H_2O\rightleftharpoons CH_3COOH+OH^-}
$$

$$
K_b(\mathrm{CH_3COO^-})=\dfrac{10^{-14}}{1.80\cdot10^{-5}}=5.556\cdot10^{-10}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\mathrm{OH^-}]&\simeq\sqrt{K_bC}=7.454\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}},\\
\mathrm{pOH}&\simeq5.13,\\
\mathrm{pH}&\simeq14.00-5.13=8.87.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Acetate accepts a proton from water and forms hydroxide, so the solution is basic. Use the constant of acetic acid's conjugate base. The reacted concentration is very small compared with $0.100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, making the square-root formula applicable. The quadratic calculation gives $\mathrm{pH}=8.87235$, hence **pH is approximately $8.87$**.

The numerical similarity to the preceding exercise comes from the chosen constants, not a rule that all acidic and basic salts have symmetric effects. A different acid or base would require recalculation.

</div>
