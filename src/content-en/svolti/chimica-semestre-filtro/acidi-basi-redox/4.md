## Exercise 1

An acetic-acid solution has analytical concentration $C=0.0100\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $K_a=1.80\cdot10^{-5}$ at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$. Calculate pH in the ideal dilute model, neglecting water autoprotolysis.

We cannot immediately use acid concentration as hydronium concentration: dissociation is partial. Introduce an unknown for the reacted amount and express equilibrium concentrations using material conservation. Then substitute them into the equilibrium constant and solve the quadratic, choosing the physically admissible root.

$$
x=[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]=[\mathrm{CH_3COO^-}],\qquad
[\mathrm{CH_3COOH}]=C-x
$$

$$
\dfrac{x^2}{0.0100-x}=1.80\cdot10^{-5}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=\dfrac{-1.80\cdot10^{-5}+\sqrt{(1.80\cdot10^{-5})^2+4(1.80\cdot10^{-5})(0.0100)}}{2}\\
&=4.154\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}},\\
\mathrm{pH}&=-\log_{10}(4.154\cdot10^{-4})=3.382\simeq3.38.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dissociation forms equal amounts of hydronium and acetate, so both concentrations are $x$. Remaining acid has concentration $C-x$. The positive equilibrium root gives the physically admissible concentration. The ratio $\dfrac{x}{C}$ is about $0.0415$, corresponding to $4.15\%$ dissociation; the quadratic calculation avoids replacing $C-x$ with $C$. The hydronium concentration is much greater than in pure water, consistent with neglecting autoprotolysis.

The other root is negative and cannot represent a concentration. pH is higher than for a strong acid at the same concentration, confirming the direction of incomplete dissociation without confusing strength with concentration.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the pH of ammonia at $0.0250\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$, with $K_b=1.80\cdot10^{-5}$ and $pK_w=14.00$. Use the ideal dilute model and neglect autoprotolysis.

First identify the ion produced by the base: hydroxide, not hydronium. The balance has the same mathematical structure as for a weak acid, but uses the basic constant, and the first logarithm gives pOH. Keeping this sequence clear avoids confusing the two measures.

$$
\mathrm{NH_3+H_2O\rightleftharpoons NH_4^++OH^-}
$$

$$
\dfrac{x^2}{0.0250-x}=1.80\cdot10^{-5},\qquad x=[\mathrm{OH^-}]
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=6.619\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}},\\
\mathrm{pOH}&=3.179,\\
\mathrm{pH}&=14.00-3.179=10.821\simeq10.82.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Ammonia accepts a proton from water and produces one hydroxide ion per ammonium ion, so $x$ represents both product concentrations. Solve using $K_b$, then first calculate pOH. **pH is $10.82$**, since the solution is basic and at $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$ pH and pOH sum to $14.00$. About $2.65\%$ has reacted.

Most ammonia remains unprotonated, consistent with the equilibrium result. A weak base need not have pH close to neutrality: total concentration and the constant also influence the result.

</div>
