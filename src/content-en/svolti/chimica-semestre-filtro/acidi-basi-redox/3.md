## Exercise 1

Calculate the pH of aqueous HCl at $4.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$. Assume complete dissociation and approximate activities by reduced concentrations.

Start with the reaction: a fully dissociated monoprotic acid supplies one mole of hydronium per mole of solute. Its concentration is high enough compared with water's contribution to neglect the latter. After making this chemical choice, apply the logarithm; pH itself has no unit.

![Limit of neglecting water autoprotolysis](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/acidi-basi-redox/diluizione-en.svg)

$$
\mathrm{HCl+H_2O\rightarrow H_3O^++Cl^-}
$$

$$
[\mathrm{H_3O^+}]\simeq4.0\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}
$$

$$
\mathrm{pH}=-\log_{10}(4.0\cdot10^{-3})=2.398\simeq2.40.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each mole of HCl produces one mole of hydronium. Because acid concentration is much greater than $10^{-7}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, water's contribution is negligible. The logarithm applies to the numerical ratio between concentration and $1\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$, giving **pH $2.40$**.

As an order-of-magnitude check, hydronium lies between one thousandth and one hundredth of a mole per litre, so pH must lie between two and three. Applying the same approximation at extreme dilution would instead require including water.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate the pH of aqueous $\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2}$ at $1.50\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ and $25\,^\circ\mathrm C$. Assume complete dissolution and dissociation, an ideal dilute solution and $pK_w=14.00$.

Analytical concentration describes the compound, not directly its hydroxide ions. Read the dissociation coefficient and obtain hydroxide concentration. Its logarithm gives pOH; converting to pH finally requires the water ion product at the stated temperature.

$$
\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2\rightarrow Ca^{2+}+2OH^-}
$$

$$
[\mathrm{OH^-}]=2\cdot1.50\cdot10^{-3}=3.00\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{pOH}&=-\log_{10}(3.00\cdot10^{-3})=2.523,\\
\mathrm{pH}&=14.00-2.523=11.477\simeq11.48.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula contains two hydroxide groups, so $\mathrm{OH^-}$ concentration is twice the solute concentration. Obtain pOH from this concentration. At the stated temperature, $\mathrm{pH+pOH}=14.00$, giving **pH $11.48$**. The factor of two comes from dissociation stoichiometry.

The result is basic, as expected. Using the compound concentration directly would underestimate hydroxide; confusing pH with pOH would even describe the solution as acidic. Complete dissolution is a given assumption, not a universal property of every hydroxide.

</div>
