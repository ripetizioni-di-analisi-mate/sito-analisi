## Exercise 1

Prepare $250\,\mathrm{mL}$ of a $0.160\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ solution using a $2.00\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ stock. Find the stock aliquot volume, assuming no reactions or losses.

$$
c_1V_1=c_2V_2
$$

$$
V_1=\dfrac{c_2V_2}{c_1}
=\dfrac{0.160\cdot0.250}{2.00}\,\mathrm L
=0.0200\,\mathrm L=20.0\,\mathrm{mL}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

All solute in the final solution comes from the original aliquot. Amount conservation gives $c_1V_1=c_2V_2$. The calculated stock portion of $20.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ is brought to the final volume of $250\,\mathrm{mL}$; this does not require an added solvent volume of exactly $230\,\mathrm{mL}$. As a check, the stock is more concentrated, so its aliquot must occupy less than the final volume. Substituting the aliquot back into the solute balance reproduces the required final amount. If preparation involved precipitation or chemical consumption of the solute, this simple dissolved-species balance would need modification.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Mix $20.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ of $0.500\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ glucose solution with $80.0\,\mathrm{mL}$ of $0.125\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$ glucose solution. Assume additive volumes and no reactions or losses. Calculate final concentration.

$$
n_1=0.500\cdot0.0200=0.0100\,\mathrm{mol},\qquad
n_2=0.125\cdot0.0800=0.0100\,\mathrm{mol}
$$

$$
c_f=\dfrac{n_1+n_2}{V_1+V_2}
=\dfrac{0.0200\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.1000\,\mathrm L}
=0.200\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each solution contributes concentration times volume in litres. These unequal volumes happen to contain equal solute amounts, so their total amount is $0.0200\,\mathrm{mol}$. Additivity is explicitly assumed, making final volume $0.1000\,\mathrm L$. A simple arithmetic mean of concentrations would be incorrect because it gives the small and large portions equal weight. The final concentration must lie between both initial values and closer to the more dilute solution, which supplies the larger volume. If measured final volume differed because of contraction, the same conserved amount would instead be divided by that measurement. Conservation of solute amount and volume additivity are therefore separate assumptions.

</div>
