## Exercise 1

![Linear Henry relation at fixed temperature, with defined axes](/figure/chimica-semestre-filtro/soluzioni-proprieta-colligative/henry-en.svg)

An ideal gas has mole fraction $25.0\%$ in a mixture at total pressure $1.00\,\mathrm{atm}$. It equilibrates with water at a temperature where $k_H=1.20\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}\,atm^{-1}}$, using $c=k_Hp$. Calculate dissolved concentration, assuming dilute solution and no gas reactions.

$$
p=xp_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.250\cdot1.00=0.250\,\mathrm{atm}
$$

$$
c=k_Hp=1.20\cdot10^{-3}\cdot0.250
=3.00\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Dalton's law first obtains the component's partial pressure. Henry's relation then uses precisely the partial pressure $0.250\,\mathrm{atm}$, not total mixture pressure. Using total pressure would overestimate this component’s dissolved concentration. The constant has inverse pressure in its units, so multiplication cancels the pressure unit and leaves amount per solution volume. No significant reaction means the calculated concentration refers to physically dissolved molecular gas. Using an inverse Henry convention would require a reciprocal constant with reciprocal units. Temperature is fixed by the supplied coefficient; changing temperature would require another value. The two-step calculation therefore separates gas-mixture composition from gas–liquid partitioning.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A humid gas mixture has total pressure $760\,\mathrm{mmHg}$ and water-vapour pressure $47\,\mathrm{mmHg}$. Oxygen represents $21.0\%$ of dry-gas moles. Treat the mixture as ideal and calculate oxygen partial pressure.

$$
p_{\mathrm{dry}}=760-47=713\,\mathrm{mmHg}
$$

$$
p_{\mathrm{O_2}}=0.210\cdot713=149.73\,\mathrm{mmHg}
\simeq150\,\mathrm{mmHg}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The oxygen fraction is normalised to dry gases alone. Water-vapour pressure must therefore be removed, giving $713\,\mathrm{mmHg}$ of dry-gas pressure and approximately $150\,\mathrm{mmHg}$ of oxygen partial pressure. Using total pressure would incorrectly assign oxygen a share of the water contribution. After calculating oxygen pressure, the remaining dry-gas pressure belongs to the other dry components; adding those contributions and water recovers total pressure. The specified vapour pressure fixes the moisture correction without requiring an additional temperature estimate. If the oxygen fraction instead referred to the whole humid mixture, total pressure would be the appropriate multiplier; identifying the reference mixture is the essential first step.

</div>
