## Exercise 1

Determine the level curves of
$$
f(x, y) = 2x - y + 1 .
$$

$$
2x - y + 1 = k
$$
$$
y = 2x + 1 - k
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The level curve $k$ is the solution set of the equation $f(x, y) = k$, so one solves $2x - y + 1 = k$ for $y$. For every $k \in \mathbb{R}$ one obtains the line $y = 2x + 1 - k$: the level curves are the **lines** with slope $2$, one for each value of $k$, all **parallel** to one another. As $k$ increases the line shifts downward, since the intercept $1 - k$ decreases. The level curves of a first-degree function are *always* parallel lines, because the graph of the function is a plane.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the level curves of
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4x .
$$

$$
x^2 + y^2 - 4x = k
$$
$$
(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = k + 4
$$

- $k > -4$: circle with center $(2, 0)$ and radius $\sqrt{k + 4}$
- $k = -4$: the point $(2, 0)$
- $k < -4$: empty set

::grafico{curve="2+cos(t),sin(t),0,6.29;2+2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29;2+3*cos(t),3*sin(t),0,6.29" punti="2,0,pieno,k = −4" etichette="2.08,1.05,k = −3;2.08,2.05,k = 0;2.08,3.05,k = 5" xmin=-1.6 xmax=5.6 ymin=-3.3 ymax=3.6 uguale=1 titolo="Level curves of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4x$: circles with center $(2, 0)$ and radii $1, 2, 3$ for $k = -3, 0, 5$; for $k = -4$ the center alone"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation $x^2 + y^2 - 4x = k$ is rewritten by **completing the square** in the variable $x$: since $x^2 - 4x = (x - 2)^2 - 4$, the equation becomes $(x - 2)^2 + y^2 = k + 4$. The left-hand side is a sum of squares, so the equation has solutions *only if* $k + 4 \geq 0$. For $k > -4$ the level curve is the **circle** with center $(2, 0)$ and radius $\sqrt{k + 4}$; for $k = -4$ the sum of squares is zero and only the point $(2, 0)$ remains; for $k < -4$ the level set is empty. The value $k = -4$ is the **minimum** of the function, attained at the common center of the circles.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the level curves of
$$
f(x, y) = e^{\,y - x^2} .
$$

$$
e^{\,y - x^2} = k, \qquad k > 0
$$
$$
y - x^2 = \ln k
$$
$$
y = x^2 + \ln k
$$

- $k > 0$: parabola $y = x^2 + \ln k$
- $k \leq 0$: empty set

::grafico{f="x^2-1;x^2;x^2+1" etichette="0.1,-1.45,ln k = −1;0.1,-0.35,ln k = 0;0.1,0.65,ln k = 1" xmin=-2.5 xmax=2.5 ymin=-1.8 ymax=4.5 titolo="Level curves of $f(x, y) = e^{\,y - x^2}$: the parabolas $y = x^2 + \ln k$ for $k = \dfrac{1}{e}, 1, e$, that is, $\ln k = -1, 0, 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The exponential takes *only* strictly positive values, so for $k \leq 0$ the equation $f(x, y) = k$ has no solutions and the level set is **empty**. For $k > 0$ one applies the logarithm to both sides, an operation that is legitimate because both sides are positive, and obtains $y - x^2 = \ln k$, that is, the parabola $y = x^2 + \ln k$. The level curves are therefore the **vertical translates** of the parabola $y = x^2$: for $k = 1$ one obtains the parabola $y = x^2$, since $\ln 1 = 0$, for $k > 1$ the translates upward and for $0 < k < 1$ the translates downward, since in that case $\ln k < 0$.

</div>
