## Exercise 1

Sketch the set
$$
D=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:\ |x|\le y\le 3\}.
$$

$$
|x|\le y\le 3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
y\ge x,\\
y\ge -x,\\
y\le 3.
\end{cases}
$$

$$
D=\{(x,y): -3\le x\le 3,\ |x|\le y\le 3\}.
$$

::grafico{area="3;abs(x);-3;3" punti="-3,3,pieno;0,0,pieno;3,3,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="The region common to the three half-planes is the triangle with vertices $(-3,3)$, $(0,0)$ and $(3,3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $|x|=\max\{x,-x\}$, the condition $|x|\le y$ requires the point to lie above both lines $y=x$ and $y=-x$. Retain the part below the line $y=3$. The three lines intersect pairwise at the **vertices** $(0,0)$, $(-3,3)$ and $(3,3)$. The inequalities are non-strict, so the triangle includes its sides and vertices.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Sketch the intersection of the closed disks
$$
D_1=\{(x,y):(x+1)^2+y^2\le 4\},\qquad
D_2=\{(x,y):(x-1)^2+y^2\le 4\}
$$
and determine the boundary of $D_1\cap D_2$.

Subtracting the two equations of the circles gives $4x=0$. Substituting $x=0$ into either equation gives $y^2=3$, so the circles intersect at $(0,\pm\sqrt{3})$.

On $\partial D_1$, $(x-1)^2+y^2=4-4x$. The point also belongs to $D_2$ if $x\ge 0$. On $\partial D_2$, $(x+1)^2+y^2=4+4x$, so the point also belongs to $D_1$ if $x\le 0$. The two boundary arcs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_1(t)&=(-1+2\cos t,\,2\sin t),&
-\frac{\pi}{3}\le t\le\frac{\pi}{3},\\[6pt]
\gamma_2(t)&=(1+2\cos t,\,2\sin t),&
\frac{2\pi}{3}\le t\le\frac{4\pi}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{area="sqrt(4-(x-1)^2);-sqrt(4-(x-1)^2);-1;0|sqrt(4-(x+1)^2);-sqrt(4-(x+1)^2);0;1" curve="-1+2*cos(t),2*sin(t),-1.04719755,1.04719755;1+2*cos(t),2*sin(t),2.0943951,4.1887902" punti="0,1.73205,pieno;0,-1.73205,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.4 uguale=1 titolo="The intersection of the disks and the two arcs that form its boundary"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **boundary of the intersection** consists of the parts of the two circles that lie inside the other disk. A point interior to both disks is also interior to their intersection. Parametrize the circles using cosine and sine, and restrict the parameter interval according to the sign of $x$. The resulting arcs meet at the two points common to the circles.

</div>
