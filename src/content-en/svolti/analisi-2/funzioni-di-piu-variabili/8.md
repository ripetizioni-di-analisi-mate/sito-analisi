## Exercise 1

Sketch the set $D=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:\ |x|\le y\le 3\}$.

$$
|x|\le y\le 3\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
y\ge x,\\
y\ge -x,\\
y\le 3.
\end{cases}
$$

::grafico{area="3;abs(x);-3;3" punti="-3,3,pieno;0,0,pieno;3,3,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-1 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="The triangle with vertices $(-3,3)$, $(0,0)$ and $(3,3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $|x|=\max\{x,-x\}$, the point lies above both lines $y=x$ and $y=-x$. Retain the part below $y=3$. The intersections give the **vertices** $(0,0)$, $(-3,3)$ and $(3,3)$. Each inequality includes equality, so the sides and vertices belong to the region.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Sketch the intersection of the closed disks
$$
D_1=\{(x,y):(x+1)^2+y^2\le 4\},\qquad
D_2=\{(x,y):(x-1)^2+y^2\le 4\}
$$
and find the boundary of $D_1\cap D_2$.

Subtracting the equations of the circles gives $4x=0$. At $x=0$ either equation gives $y^2=3$, so the circles meet at $(0,\pm\sqrt{3})$.

On $\partial D_1$, $(x-1)^2+y^2=4-4x$, so a point also belongs to $D_2$ if $x\ge 0$. On $\partial D_2$, $(x+1)^2+y^2=4+4x$, so a point also belongs to $D_1$ if $x\le 0$. The two boundary arcs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_1(t)&=(-1+2\cos t,\,2\sin t),&
-\frac{\pi}{3}\le t\le\frac{\pi}{3},\\[6pt]
\gamma_2(t)&=(1+2\cos t,\,2\sin t),&
\frac{2\pi}{3}\le t\le\frac{4\pi}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{area="sqrt(4-(x-1)^2);-sqrt(4-(x-1)^2);-1;0|sqrt(4-(x+1)^2);-sqrt(4-(x+1)^2);0;1" curve="-1+2*cos(t),2*sin(t),-1.04719755,1.04719755;1+2*cos(t),2*sin(t),2.0943951,4.1887902" punti="0,1.73205,pieno;0,-1.73205,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.4 uguale=1 titolo="The intersection of the disks and its two boundary arcs"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **boundary of the intersection** consists of the part of each circle that lies in the other disk. Points interior to both disks are interior to their intersection. Parametrize the circles using cosine and sine, then restrict the parameter according to the sign of $x$. The resulting arcs meet at the two points shared by the circles.

</div>
