## Exercise 1

Compute the limit
$$
\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^2 y^2}{x^2 + y^2} .
$$

$$
\frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \leq 1
$$
$$
0 \leq \frac{x^2 y^2}{x^2 + y^2}
= \frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \cdot y^2
\leq y^2 \xrightarrow[(x, y) \to (0, 0)]{} 0
$$
$$
\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^2 y^2}{x^2 + y^2} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression is written as the product of the ratio $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2}$ and the factor $y^2$. Since $x^2 \leq x^2 + y^2$, the ratio lies **between** $0$ and $1$, so the whole expression lies between $0$ and $y^2$. The upper bound $y^2$ tends to $0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$, therefore by the **comparison theorem** the limit **exists and equals** $0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the limit
$$
\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} .
$$

$$
|xy| \leq \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2}
$$
$$
\left| \frac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} \right|
\leq \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2 \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}
= \frac{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}{2} \xrightarrow[(x, y) \to (0, 0)]{} 0
$$
$$
\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is bounded above using the inequality $|xy| \leq \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2}$, which follows from expanding $(|x| - |y|)^2 \geq 0$. The absolute value of the fraction is therefore bounded above by $\dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$, and simplifying $x^2 + y^2$ against its square root leaves $\dfrac{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}{2}$. This quantity is **half the distance** from the origin, so it tends to $0$ as $(x, y) \to (0, 0)$. By the **comparison theorem** the limit **equals** $0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the limit
$$
\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2} \, \sin\!\left( \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right) .
$$

$$
\left| \sin\!\left( \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right) \right| \leq 1,
\qquad
\frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \leq 1
$$
$$
\left| \frac{x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2} \, \sin\!\left( \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right) \right|
\leq \frac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2} \cdot |y| \cdot 1
\leq |y| \xrightarrow[(x, y) \to (0, 0)]{} 0
$$
$$
\lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 0)} \frac{x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2} \, \sin\!\left( \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} \right) = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $\sin\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x^2 + y^2}\right)$ has no limit at the origin, since its argument tends to $+\infty$ and the sine keeps oscillating; the sine is, however, **bounded** between $-1$ and $1$ at every point. The other factor is bounded as in the previous exercises: the ratio $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2}$ is bounded above by $1$, so $\left|\dfrac{x^2 y}{x^2 + y^2}\right| \leq |y| \to 0$. The product of a bounded function and an infinitesimal one is **infinitesimal**, therefore the limit **exists and equals** $0$, even though one of the two factors has *no* limit.

</div>
