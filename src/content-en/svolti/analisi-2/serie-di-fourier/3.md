## Exercise 1

Find the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = \pi - x$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$.

$$
a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} (\pi - x)\, dx = \frac{1}{\pi} \cdot 2\pi^2 = 2\pi
$$
$$
a_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \pi \cos(nx)\, dx - \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} x \cos(nx)\, dx = 0 - 0 = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n &= -\frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} x \sin(nx)\, dx \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{1}{\pi} \left( \left[ -\frac{x \cos(nx)}{n} \right]_{-\pi}^{\pi} + \frac{1}{n} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} \cos(nx)\, dx \right) \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{1}{\pi} \cdot \left( -\frac{2\pi\,(-1)^n}{n} + 0 \right)
= \frac{2\,(-1)^{n}}{n}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\pi - x \sim \pi + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2\,(-1)^{n}}{n} \sin(nx)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is the sum of the constant $\pi$ and the odd function $-x$, therefore it is convenient to compute the coefficients by separating the two terms using the **linearity of the integral**. The constant contributes *only* to $a_0$, which equals $2\pi$, hence the constant term of the series is $\pi$. The $a_n$ with $n \geq 1$ are zero: the integral of $\cos(nx)$ over one period is zero, and $x\cos(nx)$ is an odd function on a symmetric interval. Only $-x$ contributes to the $b_n$: integration by parts produces the boundary term $-\dfrac{x\cos(nx)}{n}$, which evaluated between $-\pi$ and $\pi$ gives $-\dfrac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n}$, while the remaining integral of $\cos(nx)$ is zero. With the minus sign in front we obtain $b_n = \dfrac{2(-1)^n}{n}$, that is, the **opposite** of the coefficients of the sawtooth wave $f(x) = x$ computed in the theory.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x + x^2$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi)$.

$$
x + x^2 = \underbrace{x}_{\text{odd}} + \underbrace{x^2}_{\text{even}}
$$
$$
a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} x^2\, dx = \frac{2\pi^2}{3},
\qquad
a_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} x^2 \cos(nx)\, dx = \frac{4\,(-1)^n}{n^2}
$$
$$
b_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} x \sin(nx)\, dx
= \frac{2}{\pi} \left[ -\frac{x \cos(nx)}{n} \right]_{0}^{\pi}
= \frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n}
$$
$$
x + x^2 \sim \frac{\pi^2}{3} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{4\,(-1)^n}{n^2} \cos(nx) + \frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \sin(nx) \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function has no symmetries, but it decomposes into the **odd part** $x$ and the **even part** $x^2$. Since the product of an odd function and $\cos(nx)$ is odd, only $x^2$ contributes to the $a_n$; for the same reason only $x$ contributes to the $b_n$. The coefficients of the two pieces are computed by parts on the interval $[0, \pi]$: for $x^2$ two integrations are needed, carried out in full in the example in the theory, and the result is $a_n = \dfrac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}$; for $x$ one is enough, and the boundary term leaves $-\dfrac{\pi \cos(n\pi)}{n}$, from which $b_n = \dfrac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$, since the remaining integral of $\cos(nx)$ over $[0, \pi]$ is zero. The final series is the sum of the two series, therefore it contains *both* cosines *and* sines.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x^3 - \pi^2 x$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi]$, and determine whether the convergence is uniform on $\mathbb{R}$.

$$
f \text{ odd} \implies a_n = 0, \qquad
b_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} (x^3 - \pi^2 x) \sin(nx)\, dx
$$
$$
\int_{0}^{\pi} x \sin(nx)\, dx = \frac{\pi\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{0}^{\pi} x^3 \sin(nx)\, dx
&= \left[ -\frac{x^3 \cos(nx)}{n} \right]_{0}^{\pi} + \frac{3}{n} \int_{0}^{\pi} x^2 \cos(nx)\, dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{\pi^3 (-1)^{n+1}}{n} + \frac{3}{n} \cdot \frac{2\pi (-1)^n}{n^2}
= \frac{\pi^3 (-1)^{n+1}}{n} + \frac{6\pi (-1)^n}{n^3}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
b_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \left( \frac{\pi^3 (-1)^{n+1}}{n} + \frac{6\pi (-1)^n}{n^3} - \pi^2 \cdot \frac{\pi (-1)^{n+1}}{n} \right)
= \frac{12\,(-1)^n}{n^3}
$$
$$
x^3 - \pi^2 x \sim \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{12\,(-1)^n}{n^3} \sin(nx),
\qquad \text{uniform convergence on } \mathbb{R}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-2*pi*floor((x+pi)/(2*pi)))*(x-2*pi*floor((x+pi)/(2*pi)))*(x-2*pi*floor((x+pi)/(2*pi)))-pi*pi*(x-2*pi*floor((x+pi)/(2*pi)));-12*sin(x)" xmin=-7 xmax=7.3 ymin=-12.5 ymax=12.5 titolo="Periodic extension of $x^3 - \pi^2 x$, continuous because it equals $0$ at $\pm\pi$, and the partial sum $-12\sin x$ (dashed), already almost overlapping"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is odd, being a sum of odd powers of $x$, therefore the series contains **only sines**. The integral of $x \sin(nx)$ is computed by parts as in the previous exercises. For $x^3 \sin(nx)$ we integrate by parts once and the remaining integral contains $x^2 \cos(nx)$, whose value $\dfrac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}$ on $[0, \pi]$ was obtained in the example in the theory on the series of $x^2$ (the coefficient $a_n = \dfrac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}$ is equivalent to $\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x^2\cos(nx)\,dx = \dfrac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}$). In the final combination the terms in $\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$ coming from $x^3$ and from $-\pi^2 x$ cancel, hence *only* the term in $\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n^3}$ survives and we obtain $b_n = \dfrac{12(-1)^n}{n^3}$. Since $f(\pi) = f(-\pi) = 0$, the periodic extension is continuous, and the function is piecewise smooth; therefore the **uniform convergence theorem** applies and the series **converges uniformly** to $f$ on $\mathbb{R}$, in agreement with the convergence of the series of absolute values $\sum \dfrac{12}{n^3}$.

</div>
