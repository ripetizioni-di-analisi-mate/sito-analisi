## Exercise 1

Find the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
-1 & \text{if } -\pi \leq x < 0 \\
1 & \text{if } 0 \leq x < \pi
\end{cases}
$$

$$
f \text{ odd} \implies a_n = 0, \qquad
b_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin(nx)\, dx
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n &= \frac{2}{\pi} \left[ -\frac{\cos(nx)}{n} \right]_{0}^{\pi}
= \frac{2}{n\pi} \bigl( 1 - \cos(n\pi) \bigr) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2 \bigl( 1 - (-1)^n \bigr)}{n\pi}
= \begin{cases}
\dfrac{4}{n\pi} & \text{if } n \text{ is odd} \\[8pt]
0 & \text{if } n \text{ is even}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f(x) \sim \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{4}{(2k+1)\pi} \sin\bigl( (2k+1)x \bigr)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function equals $-1$ to the left of the origin and $1$ to the right, therefore it is **odd** (the value at $x = 0$ does *not* affect the coefficients, which are integrals). Since the function is odd, all the $a_n$ are zero and the series contains **only sines**, with $b_n$ given by twice the integral over $[0, \pi]$. On $[0, \pi]$ the function equals $1$, hence what remains is the integral of $\sin(nx)$, which equals $\dfrac{1 - \cos(n\pi)}{n}$. Since $\cos(n\pi) = (-1)^n$, the coefficient is zero for even $n$ and equals $\dfrac{4}{n\pi}$ for odd $n$; in the final series *only* the odd harmonics survive, written with the index $n = 2k + 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } -\pi \leq x < 0 \\
1 & \text{if } 0 \leq x < \pi
\end{cases}
$$

$$
a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} 1\, dx = 1
$$
$$
a_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \cos(nx)\, dx
= \frac{1}{\pi} \left[ \frac{\sin(nx)}{n} \right]_{0}^{\pi} = 0
\qquad (n \geq 1)
$$
$$
b_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin(nx)\, dx
= \frac{1 - (-1)^n}{n\pi}
$$
$$
f(x) \sim \frac{1}{2} + \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{2}{(2k+1)\pi} \sin\bigl( (2k+1)x \bigr)
$$

::grafico{f="(1+sign(sin(x)))/2;0.5;0.5+2/pi*(sin(x)+sin(3*x)/3+sin(5*x)/5)" xmin=-7 xmax=7.3 ymin=-0.35 ymax=1.3 titolo="Periodic extension ($0$ on $[-\pi, 0)$, $1$ on $[0, \pi)$), the mean $\dfrac{1}{2}$ (dashed) and the partial sum up to $\sin(5x)$ (red), which oscillates around the function"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is *neither* even nor odd, therefore all the coefficients are computed with the integrals over $[-\pi, \pi]$; since the function is zero on $[-\pi, 0)$, every integral reduces to the interval $[0, \pi]$. The coefficient $a_0$ equals $1$, therefore the constant term of the series is $\dfrac{a_0}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, which is the **mean** of the function over one period. The $a_n$ with $n \geq 1$ are zero because $\sin(n\pi) = \sin 0 = 0$. The $b_n$ are those of the previous exercise divided by $2$, hence the series is the constant $\dfrac{1}{2}$ plus half the series of the **square wave**: the result is consistent with the identity $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\, g(x)$, where $g$ is the square wave of exercise 1.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of the odd function defined on $[0, \pi)$ by
$$
f(x) =
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } 0 < x < \dfrac{\pi}{2} \\[8pt]
0 & \text{if } \dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq x < \pi
\end{cases}
$$

$$
f \text{ odd} \implies a_n = 0, \qquad
b_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin(nx)\, dx
$$
$$
b_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \left[ -\frac{\cos(nx)}{n} \right]_{0}^{\pi/2}
= \frac{2}{n\pi} \left( 1 - \cos\frac{n\pi}{2} \right)
$$
$$
\cos\frac{n\pi}{2} =
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if } n \text{ is odd} \\
1 & \text{if } n = 4, 8, 12, \dots \\
-1 & \text{if } n = 2, 6, 10, \dots
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f(x) \sim \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2}{n\pi} \left( 1 - \cos\frac{n\pi}{2} \right) \sin(nx)
= \frac{2}{\pi} \sin x + \frac{2}{\pi} \sin(2x) + \frac{2}{3\pi} \sin(3x) + \frac{2}{5\pi} \sin(5x) + \cdots
$$

::grafico{f="(sign(sin(x))+sign(sin(2*x)))/2+0*log(abs(sin(2*x))-0.05);2/pi*(sin(x)+sin(2*x)+sin(3*x)/3)" xmin=-7 xmax=7.3 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.5 titolo="Odd $2\pi$-periodic extension: $1$ on $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$, $0$ on $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)$, $-1$ on $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, 0\right)$, $0$ on $\left(-\pi, -\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$; dashed, the partial sum up to $\sin(3x)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is given on $[0, \pi)$ and extended **by oddness**, therefore the $a_n$ are zero and the $b_n$ are computed with twice the integral over $[0, \pi]$. The function is zero on $\left[\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)$, hence the integral reduces to $\left(0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$. The value $\cos\dfrac{n\pi}{2}$ follows a cycle of length four, therefore the coefficients are *no longer* split into even and odd but into **four classes** according to the remainder of $n$ modulo $4$: for odd $n$, $b_n = \dfrac{2}{n\pi}$; for $n$ a multiple of $4$ the coefficient is zero; for even $n$ not a multiple of $4$, $b_n = \dfrac{4}{n\pi}$. In the series written out in full the term in $\sin(2x)$ has coefficient $\dfrac{4}{2\pi} = \dfrac{2}{\pi}$ and the term in $\sin(4x)$ **does not appear**.

</div>
