## Exercise 1

Find the Fourier series of the $2$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x$, $x \in [-1, 1)$.

$$
L = 1, \qquad f \text{ odd} \implies a_n = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_n &= 2 \int_{0}^{1} x \sin(n\pi x)\, dx \\[6pt]
&= 2 \left( \left[ -\frac{x \cos(n\pi x)}{n\pi} \right]_{0}^{1} + \frac{1}{n\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \cos(n\pi x)\, dx \right) \\[6pt]
&= 2 \cdot \left( -\frac{(-1)^n}{n\pi} + 0 \right)
= \frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x \sim \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2\,(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi} \sin(n\pi x)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The period is $2L = 2$, therefore $L = 1$ and the harmonics are $\cos(n\pi x)$ and $\sin(n\pi x)$. The function is **odd**, hence the $a_n$ are zero and the $b_n$ are computed with twice the integral over $[0, 1]$, divided by $L = 1$. **Integration by parts** produces the boundary term $-\dfrac{\cos(n\pi)}{n\pi} = -\dfrac{(-1)^n}{n\pi}$, while the remaining integral of $\cos(n\pi x)$ over $[0,1]$ is zero because $\sin(n\pi) = 0$. The result reproduces the coefficients of the sawtooth wave on $[-\pi, \pi)$ with $n$ replaced by $n\pi$ in the denominator and in the argument of the sine: the change of scale compresses the graph *without* changing the structure of the expansion.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the Fourier series of the $2$-periodic extension of the triangle wave $f(x) = 1 - |x|$, $x \in [-1, 1]$, and compute the sum of the series at $x = 0$.

$$
L = 1, \qquad f \text{ even} \implies b_n = 0, \qquad
a_0 = 2 \int_{0}^{1} (1 - x)\, dx = 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_n &= 2 \int_{0}^{1} (1 - x) \cos(n\pi x)\, dx \\[6pt]
&= 2 \left( \left[ \frac{(1 - x) \sin(n\pi x)}{n\pi} \right]_{0}^{1} + \frac{1}{n\pi} \int_{0}^{1} \sin(n\pi x)\, dx \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{n\pi} \cdot \frac{1 - (-1)^n}{n\pi}
= \frac{2 \bigl( 1 - (-1)^n \bigr)}{n^2 \pi^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
1 - |x| \sim \frac{1}{2} + \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{4}{(2k+1)^2 \pi^2} \cos\bigl( (2k+1)\pi x \bigr)
$$
$$
x = 0: \qquad
1 = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{4}{\pi^2} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2k+1)^2}
\implies
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2k+1)^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}
$$

::grafico{f="1-abs(x-2*floor((x+1)/2));1/2+4/(pi*pi)*cos(pi*x)" xmin=-4.3 xmax=4.3 ymin=-0.3 ymax=1.3 titolo="Triangle wave with period $2$ and the partial sum $s_1 = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{4}{\pi^2}\cos(\pi x)$ (dashed)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is **even**, therefore the series contains **only cosines** and the integrals are computed over $[0, 1]$, where $|x| = x$. The coefficient $a_0$ equals $1$, that is, twice the area of the triangle with base $1$ and height $1$. For the $a_n$ integration by parts makes the boundary term vanish, since $\sin(n\pi) = 0$ and the factor $1 - x$ vanishes at $x = 1$; what remains is the integral of $\sin(n\pi x)$, which equals $\dfrac{1 - (-1)^n}{n\pi}$. Therefore $a_n$ is zero for even $n$ and equals $\dfrac{4}{n^2\pi^2}$ for odd $n$. The periodic extension is continuous, because $f(-1) = f(1) = 0$, and it is piecewise smooth; therefore the **pointwise convergence theorem** gives the equality at *every* point, and at $x = 0$, where $f(0) = 1$ and every cosine equals $1$, we obtain the sum $\sum_{k \geq 0} \dfrac{1}{(2k+1)^2} = \dfrac{\pi^2}{8}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the Fourier series of the $4$-periodic extension of $f(x) = x^2$, $x \in [-2, 2)$.

$$
L = 2, \qquad f \text{ even} \implies b_n = 0, \qquad
a_0 = \frac{2}{2} \int_{0}^{2} x^2\, dx = \frac{8}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_n &= \int_{0}^{2} x^2 \cos\!\left( \frac{n\pi x}{2} \right) dx \\[6pt]
&= \left[ \frac{2 x^2}{n\pi} \sin\!\left( \frac{n\pi x}{2} \right) \right]_{0}^{2}
- \frac{4}{n\pi} \int_{0}^{2} x \sin\!\left( \frac{n\pi x}{2} \right) dx \\[6pt]
&= 0 - \frac{4}{n\pi} \left( \left[ -\frac{2x}{n\pi} \cos\!\left( \frac{n\pi x}{2} \right) \right]_{0}^{2} + \frac{2}{n\pi} \int_{0}^{2} \cos\!\left( \frac{n\pi x}{2} \right) dx \right) \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{4}{n\pi} \cdot \left( -\frac{4\,(-1)^n}{n\pi} \right)
= \frac{16\,(-1)^n}{n^2 \pi^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x^2 \sim \frac{4}{3} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{16\,(-1)^n}{n^2 \pi^2} \cos\!\left( \frac{n\pi x}{2} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The period is $2L = 4$, therefore $L = 2$ and the harmonics are $\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{2}$ and $\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{2}$. The function is **even**, hence the $b_n$ are zero and $a_n = \dfrac{2}{L} \displaystyle\int_0^L f(x)\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\, dx$. The computation requires two **integrations by parts**, as for $x^2$ on the period $2\pi$: in the first the boundary term is zero because $\sin(n\pi) = 0$, in the second the boundary term $-\dfrac{4\cos(n\pi)}{n\pi}$ survives, while the last cosine integral is zero. The result has the same structure as the expansion of $x^2$ on $[-\pi, \pi]$, with the scale factors due to $L = 2$; the constant term $\dfrac{a_0}{2} = \dfrac{4}{3}$ is the **mean** of $x^2$ on $[-2, 2]$.

</div>
