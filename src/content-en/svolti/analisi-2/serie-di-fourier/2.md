## Exercise 1

Find the Fourier series of the $2\pi$-periodic extension of $f(x) = |x|$, $x \in [-\pi, \pi]$.

$$
f \text{ even} \implies b_n = 0, \qquad
a_0 = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} x\, dx = \frac{2}{\pi} \cdot \frac{\pi^2}{2} = \pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_n &= \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} x \cos(nx)\, dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{\pi} \left( \left[ \frac{x \sin(nx)}{n} \right]_{0}^{\pi} - \frac{1}{n} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin(nx)\, dx \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{\pi} \left( 0 - \frac{1 - (-1)^n}{n^2} \right)
= \frac{2 \bigl( (-1)^n - 1 \bigr)}{\pi n^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
|x| \sim \frac{\pi}{2} - \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{4}{\pi (2k+1)^2} \cos\bigl( (2k+1)x \bigr)
$$

::grafico{f="abs((x-2*pi*floor((x+pi)/(2*pi))));pi/2-4/pi*cos(x)" xmin=-10 xmax=10 ymin=-0.5 ymax=4 titolo="Triangle wave, the periodic extension of $|x|$, and the partial sum $\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{4}{\pi}\cos x$ (dashed)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute value is an **even** function, therefore the $b_n$ are zero and the series contains **only cosines**, with the $a_n$ given by twice the integral over $[0, \pi]$, where $|x| = x$. The coefficient $a_0$ equals $\pi$, hence the constant term is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the mean of $|x|$ over one period. For $n \geq 1$ we **integrate by parts**: the term with $\sin(n\pi)$ is zero and what remains is the integral of $\sin(nx)$, which equals $\dfrac{1 - (-1)^n}{n^2}$ after division by $n$. Therefore $a_n$ is zero for even $n$ and equals $-\dfrac{4}{\pi n^2}$ for odd $n$: the series contains *only* the cosines of the odd harmonics, with negative sign.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the Fourier series of $f(x) = \cos^2 x$.

$$
\cos^2 x = \frac{1 + \cos(2x)}{2} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \cos(2x)
$$
$$
a_0 = 1, \qquad a_2 = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad a_n = 0 \ \ (n \neq 0, 2), \qquad b_n = 0
$$
$$
\cos^2 x \sim \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \cos(2x)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **cosine double-angle formula** rewrites $\cos^2 x$ as $\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\cos(2x)$, which is already a trigonometric polynomial. Since the Fourier coefficients of a trigonometric polynomial coincide with its coefficients, *no* integral needs to be computed: the Fourier series has only the terms $\dfrac{a_0}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $a_2 \cos(2x) = \dfrac{1}{2}\cos(2x)$, and it **coincides with the function** at every point. It is always worth checking whether the given function reduces to a trigonometric polynomial by an identity, before setting up the integrals.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the Fourier series of $f(x) = |\sin x|$.

$$
f \text{ even, } \pi\text{-periodic}, \qquad
a_0 = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x\, dx = \frac{2}{\pi} \bigl[ -\cos x \bigr]_{0}^{\pi} = \frac{4}{\pi}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_n &= \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x \cos(nx)\, dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \bigl( \sin\bigl((1+n)x\bigr) + \sin\bigl((1-n)x\bigr) \bigr)\, dx \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{\pi} \left( \frac{1 - (-1)^{1+n}}{1+n} + \frac{1 - (-1)^{1-n}}{1-n} \right)
\qquad (n \neq 1)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
n \text{ odd} \implies a_n = 0, \qquad
n \text{ even} \implies a_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \left( \frac{1}{1+n} + \frac{1}{1-n} \right) = -\frac{4}{\pi (n^2 - 1)}
$$
$$
a_1 = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x \cos x\, dx
= \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin(2x)\, dx = 0
$$
$$
|\sin x| \sim \frac{2}{\pi} - \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{4}{\pi (4k^2 - 1)} \cos(2kx)
$$

::grafico{f="abs(sin(x));2/pi-4/(3*pi)*cos(2*x)" xmin=-7 xmax=7.3 ymin=-0.3 ymax=1.3 titolo="$y = |\sin x|$ has period $\pi$; the partial sum $\dfrac{2}{\pi} - \dfrac{4}{3\pi}\cos(2x)$ (dashed) contains only a cosine of period $\pi$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function $|\sin x|$ is **even**, therefore the $b_n$ are zero; on $[0, \pi]$ the sine is nonnegative and the absolute value can be removed. The coefficient $a_0$ equals $\dfrac{4}{\pi}$, hence the mean of the function is $\dfrac{2}{\pi}$. For the $a_n$ the product $\sin x \cos(nx)$ is transformed into the sum of two sines with the **product-to-sum formulas**, and each sine is integrated directly; the case $n = 1$ must be treated *separately* because the second term would have a zero denominator, and the direct computation with $\sin x \cos x = \dfrac{\sin(2x)}{2}$ gives $a_1 = 0$. Since $(-1)^{1+n} = -(-1)^n$, for odd $n$ the numerators vanish and for even $n$ both equal $2$; the sum of the two fractions gives $a_n = -\dfrac{4}{\pi(n^2-1)}$. *Only* the even harmonics remain in the series, written with $n = 2k$: the result reflects the fact that $|\sin x|$ has period $\pi$, therefore only the cosines of compatible period appear in its expansion.

</div>
