## Exercise 1

Compute the volume of the solid lying between the plane $z = 0$ and the paraboloid $z = 4 - x^2 - y^2$.

$$
4 - x^2 - y^2 \geq 0
\iff x^2 + y^2 \leq 4
$$
$$
\operatorname{Vol}(E)
= \iint_{x^2 + y^2 \leq 4} (4 - x^2 - y^2)\, dx\, dy
= \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^2 (4 - \rho^2)\, \rho \, d\rho\, d\theta
$$
$$
2\pi \int_0^2 (4\rho - \rho^3)\, d\rho
= 2\pi \left[ 2\rho^2 - \frac{\rho^4}{4} \right]_0^2
= 2\pi (8 - 4) = 8\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solid lies between the plane $z = 0$ and the graph of the function $4 - x^2 - y^2$, so the volume is the **double integral** of the function over its projection. The projection is the region where the function is nonnegative, that is, the disk of radius $2$. Since the domain is a disk and the function depends *only* on $x^2 + y^2$, we pass to **polar coordinates**: the function becomes $4 - \rho^2$ and the area element carries the factor $\rho$. The integral of the polynomial $4\rho - \rho^3$ over $[0, 2]$ equals $4$, so the volume is $8\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the volume of the solid bounded by the paraboloids $z = x^2 + y^2$ and $z = 2 - x^2 - y^2$.

$$
x^2 + y^2 = 2 - x^2 - y^2
\iff x^2 + y^2 = 1
$$
$$
\operatorname{Vol}(E)
= \iint_{x^2 + y^2 \leq 1} \bigl( (2 - x^2 - y^2) - (x^2 + y^2) \bigr)\, dx\, dy
$$
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 (2 - 2\rho^2)\, \rho \, d\rho\, d\theta
= 2\pi \left[ \rho^2 - \frac{\rho^4}{2} \right]_0^1
= 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \pi
$$

::grafico{xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.6 uguale=1 nomi="x,z" f="x^2;2-x^2" legenda="z = x^2;z = 2 - x^2" area="2-x^2;x^2;-1;1" punti="-1,1;1,1" titolo="Cross-section with the plane $y = 0$: the two paraboloids meet at height $z = 1$, above the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The two paraboloids intersect where $x^2 + y^2 = 2 - x^2 - y^2$, that is, on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ at height $z = 1$; inside that circle the paraboloid $z = 2 - x^2 - y^2$ lies *above* the other one. The volume of the solid between two graphs is the **integral of the difference** between the upper and the lower function, taken over the projection, which is the unit disk. The difference equals $2 - 2\rho^2$ in polar coordinates; the integral of $(2 - 2\rho^2)\rho = 2\rho - 2\rho^3$ over $[0, 1]$ equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the volume is $\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the centroid of the homogeneous lamina
$$
D = \{ (x, y) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 1, \ y \geq 0 \} .
$$

$$
\bar{x} = 0 \quad \text{(symmetry with respect to the } y \text{-axis)}
$$
$$
\operatorname{Area}(D) = \frac{\pi}{2},
\qquad
\iint_D y \, dx\, dy
= \int_0^{\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 \rho \sin\theta \cdot \rho \, d\rho\, d\theta
$$
$$
\int_0^1 \rho^2 d\rho \cdot \int_0^{\pi} \sin\theta \, d\theta
= \frac{1}{3} \cdot 2 = \frac{2}{3}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = \frac{1}{\operatorname{Area}(D)} \iint_D y \, dx\, dy
= \frac{2}{\pi} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{3\pi}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 area="sqrt(abs(1-x^2));0;-1;1" curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,3.1416" punti="0,0.4244,pieno,G" titolo="The homogeneous semicircular lamina and its centroid $G = \left( 0, \dfrac{4}{3\pi} \right)$, on the axis of symmetry"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The lamina is the upper unit half-disk, which is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis; since the lamina is homogeneous, the centroid **lies on the axis of symmetry**, so $\bar{x} = 0$. The explicit computation confirms this: in the integral of $x$ over $D$ the function is **odd** in $x$ and the domain is symmetric, therefore the integral is zero. For $\bar{y}$ we divide the integral of $y$ by the area, which is half that of the unit disk. In polar coordinates the half-disk corresponds to $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ and the integral factors: $\dfrac{1}{3}$ from $\rho^2$ and $2$ from $\sin\theta$. The centroid is the point $\left( 0, \dfrac{4}{3\pi} \right)$, inside the lamina and **closer to the diameter** than to the curved boundary.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Compute the mass and the centroid of the lamina $D = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$ with density $\delta(x, y) = x + y$.

$$
m = \int_0^1 \!\! \int_0^1 (x + y)\, dy\, dx
= \int_0^1 \left( x + \frac{1}{2} \right) dx
= \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\iint_D x\, (x + y)\, dx\, dy
&= \int_0^1 \!\! \int_0^1 (x^2 + xy)\, dy\, dx \\[6pt]
&= \int_0^1 \left( x^2 + \frac{x}{2} \right) dx
= \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\bar{x} = \frac{1}{m} \cdot \frac{7}{12} = \frac{7}{12},
\qquad
\bar{y} = \frac{7}{12}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **mass** is the integral of the density over the lamina: integrating $x + y$ first in $y$ we obtain $x + \dfrac{1}{2}$, whose integral over $[0, 1]$ equals $1$. For the abscissa of the centroid we integrate $x \,\delta(x, y) = x^2 + xy$ and divide by the mass; the result is $\dfrac{7}{12}$. For the ordinate *no* new computation is needed, since the density is **symmetric** under the exchange of $x$ and $y$ and the square is invariant under the same exchange, so $\bar{y} = \bar{x}$. The centroid $\left( \dfrac{7}{12}, \dfrac{7}{12} \right)$ is shifted with respect to the center $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ toward the vertex $(1, 1)$, where the **density is greatest**.

</div>
