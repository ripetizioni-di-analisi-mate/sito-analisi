## Exercise 1

Compute the double integral
$$
\iint_D x y \, dx\, dy ,
\qquad D = \{ (x, y) : 0 \leq x \leq 1, \ x^2 \leq y \leq x \} .
$$

$$
\iint_D x y \, dx\, dy
= \int_0^1 \left( \int_{x^2}^{x} x y \, dy \right) dx
$$
$$
\int_{x^2}^{x} x y \, dy
= x \left[ \frac{y^2}{2} \right]_{y = x^2}^{y = x}
= \frac{x (x^2 - x^4)}{2}
= \frac{x^3 - x^5}{2}
$$
$$
\int_0^1 \frac{x^3 - x^5}{2}\, dx
= \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6} \right)
= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{12} = \frac{1}{24}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain is given directly in **normal form** with respect to the $x$-axis: $x$ ranges over the interval $[0, 1]$ and, for each fixed $x$, $y$ goes from the parabola $y = x^2$ to the line $y = x$. The **reduction formula** therefore gives an inner integral in $y$ with limits $x^2$ and $x$. In the inner integral the factor $x$ is constant and comes out of the integral sign; the antiderivative of $y$ is $\dfrac{y^2}{2}$ and evaluating at the limits produces the polynomial $\dfrac{x^3 - x^5}{2}$. The integral of this polynomial over $[0, 1]$ equals $\dfrac{1}{24}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the double integral
$$
\iint_T x \, dx\, dy ,
$$
where $T$ is the triangle with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$.

$$
T = \left\{ (x, y) :
\begin{cases}
0 \leq x \leq 2 \\
0 \leq y \leq 2 - x
\end{cases}
\right\}
$$
$$
\iint_T x \, dx\, dy
= \int_0^2 \left( \int_0^{2 - x} x \, dy \right) dx
= \int_0^2 x (2 - x)\, dx
$$
$$
\int_0^2 (2x - x^2)\, dx
= \left[ x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^2
= 4 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{4}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step is the description of the triangle in **normal form**. The slanted side joins $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$, so it lies on the line $x + y = 2$, that is, $y = 2 - x$. For each $x \in [0, 2]$ the vertical segment inside $T$ starts at the $x$-axis and reaches the slanted line, therefore $0 \leq y \leq 2 - x$. In the inner integral the function $x$ is constant with respect to $y$, so the integral equals $x$ times the **length of the segment**, that is, $x(2 - x)$. The integral of the polynomial $2x - x^2$ over $[0, 2]$ equals $\dfrac{4}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the double integral
$$
\iint_D y \, dx\, dy ,
\qquad D = \{ (x, y) : -1 \leq y \leq 2, \ y^2 \leq x \leq y + 2 \} .
$$

$$
\iint_D y \, dx\, dy
= \int_{-1}^{2} \left( \int_{y^2}^{y + 2} y \, dx \right) dy
= \int_{-1}^{2} y \,(y + 2 - y^2)\, dy
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{-1}^{2} (y^2 + 2y - y^3)\, dy
&= \left[ \frac{y^3}{3} + y^2 - \frac{y^4}{4} \right]_{-1}^{2} \\[6pt]
&= \left( \frac{8}{3} + 4 - 4 \right) - \left( -\frac{1}{3} + 1 - \frac{1}{4} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{8}{3} - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{27}{12} = \frac{9}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain is **normal with respect to the $y$-axis**: $y$ ranges over $[-1, 2]$ and, for each fixed $y$, $x$ goes from the parabola $x = y^2$ to the line $x = y + 2$. The limits $-1$ and $2$ are the **ordinates of the intersection points** of the two curves, solutions of $y^2 = y + 2$. Since the domain is described with respect to $y$, the inner integral is in $x$; the function $y$ is constant with respect to $x$, so the inner integral equals $y$ times the length of the horizontal segment, that is, $y(y + 2 - y^2)$. Expanding the product gives a polynomial in $y$, whose integral over $[-1, 2]$ equals $\dfrac{9}{4}$. The description with respect to the $x$-axis would have been more laborious, since the lower boundary would change expression at the point $x = 1$ and the domain would have to be *split* into two parts.

</div>
