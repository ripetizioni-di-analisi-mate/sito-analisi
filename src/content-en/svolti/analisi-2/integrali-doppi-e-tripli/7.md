## Exercise 1

Compute the triple integral
$$
\iiint_E z \, dx\, dy\, dz ,
\qquad E = \{ (x, y, z) : x^2 + y^2 \leq 4, \ 0 \leq z \leq 3 \} .
$$

$$
0 \leq \rho \leq 2, \qquad 0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi, \qquad 0 \leq z \leq 3
$$
$$
\iiint_E z \, dx\, dy\, dz
= \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^2 \!\! \int_0^3 z \, \rho \, dz\, d\rho\, d\theta
$$
$$
\int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \cdot \int_0^2 \rho \, d\rho \cdot \int_0^3 z \, dz
= 2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{9}{2} = 18\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The solid is a cylinder with axis $z$, so in **cylindrical coordinates** it becomes a box: $\rho \in [0, 2]$, $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$, $z \in [0, 3]$. The volume element is $\rho\, d\rho\, d\theta\, dz$, with the factor $\rho$ of the Jacobian. The integrand $z \rho$ is a product of factors each in *a single* variable and the limits are constant, therefore the triple integral is the **product of the three single integrals**: $2\pi$ from the angle, $2$ from $\rho$ and $\dfrac{9}{2}$ from $z$, for a total of $18\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the triple integral
$$
\iiint_E (x^2 + y^2)\, dx\, dy\, dz ,
\qquad E = \left\{ (x, y, z) : \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \leq z \leq 2 \right\} .
$$

$$
\rho \leq z \leq 2, \qquad 0 \leq \rho \leq 2, \qquad 0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi
$$
$$
\iiint_E (x^2 + y^2)\, dx\, dy\, dz
= \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^2 \rho^2 \cdot \rho \left( \int_{\rho}^2 dz \right) d\rho\, d\theta
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\pi \int_0^2 \rho^3 (2 - \rho)\, d\rho
&= 2\pi \left[ \frac{\rho^4}{2} - \frac{\rho^5}{5} \right]_0^2 \\[6pt]
&= 2\pi \left( 8 - \frac{32}{5} \right)
= 2\pi \cdot \frac{8}{5} = \frac{16\pi}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-2.8 xmax=3.2 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.8 uguale=1 nomi="x,z" f="abs(x);2+0*sqrt(4-x^2)" area="2;abs(x);-2;2" vettori="1,1,1,2" etichette="1.6,0.95,z = ρ;-1.9,2.15,z = 2" titolo="Cross-section with the plane $y = 0$: the solid lies between the cone $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ and the plane $z = 2$, so $\rho \leq z \leq 2$ with $0 \leq \rho \leq 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The solid is the region lying between the cone $z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ and the plane $z = 2$. In **cylindrical coordinates** the cone has equation $z = \rho$, so the vertical line above the point with coordinates $(\rho, \theta)$ goes from $z = \rho$ to $z = 2$; the projection onto the $xy$-plane is the disk of radius $2$, obtained by imposing $\rho \leq 2$. The function $x^2 + y^2$ becomes $\rho^2$ and the volume element carries the factor $\rho$. The integral in $z$ gives the **length** $2 - \rho$ of the vertical line, since the function does *not* depend on $z$; what remains is the integral of the polynomial $\rho^3(2 - \rho) = 2\rho^3 - \rho^4$, which multiplied by $2\pi$ gives $\dfrac{16\pi}{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the triple integral
$$
\iiint_E z^2 \, dx\, dy\, dz ,
\qquad E = \{ (x, y, z) : x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \leq 1 \} .
$$

$$
z = r\cos\varphi, \qquad dx\, dy\, dz = r^2 \sin\varphi \, dr\, d\varphi\, d\theta
$$
$$
\iiint_E z^2 \, dx\, dy\, dz
= \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^{\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 r^2 \cos^2\varphi \cdot r^2 \sin\varphi \, dr\, d\varphi\, d\theta
$$
$$
\int_0^1 r^4 \, dr = \frac{1}{5},
\qquad
\int_0^{\pi} \cos^2\varphi \, \sin\varphi \, d\varphi
= \left[ -\frac{\cos^3\varphi}{3} \right]_0^{\pi}
= \frac{2}{3}
$$
$$
\iiint_E z^2 \, dx\, dy\, dz
= 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{15}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain is the unit ball, so in **spherical coordinates** it becomes the box $r \in [0, 1]$, $\varphi \in [0, \pi]$, $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$. The function $z^2$ becomes $r^2 \cos^2\varphi$ and the volume element carries the factor $r^2 \sin\varphi$. The integrand is a product of factors in separated variables with constant limits, therefore the triple integral is the **product of three single integrals**. In the integral in $\varphi$ the factor $\sin\varphi$ is, up to sign, the derivative of $\cos\varphi$, so the antiderivative of $\cos^2\varphi \sin\varphi$ is $-\dfrac{\cos^3\varphi}{3}$ and the integral equals $\dfrac{2}{3}$. The product of the three factors gives $\dfrac{4\pi}{15}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Compute the triple integral
$$
\iiint_E e^{\left( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \right)^{3/2}} dx\, dy\, dz ,
\qquad E = \{ (x, y, z) : x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \leq 1 \} .
$$

$$
\left( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \right)^{3/2} = r^3
$$
$$
\iiint_E e^{\left( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \right)^{3/2}} dx\, dy\, dz
= \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^{\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 e^{r^3} r^2 \sin\varphi \, dr\, d\varphi\, d\theta
$$
$$
\int_0^1 r^2 e^{r^3} dr
= \left[ \frac{e^{r^3}}{3} \right]_0^1
= \frac{e - 1}{3},
\qquad
\int_0^{\pi} \sin\varphi \, d\varphi = 2
$$
$$
\iiint_E e^{\left( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \right)^{3/2}} dx\, dy\, dz
= 2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{e - 1}{3} = \frac{4\pi (e - 1)}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function depends *only* on the **distance from the origin**, since $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = r^2$ and hence the exponent is $r^3$; the domain is the unit ball. In spherical coordinates the integral factors into the three single integrals. The factor $r^2$ contributed by the Jacobian is, up to the constant $3$, the **derivative of the exponent** $r^3$, therefore the antiderivative of $r^2 e^{r^3}$ is $\dfrac{e^{r^3}}{3}$: without the Jacobian the integral in $r$ could not be computed in elementary form. The integral of $\sin\varphi$ over $[0, \pi]$ equals $2$ and the integral in $\theta$ gives $2\pi$. The product is $\dfrac{4\pi(e - 1)}{3}$.

</div>
