## Exercise 1

Determine whether the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (3t + 1,\; t^2), \qquad t \in [0, 2],
$$
is regular and compute the tangent vector at the point $\gamma(1)$.

$$
\gamma'(t) = (3,\; 2t)
$$
$$
\gamma'(t) \neq (0, 0) \ \text{for every } t \in [0, 2]
$$
$$
\gamma(1) = (4, 1), \qquad \gamma'(1) = (3, 2)
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.5 xmax=8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=5 uguale=1 curve="3*t+1,t^2,0,2" vettori="4,1,7,3,γ′(1)" punti="1,0,pieno,γ(0);4,1,pieno,γ(1);7,4,pieno,γ(2)" titolo="The trace of $\gamma$ and the tangent vector $\gamma'(1) = (3, 2)$ applied at the point $\gamma(1) = (4, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The components $3t + 1$ and $t^2$ are polynomials, hence the curve is of class $C^1$ and the derivative vector is computed component by component. The first component of $\gamma'(t)$ equals $3$ for *every* $t$, therefore the vector $\gamma'(t)$ is never zero and the curve is **regular** on the whole of $[0, 2]$. The **tangent vector** at the point $\gamma(1) = (4, 1)$ is $\gamma'(1) = (3, 2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that the helix
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; t), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi],
$$
is regular and compute the unit tangent vector at the point $\gamma(\pi)$.

$$
\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; 1)
$$
$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + 1} = \sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\gamma'(\pi) = (0,\; -1,\; 1), \qquad
T(\pi) = \frac{\gamma'(\pi)}{\sqrt{2}} = \left( 0,\; -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The components are functions of class $C^1$ and the derivative vector has third component constantly equal to $1$, hence $\gamma'(t)$ *never* vanishes and the curve is **regular**. The norm of the tangent vector equals $\sqrt{2}$ for every $t$, since $\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$. At the point $\gamma(\pi) = (-1, 0, \pi)$ the tangent vector is $\gamma'(\pi) = (0, -1, 1)$; dividing by the norm $\sqrt{2}$ one obtains the **unit tangent vector**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (t^2,\; t^3), \qquad t \in [-1, 1],
$$
is regular or piecewise regular.

$$
\gamma'(t) = (2t,\; 3t^2)
$$
$$
\gamma'(t) = (0, 0) \iff
\begin{cases}
2t = 0 \\[4pt]
3t^2 = 0
\end{cases}
\iff t = 0
$$
$$
\gamma \ \text{regular on } [-1, 0] \ \text{and on } [0, 1] \implies \gamma \ \text{piecewise regular on } [-1, 1]
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The curve is of class $C^1$ because the components are polynomials. The derivative vector vanishes when both components are zero, and the system has the unique solution $t = 0$, which is an *interior* point of the interval $[-1, 1]$; hence the curve **is not regular** on $[-1, 1]$. On the two subintervals $[-1, 0]$ and $[0, 1]$ the point $t = 0$ is an endpoint, therefore $\gamma'(t)$ does not vanish at any interior point of the two pieces and each restriction is regular. The curve is therefore **piecewise regular** with the partition $-1 < 0 < 1$. At the point $\gamma(0) = (0, 0)$ the trace has a **cusp**: at the instant $t = 0$ the velocity vanishes and the first component $x'(t) = 2t$ changes sign, so the horizontal direction of motion is reversed.

</div>
