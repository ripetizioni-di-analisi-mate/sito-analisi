## Exercise 1

Determine the center of mass of the homogeneous wire
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t), \qquad t \in [0, \pi].
$$

$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 1, \qquad L = \int_0^\pi 1 \, dt = \pi
$$
$$
\bar{x} = 0 \quad \text{(symmetry with respect to the } y\text{-axis)}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \sin t \, dt
= \frac{1}{\pi} \Bigl[ -\cos t \Bigr]_0^\pi = \frac{2}{\pi}
$$
$$
G = \left( 0,\; \frac{2}{\pi} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire is the upper unit semicircle and the density is constant, hence the coordinates of the center of mass are the **integral means** of the coordinates with respect to length. The velocity has norm $1$, therefore the length coincides with the width of the interval, that is, $\pi$. The trace is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis and the wire is homogeneous, hence the center of mass **belongs to the axis of symmetry** and $\bar{x} = 0$. For $\bar{y}$ one integrates the second component $\sin t$: an antiderivative is $-\cos t$ and evaluation at the endpoints gives $-\cos \pi + \cos 0 = 2$. The center of mass $\left(0, \dfrac{2}{\pi}\right)$ does *not* belong to the trace, since the point has distance $\dfrac{2}{\pi} < 1$ from the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the center of mass of the homogeneous wire
$$
\gamma(t) = (2\cos t,\; 2\sin t), \qquad t \in \left[ 0, \frac{\pi}{2} \right].
$$

$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = 2, \qquad L = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} 2 \, dt = \pi
$$
$$
\bar{x} = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} 2\cos t \cdot 2 \, dt
= \frac{4}{\pi} \Bigl[ \sin t \Bigr]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{4}{\pi}
$$
$$
\bar{y} = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} 2\sin t \cdot 2 \, dt
= \frac{4}{\pi} \Bigl[ -\cos t \Bigr]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{4}{\pi}
$$
$$
G = \left( \frac{4}{\pi},\; \frac{4}{\pi} \right)
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.5 xmax=2.8 ymin=-0.5 ymax=2.6 uguale=1 curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,1.5708" segmenti="0,0,2.2,2.2" punti="1.273,1.273,pieno,G" etichette="2.25,2.05,y = x" titolo="The quarter circle of radius $2$ and its center of mass $G = \left( \dfrac{4}{\pi}, \dfrac{4}{\pi} \right)$ on the bisector $y = x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The wire is the quarter circle of radius $2$ in the first quadrant. The norm of the derivative vector equals $2$, hence the length is $2 \cdot \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \pi$. In the two integral means the coordinate is replaced by the corresponding component of the parametrization and the length element brings the factor $2$. The two trigonometric integrals *both* equal $1$, therefore $\bar{x} = \bar{y} = \dfrac{4}{\pi}$. The equality of the two coordinates is consistent with the **symmetry of the trace** with respect to the bisector $y = x$, given that the wire is homogeneous and the center of mass belongs to the axis of symmetry.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the center of mass of the homogeneous wire placed along the helix
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; t), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi].
$$

$$
\lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{2}, \qquad L = 2\sqrt{2}\, \pi
$$
$$
\bar{x} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \cos t \cdot \sqrt{2} \, dt = 0,
\qquad
\bar{y} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} \sin t \cdot \sqrt{2} \, dt = 0
$$
$$
\bar{z} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi} \int_0^{2\pi} t \cdot \sqrt{2} \, dt
= \frac{1}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{(2\pi)^2}{2} = \pi
$$
$$
G = (0,\; 0,\; \pi)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The velocity on the helix has constant norm $\sqrt{2}$, hence the length of the full turn is $2\sqrt{2}\,\pi$. The integrals of $\cos t$ and of $\sin t$ over a full period are zero, therefore the first two coordinates of the center of mass equal $0$: the center of mass lies **on the axis of the helix**. For the height, the factor $\sqrt{2}$ of the length element cancels with that of the total length and what remains is the mean of $t$ over $[0, 2\pi]$, which equals $\pi$. The result corresponds to the **midpoint of the axis**: the mass is distributed uniformly with respect to height.

</div>
