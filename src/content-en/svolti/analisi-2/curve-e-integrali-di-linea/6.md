## Exercise 1

Compute the work of the field $F(x, y) = (y,\; x)$ along the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (t,\; t^2), \qquad t \in [0, 1].
$$

$$
\gamma'(t) = (1,\; 2t), \qquad F(\gamma(t)) = (t^2,\; t)
$$
$$
F(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t) = t^2 \cdot 1 + t \cdot 2t = 3t^2
$$
$$
\int_\gamma F \cdot d\gamma = \int_0^1 3t^2 \, dt = \Bigl[ t^3 \Bigr]_0^1 = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the field the variables are replaced by the components of the parametrization, hence the first component of $F(\gamma(t))$ is $y = t^2$ and the second is $x = t$. The **scalar product** with the velocity vector $(1, 2t)$ adds the products of corresponding components and gives $t^2 + 2t^2 = 3t^2$. The integral of $3t^2$ over $[0, 1]$ equals $1$, since an antiderivative is $t^3$. The **work** is therefore $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the work of the field $F(x, y) = (-y,\; x)$ along the unit circle traversed counterclockwise,
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi].
$$

$$
\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t), \qquad F(\gamma(t)) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t)
$$
$$
F(\gamma(t)) \cdot \gamma'(t) = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1
$$
$$
\int_\gamma F \cdot d\gamma = \int_0^{2\pi} 1 \, dt = 2\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Along the circle the field evaluated on the curve **coincides with the velocity vector**: both equal $(-\sin t, \cos t)$. The scalar product of a vector with itself is the square of its norm, which by the fundamental identity equals $1$ at every point. The work is therefore the length of the interval, that is, $2\pi$. The value is **positive** because the field is at *every* point tangent to the circle and agrees with the counterclockwise direction of travel; on the opposite curve, traversed clockwise, the work would equal $-2\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the line integral
$$
\int_\gamma z \, dx + x \, dy + y \, dz, \qquad \gamma(t) = (t,\; t^2,\; t^3), \quad t \in [0, 1].
$$

$$
\gamma'(t) = (1,\; 2t,\; 3t^2)
$$
$$
z\, x' + x\, y' + y\, z' = t^3 \cdot 1 + t \cdot 2t + t^2 \cdot 3t^2 = t^3 + 2t^2 + 3t^4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\gamma z \, dx + x \, dy + y \, dz
&= \int_0^1 \left( t^3 + 2t^2 + 3t^4 \right) dt \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{4} + \frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{5} = \frac{15 + 40 + 36}{60} = \frac{91}{60}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The integral is written in the notation of **differential forms**: the associated field is $F = (z, x, y)$ and each differential is replaced by the derivative of the corresponding component, that is, $dx = 1 \cdot dt$, $dy = 2t \, dt$ and $dz = 3t^2 \, dt$. After substituting $x = t$, $y = t^2$ and $z = t^3$, the integrand is the polynomial $t^3 + 2t^2 + 3t^4$. The three monomials are integrated with the **power rule** and give $\dfrac{1}{4}$, $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{3}{5}$; the common denominator is $60$ and the **sum** equals $\dfrac{91}{60}$.

</div>
