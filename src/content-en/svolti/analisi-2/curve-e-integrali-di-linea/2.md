## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the tangent line to the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (t,\; t^2), \qquad t \in [-2, 2],
$$
at the point $\gamma(1)$.

$$
\gamma(1) = (1, 1), \qquad \gamma'(t) = (1,\; 2t), \qquad \gamma'(1) = (1, 2)
$$
$$
r(s) = (1 + s,\; 1 + 2s), \qquad s \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
y - 1 = 2(x - 1) \implies y = 2x - 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point of tangency is $\gamma(1) = (1, 1)$ and the direction of the line is given by the tangent vector $\gamma'(1) = (1, 2)$, which is *not* zero, hence the tangent line is **defined**. The parametric equation is written by adding to the point the direction vector multiplied by the parameter $s$. For the Cartesian form one observes that the direction $(1, 2)$ corresponds to the slope $2$, therefore the line through $(1, 1)$ with slope $2$ has **equation** $y = 2x - 1$. The trace of the curve is the parabola $y = x^2$ and the result **coincides with the tangent to the parabola** computed with the derivative of $x^2$ at $x = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the tangent line to the curve
$$
\gamma(t) = (e^t,\; e^{-t}), \qquad t \in [-1, 1],
$$
at the point $\gamma(0)$.

$$
\gamma(0) = (1, 1), \qquad \gamma'(t) = (e^t,\; -e^{-t}), \qquad \gamma'(0) = (1, -1)
$$
$$
r(s) = (1 + s,\; 1 - s), \qquad s \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
y - 1 = -(x - 1) \implies y = 2 - x
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=3.4 ymin=-0.5 ymax=3 uguale=1 curve="exp(t),exp(-t),-1,1;t,2-t,-0.4,2.5" vettori="1,1,2,0,γ′(0)" punti="1,1,pieno,γ(0)" titolo="The trace of $\gamma$, an arc of the hyperbola $xy = 1$, and the tangent line $y = 2 - x$ at the point $(1, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The components are differentiated with the rules for the exponential: the derivative of $e^{-t}$ is $-e^{-t}$ by the **chain rule**. At the point $t = 0$ the tangent vector is $(1, -1)$, not zero, hence the tangent line exists and passes through $\gamma(0) = (1, 1)$ with that direction. The slope associated with the direction $(1, -1)$ is $-1$, therefore the **Cartesian form** is $y = 2 - x$. The trace of the curve lies on the hyperbola $xy = 1$, since $e^t \cdot e^{-t} = 1$, and the line found is the **tangent to the hyperbola** at the point $(1, 1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the parametric equation of the tangent line to the helix
$$
\gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; t), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi],
$$
at the point $\gamma\!\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$.

$$
\gamma\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \left( 0,\; 1,\; \frac{\pi}{2} \right)
$$
$$
\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; 1), \qquad
\gamma'\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = (-1,\; 0,\; 1)
$$
$$
r(s) = \left( -s,\; 1,\; \frac{\pi}{2} + s \right), \qquad s \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point of tangency is obtained by evaluating the parametrization at $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, where $\cos \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\sin \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$. The tangent vector at the same instant is $(-1, 0, 1)$, not zero because the third component of $\gamma'$ is always $1$. In space the tangent line does *not* admit a single Cartesian equation, hence the answer stays in **parametric form**: each value of $s$ corresponds to the point obtained by starting from $\left(0, 1, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ and moving by $s$ times the vector $(-1, 0, 1)$. The second coordinate of the line is **constant**, since the $y$ component of the tangent vector at that point is zero.

</div>
