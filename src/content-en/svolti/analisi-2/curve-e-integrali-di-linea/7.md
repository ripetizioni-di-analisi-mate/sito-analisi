## Exercise 1

Compute the line integral
$$
\int_\gamma x \, ds,
$$
where $\gamma$ is the polygonal path that joins $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 0)$ and then $(1, 0)$ to $(1, 1)$.

$$
\gamma_1(t) = (t,\; 0), \quad t \in [0, 1], \qquad \lVert \gamma_1'(t) \rVert = 1
$$
$$
\gamma_2(t) = (1,\; t), \quad t \in [0, 1], \qquad \lVert \gamma_2'(t) \rVert = 1
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma_1} x \, ds = \int_0^1 t \, dt = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
\int_{\gamma_2} x \, ds = \int_0^1 1 \, dt = 1
$$
$$
\int_\gamma x \, ds = \frac{1}{2} + 1 = \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polygonal path is *not* a regular curve, since at the vertex $(1, 0)$ the direction changes abruptly, but it is **piecewise regular**: each segment is parametrized separately and the integral is the **sum of the two contributions**. On the horizontal piece the coordinate $x$ coincides with the parameter and the length element is $dt$, hence the integral equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$. On the vertical piece the coordinate $x$ is constantly equal to $1$ and the piece has length $1$, therefore the integral equals $1$. The sum gives $\dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the work of the field $F(x, y) = (x + y,\; xy)$ along the polygonal path that joins $(0, 0)$ to $(2, 0)$ and then $(2, 0)$ to $(2, 1)$.

$$
\gamma_1(t) = (t,\; 0), \quad t \in [0, 2], \qquad \gamma_1'(t) = (1,\; 0)
$$
$$
F(\gamma_1(t)) \cdot \gamma_1'(t) = (t + 0) \cdot 1 + (t \cdot 0) \cdot 0 = t
\implies
\int_{\gamma_1} F \cdot d\gamma = \int_0^2 t \, dt = 2
$$
$$
\gamma_2(t) = (2,\; t), \quad t \in [0, 1], \qquad \gamma_2'(t) = (0,\; 1)
$$
$$
F(\gamma_2(t)) \cdot \gamma_2'(t) = (2 + t) \cdot 0 + 2t \cdot 1 = 2t
\implies
\int_{\gamma_2} F \cdot d\gamma = \int_0^1 2t \, dt = 1
$$
$$
\int_\gamma F \cdot d\gamma = 2 + 1 = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the two segments the scalar product simplifies because one component of the velocity vector is zero. On the horizontal piece one has $y = 0$ and $\gamma_1' = (1, 0)$, hence *only* the first component of the field survives, which evaluated on the curve equals $t$; the integral over $[0, 2]$ gives $2$. On the vertical piece one has $x = 2$ and $\gamma_2' = (0, 1)$, hence the second component $xy = 2t$ survives; the integral over $[0, 1]$ gives $1$. Since the work on a piecewise regular curve is the **sum of the works** on the pieces, the result is $3$. The order of the pieces respects the assigned orientation, given that in integrals of the second kind the direction of travel **determines the sign**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the line integral
$$
\int_\gamma y \, ds,
$$
where $\gamma$ is the concatenation of the arc of the unit circle from $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$ and of the segment from $(0, 1)$ to $(0, 0)$.

$$
\gamma_1(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t), \quad t \in \left[ 0, \frac{\pi}{2} \right], \qquad \lVert \gamma_1'(t) \rVert = 1
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma_1} y \, ds = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin t \, dt = \Bigl[ -\cos t \Bigr]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = 1
$$
$$
\gamma_2(t) = (0,\; 1 - t), \quad t \in [0, 1], \qquad \lVert \gamma_2'(t) \rVert = 1
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma_2} y \, ds = \int_0^1 (1 - t) \, dt = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\int_\gamma y \, ds = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.6 xmax=1.6 ymin=-0.4 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,1.5708" vettori="0,1,0,0" punti="1,0;0,1" etichette="0.75,0.85,γ₁;0.08,0.45,γ₂" titolo="The curve $\gamma$: the arc $\gamma_1$ from $(1, 0)$ to $(0, 1)$ followed by the segment $\gamma_2$ down to the origin"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The curve is formed by **two regular arcs** concatenated: the quarter circle in the first quadrant and a vertical segment. On the arc the coordinate $y$ equals $\sin t$ and the length element is $dt$, since the velocity has norm $1$; the integral of $\sin t$ over $\left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$ equals $1$. On the segment the parametrization goes down from $(0, 1)$ to $(0, 0)$, the coordinate $y$ equals $1 - t$ and the velocity has norm $1$; the integral gives $\dfrac{1}{2}$. The integral of the first kind **does not depend on the direction** of travel of the pieces, hence the sum $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is the required value *whatever* orientation is chosen.

</div>
