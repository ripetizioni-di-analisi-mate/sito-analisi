## Exercise 1

Compute the line integral
$$
\int_\gamma x \, ds, \qquad \gamma(t) = (t,\; 2t), \quad t \in [0, 1].
$$

$$
\gamma'(t) = (1,\; 2), \qquad \lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{1 + 4} = \sqrt{5}
$$
$$
\int_\gamma x \, ds = \int_0^1 t \cdot \sqrt{5} \, dt
= \sqrt{5} \left[ \frac{t^2}{2} \right]_0^1 = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The curve is the segment from the origin to the point $(1, 2)$. The derivative vector is constant, hence the norm equals $\sqrt{5}$ at *every* point and the **length element** is $ds = \sqrt{5} \, dt$. In the integrand the variable $x$ is replaced by the first component of the parametrization, that is, $x = t$. What remains is the integral of $t$ over $[0, 1]$, which equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, **multiplied by the constant** $\sqrt{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the line integral
$$
\int_\gamma x \, ds, \qquad \gamma(t) = (t,\; t^2), \quad t \in [0, 1].
$$

$$
\gamma'(t) = (1,\; 2t), \qquad \lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{1 + 4t^2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\gamma x \, ds &= \int_0^1 t \sqrt{1 + 4t^2} \; dt \\[6pt]
&= \left[ \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \left( 1 + 4t^2 \right)^{3/2} \right]_0^1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{12} \left( 5^{3/2} - 1 \right) = \frac{5\sqrt{5} - 1}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The curve is the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$ between the origin and the point $(1, 1)$. The length element is $ds = \sqrt{1 + 4t^2} \, dt$ and the integrand becomes $t$. The integral is solved by recognizing that the factor $t$ is, up to the constant $8$, the **derivative of the argument** $1 + 4t^2$: an antiderivative of $t\left(1 + 4t^2\right)^{1/2}$ is $\dfrac{1}{8} \cdot \dfrac{2}{3}\left(1 + 4t^2\right)^{3/2}$, that is, $\dfrac{1}{12}\left(1 + 4t^2\right)^{3/2}$. At $t = 1$ the argument equals $5$ and at $t = 0$ it equals $1$, hence the **result** is $\dfrac{5\sqrt{5} - 1}{12}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the line integral
$$
\int_\gamma z \, ds, \qquad \gamma(t) = (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; t), \quad t \in [0, 2\pi].
$$

$$
\gamma'(t) = (-\sin t,\; \cos t,\; 1), \qquad \lVert \gamma'(t) \rVert = \sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\int_\gamma z \, ds = \int_0^{2\pi} t \cdot \sqrt{2} \, dt
= \sqrt{2} \left[ \frac{t^2}{2} \right]_0^{2\pi} = 2\sqrt{2}\, \pi^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the helix the norm of the derivative vector is **constant** and equals $\sqrt{2}$, since $\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t + 1 = 2$; hence $ds = \sqrt{2} \, dt$. The integrand is the height $z$, which along the curve **coincides with the parameter** $t$. The integral of $t$ over $[0, 2\pi]$ equals $\dfrac{(2\pi)^2}{2} = 2\pi^2$, and multiplying by $\sqrt{2}$ one obtains $2\sqrt{2}\,\pi^2$.

</div>
