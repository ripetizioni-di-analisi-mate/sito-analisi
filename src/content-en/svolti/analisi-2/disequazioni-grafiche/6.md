## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
\frac{x^2}{4} - y^2 > 1 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2 = 4 \quad &\Longrightarrow \quad a = 2 \\[6pt]
b^2 = 1 \quad &\Longrightarrow \quad b = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(\pm 2,\, 0)
$$
$$
y = \pm \frac{b}{a}\, x = \pm \frac{1}{2}\, x
$$
$$
F(x, y) = \frac{x^2}{4} - y^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 0) &= 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(4, 0) &= \frac{16}{4} - 0 = 4 > 1 \\[6pt]
F(-4, 0) &= \frac{16}{4} - 0 = 4 > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2/4-y^2>1" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 segmenti="-5,-2.5,5,2.5;-5,2.5,5,-2.5" punti="0,0,pieno;4,0,pieno;-4,0,pieno" titolo="$\dfrac{x^2}{4} - y^2 > 1$ with the asymptotes and the three test points"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The terms $x^2$ and $y^2$ have coefficients of opposite signs, so the boundary is a hyperbola, already in canonical form with center at the origin. The term with the plus sign is the one in $x^2$, so the branches are one to the right and one to the left, with vertices at $(\pm 2, 0)$. From $a = 2$ and $b = 1$ we obtain the asymptotes $y = \pm \dfrac{1}{2} x$. The two branches divide the plane into three regions, and in each of them we compute the left-hand side $F$ at a **test point**. At the origin we have $F = 0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is false in the region between the two branches. At the points $(4, 0)$ and $(-4, 0)$ we have $F = 4$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is true in the two regions that contain them. The solutions are the two **regions inside the branches**. The symbol is $>$, hence the branches are **excluded** and are drawn as dashed lines.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
y^2 - x^2 \leq 4 .
$$

$$
\frac{y^2}{4} - \frac{x^2}{4} \leq 1
$$
$$
a = 2, \qquad b = 2
$$
$$
(0,\, \pm 2)
$$
$$
y = \pm \frac{b}{a}\, x = \pm x
$$
$$
F(x, y) = \frac{y^2}{4} - \frac{x^2}{4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 0) &= 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(0, 3) &= \frac{9}{4} - 0 = \frac{9}{4} > 1 \\[6pt]
F(0, -3) &= \frac{9}{4} - 0 = \frac{9}{4} > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="y^2-x^2<=4" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 segmenti="-5,-5,5,5;-5,5,5,-5" punti="0,0,pieno;0,3,pieno;0,-3,pieno" titolo="$y^2 - x^2 \leq 4$, that is, $\dfrac{y^2}{4} - \dfrac{x^2}{4} \leq 1$, with the asymptotes $y = \pm x$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $y^2$ and $x^2$ have opposite signs, so the boundary is a hyperbola with center at the origin. We divide both sides by $4$, which is positive, and obtain the canonical form with $1$ on the right-hand side. The term with the plus sign is the one in $y^2$, so the branches are one above and one below, with vertices at $(0, \pm 2)$. Since $a = b = 2$, the asymptotes are the lines $y = \pm x$. At the origin the left-hand side $F$ equals $0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is true in the region between the two branches. At the points $(0, 3)$ and $(0, -3)$ we have $F = \dfrac{9}{4}$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is false in the two regions inside the branches. The solutions form the **region between the two branches**. The symbol is $\leq$, hence the branches are **included** and are drawn as solid lines.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
4x^2 - y^2 + 8x + 2y - 1 \geq 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(x^2 + 2x) - (y^2 - 2y) - 1 &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
4\left[ (x + 1)^2 - 1 \right] - \left[ (y - 1)^2 - 1 \right] - 1 &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
4(x + 1)^2 - 4 - (y - 1)^2 + 1 - 1 &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
4(x + 1)^2 - (y - 1)^2 &\geq 4 \\[6pt]
(x + 1)^2 - \frac{(y - 1)^2}{4} &\geq 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(-1, 1), \qquad a = 1, \qquad b = 2
$$
$$
(-2,\, 1), \qquad (0,\, 1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 1 &= \pm 2 (x + 1) \\[6pt]
y = 2x + 3, &\qquad y = -2x - 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
F(x, y) = (x + 1)^2 - \frac{(y - 1)^2}{4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(-1, 1) &= 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(1, 1) &= 4 - 0 = 4 > 1 \\[6pt]
F(-3, 1) &= 4 - 0 = 4 > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="4*x^2-y^2+8*x+2*y-1>=0" xmin=-6 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=6 uguale=1 segmenti="-3.5,-4,1.5,6;-3.5,6,1.5,-4" punti="-1,1,pieno,C" titolo="$(x + 1)^2 - \dfrac{(y - 1)^2}{4} \geq 1$ with the asymptotes"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are $4$ and $-1$, of opposite signs, so the boundary is a hyperbola. We factor out $4$ in the terms in $x$ and $-1$ in the terms in $y$, then we **complete the squares** with $x^2 + 2x = (x + 1)^2 - 1$ and $y^2 - 2y = (y - 1)^2 - 1$. We move the constant term to the right-hand side and divide both sides by $4$, which is positive, so the direction does not change. The center is $C(-1, 1)$ and the term with the plus sign is the one in $x$, so the branches are to the right and to the left of the center, with vertices at $(-1 \pm 1, 1)$. From $a = 1$ and $b = 2$ we obtain the asymptotes $y - 1 = \pm 2(x + 1)$, that is, the lines $y = 2x + 3$ and $y = -2x - 1$. At the center the left-hand side $F$ equals $0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is false in the region between the two branches. At the points $(1, 1)$ and $(-3, 1)$ we have $F = 4$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is true in the two regions that contain them. The solutions are the two **regions inside the branches**, and since the symbol is $\geq$ the branches are **included** and are drawn as solid lines.

</div>
