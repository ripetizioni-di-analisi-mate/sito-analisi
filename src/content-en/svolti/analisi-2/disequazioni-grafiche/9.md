## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
y \geq e^x - 1 .
$$

$$
f(x) = e^x - 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(0) &= e^0 - 1 = 0 \\[6pt]
f(1) &= e - 1 \approx 1.72
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -\infty} \left( e^x - 1 \right) = 0 - 1 = -1
$$

::grafico{regione="y>=exp(x)-1" xmin=-4 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=5 uguale=1 segmenti="-4,-1,3,-1" punti="0,0,pieno;1,1.7183,pieno" titolo="$y \geq e^x - 1$ with the asymptote $y = -1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The variable $y$ is isolated and on the right-hand side there is the function $f(x) = e^x - 1$, defined for every $x$, so the boundary is the graph of $f$. This graph is that of $e^x$ translated downward by $1$. It passes through the origin, because $e^0 - 1 = 0$, and through the point $(1, e - 1)$, with $e - 1 \approx 1.72$. For $x \to -\infty$ we have $e^x \to 0$, so the line $y = -1$ is a horizontal asymptote, and the graph lies above this line because $e^x > 0$. By the **isolated variable rule**, the inequality $y \geq f(x)$ is true at the points **above the graph**. The symbol is $\geq$, hence the graph is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
y < \ln(x + 1) .
$$

$$
x + 1 > 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x > -1
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x > -1 \\[4pt]
y < \ln(x + 1)
\end{cases}
$$
$$
f(x) = \ln(x + 1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(0) &= \ln 1 = 0 \\[6pt]
f(e - 1) &= \ln e = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lim_{x \to -1^+} \ln(x + 1) = -\infty
$$

::grafico{regione="y<log(x+1)" xmin=-2 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=3 uguale=1 segmenti="-1,-4,-1,3" titolo="$y < \ln(x + 1)$ with the asymptote $x = -1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The logarithm is defined when its argument is positive, so the inequality is equivalent to the system formed by the condition $x > -1$ and by the inequality itself. The boundary is the graph of $f(x) = \ln(x + 1)$, which is the graph of $\ln x$ translated to the left by $1$. It passes through the origin, because $\ln 1 = 0$, and through the point $(e - 1, 1)$, with $e - 1 \approx 1.72$. For $x \to -1^+$ we have $f(x) \to -\infty$, so the line $x = -1$ is a vertical asymptote. The variable $y$ is isolated with the symbol $<$, so by the **isolated variable rule** the solutions are the points **below the graph**, which all lie to the right of the line $x = -1$. The graph is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
\ln(x^2 + y^2) \leq 0 .
$$

$$
x^2 + y^2 > 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad (x, y) \neq (0, 0)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln(x^2 + y^2) &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
\ln(x^2 + y^2) &\leq \ln 1 \\[6pt]
x^2 + y^2 &\leq 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 > 0 \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 \leq 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
0 < x^2 + y^2 \leq 1
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+y^2<=1; x^2+y^2>0" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="0,0,vuoto" titolo="$\ln(x^2 + y^2) \leq 0$, that is, $0 < x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The logarithm is defined when $x^2 + y^2 > 0$. A sum of squares is zero only if both squares are zero, so this condition excludes the origin alone. We write $0$ as $\ln 1$. The function $\ln t$ is strictly increasing, so it preserves the direction, and the inequality between the logarithms is equivalent to $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$. The two conditions must hold together, so they form a system, which is summarized in the double inequality $0 < x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$. The left-hand side of $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$ is the square of the distance from the origin, so this condition describes the interior points of the circle with center the origin and radius $1$, together with the circle. The solutions form the **disk with the center removed**. The circle is **included** and is drawn as a solid line, while the origin is excluded and is marked with a hollow point.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
\sqrt{x - y^2} < 2 .
$$

$$
x - y^2 \geq 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x - y^2} &< 2 \\[6pt]
x - y^2 &< 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x - y^2 \geq 0 \\[4pt]
x - y^2 < 4
\end{cases}
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\begin{cases}
x \geq y^2 \\[4pt]
x < y^2 + 4
\end{cases}
$$
$$
V_1(0, 0), \qquad V_2(4, 0)
$$
$$
y^2 = y^2 + 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 0 = 4
$$

::grafico{regione="x>=y^2; x<y^2+4" xmin=-1 xmax=9 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,V₁;4,0,pieno,V₂" titolo="$\sqrt{x - y^2} < 2$, that is, $y^2 \leq x < y^2 + 4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The root is defined when the radicand is greater than or equal to zero, so the first condition is $x - y^2 \geq 0$. Both sides of the inequality are greater than or equal to zero, so squaring them preserves the direction and we obtain $x - y^2 < 4$. The two conditions must hold together, so they form a system, in which we isolate $x$. The curve $x = y^2$ is the parabola with horizontal axis and vertex at the origin, which opens to the right, and the curve $x = y^2 + 4$ is the same parabola translated to the right by $4$, with vertex $(4, 0)$. Subtracting $y^2$ from both sides of the equation $y^2 = y^2 + 4$ we obtain the equality $0 = 4$, which is false, so the two parabolas do not meet. By the isolated variable rule the solutions are the points to the right of the first parabola and to the left of the second, that is, the **region between the two parabolas**. The parabola $x = y^2$ is **included** and is drawn as a solid line, while the parabola $x = y^2 + 4$ is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>
