## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
3x - 2y < 6 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2y &< -3x + 6 \\[6pt]
y &> \frac{3}{2} x - 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0: \quad & y = -3 \\[6pt]
y = 0: \quad & \frac{3}{2} x - 3 = 0, \quad x = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="3*x-2*y<6" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="$3x - 2y < 6$, that is, $y > \dfrac{3}{2} x - 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is of first degree in $x$ and in $y$, so the boundary is the line $3x - 2y = 6$. To isolate $y$ we move $3x$ to the right-hand side and obtain $-2y < -3x + 6$. Dividing both sides by $-2$, which is a negative number, the direction of the inequality changes, so $y > \dfrac{3}{2} x - 3$. The line $y = \dfrac{3}{2} x - 3$ passes through the points $(0, -3)$ and $(2, 0)$, and it is drawn with these two points. The variable $y$ is isolated with the symbol $>$, hence by the **isolated variable rule** the solutions are the points **above the line**. The symbol is strict, so the line is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x + 2y \geq 0 .
$$

$$
x + 2y = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0: \quad & 2y = 0, \quad y = 0 \\[6pt]
x = 2: \quad & 2 + 2y = 0, \quad y = -1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
P(0, 1): \quad 0 + 2 \cdot 1 = 2 \geq 0
$$

::grafico{regione="x+2*y>=0" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="0,1,pieno,P" titolo="$x + 2y \geq 0$ with the test point $P(0, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality is of first degree in $x$ and in $y$, so the boundary is the line $x + 2y = 0$. The constant term is zero, hence the line passes through the origin; a second point of the line is $(2, -1)$. The left-hand side has constant sign in each of the two half-planes, so it suffices to compute it at a **test point**. At the origin, which belongs to the line, the left-hand side equals $0$, hence as a test point we take $P(0, 1)$, which does not lie on the line. At $P$ the left-hand side equals $0 + 2 \cdot 1 = 2$ and $2 \geq 0$ is true, so the solutions are the points of the **half-plane that contains** $P$, that is, the one above the line. The symbol $\geq$ includes equality, hence the line is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
-1 < x \leq 3 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x > -1 \\[4pt]
x \leq 3
\end{cases}
$$

::grafico{regione="x>-1; x<=3" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="$-1 < x \leq 3$: line $x = -1$ excluded, line $x = 3$ included"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The double inequality is equivalent to the system formed by $x > -1$ and $x \leq 3$, because the two conditions must hold together. The variable $y$ does not appear, so the $y$-coordinate of the points is free and the two boundaries are the vertical lines $x = -1$ and $x = 3$. By the isolated variable rule, $x > -1$ describes the half-plane to the right of the line $x = -1$ and $x \leq 3$ describes the half-plane to the left of the line $x = 3$. The solutions are the points common to the two half-planes, that is, the **vertical strip** lying between the two lines. The line $x = -1$ has the symbol $<$, so it is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line. The line $x = 3$ has the symbol $\leq$, so it is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
|x + y| \leq 2 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2 \leq x + y &\leq 2 \\[6pt]
-x - 2 \leq y &\leq -x + 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y \geq -x - 2 \\[4pt]
y \leq -x + 2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y = -x - 2: \quad & (-2, 0), \quad (0, -2) \\[6pt]
y = -x + 2: \quad & (2, 0), \quad (0, 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="y>=-x-2; y<=-x+2" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="$|x + y| \leq 2$: strip between $y = -x - 2$ and $y = -x + 2$, lines included"}

<div class="spiegazione">

For $c \geq 0$ the inequality $|F| \leq c$ is equivalent to $-c \leq F \leq c$, so $|x + y| \leq 2$ is equivalent to $-2 \leq x + y \leq 2$. Subtracting $x$ from the three members we isolate $y$ and obtain $-x - 2 \leq y \leq -x + 2$, that is, a system of two inequalities. The boundaries are the lines $y = -x - 2$ and $y = -x + 2$, which have the same slope $-1$ and are therefore parallel. The first passes through $(-2, 0)$ and $(0, -2)$, the second through $(2, 0)$ and $(0, 2)$. By the **isolated variable rule** the solutions lie above the line $y = -x - 2$ and below the line $y = -x + 2$, so they form the **strip** lying between the two lines. The symbols of the two inequalities include equality, hence both lines are **included** and are drawn as solid lines.

</div>
