## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 \leq 9 \\[4pt]
x + y \geq 3
\end{cases}
$$

$$
x^2 + y^2 \leq 3^2
$$
$$
x + y \geq 3 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y \geq 3 - x
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + (3 - x)^2 &= 9 \\[6pt]
2x^2 - 6x + 9 &= 9 \\[6pt]
2x(x - 3) &= 0 \\[6pt]
x = 0 \quad &\text{or} \quad x = 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
A(3, 0), \qquad B(0, 3)
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+y^2<=9; x+y>=3" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="3,0,pieno,A;0,3,pieno,B" titolo="$x^2 + y^2 \leq 9$ and $x + y \geq 3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first inequality contains $x^2$ and $y^2$ with the same coefficient, so its boundary is the circle with center the origin and radius $3$. The square of the distance from the origin is less than or equal to $9$, so the first inequality describes the interior points together with the circle. In the second inequality we isolate $y$ and obtain $y \geq 3 - x$. By the **isolated variable rule** its solutions are the points above the line $y = 3 - x$, line included. The solutions of the system are the points that satisfy both inequalities, that is, the intersection of the two regions. Substituting $y = 3 - x$ into the equation of the circle we obtain $2x(x - 3) = 0$, so the line meets the circle at the points $A(3, 0)$ and $B(0, 3)$. The region is the **part of the disk that lies above the line**, bounded by the segment $AB$ and by the arc of the circle in the first quadrant. The two symbols are $\leq$ and $\geq$, so the segment and the arc are **included** and drawn as solid lines, and the points $A$ and $B$ belong to the region.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the system
$$
\begin{cases}
y \geq x^2 - 2 \\[4pt]
y < x
\end{cases}
$$

$$
y = x^2 - 2 \qquad x_V = 0, \quad y_V = -2
$$
$$
x^2 - 2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \pm\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 - 2 &= x \\[6pt]
x^2 - x - 2 &= 0 \\[6pt]
(x + 1)(x - 2) &= 0 \\[6pt]
x = -1 \quad &\text{or} \quad x = 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(-1, -1), \qquad (2, 2)
$$

::grafico{regione="y>=x^2-2; y<x" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-3 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="0,-2,pieno,V;-1,-1,vuoto;2,2,vuoto" titolo="$y \geq x^2 - 2$ and $y < x$: the points $(-1, -1)$ and $(2, 2)$ are excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In both inequalities the variable $y$ is isolated, so the two regions are read off with the **isolated variable rule**. The boundary of the first is the parabola $y = x^2 - 2$, which has a vertical axis, vertex $V(0, -2)$ and opens upward, and meets the $x$-axis at $x = \pm\sqrt{2}$. The symbol is $\geq$, so the first inequality describes the points above the parabola, parabola included. The second inequality describes the points below the line $y = x$, line excluded. Equating the right-hand sides we obtain $x^2 - x - 2 = 0$, so the parabola and the line meet at $(-1, -1)$ and $(2, 2)$. The region is the one **lying between the parabola and the line**, formed by the points with $-1 < x < 2$ and $x^2 - 2 \leq y < x$. The arc of the parabola is included and drawn as a solid line, while the segment of the line is excluded and dashed. The points $(-1, -1)$ and $(2, 2)$ lie on the line, which is excluded, so they **do not belong to the region** and are drawn as hollow points.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq y^2 \\[4pt]
x \leq 2 - y^2
\end{cases}
$$

$$
x = y^2 \qquad y_V = 0, \quad x_V = 0
$$
$$
x = -y^2 + 2 \qquad y_V = 0, \quad x_V = 2
$$
$$
-y^2 + 2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y = \pm\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y^2 &= 2 - y^2 \\[6pt]
2y^2 &= 2 \\[6pt]
y &= \pm 1 \\[6pt]
x &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
A(1, 1), \qquad B(1, -1)
$$

::grafico{regione="x>=y^2; x<=2-y^2" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="2,0,pieno;1,1,pieno;1,-1,pieno" etichette="2.12,0.18,V;1.1,1.2,A;1.1,-1.38,B" titolo="$x \geq y^2$ and $x \leq 2 - y^2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In both inequalities only $y$ appears squared, so the boundaries are two parabolas with horizontal axis, and the variable $x$ is already isolated. The parabola $x = y^2$ has its vertex at the origin and opens to the right. The parabola $x = 2 - y^2$ has a negative coefficient of $y^2$, so it opens to the left, has vertex $V(2, 0)$ and meets the $y$-axis at $y = \pm\sqrt{2}$. By the **isolated variable rule** the first inequality describes the points to the right of the parabola $x = y^2$ and the second the points to the left of the parabola $x = 2 - y^2$. Equating the right-hand sides we obtain $2y^2 = 2$, so the two parabolas meet at $A(1, 1)$ and $B(1, -1)$. The region is the one **lying between the two parabolas**, formed by the points with $-1 \leq y \leq 1$ and $y^2 \leq x \leq 2 - y^2$. The symbols are $\geq$ and $\leq$, hence the two arcs of parabola are **included** and drawn as solid lines, and the points $A$ and $B$ belong to the region.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve graphically the system
$$
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x^2}{4} + y^2 \leq 1 \\[8pt]
xy > 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2 = 4 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a = 2 \\[6pt]
b^2 = 1 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(\pm 2, 0), \qquad (0, \pm 1)
$$
$$
(0, 0): \quad \frac{0^2}{4} + 0^2 = 0 \leq 1
$$
$$
xy = 0 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ x = 0 \ \text{ or } \ y = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(1, 1): &\quad xy = 1 > 0 \\[6pt]
(-1, 1): &\quad xy = -1 < 0 \\[6pt]
(-1, -1): &\quad xy = 1 > 0 \\[6pt]
(1, -1): &\quad xy = -1 < 0
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2/4+y^2<=1; x*y>0" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="2,0,vuoto;-2,0,vuoto;0,1,vuoto;0,-1,vuoto" titolo="$\dfrac{x^2}{4} + y^2 \leq 1$ and $xy > 0$: the four vertices of the ellipse are excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first inequality $x^2$ and $y^2$ have coefficients of the same sign and different from each other, so the boundary is an ellipse. The equation is already in canonical form, with center the origin and semi-axes $a = 2$ and $b = 1$, hence the vertices are $(\pm 2, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 1)$. At the center the left-hand side equals $0$, which is less than $1$, so the first inequality describes the interior points together with the ellipse. The boundary of the second inequality is $xy = 0$, that is, the pair of coordinate axes, which divides the plane into the four quadrants. With a **test point** we find that the product $xy$ equals $1$ at $(1, 1)$ and at $(-1, -1)$ and equals $-1$ at $(-1, 1)$ and at $(1, -1)$, hence $xy > 0$ describes the first and third quadrants, axes excluded. The region is formed by the **two quarters of the region bounded by the ellipse** in the first and third quadrants. The arcs of the ellipse are included and drawn as solid lines, while the pieces of the axes are excluded and dashed. The four vertices of the ellipse lie on the axes, so they **do not belong to the region** and are drawn as hollow points.

</div>
