## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x^2 + y^2 > 9 .
$$

$$
C(0, 0), \qquad r = \sqrt{9} = 3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + y^2 &> 3^2 \\[6pt]
\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} &> 3
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+y^2>9" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 titolo="$x^2 + y^2 > 9$: exterior points of the circle with center the origin and radius $3$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $x^2$ and of $y^2$ are equal, so the boundary is a circle. The equation $x^2 + y^2 = 9$ has the form $(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2 = r^2$ with $x_0 = y_0 = 0$ and $r^2 = 9$, hence the center is the origin and the radius is $r = 3$. The left-hand side $x^2 + y^2$ is the square of the distance of the point $(x, y)$ from the origin. The inequality is therefore satisfied by the points whose distance from the origin is greater than $3$, that is, by the **exterior points** of the circle. The symbol is strict, hence the circle is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x^2 + y^2 + 4x - 6y + 9 \leq 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2 + 4x) + (y^2 - 6y) + 9 &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
(x + 2)^2 - 4 + (y - 3)^2 - 9 + 9 &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
(x + 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2 &\leq 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(-2, 3), \qquad r = \sqrt{4} = 2
$$
$$
C(-2, 3): \quad (-2)^2 + 3^2 + 4 \cdot (-2) - 6 \cdot 3 + 9 = -4 \leq 0
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+y^2+4*x-6*y+9<=0" xmin=-6 xmax=2 ymin=-1 ymax=7 uguale=1 punti="-2,3,pieno,C" titolo="$x^2 + y^2 + 4x - 6y + 9 \leq 0$, that is, $(x + 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2 \leq 4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The inequality contains $x^2$ and $y^2$ with the same coefficient, so the boundary is a circle and we complete the squares. The binomial $x^2 + 4x$ is equal to $(x + 2)^2 - 4$ and the binomial $y^2 - 6y$ is equal to $(y - 3)^2 - 9$. Substituting and moving the constant terms to the right-hand side we obtain $(x + 2)^2 + (y - 3)^2 \leq 4$, hence the center is $C(-2, 3)$ and the radius is $r = 2$. As a **test point** we take the center, at which the left-hand side of the initial inequality equals $-4$. Since $-4 \leq 0$ is true, the solutions are the **interior points** of the circle. The symbol $\leq$ includes equality, so the circle is **included** and is drawn as a solid line. The region is the disk with center $C$ and radius $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
4 \leq (x - 1)^2 + y^2 < 9 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
(x - 1)^2 + y^2 \geq 4 \\[4pt]
(x - 1)^2 + y^2 < 9
\end{cases}
$$
$$
C(1, 0), \qquad r_1 = \sqrt{4} = 2, \qquad r_2 = \sqrt{9} = 3
$$
$$
2 \leq \sqrt{(x - 1)^2 + y^2} < 3
$$

::grafico{regione="(x-1)^2+y^2>=4; (x-1)^2+y^2<9" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="1,0,pieno,C" titolo="$4 \leq (x - 1)^2 + y^2 < 9$: inner circle included, outer circle excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The double inequality is equivalent to the system formed by $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 \geq 4$ and $(x - 1)^2 + y^2 < 9$. The two boundaries are circles with the same center $C(1, 0)$ and with radii $r_1 = 2$ and $r_2 = 3$. The left-hand side $(x - 1)^2 + y^2$ is the square of the distance of the point $(x, y)$ from $C$. The first inequality is therefore satisfied by the points at distance from $C$ greater than or equal to $2$, that is, by the exterior points of the circle of radius $2$ and by the points of the circle itself. The second is satisfied by the points at distance from $C$ less than $3$, that is, by the interior points of the circle of radius $3$. The solutions of the system therefore form the **annulus** lying between the two circles. The inner circle has the symbol $\geq$, so it is **included** and is drawn as a solid line; the outer one has the symbol $<$, so it is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x^2 + y^2 \leq 4y .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + y^2 - 4y &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
x^2 + (y - 2)^2 - 4 &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
x^2 + (y - 2)^2 &\leq 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(0, 2), \qquad r = \sqrt{4} = 2
$$
$$
(0, 0): \quad 0^2 + 0^2 = 4 \cdot 0
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+y^2<=4*y" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="0,2,pieno,C" titolo="$x^2 + y^2 \leq 4y$, that is, $x^2 + (y - 2)^2 \leq 4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

We move $4y$ to the left-hand side and obtain $x^2 + y^2 - 4y \leq 0$. The coefficients of $x^2$ and of $y^2$ are equal, so the boundary is a circle. The first-degree term in $x$ does not appear, hence we complete the square only in $y$, with $y^2 - 4y = (y - 2)^2 - 4$. The inequality becomes $x^2 + (y - 2)^2 \leq 4$, so the center is $C(0, 2)$ and the radius is $r = 2$. The pair $(0, 0)$ satisfies the equation $x^2 + y^2 = 4y$, hence the circle passes through the origin. The left-hand side $x^2 + (y - 2)^2$ is the square of the distance of the point $(x, y)$ from $C$, so the inequality is satisfied by the points at distance from $C$ less than or equal to $2$, that is, by the **interior points** and by the points of the circle. The symbol $\leq$ includes equality, so the circle is **included** and is drawn as a solid line. The region is the disk with center $C$ and radius $2$.

</div>
