## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x < y^2 - 1 .
$$

$$
a = 1, \qquad b = 0, \qquad c = -1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y_V &= -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{0}{2 \cdot 1} = 0 \\[6pt]
x_V &= 0^2 - 1 = -1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0: \quad & y^2 - 1 = 0 \\[6pt]
& y^2 = 1 \\[6pt]
& y = \pm 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x<y^2-1" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="-1,0,pieno,V" titolo="$x < y^2 - 1$: vertex $V(-1, 0)$, intersections with the $y$-axis at $-1$ and $1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The variable $y$ appears squared and $x$ to the first degree, so the boundary is the parabola with horizontal axis $x = y^2 - 1$. The coefficient of $y^2$ is $a = 1$, positive, hence the parabola opens to the right. In parabolas with horizontal axis the formula $-\dfrac{b}{2a}$ gives the $y$-coordinate of the vertex, so $y_V = 0$ and, substituting into the equation, $x_V = -1$. The vertex is $V(-1, 0)$. Setting $x = 0$ we obtain $y = \pm 1$, hence the parabola meets the $y$-axis at the points $(0, -1)$ and $(0, 1)$. The variable $x$ is isolated with the symbol $<$, so by the **isolated variable rule** the solutions are the points **to the left of the parabola**. The symbol is strict, hence the parabola is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x \geq 4 - y^2 .
$$

$$
x - 4 = -y^2
$$
$$
V(4, 0), \qquad a = -1 < 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0: \quad & 4 - y^2 = 0 \\[6pt]
& y^2 = 4 \\[6pt]
& y = \pm 2
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x>=4-y^2" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="4,0,pieno,V" titolo="$x \geq 4 - y^2$: vertex $V(4, 0)$, intersections with the $y$-axis at $-2$ and $2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The variable $y$ appears squared and $x$ to the first degree, so the boundary is the parabola with horizontal axis $x = 4 - y^2$. The equation is written in the form $x - 4 = -y^2$, so the boundary is the parabola $x = -y^2$ translated so that the vertex is $V(4, 0)$. The coefficient of $y^2$ is $-1$, negative, hence the parabola opens to the left. Setting $x = 0$ we obtain $y = \pm 2$, so the parabola meets the $y$-axis at the points $(0, -2)$ and $(0, 2)$. The variable $x$ is isolated with the symbol $\geq$, hence by the **isolated variable rule** the solutions are the points **to the right of the parabola**. The symbol includes equality, so the parabola is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
y^2 + 2y - x - 3 \leq 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^2 + 2y - 3 &\leq x \\[6pt]
x &\geq y^2 + 2y - 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y^2 + 2y - 3 &= (y + 1)^2 - 1 - 3 \\[6pt]
&= (y + 1)^2 - 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x \geq (y + 1)^2 - 4, \qquad V(-4, -1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0: \quad & y^2 + 2y - 3 = 0 \\[6pt]
& (y + 3)(y - 1) = 0 \\[6pt]
& y_1 = -3, \quad y_2 = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
y = 0: \quad x = -3
$$

::grafico{regione="y^2+2*y-x-3<=0" xmin=-5 xmax=3 ymin=-5 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="-4,-1,pieno,V" titolo="$y^2 + 2y - x - 3 \leq 0$, that is, $x \geq (y + 1)^2 - 4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the inequality $y^2$ appears and $x^2$ does not, so the boundary is a parabola with horizontal axis and the variable to isolate is $x$. Moving $x$ to the right-hand side we obtain $y^2 + 2y - 3 \leq x$, that is, $x \geq y^2 + 2y - 3$. By **completing the square** the binomial $y^2 + 2y$ becomes $(y + 1)^2 - 1$, so the inequality is written $x \geq (y + 1)^2 - 4$ and the vertex is $V(-4, -1)$. The coefficient of $y^2$ is $1$, positive, hence the parabola opens to the right. The trinomial $y^2 + 2y - 3$ factors into the product $(y + 3)(y - 1)$, so the parabola meets the $y$-axis at the points $(0, -3)$ and $(0, 1)$. For $y = 0$ we have $x = -3$, hence the parabola meets the $x$-axis at the point $(-3, 0)$. The variable $x$ is isolated with the symbol $\geq$, so the solutions are the points **to the right of the parabola**. The symbol includes equality, hence the parabola is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
y^2 + 4x - 4y \leq 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x &\leq -y^2 + 4y \\[6pt]
x &\leq -\frac{1}{4} y^2 + y
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{1}{4} y^2 + y &= -\frac{1}{4} \left( y^2 - 4y \right) \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{1}{4} \left[ (y - 2)^2 - 4 \right] \\[6pt]
&= -\frac{1}{4} (y - 2)^2 + 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x \leq -\frac{1}{4} (y - 2)^2 + 1, \qquad V(1, 2)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0: \quad & y^2 - 4y = 0 \\[6pt]
& y (y - 4) = 0 \\[6pt]
& y_1 = 0, \quad y_2 = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="y^2+4*x-4*y<=0" xmin=-5 xmax=3 ymin=-2 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="1,2,pieno,V" titolo="$y^2 + 4x - 4y \leq 0$, that is, $x \leq -\dfrac{1}{4} (y - 2)^2 + 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the inequality $y^2$ appears and $x^2$ does not, so the boundary is a parabola with horizontal axis and the variable to isolate is $x$. We move $y^2$ and $-4y$ to the right-hand side and divide by $4$, which is positive, hence the direction stays the same and we obtain $x \leq -\dfrac{1}{4} y^2 + y$. To complete the square we factor out the coefficient of $y^2$ and write $y^2 - 4y = (y - 2)^2 - 4$, so the right-hand side becomes $-\dfrac{1}{4} (y - 2)^2 + 1$ and the vertex is $V(1, 2)$. The coefficient of $y^2$ is negative, hence the parabola opens to the left. Setting $x = 0$ in the equation $y^2 + 4x - 4y = 0$ we obtain $y^2 - 4y = 0$, that is, $y(y - 4) = 0$, so the parabola meets the $y$-axis at the points $(0, 0)$ and $(0, 4)$. The variable $x$ is isolated with the symbol $\leq$, hence by the **isolated variable rule** the solutions are the points **to the left of the parabola**. The symbol includes equality, so the parabola is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>
