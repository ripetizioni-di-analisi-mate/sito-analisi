## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
xy < 2 .
$$

$$
xy = 2
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y = x \\[4pt]
xy = 2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
x^2 = 2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = \pm\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\left( \sqrt{2},\, \sqrt{2} \right), \qquad \left( -\sqrt{2},\, -\sqrt{2} \right)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = xy
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 0) &= 0 < 2 \\[6pt]
F(2, 2) &= 4 > 2 \\[6pt]
F(-2, -2) &= 4 > 2
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x*y<2" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno;2,2,pieno;-2,-2,pieno" titolo="$xy < 2$ with the three test points"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The boundary $xy = 2$ is a rectangular hyperbola referred to its asymptotes, which are the coordinate axes. The right-hand side is positive, so the branches lie in the first and third quadrants, where $x$ and $y$ have the same sign. The vertices are the points of the branches on the line $y = x$, and from the system we obtain $x^2 = 2$, so they are $\left( \sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2} \right)$ and $\left( -\sqrt{2}, -\sqrt{2} \right)$, with $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$. The two branches divide the plane into three regions, and in each of them we compute the product $F = xy$ at a **test point**. At the origin we have $F = 0$, which is less than $2$, so the inequality is true in the region between the two branches. At the points $(2, 2)$ and $(-2, -2)$ we have $F = 4$, which is greater than $2$, so the inequality is false in the two regions beyond the branches. The solutions form the **region between the two branches**, which contains the axes. The symbol is $<$, hence the branches are **excluded** and are drawn as dashed lines.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
xy \leq -1 .
$$

$$
xy = -1
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y = -x \\[4pt]
xy = -1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
-x^2 = -1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = \pm 1
$$
$$
(1,\, -1), \qquad (-1,\, 1)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = xy
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 0) &= 0 > -1 \\[6pt]
F(2, -2) &= -4 < -1 \\[6pt]
F(-2, 2) &= -4 < -1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x*y<=-1" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno;2,-2,pieno;-2,2,pieno" titolo="$xy \leq -1$ with the three test points"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The boundary $xy = -1$ is a rectangular hyperbola that has the coordinate axes as asymptotes. The right-hand side is negative, so the branches lie in the second and fourth quadrants, where $x$ and $y$ have opposite signs. The vertices are the points of the branches on the line $y = -x$, and from the system we obtain $x^2 = 1$, so they are $(1, -1)$ and $(-1, 1)$. In each of the three regions we compute the product $F = xy$ at a **test point**. At the origin we have $F = 0$, which is greater than $-1$, so the inequality is false in the region between the two branches. At the points $(2, -2)$ and $(-2, 2)$ we have $F = -4$, which is less than $-1$, so the inequality is true in the two regions that contain them. The solutions are the two **regions beyond the branches**, one in the second and one in the fourth quadrant. The symbol is $\leq$, hence the branches are **included** and are drawn as solid lines.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
(x - 1)(y + 2) > 1 .
$$

$$
(x - 1)(y + 2) = 1
$$
$$
x = 1, \qquad y = -2
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y + 2 = x - 1 \\[4pt]
(x - 1)(y + 2) = 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
(x - 1)^2 = 1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x - 1 = \pm 1
$$
$$
(2,\, -1), \qquad (0,\, -3)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = (x - 1)(y + 2)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(1, -2) &= 0 \cdot 0 = 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(3, 0) &= 2 \cdot 2 = 4 > 1 \\[6pt]
F(-1, -4) &= (-2) \cdot (-2) = 4 > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="(x-1)*(y+2)>1" xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-7 ymax=3 uguale=1 segmenti="1,-7,1,3;-4,-2,6,-2" punti="1,-2,pieno;3,0,pieno;-1,-4,pieno" titolo="$(x - 1)(y + 2) > 1$ with the asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = -2$ and the three test points"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The boundary $(x - 1)(y + 2) = 1$ is the rectangular hyperbola $xy = 1$ translated, with asymptotes $x = 1$ and $y = -2$, which meet at the point $(1, -2)$. The right-hand side is positive, so the branches are one above and to the right and one below and to the left of this point, where the factors $x - 1$ and $y + 2$ have the same sign. The vertices lie on the line $y + 2 = x - 1$, and from the system we obtain $(x - 1)^2 = 1$, so they are $(2, -1)$ and $(0, -3)$. In each of the three regions we compute the product $F$ at a **test point**. At the point $(1, -2)$ we have $F = 0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is false in the region between the two branches. At the points $(3, 0)$ and $(-1, -4)$ we have $F = 4$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is true in the two regions that contain them. The solutions are the two **regions beyond the branches**. The symbol is $>$, hence the branches are **excluded** and are drawn as dashed lines.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
y \leq \frac{2}{x - 1} .
$$

$$
x - 1 \neq 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x \neq 1
$$
$$
f(x) = \frac{2}{x - 1}
$$
$$
y = \frac{2}{x - 1} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad (x - 1)\, y = 2
$$
$$
x = 1, \qquad y = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(2) &= \frac{2}{1} = 2 \\[6pt]
f(3) &= \frac{2}{2} = 1 \\[6pt]
f(0) &= \frac{2}{-1} = -2 \\[6pt]
f(-1) &= \frac{2}{-2} = -1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x>1; (x-1)*y<=2 | x<1; (x-1)*y>=2" xmin=-4 xmax=6 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 tracce=0 punti="2,2,pieno;3,1,pieno;0,-2,pieno;-1,-1,pieno" titolo="$y \leq \dfrac{2}{x - 1}$: the points below the graph, with the line $x = 1$ excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction is defined for $x \neq 1$, so the vertical line $x = 1$ contains no solutions. The graph of $f(x) = \dfrac{2}{x - 1}$ is the rectangular hyperbola $(x - 1)\, y = 2$, which has the line $x = 1$ and the $x$-axis as asymptotes. The right branch, for $x > 1$, lies above the $x$-axis and passes through $(2, 2)$ and $(3, 1)$. The left branch, for $x < 1$, lies below the $x$-axis and passes through $(0, -2)$ and $(-1, -1)$. The variable $y$ is isolated with the symbol $\leq$, so by the **isolated variable rule** the solutions are the points **below the graph**, restricted to the $x$-coordinates $x \neq 1$. To the right of the line $x = 1$ the solutions are the points below the right branch, to the left they are the points below the left branch. The two branches are **included** and are drawn as solid lines, while the line $x = 1$ is excluded and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>
