## Exercise 1

Decompose into interior points, boundary pieces and vertices the set described by the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 \leq 9 \\[4pt]
x + y \geq 3
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 = 9 \\[4pt]
x + y = 3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 + (3 - x)^2 &= 9 \\[6pt]
2x^2 - 6x &= 0 \\[6pt]
2x(x - 3) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{vertices:} \qquad A(3, 0), \qquad B(0, 3)
$$
$$
\text{interior points:} \qquad
\begin{cases}
x^2 + y^2 < 9 \\[4pt]
x + y > 3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 3 \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 \leq 9
\end{cases}
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\begin{cases}
y = 3 - x \\[4pt]
2x(x - 3) \leq 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\text{first piece:} \quad
\begin{cases}
y = 3 - x \\[4pt]
0 \leq x \leq 3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
x = 3\cos t, \qquad y = 3\sin t
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
t = \frac{\pi}{4}: \quad x + y &= \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} + \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} \\[6pt]
&= 3\sqrt{2} > 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{second piece:} \quad
\begin{cases}
x = 3\cos t \\[4pt]
y = 3\sin t \\[4pt]
0 \leq t \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}
\end{cases}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+y^2<=9; x+y>=3" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="3,0,pieno,A;0,3,pieno,B" titolo="The segment $AB$, the arc of the circle and the vertices $A$, $B$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The system describes the part of the disk with center the origin and radius $3$ that lies above the line $y = 3 - x$, with the boundary included because the symbols are $\leq$ and $\geq$. The **interior points** are those at which both inequalities hold with the strict symbol. The **boundary pieces** are obtained by turning one inequality at a time into an equality and keeping the other. On the line $x + y = 3$ the condition $x^2 + y^2 \leq 9$ becomes $2x(x - 3) \leq 0$, that is, $0 \leq x \leq 3$, so the first piece is the segment $y = 3 - x$ with $0 \leq x \leq 3$. On the circle we use the parametrization $x = 3\cos t$, $y = 3\sin t$, in which $A$ and $B$ correspond to $t = 0$ and $t = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and divide the circle into two arcs. At the point $t = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ we have $x + y = 3\sqrt{2} > 3$, so the second piece is the arc with $0 \leq t \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The **vertices** are the points $A(3, 0)$ and $B(0, 3)$, where the two pieces meet. In the search for the maximum and the minimum of a differentiable function $f$ on this set, the candidates are the interior critical points, the extremum points of the restrictions $f(x, 3 - x)$ on $[0, 3]$ and $f(3\cos t, 3\sin t)$ on $\left[ 0, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right]$, and the vertices $A$ and $B$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Decompose into interior points, boundary pieces and vertices the set described by the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x \geq y^2 \\[4pt]
x \leq 4
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
x = y^2 \\[4pt]
x = 4
\end{cases}
$$
$$
y^2 = 4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y = \pm 2
$$
$$
\text{vertices:} \qquad A(4, 2), \qquad B(4, -2)
$$
$$
\text{interior points:} \qquad y^2 < x < 4
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x = y^2 \\[4pt]
x \leq 4
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x = y^2 \\[4pt]
y^2 \leq 4
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\text{first piece:} \quad
\begin{cases}
x = y^2 \\[4pt]
-2 \leq y \leq 2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x = 4 \\[4pt]
x \geq y^2
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x = 4 \\[4pt]
y^2 \leq 4
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\text{second piece:} \quad
\begin{cases}
x = 4 \\[4pt]
-2 \leq y \leq 2
\end{cases}
$$

::grafico{regione="x>=y^2; x<=4" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-3.5 ymax=3.5 uguale=1 punti="4,2,pieno,A;4,-2,pieno,B" titolo="The arc of the parabola, the segment $AB$ and the vertices $A$, $B$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The set is the region lying between the parabola $x = y^2$, which has a horizontal axis and its vertex at the origin, and the vertical line $x = 4$, with the boundary included. The **interior points** satisfy the two inequalities with the strict symbol, that is, $y^2 < x < 4$. From the equation $y^2 = 4$ we obtain the **vertices** $A(4, 2)$ and $B(4, -2)$, where the parabola meets the line. On the parabola the free variable is $y$ and the condition $x \leq 4$ becomes $y^2 \leq 4$, so the first **boundary piece** is the arc $x = y^2$ with $-2 \leq y \leq 2$. On the line $x = 4$ the condition $x \geq y^2$ becomes $y^2 \leq 4$, so the second piece is the segment $x = 4$ with $-2 \leq y \leq 2$. On these two pieces a function $f(x, y)$ becomes $f(y^2, y)$ and $f(4, y)$, two functions of $y$ alone to be studied on $[-2, 2]$. The origin is the vertex of the parabola and is a point of the arc, so it is covered by the study of the restriction $f(y^2, y)$ and is not added to the vertices of the set.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Decompose into interior points, boundary pieces and vertices the set described by the inequality
$$
|x| + |y| \leq 1 .
$$

We distinguish four cases, according to the sign of $x$ and of $y$.

- $\begin{cases} x \geq 0 \\ y \geq 0 \\ x + y \leq 1 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} x \leq 0 \\ y \geq 0 \\ -x + y \leq 1 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} x \leq 0 \\ y \leq 0 \\ -x - y \leq 1 \end{cases}$
- $\begin{cases} x \geq 0 \\ y \leq 0 \\ x - y \leq 1 \end{cases}$

$$
\text{interior points:} \qquad |x| + |y| < 1
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 1 \\[4pt]
x \geq 0 \\[4pt]
y \geq 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
y = 1 - x \\[4pt]
0 \leq x \leq 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\text{boundary pieces:}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 1 - x, & 0 &\leq x \leq 1 \\[6pt]
y &= 1 + x, & -1 &\leq x \leq 0 \\[6pt]
y &= -1 - x, & -1 &\leq x \leq 0 \\[6pt]
y &= x - 1, & 0 &\leq x \leq 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{gathered}
\text{vertices:} \qquad A(1, 0), \qquad B(0, 1) \\[6pt]
C(-1, 0), \qquad D(0, -1)
\end{gathered}
$$

::grafico{regione="abs(x)+abs(y)<=1" xmin=-2 xmax=2 ymin=-2 ymax=2 uguale=1 punti="1,0,pieno;0,1,pieno;-1,0,pieno;0,-1,pieno" etichette="1.08,0.12,A;0.1,1.1,B;-1.25,0.12,C;0.1,-1.22,D" titolo="$|x| + |y| \leq 1$: four sides and four vertices"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute values are removed by distinguishing four cases according to the sign of $x$ and of $y$, one for each quadrant, and each case is a system. In each quadrant the inequality describes the half-plane bounded by a line, so the set is the square with its vertices on the axes, sides included. The **interior points** are those at which the inequality holds with the strict symbol, that is, $|x| + |y| < 1$. The boundary is formed by four **pieces**, the sides of the square, one per quadrant. In the first quadrant the inequality becomes the equality $x + y = 1$, and the conditions $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$ give $0 \leq x \leq 1$, so the first piece is the segment $y = 1 - x$ with $0 \leq x \leq 1$. The other three pieces are obtained in the same way from the other three systems, in the order of the cases. The **vertices** are the points $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$, where two consecutive sides meet. On each side a function $f(x, y)$ becomes a function of $x$ alone, which on the first piece is $f(x, 1 - x)$ with $0 \leq x \leq 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Recognize the curve described by the constraint
$$
x^2 + 4y^2 - 2x = 3 ,
$$
parametrize it and determine whether it is a bounded and closed set.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2 - 2x) + 4y^2 &= 3 \\[6pt]
(x - 1)^2 - 1 + 4y^2 &= 3 \\[6pt]
(x - 1)^2 + 4y^2 &= 4 \\[6pt]
\frac{(x - 1)^2}{4} + y^2 &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(1, 0), \qquad a = 2, \qquad b = 1
$$
$$
\begin{gathered}
\text{vertices of the ellipse:} \\[6pt]
(-1, 0), \quad (3, 0), \quad (1, -1), \quad (1, 1)
\end{gathered}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + 2\cos t \\[4pt]
y = \sin t
\end{cases}
\qquad 0 \leq t \leq 2\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{(2\cos t)^2}{4} + \sin^2 t &= \cos^2 t + \sin^2 t \\[6pt]
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 1)^2 + y^2 &= 4\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t \\[6pt]
&= 1 + 3\cos^2 t \leq 4
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+4*y^2-2*x=3" xmin=-2 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="1,0,pieno,C" titolo="The constraint $x^2 + 4y^2 - 2x = 3$, that is, $\dfrac{(x - 1)^2}{4} + y^2 = 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

In the constraint the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are $1$ and $4$, of the same sign and different from each other, so the curve is an **ellipse**. The term $-2x$ appears in the constraint, so we complete the square in $x$, while in $y$ the first-degree term is absent and $4y^2$ is already a square. Dividing by $4$ we obtain the canonical form, from which we read off the center $C(1, 0)$, the semi-axes $a = 2$ and $b = 1$ and the vertices of the ellipse. The **parametrization** is obtained by setting $\dfrac{x - 1}{2} = \cos t$ and $y = \sin t$, because with this choice the left-hand side of the canonical form equals $\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 1$ for every $t$. The set is the curve alone, without interior points, and it is formed by a single closed piece, so it has no points where two different pieces meet. The square of the distance of a point of the ellipse from the center $C$ equals $1 + 3\cos^2 t$, which is at most $4$, so the ellipse is contained in the disk with center $C$ and radius $2$ and is a bounded set. The constraint is an equality between continuous functions, so the set is also closed. The ellipse is therefore a **bounded and closed** set, and by Weierstrass's extreme value theorem every continuous function has an absolute maximum and minimum on the constraint.

</div>
