## Exercise 1

Recognize the boundary curve from the coefficients and solve graphically the inequality
$$
x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 5 < 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2 - 6x) + y^2 + 5 &< 0 \\[6pt]
(x - 3)^2 - 9 + y^2 + 5 &< 0 \\[6pt]
(x - 3)^2 + y^2 &< 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(3, 0), \qquad r = \sqrt{4} = 2
$$
$$
(3 - 2,\, 0) = (1, 0), \qquad (3 + 2,\, 0) = (5, 0)
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+y^2-6*x+5<0" xmin=-1 xmax=7 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="3,0,pieno,C" titolo="$x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 5 < 0$, that is, $(x - 3)^2 + y^2 < 4$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are equal, so the form to look for is that of a circle, with center and radius. The term in $y$ is missing, so we **complete the square** only in $x$, with $x^2 - 6x = (x - 3)^2 - 9$. The right-hand side is $4$, which is positive, so the boundary is the circle with center $C(3, 0)$ and radius $2$, which meets the $x$-axis at the points $(1, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$. The left-hand side is the square of the distance of the point $(x, y)$ from the center. The inequality is true when this distance is less than $2$, so the solutions are the **interior points** of the circle. The symbol is $<$, hence the circle is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Recognize the boundary curve from the coefficients and solve graphically the inequality
$$
y^2 - 2y - 2x - 3 > 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^2 - 2y - 3 &> 2x \\[6pt]
2x &< y^2 - 2y - 3 \\[6pt]
x &< \frac{1}{2} y^2 - y - \frac{3}{2} \\[6pt]
x &< \frac{1}{2} \left[ (y - 1)^2 - 1 \right] - \frac{3}{2} \\[6pt]
x &< \frac{1}{2} (y - 1)^2 - 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
V(-2, 1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0 \quad &\Longrightarrow \quad y^2 - 2y - 3 = 0 \\[6pt]
&\Longrightarrow \quad (y + 1)(y - 3) = 0 \\[6pt]
y = 0 \quad &\Longrightarrow \quad x = -\frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(0, -1), \qquad (0, 3), \qquad \left( -\frac{3}{2},\, 0 \right)
$$

::grafico{regione="y^2-2*y-2*x-3>0" xmin=-5 xmax=4 ymin=-3.5 ymax=5.5 uguale=1 punti="-2,1,pieno,V" titolo="$y^2 - 2y - 2x - 3 > 0$, that is, $x < \dfrac{1}{2}(y - 1)^2 - 2$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

$y^2$ appears and $x^2$ is missing, while $x$ appears to the first degree, so the boundary is a parabola with horizontal axis and the variable to isolate is $x$. We move $2x$ to the right-hand side and divide both sides by $2$, which is positive, so the direction does not change. By **completing the square** $y^2 - 2y = (y - 1)^2 - 1$ we obtain the form with the vertex $V(-2, 1)$. The coefficient of $(y - 1)^2$ is positive, so the parabola opens to the right. For $x = 0$ we obtain $y = -1$ or $y = 3$, so the parabola meets the $y$-axis at the points $(0, -1)$ and $(0, 3)$, and for $y = 0$ it meets the $x$-axis at the point $\left( -\dfrac{3}{2}, 0 \right)$. The variable $x$ is isolated with the symbol $<$, so by the isolated variable rule the solutions are the points **to the left of the parabola**. The parabola is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Recognize the boundary curve from the coefficients and solve graphically the inequality
$$
2x^2 + y^2 - 4y \geq 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^2 + (y^2 - 4y) &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
2x^2 + (y - 2)^2 - 4 &\geq 0 \\[6pt]
2x^2 + (y - 2)^2 &\geq 4 \\[6pt]
\frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{(y - 2)^2}{4} &\geq 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(0, 2), \qquad a = \sqrt{2}, \qquad b = 2
$$
$$
\left( \pm\sqrt{2},\, 2 \right), \qquad (0, 0), \qquad (0, 4)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{(y - 2)^2}{4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 2) &= 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(3, 2) &= \frac{9}{2} + 0 = \frac{9}{2} > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="2*x^2+y^2-4*y>=0" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=6 uguale=1 punti="0,2,pieno,C;3,2,pieno" titolo="$2x^2 + y^2 - 4y \geq 0$, that is, $\dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{(y - 2)^2}{4} \geq 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are $2$ and $1$, of the same sign and different from each other, so the boundary is an ellipse. The term in $x$ is missing, so we **complete the square** only in $y$, with $y^2 - 4y = (y - 2)^2 - 4$. We move $4$ to the right-hand side and divide both sides by $4$, which is positive, so the direction does not change. The center is $C(0, 2)$ and the semi-axes are $a = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$ and $b = 2$, so the vertices are $\left( \pm\sqrt{2}, 2 \right)$, $(0, 0)$ and $(0, 4)$. The ellipse passes through the origin, so as test points we take the center and the point $(3, 2)$. At the center the left-hand side $F$ equals $0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is false at all interior points. At the point $(3, 2)$ we have $F = \dfrac{9}{2}$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is true at all exterior points. The solutions are the **exterior points** of the ellipse, and since the symbol is $\geq$ the ellipse is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Recognize the boundary curve from the coefficients and solve graphically the inequality
$$
x^2 - y^2 - 4x + 4 > 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2 - 4x + 4) - y^2 &> 0 \\[6pt]
(x - 2)^2 - y^2 &> 0 \\[6pt]
(x - 2 - y)(x - 2 + y) &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
y = x - 2, \qquad y = -x + 2
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y = x - 2 \\[4pt]
y = -x + 2
\end{cases}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad (2, 0)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = (x - 2)^2 - y^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 0) &= 4 - 0 = 4 > 0 \\[6pt]
F(4, 0) &= 4 - 0 = 4 > 0 \\[6pt]
F(2, 2) &= 0 - 4 = -4 < 0 \\[6pt]
F(2, -2) &= 0 - 4 = -4 < 0
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2-y^2-4*x+4>0" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 punti="2,0,vuoto;0,0,pieno;4,0,pieno;2,2,pieno;2,-2,pieno" titolo="$x^2 - y^2 - 4x + 4 > 0$: the lines $y = x - 2$ and $y = -x + 2$ with the four test points"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ have opposite signs, so the expected curve is a hyperbola. The trinomial $x^2 - 4x + 4$ is the square of the binomial $x - 2$, so the left-hand side becomes $(x - 2)^2 - y^2$, with no constant term. This difference of squares factors into the product $(x - 2 - y)(x - 2 + y)$, so the boundary is the degenerate case formed by the **pair of lines** $y = x - 2$ and $y = -x + 2$, which meet at the point $(2, 0)$. The two lines divide the plane into four angles, and in each of them we compute $F$ at a **test point**. At the points $(0, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$ we have $F = 4$, which is positive, so the inequality is true in the two angles that contain the $x$-axis. At the points $(2, 2)$ and $(2, -2)$ we have $F = -4$, which is negative, so the inequality is false in the other two angles. The solutions are the two **opposite angles** that contain the $x$-axis, one to the left and one to the right of the point $(2, 0)$. The symbol is $>$, hence the two lines are excluded and dashed, and the point $(2, 0)$ is excluded too.

</div>
