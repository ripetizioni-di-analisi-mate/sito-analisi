## Exercise 1

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{9} \geq 1 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^2 = 4 \quad &\Longrightarrow \quad a = 2 \\[6pt]
b^2 = 9 \quad &\Longrightarrow \quad b = 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(\pm 2,\, 0), \qquad (0,\, \pm 3)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = \frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{9}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 0) &= 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(4, 0) &= \frac{16}{4} + 0 = 4 > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2/4+y^2/9>=1" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,F < 1;4,0,pieno,F > 1" titolo="$\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} \geq 1$ with the two test points"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The terms $x^2$ and $y^2$ have coefficients of the same sign and different from each other, so the boundary is an ellipse. The inequality is already written in canonical form, and the center of the ellipse is the origin. The denominator of $x^2$ is $a^2 = 4$, so the horizontal semi-axis is $a = 2$ and the vertices on the $x$-axis are $(\pm 2, 0)$. The denominator of $y^2$ is $b^2 = 9$, so the vertical semi-axis is $b = 3$ and the vertices on the $y$-axis are $(0, \pm 3)$. The ellipse divides the plane into two regions, and in each of them we compute the left-hand side $F$ at a **test point**. At the origin we have $F = 0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is false at all interior points. At the point $(4, 0)$ we have $F = 4$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is true at all exterior points. The solutions are the **exterior points** of the ellipse, and since the symbol is $\geq$ the ellipse is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x^2 + 4y^2 < 16 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{4y^2}{16} &< 1 \\[6pt]
\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{4} &< 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
a = 4, \qquad b = 2
$$
$$
(\pm 4,\, 0), \qquad (0,\, \pm 2)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = \frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(0, 0) &= 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(0, 3) &= 0 + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{9}{4} > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+4*y^2<16" xmin=-5 xmax=5 ymin=-5 ymax=5 uguale=1 punti="0,0,pieno,F < 1;0,3,pieno,F > 1" titolo="$x^2 + 4y^2 < 16$, that is, $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} < 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ are $1$ and $4$, of the same sign and different from each other, so the boundary is an ellipse with center at the origin. To have $1$ on the right-hand side we divide both sides by $16$, which is positive, so the direction does not change. From the canonical form we read off $a^2 = 16$ and $b^2 = 4$, so the semi-axes are $a = 4$ and $b = 2$ and the vertices are $(\pm 4, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 2)$. In each of the two regions we compute the left-hand side $F$ at a **test point**. At the origin we have $F = 0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is true at all interior points. At the point $(0, 3)$ we have $F = \dfrac{9}{4}$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is false at all exterior points. The solutions are the **interior points** of the ellipse. The symbol is $<$, hence the ellipse is **excluded** and is drawn as a dashed line.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve graphically the inequality
$$
x^2 + 4y^2 - 6x + 8y + 9 \leq 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2 - 6x) + 4(y^2 + 2y) + 9 &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
(x - 3)^2 - 9 + 4\left[ (y + 1)^2 - 1 \right] + 9 &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
(x - 3)^2 - 9 + 4(y + 1)^2 - 4 + 9 &\leq 0 \\[6pt]
(x - 3)^2 + 4(y + 1)^2 &\leq 4 \\[6pt]
\frac{(x - 3)^2}{4} + (y + 1)^2 &\leq 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
C(3, -1), \qquad a = 2, \qquad b = 1
$$
$$
(1, -1), \qquad (5, -1), \qquad (3, 0), \qquad (3, -2)
$$
$$
F(x, y) = \frac{(x - 3)^2}{4} + (y + 1)^2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
F(3, -1) &= 0 < 1 \\[6pt]
F(0, 0) &= \frac{9}{4} + 1 = \frac{13}{4} > 1
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{regione="x^2+4*y^2-6*x+8*y+9<=0" xmin=-1 xmax=6 ymin=-4 ymax=3 uguale=1 punti="3,-1,pieno,C" titolo="$\dfrac{(x - 3)^2}{4} + (y + 1)^2 \leq 1$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ have the same sign and are different from each other, so the boundary is an ellipse. We factor out the coefficient $4$ in the terms in $y$ and **complete the squares** with $x^2 - 6x = (x - 3)^2 - 9$ and $y^2 + 2y = (y + 1)^2 - 1$. We move the constant term to the right-hand side and divide both sides by $4$, which is positive, so the direction does not change. From the canonical form we read off the center $C(3, -1)$ and the semi-axes $a = 2$ and $b = 1$, so the vertices are $(3 \pm 2, -1)$ and $(3, -1 \pm 1)$. At the center the left-hand side $F$ equals $0$, which is less than $1$, so the inequality is true at all interior points. At the origin we have $F = \dfrac{13}{4}$, which is greater than $1$, so the inequality is false at all exterior points. The solutions are the **interior points** of the ellipse. The symbol is $\leq$, hence the ellipse is **included** and is drawn as a solid line.

</div>
