## Exercise 1

Find the normal vector and the tangent plane to the surface
$$
\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( u + v,\; u - v,\; uv \bigr)
$$
at the point corresponding to $(u_0, v_0) = (1, 1)$.

$$
P_0 = \mathbf{r}(1, 1) = (2,\; 0,\; 1)
$$
$$
\mathbf{r}_u = (1,\; 1,\; v), \qquad \mathbf{r}_v = (1,\; -1,\; u)
$$
$$
\mathbf{r}_u(1,1) = (1,\; 1,\; 1), \qquad \mathbf{r}_v(1,1) = (1,\; -1,\; 1)
$$
$$
\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v =
\begin{vmatrix}
\mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & -1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= (2,\; 0,\; -2)
$$
$$
2(x - 2) - 2(z - 1) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
x - z = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point of the surface is obtained by substituting $(u_0, v_0) = (1, 1)$ into the parametrization, so $P_0 = (2, 0, 1)$. The derivative vectors $\mathbf{r}_u$ and $\mathbf{r}_v$ are computed by differentiating the three components with respect to $u$ and with respect to $v$, and are then evaluated at the point. The normal vector is their **cross product**, which is expanded with the symbolic determinant: the first component is $1 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot (-1) = 2$, the second is $-(1 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1) = 0$, the third is $1 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1 = -2$. Since $\mathbf{N} = (2, 0, -2) \neq \mathbf{0}$, the surface is **regular** at the point and the tangent plane exists. The tangent plane passes through $P_0$ and is orthogonal to $\mathbf{N}$, therefore its equation is $2(x - 2) + 0 \cdot (y - 0) - 2(z - 1) = 0$, which **simplified** becomes $x - z = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the normal vector and the tangent plane to the helicoid
$$
\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \bigl( u \cos v,\; u \sin v,\; 2v \bigr)
$$
at the point corresponding to $(u_0, v_0) = \left( 1,\; \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$.

$$
P_0 = \mathbf{r}\!\left( 1, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = (0,\; 1,\; \pi)
$$
$$
\mathbf{r}_u = (\cos v,\; \sin v,\; 0), \qquad \mathbf{r}_v = (-u \sin v,\; u \cos v,\; 2)
$$
$$
\mathbf{r}_u\!\left( 1, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = (0,\; 1,\; 0), \qquad \mathbf{r}_v\!\left( 1, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) = (-1,\; 0,\; 2)
$$
$$
\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{r}_u \wedge \mathbf{r}_v =
\begin{vmatrix}
\mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
-1 & 0 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= (2,\; 0,\; 1)
$$
$$
2(x - 0) + (z - \pi) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
2x + z = \pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point is obtained by substitution: since $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1$, we get $P_0 = (0, 1, \pi)$. The vector $\mathbf{r}_u$ is obtained by differentiating with respect to $u$ with $v$ held *constant*, the vector $\mathbf{r}_v$ by differentiating with respect to $v$ with $u$ held constant. The cross product of the two vectors evaluated at the point gives $\mathbf{N} = (1 \cdot 2 - 0 \cdot 0,\; -(0 \cdot 2 - 0 \cdot (-1)),\; 0 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot (-1)) = (2, 0, 1)$. The normal vector **is not zero**, so the **tangent plane** is the plane through $P_0$ orthogonal to $\mathbf{N}$, that is, $2x + z = \pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given the sphere of radius $2$ parametrized by
$$
\mathbf{r}(\varphi, \theta) = \bigl( 2 \sin\varphi \cos\theta,\; 2 \sin\varphi \sin\theta,\; 2 \cos\varphi \bigr),
$$
compute the normal vector at the point corresponding to $(\varphi_0, \theta_0) = \left( \dfrac{\pi}{3},\; 0 \right)$, verify that it is parallel to the position vector and write the tangent plane.

$$
P_0 = \mathbf{r}\!\left( \frac{\pi}{3}, 0 \right) = \left( \sqrt{3},\; 0,\; 1 \right)
$$
$$
\mathbf{r}_\varphi = (2 \cos\varphi \cos\theta,\; 2 \cos\varphi \sin\theta,\; -2 \sin\varphi),
\qquad
\mathbf{r}_\theta = (-2 \sin\varphi \sin\theta,\; 2 \sin\varphi \cos\theta,\; 0)
$$
$$
\mathbf{r}_\varphi\!\left( \frac{\pi}{3}, 0 \right) = \bigl( 1,\; 0,\; -\sqrt{3} \bigr),
\qquad
\mathbf{r}_\theta\!\left( \frac{\pi}{3}, 0 \right) = \bigl( 0,\; \sqrt{3},\; 0 \bigr)
$$
$$
\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{r}_\varphi \wedge \mathbf{r}_\theta =
\begin{vmatrix}
\mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\
1 & 0 & -\sqrt{3} \\
0 & \sqrt{3} & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= \bigl( 3,\; 0,\; \sqrt{3} \bigr) = \sqrt{3} \, \bigl( \sqrt{3},\; 0,\; 1 \bigr)
$$
$$
\sqrt{3}\,(x - \sqrt{3}) + (z - 1) = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\sqrt{3}\, x + z = 4
$$

::grafico{xmin=-2.6 xmax=3.4 ymin=-2.4 ymax=2.8 uguale=1 nomi="x,z" curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29;t,4-1.7321*t,0.8,2.7" vettori="1.7321,1,2.7713,1.6,N" punti="1.7321,1" etichette="0.95,2.75,√3 x + z = 4;1.3,0.7,P₀" titolo="Cross-section with the plane $y = 0$: the normal vector at $P_0$ has the direction of the radius and the trace of the tangent plane $\sqrt{3}\, x + z = 4$ is tangent to the circle"}

<div class="spiegazione">

At the given point we have $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $P_0 = (\sqrt{3}, 0, 1)$. The two derivative vectors, evaluated at the point, are $(1, 0, -\sqrt{3})$ and $(0, \sqrt{3}, 0)$, and their cross product is $\mathbf{N} = (0 \cdot 0 - (-\sqrt{3}) \cdot \sqrt{3},\; -(1 \cdot 0 - (-\sqrt{3}) \cdot 0),\; 1 \cdot \sqrt{3} - 0) = (3, 0, \sqrt{3})$. Factoring out $\sqrt{3}$ we obtain $\mathbf{N} = \sqrt{3}\,(\sqrt{3}, 0, 1)$, that is, a *positive* multiple of the position vector $P_0$; therefore the normal to the sphere is **radial** and, with this parametrization, **points outward**. The tangent plane is the plane through $P_0$ orthogonal to the direction $(\sqrt{3}, 0, 1)$, that is, $\sqrt{3}\, x + z = 4$. The constant term $4$ coincides with the **square of the radius**, in agreement with the general formula $x\, x_0 + y\, y_0 + z\, z_0 = R^2$ for the tangent plane to a sphere centered at the origin.

</div>
