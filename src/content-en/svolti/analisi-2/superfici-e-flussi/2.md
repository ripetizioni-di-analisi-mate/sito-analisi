## Exercise 1

Compute the area of the portion of the plane $z = 2x + y$ that projects onto the triangle $T$ with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$.

$$
f(x, y) = 2x + y, \qquad f_x = 2, \qquad f_y = 1
$$
$$
\sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 1} = \sqrt{6}
$$
$$
\operatorname{Area} = \iint_T \sqrt{6} \; dx \, dy
= \sqrt{6} \cdot \frac{1}{2}
= \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The surface is the graph of $f(x, y) = 2x + y$ above the triangle $T$, so the **formula for the area of a Cartesian surface** applies. The partial derivatives are constant, therefore the integrand $\sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2} = \sqrt{6}$ is constant as well and the double integral is the **product** of $\sqrt{6}$ and the area of $T$. The triangle has legs of length $1$, so its area is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the area of the portion of the plane is $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the area of the portion of the paraboloid
$$
z = \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2}
$$
that projects onto the disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 1$.

$$
f_x = x, \qquad f_y = y, \qquad \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2} = \sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2}
$$
$$
\operatorname{Area} = \iint_{x^2 + y^2 \leq 1} \sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2} \; dx \, dy
= \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + \rho^2} \; \rho \, d\rho \, d\theta
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Area}
&= 2\pi \left[ \frac{1}{3} \bigl( 1 + \rho^2 \bigr)^{\frac{3}{2}} \right]_0^1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2\pi}{3} \bigl( 2\sqrt{2} - 1 \bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The partial derivatives of $f(x, y) = \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2}$ are $f_x = x$ and $f_y = y$, so the area integrand is $\sqrt{1 + x^2 + y^2}$. Since the domain is a disk and the integrand depends *only* on $x^2 + y^2$, it is convenient to pass to **polar coordinates**, in which $x^2 + y^2 = \rho^2$ and the area element becomes $\rho \, d\rho \, d\theta$. The integral in $\theta$ gives the factor $2\pi$. The integral in $\rho$ has integrand $\rho \sqrt{1 + \rho^2}$, which is the derivative of $\dfrac{1}{3}(1 + \rho^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}$, so an antiderivative is $\dfrac{1}{3}(1 + \rho^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}$. Evaluating between $0$ and $1$ we obtain $\dfrac{2\sqrt{2} - 1}{3}$, so the **area** is $\dfrac{2\pi}{3} \bigl( 2\sqrt{2} - 1 \bigr)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the area of the graph of
$$
f(x, y) = \frac{2}{3} \left( x^{\frac{3}{2}} + y^{\frac{3}{2}} \right)
$$
over the square $Q = [0, 1] \times [0, 1]$.

$$
f_x = \sqrt{x}, \qquad f_y = \sqrt{y}, \qquad \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2} = \sqrt{1 + x + y}
$$
$$
\int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + x + y} \; dx
= \left[ \frac{2}{3} (1 + x + y)^{\frac{3}{2}} \right]_{x=0}^{x=1}
= \frac{2}{3} \Bigl( (2 + y)^{\frac{3}{2}} - (1 + y)^{\frac{3}{2}} \Bigr)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Area}
&= \frac{2}{3} \int_0^1 \Bigl( (2 + y)^{\frac{3}{2}} - (1 + y)^{\frac{3}{2}} \Bigr) dy \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{5} \Bigl[ (2 + y)^{\frac{5}{2}} - (1 + y)^{\frac{5}{2}} \Bigr]_0^1 \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{15} \Bigl( 3^{\frac{5}{2}} - 2^{\frac{5}{2}} - 2^{\frac{5}{2}} + 1 \Bigr) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{4}{15} \bigl( 9\sqrt{3} - 8\sqrt{2} + 1 \bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The partial derivatives are $f_x = \sqrt{x}$ and $f_y = \sqrt{y}$, since the derivative of $\dfrac{2}{3} x^{\frac{3}{2}}$ is $x^{\frac{1}{2}}$. The area integrand becomes $\sqrt{1 + x + y}$, and the function $f$ of the exercise is constructed *precisely* so that the squares of the derivatives under the root give a **linear expression**, which can be integrated in elementary terms. We integrate first in $x$ with $y$ constant: an antiderivative of $(1 + x + y)^{\frac{1}{2}}$ with respect to $x$ is $\dfrac{2}{3}(1 + x + y)^{\frac{3}{2}}$. We then integrate the result in $y$, with antiderivatives of the form $\dfrac{2}{5}(c + y)^{\frac{5}{2}}$. In the final evaluation $3^{\frac{5}{2}} = 9\sqrt{3}$ and $2^{\frac{5}{2}} = 4\sqrt{2}$ appear, the latter twice, therefore the **area** equals $\dfrac{4}{15} \bigl( 9\sqrt{3} - 8\sqrt{2} + 1 \bigr)$.

</div>
