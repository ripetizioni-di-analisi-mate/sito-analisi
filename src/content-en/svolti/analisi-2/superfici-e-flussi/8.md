## Exercise 1

Compute the circulation of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (-y,\; x,\; z)$ along the circle
$$
\gamma = \bigl\{ x^2 + y^2 = 1,\; z = 2 \bigr\},
$$
traversed counterclockwise as seen from above, and verify the result by direct computation.

$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} = \bigl( 0,\; 0,\; 2 \bigr)
$$
$$
\Sigma = \bigl\{ x^2 + y^2 \leq 1,\; z = 2 \bigr\}, \qquad \mathbf{n} = (0,\; 0,\; 1)
$$
$$
\oint_\gamma \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}
= \iint_\Sigma \operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \; d\sigma
= 2 \cdot \operatorname{Area}(\Sigma) = 2\pi
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Check: } \quad
\gamma(t) &= (\cos t,\; \sin t,\; 2), \qquad t \in [0, 2\pi] \\[4pt]
\oint_\gamma \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}
&= \int_0^{2\pi} \bigl( (-\sin t)(-\sin t) + \cos t \cdot \cos t + 2 \cdot 0 \bigr) \, dt \\[6pt]
&= \int_0^{2\pi} 1 \; dt = 2\pi
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

As the surface with boundary $\gamma$ we choose the flat disk at height $z = 2$. The boundary is traversed counterclockwise as seen from above, so the **compatible orientation** of the surface is the one with upward normal, $\mathbf{n} = (0, 0, 1)$. The curl of the field is constant and equals $(0, 0, 2)$, therefore the flux of the curl is $2$ times the area of the disk, that is, $2\pi$. The direct check uses the parametrization $\gamma(t) = (\cos t, \sin t, 2)$: the term $z \, dz$ does not contribute because the height is constant, while $(-y)\,dx + x\,dy$ gives the integrand $\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$, so the circulation equals $2\pi$. The two computations **coincide**, as **Stokes' theorem** guarantees.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute with Stokes' theorem the circulation of the field
$$
\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( z^2,\; x^2,\; y^2 \bigr)
$$
along the boundary of the triangle with vertices $(1, 0, 0)$, $(0, 1, 0)$, $(0, 0, 1)$, traversed in the order of the vertices.

$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} = \bigl( 2y,\; 2z,\; 2x \bigr)
$$
$$
\Sigma : \; z = 1 - x - y, \quad (x, y) \in T, \qquad \mathbf{N} = (1,\; 1,\; 1)
$$
$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{N} = 2y + 2z + 2x = 2 \qquad (x + y + z = 1 \text{ on } \Sigma)
$$
$$
\oint_{\partial\Sigma} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}
= \iint_T 2 \; dx \, dy = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 1
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.4 xmax=1.6 ymin=-0.3 ymax=1.4 uguale=1 area="1-x;0;0;1" vettori="1,0,0,1;0,1,0,0;0,0,1,0" etichette="0.25,0.3,T" titolo="The projection of the triangle onto the $xy$-plane: the vertices $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(0, 0)$, in the given order, are traversed counterclockwise, compatibly with the upward normal $\mathbf{N} = (1, 1, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangle lies on the plane $x + y + z = 1$, which is the graph of $f(x, y) = 1 - x - y$ above the triangle $T$ with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$ in the $xy$-plane. The normal vector of the Cartesian parametrization is $(-f_x, -f_y, 1) = (1, 1, 1)$, pointing upward; the path $(1,0,0) \to (0,1,0) \to (0,0,1)$ is counterclockwise as seen from above, so the orientation of the boundary is **compatible** with this normal and **Stokes' theorem** applies without changes of sign. The curl is computed with the symbolic determinant and equals $(2y, 2z, 2x)$. The dot product with $\mathbf{N}$ is $2(x + y + z)$, and on the surface the sum $x + y + z$ equals $1$, therefore the integrand is the constant $2$. The flux of the curl is therefore $2$ times the area of $T$, which is $\dfrac{1}{2}$, and the **circulation** equals $1$. The direct computation would require three line integrals, one per side.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the circulation of the field
$$
\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( yz,\; -xz,\; z^2 \bigr)
$$
along the curve of intersection of the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ with the plane $z = y + 2$, traversed counterclockwise as seen from above.

$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} = \bigl( x,\; y,\; -2z \bigr)
$$
$$
\Sigma : \; z = y + 2, \quad x^2 + y^2 \leq 1, \qquad \mathbf{N} = (0,\; -1,\; 1)
$$
$$
\operatorname{rot} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{N} = -y - 2(y + 2) = -3y - 4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\oint_{\partial\Sigma} \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}
&= \iint_{x^2 + y^2 \leq 1} \bigl( -3y - 4 \bigr) \, dx \, dy \\[6pt]
&= -3 \iint_{x^2 + y^2 \leq 1} y \; dx \, dy \; - \; 4 \iint_{x^2 + y^2 \leq 1} dx \, dy \\[6pt]
&= 0 - 4\pi = -4\pi
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The curve is an ellipse in space and its direct parametrization produces laborious trigonometric integrals; with **Stokes' theorem** we choose as the surface the portion of the plane $z = y + 2$ inside the cylinder, which has the curve as its boundary. The plane is the graph of $f(x, y) = y + 2$, so the upward normal vector is $(-f_x, -f_y, 1) = (0, -1, 1)$, compatible with the counterclockwise direction as seen from above. The first component of the curl is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(z^2) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(-xz) = x$, the second is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(yz) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(z^2) = y$, the third is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(-xz) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(yz) = -2z$. On the plane we substitute $z = y + 2$, and the dot product with $(0, -1, 1)$ gives $-y - 2(y + 2) = -3y - 4$. The integral of $y$ over the unit disk is **zero by symmetry**, since the disk is symmetric with respect to the $x$-axis and the integrand is *odd* in $y$; what remains is $-4$ times the area $\pi$ of the disk, so the **circulation** equals $-4\pi$.

</div>
