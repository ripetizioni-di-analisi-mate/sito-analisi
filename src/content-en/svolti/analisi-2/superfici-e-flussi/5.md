## Exercise 1

Compute the divergence and the curl of the field
$$
\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( x^2 y,\; y z,\; x z^2 \bigr)
$$
and evaluate them at the point $(1, 2, -1)$.

$$
\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F}
= \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl( x^2 y \bigr) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\bigl( y z \bigr) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\bigl( x z^2 \bigr)
= 2xy + z + 2xz
$$
$$
\operatorname{curl} \mathbf{F} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\[2pt]
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z} \\[8pt]
x^2 y & y z & x z^2
\end{vmatrix}
= \bigl( -y,\; -z^2,\; -x^2 \bigr)
$$
$$
\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F}(1, 2, -1) = 4 - 1 - 2 = 1,
\qquad
\operatorname{curl} \mathbf{F}(1, 2, -1) = (-2,\; -1,\; -1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **divergence** is the sum of the derivatives of each component with respect to *its own* variable: the first component differentiated with respect to $x$ gives $2xy$, the second with respect to $y$ gives $z$, the third with respect to $z$ gives $2xz$. The **curl** is expanded with the **symbolic determinant**. The first component is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(x z^2) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(y z) = 0 - y = -y$; the second is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(x^2 y) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(x z^2) = 0 - z^2 = -z^2$; the third is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(y z) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(x^2 y) = 0 - x^2 = -x^2$. Substituting $(1, 2, -1)$ we obtain $\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F} = 1$ and $\operatorname{curl} \mathbf{F} = (-2, -1, -1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given the function $f(x, y, z) = x^2 y + x z^3$, compute the gradient $\nabla f$, verify that $\operatorname{curl}(\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}$ and compute $\operatorname{div}(\nabla f)$.

$$
\nabla f = \bigl( 2xy + z^3,\; x^2,\; 3x z^2 \bigr)
$$
$$
\operatorname{curl}(\nabla f) =
\Bigl(
\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\bigl( 3xz^2 \bigr) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\bigl( x^2 \bigr),\;
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\bigl( 2xy + z^3 \bigr) - \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl( 3xz^2 \bigr),\;
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\bigl( x^2 \bigr) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\bigl( 2xy + z^3 \bigr)
\Bigr)
$$
$$
\operatorname{curl}(\nabla f) = \bigl( 0 - 0,\; 3z^2 - 3z^2,\; 2x - 2x \bigr) = (0,\; 0,\; 0)
$$
$$
\operatorname{div}(\nabla f) = 2y + 0 + 6xz
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gradient is obtained by differentiating $f$ with respect to the three variables: $f_x = 2xy + z^3$, $f_y = x^2$, $f_z = 3xz^2$. The curl of the gradient is computed component by component, and each component is a **difference of mixed second derivatives** of $f$: for example the second component is $f_{xz} - f_{zx} = 3z^2 - 3z^2 = 0$. Since $f$ is a polynomial, hence of class $C^2$, **Schwarz's theorem** guarantees that *all three* differences are zero, and the explicit computation confirms it. The divergence of the gradient is the sum of the pure second derivatives, $f_{xx} + f_{yy} + f_{zz} = 2y + 0 + 6xz$, that is, the **Laplacian** of $f$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $a \in \mathbb{R}$ such that the field
$$
\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( 2xy,\; a y^2,\; 4yz \bigr)
$$
is solenoidal, and determine whether for that value the field is also irrotational.

$$
\operatorname{div} \mathbf{F} = 2y + 2ay + 4y = (6 + 2a)\, y
$$
$$
(6 + 2a)\, y = 0 \ \text{ for every } (x,y,z)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
a = -3
$$
$$
\mathbf{F} = \bigl( 2xy,\; -3y^2,\; 4yz \bigr),
\qquad
\operatorname{curl} \mathbf{F} = \bigl( 4z,\; 0,\; -2x \bigr) \neq \mathbf{0}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divergence of the field is $2y + 2ay + 4y = (6 + 2a)y$. The field is **solenoidal** when the divergence is zero at every point, and the function $(6 + 2a)y$ is identically zero *only if* the coefficient $6 + 2a$ is zero, so $a = -3$. For this value we compute the curl: the first component is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(4yz) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(-3y^2) = 4z$, the second is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(2xy) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(4yz) = 0$, the third is $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(-3y^2) - \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(2xy) = -2x$. The curl $(4z, 0, -2x)$ is not identically zero, therefore the field is **solenoidal but not irrotational**: the two properties are **independent**.

</div>
