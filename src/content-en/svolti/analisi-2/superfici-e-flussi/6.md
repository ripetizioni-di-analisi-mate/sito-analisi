## Exercise 1

Compute the flux of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (x,\; y,\; z)$ through the portion of the paraboloid
$$
z = x^2 + y^2, \qquad x^2 + y^2 \leq 1,
$$
oriented upward.

$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2, \qquad f_x = 2x, \qquad f_y = 2y
$$
$$
\mathbf{F} \cdot \bigl( -f_x,\, -f_y,\, 1 \bigr)
= -2x^2 - 2y^2 + \bigl( x^2 + y^2 \bigr)
= -\bigl( x^2 + y^2 \bigr)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi
&= -\iint_{x^2 + y^2 \leq 1} \bigl( x^2 + y^2 \bigr) \, dx \, dy \\[6pt]
&= -\int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^1 \rho^3 \; d\rho \, d\theta
= -2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{4} = -\frac{\pi}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-1.6 xmax=1.8 ymin=-0.4 ymax=2.2 uguale=1 nomi="x,z" f="x^2" vettori="0.5,0.25,-0.1,0.85,N;0.5,0.25,1,0.5,F" punti="0.5,0.25" titolo="Cross-section with the plane $y = 0$: at the point $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, 0, \dfrac{1}{4} \right)$ the field $\mathbf{F}$ forms an obtuse angle with the upward normal vector $\mathbf{N}$, so $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{N} < 0$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The surface is the graph of $f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2$ oriented upward, so the flux is computed with the **formula for a Cartesian surface**, by integrating $-F_1 f_x - F_2 f_y + F_3$ over the projection disk. On the surface the third component of the field equals $z = x^2 + y^2$, therefore the integrand is $-2x^2 - 2y^2 + x^2 + y^2 = -(x^2 + y^2)$. In polar coordinates the integrand becomes $\rho^2$ and the area element $\rho \, d\rho \, d\theta$, so the integral equals $2\pi \displaystyle\int_0^1 \rho^3 d\rho = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and the flux is $-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. The **negative sign** is consistent with the geometry: near the boundary the radial field points outward and upward less steeply than the paraboloid rises, so it crosses the surface mainly from the side *opposite* to the normal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the outward flux of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = (x,\; y,\; 0)$ through the lateral surface of the cylinder
$$
x^2 + y^2 = 4, \qquad 0 \leq z \leq 3 .
$$

$$
\mathbf{r}(\theta, z) = (2\cos\theta,\; 2\sin\theta,\; z)
$$
$$
\mathbf{r}_\theta = (-2\sin\theta,\; 2\cos\theta,\; 0), \qquad \mathbf{r}_z = (0,\; 0,\; 1)
$$
$$
\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{r}_\theta \wedge \mathbf{r}_z = (2\cos\theta,\; 2\sin\theta,\; 0)
$$
$$
\mathbf{F}\bigl( \mathbf{r}(\theta, z) \bigr) \cdot \mathbf{N}
= 4\cos^2\theta + 4\sin^2\theta = 4
$$
$$
\Phi = \int_0^3 \!\! \int_0^{2\pi} 4 \; d\theta \, dz = 4 \cdot 2\pi \cdot 3 = 24\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cylinder is parametrized by the angle $\theta$ and the height $z$. The normal vector induced by the parametrization is $\mathbf{N} = \mathbf{r}_\theta \wedge \mathbf{r}_z = (2\cos\theta, 2\sin\theta, 0)$, which is the horizontal radial vector: it points out of the cylinder, so the induced orientation **coincides with the required one** and *no* change of sign is needed. On the cylinder the field equals $\mathbf{F} = (2\cos\theta, 2\sin\theta, 0)$, therefore the dot product $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{N}$ is $4(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = 4$, constant. The **flux** is therefore $4$ times the area of the parameter rectangle $[0, 2\pi] \times [0, 3]$, that is, $24\pi$. Alternatively, on the cylinder the outward unit normal is $\mathbf{n} = \left( \dfrac{x}{2}, \dfrac{y}{2}, 0 \right)$ and $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = \dfrac{x^2 + y^2}{2} = 2$, to be multiplied by the lateral area $2\pi \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 12\pi$: the result is *again* $24\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the flux of the field $\mathbf{F}(x, y, z) = \bigl( xz,\; yz,\; z^2 \bigr)$ through the hemisphere
$$
\Sigma = \bigl\{ x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1,\; z \geq 0 \bigr\},
$$
oriented with the normal pointing out of the sphere.

$$
\mathbf{F} = z \, (x,\; y,\; z), \qquad \mathbf{n} = (x,\; y,\; z) \ \text{ on } \Sigma
$$
$$
\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = z \bigl( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \bigr) = z
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi = \iint_\Sigma z \; d\sigma
&= \int_0^{2\pi} \!\! \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos\varphi \cdot \sin\varphi \; d\varphi \, d\theta \\[6pt]
&= 2\pi \left[ \frac{\sin^2\varphi}{2} \right]_0^{\pi/2} = \pi
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field can be factored in the form $\mathbf{F} = z\,(x, y, z)$, that is, $z$ times the position vector. On the sphere of radius $1$ the outward unit normal at a point is the **position vector** itself, $\mathbf{n} = (x, y, z)$, since it has norm $1$ and is radial. The dot product is therefore $z (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$, and on the surface the sum $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ equals $1$, therefore $\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} = z$. The flux reduces to the **surface integral** of the function $z$ over the upper hemisphere: in spherical coordinates with radius $1$ we have $z = \cos\varphi$ and $d\sigma = \sin\varphi \, d\varphi \, d\theta$, with $\varphi \in \left[0, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$. The integral of $\cos\varphi \sin\varphi$ equals $\dfrac{1}{2}$, so the **flux** is $2\pi \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} = \pi$.

</div>
