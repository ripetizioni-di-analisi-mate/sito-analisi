## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the tangent line to the curve
$$
x^2 + xy + y^2 = 3
$$
at the point $(1, 1)$.

$$
F(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3, \qquad F(1, 1) = 1 + 1 + 1 - 3 = 0
$$
$$
F_x(1, 1) = 2x + y \big|_{(1,1)} = 3, \qquad F_y(1, 1) = x + 2y \big|_{(1,1)} = 3
$$
$$
3(x - 1) + 3(y - 1) = 0 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad y = -x + 2
$$

::grafico{curve="cos(t)+sqrt(3)*sin(t),cos(t)-sqrt(3)*sin(t),0,6.29;t,2-t,-1,3" punti="1,1,pieno" xmin=-2.5 xmax=3.2 ymin=-2.4 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="The ellipse $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 3$ and the tangent line $y = -x + 2$ at the point $(1, 1)$ (dashed)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The point belongs to the curve, since $F(1, 1) = 0$. The gradient at the point is $\nabla F(1, 1) = (3, 3)$, which is different from the zero vector, therefore the **tangent line exists** and has equation $F_x(1,1)(x - 1) + F_y(1,1)(y - 1) = 0$. Dividing by $3$ we obtain $x + y = 2$, that is $y = -x + 2$. The same result is reached with **Dini's theorem**: since $F_y(1,1) = 3 \neq 0$, near the point the curve is the graph of an implicit function with $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{3}{3} = -1$, and the tangent is $y = 1 - (x - 1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the tangent line to the curve
$$
x^2 y^3 + x^3 - 2y = 0
$$
at the point $(1, 1)$.

$$
F(x, y) = x^2 y^3 + x^3 - 2y, \qquad F(1, 1) = 1 + 1 - 2 = 0
$$
$$
F_x(1, 1) = 2x y^3 + 3x^2 \big|_{(1,1)} = 5, \qquad F_y(1, 1) = 3x^2 y^2 - 2 \big|_{(1,1)} = 1
$$
$$
5(x - 1) + 1 \cdot (y - 1) = 0 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad y = -5x + 6
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substituting the coordinates of the point we obtain $F(1, 1) = 0$, hence the point belongs to the curve. In computing $F_x$ the term $x^2 y^3$ is differentiated with respect to $x$ holding $y$ **constant**, and gives $2x y^3$; the term $x^3$ gives $3x^2$; at the point the sum equals $5$. In computing $F_y$ the same term gives $3 x^2 y^2$ and the term $-2y$ gives $-2$, with value $1$ at the point. Since the gradient $(5, 1)$ is *not* zero, the tangent line has equation $5(x - 1) + (y - 1) = 0$; solving for $y$ we obtain $y = -5x + 6$. The **slope** $-5$ is the value $\varphi'(1) = -\dfrac{F_x}{F_y}$ of the derivative of the implicit function.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the equation of the tangent line to the curve
$$
e^{xy} - x - y = 0
$$
at the point $(0, 1)$.

$$
F(x, y) = e^{xy} - x - y, \qquad F(0, 1) = 1 - 0 - 1 = 0
$$
$$
F_x(0, 1) = y\,e^{xy} - 1 \big|_{(0,1)} = 1 - 1 = 0, \qquad F_y(0, 1) = x\,e^{xy} - 1 \big|_{(0,1)} = -1
$$
$$
0 \cdot (x - 0) + (-1)(y - 1) = 0 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad y = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point belongs to the curve because $e^0 = 1$ and $1 - 0 - 1 = 0$. The partial derivatives at the point are $F_x(0, 1) = 0$ and $F_y(0, 1) = -1$: the gradient $(0, -1)$ is not zero, therefore the tangent exists, and since $F_y(0,1) \neq 0$ **Dini's theorem** applies with respect to $y$. The derivative of the implicit function is $\varphi'(0) = -\dfrac{0}{-1} = 0$, therefore the tangent line is **horizontal** and has equation $y = 1$. The vanishing of $F_x$ at the point means that $x = 0$ is a **stationary point** of the implicit function; the nature of such points is decided with the second derivative.

</div>
