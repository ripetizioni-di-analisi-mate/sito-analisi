## Exercise 1

Compute the work of the field
$$
F(x, y) = \bigl( 2x + y, \; x + 2y \bigr)
$$
along the curve $r(t) = \bigl( t, \, 2t^3 \bigr)$, $t \in [0, 1]$.

$$
\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 1 = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}
$$
$$
U(x, y) = \int (2x + y) \, dx + c(y) = x^2 + xy + c(y)
$$
$$
U_y = x + c'(y) = x + 2y \implies c(y) = y^2 + k
$$
$$
r(0) = (0, 0), \qquad r(1) = (1, 2)
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma} F \cdot dr = U(1, 2) - U(0, 0) = 1 + 2 + 4 = 7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The direct computation of the line integral along the curve $r(t) = (t, 2t^3)$ is possible but laborious, hence it is convenient to check *first* whether the field is conservative. The cross derivatives both equal $1$ and the domain $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, therefore the field is **conservative**. A potential, constructed by successive integration, is $U(x, y) = x^2 + xy + y^2$. By the **theorem on the work of a conservative field** the work is the difference of the values of the potential at the endpoints of the curve: the final point is $r(1) = (1, 2)$, the initial point is $r(0) = (0, 0)$, hence the work equals $U(1, 2) - U(0, 0) = 7$. The shape of the curve between the two endpoints **does not affect the result**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the work of the field
$$
F(x, y) = \bigl( e^x \sin y, \; e^x \cos y \bigr)
$$
along any piecewise regular curve from $(0, 0)$ to $\left( \ln 2, \dfrac{\pi}{2} \right)$.

$$
\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = e^x \cos y = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}
$$
$$
U(x, y) = \int e^x \sin y \, dx + c(y) = e^x \sin y + c(y)
$$
$$
U_y = e^x \cos y + c'(y) = e^x \cos y \implies c(y) = k
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma} F \cdot dr
= U\!\left( \ln 2, \frac{\pi}{2} \right) - U(0, 0)
= 2 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0 = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The statement asks for the work along an arbitrary curve with assigned endpoints, hence the request makes sense *only if* the work does not depend on the path, that is, if the field is conservative. The cross derivatives coincide on $\mathbb{R}^2$, which is simply connected, therefore the field is **conservative** and the work is well defined by the endpoints alone. A potential is $U(x, y) = e^x \sin y$, since in integrating $e^x \sin y$ with respect to $x$ the function $\sin y$ is a constant and the condition on $U_y$ gives $c'(y) = 0$. At the final point the potential equals $e^{\ln 2} \sin \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 2$, at the initial point it equals $e^0 \sin 0 = 0$, hence the **work** is $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the circulation of the field
$$
F(x, y) = \bigl( 2xy, \; x^2 + 1 \bigr)
$$
along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ traversed counterclockwise.

$$
\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 2x = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}
$$
$$
U(x, y) = x^2 y + y + k
$$
$$
\oint_{\gamma} F \cdot dr = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross derivatives both equal $2x$ and the domain $\mathbb{R}^2$ is simply connected, hence the field is **conservative**; a potential is $U(x, y) = x^2 y + y$. By the **characterization theorem** for conservative fields the circulation of a conservative field along *every* closed curve is zero, therefore the integral equals $0$ without any computation with a parametrization. The direct computation with $r(t) = (\cos t, \sin t)$ would lead to the same result after integrating trigonometric functions over $[0, 2\pi]$.

</div>
