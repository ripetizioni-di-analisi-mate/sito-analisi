## Exercise 1

Determine whether the field
$$
F(x, y, z) = \bigl( 2xz, \; 3y^2, \; x^2 + 1 \bigr)
$$
is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^3$ and determine its potentials.

$$
\operatorname{curl} F = \bigl( 0 - 0, \;\; 2x - 2x, \;\; 0 - 0 \bigr) = (0, 0, 0)
$$
$$
U(x, y, z) = \int 2xz \, dx + c(y, z) = x^2 z + c(y, z)
$$
$$
U_y = c_y = 3y^2 \implies c(y, z) = y^3 + d(z)
$$
$$
U_z = x^2 + d'(z) = x^2 + 1 \implies d(z) = z + k
$$
$$
U(x, y, z) = x^2 z + y^3 + z + k, \qquad k \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Nonzero components of the curl could come *only* from the pairs of variables that appear together in a component of the field: the derivative of $F_3 = x^2 + 1$ with respect to $x$ equals $2x$ and the derivative of $F_1 = 2xz$ with respect to $z$ equals $2x$, hence the second component of the curl is zero, and the other two are zero because the derivatives involved are all zero. The field is irrotational on $\mathbb{R}^3$, which is simply connected, therefore it is **conservative**. Successive integration proceeds in three steps: from the first component one obtains $x^2 z$ plus a function $c(y, z)$; the condition on $U_y$ gives $c_y = 3y^2$, hence $c = y^3 + d(z)$; the condition on $U_z$ gives $d'(z) = 1$. The **potentials** are $x^2 z + y^3 + z + k$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the work of the field
$$
F(x, y, z) = \bigl( y \cos z, \; x \cos z, \; -xy \sin z \bigr)
$$
along any piecewise regular curve from $(1, 1, 0)$ to $(2, 1, \pi)$.

$$
\operatorname{curl} F = \bigl( -x \sin z + x \sin z, \;\; -y \sin z + y \sin z, \;\; \cos z - \cos z \bigr) = (0, 0, 0)
$$
$$
U(x, y, z) = xy \cos z
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma} F \cdot dr
= U(2, 1, \pi) - U(1, 1, 0)
= 2 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1 = -3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The curl is zero: in the first component the derivative of $-xy \sin z$ with respect to $y$ and the derivative of $x \cos z$ with respect to $z$ both equal $-x \sin z$, and the other two components vanish with analogous computations. Since $\mathbb{R}^3$ is simply connected, the field is **conservative** and the work depends *only* on the endpoints. The potential is recognized by observing that the three components are the partial derivatives of $xy \cos z$; alternatively it is constructed by successive integration. At the final point the potential equals $2 \cdot 1 \cdot \cos \pi = -2$, at the initial point it equals $1 \cdot 1 \cdot \cos 0 = 1$, hence the **work** is $-2 - 1 = -3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the field
$$
F(x, y, z) = \bigl( e^y, \; x e^y + z, \; y \bigr)
$$
is conservative on $\mathbb{R}^3$ and determine a potential as the work along a polygonal path starting from the origin.

$$
\operatorname{curl} F = \bigl( 1 - 1, \;\; 0 - 0, \;\; e^y - e^y \bigr) = (0, 0, 0)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
U(x, y, z)
&= \int_0^x F_1(t, 0, 0) \, dt + \int_0^y F_2(x, t, 0) \, dt + \int_0^z F_3(x, y, t) \, dt \\[6pt]
&= \int_0^x 1 \, dt + \int_0^y x e^t \, dt + \int_0^z y \, dt \\[6pt]
&= x + x (e^y - 1) + yz = x e^y + yz
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The curl is zero, since in the first component the derivatives of $y$ with respect to $y$ and of $x e^y + z$ with respect to $z$ both equal $1$, and the other components vanish in the same way; the domain $\mathbb{R}^3$ is simply connected, hence the field is **conservative**. The potential is the **work along the polygonal path** that goes from the origin to $(x, 0, 0)$, then to $(x, y, 0)$, then to $(x, y, z)$: on each piece *only one* coordinate varies, therefore the work is the integral of the corresponding component alone, with the other variables fixed at the values reached. On the first piece $F_1(t, 0, 0) = e^0 = 1$, on the second $F_2(x, t, 0) = x e^t$, on the third $F_3(x, y, t) = y$. The sum of the integrals gives $U(x, y, z) = x e^y + yz$, and the check of the gradient confirms $\nabla U = F$.

</div>
