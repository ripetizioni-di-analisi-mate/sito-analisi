## Exercise 1

Compute the work of the field
$$
F(x, y) = \bigl( y, \; x^2 \bigr)
$$
along the segment from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 1)$.

$$
\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = 1, \qquad \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = 2x
$$
$$
r(t) = (t, t), \quad t \in [0, 1], \qquad r'(t) = (1, 1)
$$
$$
F(r(t)) \cdot r'(t) = t \cdot 1 + t^2 \cdot 1 = t + t^2
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma} F \cdot dr = \int_0^1 (t + t^2) \, dt = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross derivatives equal $1$ and $2x$ and do not coincide, hence the field **is not conservative** and the work must be computed with the **definition**. The segment is parametrized by $r(t) = (t, t)$ for $t \in [0, 1]$, with derivative $r'(t) = (1, 1)$. Substituting the parametrization into the components of the field one obtains $F(r(t)) = (t, t^2)$, and the scalar product with $r'(t)$ equals $t + t^2$. The integral of this function over $[0, 1]$ gives $\dfrac{5}{6}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the work of the field
$$
F(x, y) = \bigl( y^2, \; -x \bigr)
$$
along the arc of the parabola $y = x^2$ from $(0, 0)$ to $(1, 1)$.

$$
r(t) = (t, t^2), \quad t \in [0, 1], \qquad r'(t) = (1, 2t)
$$
$$
F(r(t)) \cdot r'(t) = t^4 \cdot 1 + (-t) \cdot 2t = t^4 - 2t^2
$$
$$
\int_{\gamma} F \cdot dr = \int_0^1 (t^4 - 2t^2) \, dt = \frac{1}{5} - \frac{2}{3} = -\frac{7}{15}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The field **is not conservative**, since the derivative of $y^2$ with respect to $y$ equals $2y$ and the derivative of $-x$ with respect to $x$ equals $-1$. The arc of the parabola is parametrized by $x = t$ and $y = t^2$ for $t \in [0, 1]$, hence $r'(t) = (1, 2t)$. The components of the field along the curve are $F_1 = (t^2)^2 = t^4$ and $F_2 = -t$, therefore the integrand is $t^4 - 2t^2$. The work is **negative**: along this arc the field does **resistive work**, since on average it forms an obtuse angle with the direction of motion.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the circulation of the field
$$
F(x, y) = \bigl( -y, \; x \bigr)
$$
along the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ traversed counterclockwise, and deduce that the field is not conservative.

$$
r(t) = (\cos t, \sin t), \quad t \in [0, 2\pi], \qquad r'(t) = (-\sin t, \cos t)
$$
$$
F(r(t)) \cdot r'(t) = (-\sin t)(-\sin t) + \cos t \cdot \cos t = 1
$$
$$
\oint_{\gamma} F \cdot dr = \int_0^{2\pi} 1 \, dt = 2\pi
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the unit circle the field equals $F(r(t)) = (-\sin t, \cos t)$, which coincides with the tangent vector $r'(t)$: the field is at *every* point tangent to the circle and agrees with the direction of travel. The scalar product is therefore $\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1$ and the circulation equals $2\pi$. By the **characterization theorem** a conservative field has zero circulation along every closed curve; since this circulation is nonzero, the field **is not conservative** on $\mathbb{R}^2$. The same result is reached with the cross derivatives, which equal $-1$ and $1$.

</div>
