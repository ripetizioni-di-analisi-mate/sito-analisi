## Exercise 1

Study the field
$$
F(x, y) = \left( \frac{-y}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2} \right)
$$
on the domain $A = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$: determine whether it is irrotational and whether it is conservative; find an open set on which it is conservative and a potential.

$$
\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}
= \frac{-(x^2 + y^2) + y \cdot 2y}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}
= \frac{y^2 - x^2}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}
$$
$$
\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}
= \frac{(x^2 + y^2) - x \cdot 2x}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}
= \frac{y^2 - x^2}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}
$$
$$
r(t) = (\cos t, \sin t): \qquad
\oint_{\gamma} F \cdot dr = \int_0^{2\pi} (\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t) \, dt = 2\pi \neq 0
$$
The field is **irrotational but not conservative** on $A$. On the half-plane $x > 0$:
$$
U(x, y) = \arctan \frac{y}{x}, \qquad
\nabla U = \left( \frac{-\dfrac{y}{x^2}}{1 + \dfrac{y^2}{x^2}}, \; \frac{\dfrac{1}{x}}{1 + \dfrac{y^2}{x^2}} \right)
= \left( \frac{-y}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2} \right) = F
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross derivatives are computed with the **quotient rule** and coincide at every point of $A$, hence the field is irrotational. The domain $A$ is *not* simply connected, since the removed origin is a hole, therefore the theorem on simply connected domains does not apply and the nature of the field must be decided with a circulation around the hole. On the unit circle the field coincides with the tangent vector, hence the circulation equals $2\pi$; since it is nonzero, by the characterization theorem the field **is not conservative** on $A$. The half-plane $x > 0$ is a convex open set, hence simply connected, and does not contain the origin: on it the irrotational field is **conservative**, and the direct check of the gradient shows that $U(x, y) = \arctan \dfrac{y}{x}$ is a potential.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Study the field
$$
F(x, y) = \left( \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \frac{y}{x^2 + y^2} \right)
$$
on $A = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$: determine whether it is conservative and, if so, determine a potential.

$$
\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} = \frac{-2xy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}
$$
$$
r(t) = (\cos t, \sin t): \qquad
F(r(t)) \cdot r'(t) = \cos t \, (-\sin t) + \sin t \cos t = 0
\implies \oint_{\gamma} F \cdot dr = 0
$$
$$
U(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} \ln (x^2 + y^2), \qquad
\nabla U = \left( \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \frac{y}{x^2 + y^2} \right) = F
$$
The field is **conservative** on the whole of $A$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The field is irrotational, since both cross derivatives equal $\dfrac{-2xy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}$, and the domain has a hole at the origin as in the previous exercise. This time, however, the circulation on the unit circle is **zero**, since the field is at every point **radial** and hence perpendicular to the tangent vector. The hole in the domain does *not* prevent the field from being conservative: the function $U(x, y) = \dfrac{1}{2} \ln(x^2 + y^2)$ is defined on the whole of $A$ and the computation of the gradient shows that $\nabla U = F$ at every point, therefore the field is **conservative** on $A$. The comparison with the previous exercise shows that on a domain that is not simply connected an irrotational field *may* or may not be conservative, and the answer requires an explicit potential or a nonzero circulation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Study the field
$$
F(x, y) = \left( \frac{x - y}{x^2 + y^2}, \; \frac{x + y}{x^2 + y^2} \right)
$$
on $A = \mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$: determine whether it is irrotational and whether it is conservative.

$$
F = G + H, \qquad
G = \left( \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2}, \frac{y}{x^2 + y^2} \right), \quad
H = \left( \frac{-y}{x^2 + y^2}, \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2} \right)
$$
$$
\oint_{\gamma} F \cdot dr
= \oint_{\gamma} G \cdot dr + \oint_{\gamma} H \cdot dr
= 0 + 2\pi = 2\pi \neq 0
$$
The field is **irrotational but not conservative** on $A$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The field decomposes into the sum of the radial field $G$ and the rotation field $H$, studied in the previous exercises, since the components add term by term. Both summands are irrotational, and the irrotationality condition is **linear** in the components, hence $F$ is also irrotational on $A$. The circulation along the unit circle is the sum of the two circulations: that of $G$ is zero, since $G$ is conservative, and that of $H$ equals $2\pi$. The circulation of $F$ therefore equals $2\pi$, which is nonzero, and by the **characterization theorem** the field **is not conservative** on $A$. The decomposition avoids repeating the computations of the derivatives and of the line integral.

</div>
