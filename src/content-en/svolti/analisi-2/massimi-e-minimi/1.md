## Exercise 1

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 2y .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x - 4 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y + 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (2, -1)
$$
$$
H_f(2, -1) =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det H = 4 > 0, \qquad f_{xx} = 2 > 0
$$
$$
f(2, -1) = 4 + 1 - 8 - 2 = -5
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gradient vanishes *only* at $(2, -1)$, hence by **Fermat's theorem** any unconstrained extremum is at that point. The second derivatives are constant: $f_{xx} = 2$, $f_{yy} = 2$, $f_{xy} = 0$. Since $\det H = 4 > 0$ and $f_{xx} = 2 > 0$, the Hessian matrix is positive definite, therefore $(2, -1)$ is a **local minimum point** with value $f(2, -1) = -5$. The minimum is also **absolute**, since completing the squares gives $f(x, y) = (x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 - 5 \geq -5$ for every $(x, y)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^3 - 3x + y^2 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 3x^2 - 3 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (1, 0), \ (-1, 0)
$$
$$
H_f(x, y) =
\begin{pmatrix}
6x & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_f(1, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
6 & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= 12 > 0, \ f_{xx} = 6 > 0 \\[8pt]
H_f(-1, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
-6 & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= -12 < 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f(1, 0) = -2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first equation of the system gives $x^2 = 1$, that is $x = \pm 1$, and the second gives $y = 0$, hence there are two critical points. The Hessian matrix depends only on $x$ and must be evaluated at *each* point. At $(1, 0)$ the determinant is positive and $f_{xx} = 6 > 0$, therefore the point is a **local minimum point** with $f(1, 0) = 1 - 3 = -2$. At $(-1, 0)$ the determinant is negative, therefore the point is a **saddle point**. The minimum **is not absolute**, since along the $x$-axis we have $f(x, 0) = x^3 - 3x \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^3 + y^3 - 6xy .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 3x^2 - 6y = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 3y^2 - 6x = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = \frac{x^2}{2}, \qquad
3 \cdot \frac{x^4}{4} - 6x = 0
$$
$$
\frac{3x}{4}\,(x^3 - 8) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = 0 \ \text{ or } \ x = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, 0), \ (2, 2)
$$
$$
H_f(x, y) =
\begin{pmatrix}
6x & -6 \\
-6 & 6y
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_f(0, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & -6 \\
-6 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= -36 < 0 \\[8pt]
H_f(2, 2) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
12 & -6 \\
-6 & 12
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= 144 - 36 = 108 > 0, \ f_{xx} = 12 > 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f(2, 2) = 8 + 8 - 24 = -8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system is *not* decoupled, hence we proceed **by substitution**: from the first equation we obtain $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$ and substitute it into the second. The resulting equation factors as $\dfrac{3x}{4}(x^3 - 8) = 0$ and has only the real solutions $x = 0$ and $x = 2$; the corresponding values $y = \dfrac{x^2}{2}$ give the critical points $(0, 0)$ and $(2, 2)$. At the origin the determinant of the Hessian is negative, therefore the point is a **saddle point**. At $(2, 2)$ the determinant is positive and $f_{xx} = 12 > 0$, therefore the point is a **local minimum point** with $f(2, 2) = -8$. Here too the minimum is not absolute: along the $x$-axis we have $f(x, 0) = x^3 \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.

</div>
