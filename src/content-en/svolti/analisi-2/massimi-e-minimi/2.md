## Exercise 1

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = (x^2 + y^2)\, e^{-x} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = (2x - x^2 - y^2)\, e^{-x} = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y\, e^{-x} = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = 0, \quad 2x - x^2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, 0), \ (2, 0)
$$
$$
f_{xx} = (x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 2)\, e^{-x},
\qquad f_{yy} = 2e^{-x},
\qquad f_{xy} = -2y\, e^{-x}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_f(0, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= 4 > 0, \ f_{xx} = 2 > 0 \\[8pt]
H_f(2, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
-2e^{-2} & 0 \\
0 & 2e^{-2}
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= -4e^{-4} < 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f(0, 0) = 0, \qquad f(2, 0) = 4e^{-2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $e^{-x}$ is positive for *every* $x$, hence the system reduces to setting the remaining factors to zero: from the second equation it follows that $y = 0$, and the first becomes $2x - x^2 = x(2 - x) = 0$, from which $x = 0$ or $x = 2$. To compute $f_{xx}$ we differentiate the product $(2x - x^2 - y^2)e^{-x}$ with respect to $x$ and factor out $e^{-x}$. At the origin the Hessian is positive definite, therefore $(0, 0)$ is a **local minimum point**; the minimum is also **absolute**, since $f(x, y) \geq 0 = f(0, 0)$ for every $(x, y)$, as $f$ is the product of two nonnegative quantities. At $(2, 0)$ the determinant is negative, therefore the point is a **saddle point**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = xy\, e^{-x-y} .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = y\,(1 - x)\, e^{-x-y} = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = x\,(1 - y)\, e^{-x-y} = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, 0), \ (1, 1)
$$
$$
f_{xx} = -y\,(2 - x)\, e^{-x-y},
\qquad f_{yy} = -x\,(2 - y)\, e^{-x-y},
\qquad f_{xy} = (1 - x)(1 - y)\, e^{-x-y}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_f(0, 0) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 1 \\
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= -1 < 0 \\[8pt]
H_f(1, 1) &=
\begin{pmatrix}
-e^{-2} & 0 \\
0 & -e^{-2}
\end{pmatrix},
& \det H &= e^{-4} > 0, \ f_{xx} = -e^{-2} < 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f(1, 1) = e^{-2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $e^{-x-y} > 0$, the system is equivalent to $y(1 - x) = 0$ and $x(1 - y) = 0$ and is solved **by cases**. If $y = 0$, the second equation becomes $x = 0$ and we obtain the origin; if $x = 1$, the second equation becomes $1 - y = 0$, that is $y = 1$, and we obtain $(1, 1)$. At the origin the determinant of the Hessian is negative, therefore the point is a **saddle point**; on the two axes the function is zero, while in the first and third quadrants it is positive and in the second and fourth it is negative, in agreement with the classification. At $(1, 1)$ the determinant is positive and $f_{xx} < 0$, therefore the point is a **local maximum point** with $f(1, 1) = e^{-2}$. The maximum is *not* absolute, since along the bisector $y = x$, as $x \to -\infty$, we have $f(x, x) = x^2 e^{-2x} \to +\infty$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - \ln(xy) .
$$

$$
\text{domain: } xy > 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x - \dfrac{1}{x} = 0 \\[8pt]
f_y = 2y - \dfrac{1}{y} = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \pm\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad y = \pm\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$
$$
P_1 = \left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right),
\qquad
P_2 = \left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\, -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)
$$
$$
H_f(x, y) =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2} & 0 \\[8pt]
0 & 2 + \dfrac{1}{y^2}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H_f(P_1) = H_f(P_2) =
\begin{pmatrix}
4 & 0 \\
0 & 4
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
f(P_1) = f(P_2) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} - \ln\frac{1}{2} = 1 + \ln 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain is the set $xy > 0$, that is, the union of the open **first and third quadrants**: every critical point must be sought there. The equation $2x - \dfrac{1}{x} = 0$ is equivalent to $x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$, hence $x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$, and the same holds for $y$. The combinations with opposite signs do not belong to the domain, therefore the critical points are *only* $P_1$ and $P_2$. At both of them the Hessian is the diagonal matrix with entries $4$ and $4$, which is positive definite, hence both are **local minimum points** with value $1 + \ln 2$; the two values coincide because $f(-x, -y) = f(x, y)$. In each of the two quadrants the minimum is **absolute**, since $f \to +\infty$ both toward the boundary of the quadrant, where $\ln(xy) \to -\infty$, and as $x^2 + y^2 \to +\infty$.

</div>
