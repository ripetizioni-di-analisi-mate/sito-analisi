## Exercise 1

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of the function
$$
f(x, y) = xy
\qquad \text{on the circle } x^2 + y^2 = 1 .
$$

$$
x = \cos t, \quad y = \sin t, \quad t \in [0, 2\pi]
$$
$$
g(t) = \cos t \, \sin t = \frac{1}{2}\sin 2t
$$
$$
-\frac{1}{2} \leq g(t) \leq \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(t) = \frac{1}{2} \ &\Longleftrightarrow\ \sin 2t = 1
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ t = \frac{\pi}{4}, \ \frac{5\pi}{4} \\[6pt]
g(t) = -\frac{1}{2} \ &\Longleftrightarrow\ \sin 2t = -1
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ t = \frac{3\pi}{4}, \ \frac{7\pi}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\max f = \frac{1}{2} \ \text{ at } \pm\left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right),
\qquad
\min f = -\frac{1}{2} \ \text{ at } \pm\left( \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The set is the circle alone, *without* its interior: it is a compact curve, hence by **Weierstrass's theorem** the extrema exist, and the search takes place entirely on the curve. The parametrization $x = \cos t$, $y = \sin t$ turns $f$ into the one-variable function $g(t) = \dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2t$, by the double-angle formula for the sine. Since the sine takes values between $-1$ and $1$, and the values $\pm 1$ are attained, the **maximum** is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and the **minimum** is $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. The values of $t$ found give the four points on the bisectors: the maximum at the points of the bisector $y = x$, the minimum at those of the bisector $y = -x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 - y^2 + 2y
\qquad \text{on the disk } x^2 + y^2 \leq 4 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = -2y + 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, 1) \ \text{interior},
\qquad f(0, 1) = 1
$$
$$
\text{boundary } x^2 = 4 - y^2, \ y \in [-2, 2]:
\qquad
g(y) = 4 - y^2 - y^2 + 2y = -2y^2 + 2y + 4
$$
$$
g'(y) = -4y + 2 = 0
\ \Longrightarrow\ y = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
g\!\left( \frac{1}{2} \right) = \frac{9}{2},
\qquad
g(2) = 0, \quad g(-2) = -8
$$
$$
y = \frac{1}{2}
\ \Longrightarrow\
x = \pm\sqrt{4 - \frac{1}{4}} = \pm\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}
$$
$$
\max f = \frac{9}{2} \ \text{ at } \left( \pm\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right),
\qquad
\min f = -8 \ \text{ at } (0, -2)
$$

::grafico{curve="2*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29" area="sqrt(abs(4-x^2));-sqrt(abs(4-x^2));-2;2" punti="0,1,pieno;1.9365,0.5,pieno;-1.9365,0.5,pieno;0,-2,pieno;0,2,pieno" etichette="0.12,1.12,f = 1;0.12,2.12,f = 0;0.12,-2.42,f = −8;2.05,0.62,max;-2.65,0.62,max" xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-2.6 ymax=2.6 uguale=1 titolo="The disk $x^2 + y^2 \leq 4$ with the candidates: maximum $\dfrac{9}{2}$ at the points $\left(\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$, minimum $-8$ at $(0, -2)$, value $1$ at the critical point $(0, 1)$ and $0$ at $(0, 2)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The disk with its boundary is compact and the function is continuous, hence the absolute extrema exist. The only critical point is $(0, 1)$, interior because $0 + 1 < 4$, with value $1$. On the circle the function depends on $y$ directly and on $x$ *only* through $x^2$, therefore the **substitution** $x^2 = 4 - y^2$ is convenient; it gives the parabola $g(y) = -2y^2 + 2y + 4$ on the interval $[-2, 2]$; the endpoints of the interval correspond to the points $(0, 2)$ and $(0, -2)$, where $x = 0$, and must be included among the candidates. The vertex of the parabola is at $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$ with value $\dfrac{9}{2}$, attained at the two symmetric points with $x = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{15}}{2}$. The comparison of the candidates $1$, $\dfrac{9}{2}$, $0$ and $-8$ gives the **absolute maximum** $\dfrac{9}{2}$ and the **absolute minimum** $-8$ at the point $(0, -2)$.

</div>
