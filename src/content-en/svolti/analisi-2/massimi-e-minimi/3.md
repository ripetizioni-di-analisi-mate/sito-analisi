## Exercise 1

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^4 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 4y^3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, 0)
$$
$$
H_f(0, 0) =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det H = 0
$$
$$
f(x, y) - f(0, 0) = x^2 + y^4 \geq 0
\quad \text{for every } (x, y)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The only critical point is the origin. The determinant of the Hessian is zero, hence the criterion does *not* decide and we study **directly the sign** of the difference $f(x, y) - f(0, 0)$. The difference is the sum of the two terms $x^2$ and $y^4$, both nonnegative, therefore it is $\geq 0$ on the whole plane and the origin is an **absolute minimum point**, with value $0$. The minimum is also **strict**, since the sum vanishes only when $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ simultaneously.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^3 .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 3y^2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, 0)
$$
$$
H_f(0, 0) =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det H = 0
$$
$$
f(0, y) = y^3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
f(0, y) > 0 \ \text{ for } y > 0, \qquad f(0, y) < 0 \ \text{ for } y < 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here too the only critical point is the origin and the Hessian is degenerate, with the same matrix as in the previous exercise: the criterion does *not* distinguish the two situations, which are in fact different. For the direct study we look at the **restriction** to the $y$-axis, which equals $y^3$ and **changes sign** across the origin. Hence in every neighborhood of the origin the function takes values both greater and smaller than $f(0, 0) = 0$, therefore the origin is a **saddle point**. To rule out an extremum it is *sufficient* to have one restriction on which the sign of the difference changes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find and classify the critical points of the function
$$
f(x, y) = (y - x^2)(y - 4x^2) .
$$

$$
f(x, y) = y^2 - 5x^2 y + 4x^4
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = -10xy + 16x^3 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y - 5x^2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = \frac{5}{2}x^2, \qquad -25x^3 + 16x^3 = -9x^3 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, 0)
$$
$$
H_f(0, 0) =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det H = 0
$$
$$
f(x, 2x^2) = (2x^2 - x^2)(2x^2 - 4x^2) = -2x^4 < 0 \ \text{ for } x \neq 0
$$
$$
f(0, y) = y^2 > 0 \ \text{ for } y \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Expanding the product we obtain $f(x, y) = y^2 - 5x^2 y + 4x^4$ and the system is solved by substitution: from the second equation $y = \dfrac{5}{2}x^2$, and the first becomes $-9x^3 = 0$, hence the only critical point is the origin, with a degenerate Hessian. The restriction to any line through the origin has **positive sign** near $0$: on $y = mx$ with $m \neq 0$ we have $f(x, mx) = x^2 (m - x)(m - 4x)$, which for small $x$ behaves like $m^2 x^2 > 0$, and on the $x$-axis we have $f(x, 0) = 4x^4 > 0$. The lines alone would suggest a minimum, but the conclusion is different: on the **parabola** $y = 2x^2$, which lies between the two parabolas $y = x^2$ and $y = 4x^2$ where the two factors have opposite signs, the function equals $-2x^4 < 0$, while on the $y$-axis it equals $y^2 > 0$. Hence in every neighborhood of the origin the difference $f(x, y) - f(0, 0)$ takes *both* signs, therefore the origin is a **saddle point**.

</div>
