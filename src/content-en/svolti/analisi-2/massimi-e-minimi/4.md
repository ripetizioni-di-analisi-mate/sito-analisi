## Exercise 1

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2x
\qquad \text{on } K = [0, 2] \times [-1, 1] .
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = 2x - 2 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = 2y = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (1, 0) \ \text{interior to } K,
\qquad f(1, 0) = -1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x = 0 : \quad & g(y) = y^2, \ y \in [-1, 1] & &\text{values from } 0 \text{ to } 1 \\[4pt]
x = 2 : \quad & g(y) = y^2, \ y \in [-1, 1] & &\text{values from } 0 \text{ to } 1 \\[4pt]
y = \pm 1 : \quad & g(x) = x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2, \ x \in [0, 2] & &\text{values from } 0 \text{ to } 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\max_K f = 1 \ \text{ at } (0, \pm 1), (2, \pm 1),
\qquad
\min_K f = -1 \ \text{ at } (1, 0)
$$

::grafico{area="1;-1;0;2" punti="1,0,pieno;0,1,pieno;0,-1,pieno;2,1,pieno;2,-1,pieno" etichette="1.08,-0.32,f = −1;2.08,1.12,f = 1" xmin=-0.8 xmax=2.8 ymin=-1.5 ymax=1.6 uguale=1 titolo="The rectangle $K = [0, 2] \times [-1, 1]$: minimum $-1$ at the interior critical point $(1, 0)$, maximum $1$ at the four vertices"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is continuous and $K$ is closed and bounded, hence by **Weierstrass's theorem** the absolute maximum and minimum exist. The only critical point is $(1, 0)$, interior to the rectangle, with value $-1$. The boundary consists of the four sides and on each of them the restriction is a function of one variable: on the vertical sides it equals $y^2$, with minimum $0$ and maximum $1$; on the horizontal sides it equals $(x - 1)^2$, again with values between $0$ and $1$, and the vertices are included as endpoints of the intervals. The comparison of all the candidate values, namely $-1$, $0$ and $1$, gives the **absolute maximum** $1$, attained at the four vertices, and the **absolute minimum** $-1$, attained at the interior critical point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of the function
$$
f(x, y) = xy - x - y
$$
on the triangle $T$ with vertices $(0, 0)$, $(3, 0)$, $(0, 3)$, boundary included.

$$
\begin{cases}
f_x = y - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
f_y = x - 1 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (1, 1) \ \text{interior to } T,
\qquad f(1, 1) = -1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y = 0 : \quad & g(x) = -x, \ x \in [0, 3] & &\text{values from } -3 \text{ to } 0 \\[4pt]
x = 0 : \quad & g(y) = -y, \ y \in [0, 3] & &\text{values from } -3 \text{ to } 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
y = 3 - x : \quad
g(x) = x(3 - x) - x - (3 - x) = -x^2 + 3x - 3, \ x \in [0, 3]
$$
$$
g'(x) = -2x + 3 = 0
\ \Longrightarrow\ x = \frac{3}{2},
\qquad
g\!\left( \frac{3}{2} \right) = -\frac{3}{4},
\qquad g(0) = g(3) = -3
$$
$$
\max_T f = 0 \ \text{ at } (0, 0),
\qquad
\min_T f = -3 \ \text{ at } (3, 0) \text{ and } (0, 3)
$$

::grafico{area="3-x;0;0;3" punti="1,1,pieno;1.5,1.5,pieno;0,0,pieno;3,0,pieno;0,3,pieno" etichette="0.5,0.6,f = −1;-0.95,-0.4,f = 0;3.05,0.15,f = −3;0.1,3.1,f = −3" xmin=-1 xmax=4 ymin=-0.7 ymax=3.6 uguale=1 titolo="The triangle $T$ with the candidates: the critical point $(1, 1)$, the vertex of the parabola on the hypotenuse $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$ and the three vertices; maximum $0$ at the origin, minimum $-3$ at $(3, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangle with its boundary is compact and the function is continuous, hence the absolute extrema **exist**. The critical point $(1, 1)$ is interior to the triangle, since it has positive coordinates and $1 + 1 = 2 < 3$, and it gives the value $-1$. The boundary has three sides. On the two legs the restriction is linear and the values range from $0$, at the origin, to $-3$, at the other two vertices. On the hypotenuse we substitute $y = 3 - x$ and study the parabola $g(x) = -x^2 + 3x - 3$ on $[0, 3]$: the vertex is at $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$ with value $-\dfrac{3}{4}$, while at the endpoints the value is $-3$, already counted at the vertices. Comparing the candidates $-1$, $0$, $-3$ and $-\dfrac{3}{4}$, the **absolute maximum** is $0$ at the origin and the **absolute minimum** is $-3$, attained at the vertices $(3, 0)$ and $(0, 3)$.

</div>
