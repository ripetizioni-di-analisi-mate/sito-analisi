## Exercise 1

Find the maximum and minimum of the function
$$
f(x, y) = x + 2y
\qquad \text{subject to the constraint } x^2 + y^2 = 5 .
$$

$$
g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 5,
\qquad \nabla g = (2x, 2y) \neq (0, 0) \ \text{on the constraint}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
1 = 2\lambda x \\[4pt]
2 = 2\lambda y \\[4pt]
x^2 + y^2 = 5
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{1}{2\lambda}, \quad y = \frac{1}{\lambda} = 2x
$$
$$
x^2 + 4x^2 = 5
\ \Longrightarrow\ x = \pm 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (1, 2), \ (-1, -2)
$$
$$
f(1, 2) = 5,
\qquad f(-1, -2) = -5
$$
$$
\max f = 5 \ \text{ at } (1, 2),
\qquad
\min f = -5 \ \text{ at } (-1, -2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The gradient of the constraint vanishes only at the origin, which does not satisfy $x^2 + y^2 = 5$, hence *all* candidates solve the Lagrange system. From the first equation it follows that $\lambda \neq 0$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{2\lambda}$; from the second $y = \dfrac{1}{\lambda}$, therefore $y = 2x$. Substituting into the constraint we obtain $5x^2 = 5$, that is $x = \pm 1$, and the candidates are the two points $(1, 2)$ and $(-1, -2)$. Since the circle is compact and $f$ is continuous, by **Weierstrass's theorem** the maximum and minimum exist and are attained at the candidates: the comparison of the values gives **maximum** $5$ and **minimum** $-5$. The value of $\lambda$ is not needed in the answer and was eliminated during the solution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the maximum and minimum of the function
$$
f(x, y) = xy
\qquad \text{on the ellipse } \frac{x^2}{8} + \frac{y^2}{2} = 1 .
$$

$$
g(x, y) = \frac{x^2}{8} + \frac{y^2}{2} - 1,
\qquad \nabla g = \left( \frac{x}{4},\, y \right)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y = \lambda\, \dfrac{x}{4} \\[8pt]
x = \lambda\, y \\[4pt]
\dfrac{x^2}{8} + \dfrac{y^2}{2} = 1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \lambda \cdot \frac{\lambda x}{4} = \frac{\lambda^2}{4}\, x
$$
$$
x \neq 0
\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda^2 = 4
\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda = \pm 2
$$
$$
\lambda = 2 : \quad y = \frac{x}{2},
\qquad \frac{x^2}{8} + \frac{x^2}{8} = 1
\ \Longrightarrow\ x = \pm 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (2, 1), \ (-2, -1), \quad f = 2
$$
$$
\lambda = -2 : \quad y = -\frac{x}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (2, -1), \ (-2, 1), \quad f = -2
$$
$$
\max f = 2 \ \text{ at } (2, 1) \text{ and } (-2, -1),
\qquad
\min f = -2 \ \text{ at } (2, -1) \text{ and } (-2, 1)
$$

::grafico{curve="2*sqrt(2)*cos(t),sqrt(2)*sin(t),0,6.29;t,2/t,-4,4;t,-2/t,-4,4" punti="2,1,pieno;-2,-1,pieno;2,-1,pieno;-2,1,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-3 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="The constraint ellipse and the level hyperbolas $xy = 2$ (dashed) and $xy = -2$ (red): they touch the ellipse at the maximum points $(2, 1)$, $(-2, -1)$ and at the minimum points $(2, -1)$, $(-2, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The gradient of the constraint vanishes only at the origin, which does not belong to the ellipse, hence the **Lagrange system** finds *all* the candidates. Substituting the first equation into the second we obtain $x = \dfrac{\lambda^2}{4}x$; the case $x = 0$ implies $y = 0$ from the second equation, and the origin does not satisfy the constraint, therefore $\lambda^2 = 4$. For $\lambda = 2$ we have $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ and the constraint gives the points $(2, 1)$ and $(-2, -1)$, with $f = 2$; for $\lambda = -2$ we have $y = -\dfrac{x}{2}$ and the points $(2, -1)$ and $(-2, 1)$, with $f = -2$. The ellipse is compact, hence the **constrained maximum** is $2$ and the **constrained minimum** is $-2$, each attained at two points symmetric with respect to the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the point of the plane $x + 2y + 2z = 9$ at minimum distance from the origin and compute that distance.

$$
f(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2,
\qquad g(x, y, z) = x + 2y + 2z - 9,
\qquad \nabla g = (1, 2, 2) \neq (0, 0, 0)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
2x = \lambda \\[4pt]
2y = 2\lambda \\[4pt]
2z = 2\lambda \\[4pt]
x + 2y + 2z = 9
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{\lambda}{2}, \quad y = \lambda, \quad z = \lambda
$$
$$
\frac{\lambda}{2} + 2\lambda + 2\lambda = \frac{9}{2}\lambda = 9
\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (1, 2, 2)
$$
$$
f(1, 2, 2) = 1 + 4 + 4 = 9,
\qquad d = \sqrt{9} = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We minimize the **square of the distance** from the origin, which has the same minimum points as the distance and avoids the square root in the computations. The gradient of the constraint is constant and never zero, hence all candidates solve the Lagrange system, which here has four equations in the unknowns $x$, $y$, $z$, $\lambda$. From the first three equations the coordinates are expressed in terms of $\lambda$, and the constraint gives $\lambda = 2$: the only candidate is $(1, 2, 2)$. The plane is *not* compact, therefore the existence of the minimum must be justified: on the plane $f(x, y, z) \to +\infty$ as the point moves arbitrarily far away, hence the minimum exists and is attained at the only candidate. The **minimum distance** is $3$, in agreement with the point-plane distance formula, which gives $\dfrac{|{-9}|}{\sqrt{1 + 4 + 4}} = 3$; a maximum **does not exist**, since $f$ is not bounded above on the plane.

</div>
