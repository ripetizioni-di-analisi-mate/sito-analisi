## Exercise 1

Among the rectangular boxes with a square base and total surface area $150\ \mathrm{cm}^2$, find the one with maximum volume.

$$
x = \text{side of the base}, \quad h = \text{height}, \qquad x > 0, \ h > 0
$$
$$
2x^2 + 4xh = 150
\ \Longrightarrow\
h = \frac{75 - x^2}{2x},
\qquad h > 0 \ \Longrightarrow\ 0 < x < \sqrt{75}
$$
$$
V(x) = x^2 h = \frac{75x - x^3}{2}
$$
$$
V'(x) = \frac{75 - 3x^2}{2} = 0
\ \Longrightarrow\ x = 5,
\qquad
h = \frac{75 - 25}{10} = 5
$$
$$
V(5) = \frac{375 - 125}{2} = 125\ \mathrm{cm}^3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The variables are the side of the base $x$ and the height $h$, both positive. The total surface area is the sum of the two square bases, $2x^2$, and of the four lateral faces, $4xh$; from the constraint we obtain $h$ and the volume becomes a function of $x$ alone on the interval $\left(0, \sqrt{75}\right)$, where the condition $h > 0$ is satisfied. The derivative vanishes *only* at $x = 5$, is positive before and negative after, hence $V$ is increasing and then decreasing and the point is an **absolute maximum point**. At the ends of the interval the volume tends to $0$, in agreement with the fact that the maximum is interior. Since $h = 5 = x$, the solid of maximum volume is the **cube** of side $5\ \mathrm{cm}$, with volume $125\ \mathrm{cm}^3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the rectangle of maximum area, with sides parallel to the axes, inscribed in the ellipse
$$
\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1 .
$$

$$
\text{vertices } (\pm x, \pm y), \quad x, y > 0,
\qquad A = 4xy
$$
$$
f(x, y) = xy,
\qquad g(x, y) = \frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} - 1
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y = \lambda\, \dfrac{2x}{9} \\[8pt]
x = \lambda\, \dfrac{y}{2} \\[8pt]
\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{\lambda^2}{9}\, x
\ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda^2 = 9
$$
$$
x, y > 0 \ \Longrightarrow\ \lambda = 3,
\qquad y = \frac{2}{3}x
$$
$$
\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{x^2}{9} = 1
\ \Longrightarrow\
x = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2},
\qquad y = \sqrt{2}
$$
$$
A = 4 \cdot \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 12
$$

::grafico{curve="3*cos(t),2*sin(t),0,6.29;t,3/t,0.9,3.6" area="1.4142;-1.4142;-2.1213;2.1213" punti="2.1213,1.4142,pieno" xmin=-3.6 xmax=3.8 ymin=-2.6 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="The rectangle of maximum area inscribed in the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$: the vertex $\left(\dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}, \sqrt{2}\right)$ is the point where the hyperbola $xy = 3$ (dashed) touches the ellipse"}

<div class="spiegazione">

By the symmetry of the ellipse the rectangle has vertices $(\pm x, \pm y)$ with $x, y > 0$, hence the area is $4xy$ and it suffices to maximize $f(x, y) = xy$ on the quarter of the ellipse in the first quadrant, where the vertex $(x, y)$ satisfies the constraint. The gradient of the constraint does not vanish on the ellipse, therefore the **Lagrange system** applies: substituting the first equation into the second we obtain $\lambda^2 = 9$, and the condition $x, y > 0$ forces $\lambda = 3$, from which $y = \dfrac{2}{3}x$. The constraint then gives $x = \dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $y = \sqrt{2}$. The closed arc of the ellipse with $x, y \geq 0$ is compact, hence the maximum **exists**; at the endpoints of the arc, on the axes, the area is zero, therefore the maximum is attained at the *only* interior candidate and **equals** $12$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the point of the parabola $y = x^2$ at minimum distance from the point $(3, 0)$ and compute that distance.

$$
\text{generic point of the parabola: } (x, x^2)
$$
$$
g(x) = (x - 3)^2 + (x^2)^2 = (x - 3)^2 + x^4
$$
$$
g'(x) = 2(x - 3) + 4x^3 = 2\,(2x^3 + x - 3)
$$
$$
2x^3 + x - 3 = (x - 1)(2x^2 + 2x + 3),
\qquad \Delta = 4 - 24 < 0
\ \Longrightarrow\ x = 1
$$
$$
g''(x) = 2 + 12x^2 > 0
$$
$$
g(1) = 4 + 1 = 5,
\qquad d = \sqrt{5}, \qquad \text{point } (1, 1)
$$

::grafico{f="x^2" curve="3+sqrt(5)*cos(t),sqrt(5)*sin(t),0,6.29" segmenti="3,0,1,1" punti="3,0,pieno;1,1,pieno" etichette="2.1,0.68,√5" xmin=-1.5 xmax=5.6 ymin=-2.5 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="The parabola $y = x^2$ and the circle with center $(3, 0)$ and radius $\sqrt{5}$ (dashed): they touch at the nearest point $(1, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The generic point of the parabola has coordinates $(x, x^2)$, hence the square of the distance from $(3, 0)$ is the one-variable function $g(x) = (x - 3)^2 + x^4$; we work with the **square** because it has the *same* minimum points as the distance. The derivative vanishes where $2x^3 + x - 3 = 0$: the value $x = 1$ is a root, and dividing the polynomial by $x - 1$ gives the factor $2x^2 + 2x + 3$, which has no real roots because the discriminant is negative. Hence $x = 1$ is the only critical point. Since $g''(x) = 2 + 12x^2 > 0$ everywhere, the function is **convex** on $\mathbb{R}$, therefore the critical point is an **absolute minimum point**. The nearest point is $(1, 1)$ and the minimum distance is $\sqrt{5}$.

</div>
