## Exercise 1

Compute the partial derivatives of
$$
f(x, y) = x^3 y^2 - 2xy + 5y
$$
and the gradient $\nabla f(1, 2)$.

$$
f_x(x, y) = 3x^2 y^2 - 2y, \qquad f_y(x, y) = 2x^3 y - 2x + 5
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_x(1, 2) &= 3 \cdot 1 \cdot 4 - 2 \cdot 2 = 8 \\[6pt]
f_y(1, 2) &= 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 - 2 + 5 = 7
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\nabla f(1, 2) = (8,\, 7)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compute $f_x$ one differentiates with respect to $x$ treating $y$ as a **constant**: the term $x^3 y^2$ has derivative $3x^2 y^2$, the term $-2xy$ has derivative $-2y$, and the term $5y$ is constant with respect to $x$, so its derivative is zero. For $f_y$ the roles **are swapped**: $x^3 y^2$ gives $2x^3 y$, $-2xy$ gives $-2x$ and $5y$ gives $5$. The gradient at $(1, 2)$ is obtained by substituting the coordinates of the point into the two derivatives, therefore $\nabla f(1,2) = (8, 7)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the gradient of
$$
f(x, y) = (x^2 + y)\, e^{xy}
$$
at the point $(1, 0)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_x(x, y) &= 2x\, e^{xy} + (x^2 + y)\, y\, e^{xy} \\[6pt]
&= e^{xy} \left( 2x + x^2 y + y^2 \right)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_y(x, y) &= e^{xy} + (x^2 + y)\, x\, e^{xy} \\[6pt]
&= e^{xy} \left( 1 + x^3 + xy \right)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
f_x(1, 0) = e^{0} (2 + 0 + 0) = 2, \qquad f_y(1, 0) = e^{0} (1 + 1 + 0) = 2
$$
$$
\nabla f(1, 0) = (2,\, 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a product, so for each partial derivative one applies the **product rule** in the variable of differentiation. Differentiating with respect to $x$, the factor $x^2 + y$ has derivative $2x$ and the factor $e^{xy}$ has derivative $y\, e^{xy}$, because the exponent $xy$ has derivative $y$ with respect to $x$. Differentiating with respect to $y$, the first factor has derivative $1$ and the exponential has derivative $x\, e^{xy}$. In both cases one **factors out** $e^{xy}$ to make the result compact. At the point $(1, 0)$ the exponential equals $e^{0} = 1$, so the polynomials $2x + x^2 y + y^2$ and $1 + x^3 + xy$ remain, and both equal $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the gradient of
$$
f(x, y) = \ln\!\left( x^2 + y^2 \right) + \arctan \frac{y}{x}
$$
at the point $(1, 1)$, in the half-plane $x > 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_x(x, y) &= \frac{2x}{x^2 + y^2} + \frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{y^2}{x^2}} \cdot \left( -\frac{y}{x^2} \right) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2x}{x^2 + y^2} - \frac{y}{x^2 + y^2}
= \frac{2x - y}{x^2 + y^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f_y(x, y) &= \frac{2y}{x^2 + y^2} + \frac{1}{1 + \dfrac{y^2}{x^2}} \cdot \frac{1}{x} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2y}{x^2 + y^2} + \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2}
= \frac{2y + x}{x^2 + y^2}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\nabla f(1, 1) = \left( \frac{2 - 1}{2},\; \frac{2 + 1}{2} \right) = \left( \frac{1}{2},\; \frac{3}{2} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The logarithm is differentiated as a **composite function**: the derivative is the reciprocal of the argument times the derivative of the argument, so with respect to $x$ one obtains $\dfrac{2x}{x^2 + y^2}$ and with respect to $y$ one obtains $\dfrac{2y}{x^2 + y^2}$. The arctangent is also composite: its derivative is $\dfrac{1}{1 + u^2}$ times the derivative of $u = \dfrac{y}{x}$, which is $-\dfrac{y}{x^2}$ with respect to $x$ and $\dfrac{1}{x}$ with respect to $y$. Multiplying numerator and denominator by $x^2$, the factor $\dfrac{1}{1 + \frac{y^2}{x^2}}$ becomes $\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + y^2}$, so the two terms of each derivative have the **same denominator** $x^2 + y^2$ and can be added. At the point $(1, 1)$ the denominator equals $2$, therefore the gradient is $\left( \dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2} \right)$.

</div>
