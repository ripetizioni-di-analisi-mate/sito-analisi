## Exercise 1

Verify with the definition that
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + 3y^2
$$
is differentiable at $(0, 0)$.

$$
f_x(0, 0) = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{f(t, 0) - f(0,0)}{t} = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{t^2}{t} = 0
$$
$$
f_y(0, 0) = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{f(0, t) - f(0,0)}{t} = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{3t^2}{t} = 0
$$
$$
0 \leq \left| \frac{f(h, k) - f(0,0) - 0 \cdot h - 0 \cdot k}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}} \right|
= \frac{h^2 + 3k^2}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}}
\leq \frac{3(h^2 + k^2)}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}} = 3\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}
$$
$$
\lim_{(h,k) \to (0,0)} 3\sqrt{h^2 + k^2} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f \text{ is differentiable at } (0,0)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step is computing the **partial derivatives** at the origin with the definition: along the $x$-axis the function equals $t^2$ and the difference quotient $\dfrac{t^2}{t} = t$ tends to $0$, so $f_x(0,0) = 0$; along the $y$-axis the quotient equals $3t$ and tends to $0$, so $f_y(0,0) = 0$. The second step is the limit in the definition of differentiability, with numerator $f(h,k) - f(0,0) - f_x h - f_y k = h^2 + 3k^2$. Since $h^2 + 3k^2 \leq 3(h^2 + k^2)$, the ratio lies between $0$ and $3\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}$, which tends to $0$; by the **comparison theorem** the limit equals $0$, so the definition is satisfied and $f$ is **differentiable** at the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Study the differentiability at $(0, 0)$ of
$$
f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} .
$$

$$
\frac{f(t, 0) - f(0, 0)}{t} = \frac{\sqrt{t^2}}{t} = \frac{|t|}{t}
$$
$$
\lim_{t \to 0^+} \frac{|t|}{t} = 1, \qquad \lim_{t \to 0^-} \frac{|t|}{t} = -1
$$
$$
f_x(0,0) \text{ does not exist} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f \text{ is not differentiable at } (0,0)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

One starts from the difference quotient of the partial derivative with respect to $x$: along the $x$-axis the function equals $\sqrt{t^2} = |t|$, so the quotient is $\dfrac{|t|}{t}$. The one-sided limits equal $1$ and $-1$ and are different, therefore the limit does not exist and $f$ **is not partially differentiable** with respect to $x$ at the origin. Since differentiability requires the existence of *both* partial derivatives, the *necessary* condition is not satisfied and $f$ **is not differentiable** at $(0,0)$. The graph of $f$ is a **cone** with vertex at the origin, a point at which no plane approximates the graph to first order.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Study the differentiability at $(0, 0)$ of
$$
f(x, y) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} & (x, y) \neq (0, 0) \\[10pt]
0 & (x, y) = (0, 0) .
\end{cases}
$$

$$
f_x(0,0) = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{f(t, 0) - 0}{t} = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{0}{t} = 0,
\qquad
f_y(0,0) = 0
$$
$$
\frac{f(h, k) - 0 - 0 \cdot h - 0 \cdot k}{\sqrt{h^2 + k^2}} = \frac{hk}{h^2 + k^2}
$$
$$
\text{along } k = h: \quad \frac{h^2}{2h^2} = \frac{1}{2},
\qquad
\text{along } k = 0: \quad 0
$$
$$
\text{the limit does not exist} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f \text{ is not differentiable at } (0,0)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

On the axes the function is identically zero, because the numerator $xy$ vanishes when one of the two coordinates is zero; hence both difference quotients are zero and the partial derivatives at the origin exist and equal $0$. The differentiability limit has the function $\dfrac{hk}{h^2 + k^2}$ as its ratio. Along the line $k = h$ the ratio is constantly $\dfrac{1}{2}$, while along the axis $k = 0$ it equals $0$: two restrictions have different limits, so the limit does not exist and in particular it does *not* equal $0$. The definition of differentiability is not satisfied, therefore $f$ **is not differentiable** at the origin, even though it is **partially differentiable** and also **continuous**, since $|f(x,y)| \leq \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ by the inequality $2|xy| \leq x^2 + y^2$.

</div>
