## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the tangent plane to the graph of
$$
f(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - xy
$$
at the point with coordinates $(1, 2)$.

$$
f(1, 2) = 1 + 4 - 2 = 3
$$
$$
f_x = 2x - y, \qquad f_y = 2y - x
$$
$$
f_x(1, 2) = 0, \qquad f_y(1, 2) = 3
$$
$$
z = 3 + 0 \cdot (x - 1) + 3 (y - 2) = 3y - 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is a polynomial, so it is of class $C^1$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, and by the **total differential theorem** it is differentiable at $(1, 2)$: the tangent plane **exists**. The equation requires three numbers, the value $f(1,2) = 3$ and the two partial derivatives at the point, which equal $0$ and $3$. Substituting into the general equation of the tangent plane gives $z = 3 + 3(y - 2)$, that is, $z = 3y - 3$. Since $f_x(1,2) = 0$, the plane **has no slope** in the $x$ direction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the tangent plane to the graph of
$$
f(x, y) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 4}
$$
at the point with coordinates $(2, 1)$.

$$
f(2, 1) = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3
$$
$$
f_x = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 4}}, \qquad f_y = \frac{y}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 4}}
$$
$$
f_x(2, 1) = \frac{2}{3}, \qquad f_y(2, 1) = \frac{1}{3}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
z &= 3 + \frac{2}{3}(x - 2) + \frac{1}{3}(y - 1) \\[6pt]
&= \frac{2}{3}x + \frac{1}{3}y + \frac{4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The root is differentiated as a **composite function**: the derivative of $\sqrt{u}$ is $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{u}}$ times the derivative of the argument, so with respect to $x$ one obtains $\dfrac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 4}} = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 4}}$, and analogously with respect to $y$. The argument of the root is always greater than or equal to $4$, therefore the partial derivatives are continuous on the whole plane and $f$ is **differentiable**. At the point the root equals $3$, so the derivatives equal $\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$. Expanding the products and adding the constant terms, $3 - \dfrac{4}{3} - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$, one obtains the final form of the plane.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the points of the graph of
$$
f(x, y) = x^3 - 3x + y^2
$$
at which the tangent plane is horizontal, and write the corresponding planes.

$$
f_x = 3x^2 - 3, \qquad f_y = 2y
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
3x^2 - 3 = 0 \\[4pt]
2y = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(1, 0) \ \text{ and } \ (-1, 0)
$$
$$
f(1, 0) = 1 - 3 = -2, \qquad f(-1, 0) = -1 + 3 = 2
$$
$$
\text{points } (1, 0, -2) \text{ and } (-1, 0, 2); \qquad \text{planes } z = -2 \ \text{ and } \ z = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tangent plane is horizontal when its equation reduces to $z = f(x_0, y_0)$, and this happens *if and only if* both partial derivatives **vanish** at the point. The two conditions must be satisfied together, so they form the system shown: from the first equation it follows that $x^2 = 1$, that is, $x = 1$ or $x = -1$, and from the second it follows that $y = 0$. The points of the domain are therefore $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$; the corresponding heights equal $-2$ and $2$, so the **horizontal tangent planes** are $z = -2$ and $z = 2$. These points, at which the gradient vanishes, are called **critical points** and are the candidates for maxima and minima, as seen in the chapter on maxima and minima.

</div>
