## Exercise 1

Determine the Maclaurin expansion of $f(x) = x^2 e^{-x}$ and its region of validity.

$$
e^t = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{t^n}{n!} \quad (t \in \mathbb{R}),
\qquad t = -x:
\qquad
e^{-x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^n}{n!}
$$
$$
x^2 e^{-x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{n+2}}{n!}
\qquad (x \in \mathbb{R})
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We start from the **standard expansion of the exponential**, valid for every real number, and substitute $-x$ for the variable: each power $(-x)^n$ equals $(-1)^n x^n$. Multiplication by $x^2$ raises all the exponents by two and does not affect the coefficients. Since the starting expansion holds on all of $\mathbb{R}$ and the operations performed impose no conditions, the result holds for *every* $x$; by the **uniqueness of the expansion**, the series obtained is the Maclaurin series of $f$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the Maclaurin expansion of $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{3 - x}$ and its region of validity.

$$
\frac{1}{3 - x}
= \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{x}{3}}
$$
$$
\frac{1}{1 - t} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} t^n \quad (|t| < 1),
\qquad t = \frac{x}{3}:
\qquad
\frac{1}{3 - x}
= \frac{1}{3} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{3^n}
= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{3^{n+1}}
\qquad (|x| < 3)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The function is not in the form $\dfrac{1}{1 - t}$, so the $3$ in the denominator is factored out to reduce it to the **geometric series**. The substitution is $t = \dfrac{x}{3}$ and the condition of validity of the starting expansion, $|t| < 1$, translates into $|x| < 3$. The factor $\dfrac{1}{3}$ that was factored out multiplies every coefficient, therefore $3^{n+1}$ appears in the denominator.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the Maclaurin expansion of $f(x) = \ln(2 + x)$ and its region of validity.

$$
\ln(2 + x)
= \ln\!\left( 2 \left( 1 + \frac{x}{2} \right) \right)
= \ln 2 + \ln\!\left( 1 + \frac{x}{2} \right)
$$
$$
\ln(1 + t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, t^n}{n} \quad (-1 < t \leq 1),
\qquad t = \frac{x}{2}:
$$
$$
\ln(2 + x)
= \ln 2 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n \, 2^n}
\qquad (-2 < x \leq 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The argument of the logarithm is *not* of the form $1 + t$, so the $2$ is factored out and the **property of the logarithm of a product** is used: the constant $\ln 2$ appears, which is the degree-zero term of the expansion, plus a logarithm in the right form. The substitution is $t = \dfrac{x}{2}$ and the region of validity $-1 < t \leq 1$ of the standard expansion, which includes the right endpoint by **Abel's theorem**, becomes $-2 < x \leq 2$. The powers of $t$ generate the factor $2^n$ in the denominator.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine the Maclaurin expansion of $f(x) = \ln \dfrac{1 + x}{1 - x}$ and its region of validity.

$$
\ln \frac{1 + x}{1 - x} = \ln(1 + x) - \ln(1 - x)
$$
$$
\ln(1 + x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1} \, x^n}{n},
\qquad
\ln(1 - x) = -\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}
\qquad (|x| < 1)
$$
$$
\ln \frac{1 + x}{1 - x}
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\bigl( (-1)^{n+1} + 1 \bigr) \, x^n}{n}
= 2 \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2k+1}}{2k+1}
\qquad (|x| < 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The logarithm of the quotient splits into the **difference of the two logarithms**, each of which can be expanded with the standard expansion: for the second one $-x$ is substituted for $x$, and the minus sign in front changes the sign of all the terms. In the sum, the coefficient of $x^n$ equals $(-1)^{n+1} + 1$, so for even $n$ the two contributions cancel and for odd $n$ they add up to $2$. Therefore *only* the odd powers survive, with $n = 2k + 1$. The validity is the **intersection** of the two regions: the expansion of $\ln(1+x)$ holds on $(-1, 1]$ and that of $\ln(1-x)$ on $[-1, 1)$, so the equality holds on $(-1, 1)$; at the endpoints, moreover, the function is not defined.

</div>
