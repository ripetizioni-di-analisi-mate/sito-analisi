## Exercise 1

Determine the radius and the set of convergence of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n \, 5^n} .
$$

$$
a_n = \frac{1}{n \, 5^n}, \qquad
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|}
= \frac{n \, 5^n}{(n+1) \, 5^{n+1}}
= \frac{n}{5(n+1)} \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} \frac{1}{5}
$$
$$
R = 5
$$
$$
x = 5: \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n} \ \text{ diverges}
\qquad\qquad
x = -5: \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n} \ \text{ converges}
$$
$$
E = [-5,\, 5)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio between the absolute values of two consecutive coefficients tends to $\dfrac{1}{5}$, so by the **ratio test** the radius is the reciprocal, that is, $R = 5$. The series converges absolutely for $|x| < 5$ and does not converge for $|x| > 5$; the two endpoints remain to be examined. At $x = 5$ the powers cancel with the factor $5^n$ and the harmonic series remains, which **diverges**. At $x = -5$ the alternating harmonic series remains: the term $\dfrac{1}{n}$ is decreasing and tends to $0$, so by the **Leibniz test** the series converges. The set of convergence therefore contains the left endpoint and *not* the right one.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the radius and the set of convergence of the series
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{n+1}{2^n} \, (x - 3)^n .
$$

$$
a_n = \frac{n+1}{2^n}, \qquad
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|}
= \frac{n+2}{2^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{2^n}{n+1}
= \frac{n+2}{2(n+1)} \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
R = 2, \qquad \text{open interval } (1,\, 5)
$$
$$
x = 5: \quad \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (n+1) \ \text{ diverges}
\qquad\qquad
x = 1: \quad \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^n (n+1) \ \text{ does not converge}
$$
$$
E = (1,\, 5)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The series is centered at $x_0 = 3$, so the interval of convergence is centered at $3$. The ratio between the absolute values of the coefficients tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $R = 2$ and the open interval is $(1, 5)$. At $x = 5$ we have $(x - 3)^n = 2^n$, which cancels with the denominator, and the series with general term $n + 1$ remains, which tends to $+\infty$: the **necessary condition for convergence** is not satisfied, so the series **diverges**. At $x = 1$ we have $(x - 3)^n = (-2)^n$ and the series with general term $(-1)^n (n+1)$ remains, which does not tend to $0$, so the series **does not converge**. *Neither* of the two endpoints belongs to the set of convergence.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the radius and the set of convergence of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{n^n} \, x^n .
$$

$$
a_n = \frac{n!}{n^n}, \qquad
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|}
= \frac{(n+1)! \, n^n}{(n+1)^{n+1} \, n!}
= \frac{n^n}{(n+1)^n}
= \frac{1}{\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n}}
\xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} \frac{1}{e}
$$
$$
R = e
$$
$$
x = \pm e: \quad b_n = \frac{n! \, e^n}{n^n}, \qquad
\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_n} = \frac{e}{\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n}} > 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_n \geq b_1 = e \ \text{ for every } n
$$
$$
E = (-e,\, e)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio between the coefficients simplifies to $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{\! -n}$, which tends to $\dfrac{1}{e}$ by the **standard limit** defining the number $e$; hence the radius is $R = e$. At the endpoints $x = \pm e$ the absolute value of the general term is $b_n = \dfrac{n! \, e^n}{n^n}$. The ratio $\dfrac{b_{n+1}}{b_n}$ equals $\dfrac{e}{\left( 1 + \frac{1}{n} \right)^{n}}$, and it is greater than $1$ because the sequence $\left( 1 + \dfrac{1}{n} \right)^{\! n}$ is increasing with limit $e$, so it always stays less than $e$. Therefore the sequence $b_n$ is increasing and all its terms are greater than or equal to $b_1 = e$: the general term does not tend to $0$, so the series **does not converge** at *either* of the two endpoints. The set of convergence is the **open interval** $(-e, e)$.

</div>
