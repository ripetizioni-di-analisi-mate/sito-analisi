## Exercise 1

Determine the set of convergence of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x - 2)^n}{n \, 4^n} .
$$

$$
a_n = \frac{1}{n \, 4^n}, \qquad
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|}
= \frac{n}{4(n+1)} \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} \frac{1}{4}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R = 4
$$
$$
\text{open interval: } (-2,\, 6)
$$
$$
x = 6: \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{4^n}{n \, 4^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n} \ \text{ diverges}
$$
$$
x = -2: \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-4)^n}{n \, 4^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n} \ \text{ converges}
$$
$$
E = [-2,\, 6)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The center is $x_0 = 2$ and the ratio between the absolute values of the coefficients tends to $\dfrac{1}{4}$, so $R = 4$ and the open interval of convergence is $(2 - 4,\, 2 + 4) = (-2, 6)$. At each endpoint the value of $x$ is substituted and the resulting numerical series is studied. At $x = 6$ we have $(x-2)^n = 4^n$, the power cancels and the harmonic series remains, which **diverges**. At $x = -2$ we have $(x-2)^n = (-4)^n$ and the alternating harmonic series remains, which converges by the **Leibniz test**, since $\dfrac{1}{n}$ is decreasing and tends to $0$. The set of convergence therefore includes *only* the left endpoint.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the set of convergence of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, (x + 1)^n}{n^2 \, 2^n} .
$$

$$
|a_n| = \frac{1}{n^2 \, 2^n}, \qquad
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|}
= \frac{n^2}{2(n+1)^2} \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} \frac{1}{2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R = 2
$$
$$
\text{open interval: } (-3,\, 1)
$$
$$
x = 1: \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, 2^n}{n^2 \, 2^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2} \ \text{ converges absolutely}
$$
$$
x = -3: \quad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, (-2)^n}{n^2 \, 2^n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} \ \text{ converges}
$$
$$
E = [-3,\, 1]
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $(-1)^n$ has absolute value $1$, so it does *not* affect the radius: the ratio between the absolute values of the coefficients tends to $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $R = 2$. The series is centered at $x_0 = -1$, therefore the open interval is $(-3, 1)$. At $x = 1$ the absolute value of the general term is $\dfrac{1}{n^2}$, the term of a convergent generalized harmonic series, so the series **converges absolutely**. At $x = -3$ the product $(-1)^n (-2)^n$ equals $2^n$, because the two negative signs cancel; the power cancels and the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n^2}$ remains, which converges. *Both* endpoints belong to the set of convergence, so the interval is **closed**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the set of convergence of the series
$$
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{(x - 1)^n}{n \ln n} .
$$

$$
a_n = \frac{1}{n \ln n}, \qquad
\frac{|a_{n+1}|}{|a_n|}
= \frac{n \ln n}{(n+1) \ln(n+1)} \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{} 1
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R = 1
$$
$$
\text{open interval: } (0,\, 2)
$$
$$
x = 2: \quad \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n \ln n} \ \text{ diverges} \qquad
\left( \int_2^{+\infty} \frac{dt}{t \ln t} = \Bigl[ \ln(\ln t) \Bigr]_2^{+\infty} = +\infty \right)
$$
$$
x = 0: \quad \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n \ln n} \ \text{ converges}
$$
$$
E = [0,\, 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio between the coefficients tends to $1$, since $\dfrac{n}{n+1} \to 1$ and $\dfrac{\ln n}{\ln(n+1)} \to 1$, so $R = 1$ and the open interval, centered at $x_0 = 1$, is $(0, 2)$. At $x = 2$ the series $\sum \dfrac{1}{n \ln n}$ remains: comparison with the harmonic series is *not* conclusive, because the term is smaller than $\dfrac{1}{n}$, so the **integral test** is applied. The function $\dfrac{1}{t \ln t}$ is positive and decreasing for $t \geq 2$ and one of its antiderivatives is $\ln(\ln t)$, which tends to $+\infty$: the integral diverges, therefore the series **diverges**. At $x = 0$ the alternating series with term $\dfrac{1}{n \ln n}$ remains, which is decreasing and infinitesimal, and it converges by the **Leibniz test**. The set of convergence is $[0, 2)$.

</div>
