## Exercise 1

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n! \, 2^n} .
$$

$$
\frac{(-1)^n}{n! \, 2^n} = \frac{1}{n!} \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right)^{\! n}
$$
$$
e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!} \quad (x \in \mathbb{R}),
\qquad x = -\frac{1}{2}:
\qquad
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n! \, 2^n} = e^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{e}}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factors $(-1)^n$ and $2^n$ have the same exponent as the factorial in the denominator, so the general term is rewritten as $\dfrac{x^n}{n!}$ with $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$: the series is the **exponential series** evaluated at that point. The expansion of $e^x$ holds on all of $\mathbb{R}$, therefore the substitution is **legitimate** and the sum is $e^{-\frac{1}{2}}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n \, 4^n} .
$$

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n} = -\ln(1 - x) \quad (-1 \leq x < 1),
\qquad x = \frac{1}{4}:
$$
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n \, 4^n}
= -\ln\!\left( 1 - \frac{1}{4} \right)
= -\ln \frac{3}{4}
= \ln \frac{4}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The general term is $\dfrac{x^n}{n}$ with $x = \dfrac{1}{4}$. The suitable expansion is $\sum\limits_{n \geq 1} \dfrac{x^n}{n} = -\ln(1 - x)$, which is obtained from the standard expansion of $\ln(1 + x)$ by substituting $-x$ for $x$ and changing sign. The point $x = \dfrac{1}{4}$ is interior to the **region of validity**, so the sum equals $-\ln \dfrac{3}{4}$, and the minus sign is brought inside the logarithm by passing to the reciprocal of the argument.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{3^n} .
$$

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \, x^n = \frac{x}{(1 - x)^2} \quad (|x| < 1),
\qquad x = \frac{1}{3}:
$$
$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{3^n}
= \frac{\dfrac{1}{3}}{\left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^{\! 2}}
= \frac{\dfrac{1}{3}}{\dfrac{4}{9}}
= \frac{3}{4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $n$ in the numerator points to an expansion obtained **by differentiation**: differentiating the geometric series term by term gives $\sum\limits_{n \geq 1} n \, x^{n-1} = \dfrac{1}{(1-x)^2}$, and multiplying by $x$ leads to $\sum n \, x^n = \dfrac{x}{(1-x)^2}$, with the *same* radius $1$. The point to substitute is $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$, interior to $(-1, 1)$, so the sum equals $\dfrac{1}{3}$ divided by $\dfrac{4}{9}$, that is, $\dfrac{3}{4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Compute the sum of the series
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{9^n \, (2n+1)} .
$$

$$
\arctan x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n \, x^{2n+1}}{2n+1} \quad (|x| \leq 1),
\qquad x = \frac{1}{3}:
$$
$$
\arctan \frac{1}{3}
= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{3^{2n+1} \, (2n+1)}
= \frac{1}{3} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{9^n \, (2n+1)}
$$
$$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{9^n \, (2n+1)} = 3 \arctan \frac{1}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $2n + 1$ in the denominator, together with the alternating sign, recalls the **expansion of the arctangent**. Evaluating that expansion at $x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ produces $3^{2n+1} = 3 \cdot 9^n$: factoring out $\dfrac{1}{3}$, the arctangent series at $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is *exactly* one third of the given series. Since the point $\dfrac{1}{3}$ is interior to the region of validity, the equality is inverted by multiplying by $3$ and the sum equals $3 \arctan \dfrac{1}{3}$.

</div>
