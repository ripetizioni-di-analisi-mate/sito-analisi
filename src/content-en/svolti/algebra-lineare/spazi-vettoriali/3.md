## Exercise 1

Find a basis and the dimension of the subspace
$$
W = \{ (x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : x - 2y + z = 0 \} .
$$

$$
x = 2y - z
$$
$$
(x, y, z) = (2y - z, \, y, \, z) = y\,(2, 1, 0) + z\,(-1, 0, 1)
$$
$$
W = \langle (2, 1, 0), (-1, 0, 1) \rangle , \qquad \dim W = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There is only one equation and it has rank $1$, so by the formula $\dim W = n - \operatorname{rk} A$ of the **Cartesian form** we have $\dim W = 3 - 1 = 2$. To find a basis we solve for $x$ in terms of the free variables $y$ and $z$ and write the generic vector of $W$ factoring out $y$ and $z$. The two vectors $(2, 1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0, 1)$ span $W$ and are not proportional, so they are independent: they form a **basis** of $W$, which is a plane through the origin. The check is done by substituting into the equation: $2 - 2 + 0 = 0$ and $-1 - 0 + 1 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find a basis and the dimension of the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^4$
$$
W :
\begin{cases}
x_1 + x_2 - x_3 = 0 \\[4pt]
x_2 + x_3 - x_4 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
x_2 = x_4 - x_3 ,
\qquad
x_1 = x_3 - x_2 = x_3 - (x_4 - x_3) = 2x_3 - x_4
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) &= (2x_3 - x_4, \, x_4 - x_3, \, x_3, \, x_4) \\[6pt]
&= x_3\,(2, -1, 1, 0) + x_4\,(-1, 1, 0, 1)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
W = \langle (2, -1, 1, 0), (-1, 1, 0, 1) \rangle , \qquad \dim W = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient matrix has rows $(1, 1, -1, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 1, -1)$, which are not proportional, so it has rank $2$ and $\dim W = 4 - 2 = 2$. The system is already in echelon form, with pivots on the variables $x_1$ and $x_2$, therefore $x_3$ and $x_4$ are chosen as free variables. From the second equation we obtain $x_2$, then we substitute into the first and obtain $x_1$. Factoring out $x_3$ and $x_4$ in the generic vector we obtain two vectors that span $W$; they are independent, because their last two components are $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$. Hence they form a **basis** of $W$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find a basis and the dimension of the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^4$
$$
W :
\begin{cases}
x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4 = 0 \\[4pt]
x_1 - x_2 + x_3 - x_4 = 0 \\[4pt]
3x_1 + x_2 + 3x_3 + x_4 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & -1 & 1 & -1 \\
3 & 1 & 3 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 \to R_2 - R_1 \\ R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1}}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & -2 & 0 & -2 \\
0 & -2 & 0 & -2
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 - R_2}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & -2 & 0 & -2 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = 2 , \qquad \dim W = 4 - 2 = 2
$$
$$
-2x_2 - 2x_4 = 0 \ \Longrightarrow \ x_2 = -x_4 ,
\qquad
x_1 = -x_2 - x_3 - x_4 = -x_3
$$
$$
(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) = (-x_3, \, -x_4, \, x_3, \, x_4) = x_3\,(-1, 0, 1, 0) + x_4\,(0, -1, 0, 1)
$$
$$
W = \langle (-1, 0, 1, 0), (0, -1, 0, 1) \rangle
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three equations, but row reduction makes the third row vanish: the third equation is the sum of twice the first and the second, so it adds no conditions. The rank is $2$, therefore $\dim W = 4 - 2 = 2$, and counting the equations without computing the rank would give a wrong dimension. From the echelon system we obtain $x_2 = -x_4$ and then $x_1 = -x_3$, with $x_3$ and $x_4$ free. The two vectors obtained by factoring out the free variables are independent, so they form a **basis** of $W$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Let $B = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Find a basis and the dimension of the subspace
$$
W = \{ A \in M_2(\mathbb{R}) : AB = BA \} .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
AB = \begin{pmatrix} a & a + b \\ c & c + d \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
BA = \begin{pmatrix} a + c & b + d \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
AB = BA
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\begin{cases}
a = a + c \\[4pt]
a + b = b + d \\[4pt]
c + d = d
\end{cases}
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\begin{cases}
c = 0 \\[4pt]
d = a
\end{cases}
$$
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a \end{pmatrix}
= a \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} + b \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
W = \langle I_2, E_{12} \rangle , \qquad \dim W = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We write the generic matrix $A$ with four unknowns and impose the equality $AB = BA$ entry by entry. The entries in position $(2, 1)$ always coincide, so three equations remain, which reduce to $c = 0$ and $d = a$. The matrices of $W$ therefore depend on the two free parameters $a$ and $b$, and factoring them out we obtain $A = a\,I_2 + b\,E_{12}$. The matrices $I_2$ and $E_{12}$ span $W$ and are not proportional, therefore they form a **basis** of $W$ and $\dim W = 2$. In coordinates with respect to the standard basis of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$, $W$ is the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^4$ defined by the equations $c = 0$ and $d - a = 0$, and the dimension $4 - 2 = 2$ agrees with the formula of the Cartesian form.

</div>
