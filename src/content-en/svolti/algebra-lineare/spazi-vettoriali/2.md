## Exercise 1

Determine whether the vectors $v_1 = (1, 2, 1)$, $v_2 = (0, 1, 3)$, $v_3 = (2, 3, -1)$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ are linearly independent; if not, write a dependence relation.

$$
a\,(1, 2, 1) + b\,(0, 1, 3) + c\,(2, 3, -1) = (0, 0, 0)
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\begin{cases}
a + 2c = 0 \\[4pt]
2a + b + 3c = 0 \\[4pt]
a + 3b - c = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
a = -2c
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
-4c + b + 3c = 0 , \quad b = c
$$
$$
a + 3b - c = -2c + 3c - c = 0 \qquad \text{for every } c
$$
$$
(a, b, c) = c\,(-2, 1, 1)
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
-2v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0 ,
\qquad
v_3 = 2v_1 - v_2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the definition of linear independence we write the zero combination and solve the homogeneous system in the unknowns $a$, $b$, $c$. The first equation gives $a = -2c$; substituting into the second we obtain $b = c$. With these two expressions the third equation becomes $0 = 0$, so it is a consequence of the others and $c$ remains free. Since the system has nonzero solutions, the vectors are **linearly dependent**. With $c = 1$ we obtain the relation $-2v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0$, from which $v_3 = 2v_1 - v_2$; the direct check gives $2\,(1, 2, 1) - (0, 1, 3) = (2, 3, -1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine whether the vectors $v_1 = (1, 0, 1, 2)$, $v_2 = (0, 1, 1, -1)$, $v_3 = (1, 1, 0, 1)$ of $\mathbb{R}^4$ are linearly independent.

$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & 0 \\
2 & -1 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\substack{R_3 \to R_3 - R_1 \\ R_4 \to R_4 - 2R_1}}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & -1 \\
0 & -1 & -1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\xrightarrow{\substack{R_3 \to R_3 - R_2 \\ R_4 \to R_4 + R_2}}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & -2 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\qquad
\operatorname{rk} A = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three vectors in $\mathbb{R}^4$, so the matrix $A$ that has them as columns is $4 \times 3$ and the determinant is not defined. We use the theorem on **independence and rank**: the vectors are independent if and only if $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$. After row reduction three nonzero rows remain, with pivots in columns $1$, $2$ and $3$, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$ and the vectors are **linearly independent**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the polynomials $p_1 = 1 + x^2$, $p_2 = x - x^2$, $p_3 = 1 + x + 2x^2$ of $\mathbb{R}_2[x]$ are linearly independent.

$$
p_1 \mapsto (1, 0, 1) , \qquad p_2 \mapsto (0, 1, -1) , \qquad p_3 \mapsto (1, 1, 2)
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & -1 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot \big( 1 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot (-1) \big) - 0 + 1 \cdot \big( 0 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1 \big)
$$
$$
= 3 - 1 = 2 \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We pass to coordinates with respect to the standard basis $(1, x, x^2)$: the polynomial $a + bx + cx^2$ corresponds to the vector $(a, b, c)$. By the remark on **computations in coordinates**, the polynomials are independent if and only if their coordinate vectors are. There are three vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$, so we compute the determinant of the matrix that has them as columns, expanded along the first row. Since the determinant equals $2 \neq 0$, the coordinate vectors are independent, therefore $p_1$, $p_2$, $p_3$ are **linearly independent** and form a basis of $\mathbb{R}_2[x]$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

As $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, determine for which values the vectors
$$
v_1 = (1, 1, k) , \qquad v_2 = (1, k, 1) , \qquad v_3 = (k, 1, 1)
$$
are linearly dependent, and for those values write a dependence relation.

$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & k \\
1 & k & 1 \\
k & 1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 \to R_2 - R_1 \\ R_3 \to R_3 - kR_1}}
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & k \\
0 & k - 1 & 1 - k \\
0 & 1 - k & 1 - k^2
\end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A &= (k - 1)(1 - k^2) - (1 - k)^2 \\[6pt]
&= (1 - k)\big[ (k - 1)(1 + k) - (1 - k) \big] \\[6pt]
&= (1 - k)(k^2 + k - 2) \\[6pt]
&= -(k - 1)^2 (k + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\det A = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad k = 1 \ \text{ or } \ k = -2
$$
$$
k = 1 : \quad v_1 = v_2 = v_3 = (1, 1, 1) , \qquad v_1 - v_2 = 0
$$
$$
k = -2 : \quad (1, 1, -2) + (1, -2, 1) + (-2, 1, 1) = (0, 0, 0) , \qquad v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$, so by the theorem on **independence and rank** they are dependent if and only if the determinant of the matrix $A$ that has them as columns is zero. The operations $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - kR_1$ do not change the determinant, and the resulting matrix is expanded along the first column. Factoring out $1 - k$ and factoring $k^2 + k - 2 = (k - 1)(k + 2)$ we obtain $\det A = -(k - 1)^2 (k + 2)$, which vanishes for $k = 1$ and for $k = -2$.

For $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -2$ the vectors are independent. For $k = 1$ the three vectors coincide, so they are **dependent** and span only the line $\langle (1, 1, 1) \rangle$. For $k = -2$ the sum of the three vectors is zero, so they are **dependent**, with relation $v_1 + v_2 + v_3 = 0$; since $v_1$ and $v_2$ are not proportional, in this case they span a plane.

</div>
