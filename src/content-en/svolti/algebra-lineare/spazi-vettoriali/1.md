## Exercise 1

Determine whether the following subsets of $\mathbb{R}^3$ are vector subspaces:
$$
W_1 = \{ (x, y, z) : 2x - y + 3z = 0 \} ,
\qquad
W_2 = \{ (x, y, z) : 2x - y + 3z = 4 \} .
$$

$$
W_1 : \quad 2 \cdot 0 - 0 + 3 \cdot 0 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad (0, 0, 0) \in W_1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2(u_1 + v_1) - (u_2 + v_2) + 3(u_3 + v_3) &= (2u_1 - u_2 + 3u_3) + (2v_1 - v_2 + 3v_3) \\[6pt]
&= 0 + 0 = 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
2(\lambda u_1) - \lambda u_2 + 3(\lambda u_3) = \lambda\,(2u_1 - u_2 + 3u_3) = \lambda \cdot 0 = 0
$$
$$
W_2 : \quad 2 \cdot 0 - 0 + 3 \cdot 0 = 0 \neq 4 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad (0, 0, 0) \notin W_2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $W_1$ we check the three conditions of the **subspace criterion**. The zero vector satisfies the equation. If $u = (u_1, u_2, u_3)$ and $v = (v_1, v_2, v_3)$ lie in $W_1$, the expressions $2u_1 - u_2 + 3u_3$ and $2v_1 - v_2 + 3v_3$ equal $0$; since the left-hand side of the equation evaluated at $u + v$ is the sum of these two expressions, $u + v$ also satisfies the equation. In the same way the left-hand side evaluated at $\lambda u$ is $\lambda$ times the one evaluated at $u$, so it equals $0$. Therefore $W_1$ **is a subspace**: it is the plane through the origin with normal vector $(2, -1, 3)$.

The set $W_2$ does *not* contain the zero vector, because at $(0, 0, 0)$ the left-hand side equals $0$ and not $4$. Hence $W_2$ **is not a subspace**: it is the plane parallel to $W_1$ that does not pass through the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine whether $W = \{ (x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : y = x^2 \}$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$.

$$
(0, 0) \in W \qquad \text{since } 0 = 0^2
$$
$$
u = (1, 1) \in W , \qquad 2u = (2, 2) , \qquad 2 \neq 2^2 = 4
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad 2u \notin W
$$

::grafico{f="x^2" vettori="0,0,1,1,u" segmenti="1,1,2,2" punti="1,1,pieno;2,2,vuoto,2u" xmin=-2.5 xmax=3 ymin=-0.8 ymax=4.6 uguale=1 titolo="The parabola $y = x^2$ contains $u = (1, 1)$ and does not contain $2u = (2, 2)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The zero vector lies in $W$, so the first condition of the criterion is satisfied and is not enough to decide. We test closure under scalar multiplication with a concrete vector: $u = (1, 1)$ belongs to $W$ because $1 = 1^2$, while its double $(2, 2)$ does not belong to it, because $2 \neq 4$. Since the third condition is *not* satisfied, $W$ **is not a subspace**. A single counterexample is sufficient; geometrically, the parabola does not contain the line through the origin and $u$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the following subsets of $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ are subspaces:
$$
W = \{ A \in M_2(\mathbb{R}) : \operatorname{tr} A = 0 \} ,
\qquad
U = \{ A \in M_2(\mathbb{R}) : \det A = 0 \} .
$$

$$
\operatorname{tr} O = 0 + 0 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad O \in W
$$
$$
\operatorname{tr}(A + B) = (a_{11} + b_{11}) + (a_{22} + b_{22}) = \operatorname{tr} A + \operatorname{tr} B = 0
$$
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\lambda A) = \lambda a_{11} + \lambda a_{22} = \lambda \operatorname{tr} A = 0
$$
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \in U ,
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \in U ,
\qquad
\det (A + B) = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 1 \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The trace of the zero matrix $O$ is $0$, so $O \in W$. The trace is additive and homogeneous, that is $\operatorname{tr}(A + B) = \operatorname{tr} A + \operatorname{tr} B$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\lambda A) = \lambda \operatorname{tr} A$, as the entrywise computations show; therefore if $A$ and $B$ have zero trace, $A + B$ and $\lambda A$ also have zero trace. By the **subspace criterion** $W$ **is a subspace**.

The set $U$ contains the zero matrix and is closed under scalar multiplication, since $\det(\lambda A) = \lambda^2 \det A$. The sum, however, can leave $U$: the matrices $A$ and $B$ have determinant $0$, while their sum is the identity, with determinant $1$. Hence $U$ **is not a subspace**.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether the following subsets of $\mathbb{R}_2[x]$ are subspaces:
$$
W = \{ p \in \mathbb{R}_2[x] : p(1) = 0 \} ,
\qquad
U = \{ p \in \mathbb{R}_2[x] : p(0) \cdot p(1) = 0 \} .
$$

$$
p(x) = 0 \ \text{ for every } x \quad \Longrightarrow \quad p(1) = 0 , \quad 0 \in W
$$
$$
(p + q)(1) = p(1) + q(1) = 0 + 0 = 0 ,
\qquad
(\lambda p)(1) = \lambda\, p(1) = \lambda \cdot 0 = 0
$$
$$
p(x) = x : \quad p(0) \cdot p(1) = 0 \cdot 1 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad p \in U
$$
$$
q(x) = x - 1 : \quad q(0) \cdot q(1) = (-1) \cdot 0 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad q \in U
$$
$$
(p + q)(x) = 2x - 1 : \quad (p + q)(0) \cdot (p + q)(1) = (-1) \cdot 1 = -1 \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value of a polynomial at a point behaves linearly: the value of the sum is the sum of the values, and the value of $\lambda p$ is $\lambda$ times the value of $p$. Hence the zero polynomial lies in $W$ and, if $p(1) = q(1) = 0$, then $p + q$ and $\lambda p$ also vanish at $1$. By the **subspace criterion** $W$ **is a subspace**: it consists of the polynomials divisible by $x - 1$.

The condition that defines $U$ is a product, and the product is *not* linear. The polynomial $p(x) = x$ lies in $U$ because it vanishes at $0$, and $q(x) = x - 1$ lies in $U$ because it vanishes at $1$; the sum $2x - 1$ equals $-1$ at $0$ and $1$ at $1$, so the product of the two values is $-1 \neq 0$. Therefore $U$ **is not a subspace**: it is the union of the two subspaces $\{ p(0) = 0 \}$ and $\{ p(1) = 0 \}$.

</div>
