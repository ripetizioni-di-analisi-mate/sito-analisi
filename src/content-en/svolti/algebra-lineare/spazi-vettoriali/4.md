## Exercise 1

Verify that $\mathcal{B} = \big( (1, 0, 1), (1, 1, 0), (0, 1, 1) \big)$ is a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$ and find the coordinates of $v = (4, 3, 5)$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$.

$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 0 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (1 - 0) - 1 \cdot (0 - 1) + 0 = 2 \neq 0
$$
$$
a\,(1, 0, 1) + b\,(1, 1, 0) + c\,(0, 1, 1) = (4, 3, 5)
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\begin{cases}
a + b = 4 \\[4pt]
b + c = 3 \\[4pt]
a + c = 5
\end{cases}
$$
$$
2(a + b + c) = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad a + b + c = 6
$$
$$
a = 6 - 3 = 3 , \qquad b = 6 - 5 = 1 , \qquad c = 6 - 4 = 2
$$
$$
[v]_{\mathcal{B}} = (3, 1, 2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are three vectors in a space of dimension $3$, so by the **consequences of the dimension** they form a basis if and only if they are independent, that is, if the determinant of the matrix that has them as columns is nonzero; the determinant equals $2$, therefore $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis. The coordinates are the coefficients of the combination that gives $v$, and they are found by solving the system. Adding the three equations we obtain $a + b + c = 6$; subtracting each equation from this equality we obtain the unknown missing from it. The coordinates of $v$ are therefore $(3, 1, 2)$, and the check gives $3\,(1, 0, 1) + (1, 1, 0) + 2\,(0, 1, 1) = (4, 3, 5)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that $\mathcal{B} = \big( 1, \ x - 1, \ (x - 1)^2 \big)$ is a basis of $\mathbb{R}_2[x]$ and find the coordinates of $p(x) = 2 + 3x + x^2$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$.

$$
1 \mapsto (1, 0, 0) , \qquad x - 1 \mapsto (-1, 1, 0) , \qquad (x - 1)^2 = 1 - 2x + x^2 \mapsto (1, -2, 1)
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & -1 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & -2 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \neq 0
$$
$$
a + b\,(x - 1) + c\,(x - 1)^2 = (a - b + c) + (b - 2c)\,x + c\,x^2
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
a - b + c = 2 \\[4pt]
b - 2c = 3 \\[4pt]
c = 1
\end{cases}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
c = 1 , \quad b = 5 , \quad a = 2 + 5 - 1 = 6
$$
$$
[p]_{\mathcal{B}} = (6, 5, 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We pass to coordinates with respect to the standard basis $(1, x, x^2)$. The matrix that has the three resulting vectors as columns is upper triangular with diagonal $1, 1, 1$, so it has determinant $1$; since $\dim \mathbb{R}_2[x] = 3$, the three polynomials form a **basis**. For the coordinates we expand the generic combination, order the terms by degree and equate the coefficients to those of $p$. The system is triangular and is solved from the bottom up: $c = 1$, then $b = 3 + 2c = 5$, finally $a = 2 + b - c = 6$. The check gives $6 + 5(x - 1) + (x - 1)^2 = 6 + 5x - 5 + x^2 - 2x + 1 = x^2 + 3x + 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ consider the matrices
$$
A_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \quad
A_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \quad
A_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \quad
A_4 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$
Verify that $\mathcal{B} = (A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4)$ is a basis and find the coordinates of $M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$.

$$
a_1 A_1 + a_2 A_2 + a_3 A_3 + a_4 A_4 =
\begin{pmatrix}
a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + a_4 & a_2 + a_3 + a_4 \\
a_3 + a_4 & a_4
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
a_1 + a_2 + a_3 + a_4 = 2 \\[4pt]
a_2 + a_3 + a_4 = 3 \\[4pt]
a_3 + a_4 = -1 \\[4pt]
a_4 = 4
\end{cases}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
a_4 = 4 , \quad a_3 = -5 , \quad a_2 = 4 , \quad a_1 = -1
$$
$$
[M]_{\mathcal{B}} = (-1, 4, -5, 4)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system that gives the coordinates of an arbitrary matrix $\begin{pmatrix} p & q \\ r & s \end{pmatrix}$ has the same triangular form, with $a_4 = s$, $a_3 = r - s$, $a_2 = q - r$, $a_1 = p - q$, so it *always* has one and only one solution. Therefore the four matrices span $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ and, being four in a space of dimension $4$, they form a **basis**. For $M$ the system is solved from the bottom up: $a_4 = 4$, then $a_3 = -1 - 4 = -5$, then $a_2 = 3 - (-1) = 4$, finally $a_1 = 2 - 3 = -1$. The coordinates of $M$ are $(-1, 4, -5, 4)$; the check on the entry in position $(1, 1)$ gives $-1 + 4 - 5 + 4 = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Let $W = \{ (x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : x + y + z = 0 \}$ and let $\mathcal{B} = \big( (1, -1, 0), (0, 1, -1) \big)$. Verify that $\mathcal{B}$ is a basis of $W$, that $w = (2, 3, -5)$ belongs to $W$, and find the coordinates of $w$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$.

$$
\dim W = 3 - 1 = 2
$$
$$
1 - 1 + 0 = 0 , \qquad 0 + 1 - 1 = 0 , \qquad 2 + 3 - 5 = 0
$$
$$
a\,(1, -1, 0) + b\,(0, 1, -1) = (a, \, b - a, \, -b) = (2, 3, -5)
$$
$$
a = 2 , \qquad b = 5 , \qquad b - a = 3 \quad \text{satisfied}
$$
$$
[w]_{\mathcal{B}} = (2, 5)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

$W$ is a plane of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with one equation of rank $1$, so $\dim W = 2$. The two vectors of $\mathcal{B}$ satisfy the equation, so they lie in $W$, and they are not proportional, so they are independent; since they are two in a space of dimension $2$, they form a **basis** of $W$. The vector $w$ also satisfies the equation, therefore it belongs to $W$. The system for the coordinates has three equations and two unknowns: the first and the third give $a = 2$ and $b = 5$, and the second is satisfied, as must happen for a vector of $W$. The coordinates of $w$ with respect to $\mathcal{B}$ are $(2, 5)$, a pair, because $W$ has dimension $2$.

</div>
