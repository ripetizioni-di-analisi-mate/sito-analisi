## Exercise 1

Compute the determinant of
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 5 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\det A = a_{22}\, C_{22} = 3 \cdot (-1)^{2+2}
\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 4 & 2 \end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 4 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 4 - (-4) = 8
$$
$$
\det A = 3 \cdot 8 = 24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second row contains two zeros, so by **Laplace's theorem** it is convenient to expand along this row, since the terms $a_{21} C_{21}$ and $a_{23} C_{23}$ are zero and only the term $a_{22} C_{22}$ remains. The cofactor $C_{22}$ has sign $(-1)^{4} = +1$ and is obtained by deleting the second row and the second column of the matrix. The remaining determinant of order $2$ equals $8$, therefore $\det A = 24$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the determinant of
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 & -1 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\det A = a_{22}\, C_{22} = 2 \cdot (-1)^{2+2}
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix}
&= 1 \cdot (-1)^{1+1} \begin{vmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix}
+ 2 \cdot (-1)^{3+1} \begin{vmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 4 & 1 \end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= 1 \cdot (12 - 1) + 2 \cdot (2 + 4) \\[6pt]
&= 11 + 12 = 23
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\det A = 2 \cdot 23 = 46
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second column has only one nonzero entry, $a_{22} = 2$, so the expansion along the second column reduces to a single determinant of order $3$, with sign $(-1)^{2+2} = +1$. Deleting the second row and the second column leaves rows $1$, $3$, $4$ and columns $1$, $3$, $4$ of $A$. The determinant of order $3$ is in turn expanded along the first column, which contains a zero, so two terms remain, both with sign $+$, since $1 + 1$ and $3 + 1$ are even. The sum of the two terms is $23$, therefore $\det A = 46$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the determinant of
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 5 & -1 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\det A = a_{22}\, C_{22} = 2 \cdot (-1)^{2+2}
\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 & -1 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 & -1 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
= 5 \cdot (-1)^{2+2} \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
= 5 \cdot (3 - 4) = -5
$$
$$
\det A = 2 \cdot (-5) = -10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second row has three zeros, so the expansion along this row leaves only the term $a_{22} C_{22}$, with sign $+$. The matrix of order $3$ obtained by deleting the second row and the second column of $A$ in turn has two zeros in the second column, therefore it is expanded along that column and the entry $5$ remains, in position $(2, 2)$ of the reduced matrix, with sign $+$. The final determinant of order $2$ equals $-1$, so $\det A = -10$. Choosing a row or column with many zeros at each step reduces the computation of a determinant of order $4$ to a single determinant of order $2$.

</div>
