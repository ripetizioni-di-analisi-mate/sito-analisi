## Exercise 1

Determine whether the matrices
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 4 \\ 9 & 6 \end{pmatrix}
$$
are invertible and, if so, compute their inverse.

$$
\det A = 8 - 3 = 5 \neq 0,
\qquad
\det B = 36 - 36 = 0
$$
$$
A^{-1} = \frac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -3 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
A A^{-1} = \frac{1}{5}
\begin{pmatrix} 8 - 3 & -6 + 6 \\ 4 - 4 & -3 + 8 \end{pmatrix}
= \frac{1}{5} \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix}
= I_2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **invertibility criterion** a square matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero. Since $\det A = 5$, the matrix $A$ is invertible; since $\det B = 0$, the matrix $B$ **is not invertible**, in agreement with the fact that the second row of $B$ is $\dfrac{3}{2}$ times the first. For the inverse of order $2$ one swaps the entries of the main diagonal, $2$ and $4$, changes the sign of the entries $3$ and $1$, and divides by the determinant. The product $A A^{-1} = I_2$ confirms the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the inverse of
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\det A = a_{23}\, C_{23} = 1 \cdot (-1)^{2+3} \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = -(3 - 4) = 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{11} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 3 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = -3, &
C_{12} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = 2, &
C_{13} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = -2, \\[6pt]
C_{21} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 3 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = 0, &
C_{22} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = 0, &
C_{23} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = 1, \\[6pt]
C_{31} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 2, &
C_{32} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -1, &
C_{33} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\operatorname{cof} A = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 2 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A^{-1} = \frac{1}{1}\, (\operatorname{cof} A)^T = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The third column of $A$ has only one nonzero entry, so the determinant is computed by expanding along that column; the sign of the cofactor in position $(2, 3)$ is $-1$, and the result is $\det A = 1$. Since $\det A \neq 0$, the matrix is invertible and the **formula for the inverse** applies. Each cofactor $C_{ij}$ is the determinant of the matrix obtained by deleting row $i$ and column $j$, with the checkerboard sign. The cofactors are arranged in the matrix $\operatorname{cof} A$, which must be transposed, that is, the first row of $\operatorname{cof} A$ becomes the first column of $A^{-1}$. Since $\det A = 1$, no division remains. The product of the first row of $A$ and the columns of $A^{-1}$ gives $(1, 0, 0)$, and the other rows are checked in the same way.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the inverse of
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\det A = (1 + 0 + 4) - (0 + 3 + 0) = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{11} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -2, &
C_{12} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -2, &
C_{13} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 2, \\[6pt]
C_{21} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 2, &
C_{22} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 1, &
C_{23} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -1, \\[6pt]
C_{31} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = -2, &
C_{32} &= -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = 1, &
C_{33} &= +\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
A^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}\, (\operatorname{cof} A)^T
= \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 2 & -2 \\ -2 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 & -1 \\[4pt] -1 & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \\[4pt] 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant is computed with **Sarrus' rule**: the diagonals to the right give $1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 3 \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 5$, those to the left give $2 \cdot 1 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 3$, so $\det A = 2$ and the matrix is **invertible**. The nine cofactors are computed with the checkerboard signs and the transpose of $\operatorname{cof} A$ is written, that is, the matrix that has $C_{1i}$, $C_{2i}$, $C_{3i}$ in row $i$. Dividing by $2$ gives $A^{-1}$. The check on the second row of $A$ and the second column of $A^{-1}$ gives $2 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot \dfrac{1}{2} + 3 \cdot \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = 1$, as required by the entry $(2, 2)$ of $I_3$.

</div>
