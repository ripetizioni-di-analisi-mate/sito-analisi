## Exercise 1

Determine for which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & k & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & k \end{pmatrix}
$$
is invertible.

$$
\begin{vmatrix} k & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & k & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & k \end{vmatrix}
\xrightarrow{C_1 \to C_1 + C_2 + C_3}
\begin{vmatrix} k + 2 & 1 & 1 \\ k + 2 & k & 1 \\ k + 2 & 1 & k \end{vmatrix}
= (k + 2) \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & k & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & k \end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 \to R_2 - R_1 \\ R_3 \to R_3 - R_1}}
(k + 2) \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & k - 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & k - 1 \end{vmatrix}
= (k + 2)(k - 1)^2
$$
$$
\det A_k = 0 \iff k = -2 \ \text{ or } \ k = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three rows have the same sum $k + 2$, so adding the other two columns to the first gives a column with all entries equal to $k + 2$, and the factor $k + 2$ is pulled out. The two subsequent row subtractions do not change the determinant and produce a triangular matrix, with diagonal product $(k - 1)^2$. The determinant is thus already **factored**, and it vanishes only for $k = -2$ and for $k = 1$. By the **invertibility criterion**, $A_k$ is invertible for $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-2, 1\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine for which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} k - 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & k - 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & k + 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is invertible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A_k &= (k + 1) \cdot (-1)^{3+3} \begin{vmatrix} k - 1 & 2 \\ 2 & k - 1 \end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= (k + 1)\bigl[(k - 1)^2 - 4\bigr] \\[6pt]
&= (k + 1)(k - 1 - 2)(k - 1 + 2) \\[6pt]
&= (k + 1)^2 (k - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\det A_k = 0 \iff k = -1 \ \text{ or } \ k = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The third row has only one nonzero entry, so by **Laplace's theorem** one expands along this row and a single determinant of order $2$ remains, with sign $+$. The expression $(k - 1)^2 - 4$ is a difference of squares, therefore it factors as $(k - 1 - 2)(k - 1 + 2) = (k - 3)(k + 1)$. The factor $k + 1$ appears twice, and the determinant vanishes for $k = -1$ and for $k = 3$. Hence $A_k$ is **invertible** for $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 3\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine for which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & k \\ 1 & 4 & k^2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is invertible.

$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & k \\ 1 & 4 & k^2 \end{vmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 \to R_2 - R_1 \\ R_3 \to R_3 - R_1}}
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & k - 1 \\ 0 & 3 & k^2 - 1 \end{vmatrix}
= \begin{vmatrix} 1 & k - 1 \\ 3 & k^2 - 1 \end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A_k &= (k^2 - 1) - 3(k - 1) \\[6pt]
&= (k - 1)(k + 1) - 3(k - 1) \\[6pt]
&= (k - 1)(k + 1 - 3) \\[6pt]
&= (k - 1)(k - 2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\det A_k = 0 \iff k = 1 \ \text{ or } \ k = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two subtractions make the first column zero below the entry $a_{11} = 1$ without changing the determinant, and the expansion along the first column leaves a determinant of order $2$. In this determinant it is convenient to factor $k^2 - 1 = (k - 1)(k + 1)$ before carrying out the products, because in this way the common factor $k - 1$ is collected at once. The determinant vanishes for $k = 1$ and for $k = 2$, so $A_k$ is **invertible** for $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{1, 2\}$. For $k = 1$ the third column coincides with the first, and for $k = 2$ it coincides with the second, in agreement with the fact that a matrix with two equal columns has zero determinant.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine for which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A_k = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & k & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & k \\ k & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & k & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is invertible.

$$
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & k & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & k \\ k & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & k & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix}
\xrightarrow{\substack{R_3 \to R_3 - k R_1 \\ R_4 \to R_4 - k R_2}}
\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & k & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & k \\ 0 & 0 & 1 - k^2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 - k^2 \end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\det A_k = (1 - k^2)^2 = (1 - k)^2 (1 + k)^2
$$
$$
\det A_k = 0 \iff k = 1 \ \text{ or } \ k = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The operations $R_3 \to R_3 - k R_1$ and $R_4 \to R_4 - k R_2$ contain the parameter, but they are of the form $R_i \to R_i + h R_j$ with $i \neq j$, since the row being modified, $R_3$ or $R_4$, appears with coefficient $1$. By the **effect of elementary operations** the determinant therefore does not change for *any* value of $k$, including those that make the coefficient $h = -k$ vanish. The resulting matrix is upper triangular and the determinant is the product of the diagonal entries, $(1 - k^2)^2$. The determinant vanishes for $k = \pm 1$, therefore $A_k$ is **invertible** for $k \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{-1, 1\}$.

</div>
