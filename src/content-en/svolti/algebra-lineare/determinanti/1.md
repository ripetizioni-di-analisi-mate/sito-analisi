## Exercise 1

Compute the determinants
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 4 & -3 \\ 2 & 5 \end{vmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 3 \\[4pt] \frac{1}{3} & 4 \end{vmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix} a & a + 1 \\ a - 1 & a \end{vmatrix}
\quad (a \in \mathbb{R}) .
$$

$$
\begin{vmatrix} 4 & -3 \\ 2 & 5 \end{vmatrix} = 4 \cdot 5 - (-3) \cdot 2 = 20 + 6 = 26
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 3 \\[4pt] \frac{1}{3} & 4 \end{vmatrix}
= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 - 3 \cdot \frac{1}{3} = 2 - 1 = 1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix} a & a + 1 \\ a - 1 & a \end{vmatrix}
&= a \cdot a - (a + 1)(a - 1) \\[6pt]
&= a^2 - (a^2 - 1) \\[6pt]
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a matrix of order $2$ the determinant is the product of the main diagonal minus the product of the antidiagonal. In the first case the second product is $(-3) \cdot 2 = -6$, and subtracting it is the same as adding $6$, so the determinant is $26$. In the second case the products are $\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 4 = 2$ and $3 \cdot \dfrac{1}{3} = 1$, so the determinant is $1$. In the third case the product of the antidiagonal is the special product $(a + 1)(a - 1) = a^2 - 1$; since the $a^2$ terms cancel, the determinant equals $1$ and is **independent of** $a$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute with Sarrus' rule the determinant of
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|cc}
2 & 1 & 3 & 2 & 1 \\
0 & 4 & 1 & 0 & 4 \\
1 & 2 & 5 & 1 & 2
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
\text{diagonals to the right:} \quad 2 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 + 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 0 \cdot 2 = 40 + 1 + 0 = 41
$$
$$
\text{diagonals to the left:} \quad 3 \cdot 4 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot 0 \cdot 5 = 12 + 4 + 0 = 16
$$
$$
\det A = 41 - 16 = 25
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two columns are copied to the right of the matrix, so that each diagonal contains three entries. The three diagonals going down to the right start from $a_{11} = 2$, $a_{12} = 1$, $a_{13} = 3$ and give the products $40$, $1$ and $0$, with sum $41$. The three diagonals going down to the left start from $a_{13} = 3$ and from the two copied columns, and give the products $12$, $4$ and $0$, with sum $16$. The determinant is the difference between the two sums, hence $\det A = 25$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute with Sarrus' rule the determinant of
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 \\ -3 & 4 & 2 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|cc}
1 & -2 & 3 & 1 & -2 \\
2 & 1 & -1 & 2 & 1 \\
-3 & 4 & 2 & -3 & 4
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{to the right:} \quad & 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 + (-2) \cdot (-1) \cdot (-3) + 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 4 \\[4pt]
&= 2 - 6 + 24 = 20
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{to the left:} \quad & 3 \cdot 1 \cdot (-3) + 1 \cdot (-1) \cdot 4 + (-2) \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \\[4pt]
&= -9 - 4 - 8 = -21
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\det A = 20 - (-21) = 41
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scheme is the same as in the previous exercise; with negative numbers it is convenient to determine the sign of each product before adding. The product $(-2) \cdot (-1) \cdot (-3)$ has three negative factors, so it is negative and equals $-6$. The sum of the diagonals to the right is $20$, that of the diagonals to the left is $-21$. Since a negative number is subtracted, the result is $20 + 21$, that is, $\det A = 41$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Compute the area of the triangle with vertices $P = (1, 1)$, $Q = (5, 2)$, $R = (2, 4)$.

$$
u = Q - P = (4, 1),
\qquad
v = R - P = (1, 3)
$$
$$
\det \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = 12 - 1 = 11
$$
$$
\text{Area}(PQR) = \frac{1}{2}\, |11| = \frac{11}{2}
$$

::grafico{area="1+3*(x-1);1+(x-1)/4;1;2|4-2*(x-2)/3;1+(x-1)/4;2;5" vettori="1,1,5,2,u;1,1,2,4,v" segmenti="2,4,5,2" punti="1,1,pieno,P;5,2,pieno,Q;2,4,pieno,R" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6 ymin=-0.5 ymax=5 uguale=1 titolo="The triangle $PQR$ has two sides along the vectors $u = (4, 1)$ and $v = (1, 3)$ applied at $P$: its area is $\dfrac{11}{2}$, half the area $11$ of the parallelogram with sides $u$ and $v$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangle has two sides leaving from $P$, represented by the vectors $u = Q - P$ and $v = R - P$. The parallelogram built on $u$ and $v$ has area equal to the absolute value of the determinant of the matrix with rows $u$ and $v$, that is, $11$. The diagonal $QR$ divides the parallelogram into two congruent triangles, so the **area of the triangle** $PQR$ is $\dfrac{11}{2}$. The determinant is positive because the shortest rotation taking $u$ to $v$ is counterclockwise; the area, however, is computed with the absolute value, and the order of the vectors does not change the result.

</div>
