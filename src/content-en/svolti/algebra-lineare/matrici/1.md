## Exercise 1

Given the matrices
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & -1 & 0 \\
1 & 3 & -2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 4 & -3 \\
0 & -2 & 5
\end{pmatrix},
$$
compute $3A - 2B$.

$$
3A =
\begin{pmatrix}
6 & -3 & 0 \\
3 & 9 & -6
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
-2B =
\begin{pmatrix}
-2 & -8 & 6 \\
0 & 4 & -10
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
3A - 2B =
\begin{pmatrix}
6 - 2 & -3 - 8 & 0 + 6 \\
3 + 0 & 9 + 4 & -6 - 10
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
4 & -11 & 6 \\
3 & 13 & -16
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrices $A$ and $B$ both have two rows and three columns, so the **linear combination** is defined and is again a $2 \times 3$ matrix. Multiplication by a scalar multiplies *every* entry, therefore one first computes $3A$ and $-2B$. The sum is then performed entry by entry, adding the entries in the same position; for example the entry in position $(2, 3)$ is $3 \cdot (-2) + (-2) \cdot 5 = -16$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given the matrices
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & -2 \\
3 & 1 & 4
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
0 & -1 \\
2 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
find the matrix $X$ such that $2X - A^T = 3B$.

$$
2X = 3B + A^T
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
X = \frac{1}{2} \left( 3B + A^T \right)
$$
$$
3B =
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & 3 \\
0 & -3 \\
6 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A^T =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 3 \\
0 & 1 \\
-2 & 4
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
3B + A^T =
\begin{pmatrix}
4 & 6 \\
0 & -2 \\
4 & 4
\end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
X =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 3 \\
0 & -1 \\
2 & 2
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix $A$ is $2 \times 3$, so $A^T$ is $3 \times 2$, like $B$; therefore the equation makes sense and the unknown $X$ is a $3 \times 2$ matrix. By the **properties of the sum and of multiplication by a scalar** the equation is solved like a first-degree numerical equation: one adds $A^T$ to both sides and multiplies by $\dfrac{1}{2}$. In the transpose the first row $(1, 0, -2)$ of $A$ becomes the first column of $A^T$ and the second row $(3, 1, 4)$ becomes the second column. The result is checked by substitution: $2X - A^T$ has first row $(4 - 1, 6 - 3) = (3, 3)$, which is the first row of $3B$, and likewise for the other rows.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 4 & -2 \\
0 & 3 & 6 \\
2 & -4 & 5
\end{pmatrix}
$$
as the sum of a symmetric matrix $S$ and a skew-symmetric matrix $K$.

$$
S = \frac{1}{2} \left( A + A^T \right),
\qquad
K = \frac{1}{2} \left( A - A^T \right)
$$
$$
A^T =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 2 \\
4 & 3 & -4 \\
-2 & 6 & 5
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
A + A^T =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 4 & 0 \\
4 & 6 & 2 \\
0 & 2 & 10
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A - A^T =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 4 & -4 \\
-4 & 0 & 10 \\
4 & -10 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
S =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 0 \\
2 & 3 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 5
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
K =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 2 & -2 \\
-2 & 0 & 5 \\
2 & -5 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the **properties of the transpose** the matrix $A + A^T$ is symmetric, since $(A + A^T)^T = A^T + A$, and the matrix $A - A^T$ is skew-symmetric, since $(A - A^T)^T = A^T - A = -(A - A^T)$; multiplying by $\dfrac{1}{2}$ these properties remain true. Moreover $S + K = \dfrac{1}{2} A + \dfrac{1}{2} A^T + \dfrac{1}{2} A - \dfrac{1}{2} A^T = A$, therefore $S$ and $K$ are the required matrices. In the computation the transpose is obtained by writing the rows of $A$ as columns. The result is checked in two ways: $S$ has equal entries in mirror positions with respect to the diagonal and $K$ has zero diagonal and opposite entries in mirror positions; moreover the sum $S + K$ gives back $A$, for example $s_{23} + k_{23} = 1 + 5 = 6 = a_{23}$.

</div>
