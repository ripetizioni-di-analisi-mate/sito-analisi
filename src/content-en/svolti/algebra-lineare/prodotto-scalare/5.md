## Exercise 1

Verify that the matrix
$$
A = \frac{1}{3}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 2 \\
2 & 1 & -2 \\
2 & -2 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
is orthogonal, and compute its inverse and its determinant.

$$
c_1 = \frac{1}{3}\,(1, 2, 2),
\qquad
c_2 = \frac{1}{3}\,(2, 1, -2),
\qquad
c_3 = \frac{1}{3}\,(2, -2, 1)
$$
$$
\|c_1\|^2 = \frac{1 + 4 + 4}{9} = 1,
\qquad
\|c_2\|^2 = \frac{4 + 1 + 4}{9} = 1,
\qquad
\|c_3\|^2 = \frac{4 + 4 + 1}{9} = 1
$$
$$
\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle = \frac{2 + 2 - 4}{9} = 0,
\qquad
\langle c_1, c_3 \rangle = \frac{2 - 4 + 2}{9} = 0,
\qquad
\langle c_2, c_3 \rangle = \frac{4 - 2 - 2}{9} = 0
$$
$$
A^{-1} = A^T = A
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A &= \frac{1}{27}
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 2 & 2 \\
2 & 1 & -2 \\
2 & -2 & 1
\end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{27} \left[ 1 \cdot (1 - 4) - 2 \cdot (2 + 4) + 2 \cdot (-4 - 2) \right] \\[6pt]
&= \frac{-3 - 12 - 12}{27} = -1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A matrix is orthogonal if and only if its columns form an orthonormal basis, therefore one checks the norms and the scalar products between the columns: the three norms equal $1$ and the three scalar products are zero, so $A$ is **orthogonal**. For an orthogonal matrix the inverse is the transpose, and $A$ is symmetric, therefore $A^{-1} = A^T = A$. The determinant is computed by expanding along the first row, after factoring out $\dfrac{1}{3}$ from each of the three rows, that is, the factor $\dfrac{1}{27}$. One obtains $\det A = -1$, in agreement with the fact that an orthogonal matrix has determinant $\pm 1$; the negative sign indicates that $A$ reverses orientation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ so that the matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{3}{5} & a \\[10pt]
\dfrac{4}{5} & b
\end{pmatrix}
$$
is orthogonal, and compute the determinant of each matrix obtained.

$$
\begin{cases}
a^2 + b^2 = 1 \\[4pt]
\dfrac{3}{5}\, a + \dfrac{4}{5}\, b = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
b = -\dfrac{3}{4}\, a \\[10pt]
\dfrac{25}{16}\, a^2 = 1
\end{cases}
$$
$$
(a, b) = \left( -\frac{4}{5}, \frac{3}{5} \right)
\qquad \text{or} \qquad
(a, b) = \left( \frac{4}{5}, -\frac{3}{5} \right)
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
\dfrac{3}{5} & -\dfrac{4}{5} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{4}{5} & \dfrac{3}{5}
\end{vmatrix}
= \frac{9}{25} + \frac{16}{25} = 1,
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix}
\dfrac{3}{5} & \dfrac{4}{5} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{4}{5} & -\dfrac{3}{5}
\end{vmatrix}
= -\frac{9}{25} - \frac{16}{25} = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first column is already a unit vector, since $\dfrac{9}{25} + \dfrac{16}{25} = 1$. The matrix is orthogonal if and only if the second column $(a, b)$ is a unit vector orthogonal to the first, and the two conditions must be satisfied together, therefore they form a system. From the second equation one obtains $b$ and, substituting into the first, one gets $a^2 = \dfrac{16}{25}$, that is, $a = \pm \dfrac{4}{5}$. There are **two** solutions. The first matrix has determinant $1$ and is a **rotation**; the second has determinant $-1$ and is a reflection across a line through the origin.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the third column $c_3$ so that the matrix
$$
Q =
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \ast \\[10pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \ast \\[10pt]
0 & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \ast
\end{pmatrix}
$$
is orthogonal with $\det Q = 1$.

$$
\langle c_1, c_2 \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} + 0 = 0,
\qquad
\|c_1\| = \|c_2\| = 1
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y = 0 \\[4pt]
x - y + z = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
y = -x \\[4pt]
z = -2x
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_3 = \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,(1, -1, -2)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \\[10pt]
0 & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}}
\end{pmatrix}
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{3}\,\sqrt{6}}
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & -1 & -1 \\
0 & 1 & -2
\end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= \frac{1}{6}\, \left[ 1 \cdot (2 + 1) - 1 \cdot (-2 - 0) + 1 \cdot (1 - 0) \right] \\[6pt]
&= \frac{6}{6} = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
c_3 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,(1, -1, -2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first two columns are orthogonal unit vectors, therefore $Q$ is orthogonal if and only if $c_3$ is a unit vector orthogonal to both. The vectors orthogonal to $c_1$ and to $c_2$ are the same as those orthogonal to $(1, 1, 0)$ and to $(1, -1, 1)$, because multiplying a vector by a nonzero number does not change orthogonality; one therefore solves the system with these two rows, which gives the line spanned by $(1, -1, -2)$. On this line there are two unit vectors, and the two corresponding determinants are opposite, since changing the sign of a column changes the sign of the determinant. The computation with the positive sign gives $\det Q = 1$, therefore the required column is the one with the **positive sign**, $c_3 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,(1, -1, -2)$; with the negative sign one would obtain $\det Q = -1$.

</div>
