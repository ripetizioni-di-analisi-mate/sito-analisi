## Exercise 1

Orthogonally diagonalize the symmetric matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
2 & -2
\end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
p_A(\lambda) =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 - \lambda & 2 \\
2 & -2 - \lambda
\end{vmatrix}
= (1 - \lambda)(-2 - \lambda) - 4 = \lambda^2 + \lambda - 6 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda + 3)
$$
$$
\lambda = 2 : \quad
A - 2I =
\begin{pmatrix}
-1 & 2 \\
2 & -4
\end{pmatrix}
\ \Longrightarrow\ x = 2y,
\qquad V_2 = \langle (2, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
\lambda = -3 : \quad
A + 3I =
\begin{pmatrix}
4 & 2 \\
2 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\ \Longrightarrow\ y = -2x,
\qquad V_{-3} = \langle (1, -2) \rangle
$$
$$
P = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 1 \\
1 & -2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P^T A P = D =
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & -3
\end{pmatrix}
$$

::grafico{f="x/2;-2*x" vettori="0,0,2,1;0,0,4,2;0,0,1,-2,v₂;0,0,-3,6,Av₂" etichette="0.6,0.9,v₁;2.9,2,Av₁" xmin=-3.6 xmax=4.6 ymin=-2.6 ymax=6.4 uguale=1 titolo="The two orthogonal eigenvector directions of $A$: along the line $y = \dfrac{x}{2}$ the matrix multiplies by $2$, along the line $y = -2x$ it multiplies by $-3$ and reverses direction"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix is symmetric, therefore by the **spectral theorem** it is diagonalizable with an orthogonal matrix. The characteristic polynomial has the roots $2$ and $-3$. In each of the two homogeneous systems the rows are proportional, so a single equation suffices, and each eigenspace is a line. The two eigenvectors $(2, 1)$ and $(1, -2)$ are orthogonal, since $2 - 2 = 0$, as predicted by the property of eigenvectors associated with distinct eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix. Both have norm $\sqrt{5}$; after normalization they become the columns of $P$, and on the diagonal of $D$ the eigenvalues appear in the same order as the columns. As a check, $A\,(2, 1) = (4, 2) = 2\,(2, 1)$ and $A\,(1, -2) = (-3, 6) = -3\,(1, -2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Orthogonally diagonalize the matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & -1 & 0 \\
-1 & 2 & -1 \\
0 & -1 & 1
\end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\lambda) &= (1 - \lambda) \left[ (2 - \lambda)(1 - \lambda) - 1 \right] + 1 \cdot \left[ -(1 - \lambda) \right] \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda) \left[ (2 - \lambda)(1 - \lambda) - 2 \right] \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda) (\lambda^2 - 3\lambda) \\[6pt]
&= -\lambda (\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lambda = 0 : \quad
\begin{cases}
x_1 - x_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
-x_2 + x_3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_0 = \langle (1, 1, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
\lambda = 1 : \quad
\begin{cases}
-x_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
-x_1 + x_2 - x_3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_1 = \langle (1, 0, -1) \rangle
$$
$$
\lambda = 3 : \quad
\begin{cases}
-2x_1 - x_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
-x_2 - 2x_3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_3 = \langle (1, -2, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
P =
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & 0 & -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P^T A P = D =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of $A - \lambda I$ is expanded along the first row; the two terms have the common factor $1 - \lambda$, which is factored out, and the remaining factor factors as $\lambda(\lambda - 3)$. The eigenvalues $0$, $1$, $3$ are distinct, therefore each eigenspace has dimension $1$. In each system only two independent equations are written, because the matrix $A - \lambda I$ has rank $2$; for $\lambda = 3$, for example, one obtains $x_2 = -2x_1$ and $x_2 = -2x_3$, therefore $x_1 = x_3$ and the eigenvector $(1, -2, 1)$. The three eigenvectors are pairwise orthogonal with no need for Gram-Schmidt, because they are associated with distinct eigenvalues. The norms are $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{6}$, and the corresponding unit vectors are the columns of the **orthogonal matrix** $P$, with $P^T A P = D$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Orthogonally diagonalize the matrix
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & -1 & 0 \\
-1 & 2 & -1 \\
0 & -1 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
$$
by computing the last eigenspace as the orthogonal complement of the first two.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\lambda) &= (3 - \lambda) \left[ (2 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda) - 1 \right] + 1 \cdot \left[ -(3 - \lambda) \right] \\[6pt]
&= (3 - \lambda) \left[ (2 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda) - 2 \right] \\[6pt]
&= (3 - \lambda)(\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 4) \\[6pt]
&= -(\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 3)(\lambda - 4)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lambda = 1 : \quad
\begin{cases}
2x_1 - x_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
-x_2 + 2x_3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_1 = \langle (1, 2, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
\lambda = 3 : \quad
\begin{cases}
-x_2 = 0 \\[4pt]
-x_1 - x_2 - x_3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_3 = \langle (1, 0, -1) \rangle
$$
$$
V_4 = (V_1 \oplus V_3)^\perp : \quad
\begin{cases}
x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3 = 0 \\[4pt]
x_1 - x_3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_4 = \langle (1, -1, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
A
\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\ -1 \\ 1
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
3 + 1 \\ -1 - 2 - 1 \\ 1 + 3
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
4 \\ -4 \\ 4
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
P =
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}} & 0 & -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \\[10pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}} & -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P^T A P = D =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 3 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 4
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic polynomial is factored by factoring out $3 - \lambda$, as in the previous exercise, and it gives three distinct eigenvalues. By the **spectral theorem** $\mathbb{R}^3$ is the direct sum of the eigenspaces, which are pairwise orthogonal; therefore the eigenspace $V_4$ is the orthogonal complement of $V_1 \oplus V_3$ and is obtained by imposing orthogonality to the two eigenvectors already found, without writing the system $(A - 4I)x = 0$. The check $A\,(1, -1, 1) = 4\,(1, -1, 1)$ confirms the result. The norms of the eigenvectors are $\sqrt{6}$, $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$, and the unit vectors form the columns of the **orthogonal matrix** $P$.

</div>
