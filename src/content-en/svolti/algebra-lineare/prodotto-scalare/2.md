## Exercise 1

Compute the orthogonal projection of $v = (4, 3)$ onto the vector $u = (1, 2)$ and write $v$ as the sum of a vector parallel to $u$ and a vector orthogonal to $u$.

$$
\langle v, u \rangle = 4 + 6 = 10,
\qquad
\langle u, u \rangle = 1 + 4 = 5
$$
$$
\operatorname{pr}_u(v) = \frac{10}{5}\,(1, 2) = (2, 4),
\qquad
v - \operatorname{pr}_u(v) = (4, 3) - (2, 4) = (2, -1)
$$
$$
(4, 3) = (2, 4) + (2, -1),
\qquad
\langle (2, -1), (1, 2) \rangle = 2 - 2 = 0
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,1,2,u;0,0,4,3,v;0,0,2,4,p;2,4,4,3,v − p" segmenti="-0.4,-0.8,2.4,4.8" xmin=-0.8 xmax=4.6 ymin=-0.8 ymax=4.8 uguale=1 titolo="The projection $p = (2, 4)$ of $v = (4, 3)$ onto the line spanned by $u = (1, 2)$ and the orthogonal component $v - p = (2, -1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The orthogonal projection of $v$ onto $u$ is the multiple $\dfrac{\langle v, u \rangle}{\langle u, u \rangle}\, u$, and the coefficient equals $2$. Subtracting the projection from $v$ one obtains the orthogonal component $(2, -1)$; its scalar product with $u$ is zero, therefore the decomposition $v = (2, 4) + (2, -1)$ has the **first summand parallel** to $u$ and the **second orthogonal** to $u$, as required.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Decompose $v = (2, 3, 1)$ into the sum of a vector parallel to $u = (1, 1, 1)$ and a vector orthogonal to $u$.

$$
\langle v, u \rangle = 2 + 3 + 1 = 6,
\qquad
\langle u, u \rangle = 3
$$
$$
\operatorname{pr}_u(v) = \frac{6}{3}\,(1, 1, 1) = (2, 2, 2),
\qquad
v - \operatorname{pr}_u(v) = (0, 1, -1)
$$
$$
(2, 3, 1) = (2, 2, 2) + (0, 1, -1),
\qquad
\langle (0, 1, -1), (1, 1, 1) \rangle = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The part parallel to $u$ is the **orthogonal projection** of $v$ onto $u$, that is, $2u = (2, 2, 2)$. The orthogonal part is the difference $v - \operatorname{pr}_u(v) = (0, 1, -1)$, and checking that the scalar product is zero confirms that it is orthogonal to $u$. The decomposition is **unique**, because the projection is the only multiple of $u$ that leaves a difference orthogonal to $u$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $W = \langle (1, 1, 1), (1, -1, 0) \rangle \subseteq \mathbb{R}^3$. Compute the orthogonal projection of $v = (4, 0, -1)$ onto $W$ and the distance of $v$ from $W$.

$$
w_1 = (1, 1, 1),
\qquad
w_2 = (1, -1, 0),
\qquad
\langle w_1, w_2 \rangle = 1 - 1 + 0 = 0
$$
$$
\langle v, w_1 \rangle = 4 + 0 - 1 = 3,
\qquad
\langle w_1, w_1 \rangle = 3,
\qquad
\langle v, w_2 \rangle = 4,
\qquad
\langle w_2, w_2 \rangle = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_W(v) &= \frac{3}{3}\,(1, 1, 1) + \frac{4}{2}\,(1, -1, 0) \\[6pt]
&= (1, 1, 1) + (2, -2, 0) \\[6pt]
&= (3, -1, 1)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
v - P_W(v) = (1, 1, -2),
\qquad
d(v, W) = \|(1, 1, -2)\| = \sqrt{1 + 1 + 4} = \sqrt{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula for the projection as a sum of projections onto the generators holds only for an orthogonal basis, therefore the first step checks that $w_1$ and $w_2$ are orthogonal; their scalar product is zero, so the **theorem on orthogonal projection onto a subspace** applies directly. One obtains $P_W(v) = (3, -1, 1)$. As a check, the difference $(1, 1, -2)$ has zero scalar product both with $w_1$, since $1 + 1 - 2 = 0$, and with $w_2$, since $1 - 1 + 0 = 0$. The projection is the point of $W$ closest to $v$, therefore the **distance** of $v$ from $W$ is the norm of the difference and equals $\sqrt{6}$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Let $W = \{ (x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 : x + 2y + 2z = 0 \}$. Compute the orthogonal projection of $v = (3, 1, 2)$ onto $W$ and the distance of $v$ from $W$.

$$
W^\perp = \langle n \rangle,
\qquad
n = (1, 2, 2),
\qquad
\langle n, n \rangle = 9
$$
$$
\langle v, n \rangle = 3 + 2 + 4 = 9
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{pr}_n(v) = \frac{9}{9}\,(1, 2, 2) = (1, 2, 2)
$$
$$
P_W(v) = v - \operatorname{pr}_n(v) = (3, 1, 2) - (1, 2, 2) = (2, -1, 0)
$$
$$
d(v, W) = \|\operatorname{pr}_n(v)\| = \|(1, 2, 2)\| = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The plane $W$ is given in Cartesian form, therefore its orthogonal complement is spanned by the row of coefficients, that is, by the **normal vector** $n = (1, 2, 2)$. Since $\mathbb{R}^3 = W \oplus W^\perp$, the vector $v$ can be written uniquely as the sum of its projection onto $W$ and its projection onto $W^\perp$; so $P_W(v) = v - \operatorname{pr}_n(v)$. In this way the computation of an orthogonal basis of the plane is avoided. The result $(2, -1, 0)$ belongs to $W$, since $2 - 2 + 0 = 0$. The distance of $v$ from $W$ is the norm of $v - P_W(v) = \operatorname{pr}_n(v)$, and equals $3$.

</div>
