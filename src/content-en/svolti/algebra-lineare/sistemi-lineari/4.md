## Exercise 1

Solve with Cramer's rule the system
$$
\begin{cases}
3x + 2y = 1 \\[4pt]
4x + 5y = -1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
3 & 2 \\
4 & 5
\end{vmatrix}
= 15 - 8 = 7 \neq 0
$$
$$
x =
\frac{
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
-1 & 5
\end{vmatrix}
}{7}
= \frac{5 + 2}{7} = 1,
\qquad
y =
\frac{
\begin{vmatrix}
3 & 1 \\
4 & -1
\end{vmatrix}
}{7}
= \frac{-3 - 4}{7} = -1
$$
$$
(x, y) = (1, -1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has two equations in two unknowns and $\det A = 7 \neq 0$, so the hypothesis of **Cramer's rule** is satisfied and the solution is unique. The numerator of $x$ is the determinant of the matrix obtained by replacing the first column of $A$ with the column of constant terms $(1, -1)$; the numerator of $y$ is obtained by replacing the second column. The solution is $(1, -1)$, and the check gives $3 - 2 = 1$ and $4 - 5 = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve with Cramer's rule the system
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + y - z = 3 \\[4pt]
x - y + 2z = 1 \\[4pt]
3x + 2y + z = 8
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
2 & 1 & -1 \\
1 & -1 & 2 \\
3 & 2 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
&= 2(-1 - 4) - 1(1 - 6) - 1(2 + 3) \\[6pt]
&= -10 + 5 - 5 = -10
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A_1 =
\begin{vmatrix}
3 & 1 & -1 \\
1 & -1 & 2 \\
8 & 2 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
&= 3(-1 - 4) - 1(1 - 16) - 1(2 + 8) \\[6pt]
&= -15 + 15 - 10 = -10
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A_2 =
\begin{vmatrix}
2 & 3 & -1 \\
1 & 1 & 2 \\
3 & 8 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
&= 2(1 - 16) - 3(1 - 6) - 1(8 - 3) \\[6pt]
&= -30 + 15 - 5 = -20
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A_3 =
\begin{vmatrix}
2 & 1 & 3 \\
1 & -1 & 1 \\
3 & 2 & 8
\end{vmatrix}
&= 2(-8 - 2) - 1(8 - 3) + 3(2 + 3) \\[6pt]
&= -20 - 5 + 15 = -10
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = \frac{-10}{-10} = 1,
\qquad
y = \frac{-20}{-10} = 2,
\qquad
z = \frac{-10}{-10} = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of the coefficient matrix, expanded along the first row, equals $-10$; since it is nonzero, **Cramer's rule** applies and the system has exactly one solution. The matrices $A_1$, $A_2$, $A_3$ are obtained from $A$ by replacing respectively the first, the second and the third column with the column of constant terms $(3, 1, 8)$, and their determinants are also expanded along the first row. Dividing each by $\det A = -10$ gives the **unique solution** $(1, 2, 1)$. The check in the second equation gives $1 - 2 + 2 = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the value of the unknown $z$ in the solution of the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
2x - y + 3z = 0 \\[4pt]
x + y - 2z = 4
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 2 & 1 \\
2 & -1 & 3 \\
1 & 1 & -2
\end{vmatrix}
&= 1(2 - 3) - 2(-4 - 3) + 1(2 + 1) \\[6pt]
&= -1 + 14 + 3 = 16
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A_3 =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 2 & 1 \\
2 & -1 & 0 \\
1 & 1 & 4
\end{vmatrix}
&= 1(-4 - 0) - 2(8 - 0) + 1(2 + 1) \\[6pt]
&= -4 - 16 + 3 = -17
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
z = \frac{\det A_3}{\det A} = -\frac{17}{16}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $\det A = 16 \neq 0$, the system has exactly one solution and by **Cramer's rule** each unknown is computed separately from the others. For $z$ only the matrix $A_3$ is needed, obtained by replacing the third column of $A$ with the constant terms $(1, 0, 4)$, so the computation requires two determinants instead of four. The ratio gives $z = -\dfrac{17}{16}$. With Gaussian elimination one would have had to reduce the whole augmented matrix even to obtain a single unknown; for completeness, the other two components of the solution are $x = \dfrac{27}{16}$ and $y = \dfrac{3}{16}$.

</div>
