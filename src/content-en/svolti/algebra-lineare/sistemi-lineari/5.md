## Exercise 1

Find the solutions of the homogeneous system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y - z = 0 \\[4pt]
2x - y + 3z = 0 \\[4pt]
3x + y + 2z = 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\
2 & -1 & 3 & 0 \\
3 & 1 & 2 & 0
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & -5 & 5 & 0 \\
0 & -5 & 5 & 0
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 - R_2}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & -5 & 5 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = 2 < 3,
\qquad
z = t, \quad y = t, \quad x = -2t + t = -t
$$
$$
S_0 = \{ t\,(-1, 1, 1) : t \in \mathbb{R} \} = \langle (-1, 1, 1) \rangle
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A homogeneous system is always consistent, so the question is whether there exist solutions other than the trivial one. The reduction to echelon form shows two pivots, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2 < 3$ and by the **theorem on nontrivial solutions** the solutions are $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^1$; equivalently, $\det A = 0$ because the third equation is the sum of the first two. The unknown $z$ has no pivot and we set $z = t$. The second equation, $-5y + 5z = 0$, gives $y = t$, and the first gives $x = -2y + z = -t$. The solution set is the **line through the origin** spanned by the vector $(-1, 1, 1)$, which is a basis of it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find a basis of the solution space of the homogeneous system in the unknowns $x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4$
$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 + x_2 - x_3 + 2x_4 = 0 \\[4pt]
x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3 + x_4 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left(\begin{array}{cccc|c}
1 & 1 & -1 & 2 & 0 \\
1 & 2 & 1 & 1 & 0
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_2 \to R_2 - R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{cccc|c}
1 & 1 & -1 & 2 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 2 & -1 & 0
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
x_3 = s, \quad x_4 = t, \qquad
x_2 = -2s + t,
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1 &= -x_2 + x_3 - 2x_4 \\[6pt]
&= 2s - t + s - 2t \\[6pt]
&= 3s - 3t
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) = s\,(3, -2, 1, 0) + t\,(-3, 1, 0, 1)
$$
$$
S_0 = \langle (3, -2, 1, 0), \ (-3, 1, 0, 1) \rangle,
\qquad \dim S_0 = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The system has more unknowns than equations, so $\operatorname{rk} A \leq 2 < 4$ and nontrivial solutions *certainly* exist. The reduction gives two pivots, in the columns of $x_1$ and $x_2$, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ and the free unknowns are $x_3 = s$ and $x_4 = t$. From the second equation one obtains $x_2$, then from the first $x_1$. Collecting $s$ and $t$, every solution is a linear combination of the two vectors $(3, -2, 1, 0)$ and $(-3, 1, 0, 1)$, which are obtained by setting respectively $(s, t) = (1, 0)$ and $(s, t) = (0, 1)$. The two vectors are independent, because $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ appear in the last two components, so they form a **basis** of $S_0$ and $\dim S_0 = 4 - 2 = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the solutions of the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 + x_2 - x_3 + 2x_4 = 2 \\[4pt]
x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3 + x_4 = 3
\end{cases}
$$
and write them as a particular solution plus solutions of the associated homogeneous system.

$$
x_3 = x_4 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x_1 + x_2 = 2 \\[4pt]
x_1 + 2x_2 = 3
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x_2 = 1, \quad x_1 = 1
$$
$$
x_0 = (1, 1, 0, 0)
$$
$$
S = x_0 + S_0 = \{ (1, 1, 0, 0) + s\,(3, -2, 1, 0) + t\,(-3, 1, 0, 1) : s, t \in \mathbb{R} \}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient matrix is the same as in the previous exercise, so the associated homogeneous system has the solutions $S_0 = \langle (3, -2, 1, 0), (-3, 1, 0, 1) \rangle$. The rank of $A$ is $2$, equal to the number of rows, therefore the augmented matrix also has rank $2$ and the system is consistent. By the **theorem on the structure of the solutions** it suffices to find *one* solution: setting $x_3 = x_4 = 0$ gives a $2 \times 2$ system with determinant $1$, whose solution gives the particular solution $x_0 = (1, 1, 0, 0)$. Adding the solutions of the homogeneous system to $x_0$ gives *all* the solutions. The check of $x_0$ gives $1 + 1 = 2$ and $1 + 2 = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the homogeneous system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
x + ky + 2z = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + kz = 0
\end{cases}
$$
has nontrivial solutions and, for those values, find them.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & k & 2 \\
1 & 2 & k
\end{vmatrix}
&= (k^2 - 4) - (k - 2) + (2 - k) \\[6pt]
&= k^2 - 2k = k(k - 2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{for } k = 0
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 2z = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z = t, \quad x = -2t, \quad y = t
$$
$$
\text{for } k = 2
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + 2z = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z = t, \quad y = -t, \quad x = 0
$$
$$
S_0 =
\begin{cases}
\{ (0, 0, 0) \} & \text{if } k \neq 0, \ k \neq 2 \\[4pt]
\langle (-2, 1, 1) \rangle & \text{if } k = 0 \\[4pt]
\langle (0, -1, 1) \rangle & \text{if } k = 2
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix is square, so by the **theorem on nontrivial solutions** there exist solutions other than the trivial one if and only if $\det A = 0$. The expansion along the first row gives $\det A = k(k - 2)$, which vanishes for $k = 0$ and for $k = 2$; for every other value the only solution is $(0, 0, 0)$. For $k = 0$ the third equation, $x + 2y = 0$, is the difference between twice the first and the second, so the first two are kept: from the second $x = -2z$, and from the first $y = -x - z = z$. For $k = 2$ the second and third equations coincide; subtracting the first from the second gives $y + z = 0$, and the first then gives $x = 0$. In both cases $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ and the solutions form a **line through the origin**.

</div>
