## Exercise 1

Discuss, as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the system
$$
\begin{cases}
kx + y = 2 \\[4pt]
x + ky = 2
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
k & 1 \\
1 & k
\end{vmatrix}
= k^2 - 1 = (k - 1)(k + 1)
$$
$$
\text{for } k \neq \pm 1
\qquad
x = \frac{
\begin{vmatrix}
2 & 1 \\
2 & k
\end{vmatrix}
}{k^2 - 1}
= \frac{2(k - 1)}{(k - 1)(k + 1)} = \frac{2}{k + 1},
\qquad
y = \frac{
\begin{vmatrix}
k & 2 \\
1 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
}{k^2 - 1}
= \frac{2}{k + 1}
$$
$$
\text{for } k = 1
\qquad
\begin{cases}
x + y = 2 \\[4pt]
x + y = 2
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x, y) = (2 - t, \ t), \quad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
\text{for } k = -1
\qquad
\begin{cases}
-x + y = 2 \\[4pt]
x - y = 2
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{adding gives } 0 = 4
$$

::grafico{f="x+2;x-2" etichette="-3.7,0.6,−x + y = 2;1.3,-2.3,x − y = 2" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-4 ymax=4 uguale=1 titolo="For $k = -1$ the lines $-x + y = 2$ and $x - y = 2$ are parallel and distinct, and the system is inconsistent"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant vanishes for $k = 1$ and for $k = -1$. For every other value $\det A \neq 0$, so by **Cramer's rule** the system is determined; in the computation of $x$ the factor $k - 1$ cancels because $k \neq 1$, and the same happens for $y$, given that $\begin{vmatrix} k & 2 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 2k - 2$. For $k = 1$ the two equations coincide, $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 1$ and the solutions are $\infty^{2 - 1} = \infty^1$: geometrically the two lines coincide. For $k = -1$ the sum of the equations gives $0 = 4$, so the system is **inconsistent**; in terms of rank, $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ whereas $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$, because the minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 4$ is nonzero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Discuss, as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + kz = 1 \\[4pt]
x + ky + z = 1 \\[4pt]
kx + y + z = 1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & k \\
1 & k & 1 \\
k & 1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
&= (k - 1) - (1 - k) + k(1 - k^2) \\[6pt]
&= (k - 1)(2 - k - k^2) \\[6pt]
&= -(k - 1)^2 (k + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{for } k \neq 1, \ k \neq -2
\qquad
\begin{cases}
(k - 1)(z - y) = 0 \\[4pt]
(k - 1)(y - x) = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = y = z,
\qquad
(k + 2)\, x = 1
$$
$$
(x, y, z) = \left( \frac{1}{k + 2}, \ \frac{1}{k + 2}, \ \frac{1}{k + 2} \right)
$$
$$
\text{for } k = 1
\qquad
x + y + z = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x, y, z) = (1 - s - t, \ s, \ t), \quad s, t \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
\text{for } k = -2
\qquad
\text{adding the three equations gives } \ 0 \cdot (x + y + z) = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expansion along the first row gives three terms; the first two add up to $2(k - 1)$ and the third is $k(1 - k)(1 + k)$, so factoring out $k - 1$ one obtains $\det A = -(k - 1)^2(k + 2)$. For $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq -2$ the system is **determined**. To find the solution it is convenient to subtract the equations in pairs: the first minus the second gives $(k - 1)(z - y) = 0$ and the second minus the third gives $(k - 1)(y - x) = 0$; since $k \neq 1$, one can divide by $k - 1$ and obtain $x = y = z$, and the first equation becomes $(k + 2)x = 1$. For $k = 1$ the three equations coincide, the common rank is $1$ and the solutions are $\infty^{3 - 1} = \infty^2$. For $k = -2$ the sum of the three equations has $(k + 2)(x + y + z) = 0$ on the left and $3$ on the right, so the system is **inconsistent**; indeed $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$, by the minor $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{vmatrix} = -3$, whereas $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Discuss, as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y - z = 2 \\[4pt]
2x + 5y + kz = 5 \\[4pt]
x + ky + 4z = 3
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 2 & -1 \\
2 & 5 & k \\
1 & k & 4
\end{vmatrix}
&= (20 - k^2) - 2(8 - k) - (2k - 5) \\[6pt]
&= 9 - k^2 = (3 - k)(3 + k)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\det A_1 = -(2k + 5)(k - 3),
\qquad
\det A_2 = 3 - k,
\qquad
\det A_3 = 3 - k
$$
$$
\text{for } k \neq \pm 3
\qquad
(x, y, z) = \left( \frac{2k + 5}{k + 3}, \ \frac{1}{k + 3}, \ \frac{1}{k + 3} \right)
$$
$$
\text{for } k = 3
\qquad
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\
2 & 5 & 3 & 5 \\
1 & 3 & 4 & 3
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\
0 & 1 & 5 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 5 & 1
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
z = t, \quad y = 1 - 5t, \quad x = 2 - 2y + z = 11t,
\qquad
(x, y, z) = (11t, \ 1 - 5t, \ t)
$$
$$
\text{for } k = -3
\qquad
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\
2 & 5 & -3 & 5 \\
1 & -3 & 4 & 3
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
0 & -5 & 5 & 1
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 + 5R_2}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 2 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 6
\end{array}\right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant, expanded along the first row, equals $9 - k^2$ and vanishes for $k = 3$ and for $k = -3$. For the other values the system is **determined** and **Cramer's rule** gives the solution: the three determinants $\det A_j$ are computed with the same expansion, and in each ratio the factor $3 - k$ cancels, since $k \neq 3$. For example $x = \dfrac{-(2k + 5)(k - 3)}{(3 - k)(3 + k)} = \dfrac{2k + 5}{k + 3}$. For $k = 3$ the reduction produces two equal rows, so $\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$ and the system has $\infty^1$ solutions; with $z = t$ the second equation gives $y = 1 - 5t$ and the first gives $x = 11t$. For $k = -3$ the last reduced row is $(0 \ 0 \ 0 \mid 6)$, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$, $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$ and the system is **inconsistent**.

</div>
