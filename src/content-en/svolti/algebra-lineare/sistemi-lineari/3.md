## Exercise 1

Determine whether the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 3 \\[4pt]
x + 2y - z = 2 \\[4pt]
2x + 3y = 5
\end{cases}
$$
is consistent and, if so, find its solutions.

$$
\det A =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 2 & -1 \\
2 & 3 & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= 1(0 + 3) - 1(0 + 2) + 1(3 - 4) = 0,
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
1 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \neq 0
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 3 \\
1 & 2 & 2 \\
2 & 3 & 5
\end{vmatrix}
= 1(10 - 6) - 1(5 - 4) + 3(3 - 4) = 0
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = \operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2,
\qquad n - r = 3 - 2 = 1
$$
$$
z = t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
x + y = 3 - t \\[4pt]
x + 2y = 2 + t
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = -1 + 2t, \quad x = 4 - 3t
$$
$$
(x, y, z) = (4 - 3t, \ -1 + 2t, \ t), \qquad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of $A$ is zero, so $\operatorname{rk} A < 3$; the minor of the first two rows and the first two columns equals $1$, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. In the augmented matrix there are two bordered minors of this minor: with the column of $z$ one obtains $\det A = 0$, with the column of constant terms one again obtains $0$. By the **bordered minors theorem** $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 2$, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is consistent with $\infty^{3 - 2} = \infty^1$ solutions. To solve it, the two equations of the rows of the minor are kept, while the third, which is their sum, is discarded. The unknown $z$ does not appear in the minor, so we set $z = t$ and solve the $2 \times 2$ system in $x$ and $y$: subtracting the first equation from the second gives $y = -1 + 2t$, and the first gives $x = 3 - t - y = 4 - 3t$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine whether the system of four equations in three unknowns
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + z = 6 \\[4pt]
x - y + z = 2 \\[4pt]
2x + y - z = 1 \\[4pt]
3x + y + z = 8
\end{cases}
$$
is consistent and, if so, find its solutions.

$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & -1 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & -1
\end{vmatrix}
= 1(1 - 1) - 1(-1 - 2) + 1(1 + 2) = 6 \neq 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{rk} A = 3
$$
$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\
1 & -1 & 1 & 2 \\
2 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
3 & 1 & 1 & 8
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_1, \ R_4 \to R_4 - 3R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\
0 & -2 & 0 & -4 \\
0 & -1 & -3 & -11 \\
0 & -2 & -2 & -10
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
y = 2, \qquad -2 - 3z = -11 \ \Longrightarrow\ z = 3, \qquad -4 - 2z = -10 \ \Longrightarrow\ z = 3
$$
$$
x = 6 - y - z = 1,
\qquad
(x, y, z) = (1, 2, 3)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix $A$ is $4 \times 3$, so $\operatorname{rk} A \leq 3$; the minor of the first three rows equals $6$, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 3$. The augmented matrix is square of order $4$ and the system is consistent if and only if its rank is still $3$. Instead of $\det (A \mid b)$ it is convenient to reduce: after the first step the second row gives $y = 2$, the third gives $z = 3$ and the fourth, with $y = 2$, again gives $z = 3$. The four equations therefore have a common solution, and by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the ranks are equal; since the common rank is $3$, equal to the number of unknowns, the solution is **unique**: $(1, 2, 3)$. The check in the fourth equation gives $3 + 2 + 3 = 8$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y - z = 1 \\[4pt]
2x + 4y + z = 5 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + 2z = 3
\end{cases}
$$
is consistent.

$$
\det A = 0 \quad (\text{the column of } y \text{ is twice that of } x),
\qquad
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & -1 \\
2 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= 3 \neq 0
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} A = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & -1 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & 5 \\
1 & 2 & 3
\end{vmatrix}
&= 1(3 - 10) + 1(6 - 5) + 1(4 - 1) \\[6pt]
&= -7 + 1 + 3 = -3 \neq 0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3 \neq \operatorname{rk} A
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In every equation the coefficient of $y$ is twice that of $x$, so the first two columns of $A$ are proportional and $\det A = 0$. The minor of the first two rows and of the columns of $x$ and $z$ equals $3$, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. In the augmented matrix the bordered minor of this minor with the third row and the column of constant terms equals $-3$, so the augmented matrix has a nonzero minor of order $3$ and $\operatorname{rk} (A \mid b) = 3$. Since the two ranks are different, by the **Rouché-Capelli theorem** the system is **inconsistent**. The computation of the ranks settled the question without looking for the solutions.

</div>
