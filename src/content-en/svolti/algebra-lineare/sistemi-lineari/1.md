## Exercise 1

Solve with Gaussian elimination the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y - z = -1 \\[4pt]
2x + y + z = 4 \\[4pt]
3x - y + 2z = 9
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & -1 \\
2 & 1 & 1 & 4 \\
3 & -1 & 2 & 9
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & -1 \\
0 & -3 & 3 & 6 \\
0 & -7 & 5 & 12
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
\xrightarrow{R_2 \to -\frac{1}{3} R_2}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & -1 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & -2 \\
0 & -7 & 5 & 12
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 + 7R_2}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & -1 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & -2 \\
0 & 0 & -2 & -2
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
-2z &= -2 &&\Longrightarrow\quad z = 1 \\[4pt]
y - z &= -2 &&\Longrightarrow\quad y = -2 + 1 = -1 \\[4pt]
x + 2y - z &= -1 &&\Longrightarrow\quad x = -1 - 2(-1) + 1 = 2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(x, y, z) = (2, -1, 1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first column has the pivot $1$ at the top, so with $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$ the entries below it are made zero; the two operations can be performed in the same step because both use only the row $R_1$, which remains unchanged. The multiplication $R_2 \to -\dfrac{1}{3} R_2$ brings the second pivot to $1$ and avoids fractions in the next step, where $R_3 \to R_3 + 7R_2$ makes the entry $-7$ zero. The resulting matrix is in echelon form with three pivots, one for each unknown, therefore no unknown is free. Back substitution starts from the last equation, $-2z = -2$, and goes up to the first. The system is **determined** with solution $(2, -1, 1)$, and the check in the third equation gives $6 + 1 + 2 = 9$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve with Gaussian elimination the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + 2z = 3 \\[4pt]
2x + 3y + 3z = 7 \\[4pt]
x + 2y + z = 4
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
2 & 3 & 3 & 7 \\
1 & 2 & 1 & 4
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 1
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 - R_2}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
0 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
z = t, \qquad y = 1 + t, \qquad x = 3 - (1 + t) - 2t = 2 - 3t
$$
$$
(x, y, z) = (2 - 3t, \ 1 + t, \ t), \qquad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After the first step the second and third rows coincide, so $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$ produces a zero row, which corresponds to the equation $0 = 0$ and is discarded. Two pivots remain, in the columns of $x$ and $y$, while the column of $z$ has no pivot; therefore $z$ is a free unknown and we set $z = t$. The second equation, $y - z = 1$, gives $y = 1 + t$, and the first gives $x = 3 - y - 2z = 2 - 3t$. The system is **underdetermined** with $\infty^1$ solutions, one for each $t \in \mathbb{R}$. For $t = 0$ one obtains the solution $(2, 1, 0)$, which satisfies the second equation: $4 + 3 + 0 = 7$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine with Gaussian elimination whether the system
$$
\begin{cases}
x - y + z = 1 \\[4pt]
2x + y - z = 2 \\[4pt]
x + 2y - 2z = 3
\end{cases}
$$
has solutions.

$$
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & -1 & 2 \\
1 & 2 & -2 & 3
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 - R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 3 & -3 & 0 \\
0 & 3 & -3 & 2
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 - R_2}
\left(\begin{array}{ccc|c}
1 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 3 & -3 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 2
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
0x + 0y + 0z = 2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0 = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After the first step the rows $R_2$ and $R_3$ have the same coefficients of the unknowns and different constant terms, $0$ and $2$. The subtraction $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$ makes the coefficients zero and leaves the constant term $2$, so the last row has the form $(0 \ 0 \ 0 \mid 2)$ and corresponds to the equation $0 = 2$, which no triple satisfies. Since the reduced system is equivalent to the original one, the system **has no solutions**: it is **inconsistent**. The reason can also be read from the original equations: the sum of the first and third gives $2x + y - z = 4$, whereas the second requires $2x + y - z = 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Solve with Gaussian elimination the system in the unknowns $x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4$
$$
\begin{cases}
x_1 + 2x_2 - x_3 + x_4 = 1 \\[4pt]
2x_1 + 4x_2 - x_3 + 3x_4 = 4 \\[4pt]
-x_1 - 2x_2 + 3x_3 + x_4 = 3
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\left(\begin{array}{cccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\
2 & 4 & -1 & 3 & 4 \\
-1 & -2 & 3 & 1 & 3
\end{array}\right)
\xrightarrow[R_3 \to R_3 + R_1]{R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1}
\left(\begin{array}{cccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\
0 & 0 & 2 & 2 & 4
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_2}
\left(\begin{array}{cccc|c}
1 & 2 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}\right)
$$
$$
x_2 = s, \qquad x_4 = t, \qquad x_3 = 2 - t
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1 &= 1 - 2s + x_3 - t \\[6pt]
&= 1 - 2s + (2 - t) - t \\[6pt]
&= 3 - 2s - 2t
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) &= (3 - 2s - 2t, \ s, \ 2 - t, \ t) \\[6pt]
&= (3, 0, 2, 0) + s\,(-2, 1, 0, 0) + t\,(-2, 0, -1, 1), \qquad s, t \in \mathbb{R}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step makes the first column zero below the pivot and *also* makes the second column zero, because the coefficients of $x_2$ are twice those of $x_1$ in every equation. The pivot of the second row is therefore in the column of $x_3$, and $R_3 \to R_3 - 2R_2$ produces a zero row. There are two pivots, in the columns of $x_1$ and $x_3$, while the columns of $x_2$ and $x_4$ have none; therefore the free unknowns are $x_2 = s$ and $x_4 = t$, and the solutions are $\infty^{4 - 2} = \infty^2$. The second equation, $x_3 + x_4 = 2$, gives $x_3 = 2 - t$, and the first, after substitution, gives $x_1 = 3 - 2s - 2t$. Separating the constant terms from those in $s$ and in $t$, the solutions are written as the **particular solution** $(3, 0, 2, 0)$ plus a combination of two fixed vectors.

</div>
