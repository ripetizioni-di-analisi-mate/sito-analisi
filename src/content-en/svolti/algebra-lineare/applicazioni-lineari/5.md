## Exercise 1

Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(x, y) = (x + 2y,\ 3x + 2y)$. Write the matrix $M^{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)$ with respect to the basis $\mathcal{B} = \big( (2, 3), (1, -1) \big)$.

$$
f(2, 3) = (2 + 6,\ 6 + 6) = (8, 12) = 4\,(2, 3) + 0\,(1, -1)
$$
$$
f(1, -1) = (1 - 2,\ 3 - 2) = (-1, 1) = 0\,(2, 3) - 1\,(1, -1)
$$
$$
M^{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{B}}(f) =
\begin{pmatrix}
4 & 0 \\
0 & -1
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $j$-th column of $M^{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)$ contains the coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{B}$ of the image of the $j$-th vector of $\mathcal{B}$, so we compute $f(2, 3)$ and $f(1, -1)$ and write them as combinations of the basis vectors. The first result is $(8, 12)$, which is four times $(2, 3)$, and has coordinates $(4, 0)$; the second is $(-1, 1)$, the opposite of $(1, -1)$, and has coordinates $(0, -1)$. The matrix is therefore **diagonal**, and with respect to this basis $f$ multiplies the first coordinate by $4$ and the second by $-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $f : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, $f(x, y, z) = (x + y,\ y - z)$. Find $M^{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{C}}(f)$ with
$$
\mathcal{B} = \big( (1, 0, 0), (1, 1, 0), (1, 1, 1) \big),
\qquad
\mathcal{C} = \big( (1, 1), (0, 1) \big) .
$$

$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & -1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
1 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
Q^{-1} =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
-1 & 1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A P =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 2 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
M^{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{C}}(f) = Q^{-1} A P =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
-1 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 2 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 2 \\
-1 & -1 & -2
\end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We apply the **change-of-basis formula** starting from the matrix $A$ of $f$ with respect to the standard bases. The matrix $P$ has the vectors of $\mathcal{B}$ as columns and the matrix $Q$ has the vectors of $\mathcal{C}$ as columns. The determinant of $Q$ is $1$, and the inverse of a $2 \times 2$ matrix is obtained by swapping the entries of the main diagonal, changing the sign of the other two and dividing by the determinant.

The product $AP$ has as columns the images of the vectors of $\mathcal{B}$, that is $f(1, 0, 0) = (1, 0)$, $f(1, 1, 0) = (2, 1)$ and $f(1, 1, 1) = (2, 0)$; multiplication on the left by $Q^{-1}$ computes their coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{C}$. The result is checked on one column. According to the third column $f(1, 1, 1) = 2\,(1, 1) - 2\,(0, 1) = (2, 0)$, in agreement with the direct computation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $f$ be the endomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$ with matrix, with respect to the standard basis,
$$
A =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 0 & 2
\end{pmatrix} .
$$
Find the matrix $A'$ of $f$ with respect to the basis $\mathcal{B}' = \big( (1, 0, 0), (1, 1, 0), (1, 1, 1) \big)$ and verify that $A$ and $A'$ have the same determinant and the same trace.

$$
P =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P^{-1} =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & -1 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
A P =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 3 & 3 \\
0 & 1 & 2 \\
1 & 1 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
A' = P^{-1} A P =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & -1 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 3 & 3 \\
0 & 1 & 2 \\
1 & 1 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 1 \\
-1 & 0 & -1 \\
1 & 1 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\det A = 1 \cdot (2 - 0) - 2 \cdot (0 - 1) + 0 = 4,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr} A = 1 + 1 + 2 = 4
$$
$$
\det A' = 1 \cdot (0 + 1) - 2 \cdot (-3 + 1) + 1 \cdot (-1 - 0) = 4,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr} A' = 1 + 0 + 3 = 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For an endomorphism the change-of-basis formula is $A' = P^{-1} A P$, where $P$ has the vectors of the new basis as columns. The matrix $P$ is upper triangular with diagonal entries equal to $1$, so $\det P = 1$ and $P$ is invertible; the inverse is verified by checking that $P\,P^{-1} = I_3$. The columns of $AP$ are the images of the vectors of $\mathcal{B}'$, and multiplication by $P^{-1}$ computes their coordinates with respect to $\mathcal{B}'$. Each row of $P^{-1} (AP)$ is a combination of the rows of $AP$, with coefficients read on the corresponding row of $P^{-1}$: the first row minus the second, the second minus the third, the third.

The matrices $A$ and $A'$ represent the same endomorphism, so they are similar, and by the **similarity invariants** they have the same determinant and the same trace. The determinants, computed with the Laplace expansion along the first row, both equal $4$, and the traces both equal $4$, in agreement with the theory.

</div>
