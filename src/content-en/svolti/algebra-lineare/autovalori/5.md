## Exercise 1

Determine for which $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ k & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is diagonalizable.

$$
p_A(\lambda) = (1 - \lambda)^2 - k = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(1 - \lambda)^2 = k
$$

$$
k > 0 : \quad
\lambda = 1 \pm \sqrt{k} \ \text{ real and distinct}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{diagonalizable}
$$
$$
k = 0 : \quad
\lambda = 1, \ m_a(1) = 2,
\qquad
A - I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad m_g(1) = 2 - 1 = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{not diagonalizable}
$$
$$
k < 0 : \quad
p_A \text{ without real roots}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{not diagonalizable}
$$

$$
A \text{ diagonalizable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k > 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic polynomial vanishes when $(1 - \lambda)^2 = k$, therefore the number of real roots depends on the sign of $k$ and the discussion splits into three cases. If $k > 0$, the equation gives $1 - \lambda = \pm\sqrt{k}$, that is, the two real and distinct eigenvalues $1 \pm \sqrt{k}$, and the matrix is diagonalizable by the theorem on **distinct real eigenvalues**. If $k = 0$, the only eigenvalue is $1$ with algebraic multiplicity $2$; the matrix $A - I_2$ is nonzero, therefore it has rank $1$ and $m_g(1) = 1 < 2$. If $k < 0$, the square $(1 - \lambda)^2$ cannot be negative, so there are no real eigenvalues. In conclusion, the matrix is **diagonalizable if and only if** $k > 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine for which $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is diagonalizable.

$$
p_A(\lambda) = (k - \lambda)(2 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda)
$$

$$
k \neq 2 \ \text{and} \ k \neq 3 : \quad
k, \ 2, \ 3 \ \text{ distinct}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{diagonalizable}
$$
$$
k = 2 : \quad
m_a(2) = 2,
\qquad
A - 2I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad m_g(2) = 3 - 2 = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{not diagonalizable}
$$
$$
k = 3 : \quad
m_a(3) = 2,
\qquad
A - 3I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad m_g(3) = 3 - 1 = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{diagonalizable}
$$

$$
A \text{ diagonalizable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k \neq 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix is upper triangular, therefore the eigenvalues are the diagonal entries $k$, $2$, $3$, all real for every $k$. The critical values of the parameter are those for which $k$ coincides with one of the other two eigenvalues. For $k$ different from $2$ and from $3$ the eigenvalues are distinct and the matrix is diagonalizable. For $k = 2$ the eigenvalue $2$ is double; in the matrix $A - 2I_3$ the first and the third rows are independent, so the rank is $2$ and $m_g(2) = 1 < m_a(2)$, therefore the **diagonalizability criterion** is not satisfied. For $k = 3$ the eigenvalue $3$ is double; in the matrix $A - 3I_3$ the first two rows are opposite and the third is zero, so the rank is $1$ and $m_g(3) = 2 = m_a(3)$. The matrix is therefore **diagonalizable if and only if** $k \neq 2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine for which $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & k & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is diagonalizable.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\lambda) &= (2 - \lambda) \begin{vmatrix} 1 - \lambda & 1 \\ 1 & 1 - \lambda \end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= (2 - \lambda) \big[ (1 - \lambda)^2 - 1 \big] \\[6pt]
&= (2 - \lambda)(-\lambda)(2 - \lambda) \\[6pt]
&= -\lambda\, (2 - \lambda)^2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
m_a(0) = 1, \qquad m_a(2) = 2 \qquad \text{for every } k
$$
$$
A - 2I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & k & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 + R_1}
\begin{pmatrix} -1 & k & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & k & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\operatorname{rk}(A - 2I_3) =
\begin{cases}
2 & \text{if } k \neq 0 \\[4pt]
1 & \text{if } k = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
m_g(2) =
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } k \neq 0 \\[4pt]
2 & \text{if } k = 0
\end{cases}
$$
$$
A \text{ diagonalizable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **Laplace expansion** along the second row removes the parameter from the computation, because $k$ appears in the row that is deleted together with the second column. The difference of squares $(1 - \lambda)^2 - 1$ factors as $(1 - \lambda - 1)(1 - \lambda + 1) = (-\lambda)(2 - \lambda)$, therefore the eigenvalues are $0$, simple, and $2$, double, *independently* of $k$. The discussion therefore concerns only the geometric multiplicity of the double eigenvalue. After the operation $R_3 \to R_3 + R_1$ the third row becomes $(0, k, 0)$: if $k \neq 0$ this row is independent of the first and the rank is $2$, therefore $m_g(2) = 1 < 2$; if $k = 0$ the third row is zero and the rank is $1$, therefore $m_g(2) = 2$. The matrix is **diagonalizable if and only if** $k = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine for which $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} k & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is diagonalizable.

$$
p_A(\lambda) = (k - \lambda)(1 - \lambda)^2
$$
$$
A - I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} k - 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad \operatorname{rk}(A - I_3) = 1 \quad \text{for every } k
$$

$$
k \neq 1 : \quad
m_a(1) = 2, \quad m_g(1) = 3 - 1 = 2,
\qquad m_a(k) = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{diagonalizable}
$$
$$
k = 1 : \quad
m_a(1) = 3, \quad m_g(1) = 3 - 1 = 2 < 3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{not diagonalizable}
$$

$$
A \text{ diagonalizable} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k \neq 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix is lower triangular, therefore the eigenvalues are $k$ and $1$, with $1$ at least double. The matrix $A - I_3$ has only the first column nonzero, and this column contains the entry $1$ for every value of $k$, so the rank is always $1$ and $m_g(1) = 2$. If $k \neq 1$, the eigenvalue $1$ has algebraic multiplicity $2$, equal to the geometric one, and the eigenvalue $k$ is simple, therefore the criterion is satisfied. If $k = 1$, the eigenvalue $1$ becomes triple, while the geometric multiplicity remains $2$; since $m_g(1) < m_a(1)$, the matrix is not diagonalizable. In this exercise the critical value is the one at which a simple eigenvalue overlaps with a double one. In conclusion, the matrix is **diagonalizable if and only if** $k \neq 1$.

</div>
