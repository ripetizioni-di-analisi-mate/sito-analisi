## Exercise 1

Determine whether the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is diagonalizable and, if so, find an invertible $P$ and a diagonal $D$ such that $P^{-1} A P = D$.

$$
p_A(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 4 = (\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 4)
$$
$$
A - I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x + 2y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_1 = \langle (-2, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
A - 4I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x - y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_4 = \langle (1, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P^{-1} = \frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
AP = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 4 \\ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = PD
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The trace and the determinant are $5$ and $4$, therefore the characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 4$, with roots $1$ and $4$. The matrix has two **distinct real eigenvalues**, so it is diagonalizable with no further checks. The columns of $P$ are one generator for each eigenspace, and the diagonal of $D$ contains the eigenvalues in the same order: the first column $(-2, 1)$ is associated with $1$, the second $(1, 1)$ is associated with $4$. The matrix $P$ is invertible, since $\det P = -2 - 1 = -3 \neq 0$, and its inverse is obtained by swapping the entries of the main diagonal, changing the sign of the other two and dividing by the determinant. The check $AP = PD$ confirms the result without having to compute the product $P^{-1}AP$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Diagonalize the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 4 & 4 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\lambda) &= (1 - \lambda) \begin{vmatrix} 1 - \lambda & 1 \\ -2 & 4 - \lambda \end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda) \big[ (1 - \lambda)(4 - \lambda) + 2 \big] \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda)(\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6) \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda)(\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
A - I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
2y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
-2x + 4y + 3z = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
z = -2y \\[4pt]
x = -y
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_1 = \langle (1, -1, 2) \rangle
$$
$$
A - 2I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ -2 & 4 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
y = 0 \\[4pt]
x = z
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_2 = \langle (1, 0, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
A - 3I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ -2 & 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
y = 0 \\[4pt]
z = 2x
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_3 = \langle (1, 0, 2) \rangle
$$
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
AP = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 2 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = PD
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second row of $A - \lambda I_3$ contains only the entry $1 - \lambda$, therefore the **Laplace expansion** along that row immediately gives the factor $1 - \lambda$ multiplied by a determinant of order $2$. The trinomial $\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6$ has roots $2$ and $3$, so the eigenvalues are $1$, $2$, $3$, distinct, and the matrix is **diagonalizable**. For $\lambda = 1$ the first equation gives $z = -2y$; substituting into the second one obtains $-2x + 4y - 6y = 0$, that is, $x = -y$, and with $y = -1$ one finds $(1, -1, 2)$. For $\lambda = 2$ and $\lambda = 3$ the second row gives $y = 0$, and the first row becomes $-x + z = 0$ and $-2x + z = 0$ respectively. The columns of $P$ are the three generators in the order of the eigenvalues $1, 2, 3$; the product $AP$ has as columns the same vectors multiplied by $1$, $2$ and $3$, therefore it coincides with $PD$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$
is diagonalizable and, if so, find a diagonalizing matrix.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\lambda) &= (1 - \lambda) \begin{vmatrix} 3 - \lambda & -2 \\ 1 & -\lambda \end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda)(\lambda^2 - 3\lambda + 2) \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda)(\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 2) \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda)^2 (2 - \lambda)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
m_a(1) = 2, \qquad m_a(2) = 1
$$
$$
A - I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad \operatorname{rk}(A - I_3) = 1,
\qquad m_g(1) = 3 - 1 = 2 = m_a(1)
$$
$$
x + y - z = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x, y, x + y) = x\,(1, 0, 1) + y\,(0, 1, 1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_1 = \langle (1, 0, 1), (0, 1, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
A - 2I_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & -2 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
y = 0 \\[4pt]
x = 2z
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_2 = \langle (2, 0, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det P = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expansion along the second row gives the factor $1 - \lambda$, and the trinomial $\lambda^2 - 3\lambda + 2$ again has the root $1$, in addition to $2$: the eigenvalue $1$ is therefore **double**. By the **diagonalizability criterion** one must check that $m_g(1) = 2$. The matrix $A - I_3$ has its first row equal to twice the third and a zero second row, so it has rank $1$ and the eigenspace $V_1$ has dimension $2$, equal to the algebraic multiplicity; the eigenvalue $2$ is simple and requires no checks. The matrix is therefore **diagonalizable**. A basis of $V_1$ is obtained from the equation $z = x + y$ by setting first $x = 1, y = 0$ and then $x = 0, y = 1$. For $\lambda = 2$ the second row gives $y = 0$ and the first becomes $x - 2z = 0$. Since $\det P = -1 \neq 0$, the three columns form a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$ made of eigenvectors, as guaranteed by the criterion.

</div>
