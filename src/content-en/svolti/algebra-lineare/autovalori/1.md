## Exercise 1

Determine the eigenvalues and eigenspaces of the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\lambda) &= \begin{vmatrix} 1 - \lambda & 4 \\ 1 & 1 - \lambda \end{vmatrix} \\[6pt]
&= (1 - \lambda)^2 - 4 \\[6pt]
&= \lambda^2 - 2\lambda - 3 \\[6pt]
&= (\lambda - 3)(\lambda + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lambda_1 = 3, \qquad \lambda_2 = -1
$$
$$
A - 3I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 4 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_1 \to R_1 + 2R_2}
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x - 2y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_3 = \langle (2, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
A + I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2}
\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x + 2y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_{-1} = \langle (-2, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
A \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = 3 \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = -1 \cdot \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$

::grafico{f="x/2;-x/2" vettori="0,0,2,1,u;0,0,-2,1,w" etichette="1.2,2,V₃ : x − 2y = 0;-3.3,2,V₋₁ : x + 2y = 0" xmin=-3.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-2.5 ymax=2.5 uguale=1 titolo="The eigenspaces $V_3$ and $V_{-1}$ are the lines through the origin spanned by $u = (2, 1)$ and by $w = (-2, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The **characteristic polynomial** is obtained by subtracting $\lambda$ from the diagonal entries and computing the determinant of order $2$. The polynomial $\lambda^2 - 2\lambda - 3$ has roots $3$ and $-1$, and the check with trace and determinant confirms the result, since $3 + (-1) = 2 = \operatorname{tr} A$ and $3 \cdot (-1) = -3 = \det A$. For each eigenvalue one solves the homogeneous system with matrix $A - \lambda I_2$: the two rows of this matrix are proportional, because its determinant is zero, therefore the system reduces to a single equation. From the equation $x - 2y = 0$ one gets $x = 2y$, and with $y = 1$ one finds the generator $(2, 1)$; from the equation $x + 2y = 0$ one gets the generator $(-2, 1)$. The final check $Av = \lambda v$ confirms that the two vectors are **eigenvectors**. Both eigenspaces have dimension $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the eigenvalues and eigenspaces of the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -2 \\ 6 & -1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\operatorname{tr} A = 5, \qquad \det A = -6 + 12 = 6
$$
$$
p_A(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 3)
$$
$$
A - 2I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ 6 & -3 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2x - y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_2 = \langle (1, 2) \rangle
$$
$$
A - 3I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ 6 & -4 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3x - 2y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_3 = \langle (2, 3) \rangle
$$
$$
A \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For a matrix of order $2$ the characteristic polynomial can be written directly as $\lambda^2 - (\operatorname{tr} A)\,\lambda + \det A$, therefore trace and determinant are sufficient. The roots of $\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6$ are $2$ and $3$. The matrix $A - 2I_2$ has its second row equal to the first multiplied by $\dfrac{3}{2}$, so the system reduces to the equation $4x - 2y = 0$, that is, $2x - y = 0$, and the generator with $x = 1$ is $(1, 2)$. In the same way the matrix $A - 3I_2$ gives the equation $3x - 2y = 0$, satisfied by $(2, 3)$. The checks show that $A(1, 2) = 2\,(1, 2)$ and $A(2, 3) = 3\,(2, 3)$, therefore $V_2 = \langle (1, 2) \rangle$ and $V_3 = \langle (2, 3) \rangle$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the eigenvalues, the eigenspaces, and the algebraic and geometric multiplicities of the eigenvalues of the matrix
$$
A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_A(\lambda) &= (3 - \lambda)(1 - \lambda) + 1 \\[6pt]
&= \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 4 \\[6pt]
&= (\lambda - 2)^2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\lambda = 2, \qquad m_a(2) = 2
$$
$$
A - 2I_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_2 \to R_2 + R_1}
\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad \operatorname{rk}(A - 2I_2) = 1
$$
$$
m_g(2) = 2 - 1 = 1,
\qquad
x + y = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V_2 = \langle (1, -1) \rangle
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The characteristic polynomial is a perfect square, therefore the matrix has the **single eigenvalue** $2$, a double root: $m_a(2) = 2$. The geometric multiplicity is the dimension of the eigenspace, that is, $2 - \operatorname{rk}(A - 2I_2)$. The matrix $A - 2I_2$ is not zero and has opposite rows, so its rank is $1$ and $m_g(2) = 1$. The eigenspace is the line $x + y = 0$, spanned by $(1, -1)$; indeed $A(1, -1) = (2, -2) = 2\,(1, -1)$. In this case $m_g(2) = 1 < 2 = m_a(2)$, therefore the eigenvectors of $A$ all lie on a single line and **there is no basis** of $\mathbb{R}^2$ made of eigenvectors of $A$.

</div>
