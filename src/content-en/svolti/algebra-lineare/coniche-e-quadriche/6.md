## Exercise 1

Verify that the conic
$$
x^2 - 4y^2 - 2x + 8y - 3 = 0
$$
is degenerate and write it as the union of two lines.

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & -1 \\
0 & -4 & 4 \\
-1 & 4 & -3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = 1 \cdot (12 - 16) + (-1) \cdot (0 - 4) = 0 ,
\qquad
I_2 = -4 < 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 - 2x - 4y^2 + 8y - 3
&= (x - 1)^2 - 1 - 4(y - 1)^2 + 4 - 3 \\[6pt]
&= (x - 1)^2 - 4(y - 1)^2 \\[6pt]
&= (x - 2y + 1)(x + 2y - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

The conic is the union of the lines $x - 2y + 1 = 0$ and $x + 2y - 3 = 0$, intersecting at $(1, 1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of $A$, expanded along the first row, has two terms: $1$ times the minor $(-4)(-3) - 4 \cdot 4 = -4$ and $-1$ times the minor $0 \cdot 4 - (-4)(-1) = -4$, with sum $0$. Hence the conic is **degenerate**, and since $I_2 < 0$ it consists of two **real intersecting lines**. To find them we complete the squares: $x^2 - 2x = (x - 1)^2 - 1$ and $-4y^2 + 8y = -4(y - 1)^2 + 4$, and the constant terms cancel. What remains is a difference of squares, $(x - 1)^2 - \bigl(2(y - 1)\bigr)^2$, which factors as the product of the difference and the sum of the bases. The two lines meet at the center $(1, 1)$, where both bases vanish.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor into two lines the conic
$$
x^2 - 2xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 6y - 3 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & -1 & 1 \\
-1 & -3 & 3 \\
1 & 3 & -3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = 0 ,
\qquad
I_2 = -3 - 1 = -4 < 0
$$
$$
x^2 + (2 - 2y)\, x + (-3y^2 + 6y - 3) = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\Delta}{4} &= (1 - y)^2 + 3y^2 - 6y + 3 \\[6pt]
&= 4y^2 - 8y + 4 \\[6pt]
&= 4(y - 1)^2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x = (y - 1) \pm 2(y - 1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = 3y - 3
\quad\text{or}\quad
x = -y + 1
$$
$$
x^2 - 2xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 6y - 3 = (x - 3y + 3)(x + y - 1)
$$

The conic is the union of the lines $x - 3y + 3 = 0$ and $x + y - 1 = 0$, intersecting at $(0, 1)$.

::grafico{f="(x+3)/3;1-x" punti="0,1,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-2 ymax=3.5 uguale=1 titolo="The degenerate conic $x^2 - 2xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 6y - 3 = 0$: the lines $x - 3y + 3 = 0$ (in color) and $x + y - 1 = 0$ (dashed), intersecting at $(0, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The second and third rows of $A$ are opposite, so the rows are linearly dependent and $\det A = 0$: the conic is **degenerate**. Since $I_2 < 0$, it consists of two real intersecting lines. To factor it, we read the equation as a second-degree equation in $x$, with coefficient of $x$ equal to $2 - 2y$ and constant term $-3y^2 + 6y - 3$. The reduced discriminant is $(1 - y)^2 - (-3y^2 + 6y - 3) = 4(y - 1)^2$, a perfect square, as predicted by the theorem on degenerate conics. The quadratic formula gives $x = -(1 - y) \pm 2(y - 1)$, that is, the two linear expressions $x = 3y - 3$ and $x = -y + 1$, so the polynomial factors as $(x - 3y + 3)(x + y - 1)$. The intersection point is obtained by setting $3y - 3 = -y + 1$, which gives $y = 1$ and $x = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Classify and factor the conic
$$
x^2 + 4xy + 4y^2 - 2x - 4y - 3 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & -1 \\
2 & 4 & -2 \\
-1 & -2 & -3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = 0 ,
\qquad
I_2 = 4 - 4 = 0 ,
\qquad
\operatorname{rk} A = 2
$$
$$
u = x + 2y
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
u^2 - 2u - 3 = (u - 3)(u + 1)
$$
$$
x^2 + 4xy + 4y^2 - 2x - 4y - 3 = (x + 2y - 3)(x + 2y + 1)
$$

The conic is the union of the parallel and distinct lines $x + 2y - 3 = 0$ and $x + 2y + 1 = 0$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The second row of $A$ is twice the first, so $\det A = 0$ and the conic is **degenerate**; the third row is not proportional to the first, therefore $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$. Since also $I_2 = 0$, the theorem on degenerate conics says that the conic consists of two **parallel lines**, and $\operatorname{rk} A = 2$ excludes that they coincide. The second-degree part is $(x + 2y)^2$ and the first-degree part is $-2(x + 2y)$, so the equation depends only on $u = x + 2y$. The equation $u^2 - 2u - 3 = 0$ has the roots $u = 3$ and $u = -1$, both real, and the lines are $x + 2y = 3$ and $x + 2y = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Classify the conic
$$
x^2 + 2y^2 - 2x + 4y + 3 = 0
$$
and find its real points.

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & -1 \\
0 & 2 & 2 \\
-1 & 2 & 3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = 1 \cdot (6 - 4) + (-1) \cdot (0 + 2) = 0 ,
\qquad
I_2 = 2 > 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 - 2x + 2y^2 + 4y + 3
&= (x - 1)^2 - 1 + 2(y + 1)^2 - 2 + 3 \\[6pt]
&= (x - 1)^2 + 2(y + 1)^2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
(x - 1)^2 + 2(y + 1)^2 = \bigl( x - 1 + i\sqrt{2}\,(y + 1) \bigr) \bigl( x - 1 - i\sqrt{2}\,(y + 1) \bigr)
$$

The conic consists of two complex conjugate lines; its only real point is $(1, -1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The expansion along the first row gives $1 \cdot 2 + (-1) \cdot 2 = 0$, so the conic is **degenerate**. Since $I_2 = 2 > 0$, by the theorem on degenerate conics it is the union of two complex conjugate lines meeting at a real point. Completing the squares, the constant terms $-1 - 2 + 3$ cancel and the equation becomes $(x - 1)^2 + 2(y + 1)^2 = 0$. A sum of two real squares, the second multiplied by $2$, is zero if and only if both squares are zero, so the only real solution is $x = 1$, $y = -1$. Over the complex numbers the sum factors as the product of two conjugate linear factors, which represent the two lines.

</div>
