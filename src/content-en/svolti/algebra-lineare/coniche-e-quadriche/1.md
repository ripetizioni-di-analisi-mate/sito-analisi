## Exercise 1

Classify the conic
$$
3x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2 - 8 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
3 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 3 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -8
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
A_0 = \begin{pmatrix}
3 & 1 \\
1 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
I_3 = \det A = -8 \cdot (9 - 1) = -64 ,
\qquad
I_2 = \det A_0 = 8 ,
\qquad
I_1 = \operatorname{tr} A_0 = 6
$$
$$
I_3 \neq 0 ,
\qquad
I_2 > 0 ,
\qquad
I_1 \cdot I_3 = -384 < 0
$$

The conic is a real ellipse.

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $xy$ is $2$, so the matrix contains $a_{12} = 1$; there are no first-degree terms, therefore $a_{13} = a_{23} = 0$ and $a_{33} = -8$. Since the third row and the third column have a single nonzero entry, the determinant is computed by expanding along the third row and equals $-8$ times the determinant of the block $A_0$. Since $I_3 \neq 0$, the conic is **non-degenerate**; since $I_2 > 0$, it is an ellipse, and since $I_1$ and $I_3$ have opposite signs, by the **classification criterion** the ellipse is **real**. It is not a circle, because $a_{12} = 1 \neq 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify the conic
$$
x^2 + 4xy + y^2 + 2x - 2y - 1 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & -1 \\
1 & -1 & -1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3 &= 1 \cdot \bigl( 1 \cdot (-1) - (-1)(-1) \bigr) - 2 \cdot \bigl( 2 \cdot (-1) - (-1) \cdot 1 \bigr) + 1 \cdot \bigl( 2 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1 \bigr) \\[6pt]
&= -2 + 2 - 3 \\[6pt]
&= -3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_2 = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -3 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 2
$$

The conic is a hyperbola, not rectangular.

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients of $xy$, $x$ and $y$ are $4$, $2$ and $-2$, so the matrix contains their halves $a_{12} = 2$, $a_{13} = 1$, $a_{23} = -1$. The determinant of $A$ is computed by **Laplace expansion** along the first row and equals $-3$, so the conic is non-degenerate. Since $I_2 = -3 < 0$, the conic is a **hyperbola**. A hyperbola is rectangular if and only if $I_1 = 0$; here $I_1 = 2$, therefore the hyperbola **is not rectangular**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Classify the conic
$$
x^2 - 4xy + 4y^2 + 2x + 4y = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & -2 & 1 \\
-2 & 4 & 2 \\
1 & 2 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3 &= 1 \cdot (0 - 4) + 2 \cdot (0 - 2) + 1 \cdot (-4 - 4) \\[6pt]
&= -4 - 4 - 8 \\[6pt]
&= -16
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_2 = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 4 - 4 = 0
$$

The conic is a parabola.

<div class="spiegazione">

The matrix has $a_{12} = -2$, $a_{13} = 1$ and $a_{23} = 2$. The expansion along the first row gives three terms: $1$ times the minor $4 \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot 2 = -4$, then $-(-2) = 2$ times the minor $(-2) \cdot 0 - 2 \cdot 1 = -2$, and finally $1$ times the minor $(-2) \cdot 2 - 4 \cdot 1 = -8$. Hence $I_3 = -16 \neq 0$ and the conic is non-degenerate. Since $I_2 = 0$, the conic is a **parabola**. The block $A_0$ has zero determinant because the second-degree part is the square $(x - 2y)^2$; this is a quick check of the result.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Classify the conic
$$
x^2 + 2xy + 3y^2 + 2x + 6y + 7 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 3 & 3 \\
1 & 3 & 7
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3 &= 1 \cdot (21 - 9) - 1 \cdot (7 - 3) + 1 \cdot (3 - 3) \\[6pt]
&= 12 - 4 + 0 \\[6pt]
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_2 = 3 - 1 = 2 > 0 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 4 ,
\qquad
I_1 \cdot I_3 = 32 > 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + y + 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
x + 3y + 3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (0, -1) ,
\qquad
f(0, -1) = 3 - 6 + 7 = 4 = \frac{I_3}{I_2}
$$

The conic is an imaginary ellipse: it has no real points.

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of $A$ equals $8$, so the conic is non-degenerate, and since $I_2 = 2 > 0$ it is an ellipse. The product $I_1 I_3$ is positive, therefore by the **classification criterion** the ellipse is **imaginary**. The result is checked with the canonical form: the center solves the system of the center and equals $(0, -1)$, and the value of the polynomial at the center is $4$, equal to $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2}$. The canonical form is therefore $\lambda_1 X^2 + \lambda_2 Y^2 + 4 = 0$ with $\lambda_1, \lambda_2 > 0$, since their product $I_2$ and their sum $I_1$ are positive. The left-hand side is at least $4$ for every $(X, Y)$, so the equation **has no real solutions**.

</div>
