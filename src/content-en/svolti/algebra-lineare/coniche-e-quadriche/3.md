## Exercise 1

Find a rotation that brings the conic
$$
5x^2 + 6xy + 5y^2 - 8 = 0
$$
to canonical form, the canonical form and the axes of symmetry.

$$
A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 3 & 5 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
p_{A_0}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 10\lambda + 16 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 8)
$$
$$
V_2 = \langle (1, -1) \rangle ,
\qquad
V_8 = \langle (1, 1) \rangle ,
\qquad
R = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
\det R = 1
$$
$$
x = \frac{X + Y}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\qquad
y = \frac{-X + Y}{\sqrt{2}}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
5(x^2 + y^2) + 6xy - 8
&= 5(X^2 + Y^2) + 3(Y^2 - X^2) - 8 \\[6pt]
&= 2X^2 + 8Y^2 - 8
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{X^2}{4} + Y^2 = 1
$$

Ellipse with center at the origin, semi-axes $2$ and $1$; major axis $y = -x$, minor axis $y = x$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The conic has no first-degree terms, so the center is the origin and a rotation is enough. The eigenvalues of $A_0$ are $2$ and $8$. For $\lambda = 2$ the matrix $A_0 - 2I$, with $I$ the identity matrix, has rows equal to $(3, 3)$, so the eigenspace is given by $x + y = 0$ and is spanned by $(1, -1)$; for $\lambda = 8$ the rows are $(-3, 3)$ and $(3, -3)$, and the eigenspace is spanned by $(1, 1)$. Normalizing the two eigenvectors gives the columns of $R$, which has determinant $\dfrac{1 + 1}{2} = 1$. In the substitution $x^2 + y^2 = X^2 + Y^2$, because the rotation preserves distances from the origin, and $xy = \dfrac{Y^2 - X^2}{2}$. The equation becomes $2X^2 + 8Y^2 = 8$, that is, the **ellipse** $\dfrac{X^2}{4} + Y^2 = 1$. The semi-axis $2$ lies on the $X$-axis, which has the direction of $(1, -1)$, so the **major axis** is the line $y = -x$ and the minor axis is $y = x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Reduce to canonical form the conic
$$
17x^2 + 12xy + 8y^2 - 22x + 4y - 7 = 0 ,
$$
writing the rigid motion and the equations of the axes.

$$
A_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 17 & 6 \\ 6 & 8 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_2 = 136 - 36 = 100 ,
\qquad
p_{A_0}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 25\lambda + 100 = (\lambda - 5)(\lambda - 20)
$$
$$
V_5 = \langle (1, -2) \rangle ,
\qquad
V_{20} = \langle (2, 1) \rangle
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
17x + 6y - 11 = 0 \\[4pt]
6x + 8y + 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x_0, y_0) = (1, -1) ,
\qquad
f(1, -1) = 17 - 12 + 8 - 22 - 4 - 7 = -20
$$
$$
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}
= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} X \\ Y \end{pmatrix}
+ \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
5X^2 + 20Y^2 - 20 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{X^2}{4} + Y^2 = 1
$$
$$
\text{major axis: } 2x + y - 1 = 0 ,
\qquad
\text{minor axis: } x - 2y - 3 = 0
$$

Ellipse with center $(1, -1)$ and semi-axes $2$ and $1$.

::grafico{curve="1+(2*cos(t)+2*sin(t))/sqrt(5),-1+(-4*cos(t)+sin(t))/sqrt(5),0,6.29" segmenti="-0.118,1.236,2.118,-3.236;-0.431,-1.716,2.431,-0.284" punti="1,-1,pieno" xmin=-1.5 xmax=3.5 ymin=-3.5 ymax=1.5 uguale=1 titolo="The ellipse $17x^2 + 12xy + 8y^2 - 22x + 4y - 7 = 0$ with the axes $2x + y - 1 = 0$ and $x - 2y - 3 = 0$ (dashed), which meet at the center $(1, -1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

We compute the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $A_0$. For $\lambda = 5$ the matrix $A_0 - 5I$ has first row $(12, 6)$, so the eigenspace is $2x + y = 0$, spanned by $(1, -2)$; for $\lambda = 20$ the first row is $(-3, 6)$, the eigenspace is $x - 2y = 0$, spanned by $(2, 1)$. The two vectors have norm $\sqrt{5}$, and the matrix with columns $\dfrac{(1, -2)}{\sqrt{5}}$ and $\dfrac{(2, 1)}{\sqrt{5}}$ has determinant $\dfrac{1 + 4}{5} = 1$, so it is a rotation. The center is found with the system of the center: from the second equation $x = \dfrac{-8y - 2}{6}$, and the solution is $(1, -1)$, which is checked by substituting into both equations. The polynomial evaluated at the center equals $-20$, and by the **canonical form of central conics** the equation in the new coordinates is $5X^2 + 20Y^2 - 20 = 0$. As a check, $I_3 = I_2 \cdot f(1, -1) = -2000$, which coincides with $\det A$. The axes are the lines through the center with the directions of the eigenvectors: with direction $(1, -2)$ we obtain $2x + y - 1 = 0$, which contains the semi-axis $2$ and is therefore the **major axis**; with direction $(2, 1)$ we obtain $x - 2y - 3 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Reduce to canonical form the conic
$$
3x^2 - 8xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 14y - 13 = 0
$$
and write the equations of the axes and of the asymptotes.

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
3 & -4 & 1 \\
-4 & -3 & 7 \\
1 & 7 & -13
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_2 = -9 - 16 = -25 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
3x - 4y + 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
-4x - 3y + 7 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x_0, y_0) = (1, 1) ,
\qquad
f(1, 1) = 3 - 8 - 3 + 2 + 14 - 13 = -5
$$
$$
I_3 = I_2 \cdot f(1, 1) = 125 ,
\qquad
p_{A_0}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 25
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lambda_1 = 5 , \quad \lambda_2 = -5
$$
$$
V_5 = \langle (2, -1) \rangle ,
\qquad
V_{-5} = \langle (1, 2) \rangle ,
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}
= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} X \\ Y \end{pmatrix}
+ \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
5X^2 - 5Y^2 - 5 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
X^2 - Y^2 = 1
$$
$$
\text{axes: } x + 2y - 3 = 0 , \quad 2x - y - 1 = 0
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x, y) - f(1, 1)
&= 3x^2 - 8xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 14y - 8 \\[6pt]
&= (3x + y - 4)(x - 3y + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Rectangular hyperbola with center $(1, 1)$, canonical form $X^2 - Y^2 = 1$, asymptotes $3x + y - 4 = 0$ and $x - 3y + 2 = 0$.

::grafico{curve="1+(2/cos(t)+tan(t))/sqrt(5),1+(-1/cos(t)+2*tan(t))/sqrt(5),-1.5,4.65" segmenti="-0.3,4.9,2.3,-2.9;-3,-0.333,5,2.333" punti="1,1,pieno" xmin=-3 xmax=5 ymin=-3 ymax=5 uguale=1 titolo="The rectangular hyperbola $3x^2 - 8xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 14y - 13 = 0$ with the asymptotes $3x + y - 4 = 0$ and $x - 3y + 2 = 0$ (dashed), perpendicular and intersecting at the center $(1, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $I_2 = -25 < 0$ and $I_1 = 0$, the conic, if it is not degenerate, is a **rectangular hyperbola**. The center solves the system of the center: multiplying the first equation by $4$ and the second by $3$ and adding gives $-25y + 25 = 0$, hence $y = 1$ and $x = 1$. The value of the polynomial at the center is $-5$, hence $I_3 = I_2 \cdot (-5) = 125 \neq 0$ and the conic is indeed non-degenerate. The eigenvalues are $\pm 5$; the eigenspace of $5$ is $x + 2y = 0$, spanned by $(2, -1)$, that of $-5$ is $2x - y = 0$, spanned by $(1, 2)$, and the matrix with these normalized columns has determinant $\dfrac{4 + 1}{5} = 1$. The canonical form is $5X^2 - 5Y^2 = 5$, that is, $X^2 - Y^2 = 1$. The axes are the lines through $(1, 1)$ with directions $(2, -1)$ and $(1, 2)$; the first, $x + 2y - 3 = 0$, is the transverse axis. For the asymptotes we use the **rule** $f(x, y) - f(x_0, y_0) = 0$: the polynomial $3x^2 - 8xy - 3y^2 + 2x + 14y - 8$ factors as the product $(3x + y - 4)(x - 3y + 2)$, as can be checked by expanding. The two asymptotes have normal vectors $(3, 1)$ and $(1, -3)$, which are orthogonal, in agreement with the fact that the hyperbola is rectangular.

</div>
