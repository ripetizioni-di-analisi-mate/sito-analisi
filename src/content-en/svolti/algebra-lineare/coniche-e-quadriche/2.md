## Exercise 1

Find the center and the canonical form of the conic
$$
x^2 + 4y^2 - 2x + 16y + 13 = 0 .
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2 - 2x + 4y^2 + 16y + 13
&= (x - 1)^2 - 1 + 4(y + 2)^2 - 16 + 13 \\[6pt]
&= (x - 1)^2 + 4(y + 2)^2 - 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
X = x - 1 ,
\quad
Y = y + 2
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
X^2 + 4Y^2 = 4
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{X^2}{4} + Y^2 = 1
$$
$$
I_3 = -16 ,
\qquad
I_2 = 4 ,
\qquad
\frac{I_3}{I_2} = -4
$$

Ellipse with center $(1, -2)$ and semi-axes $2$ and $1$, with canonical form $\dfrac{X^2}{4} + Y^2 = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The equation has no $xy$ term, so $A_0$ is already diagonal and no rotation is needed. We complete the squares: $x^2 - 2x = (x - 1)^2 - 1$ and $4y^2 + 16y = 4(y + 2)^2 - 16$. Adding the constant terms, the equation becomes $(x - 1)^2 + 4(y + 2)^2 = 4$, and the translation that moves the origin to $(1, -2)$ gives $X^2 + 4Y^2 = 4$. Dividing by $4$ gives the **ellipse** with semi-axis $2$ along the $X$-axis and semi-axis $1$ along the $Y$-axis. The result agrees with the **canonical form of central conics**: the eigenvalues of $A_0$ are $1$ and $4$ and $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2} = -4$, so the canonical form is $X^2 + 4Y^2 - 4 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the center, the canonical form and the semi-axes of the conic
$$
2x^2 + 4xy + 5y^2 - 6y - 3 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
2 & 2 & 0 \\
2 & 5 & -3 \\
0 & -3 & -3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = 2 \cdot (-15 - 9) - 2 \cdot (-6 - 0) = -36 ,
\qquad
I_2 = 6 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 7
$$
$$
p_{A_0}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 6 = (\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 6)
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\lambda_1 = 1 , \quad \lambda_2 = 6
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
2x + 2y = 0 \\[4pt]
2x + 5y - 3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x_0, y_0) = (-1, 1)
$$
$$
X^2 + 6Y^2 - 6 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\frac{X^2}{6} + Y^2 = 1
$$
$$
V_1 = \langle (2, -1) \rangle ,
\qquad
V_6 = \langle (1, 2) \rangle
$$

Real ellipse with center $(-1, 1)$, semi-axes $\sqrt{6}$ and $1$; the major axis is the line $x + 2y - 1 = 0$.

::grafico{curve="-1+(2*sqrt(6)*cos(t)+sin(t))/sqrt(5),1+(-sqrt(6)*cos(t)+2*sin(t))/sqrt(5),0,6.29" segmenti="-3.7,2.35,1.7,-0.35" punti="-1,1,pieno" xmin=-4 xmax=2 ymin=-1.5 ymax=3 uguale=1 titolo="The ellipse $2x^2 + 4xy + 5y^2 - 6y - 3 = 0$, with center $(-1, 1)$, and the major axis $x + 2y - 1 = 0$ (dashed)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $I_3 = -36 \neq 0$ and $I_2 = 6 > 0$ with $I_1 I_3 < 0$, the conic is a **real ellipse**. The eigenvalues of $A_0$ are the roots of the characteristic polynomial, that is, $1$ and $6$. The center solves the system of the center: from the first equation $x = -y$, and substituting into the second gives $3y = 3$, hence $y = 1$ and $x = -1$. By the **canonical form of central conics** the constant term is $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2} = -6$, and the canonical equation is $X^2 + 6Y^2 = 6$; dividing by $6$ the semi-axes $\sqrt{6}$ and $1$ can be read off. The semi-axis $\sqrt{6}$ corresponds to the eigenvalue $1$, so the major axis is the line through $(-1, 1)$ with direction $(2, -1)$, that is, $x + 2y - 1 = 0$. As a check, the polynomial evaluated at the center equals $2 - 4 + 5 - 6 - 3 = -6$, equal to $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the center and the canonical form of the conic
$$
x^2 + 6xy + y^2 + 4x - 4y - 8 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 3 & 2 \\
3 & 1 & -2 \\
2 & -2 & -8
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3 &= 1 \cdot (-8 - 4) - 3 \cdot (-24 + 4) + 2 \cdot (-6 - 2) \\[6pt]
&= -12 + 60 - 16 \\[6pt]
&= 32
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_2 = 1 - 9 = -8 ,
\qquad
p_{A_0}(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 2\lambda - 8 = (\lambda - 4)(\lambda + 2)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 3y + 2 = 0 \\[4pt]
3x + y - 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(x_0, y_0) = (1, -1) ,
\qquad
\frac{I_3}{I_2} = -4
$$
$$
4X^2 - 2Y^2 - 4 = 0
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
X^2 - \frac{Y^2}{2} = 1
$$

Hyperbola with center $(1, -1)$, with canonical form $X^2 - \dfrac{Y^2}{2} = 1$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant of $A$ equals $32$ and $I_2 = -8 < 0$, so the conic is a **hyperbola**. The eigenvalues of $A_0$ are $4$ and $-2$, of opposite signs as they must be. The center is obtained from the second equation of the system, $y = 2 - 3x$, which substituted into the first gives $x + 6 - 9x + 2 = 0$, that is, $x = 1$ and hence $y = -1$. The constant term of the canonical form is $\dfrac{I_3}{I_2} = \dfrac{32}{-8} = -4$; dividing $4X^2 - 2Y^2 = 4$ by $4$ gives $X^2 - \dfrac{Y^2}{2} = 1$, a hyperbola with semi-axes $a = 1$ and $b = \sqrt{2}$. Since $a \neq b$, in agreement with $I_1 = 2 \neq 0$, the hyperbola **is not rectangular**.

</div>
