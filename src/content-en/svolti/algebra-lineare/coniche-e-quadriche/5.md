## Exercise 1

Classify, as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the conic
$$
x^2 + 2kxy + y^2 + 2y = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & k & 0 \\
k & 1 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = (-1)^{3 + 2} \cdot 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ k & 1 \end{vmatrix} = -1 ,
\qquad
I_2 = 1 - k^2 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 2
$$
$$
I_1 \cdot I_3 = -2 < 0
$$

- $-1 < k < 1$: real ellipse; for $k = 0$ it is the circle $x^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 1$.
- $k = \pm 1$: parabola.
- $k < -1$ or $k > 1$: hyperbola, never rectangular.

<div class="spiegazione">

The third row of $A$ has a single nonzero entry, $a_{32} = 1$, so the expansion along the third row gives $I_3 = -1$ for *every* $k$, and the conic is always **non-degenerate**. The classification then depends on the sign of $I_2 = 1 - k^2$. For $|k| < 1$ we have $I_2 > 0$, and since $I_1 I_3 = -2 < 0$ the conic is a **real ellipse**; it is a circle when $a_{12} = k = 0$, and in that case completing the square gives $x^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 1$. For $k = \pm 1$ we have $I_2 = 0$, hence a **parabola**. For $|k| > 1$ we have $I_2 < 0$, hence a **hyperbola**, which is never rectangular because $I_1 = 2 \neq 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Classify, as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the conic
$$
kx^2 + 2xy + ky^2 - 1 = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
k & 1 & 0 \\
1 & k & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -1
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = -(k^2 - 1) ,
\qquad
I_2 = k^2 - 1 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 2k
$$
$$
I_1 \cdot I_3 = -2k\,(k^2 - 1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
k = 1 : \quad & (x + y)^2 = 1 , \quad \text{parallel lines } x + y = 1 \text{ and } x + y = -1 \\[6pt]
k = -1 : \quad & (x - y)^2 = -1 , \quad \text{no real points}
\end{aligned}
$$

- $k > 1$: real ellipse.
- $k < -1$: imaginary ellipse.
- $k = \pm 1$: degenerate conic; for $k = 1$ two real parallel lines, for $k = -1$ two complex conjugate parallel lines.
- $-1 < k < 1$: hyperbola, rectangular for $k = 0$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The expansion along the third row gives $I_3 = -1 \cdot (k^2 - 1)$, which vanishes for $k = \pm 1$; these are the **degenerate** values. For $k = 1$ the equation is $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = 1$, that is, $(x + y)^2 = 1$, which is equivalent to $x + y = 1$ or $x + y = -1$. For $k = -1$ the equation is $-(x - y)^2 - 1 = 0$, that is, $(x - y)^2 = -1$, which has no real solutions; in agreement with the theorem on degenerate conics, $I_2 = 0$ and the two lines are complex and parallel. For $|k| > 1$ we have $I_2 > 0$, hence an ellipse, and the sign of $I_1 I_3 = -2k(k^2 - 1)$ is opposite to that of $k$, since $k^2 - 1 > 0$, so the ellipse is **real** for $k > 1$ and **imaginary** for $k < -1$. It is never a circle, because $a_{12} = 1$. For $|k| < 1$ we have $I_2 < 0$, hence a **hyperbola**, rectangular if and only if $I_1 = 2k = 0$, that is, for the hyperbola $2xy = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Classify, as $k \in \mathbb{R}$ varies, the conic
$$
x^2 + 2kxy + y^2 + 2x + 2y + k = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & k & 1 \\
k & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & k
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_3 &= k + k + k - 1 - 1 - k^3 \\[6pt]
&= -(k^3 - 3k + 2) \\[6pt]
&= -(k - 1)^2 (k + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_2 = 1 - k^2 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
k = 1 : \quad & (x + y + 1)^2 = 0 , \quad \operatorname{rk} A = 1 \\[6pt]
k = -2 : \quad & I_2 = -3 < 0 , \quad x = 2y - 1 \pm \sqrt{3}\,(y - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

- $-1 < k < 1$: real ellipse; for $k = 0$ it is the circle $(x + 1)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 2$.
- $k = -1$: parabola.
- $k < -2$, $-2 < k < -1$ or $k > 1$: hyperbola, never rectangular.
- $k = 1$: two coincident lines $x + y + 1 = 0$.
- $k = -2$: two real lines intersecting at the point $(1, 1)$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The determinant is computed with the **rule of Sarrus**: the three diagonal products give $k$, $k$, $k$ and the three antidiagonal products give $1$, $1$, $k^3$. The polynomial $k^3 - 3k + 2$ vanishes for $k = 1$, and division by $k - 1$ gives $k^2 + k - 2 = (k - 1)(k + 2)$, hence $I_3 = -(k - 1)^2(k + 2)$. The degenerate values are $k = 1$ and $k = -2$. For $k = 1$ all entries of $A$ equal $1$, so $\operatorname{rk} A = 1$ and the equation is the square $(x + y + 1)^2 = 0$, which represents two **coincident lines**. For $k = -2$ we have $I_2 = -3 < 0$, hence two **real intersecting lines**; solving $x^2 + (2 - 4y)x + y^2 + 2y - 2 = 0$ for $x$, the discriminant divided by $4$ equals $3(y - 1)^2$ and the two lines pass through the center $(1, 1)$. For the non-degenerate values we study the sign of $I_2 = 1 - k^2$. For $|k| < 1$ we have $I_2 > 0$ and $k + 2 > 0$, hence $I_3 < 0$ and $I_1 I_3 < 0$, therefore the conic is a **real** ellipse, which for $k = 0$ is a circle. For $k = -1$ we have $I_2 = 0$ and $I_3 = -4 \neq 0$, hence a parabola. For $|k| > 1$, excluding $k = 1$ and $k = -2$, we have $I_2 < 0$, hence a hyperbola, never rectangular because $I_1 = 2$.

</div>
