## Exercise 1

Find the axis, the vertex and the canonical form of the parabola
$$
x^2 + 2xy + y^2 - 8x + 8y = 0 .
$$

$$
A = \begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1 & -4 \\
1 & 1 & 4 \\
-4 & 4 & 0
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
I_3 = 1 \cdot (0 - 16) - 1 \cdot (0 + 16) - 4 \cdot (4 + 4) = -64 ,
\qquad
I_2 = 0 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 2
$$
$$
(x + y)^2 = 8x - 8y = 8(x - y) ,
\qquad
(1, 1) \cdot (8, -8) = 0
$$
$$
\text{axis: } x + y = 0 ,
\qquad
\text{tangent at the vertex: } x - y = 0 ,
\qquad
V = (0, 0)
$$
$$
Y = \frac{x + y}{\sqrt{2}} ,
\quad
X = \frac{x - y}{\sqrt{2}}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
2Y^2 = 8\sqrt{2}\, X
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
Y^2 = 4\sqrt{2}\, X
$$
$$
p = \sqrt{-\frac{I_3}{I_1^3}} = \sqrt{\frac{64}{8}} = 2\sqrt{2}
$$

Parabola with axis $x + y = 0$, vertex at the origin and canonical form $Y^2 = 4\sqrt{2}\, X$.

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $I_3 = -64 \neq 0$ and $I_2 = 0$, the conic is a **parabola**. The second-degree part is the square $(x + y)^2$; moving the first-degree terms to the right-hand side gives $(x + y)^2 = 8(x - y)$. The lines $x + y = 0$ and $x - y = 0$ are perpendicular, because their normal vectors $(1, 1)$ and $(1, -1)$ have zero dot product, so there is no need to add any constant $t$. By the **axis and vertex method**, the line on the left-hand side is the axis and the one on the right-hand side is the tangent at the vertex; the vertex, their intersection, is the origin. With the signed distances from the two lines as new coordinates the equation becomes $Y^2 = 4\sqrt{2}\, X$, hence $2p = 4\sqrt{2}$, in agreement with the value $p = 2\sqrt{2}$ given by the invariants.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the axis, the vertex and the canonical form of the parabola
$$
x^2 + 4xy + 4y^2 - 26x - 2y + 19 = 0 .
$$

$$
(x + 2y)^2 = 26x + 2y - 19
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 2y + t)^2
&= (x + 2y)^2 + 2t(x + 2y) + t^2 \\[6pt]
&= (26 + 2t)\, x + (2 + 4t)\, y + t^2 - 19
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
1 \cdot (26 + 2t) + 2 \cdot (2 + 4t) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
30 + 10t = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = -3
$$
$$
(x + 2y - 3)^2 = 20x - 10y - 10 = 10(2x - y - 1)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x + 2y - 3 = 0 \\[4pt]
2x - y - 1 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V = (1, 1)
$$
$$
Y = \frac{x + 2y - 3}{\sqrt{5}} ,
\quad
X = \frac{2x - y - 1}{\sqrt{5}}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
5Y^2 = 10\sqrt{5}\, X
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
Y^2 = 2\sqrt{5}\, X
$$

Axis $x + 2y - 3 = 0$, vertex $(1, 1)$, canonical form $Y^2 = 2\sqrt{5}\, X$.

<div class="spiegazione">

The second-degree part is $(x + 2y)^2$, hence $I_2 = 0$. Keeping the square on the left-hand side, the right-hand side $26x + 2y - 19$ is *not* a line perpendicular to $x + 2y = 0$, therefore a constant $t$ is added inside the square and compensated on the right-hand side. Expanding the square produces on the right-hand side the coefficients $26 + 2t$ and $2 + 4t$; the perpendicularity condition with the normal vector $(1, 2)$ of the axis gives $30 + 10t = 0$, hence $t = -3$. With this value the right-hand side becomes $10(2x - y - 1)$. By the **axis and vertex method**, the axis is $x + 2y - 3 = 0$ and the tangent at the vertex is $2x - y - 1 = 0$. From the first equation of the system $x = 3 - 2y$, which substituted into the second gives $5 - 5y = 0$, so the vertex is $(1, 1)$. In the coordinates $X$, $Y$ the equation is $Y^2 = 2\sqrt{5}\, X$, that is, $p = \sqrt{5}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the axis, the vertex and the canonical form of the parabola
$$
9x^2 - 24xy + 16y^2 - 52x - 14y + 114 = 0 .
$$

$$
(3x - 4y)^2 = 52x + 14y - 114
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(3x - 4y + t)^2
&= (3x - 4y)^2 + 2t(3x - 4y) + t^2 \\[6pt]
&= (52 + 6t)\, x + (14 - 8t)\, y + t^2 - 114
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
3 \cdot (52 + 6t) - 4 \cdot (14 - 8t) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
100 + 50t = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = -2
$$
$$
(3x - 4y - 2)^2 = 40x + 30y - 110 = 10(4x + 3y - 11)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
3x - 4y - 2 = 0 \\[4pt]
4x + 3y - 11 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V = (2, 1)
$$
$$
Y = \frac{3x - 4y - 2}{5} ,
\quad
X = \frac{4x + 3y - 11}{5}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
25Y^2 = 50X
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
Y^2 = 2X
$$
$$
I_3 = -15625 ,
\qquad
I_1 = 25 ,
\qquad
p = \sqrt{\frac{15625}{25^3}} = 1
$$

Axis $3x - 4y - 2 = 0$, vertex $(2, 1)$, canonical form $Y^2 = 2X$.

::grafico{curve="2+(2*t^2+3*t)/5,1+(1.5*t^2-4*t)/5,-3.5,3.2" segmenti="-0.4,-0.8,7.6,5.2;0.8,2.6,3.2,-0.6" punti="2,1,pieno,V" xmin=-1 xmax=8 ymin=-1.5 ymax=7 uguale=1 titolo="The parabola $9x^2 - 24xy + 16y^2 - 52x - 14y + 114 = 0$: axis $3x - 4y - 2 = 0$ and tangent at the vertex $4x + 3y - 11 = 0$ (dashed), vertex $V = (2, 1)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The second-degree part is $(3x - 4y)^2$, so the conic has $I_2 = 0$. We add the constant $t$ to the square: the right-hand side becomes $(52 + 6t)x + (14 - 8t)y + t^2 - 114$, and perpendicularity with the normal vector $(3, -4)$ requires $3(52 + 6t) - 4(14 - 8t) = 0$, that is, $100 + 50t = 0$, hence $t = -2$. The right-hand side is then $10(4x + 3y - 11)$. By the **axis and vertex method**, the axis is $3x - 4y - 2 = 0$ and the vertex is the intersection with $4x + 3y - 11 = 0$: multiplying the first equation by $3$ and the second by $4$ and adding gives $25x - 50 = 0$, hence $x = 2$ and $y = 1$. The normal vectors $(3, -4)$ and $(4, 3)$ have norm $5$, therefore $(3x - 4y - 2)^2 = 25Y^2$ and $10(4x + 3y - 11) = 50X$, and the canonical form is $Y^2 = 2X$, with $p = 1$. The value of $p$ coincides with the one given by the **invariants**, since $25^3 = 15625$.

</div>
