## Exercise 1

Compute the angle between the vectors $\vec{u} = (1, 0, 1)$ and $\vec{v} = (-2, 2, 0)$.

$$
\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 1 \cdot (-2) + 0 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot 0 = -2
$$
$$
\|\vec{u}\| = \sqrt{1 + 0 + 1} = \sqrt{2},
\qquad
\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{4 + 4 + 0} = 2\sqrt{2}
$$
$$
\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{u}\|\,\|\vec{v}\|} = \frac{-2}{\sqrt{2} \cdot 2\sqrt{2}} = \frac{-2}{4} = -\frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\theta = \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **dot product in components** is the sum of the products of the corresponding components and equals $-2$. The negative sign already indicates that the angle is obtuse. Dividing by the product of the magnitudes gives $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Since the angle between two vectors belongs to the interval $[0, \pi]$, where cosine is invertible, the only angle with this cosine is $\theta = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the angles of the triangle with vertices $A = (1, 0, 1)$, $B = (2, 1, 1)$, $C = (0, 0, 2)$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 1, 0),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 0, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{BC} = (-2, -1, 1)
$$
$$
\cos\hat{A} = \frac{\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \overrightarrow{AC}}{\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|\,\|\overrightarrow{AC}\|} = \frac{-1 + 0 + 0}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2}} = -\frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\hat{A} = \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{BA} = (-1, -1, 0),
\qquad
\cos\hat{B} = \frac{\overrightarrow{BA} \cdot \overrightarrow{BC}}{\|\overrightarrow{BA}\|\,\|\overrightarrow{BC}\|} = \frac{2 + 1 + 0}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{6}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{12}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\hat{B} = \frac{\pi}{6}
$$
$$
\hat{C} = \pi - \frac{2\pi}{3} - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{6}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle at a vertex is the angle between the two vectors leaving that vertex. For the vertex $A$ one uses $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$, and obtains $\hat{A} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. For the vertex $B$ the outgoing vectors are $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}$; using $\overrightarrow{AB}$ instead of $\overrightarrow{BA}$ would change the sign of the dot product and would give the supplementary angle. With the correct vectors one gets $\cos\hat{B} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, therefore $\hat{B} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The third angle follows from the fact that the sum of the angles of a triangle is $\pi$, therefore $\hat{C} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$; the triangle is **isosceles**, in agreement with $\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = \|\overrightarrow{AC}\| = \sqrt{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the angle between $\vec{u} = (0, 1, 1)$ and $\vec{v} = (1, k, 0)$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

$$
\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = k,
\qquad
\|\vec{u}\| = \sqrt{2},
\qquad
\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{1 + k^2}
$$
$$
\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{1 + k^2}} = \cos\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2k = \sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{1 + k^2}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
k \geq 0 \\[4pt]
4k^2 = 2\,(1 + k^2)
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
k \geq 0 \\[4pt]
k^2 = 1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The condition on the angle translates into the equation $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, with the cosine expressed through the dot product and the magnitudes. The equation $2k = \sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{1 + k^2}$ contains a square root, whose value is nonnegative; therefore squaring is allowed only together with the condition $k \geq 0$, and the two conditions must be imposed as a system. The squared equation gives $k^2 = 1$, that is, $k = \pm 1$, and the condition $k \geq 0$ excludes $k = -1$. Indeed, for $k = -1$ one would have $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, that is, the angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. The required value is therefore **unique**, $k = 1$.

</div>
