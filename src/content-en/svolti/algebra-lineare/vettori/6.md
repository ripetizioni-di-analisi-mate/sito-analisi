## Exercise 1

Find the values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the vectors $\vec{u} = (1, k, 2)$ and $\vec{v} = (k, 4, 2k)$ are parallel.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{u} \times \vec{v}
&= \big( k \cdot 2k - 2 \cdot 4,\ \ 2 \cdot k - 1 \cdot 2k,\ \ 1 \cdot 4 - k \cdot k \big) \\[6pt]
&= (2k^2 - 8,\ 0,\ 4 - k^2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \vec{0}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
2k^2 - 8 = 0 \\[4pt]
4 - k^2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = \pm 2
$$
$$
k = 2 : \ \vec{v} = (2, 4, 4) = 2\,\vec{u},
\qquad
k = -2 : \ \vec{v} = (-2, 4, -4) = -2\,\vec{u}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For every value of $k$ the two vectors are nonzero, because $\vec{u}$ has first component $1$ and $\vec{v}$ has second component $4$; therefore the **parallelism criterion** applies, which requires the three components of $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$ to be zero simultaneously. The second component is zero for every $k$, while the first and the third both vanish for $k^2 = 4$. Therefore the vectors are **parallel** for $k = 2$, with the same orientation, and for $k = -2$, with opposite orientation. The criterion avoids comparing ratios of components, which would require dividing by quantities that can be zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find $k \in \mathbb{R}$ such that the points in the plane $A = (1, 2)$, $B = (3, 5)$, $C = (k, 11)$ are collinear.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 3),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (k - 1, 9)
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ k - 1 & 9 \end{vmatrix} = 18 - 3\,(k - 1) = 21 - 3k = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = 7
$$

::grafico{f="2+1.5*(x-1)" punti="1,2,pieno,A;3,5,pieno,B;7,11,pieno,C" xmin=-1 xmax=8.5 ymin=-0.8 ymax=12.5 uguale=1 titolo="For $k = 7$ the point $C = (7, 11)$ lies on the line $AB$, with equation $y = \dfrac{3}{2}\,x + \dfrac{1}{2}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The three points are collinear when $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ are parallel. In the plane the parallelism criterion reduces to the vanishing of the $2 \times 2$ determinant formed by the components of the two vectors, so one obtains a first-degree equation in $k$, with solution $k = 7$. As a check, for $k = 7$ one has $\overrightarrow{AC} = (6, 9) = 3\,\overrightarrow{AB}$, therefore the points are **collinear** and $B$ lies between $A$ and $C$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$ such that the points $A = (1, 0, 2)$, $B = (2, 1, 0)$, $C = (a, b, -4)$ are collinear.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 1, -2),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (a - 1,\ b,\ -6)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AC} = t\,\overrightarrow{AB}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
a - 1 = t \\[4pt]
b = t \\[4pt]
-6 = -2t
\end{cases}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
t = 3 \\[4pt]
a = 4 \\[4pt]
b = 3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
C = (4, 3, -4)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is nonzero, so the points are collinear if and only if $\overrightarrow{AC}$ is a multiple of $\overrightarrow{AB}$. Equating the components gives a system of three equations in the unknowns $a$, $b$ and $t$. The third equation does not contain the parameters and gives $t = 3$; substituting into the first two gives $a = 4$ and $b = 3$. The point $C = (4, 3, -4)$ corresponds to the value $t = 3$, so it lies on the line $AB$ on the side of $B$, at a distance from $A$ equal to three times $d(A, B)$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Given the points $A = (1, 2, 0)$, $B = (3, 1, 1)$, $C = (4, 3, 2)$, verify that they are not collinear and find the point $D$ such that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, -1, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (3, 1, 2)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}
&= \big( (-1) \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 1,\ \ 1 \cdot 3 - 2 \cdot 2,\ \ 2 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot 3 \big) \\[6pt]
&= (-3, -1, 5) \neq \vec{0}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{BC}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D = A + \overrightarrow{BC} = (1, 2, 0) + (1, 2, 1) = (2, 4, 1)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{DC} = (4 - 2,\ 3 - 4,\ 2 - 1) = (2, -1, 1) = \overrightarrow{AB}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cross product $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}$ is different from the zero vector, so by the criterion for **collinear points** the three points are *not* collinear and determine a triangle. In the parallelogram $ABCD$ the opposite sides $AD$ and $BC$ are parallel, of the same length and traversed in the same direction, that is, $\overrightarrow{AD} = \overrightarrow{BC}$. Applying the vector $\overrightarrow{BC} = (1, 2, 1)$ at the point $A$ gives $D = (2, 4, 1)$. The check $\overrightarrow{DC} = \overrightarrow{AB}$ confirms that the other pair of opposite sides is also formed by equal vectors.

</div>
