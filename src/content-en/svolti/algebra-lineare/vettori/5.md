## Exercise 1

Compute the area of the triangle in the plane with vertices $A = (-1, 0)$, $B = (3, 1)$, $C = (1, 4)$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (4, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (2, 4)
$$
$$
\mathcal{A}_{ABC} = \frac{1}{2}\,\left| \begin{vmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 \end{vmatrix} \right| = \frac{1}{2}\,|16 - 2| = 7
$$

::grafico{area="2*(x+1);(x+1)/4;-1;1|4-1.5*(x-1);(x+1)/4;1;3" vettori="-1,0,3,1;-1,0,1,4" punti="-1,0,pieno,A;3,1,pieno,B;1,4,pieno,C" segmenti="3,1,1,4" xmin=-1.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-0.8 ymax=4.6 uguale=1 titolo="The triangle $ABC$ has area $7$, half of the parallelogram with sides $\overrightarrow{AB} = (4, 1)$ and $\overrightarrow{AC} = (2, 4)$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangle is half of the parallelogram built on the vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$, which both leave the vertex $A$. In the plane the area of the parallelogram is the absolute value of the $2 \times 2$ determinant whose rows are the components of the two vectors, and it equals $14$. Therefore the area of the triangle is $7$. The absolute value is needed because the determinant changes sign when the order of the vectors is swapped.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the area of the triangle with vertices $A = (1, -1, 2)$, $B = (3, 1, 1)$, $C = (2, 3, 3)$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 2, -1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (1, 4, 1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}
&= \big( 2 \cdot 1 - (-1) \cdot 4,\ \ (-1) \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 1,\ \ 2 \cdot 4 - 2 \cdot 1 \big) \\[6pt]
&= (6, -3, 6)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\left\| \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} \right\| = \sqrt{36 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{81} = 9,
\qquad
\mathcal{A}_{ABC} = \frac{9}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In space the area of the parallelogram with sides $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ is the magnitude of their **cross product**. The product equals $(6, -3, 6)$ and has magnitude $9$. The triangle $ABC$ is half of that parallelogram, therefore its area is $\dfrac{9}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given the points $A = (1, 0, 1)$, $B = (2, 2, -1)$, $C = (3, 2, 1)$, compute the area of the triangle $ABC$ and the distance of the point $C$ from the line $AB$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 2, -2),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (2, 2, 0)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}
&= \big( 2 \cdot 0 - (-2) \cdot 2,\ \ (-2) \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 0,\ \ 1 \cdot 2 - 2 \cdot 2 \big) \\[6pt]
&= (4, -4, -2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\left\| \overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} \right\| = \sqrt{16 + 16 + 4} = 6,
\qquad
\mathcal{A}_{ABC} = 3
$$
$$
\|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 3,
\qquad
h_C = \frac{2\,\mathcal{A}_{ABC}}{\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|} = \frac{6}{3} = 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of the triangle is half the magnitude of $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}$, that is, $3$. The distance of $C$ from the line $AB$ is the height of the triangle relative to the side $AB$. Since the area is half the product of base and height, the height is obtained by dividing twice the area by the length of the side $AB$, which equals $3$. Therefore the distance of $C$ from the line $AB$ is $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find the values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the parallelogram with sides $\vec{u} = (1, 1, k)$ and $\vec{v} = (1, -1, 0)$ has area $6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{u} \times \vec{v}
&= \big( 1 \cdot 0 - k \cdot (-1),\ \ k \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 0,\ \ 1 \cdot (-1) - 1 \cdot 1 \big) \\[6pt]
&= (k, k, -2)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\|\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\| = \sqrt{k^2 + k^2 + 4} = \sqrt{2k^2 + 4}
$$
$$
\sqrt{2k^2 + 4} = 6
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2k^2 + 4 = 36
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k^2 = 16
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = \pm 4
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The area of the parallelogram is the magnitude of $\vec{u} \times \vec{v}$, which depends on the parameter. Requiring it to equal $6$ gives an equation with a square root; both sides are positive, so squaring gives an equivalent equation. The equation $k^2 = 16$ has two solutions, therefore the area is $6$ for $k = 4$ and for $k = -4$. The two values give the same result because in the magnitude the parameter appears only through $k^2$.

</div>
