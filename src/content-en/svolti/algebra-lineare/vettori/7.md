## Exercise 1

Compute the volume of the parallelepiped having as edges the vectors $\vec{u} = (1, 2, 0)$, $\vec{v} = (0, 1, 3)$, $\vec{w} = (2, 0, 1)$.

$$
\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) =
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 2 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 3 \\
2 & 0 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{u} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w})
&= 1 \cdot (1 \cdot 1 - 3 \cdot 0) - 2 \cdot (0 \cdot 1 - 3 \cdot 2) + 0 \\[6pt]
&= 1 + 12 = 13
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
V = |13| = 13
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of the parallelepiped is the absolute value of the **scalar triple product** of the three edges, that is, of the determinant of the matrix whose rows are their components. The expansion along the first row contains only two terms, because the third entry of the first row is zero. The second term must be taken with the minus sign, and since its minor equals $-6$ the contribution is $+12$. The scalar triple product equals $13$, therefore the volume is $13$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Compute the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices $A = (1, 0, 0)$, $B = (2, 1, 0)$, $C = (1, 2, 1)$, $D = (0, 1, 3)$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 1, 0),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (0, 2, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AD} = (-1, 1, 3)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 2 & 1 \\
-1 & 1 & 3
\end{vmatrix}
&= 1 \cdot (2 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot 1) - 1 \cdot \big( 0 \cdot 3 - 1 \cdot (-1) \big) + 0 \\[6pt]
&= 5 - 1 = 4
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
V_{ABCD} = \frac{1}{6} \cdot |4| = \frac{2}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three edges of the tetrahedron leaving the vertex $A$ are $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}$. The volume of the tetrahedron is one sixth of the volume of the parallelepiped built on these three vectors, therefore one computes their scalar triple product, which equals $4$, and takes one sixth of its absolute value. The volume is therefore $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $k \in \mathbb{R}$ such that the points $A = (1, 1, 0)$, $B = (2, 0, 1)$, $C = (0, 2, 2)$, $D = (3, k, 1)$ are coplanar.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, -1, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 1, 2),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AD} = (2, k - 1, 1)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & -1 & 1 \\
-1 & 1 & 2 \\
2 & k - 1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
&= 1 \cdot \big( 1 - 2(k - 1) \big) + 1 \cdot (-1 - 4) + 1 \cdot \big( -(k - 1) - 2 \big) \\[6pt]
&= (3 - 2k) - 5 + (-k - 1) \\[6pt]
&= -3k - 3
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
-3k - 3 = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = -1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Four points are coplanar if and only if the three vectors leaving one of them are coplanar, and by the **coplanarity criterion** this happens if and only if their scalar triple product is zero. In the expansion along the first row the second term has a minus sign in front and the entry $-1$, so it enters with a plus sign. The scalar triple product is the first-degree polynomial $-3k - 3$, which vanishes only for $k = -1$. Therefore the four points are **coplanar** if and only if $k = -1$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Find $k \in \mathbb{R}$ such that the tetrahedron with vertices $A = (1, 0, 0)$, $B = (2, 0, 1)$, $C = (1, 1, 1)$, $D = (2, 1, k)$ has volume $2$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, 0, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (0, 1, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AD} = (1, 1, k)
$$
$$
\begin{vmatrix}
1 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 1 & 1 \\
1 & 1 & k
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot (k - 1) - 0 + 1 \cdot (0 - 1) = k - 2
$$
$$
\frac{1}{6}\,|k - 2| = 2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
|k - 2| = 12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = 14 \ \text{ or } \ k = -10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The volume of the tetrahedron is one sixth of the absolute value of the scalar triple product of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}$, which equals $k - 2$. The condition on the volume becomes the equation $|k - 2| = 12$, whose solutions are $k - 2 = 12$ and $k - 2 = -12$. The absolute value is necessary, because without it one would find only $k = 14$ and lose the solution $k = -10$, for which the scalar triple product is $-12$ and the triple has the opposite orientation. For $k = 2$, instead, the scalar triple product is zero and the four points are coplanar.

</div>
