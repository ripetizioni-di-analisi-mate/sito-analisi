## Exercise 1

Given $\vec{u} = (-2, 1, 2)$ and $\vec{v} = (-1, 1, -1)$, compute the vector $3\vec{u} - 2\vec{v}$ and its magnitude.

$$
3\vec{u} = (-6, 3, 6),
\qquad
2\vec{v} = (-2, 2, -2)
$$
$$
3\vec{u} - 2\vec{v} = \big( -6 - (-2),\ 3 - 2,\ 6 - (-2) \big) = (-4, 1, 8)
$$
$$
\|3\vec{u} - 2\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + 1^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{16 + 1 + 64} = \sqrt{81} = 9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

**Operations in components** are performed one component at a time: multiplication by a scalar multiplies each component by the scalar, and the difference subtracts the corresponding components. One therefore first computes $3\vec{u}$ and $2\vec{v}$, then their difference, paying attention to the signs of the negative components of $2\vec{v}$. The magnitude is the square root of the sum of the squares of the components, therefore the vector $(-4, 1, 8)$ has **magnitude** $9$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given the points $A = (1, 2, -1)$ and $B = (3, -2, 3)$, find the vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$, the distance $d(A, B)$, the midpoint $M$ of the segment $AB$ and the unit vector of $\overrightarrow{AB}$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (3 - 1,\ -2 - 2,\ 3 - (-1)) = (2, -4, 4)
$$
$$
d(A, B) = \|\overrightarrow{AB}\| = \sqrt{4 + 16 + 16} = \sqrt{36} = 6
$$
$$
M = \left( \frac{1 + 3}{2},\ \frac{2 + (-2)}{2},\ \frac{-1 + 3}{2} \right) = (2, 0, 1)
$$
$$
\frac{\overrightarrow{AB}}{\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|} = \frac{1}{6}\,(2, -4, 4) = \left( \frac{1}{3},\ -\frac{2}{3},\ \frac{2}{3} \right)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The components of the vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$ are the differences between the coordinates of the final point $B$ and those of the initial point $A$, in that order. The distance between the two points is the magnitude of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, therefore $d(A, B) = 6$. The coordinates of the midpoint are the averages of the coordinates of $A$ and of $B$. The unit vector is obtained by dividing each component of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ by the magnitude $6$; the result has magnitude $1$, since $\dfrac{1}{9} + \dfrac{4}{9} + \dfrac{4}{9} = 1$, and it has the same direction and the same orientation as $\overrightarrow{AB}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the vectors of magnitude $6$ parallel to $\vec{v} = (2, -1, 2)$ and, among them, the one with orientation opposite to $\vec{v}$.

$$
\|\vec{v}\| = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = 3,
\qquad
\frac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|} = \left( \frac{2}{3},\ -\frac{1}{3},\ \frac{2}{3} \right)
$$
$$
\vec{w} = \pm\, 6 \cdot \frac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|} = \pm\, (4, -2, 4)
$$
$$
\text{orientation opposite to } \vec{v} : \quad \vec{w} = (-4, 2, -4) = -2\,\vec{v}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A vector parallel to $\vec{v}$ is written as a multiple of the **unit vector** of $\vec{v}$, which has magnitude $1$; multiplying the unit vector by $6$ and by $-6$ gives the two vectors of magnitude $6$ with the direction of $\vec{v}$. The positive coefficient preserves the orientation, the negative coefficient reverses it. Therefore the required vectors are $(4, -2, 4)$ and $(-4, 2, -4)$, and the one with **opposite orientation** to $\vec{v}$ is $(-4, 2, -4)$. As a check, $(-4, 2, -4) = -2\vec{v}$ and $\|-2\vec{v}\| = 2 \cdot 3 = 6$.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine whether the vector $\vec{w} = (5, 2, 8)$ is a linear combination of $\vec{u} = (1, 1, 1)$ and $\vec{v} = (1, 0, 2)$ and, if so, find the coefficients.

$$
\alpha\,(1, 1, 1) + \beta\,(1, 0, 2) = (5, 2, 8)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
\alpha + \beta = 5 \\[4pt]
\alpha = 2 \\[4pt]
\alpha + 2\beta = 8
\end{cases}
$$
$$
\alpha = 2, \qquad \beta = 5 - 2 = 3, \qquad \alpha + 2\beta = 2 + 6 = 8
$$
$$
\vec{w} = 2\,\vec{u} + 3\,\vec{v}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Equating the components of the two sides gives a system of three equations in the two unknowns $\alpha$ and $\beta$. The second equation immediately gives $\alpha = 2$, and the first then gives $\beta = 3$. With two unknowns and three equations the third equation must be checked: if it were not satisfied, the system would have no solutions and $\vec{w}$ would not be a linear combination of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$. Since $2 + 2 \cdot 3 = 8$, the third equation is satisfied, therefore $\vec{w}$ **is a linear combination** of $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ with coefficients $2$ and $3$.

</div>
