## Exercise 1

Find the values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the vectors $\vec{u} = (k, 2, -1)$ and $\vec{v} = (k, k, 3)$ are orthogonal.

$$
\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = k \cdot k + 2 \cdot k + (-1) \cdot 3 = k^2 + 2k - 3
$$
$$
k^2 + 2k - 3 = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(k + 3)(k - 1) = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = -3 \ \text{ or } \ k = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two vectors are **orthogonal** when their dot product is zero, so the condition translates into an equation in the parameter. The dot product in components is the polynomial $k^2 + 2k - 3$, which factors as $(k + 3)(k - 1)$. Therefore the vectors are orthogonal for $k = -3$ and for $k = 1$. For $k = 1$, for example, one has $\vec{u} = (1, 2, -1)$, $\vec{v} = (1, 1, 3)$ and $\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 1 + 2 - 3 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Decompose the vector $\vec{v} = (4, 3)$ as the sum of a vector parallel to $\vec{u} = (1, 2)$ and a vector orthogonal to $\vec{u}$.

$$
\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u} = 4 + 6 = 10,
\qquad
\|\vec{u}\|^2 = 1 + 4 = 5
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{\parallel} = \frac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u}}{\|\vec{u}\|^2}\,\vec{u} = \frac{10}{5}\,(1, 2) = (2, 4)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{\perp} = \vec{v} - \vec{v}_{\parallel} = (4, 3) - (2, 4) = (2, -1)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{\perp} \cdot \vec{u} = 2 - 2 = 0
$$

::grafico{vettori="0,0,1,2,u;0,0,4,3,v;0,0,2,4,v∥;2,4,4,3,v⊥" segmenti="-0.4,-0.8,2.4,4.8" xmin=-0.8 xmax=4.8 ymin=-0.8 ymax=4.8 uguale=1 titolo="$\vec{v} = (4, 3)$ is the sum of $\vec{v}_{\parallel} = (2, 4)$, on the line of $\vec{u}$ (dashed), and $\vec{v}_{\perp} = (2, -1)$, orthogonal to that line"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The required decomposition is the one in the theorem on the **orthogonal projection**: the parallel part is the projection of $\vec{v}$ onto the direction of $\vec{u}$, the orthogonal part is the difference between $\vec{v}$ and the projection. The coefficient $\dfrac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u}}{\|\vec{u}\|^2}$ equals $2$, therefore $\vec{v}_{\parallel} = 2\vec{u} = (2, 4)$ and $\vec{v}_{\perp} = (2, -1)$. The dot product $\vec{v}_{\perp} \cdot \vec{u}$ is zero, therefore the orthogonal part is indeed orthogonal to $\vec{u}$, and the sum of the two parts gives back $\vec{v}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the orthogonal projection of $\vec{v} = (3, 4, -4)$ onto the direction of $\vec{u} = (2, 1, -2)$ and the component of $\vec{v}$ orthogonal to $\vec{u}$.

$$
\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u} = 6 + 4 + 8 = 18,
\qquad
\|\vec{u}\|^2 = 4 + 1 + 4 = 9
$$
$$
\operatorname{pr}_{\vec{u}}(\vec{v}) = \frac{18}{9}\,(2, 1, -2) = (4, 2, -4)
$$
$$
\vec{v}_{\perp} = (3, 4, -4) - (4, 2, -4) = (-1, 2, 0),
\qquad
\vec{v}_{\perp} \cdot \vec{u} = -2 + 2 + 0 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The procedure is the same as in the plane, with three components. The dot product $\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u}$ equals $18$ and the square of the magnitude of $\vec{u}$ equals $9$, so the coefficient of the projection is $2$ and the **orthogonal projection** is $(4, 2, -4)$. Subtracting the projection from $\vec{v}$ gives the orthogonal component $(-1, 2, 0)$, and the zero dot product with $\vec{u}$ confirms the computation. The number $\dfrac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{u}}{\|\vec{u}\|} = \dfrac{18}{3} = 6$ is the signed length of the projection, positive because the angle between $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{u}$ is acute.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Given the triangle with vertices $A = (0, 1, 1)$, $B = (4, -3, 3)$, $C = (1, -2, 2)$, find the foot $H$ of the altitude from $C$ and the length of the altitude $CH$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (4, -4, 2),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (1, -3, 1)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AC} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = 4 + 12 + 2 = 18,
\qquad
\|\overrightarrow{AB}\|^2 = 16 + 16 + 4 = 36
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AH} = \operatorname{pr}_{\overrightarrow{AB}}\big(\overrightarrow{AC}\big) = \frac{18}{36}\,(4, -4, 2) = (2, -2, 1)
$$
$$
H = A + \overrightarrow{AH} = (0 + 2,\ 1 - 2,\ 1 + 1) = (2, -1, 2)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{HC} = \overrightarrow{AC} - \overrightarrow{AH} = (1, -3, 1) - (2, -2, 1) = (-1, -1, 0),
\qquad
\|\overrightarrow{HC}\| = \sqrt{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The foot $H$ of the altitude is the point of the line $AB$ such that $\overrightarrow{HC}$ is orthogonal to $\overrightarrow{AB}$. Applying the vector $\overrightarrow{AC}$ at the point $A$ and decomposing it with respect to the direction of $\overrightarrow{AB}$, the parallel part is $\overrightarrow{AH}$ and the orthogonal part is $\overrightarrow{HC}$. The coefficient of the projection equals $\dfrac{18}{36} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, therefore $H$ is the **midpoint** of the side $AB$, that is, $H = (2, -1, 2)$. The altitude is the magnitude of the orthogonal part, therefore $CH = \sqrt{2}$. As a check, $\overrightarrow{HC} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = -4 + 4 + 0 = 0$.

</div>
