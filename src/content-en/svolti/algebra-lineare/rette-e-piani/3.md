## Exercise 1

Determine the relative position of the line $r : x = 1 + 2t$, $y = -1 + t$, $z = 3t$ with respect to each of the planes
$$
\pi_1 : 2x - y + z = 9,
\qquad
\pi_2 : x + y - z = 2 .
$$

$$
\vec{v} = (2, 1, 3),
\qquad
A = (1, -1, 0)
$$
$$
\vec{n}_1 \cdot \vec{v} = (2, -1, 1) \cdot (2, 1, 3) = 4 - 1 + 3 = 6 \neq 0
$$
$$
2(1 + 2t) - (-1 + t) + 3t = 9
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
3 + 6t = 9
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r \cap \pi_1 = \{ (3, 0, 3) \}
$$
$$
\vec{n}_2 \cdot \vec{v} = (1, 1, -1) \cdot (2, 1, 3) = 2 + 1 - 3 = 0,
\qquad
1 + (-1) - 0 = 0 \neq 2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **relative position of a line and a plane** is decided by the dot product of the direction vector of the line and the normal vector of the plane. For $\pi_1$ the dot product equals $6$, so the line and the plane are **intersecting** at a single point. To find it, substitute the parametric equations into the equation of the plane; this gives $t = 1$ and the point $(3, 0, 3)$, which satisfies the equation of $\pi_1$ since $6 - 0 + 3 = 9$. For $\pi_2$ the dot product is zero, therefore the line is parallel to the plane or contained in it. The point $A$ of $r$ gives $1 - 1 - 0 = 0$, a value different from the constant term $2$, hence $A \notin \pi_2$ and the line is **parallel** to $\pi_2$ and has no points in common with it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine the relative position of the lines
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + t \\[4pt]
y = t \\[4pt]
z = 2 - t
\end{cases}
\qquad
s :
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + u \\[4pt]
y = 2 - u \\[4pt]
z = -1 + 2u
\end{cases}
$$
and, if they are intersecting, find the common point.

$$
A = (1, 0, 2), \quad \vec{v} = (1, 1, -1),
\qquad
B = (1, 2, -1), \quad \vec{w} = (1, -1, 2)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (0, 2, -3),
\qquad
\vec{v} \times \vec{w} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & 1 & -1 \\
1 & -1 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= (1, -3, -2)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) = 0 - 6 + 6 = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
1 + t = 1 + u \\[4pt]
t = 2 - u \\[4pt]
2 - t = -1 + 2u
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = u = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r \cap s = \{ (2, 1, 1) \}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The direction vectors $(1, 1, -1)$ and $(1, -1, 2)$ are not proportional, so the lines are not parallel. By the **coplanarity criterion for two lines** we compute the scalar triple product $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w})$, which turns out to be zero; therefore the lines are coplanar, and since they are not parallel they are **intersecting**. The common point is found by equating the coordinates of the generic points of the two lines. The first equation gives $t = u$, the second becomes $t = 2 - t$, hence $t = u = 1$; the third equation is satisfied, since $2 - 1 = -1 + 2$. Substituting $t = 1$ into the equations of $r$ gives the point $(2, 1, 1)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the relative position of the lines
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + t \\[4pt]
y = 2t \\[4pt]
z = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
s :
\begin{cases}
x - z = 0 \\[4pt]
y = 2
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\vec{w} = (1, 0, -1) \times (0, 1, 0) =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & 0 & -1 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= (1, 0, 1),
\qquad
B = (0, 2, 0) \in s
$$
$$
A = (1, 0, 0), \quad \vec{v} = (1, 2, 0),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, 2, 0)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) =
\begin{vmatrix}
-1 & 2 & 0 \\
1 & 2 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} -1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix}
= -2 - 2 = -4 \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line $s$ is given in Cartesian form, so first we compute a direction vector and a point of it. The direction vector is the cross product of the normal vectors, $\vec{w} = (1, 0, 1)$; a point is obtained by setting $x = 0$, which gives $z = 0$ and $y = 2$. The direction vectors $(1, 2, 0)$ and $(1, 0, 1)$ are not parallel. The scalar triple product is computed as the determinant of the matrix with rows $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\vec{v}$, $\vec{w}$, expanded along the third column, which has a single nonzero entry. The determinant equals $-4 \neq 0$, therefore by the **coplanarity criterion for two lines** the lines **are skew**.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Determine for which values of $k \in \mathbb{R}$ the lines
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + t \\[4pt]
y = t \\[4pt]
z = 1
\end{cases}
\qquad
s :
\begin{cases}
x = u \\[4pt]
y = 1 \\[4pt]
z = k + u
\end{cases}
$$
are coplanar and, for those values, determine their relative position.

$$
A = (1, 0, 1), \quad \vec{v} = (1, 1, 0),
\qquad
B = (0, 1, k), \quad \vec{w} = (1, 0, 1)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) =
\begin{vmatrix}
-1 & 1 & k - 1 \\
1 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= -1 \cdot 1 - 1 \cdot 1 + (k - 1) \cdot (-1) = -1 - k
$$
$$
-1 - k = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k = -1
$$
$$
k = -1 :
\quad
\begin{cases}
1 + t = u \\[4pt]
t = 1 \\[4pt]
1 = -1 + u
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = 1, \quad u = 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r \cap s = \{ (2, 1, 1) \}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The direction vectors $(1, 1, 0)$ and $(1, 0, 1)$ are not parallel for any value of $k$, so the lines are never parallel. By the **coplanarity criterion for two lines** they are coplanar if and only if the scalar triple product is zero. The determinant, expanded along the first row, equals $-1 - k$, and vanishes only for $k = -1$. For $k \neq -1$ the lines are therefore **skew**. For $k = -1$ the lines are coplanar and not parallel, therefore they are **intersecting**. The system obtained by equating the coordinates gives $t = 1$ from the second equation and $u = 2$ from the third, and the first is satisfied, since $1 + 1 = 2$; the common point is $(2, 1, 1)$.

</div>
