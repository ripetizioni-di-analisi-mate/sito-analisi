## Exercise 1

Compute the distance from the point $P = (1, -2, 3)$ to the plane $\pi : 2x - 2y + z - 1 = 0$.

$$
d(P, \pi) = \frac{|2 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot (-2) + 3 - 1|}{\sqrt{2^2 + (-2)^2 + 1^2}}
= \frac{|2 + 4 + 3 - 1|}{\sqrt{9}}
= \frac{8}{3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We apply the formula for the **distance from a point to a plane**. In the numerator the coordinates of $P$ are substituted into the left-hand side of the equation of the plane, written in the form $ax + by + cz + d = 0$, and the absolute value is taken; in the denominator there is the norm of the normal vector $(2, -2, 1)$, which equals $3$. The point **is at distance** $\dfrac{8}{3}$ from the plane. The numerator is nonzero, in agreement with the fact that $P$ does not belong to $\pi$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Verify that the planes $\pi_1 : x + 2y - 2z = 3$ and $\pi_2 : 2x + 4y - 4z + 3 = 0$ are parallel and compute their distance.

$$
\vec{n}_2 = (2, 4, -4) = 2\,(1, 2, -2) = 2\,\vec{n}_1
$$
$$
P_1 = (3, 0, 0) \in \pi_1
$$
$$
d(\pi_1, \pi_2) = d(P_1, \pi_2) = \frac{|2 \cdot 3 + 0 - 0 + 3|}{\sqrt{4 + 16 + 16}} = \frac{9}{6} = \frac{3}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The normal vectors are proportional, so the planes are parallel. The whole equations are *not* proportional, because multiplying the equation $x + 2y - 2z - 3 = 0$ by $2$ gives the constant term $-6$ instead of $3$; therefore the planes are distinct. The distance between two parallel planes is the distance from any point of the first to the second. The point $P_1 = (3, 0, 0)$ belongs to $\pi_1$, since $3 + 0 - 0 = 3$, and the formula for the **distance from a point to a plane** applied to $\pi_2$ gives $\dfrac{3}{2}$. The same result is reached by dividing the equation of $\pi_2$ by $2$, which becomes $x + 2y - 2z + \dfrac{3}{2} = 0$, and using the formula $\dfrac{|d_1 - d_2|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}} = \dfrac{\left| -3 - \frac{3}{2} \right|}{3} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Compute the distance from the point $P = (5, -2, 1)$ to the line $r : x = 1 + 2t$, $y = -1 + t$, $z = 2 - 2t$ and find the foot $H$ of the perpendicular drawn from $P$ to $r$.

$$
A = (1, -1, 2),
\qquad
\vec{v} = (2, 1, -2),
\qquad
\|\vec{v}\| = 3,
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AP} = (4, -1, -1)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AP} \times \vec{v} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
4 & -1 & -1 \\
2 & 1 & -2
\end{vmatrix}
= (2 + 1,\ -2 + 8,\ 4 + 2) = (3, 6, 6)
$$
$$
d(P, r) = \frac{\|(3, 6, 6)\|}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{9 + 36 + 36}}{3} = \frac{9}{3} = 3
$$
$$
t_H = \frac{\overrightarrow{AP} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|^2} = \frac{8 - 1 + 2}{9} = 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H = (3, 0, 0)
$$
$$
\|\overrightarrow{HP}\| = \|(2, -2, 1)\| = \sqrt{4 + 4 + 1} = 3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The formula for the **distance from a point to a line** requires a point $A$ of the line, the direction vector $\vec{v}$ and the vector $\overrightarrow{AP}$. The norm of the cross product $\overrightarrow{AP} \times \vec{v}$ is the area of the parallelogram spanned by the two vectors, and dividing it by the base $\|\vec{v}\| = 3$ gives the height, that is, the distance $3$. The foot $H$ is the orthogonal projection of $P$ onto $r$, and the value of the parameter is obtained by imposing that $\overrightarrow{HP}$ be orthogonal to $\vec{v}$; it turns out to be $t = 1$, hence $H = (3, 0, 0)$. Computing $\|\overrightarrow{HP}\|$ confirms the distance, and $\overrightarrow{HP} \cdot \vec{v} = 4 - 2 - 2 = 0$ confirms the orthogonality.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Verify that the lines
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = t \\[4pt]
y = 1 + 2t \\[4pt]
z = 1 + 2t
\end{cases}
\qquad
s :
\begin{cases}
2x - y - 4 = 0 \\[4pt]
2x - z - 3 = 0
\end{cases}
$$
are parallel and compute their distance.

$$
\vec{w} = (2, -1, 0) \times (2, 0, -1) =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
2 & -1 & 0 \\
2 & 0 & -1
\end{vmatrix}
= (1, 2, 2) = \vec{v}
$$
$$
x = 2 \ \Longrightarrow\ y = 0, \ z = 1,
\qquad
B = (2, 0, 1) \in s
$$
$$
A = (0, 1, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AB} = (2, -1, 0)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \vec{v} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
2 & -1 & 0 \\
1 & 2 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= (-2 - 0,\ 0 - 4,\ 4 + 1) = (-2, -4, 5)
$$
$$
d(r, s) = d(B, r) = \frac{\sqrt{4 + 16 + 25}}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{45}}{3} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{3} = \sqrt{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The direction vector of $s$ is the cross product of the normal vectors and coincides with the direction vector $(1, 2, 2)$ of $r$, so the lines are parallel. The lines are distinct, because the point $A = (0, 1, 1)$ of $r$ does not satisfy the first equation of $s$, given that $0 - 1 - 4 \neq 0$. The distance between two parallel lines is the distance from any point of one to the other. We choose the point $B$ of $s$ with $x = 2$, obtained from the equations of $s$, and apply the formula for the **distance from a point to a line** with the point $A$ and the vector $\vec{v}$ of $r$. The distance between the lines is $\sqrt{5}$.

</div>
