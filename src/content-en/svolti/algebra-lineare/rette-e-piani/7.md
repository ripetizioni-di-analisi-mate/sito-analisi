## Exercise 1

Verify that the lines $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2 - t$, $z = t$ and $s : x = 2u$, $y = 1 + u$, $z = 3 - u$ are skew and compute their distance.

$$
A = (1, 2, 0), \quad \vec{v} = (1, -1, 1),
\qquad
B = (0, 1, 3), \quad \vec{w} = (2, 1, -1)
$$
$$
\vec{v} \times \vec{w} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & -1 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & -1
\end{vmatrix}
= (1 - 1,\ 2 + 1,\ 1 + 2) = (0, 3, 3)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, -1, 3),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w}) = 0 - 3 + 9 = 6 \neq 0
$$
$$
d(r, s) = \frac{|6|}{\|(0, 3, 3)\|} = \frac{6}{3\sqrt{2}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scalar triple product $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w})$ equals $6$, so by the **coplanarity criterion for two lines** the lines are not coplanar, that is, they **are skew**. For the formula for the **distance between two skew lines** we divide the absolute value of the scalar triple product by the norm of $\vec{v} \times \vec{w}$, which is $\sqrt{0 + 9 + 9} = 3\sqrt{2}$. Simplifying and rationalizing, the distance is $\sqrt{2}$. The numerator of the formula is the same number used to establish that the lines are skew, therefore the two computations are done together.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Given the skew lines $r : x = t$, $y = 1$, $z = -1 + t$ and $s : x = -1$, $y = u$, $z = 3 + u$, find the points $H \in r$ and $K \in s$ of minimum distance, the distance between the lines, and the equations of the line of minimum distance.

$$
H = (t,\ 1,\ -1 + t),
\qquad
K = (-1,\ u,\ 3 + u)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{HK} = (-1 - t,\ u - 1,\ 4 + u - t)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
\overrightarrow{HK} \cdot (1, 0, 1) = -1 - t + 4 + u - t = 0 \\[4pt]
\overrightarrow{HK} \cdot (0, 1, 1) = u - 1 + 4 + u - t = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
-2t + u = -3 \\[4pt]
-t + 2u = -3
\end{cases}
$$
$$
u = 2t - 3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
-t + 4t - 6 = -3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = 1, \quad u = -1
$$
$$
H = (1, 1, 0),
\qquad
K = (-1, -1, 2),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{HK} = (-2, -2, 2)
$$
$$
d(r, s) = \|\overrightarrow{HK}\| = \sqrt{4 + 4 + 4} = 2\sqrt{3}
$$
$$
x = 1 + \lambda,
\qquad
y = 1 + \lambda,
\qquad
z = -\lambda,
\qquad \lambda \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

We apply the **generic points method**. The points $H$ and $K$ are written with the parameters of the two lines, and the line $HK$ is the common perpendicular if and only if $\overrightarrow{HK}$ is orthogonal to both direction vectors $(1, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$. The two orthogonality conditions form a linear system in $t$ and $u$; solving the first equation for $u$ and substituting into the second gives $3t = 3$, hence $t = 1$ and $u = -1$. The points of minimum distance are $H = (1, 1, 0)$ and $K = (-1, -1, 2)$, and the **distance** between the lines is $\|\overrightarrow{HK}\| = 2\sqrt{3}$. The line of minimum distance passes through $H$ with direction vector parallel to $\overrightarrow{HK}$, that is, $(1, 1, -1)$. As a check, the formula with the scalar triple product gives the same value, since $\vec{v} \times \vec{w} = (-1, -1, 1)$, $\overrightarrow{AB} = (-1, -1, 4)$ with $A = (0, 1, -1)$ and $B = (-1, 0, 3)$, and $\dfrac{|1 + 1 + 4|}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = 2\sqrt{3}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Given the lines
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = t \\[4pt]
y = 1 + t \\[4pt]
z = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
s :
\begin{cases}
x = 0 \\[4pt]
y + z = 4
\end{cases}
$$
verify that they are skew and write the Cartesian equations of the line of minimum distance with the two planes method.

$$
A = (0, 1, 0), \quad \vec{v} = (1, 1, 0),
\qquad
\vec{w} = (1, 0, 0) \times (0, 1, 1) = (0, -1, 1), \quad B = (0, 4, 0)
$$
$$
\vec{n} = \vec{v} \times \vec{w} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & -1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= (1, -1, -1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot \vec{n} = (0, 3, 0) \cdot (1, -1, -1) = -3 \neq 0
$$
$$
\vec{v} \times \vec{n} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & 1 & 0 \\
1 & -1 & -1
\end{vmatrix}
= (-1, 1, -2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha : (x - 0) - (y - 1) + 2(z - 0) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha : x - y + 2z + 1 = 0
$$
$$
\vec{w} \times \vec{n} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
0 & -1 & 1 \\
1 & -1 & -1
\end{vmatrix}
= (2, 1, 1)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta : 2x + (y - 4) + z = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta : 2x + y + z - 4 = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x - y + 2z + 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
2x + y + z - 4 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First we obtain a direction vector and a point of $s$; the direction vector is the cross product of the normal vectors $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 1)$, and setting $z = 0$ gives the point $B = (0, 4, 0)$. The scalar triple product $\overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\vec{v} \times \vec{w})$ equals $-3$, so the lines **are skew**. The common perpendicular has direction vector $\vec{n} = \vec{v} \times \vec{w}$. With the **two planes method**, it lies in the plane $\alpha$ through $A$ parallel to $\vec{v}$ and to $\vec{n}$, which has normal vector $\vec{v} \times \vec{n} = (-1, 1, -2)$ or, equivalently, its opposite $(1, -1, 2)$. Likewise, it lies in the plane $\beta$ through $B$ parallel to $\vec{w}$ and to $\vec{n}$, with normal vector $\vec{w} \times \vec{n} = (2, 1, 1)$. The system of the equations of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ is therefore the line of minimum distance. As a check, the points $H = (1, 2, 0) \in r$ and $K = (0, 3, 1) \in s$ satisfy both equations, and $\overrightarrow{HK} = (-1, 1, 1)$ is parallel to $\vec{n}$; the distance between the lines is $\|\overrightarrow{HK}\| = \sqrt{3}$, in agreement with the formula $\dfrac{|-3|}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}$.

</div>
