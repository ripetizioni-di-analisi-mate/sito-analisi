## Exercise 1

Write the equation of the plane passing through $P_0 = (1, -2, 3)$ and parallel to the plane $\pi : 2x + y - z + 5 = 0$.

$$
\vec{n} = (2, 1, -1)
$$
$$
2(x - 1) + (y + 2) - (z - 3) = 0
$$
$$
2x - 2 + y + 2 - z + 3 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2x + y - z + 3 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two parallel planes have parallel normal vectors, so the required plane has the same normal vector as $\pi$, which is read off the coefficients of $x$, $y$, $z$: $\vec{n} = (2, 1, -1)$. By the **Cartesian equation of the plane** through a point with a given normal vector we write $a(x - x_0) + b(y - y_0) + c(z - z_0) = 0$ with $(a, b, c) = (2, 1, -1)$ and $(x_0, y_0, z_0) = (1, -2, 3)$. Expanding the products gives the plane $2x + y - z + 3 = 0$. As a check, the point $P_0$ satisfies the equation, since $2 - 2 - 3 + 3 = 0$. The plane found is distinct from $\pi$, because the constant term $3$ is different from $5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the equation of the plane passing through the points $A = (1, 1, 0)$, $B = (2, 0, 1)$, $C = (0, 1, 2)$.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB} = (1, -1, 1),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AC} = (-1, 0, 2)
$$
$$
\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & -1 & 1 \\
-1 & 0 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= (-2 - 0,\ -1 - 2,\ 0 - 1) = (-2, -3, -1)
$$
$$
\vec{n} = (2, 3, 1)
$$
$$
2(x - 1) + 3(y - 1) + z = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
2x + 3y + z - 5 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ are parallel to the plane and are not parallel to each other, since their components are not proportional; therefore the three points are not collinear and the **plane through three points** is unique. The cross product $\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AC}$ is orthogonal to both, so it is a normal vector to the plane. The result $(-2, -3, -1)$ can be replaced by its opposite $(2, 3, 1)$, because the normal vector is defined up to a nonzero factor. Writing the equation through the point $A$ gives the plane $2x + 3y + z - 5 = 0$. As a check, $B$ and $C$ satisfy the equation, since $4 + 0 + 1 - 5 = 0$ and $0 + 3 + 2 - 5 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the equation of the plane that contains the line $r : x = 1 + t$, $y = 2 - t$, $z = 2t$ and the point $P = (2, 1, 1)$.

$$
A = (1, 2, 0) \in r,
\qquad
\vec{v} = (1, -1, 2),
\qquad
\overrightarrow{AP} = (1, -1, 1)
$$
$$
\vec{n} = \vec{v} \times \overrightarrow{AP} =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & -1 & 2 \\
1 & -1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= (-1 + 2,\ 2 - 1,\ -1 + 1) = (1, 1, 0)
$$
$$
(x - 1) + (y - 2) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x + y - 3 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First we check that $P$ does not belong to $r$. The first two equations with $x = 2$, $y = 1$ give $t = 1$, while the third with $z = 1$ gives $t = \dfrac{1}{2}$; hence $P \notin r$ and the plane containing $r$ and $P$ is unique. The plane contains the point $A$ of $r$, obtained for $t = 0$, and is parallel both to the direction vector $\vec{v}$ and to the vector $\overrightarrow{AP}$. These two vectors are not parallel, therefore their cross product $(1, 1, 0)$ is a normal vector. The equation through $A$ gives the plane $x + y - 3 = 0$. As a check, the generic point of $r$ satisfies the equation, since $(1 + t) + (2 - t) - 3 = 0$ for every $t$, and $P$ satisfies it as well, since $2 + 1 - 3 = 0$. The variable $z$ does not appear in the equation, so the plane is **parallel to the $z$-axis**.

</div>

## Exercise 4

Write the equation of the plane passing through $P = (1, 0, -1)$ and perpendicular to the two planes $\pi_1 : x + y - z = 2$ and $\pi_2 : 2x - y + z = 0$.

$$
\vec{n}_1 = (1, 1, -1),
\qquad
\vec{n}_2 = (2, -1, 1)
$$
$$
\vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2 =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & 1 & -1 \\
2 & -1 & 1
\end{vmatrix}
= (1 - 1,\ -2 - 1,\ -1 - 2) = (0, -3, -3)
$$
$$
\vec{n} = (0, 1, 1)
$$
$$
y + (z + 1) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y + z + 1 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two planes are perpendicular if and only if their normal vectors are orthogonal, so the normal vector $\vec{n}$ of the required plane must be orthogonal both to $\vec{n}_1$ and to $\vec{n}_2$. The vectors $\vec{n}_1$ and $\vec{n}_2$ are not parallel, therefore the vectors orthogonal to both are the multiples of the cross product $\vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2 = (0, -3, -3)$, and one can choose $\vec{n} = (0, 1, 1)$. The plane through $P$ with this normal vector is $y + z + 1 = 0$. As a check, $\vec{n} \cdot \vec{n}_1 = 0 + 1 - 1 = 0$ and $\vec{n} \cdot \vec{n}_2 = 0 - 1 + 1 = 0$, and the point $P$ satisfies the equation, since $0 - 1 + 1 = 0$. The plane found is perpendicular to the line of intersection of $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$, which has direction vector precisely $\vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2$.

</div>
