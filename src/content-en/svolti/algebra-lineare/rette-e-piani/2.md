## Exercise 1

Write the parametric and Cartesian equations of the line passing through $A = (1, 0, 2)$ and $B = (3, 1, -1)$.

$$
\vec{v} = \overrightarrow{AB} = (2, 1, -3)
$$
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + 2t \\[4pt]
y = t \\[4pt]
z = 2 - 3t
\end{cases}
\qquad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
t = y
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = 1 + 2y, \qquad z = 2 - 3y
$$
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x - 2y - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
3y + z - 2 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line through two points has as direction vector the vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$, obtained by subtracting the coordinates of $A$ from those of $B$. The **parametric equations** are written starting from the point $A$, adding to each coordinate $t$ times the corresponding component of $\vec{v}$. For the Cartesian equations the parameter is eliminated. The second equation states directly $t = y$, so substituting into the first and the third gives $x = 1 + 2y$ and $z = 2 - 3y$, that is, two planes containing the line. As a check, the point $B = (3, 1, -1)$ satisfies both equations, since $3 - 2 - 1 = 0$ and $3 - 1 - 2 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Write the parametric equations of the line
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x + y - z = 2 \\[4pt]
x - y + 2z = 1
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\vec{v} = \vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2 =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & 1 & -1 \\
1 & -1 & 2
\end{vmatrix}
= (2 - 1,\ -1 - 2,\ -1 - 1) = (1, -3, -2)
$$
$$
x = 0 :
\quad
\begin{cases}
y - z = 2 \\[4pt]
-y + 2z = 1
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
z = 3, \quad y = 5
$$
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = t \\[4pt]
y = 5 - 3t \\[4pt]
z = 3 - 2t
\end{cases}
\qquad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line is the intersection of two planes, and the normal vectors $\vec{n}_1 = (1, 1, -1)$ and $\vec{n}_2 = (1, -1, 2)$ are not parallel, so the system does represent a line. The direction vector is orthogonal to both normal vectors, therefore we take $\vec{v} = \vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2 = (1, -3, -2)$. To find a point we fix one coordinate: with $x = 0$ the system becomes two equations in $y$ and $z$, and adding them gives $z = 3$, hence $y = 2 + z = 5$. The point $(0, 5, 3)$ and the vector $\vec{v}$ give the **parametric equations**. As a check, the generic point satisfies both equations for every $t$, since $t + (5 - 3t) - (3 - 2t) = 2$ and $t - (5 - 3t) + 2(3 - 2t) = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Write the parametric and Cartesian equations of the line passing through $P = (1, 2, -1)$ and parallel to the line
$$
s :
\begin{cases}
x - 2y + z = 0 \\[4pt]
2x + y - z = 3
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\vec{v} = (1, -2, 1) \times (2, 1, -1) =
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
1 & -2 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & -1
\end{vmatrix}
= (2 - 1,\ 2 + 1,\ 1 + 4) = (1, 3, 5)
$$
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
x = 1 + t \\[4pt]
y = 2 + 3t \\[4pt]
z = -1 + 5t
\end{cases}
\qquad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$
$$
t = x - 1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = 2 + 3(x - 1) = 3x - 1,
\qquad
z = -1 + 5(x - 1) = 5x - 6
$$
$$
r :
\begin{cases}
3x - y - 1 = 0 \\[4pt]
5x - z - 6 = 0
\end{cases}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Parallel lines have parallel direction vectors, so a direction vector of $s$ is needed. The line $s$ is given in Cartesian form, therefore its direction vector is the cross product of the two normal vectors, $(1, 3, 5)$. The parametric equations of $r$ are written with the point $P$ and the same direction vector. For the **Cartesian equations** we solve the first equation for $t = x - 1$ and substitute it into the other two. As a check, the point $P$ satisfies the two equations, since $3 - 2 - 1 = 0$ and $5 + 1 - 6 = 0$, and the normal vectors $(3, -1, 0)$ and $(5, 0, -1)$ are both orthogonal to $(1, 3, 5)$.

</div>
