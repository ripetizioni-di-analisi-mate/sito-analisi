## Exercise 1

Solve $2(x-8)<6$, then check by expanding the product.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x-8)<6&\iff x-8<3\\[6pt]
&\iff x<11,\\[12pt]
2x-16<6&\iff2x<22\\[6pt]
&\iff x<11.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $2$ multiplies the entire difference $x-8$, so divide by $2$ before expanding. The sign stays the same because the divisor is positive. After reaching $x-8<3$, add $8$ to isolate $x$. Expanding first starts with $2x-16<6$ and gives the same boundary. The value $11$ is excluded because it gives $2(11-8)=6$. The resulting interval is $(-\infty,11)$ and contains, for example, $10$, which gives $4<6$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{2}\le9$ and check the boundary and the next integer.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x+1}{2}\le9&\iff x+1\le18\\[6pt]
&\iff x\le17.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=(-\infty,17].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $2$ is positive. Multiplying both sides by $2$ compares the numerator with $18$ while preserving the direction. Leaving the right-hand side unchanged would produce an incorrect bound of $9$ for the numerator. Subtracting $1$ from both sides then gives the boundary $17$. Substituting $17$ gives numerator $18$, and the fraction is $9$. At the next integer the numerator is $19$, so $\dfrac{19}{2}>9$ and the bound is exceeded.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{2-x}{3}-\dfrac{x+2}{6}>1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(2-x)-(x+2)&>6\\[6pt]
4-2x-x-2&>6\\[6pt]
-3x&>4\\[6pt]
x&<-\frac43.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=\left(-\infty,-\frac43\right).
$$

The endpoint $-\dfrac43$ is open."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The common denominator is $6$, so multiplying by $6$ clears both fractions. The entire second fraction is subtracted, so its numerator contributes $-x-2$. After collecting the terms, the coefficient $-3$ calls for a division that reverses the sign. The endpoint $x=-\dfrac43$ gives exactly $1$ and is excluded. At $x=-2$, the second fraction is zero and the first is $\dfrac43$. The comparison is true, as shown by the point to the left of the boundary.

</div>
