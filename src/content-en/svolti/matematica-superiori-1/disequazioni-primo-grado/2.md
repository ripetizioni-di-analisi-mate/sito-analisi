## Exercise 1

Solve $2(x-8)<6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-8&<3 \\[6pt]
x&<11
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

You can divide by $2$ before expanding the binomial because $2$ is a factor of the entire left-hand side. Adding $8$ gives $(-\infty,11)$. The endpoint $11$ makes the left-hand side equal to $6$ and is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{2}\le9$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+1&\le18 \\[6pt]
x&\le17
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $2$ is positive, so multiplying both sides by $2$ preserves the inequality sign. Subtracting $1$ gives $(-\infty,17]$. At $x=17$, the numerator is $18$, which gives exactly $9$ when divided by $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{2-x}{3}-\dfrac{x+2}{6}>1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(2-x)-(x+2)&>6 \\[6pt]
2-3x&>6 \\[6pt]
-3x&>4 \\[6pt]
x&<-\frac43
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use $6$ as a common denominator. The minus sign applies to the whole expression $x+2$, giving $-x-2$ after expansion. Only division by $-3$ reverses the sign. The solution is $(-\infty,-\dfrac43)$. The value $x=-2$ lies in the interval and gives $\dfrac43>1$ on the left.

</div>
