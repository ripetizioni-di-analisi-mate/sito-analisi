## Exercise 1

Count the integers that satisfy $8<x\le14$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{x\in\mathbb Z:8<x\le14\}&=\{9,10,11,12,13,14\},\\[6pt]
14-9+1&=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lower bound $8$ is excluded and the first allowed integer is $9$. The upper bound $14$ is an integer and satisfies the second comparison with equality. The difference $14-9=5$ counts five steps along the number line, each one unit long. Add one to count the starting point too, giving six integers. In the diagram the highlighted segment gives the real interval and the filled points show exactly which values to count.

## Exercise 2

You have $29$ euros and each ticket costs $3$ euros. Find the maximum number of tickets you can buy and the money left over.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n&\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\},\\[6pt]
3n\le29&\iff n\le9+\frac23,\\[6pt]
n_{\max}&=9,\\[6pt]
29-3\cdot9&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For $n$ tickets the price is $3n$ euros. The budget allows equality, but the real-valued upper bound is between $9$ and $10$ and the ticket count is an integer. The last permitted integer is therefore $9$. Nine tickets cost $27$ euros and leave $2$ euros. Those two euros are insufficient for another ticket. The next integer gives a cost of $30$ euros, one euro over budget.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A pack contains $4$ pencils. Find the minimum number of packs needed to have at least $41$ pencils, and how many pencils are left over.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4n\ge41&\iff n\ge10+\frac14,\\[6pt]
n_{\min}&=11,\\[6pt]
4\cdot11-41&=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The requirement of at least $41$ sets a lower bound on the total pencil count. Dividing by $4$ gives a boundary of ten and a quarter packs. Only full packs can be bought, so take the first integer to reach the boundary, which is $11$. Ten packs contain $40$ pencils, one too few. Eleven contain $44$, three more than required. Comparing the two consecutive integers shows that eleven is the minimum.

</div>
