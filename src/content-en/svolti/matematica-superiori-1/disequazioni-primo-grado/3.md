## Exercise 1

Find the interval on which both $x>8$ and $x\le12$ hold.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=(8,+\infty),\\[6pt]
B&=(-\infty,12],\\[6pt]
A\cap B&=(8,12].
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=6 xmax=14 ymin=-0.67 ymax=0.81 vettori="6.28,0,13.72,0" area="0.07;-0.07;8;12" punti="8,0,vuoto;12,0,pieno" etichette="8,-0.32,8;12,-0.32,12;13.59,-0.32,x" titolo="The lower endpoint is excluded and the upper endpoint is included."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first comparison allows only values to the right of $8$. The second allows $12$ and all values to its left. Their overlap is the stretch between the endpoints. An endpoint must satisfy both comparisons to be included. The number $8$ meets $x\le12$ and fails $x>8$, so its circle is open. The number $12$ satisfies $12>8$ and $12\le12$, so its circle is filled. For example, $10$ is allowed because it satisfies both bounds.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $19\le2x+1<23$ and check both endpoints.

$$
\begin{aligned}
19\le2x+1<23&\iff18\le2x<22\\[6pt]
&\iff9\le x<11.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=[9,11).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The central expression must lie between the two bounds. Subtract $1$ from all three parts, then divide all three by $2$. This factor is positive, so both directions stay the same and each endpoint keeps its inclusion status. Substituting $9$ gives $19$, which is allowed. Substituting $11$ gives $23$, excluded by the upper bound. Between them, $x=10$ gives $21$ for the central expression and satisfies both comparisons.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the common solutions of $2x+1\le7$ and $5-x<2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x+1\le7&\iff 2x\le6\iff x\le3,\\[6pt]
5-x<2&\iff -x<-3\iff x>3,\\[6pt]
(-\infty,3]\cap(3,+\infty)&=\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-0.3 xmax=6.2 ymin=-0.49 ymax=2.75 vettori="0.08,1.72,5.8,1.72;0.08,0.63,5.8,0.63" area="1.8;1.64;0.13;3|0.71;0.55;3;5.6" punti="3,1.72,pieno;3,0.63,vuoto" segmenti="3,0.63,3,1.72" etichette="3,0.19,3" titolo="The half-lines meet the same boundary. Only the upper bound includes $3$."}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first inequality is solved by dividing by $2$ and allows all values up to and including $3$. The second divides by $-1$, reversing its direction and giving only values strictly above $3$. The half-lines meet at the same boundary without overlapping. At $x=3$, the left side of the second comparison is $2$, which the sign $<$ excludes. The strict condition makes the intersection empty. With $\le$, the point $3$ would remain.

</div>
