## Exercise 1

Print plan A costs $6$ euros plus $2$ euros per copy. Plan B costs $21$ euros plus $1$ euro per copy. For which positive copy counts does A cost no more than B? Check where the plans cost the same.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6+2n\le21+n&\iff n\le15,\\[6pt]
n&\in\{1,2,\ldots,15\},\\[6pt]
6+2\cdot15&=21+15=36.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The price comparison includes equal costs. Subtracting $6+n$ gives the boundary $15$. The copy count is a positive integer, so A costs no more than B for orders of one through fifteen copies. At $15$ both prices are $36$ euros. At $16$ copies, A costs $38$ euros and B costs $37$. The graph shows how the ordering changes because the price rises by $2$ euros per copy for A and by $1$ euro for B.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A sequence starts at $3$ and each term is $2$ greater than the previous one. At which positions is the value at least $17$ and less than $21$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_n&=3+2(n-1)=2n+1,\\[6pt]
17\le2n+1<21&\iff16\le2n<20\\[6pt]
&\iff8\le n<10,\\[6pt]
n&\in\{8,9\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The index $n$ starts at one. Reaching term number $n$ therefore takes $n-1$ increases of two units. This gives $2n+1$, which must satisfy both comparisons. Subtracting $1$ and dividing by $2$ in all three parts gives the whole-number indices $8$ and $9$. The term at position $8$ equals $17$ and is included. The ninth equals $19$, and the tenth equals $21$, which the strict bound excludes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A shipment may weigh at most $62\,\mathrm{kg}$ altogether. The packaging weighs $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ and each item weighs $6\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the maximum number of items? What changes if the shipment must weigh under $62\,\mathrm{kg}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
8+6n\le62&\iff6n\le54\\[6pt]
&\iff n\le9,\\[6pt]
8+6n<62&\iff n<9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fixed packaging weight of $8$ kilograms leaves $54$ kilograms for items. Dividing by the weight of one item gives a boundary of exactly $9$. The original constraint allows equality, so nine items can be shipped and weigh $62$ kilograms in total. Ten items would weigh $68$ kilograms. Under the strict rule, nine is excluded and the maximum is the preceding integer, eight. The new maximum load weighs $8+6\cdot8=56$ kilograms.

</div>
