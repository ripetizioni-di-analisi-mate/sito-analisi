## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{2}\le8$ and check the boundary value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+1&\le16 \\[6pt]
x&\le15 \\[6pt]
\frac{15+1}{2}&=8
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive denominator allows multiplication by $2$ without reversing the sign. The boundary $15$ gives equality, which is allowed by $\le$. The solution is $(-\infty,15]$. The value $16$ is outside the interval and gives $\dfrac{17}{2}>8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the integers satisfying $17\le2x+1<21$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
16&\le2x<20 \\[6pt]
8&\le x<10 \\[6pt]
x&\in\{8,9\}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract $1$ from all three expressions and divide them by $2$. The resulting interval includes $8$ and excludes $10$, so its only integers are $8$ and $9$. Substituting gives $17$ and $19$, respectively. The next value would give $21$, which violates the upper bound.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A shipment may weigh at most $62\,\mathrm{kg}$. Its packaging weighs $8\,\mathrm{kg}$ and each item weighs $6\,\mathrm{kg}$. What is the greatest number of items allowed?

$$
\begin{aligned}
8+6x&\le62 \\[6pt]
6x&\le54 \\[6pt]
x&\le9
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Count the packaging weight once, leaving $54\,\mathrm{kg}$ for the items. Dividing by the weight of one item gives the integer bound $9$. Nine items bring the total to exactly $62\,\mathrm{kg}$, which is allowed because the bound is inclusive.

</div>
