## Exercise 1

Solve $9-2x>27$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x&>27-9=18 \\[6pt]
x&<-9
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $9$ gives an inequality with a negative coefficient. Dividing by $-2$ reverses the sign, so $x$ belongs to $(-\infty,-9)$. At $x=-10$, the left-hand side is $29>27$. At $x=-9$, it is $27$, and the endpoint is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-10(x-2)\ge0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-2&\le0 \\[6pt]
x&\le2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the product to be nonnegative, $x-2$ must be nonpositive because the other factor is negative. Dividing by $-10$ therefore gives $x-2\le0$. The solution is $(-\infty,2]$, including the value that makes the product zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $2<-3x+5\le11$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\quad -3&<-3x\le6 \\[6pt]
1&>x\ge-2 \\[6pt]
-2&\le x<1
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract $5$ from all three expressions to isolate the product $-3x$. Dividing by $-3$ reverses both inequality signs. The condition that includes equality becomes the lower bound. The solution is $[-2,1)$. At the endpoints, the middle expression equals $11$ and $2$, respectively.

</div>
