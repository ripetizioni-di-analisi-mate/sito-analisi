## Exercise 1

Solve $9-2x>27$ and check the direction with two integers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
9-2x>27&\iff -2x>18\\[6pt]
&\iff x<-9.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=(-\infty,-9).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $9$ from both sides leaves $-2x>18$. Dividing by $-2$ reverses the order, so $x$ must be below $-9$. At $x=-9$, the result is $9+18=27$, excluded by the strict comparison. The previous integer $-10$ gives $29>27$, while the next integer $-8$ gives $25<27$. The expression $9-2x$ decreases as $x$ increases. Values giving more than $27$ are therefore to the left of the boundary.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-10(x-2)\ge0$ and explain the signs of the two factors in a solution.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-10(x-2)\ge0&\iff x-2\le0\\[6pt]
&\iff x\le2.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=(-\infty,2].
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant factor $-10$ is negative. For the product to be nonnegative, the other factor must be less than or equal to zero. Dividing both sides by $-10$ and reversing the sign gives the same condition. The value $x=2$ makes the product zero and belongs to the interval. At $x=1$, the factors are $-10$ and $-1$, and the product is $10$. At $x=3$, the second factor is $1$, so the negative product $-10$ is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Use $u=-3x+5$ to solve $2<-3x+5\le11$ and recover the endpoints for $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2&<u\le11,\\[6pt]
x&=\frac{5-u}{3},\\[6pt]
u=11&\iff x=-2,\\[6pt]
u=2&\iff x=1,\\[6pt]
S&=[-2,1).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constraint on $u$ includes $11$ and excludes $2$. In the inverse formula, the numerator $5-u$ decreases as $u$ increases, and division by $3$ maintains that order. The upper endpoint $u=11$ therefore becomes the included lower bound $x=-2$. The lower endpoint $u=2$ becomes the excluded upper bound $x=1$. As a check, $x=0$ gives $u=5$, inside the original interval. The circles keep their types while switching their relative positions.

</div>
