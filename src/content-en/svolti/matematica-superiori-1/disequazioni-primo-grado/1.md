## Exercise 1

Solve $2x+8>24$ and show the solution set on a number line.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x+8>24&\iff 2x>16\\[6pt]
&\iff x>8.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=(8,+\infty).
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=5.7 xmax=12.1 ymin=-0.62 ymax=0.78 area="0.075;-0.075;8;11.7" vettori="6,0,11.8,0" punti="8,0,vuoto" etichette="8,-0.26,8;11.68,-0.26,x" titolo="The solution half-line extends to the right of $8$, with $8$ excluded."}

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract $8$ from both sides. To isolate $x$, divide by $2$, which is positive, so the symbol remains $>$. The highlighted interval continues to the right on the number line. The circle at $8$ is open because substitution gives $24$, exactly the value to be exceeded. To the right, for example at $9$, the result is $26>24$. To the left, $7$ gives $22<24$. These checks confirm the direction shown.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $5x-9\ge2x+18$ and check the endpoint.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x-9&\ge2x+18\\[6pt]
3x-9&\ge18\\[6pt]
3x&\ge27\\[6pt]
x&\ge9.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=[9,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The term $2x$ appears on the right. Subtracting it from both sides leaves $3x-9$ on one side and $18$ on the other. Adding $9$ leaves the term $3x$ alone. Dividing by $3$ preserves the sign, so the boundary is a lower bound. At $x=9$, both sides originally equal $36$, allowed by $\ge$. At $x=8$, the expressions are $31$ and $34$. Their difference $31-34=-3$ confirms that a number below the boundary is excluded.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $-3x\le30$ and check one value on each side of the boundary.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3x\le30&\iff 3x\ge-30\\[6pt]
&\iff x\ge-10.
\end{aligned}
\qquad S=[-10,+\infty).
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $x$ is negative. Multiplying by $-1$ reverses the sign and gives a positive coefficient. Dividing by $3$ preserves that new sign, so the allowed values are at least $-10$. Substituting $x=-10$ gives $30$. On the left, $x=-11$ gives $33$, above the limit. On the right, $x=-9$ gives $27$. This agrees with the fact that $-3x$ decreases by three units every time $x$ increases by one.

</div>
