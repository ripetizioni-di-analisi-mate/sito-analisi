## Exercise 1

What is the expansion of $(x+8)^2$?

$$
(x+8)^2=x^2+2\cdot8x+64=x^{2} + 16 x + 64
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of a sum contains the square of each term and twice their product. The doubled product is positive here. Include all three terms.

The result is $x^{2} + 16 x + 64$.

In the product $(x+8)(x+8)$, the linear term receives one contribution from $x\cdot 8$ and one from $8\cdot x$. Its coefficient is therefore $16$. When $x=1$, the original square is $9^2=81$ and the expanded sum is $1+16+64=81$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand $(x-9)^2$.

$$
(x-9)^2=x^2-18x+81
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the square of a difference, the doubled product is negative. The squares of the two terms remain positive. The constant term is therefore the square of the number subtracted.

The result is $x^{2} - 18 x + 81$.

The two mixed products are both $-9x$, because each contains one factor of $-9$. The constant term instead comes from the product of two negative factors, so it is $81$. At $x=9$, the three terms give $81-162+81=0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Expand $(2x+10)^2$.

$$
(2x+10)^2=4x^2+40x+100
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first term is twice x, so its square is four times x squared. The doubled product also contains that factor of two. The square of the second term completes the expansion.

The result is $4 x^{2} + 40 x + 100$.

The square of the first term is $(2x)^2=4x^2$. Twice the product is $2\cdot(2x)\cdot10=40x$, since it includes the coefficient of $x$. The constant term $100$ is the value of the square at $x=0$.

</div>
