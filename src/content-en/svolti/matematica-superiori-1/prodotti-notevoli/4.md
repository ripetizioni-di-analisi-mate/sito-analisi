## Exercise 1

Find $c$ so that $x^2+10x+c$ is a perfect square. For $x>0$, describe the area that completes the figure.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+b)^2&=x^2+2bx+b^2,\\[6pt]
2b=10&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=5,\\[6pt]
c&=25, & x^2+10x+25&=(x+5)^2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mixed term fixes $b$ as half of $10$. In the figure, distribute $10x$ between two rectangles of area $5x$ adjacent to the square of area $x^2$. The missing piece is a square with side length $5$, occupying $25$ units of area. The drawing assumes $x>0$ so the lengths $x$ and $5$ are positive. Expansion establishes the identity for all real inputs.

The completed form also reveals the minimum of the polynomial. It is zero and occurs when the new binomial vanishes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Complete $9y^2-24y+d$ to the square of a binomial. Find the value of $y$ at which the resulting square is zero.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3y+b)^2&=9y^2+6by+b^2,\\[6pt]
6b=-24&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=-4,\\[6pt]
9y^2-24y+16&=(3y-4)^2,\\[6pt]
3y-4=0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=\dfrac43 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first term comes from squaring $3y$. The constant in the binomial must therefore be recovered from $2\cdot3y\cdot b$. Halving $-24$ alone would give $-12$ and miss the factor $3$. Square the constant only once you have found $b=-4$. To find the zero, set $3y-4=0$ directly. At that point the three terms of the polynomial cancel as $16-32+16=0$.

The root visible in the factored form serves two purposes. It answers the second part and checks the coefficients reconstructed in the first.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find all real values of $q$ for which $4x^2+qx+9$ is a perfect square. Which choice makes the square zero at a positive $x$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x\pm3)^2&=4x^2\pm12x+9,\\[6pt]
(2x+3)^2=0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=-\dfrac32,\\[6pt]
(2x-3)^2=0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=\dfrac32 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The squares of the first and last terms fix the absolute values of the coefficients in the binomial. We can take $2x$ with a positive sign, because reversing the sign of the entire binomial leaves its square unchanged. This leaves $+3$ and $-3$, which give $q=12$ and $q=-12$, respectively. To make $2x+b$ zero with $x>0$, the constant $b$ must be negative. So the requested choice is $q=-12$.

The two polynomials agree at zero because they have the same constant term. Vanishing at a positive input distinguishes the two choices.

</div>
