## Exercise 1

What coefficient replaces $c$ so that $x^2+cx+64$ equals $(x+8)^2$ for every $x$?

$$
(x+8)^2=x^2+16x+64,\qquad c=16
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Expand the square of the binomial. The linear coefficient is twice the number added to x. For an identity, the corresponding coefficients in the two polynomials must agree.

The result is $16$.

The comparison can also be made after subtracting $x^2+64$ from both sides. This leaves $cx=16x$ for every $x$. In particular, at $x=1$, this gives $c=16$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which constant term completes $x^2-18x+\square=(x-9)^2$?

$$
(x-9)^2=x^2-18x+81
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The linear term already equals the negative doubled product. The square of the second term of the binomial is missing. That square is always nonnegative.

The result is $81$.

When $x=0$, the right-hand side is $(-9)^2=81$. The left-hand side also reduces to its constant term alone, so the missing value is $81$. The expansion confirms that the linear term is already correct.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A student writes $(x+10)^2=x^2+100$. Which term is missing on the right-hand side?

$$
x\cdot10+10\cdot x=20x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the binomial by itself gives two equal mixed products. The proposed expression contains only the squares, so the sum of the mixed products is missing.

The result is $20 x$.

The expansion contains $x\cdot10$ and $10\cdot x$. At $x=1$, the proposed result is $101$ and the square is $121$. The difference $20$ agrees with the term $20x$ evaluated at $x=1$.

</div>
