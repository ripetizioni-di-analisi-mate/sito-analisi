## Exercise 1

Simplify $(x+8)^2-(x-8)^2$.

$$
(x^2+16x+64)-(x^2-16x+64)=32x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Expand both squares, retaining the minus sign in front of the second. The quadratic and constant terms cancel. The linear terms add because the second doubled product is negative and is being subtracted.

The result is $32 x$.

The difference can also be calculated as the difference $(x+8)-(x-8)=16$ times the sum $(x+8)+(x-8)=2x$. This gives $32x$ from the same values.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $(x+9)^2+(x-9)^2$.

$$
(x^2+18x+81)+(x^2-18x+81)=2 x^{2} + 162
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two squares contain doubled products with opposite signs. These terms cancel in the sum. The squares of x and the number each occur twice.

The result is $2 x^{2} + 162$.

The coefficient of $x$ is zero because $18x$ and $-18x$ are opposites. At $x=0$, both squares are $81$, so the constant term of the sum must be $162$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The side length of a square increases from $x$ to $x+8$, where $x>0$. How much does its area increase?

$$
(x+8)^2-x^2=16x+64
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The increase in area is the final area minus the original area. Expand the square of the new side length. The x-squared term cancels in the difference.

The result is $16 x + 64$.

The difference of the squares of the side lengths is also the difference $8$ times the sum $2x+8$. Distributing $8$ gives $16x+64$. Both parts are positive for $x>0$.

</div>
