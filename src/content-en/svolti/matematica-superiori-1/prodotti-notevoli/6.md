## Exercise 1

Simplify $(x+4)^2-(x-4)^2$ and find the values of $x$ for which the first square is larger.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+4)^2-(x-4)^2
&=\big[(x+4)-(x-4)\big]\big[(x+4)+(x-4)\big]\\[6pt]
&=8\cdot2x=16x,\\[6pt]
16x>0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x>0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Applying the difference of squares to the binomials $x+4$ and $x-4$ reveals that one factor is fixed at $8$ and the other is $2x$. The difference therefore has the sign of $x$. For $x<0$ the second square is larger, and at $x=0$ both equal $16$. The figure shows the two parabolas crossing at $(0,16)$. At $x=1$ the gap is already $16$, since $25-9=16$.

Swapping the two squares would reverse the slope of their difference. Their point of agreement would stay at the crossing shown.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $(x+4)^2+(x-4)^2$. At which values of $x$ does the sum equal $50$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+4)^2+(x-4)^2&=x^2+8x+16+x^2-8x+16\\[6pt]
&=2x^2+32,\\[6pt]
2x^2+32=50&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^2=9\\[6pt]
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=3\ \text{ or }\ x=-3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Adding the two expansions cancels the linear terms because they are opposites. The constant is $32$, the value at $x=0$. Reaching $50$ requires another $18$ units, so $x^2$ must equal $9$. Changing the sign of $x$ swaps the squares. At $x=3$ their values are $49$ and $1$, while at $x=-3$ their values are $1$ and $49$.

A sum below $32$ would give no real solution, because the quadratic term being added is always nonnegative.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two squares have values $A=(x+b)^2$ and $B=(x-b)^2$. Given $A+B=34$ and $A-B=-16$, find $A$ and $B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(A+B)+(A-B)&=2A=34-16=18,\\[6pt]
(A+B)-(A-B)&=2B=34+16=50,\\[6pt]
A&=9, & B&=25 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Combining the two pieces of information determines the pair without knowing $x$ and $b$. The negative difference tells you in advance that $A$ is the smaller square. Adding the equalities eliminates the contributions from $B$, while subtracting them eliminates the contributions from $A$. One valid realisation uses $x=4$ and $b=-1$, which give $A=3^2$ and $B=5^2$ directly.

As a check on the given values, the absolute difference cannot exceed the sum of two nonnegative numbers.

</div>
