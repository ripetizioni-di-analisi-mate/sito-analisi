## Exercise 1

Calculate $18^2$ using $18=10+8$.

$$
(10+8)^2=100+160+64=324
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the number as the sum of ten and the other addend. The square also includes twice the product of the two addends. Add the three numerical contributions.

The result is $324$.

The calculation is also the area of a square of side length $18$. Dividing each side into $10$ and $8$ gives areas of $100$, $64$, and two regions each of area $80$. The four contributions total $324$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $108\cdot92$ using a standard product identity.

$$
(100+8)(100-8)=10000-64=9936
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two factors are one hundred plus the same number and one hundred minus it. Their product is a difference of squares. Subtract the square of the number from the square of one hundred.

The result is $9936$.

The sum and difference are taken with respect to the same base $100$, so the mixed products are $800$ and $-800$. The only contribution beyond $10000$ is $-64$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The side length of a square increases from $x$ to $x+10$, where $x>0$. How much does its area increase?

::struttura{linee="0,0 16,0 16,16 0,16 0,0" poligoni="16,0 26,0 26,16 16,16;0,16 16,16 16,26 0,26;16,16 26,16 26,26 16,26" riferimento=0 etichette="8.0,-1.2,x;21.0,-1.2,10;-1.15,8.0,x;-1.15,21.0,10" titolo="Areas added when the side length grows"}

$$
(x+10)^2-x^2=20x+100
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The increase in area is the final area minus the original area. Expand the square of the new side length. The x-squared term cancels in the difference.

The result is $20 x + 100$.

The side length increases by $10$. Each strip has area $10x$, and the corner region has area $100$. The three contributions make up the entire difference in area.

</div>
