## Exercise 1

Calculate $97^2$ mentally using $100$ as a reference. Predict the sign of its change from $100^2$ first.

$$
\begin{aligned}
97^2&=(100-3)^2\\[6pt]
&=10000-600+9=9409,\\[6pt]
100^2-97^2&=(100-97)(100+97)=3\cdot197=591 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $0<97<100$, the required square is below $10000$. Twice the product subtracts $600$ and the square of the offset adds $9$, giving a decrease of $591$. Check it by finding the difference of the two squares directly. The product $3\cdot197$ confirms the offset and rules out $9391$, which would come from subtracting $9$ as well. The expected last digit is $9$, the same as for $7^2$.

Combine the last-digit check with the size of the gap, since different candidate results can end in the same digit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Without long multiplication, compare $103\cdot97$ and $104\cdot96$. What is their difference?

$$
\begin{aligned}
103\cdot97&=100^2-3^2,\\[6pt]
104\cdot96&=100^2-4^2,\\[6pt]
103\cdot97-104\cdot96&=4^2-3^2=(4-3)(4+3)=7 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both pairs have midpoint $100$, but the first pair is offset by $3$ and the second by $4$. The first pair gives the larger product because it subtracts the smaller square. Subtracting the products cancels the $100^2$ terms, leaving the difference of two small squares. So $103\cdot97$ is $7$ larger. As a check, the two products are $9991$ and $9984$.

Reversing the order of the factors in a pair leaves its offset and therefore its product unchanged. The comparison uses the distance without a sign.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A square increases its side length by $3$ m and gains $45$ m$^2$ of area. Find the original side length.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+3)^2-x^2&=6x+9,\\[6pt]
6x+9=45&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=6\ \mathrm m,\\[6pt]
9^2-6^2&=81-36=45 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each added strip has dimensions $x\times3$. The corner joining them contributes another square of area $9$ m$^2$. Subtracting $9$ from the increase leaves $36$ m$^2$ for the strips, hence $18$ m$^2$ per strip. Divide by their width, $3$, to obtain the side length $6$ m, which satisfies $x>0$. The new side is $9$ m, and the difference of areas verifies the original data.

If the stated increase were only $9$ square metres, the strips would have zero area and there would be no positive original side length.

</div>
