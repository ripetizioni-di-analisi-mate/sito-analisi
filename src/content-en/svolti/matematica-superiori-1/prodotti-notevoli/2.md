## Exercise 1

Calculate $(x+8)(x-8)$.

$$
(x+8)(x-8)=x^2-64
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying a sum by the corresponding difference gives a difference of squares. The two mixed products have opposite signs and cancel. The square of the variable and the negative of the square of the number remain.

The result is $x^{2} - 64$.

The first term of both binomials is $x$, while their constant terms are opposites. The expansion therefore contains $-8x$ and $+8x$, which cancel. The constant term is $8\cdot(-8)=-64$, as can also be checked at $x=0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $109\cdot91$ using a standard product identity.

$$
(100+9)(100-9)=10000-81=9919
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two factors are one hundred plus the same number and one hundred minus it. Their product is a difference of squares. Subtract the square of the number from the square of one hundred.

The result is $9919$.

Both factors are $9$ away from $100$. The product is the square $100^2=10000$ reduced by $9^2=81$. It is therefore exactly $81$ less than $10000$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Expand $(3x+10y)(3x-10y)$.

$$
(3x)^2-(10y)^2=9x^2-100y^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Identify a sum and the corresponding difference. Square the entire first term, three times x, and the entire second term. The identity takes the difference of these two squares.

The result is $9 x^{2} - 100 y^{2}$.

The first square is $9x^2$ because the whole term $3x$ is squared. The second is $100y^2$. When $3x=10y$, the second original factor is zero and the two squares are equal.

</div>
