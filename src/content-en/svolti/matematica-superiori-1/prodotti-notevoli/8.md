## Exercise 1

A student writes $(x+8)^2=x^2+64$. Which term is missing on the right-hand side?

$$
x\cdot8+8\cdot x=16x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the binomial by itself gives two equal mixed products. The proposed expression contains only the squares, so the sum of the mixed products is missing.

The result is $16 x$.

When the binomial is multiplied by itself, both squares are already present in the given expression. Add $x\cdot8+8\cdot x=16x$. When $x=2$, the missing term is $32$ and changes the result from $68$ to $100$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand $(x+y+9)^2$.

$$
(x+y+9)^2=x^2+y^2+81+2xy+18x+18y
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of a sum of three terms contains the three squares and three doubled products. Each pair occurs once among the doubled products. Include all these contributions.

The result is $x^{2} + 2 x y + 18 x + y^{2} + 18 y + 81$.

In the expansion, the term $2xy$ comes from the products $x\cdot y$ and $y\cdot x$. At $x=0$, this term disappears along with every other term containing $x$. What remains is $y^2+18y+81$, the square of $y+9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Expand $(3x+8y)(3x-8y)$.

$$
(3x)^2-(8y)^2=9x^2-64y^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Identify a sum and the corresponding difference. Square the entire first term, three times x, and the entire second term. The identity takes the difference of these two squares.

The result is $9 x^{2} - 64 y^{2}$.

The mixed products $-24xy$ and $24xy$ cancel. Squaring the individual terms gives $9x^2-64y^2$. At $y=0$, only the square of the first term remains.

</div>
