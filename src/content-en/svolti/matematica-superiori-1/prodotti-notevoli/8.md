## Exercise 1

Recognize a square in $4a^2+b^2+9+4ab-12a-6b$. Check each of the mixed terms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2a+b-3)^2
&=(2a)^2+b^2+(-3)^2+2\cdot2a\cdot b+2\cdot2a\cdot(-3)+2\cdot b\cdot(-3)\\[6pt]
&=4a^2+b^2+9+4ab-12a-6b .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The simple squares identify the magnitudes of the three terms $2a$, $b$, and $3$. With $2a$ chosen positive, the coefficient $4$ of $ab$ requires a positive sign for $b$. The coefficient $-12$ of $a$ then requires $-3$. The remaining check is $-6b$, because that term connects the sign choices for $b$ and $-3$. The full expansion confirms that all constraints are satisfied.

Reversing all three signs gives the same expression. Reversing just one changes the signs of the two mixed terms that use it.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A student compares $(x+4)^2$ with $x^2+16$. Find all real values where they agree and explain how to decide whether they are identical.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+4)^2-(x^2+16)&=x^2+8x+16-x^2-16=8x,\\[6pt]
8x=0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=0,\\[6pt]
(x+4)^2\big|_{x=1}&=25, & (x^2+16)\big|_{x=1}&=17 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find all points of agreement by setting the difference to zero. Expansion reduces the difference to the mixed term $8x$, so there is a single point of agreement. Substituting $x=1$ gives a counterexample you can check without expanding. In the graph the vertical distance between the points at $x=1$ is exactly $8$, while the two graphs meet at height $16$ when $x=0$.

Showing that the agreement is unique requires computing the difference. The single counterexample settles the question about the identity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $x^2+6x+9-(x+3)(x-3)$ by first recognizing the two identities. For which $x$ is the result zero?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+6x+9-(x+3)(x-3)
&=(x+3)^2-(x+3)(x-3)\\[6pt]
&=(x+3)\big[(x+3)-(x-3)\big]\\[6pt]
&=6(x+3),\\[6pt]
6(x+3)=0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=-3 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original trinomial contains $x^2$, $3^2$, and twice their product $6x$, so it can be rewritten as $(x+3)^2$. The product being subtracted already contains $x+3$ and represents a difference of squares. Factoring out $x+3$ leaves the difference of the two binomials, equal to $6$ for every $x$. The only zero therefore comes from the common factor. At $x=-3$ both parts of the original expression vanish.

The same form shows that the difference is positive above the zero just found, since the constant factor is positive.

</div>
