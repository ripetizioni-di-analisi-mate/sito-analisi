## Exercise 1

Expand $\left(\dfrac{3x}{2}-\dfrac23\right)^2$. Explain why the mixed term has an integer coefficient.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\dfrac{3x}{2}-\dfrac23\right)^2
&=\left(\dfrac{3x}{2}\right)^2-2\cdot\dfrac{3x}{2}\cdot\dfrac23+\left(\dfrac23\right)^2\\[6pt]
&=\dfrac94x^2-2x+\dfrac49 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fractions $\dfrac32$ and $\dfrac23$ are reciprocals. In the mixed term their product is $1$, so the coefficient is exactly $-2$. In the separate squares each fraction is multiplied by itself, so the denominators are squared too. Check at $x=\dfrac49$, where the two terms inside the brackets each have magnitude $\dfrac23$ and the expansion must give zero.

At that value the expansion gives $\dfrac49-\dfrac89+\dfrac49=0$. The check uses all parts of the trinomial, including both denominators.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand $-\left(\dfrac{x}{3}-2\right)^2$ and $\left(-\dfrac{x}{3}+2\right)^2$. At which values of $x$ are they equal?

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\left(\dfrac{x}{3}-2\right)^2&=-\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac43x-4,\\[6pt]
\left(-\dfrac{x}{3}+2\right)^2&=\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac43x+4,\\[6pt]
\left(\dfrac{x}{3}-2\right)^2=0&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=6 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first expression the outside minus changes every sign of the expansion. The second expression squares the opposite of the first binomial, so the internal sign changes occur twice in the products. The expressions are opposites. A real number equals its own opposite only when it is zero. This occurs at $x=6$, where the graphs have their only common point.

For example, the vertical intercepts are $-4$ and $4$. The constant changes sign along with the other coefficients when the minus is placed outside.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $m$ so that $\dfrac{x^2}{4}+mx+\dfrac19$ agrees with $\left(\dfrac x2-\dfrac13\right)^2$ for every real $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\dfrac x2-\dfrac13\right)^2
&=\dfrac{x^2}{4}-2\cdot\dfrac x2\cdot\dfrac13+\dfrac19\\[6pt]
&=\dfrac{x^2}{4}-\dfrac13x+\dfrac19,\\[6pt]
m&=-\dfrac13 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two simple squares already fix the coefficient of $x^2$ and the constant. Only the linear term needs to be matched. The factor $2$ in twice the product cancels the denominator of $\dfrac x2$, leaving $-\dfrac13x$. Since agreement is required at every $x$, the value of $m$ is unique. To check that particular coefficient, substitute $x=2$, which makes the binomial equal to $\dfrac23$.

At the same point the expansion gives $1-\dfrac23+\dfrac19=\dfrac49$. At $x=0$ the term in $m$ would vanish, so the check could not distinguish its possible values.

</div>
