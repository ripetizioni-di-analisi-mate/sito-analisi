## Exercise 1

In the expansion of $(x-8)^2$, what is the coefficient of $x$?

$$
2\cdot x\cdot(-8)=-16x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The linear term comes from the doubled product. Since the second addend is negative, the product is negative too. The required coefficient is therefore the negative of twice the number.

The result is $-16$.

Finding the coefficient requires only the linear term $2\cdot x\cdot(-8)$. The square of $x$ has degree two, and the square of $-8$ is constant. Neither changes the required coefficient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand $\left(\dfrac x2+9\right)^2$.

$$
\left(\frac x2+9\right)^2=\frac{x^2}4+9x+81
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of x divided by two has coefficient one quarter. In the doubled product the factor of two cancels its denominator. The linear coefficient is the second term of the binomial.

The result is $\dfrac{x^{2}}{4} + 9 x + 81$.

Squaring the fractional term divides $x^2$ by $4$. The doubled product is calculated as $2\cdot\dfrac{x}{2}\cdot9=9x$. When $x=2$, the expansion becomes $1+18+81=100$, the square of $1+9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Expand $(x-10)^2$.

$$
(x-10)^2=x^2-20x+100
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the square of a difference, the doubled product is negative. The squares of the two terms remain positive. The constant term is therefore the square of the number subtracted.

The result is $x^{2} - 20 x + 100$.

The negative sign occurs in both mixed products, which are each $-10x$. The constant term is $(-10)(-10)=100$. At $x=10$, the contributions $100$, $-200$, and $100$ add to zero.

</div>
