## Exercise 1

Expand $(x+2)^3$ starting from $(x+2)^2$. Which products give the term in $x^2$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+2)^3&=(x^2+4x+4)(x+2)\\[6pt]
&=x^3+2x^2+4x^2+8x+4x+8\\[6pt]
&=x^3+6x^2+12x+8 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two contributions to $x^2$ are $x^2\cdot2=2x^2$ and $4x\cdot x=4x^2$. The second already includes the mixed term of the square. In the original three brackets, the $x^2$ term comes from choosing $2$ from only one bracket, which can be done three ways. The linear contributions come from choosing $x$ from one bracket and taking $2$ from the other two. Each of the three products then contains $2^2=4$.

Choosing the same term from all three brackets gives the two outside contributions, each of which occurs once.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand $(2x-3)^3$. Predict the signs of the two mixed terms first.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x-3)^3&=(2x)^3+3(2x)^2(-3)+3(2x)(-3)^2+(-3)^3\\[6pt]
&=8x^3-36x^2+54x-27 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient of $x^2$ is negative because its product contains only one factor of $-3$. The coefficient of $x$ is positive because two factors of $-3$ give $9$. The polynomial is zero at $x=\dfrac32$, just as the binomial is. The graph crosses the axis there because the cube has the same sign as the binomial. Its vertical intercept is $-27$, as given by the constant term.

The cube form also gives a check at $x=1$, where $(-1)^3=-1$. The expansion gives $8-36+54-27=-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $(x+1)^3-(x-1)^3$ and find its minimum value as $x$ varies over the reals.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x+1)^3-(x-1)^3&=(x^3+3x^2+3x+1)-(x^3-3x^2+3x-1)\\[6pt]
&=6x^2+2\ge 2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the second cube cancels the $x^3$ and $x$ terms because their coefficients match. The $x^2$ terms have opposite signs in the two cubes, so the subtraction adds them. The two constants also end up adding. In the resulting form the only variable term is $6x^2$, which is nonnegative. It vanishes at $x=0$, so the minimum is exactly $2$.

The difference has equal values at $x=1$ and $x=-1$. This symmetry follows because only an even power of positive degree remains.

</div>
