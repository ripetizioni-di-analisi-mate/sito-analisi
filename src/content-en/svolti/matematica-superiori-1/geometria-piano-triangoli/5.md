## Exercise 1

Two sides of a triangle measure $8$ cm and $10$ cm. Which interval gives the allowed lengths in centimeters for the third side?

$$
|10-8|<c<8+10,\qquad2<c<18
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A side is shorter than the sum of the other two sides and longer than the absolute value of their difference. Equality would place the vertices on a line, so both endpoints are excluded. Apply both inequalities to the unknown length.

The result is $\left(2, 18\right)$.

If the third side measured $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, the shorter sides would sum to $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, equal to the longest side. If it measured $18\,\mathrm{cm}$, it would equal the sum $8+10$. In both cases the vertices would be collinear.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isosceles triangle is supposed to have perimeter $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and base $12\,\mathrm{cm}$. Are these measurements compatible?

$$
\begin{aligned}
2l&=(20-12)\,\mathrm{cm}=8\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
l&=4\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
12&>4+4
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perimeter requires equal sides of $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. The base would be the longest side, but it exceeds the sum of the other two. The triangle inequality therefore fails, so the measurements are incompatible.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two sides measure $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $7\,\mathrm{cm}$. How many integer lengths in centimeters are possible for the third side?

$$
\begin{aligned}
|7-5|&<c<7+5 \\[6pt]
2&<c<12 \\[6pt]
c&\in\{3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10,11\}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The third side must exceed the difference $2$ and remain below the sum $12$. Both endpoints are excluded because they would make the vertices collinear. There are $11-3+1=9$ integers from $3$ to $11$, inclusive.

</div>
