## Exercise 1

Two sides measure $7$ cm and $11$ cm. The third side has an integer length in centimetres. Find the minimum and maximum perimeters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
11-7&<c<11+7,\\[6pt]
4&<c<18,\\[6pt]
P_{\min}&=7+11+5=23\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
P_{\max}&=7+11+17=35\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting and adding the two given lengths gives the ends of the open interval. Since the side length is an integer, the smallest possible value is $5$ and the largest is $17$. The endpoints $4$ and $18$ would make the longest side equal to the sum of the others. The perimeter increases with the third side since the first two contribute a fixed amount. So both extreme perimeters occur in actual triangles.

With real-valued lengths the perimeters could approach $22$ and $36$ without attaining them.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A proposed triangle has perimeter $35$ cm and sides of $8$ cm, $x$ cm, and $2x$ cm. Can it be built?

$$
\begin{aligned}
8+x+2x&=35,\\[6pt]
3x&=27,\qquad x=9,\\[6pt]
2x&=18>8+9=17.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perimeter equation gives a single candidate. The resulting side lengths are positive and do add to $35$ cm. The geometric condition still needs checking. The longest side would measure $18$ cm, and the other two span only $17$ cm together. Starting from the endpoints of the longest side, the two shorter segments cannot meet at a common point. The equation is solvable, while the proposed construction is impossible.

Predict the problem using $2x<x+8$. This necessary condition requires $x<8$ even before using the perimeter.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Construct a triangle with $AB=8$ cm and $AC=BC=5$ cm. How many positions can $C$ have relative to the fixed segment $AB$? How many triangles are there up to congruence?

$$
\begin{aligned}
0=|5-5|&<8<5+5=10,\\[6pt]
ABC&\cong ABC'.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Draw two circles of radius $5$ cm, one centred at $A$ and one at $B$. The inequalities are strict, so the circles meet at two different points, one in each half-plane of line $AB$. Each point is $5$ cm from both endpoints. The two drawings have all their corresponding sides congruent, so SSS makes them one triangle up to congruence. Reflecting across line $AB$ takes one to the other.

At a distance of $10$ cm between the centres, tangency would give a single common point on the segment.

</div>
