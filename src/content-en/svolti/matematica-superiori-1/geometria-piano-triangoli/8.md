## Exercise 1

On one ray, the points are in order $A,B_1,H,B_2$, with $AH=6$ and $HB_1=HB_2=3$. The perpendicular at $H$ contains a point $C$ with $HC=4$. Show that triangles $AB_1C$ and $AB_2C$ match in two corresponding sides and one angle, and compare their perimeters.

$$
\begin{aligned}
HB_1C&\cong HB_2C,\qquad CB_1=CB_2,\\[6pt]
\widehat{B_1AC}&=\widehat{B_2AC},\\[6pt]
AB_1&=6-3=3,\qquad AB_2=6+3=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two right triangles around $H$ are congruent by SAS, because they share $HC$, have $HB_1=HB_2$, and have an included right angle. Hence sides $CB_1$ and $CB_2$ are congruent. The triangles in question have those sides, the shared side $AC$, and the shared angle at $A$. The angle included between the given sides would be at $C$. The different bases give different perimeters and supply the counterexample.

A reflection across the perpendicular through $H$ swaps points $B_1$ and $B_2$ but also moves $A$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two triangles have angles of $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$, and each has a side of $6$ cm. In the first triangle the given side is opposite $30^\circ$, while in the second it is opposite $90^\circ$. Are they congruent?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{CAC'}&=30^\circ+30^\circ=60^\circ,\\[6pt]
CC'&=CB+BC'=6+6=12\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
AC&=CC'=12\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first triangle put the right angle at $B$ and the $30^\circ$ angle at $A$. The given side is $BC=6$ cm. Reflecting $C$ across $AB$ produces $C'$, with $B$ between $C$ and $C'$ because $BC$ is perpendicular to the reflection line. Triangle $ACC'$ has two $30^\circ$ angles at $A$ making the vertex angle and has $60^\circ$ angles at the other vertices. It is equilateral, so its base of $12$ cm matches the hypotenuse $AC$. In the second triangle the side opposite the right angle measures $6$ cm. Different hypotenuse lengths rule out congruence.

The shared angle measures determine the shape, and the given length determines its scale.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Triangle $ABC$ is right at $B$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of side $BC$. Which segment is the median to $BC$, and which is the altitude to that side?

$$
\begin{aligned}
BM&=MC\quad\Longrightarrow\quad AM\text{ is the median},\\[6pt]
AB&\perp BC\quad\Longrightarrow\quad AB\text{ is the altitude}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both segments start at $A$, the vertex opposite side $BC$. The midpoint is $M$, so the median is $AM$. The right angle at $B$ makes $AB$ perpendicular to $BC$. Thus the foot of the altitude is $B$ itself and the altitude is the leg $AB$. In a nondegenerate triangle the midpoint of $BC$ lies inside the side. The two endpoints are therefore distinct.

The perpendicular side already identifies the foot, even before finding the midpoint.

</div>
