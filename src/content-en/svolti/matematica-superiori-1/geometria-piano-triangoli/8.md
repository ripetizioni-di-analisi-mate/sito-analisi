## Exercise 1

Two triangles each have perimeter $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. In each triangle two sides measure $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Do the data guarantee congruence?

$$
c=(18-4-6)\,\mathrm{cm}=8\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perimeter and the first two lengths determine the third. The lengths are compatible because $8<4+6$. Each triangle therefore has sides of $4$, $6$, and $8$ centimeters. The SSS criterion guarantees congruence.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two equilateral triangles have sides of $9$ cm and $18$ cm, respectively. All of their angles measure $60^\circ$. Are the triangles congruent?

- No, because corresponding sides have different lengths
- Yes, because three equal corresponding angles suffice
- Yes, because both triangles are equilateral
- The altitudes are needed to decide

<div class="spiegazione">

Congruence requires equal corresponding lengths. Here the side of the second triangle is twice that of the first. Equal angles alone do not determine the size.

The triangles are not congruent because corresponding sides have different lengths.

The perimeters differ as well. The first is $27\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the second is $54\,\mathrm{cm}$. This difference rules out an exact match.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In triangle $ABC$, $AB=AC$. The point $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$. Prove that $AM$ is perpendicular to $BC$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-3 xmax=3 ymin=-1 ymax=4 linee="-2,0 2,0 0,3 -2,0" segmenti="0,3,0,0" punti="0,3,pieno,A;-2,0;2,0,pieno,C;0,0" etichette="-2.25,-0.35,B;0,-0.35,M" titolo="Segment AM divides the triangle into two"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\triangle ABM&\cong\triangle ACM \\[6pt]
\widehat{AMB}&=\widehat{AMC} \\[6pt]
\widehat{AMB}+\widehat{AMC}&=180^\circ \\[6pt]
\widehat{AMB}&=\widehat{AMC}=90^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Sides $AB$ and $AC$ are congruent by hypothesis. The midpoint gives $BM=CM$, and $AM$ is shared. By SSS, triangles $ABM$ and $ACM$ are congruent, so their angles at $M$ are congruent. They also form a linear pair because $B,M,C$ are collinear. Each therefore measures $90^\circ$, and $AM$ is perpendicular to $BC$.

</div>
