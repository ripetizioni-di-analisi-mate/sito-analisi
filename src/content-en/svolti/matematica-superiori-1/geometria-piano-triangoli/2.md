## Exercise 1

An angle measures $38^\circ$. What is the measure of its complement?

$$
\beta=90^\circ-38^\circ=52^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Complementary angles sum to a right angle. Subtract the given measure from ninety degrees. The result is positive because the given angle is acute.

The result is $52^\circ$.

The check is $38^\circ+52^\circ=90^\circ$. A value of $142^\circ$ would complete a straight angle instead.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An angle measures $98^\circ$. What is the measure of its supplement?

$$
\beta=180^\circ-98^\circ=82^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Supplementary angles sum to a straight angle. The unknown and given measures add to one hundred eighty degrees. Their difference from the total gives the required measure.

The result is $82^\circ$.

The $98^\circ$ angle is obtuse and the $82^\circ$ angle is acute. Their sum is exactly $180^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two lines intersect. One angle measures $51^\circ$. What is the measure of its vertical angle?

$$
\beta=\alpha=51^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sides of two vertical angles lie on the same lines and point in opposite directions. Each angle is supplementary to the same adjacent angle. They therefore have the same measure.

The result is $51^\circ$.

The angle adjacent to the given angle measures $180^\circ-51^\circ=129^\circ$. Its other adjacent angle is the required vertical angle, so it measures $180^\circ-129^\circ=51^\circ$.

</div>
