## Exercise 1

In $ABC$, $AB=AC$. Take interior points $D$ on $AB$ and $E$ on $AC$ with $AD=AE$. Prove that $BE=CD$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB&=AC,\qquad AE=AD,\\[6pt]
\widehat{BAE}&=\widehat{CAD},\\[6pt]
ABE&\cong ACD,\qquad BE=CD.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To prove the claim, look for two triangles containing the sides to be compared. In triangles $ABE$ and $ACD$, both pairs of given sides share the endpoint $A$. The rays bounding the angle are the same, since $D$ lies on $AB$ and $E$ lies on $AC$. The included angle is therefore the full vertex angle in both triangles. SAS gives congruence, and $BE$ matches $CD$.

The triangles overlap in the figure. Keeping the vertex order helps identify the matching side in the drawing as well.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A student claims that two triangles with equal perimeters must be congruent. Check the claim using side lengths $3,4,5$ and $4,4,4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5&<3+4,\qquad 4<4+4,\\[6pt]
3+4+5&=4+4+4=12.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First check that both triples can form triangles. In each triple, the longest side is shorter than the sum of the other two. The perimeters agree, but the first triangle has a side of length $5$ and the second has every side of length $4$. This is enough to disprove the claim. Congruence implies equal perimeters because every pair of corresponding sides is equal. The two examples show that the reverse implication can fail.

Renaming the vertices or moving them rigidly leaves the three side lengths of each triangle unchanged.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Triangles $ABC$ and $ADC$ satisfy $AB=AD$ and $BC=DC$. Ray $AC$ is inside $\widehat{BAD}$. Prove that it divides this angle into two congruent angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB&=AD,\quad BC=DC,\quad AC=AC,\\[6pt]
ABC&\cong ADC,\\[6pt]
\widehat{BAC}&=\widehat{DAC}=\widehat{CAD}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two given equalities establish two pairs of sides, and the third side $AC$ belongs to both triangles. After applying SSS, match $B$ with $D$ and keep $A$ and $C$ fixed. The corresponding angles at $A$ are congruent. The assumption about the angle's interior matters in the last step. Since $AC$ lies between the two rays bounding $\widehat{BAD}$, equality of the parts is exactly the bisector condition.

The ray opposite $AC$ would lie on the same line but leave the angle. The claim concerns precisely the interior ray.

</div>
