## Exercise 1

Each of two triangles has sides of length $10$ cm and $12$ cm with included angle $46^\circ$. Which criterion guarantees congruence?

- Side-side-side, because three sides are known
- Side-angle-side, with the included angle between the given sides
- Angle-side-angle, because two angles are known
- No criterion, even though the stated angle is included

<div class="spiegazione">

The two pairs of corresponding sides have equal lengths. The angle between those two sides is also equal in each triangle. These are exactly the hypotheses of the side-angle-side criterion.

The criterion is side-angle-side, with the included angle between the given sides.

The $46^\circ$ angle is at the vertex shared by the $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ sides. Starting at that vertex makes the correspondence of the sides explicit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Each of two triangles has a side of length $12$ cm, with adjacent angles of measures $37^\circ$ and $57^\circ$. Which criterion applies?

- Side-side-side
- Side-angle-side
- Angle-side-angle
- The given information does not guarantee congruence

<div class="spiegazione">

The known side lies between the two given angles. Both angle measures and the side length match in the two triangles. These data satisfy the angle-side-angle criterion.

The criterion is angle-side-angle.

The endpoints of the $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ side match by their angles, $37^\circ$ and $57^\circ$. The remaining angle is $86^\circ$ in both triangles, but the given side and its two adjacent angles already satisfy the criterion.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two triangles each have side lengths $11$, $12$, and $13$ cm. Which criterion proves congruence?

- Angle-side-angle, because two angles are given
- Side-angle-side, because an angle is given
- Equal perimeters alone, without using the side lengths
- Side-side-side

<div class="spiegazione">

The three lengths satisfy the triangle inequality. All three pairs of corresponding sides have equal lengths. The side-side-side criterion therefore guarantees congruence.

The criterion is side-side-side.

Since $13<11+12$, the three lengths can form a triangle. Match the shortest side in one triangle to the shortest in the other, the middle to the middle, and the longest to the longest.

</div>
