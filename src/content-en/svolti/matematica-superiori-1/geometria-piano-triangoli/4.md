## Exercise 1

An isosceles triangle has vertex angle $64^\circ$. What is the measure of each base angle?

$$
\beta=\frac{180^\circ-64^\circ}2=58^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The base angles of an isosceles triangle are congruent. Their sum is one hundred eighty degrees minus the vertex angle. Divide this sum by two.

The result is $58^\circ$.

The two $58^\circ$ angles complete the vertex angle because $64^\circ+2\cdot58^\circ=180^\circ$. The division by two applies to the total available for the base angles.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One base angle of an isosceles triangle measures $44^\circ$. What is the measure of the vertex angle?

$$
\alpha=180^\circ-2\cdot44^\circ=92^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The other base angle has the same measure as the known one. The two base angles therefore use twice the given measure. The vertex angle brings the total to one hundred eighty degrees.

The result is $92^\circ$.

The two known angles together use $88^\circ$. The vertex angle is $92^\circ$, so the triangle is obtuse.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An isosceles triangle has perimeter $64$ cm and base $16$ cm. What is the length of each of the equal sides?

$$
2l=64-16=48,\qquad l=24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the base from the perimeter leaves the sum of the two equal sides. Since the lengths are equal, each is half that sum. The result also satisfies the triangle inequality.

The result is $24\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The perimeter check gives $16+24+24=64$. For the triangle inequality, it is enough to check a longest side of $24$, which is shorter than $24+16$.

</div>
