## Exercise 1

The points $A,B,C$ lie on a line in the stated order. Suppose that $AC=40$ cm and $AB=16$ cm. What is the length of $BC$?

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-3 xmax=43 ymin=-2 ymax=2 linee="0,0 40,0" punti="0,0;16,0;40,0" etichette="0,-0.5,A;16,-0.5,B;40,-0.5,C;8,0.40,16 cm;28,0.40,BC" titolo="B divides the 40 cm segment AC"}

$$
BC=AC-AB=40-16=24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since B lies inside segment AC, the length of AC is the sum of AB and BC. Subtract AB from the total length. The order of the points is needed to choose this relation.

The result is $24\,\mathrm{cm}$.

As a check, the two parts give $16+24=40$. If $C$ were between $A$ and $B$ instead, the relation would be $AB=AC+CB$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

$M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $AM=27$ cm. What is the length of $AB$?

$$
AB=AM+MB=27+27=54
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The midpoint divides the segment into two congruent parts. The length of MB therefore equals the length of AM. The whole segment is twice either half.

The result is $54\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Because the midpoint lies on the segment, its two distances can be added. Equal distances from $A$ and $B$ alone hold for every point on the perpendicular bisector.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The points $A,M,B,C$ lie on a line in that order. The point $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, $MC=21\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $BC=7\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find $AB$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
MB&=MC-BC=(21-7)\,\mathrm{cm}=14\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
AB&=2MB=28\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The point $B$ lies between $M$ and $C$, so $MC$ consists of $MB$ and $BC$. This gives $MB=14\,\mathrm{cm}$. Since $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$, it also gives $AM=14\,\mathrm{cm}$. The length $AB$ is therefore $28\,\mathrm{cm}$. The full segment checks as $14+14+7=35$ centimeters, and $AC=AM+MC=35\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
