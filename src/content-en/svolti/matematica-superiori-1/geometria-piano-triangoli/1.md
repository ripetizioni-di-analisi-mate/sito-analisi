## Exercise 1

On segment $AC$, the points are in order $A,M,B,C$. Suppose $AC=37$ cm, $AB=14$ cm, and $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$. Find $BC$ and $MC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC&=AC-AB=37-14=23\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
MB&=\dfrac{AB}{2}=7\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
MC&=MB+BC=7+23=30\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The order of the points determines which lengths to add and which to subtract. To get the length from $B$ to $C$, take the whole segment and remove $AB$. Then apply the midpoint definition to $AB$, giving $MB=7$ cm. Segment $MC$ consists of $MB$ and $BC$. As an independent check, going from $A$ to $C$ along $AM$ and $MC$ gives $7+30=37$ cm.

Take half of the part ending at $B$, because the stated midpoint belongs to that segment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The points $A,B,C$ are collinear, with $AB=8$ cm and $AC=5$ cm. Find all possible lengths of $BC$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C\in\overline{AB}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad BC=8-5=3\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
A\in\overline{BC}&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad BC=8+5=13\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The line through the three points contains two opposite rays starting at $A$. If $C$ is on the same ray as $B$, the comparison $AC<AB$ places it between $A$ and $B$. Then subtract the shorter length. On the opposite ray, $A$ lies between $B$ and $C$, and the required segment has the sum of the two lengths. Both orders satisfy the data. The third possibility, with $B$ between $A$ and $C$, would require $AC>AB$.

Collinearity is essential to the list of just two lengths. A point off the line could give intermediate distances.

</div>

## Exercise 3

$M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $N$ the midpoint of $AM$. If $MN=12$ cm, what is the length of $AB$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
AM&=AN+NM=2MN=24\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
AB&=AM+MB=2AM=48\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Apply the midpoint definition first to the shorter segment, $AM$, because $MN$ is one known half. Then $AM$ is itself half of $AB$. The factor of two applies twice, giving an overall factor of four. Check using the order $A,N,M,B$. The first two pieces are $12$ cm each and the third is $24$ cm, for a sum of $48$ cm.

Reversing the question, take one quarter of the entire segment's length to recover the length of $MN$.

</div>
