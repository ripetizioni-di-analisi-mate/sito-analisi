## Exercise 1

In $ABC$, $\widehat A=72^\circ$ and $\widehat B=63^\circ$. The bisector from $A$ meets $BC$ at $D$. Find $\widehat{ADB}$ and determine whether $AD$ is an altitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{BAD}&=\dfrac{72^\circ}{2}=36^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{ADB}&=180^\circ-36^\circ-63^\circ=81^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{ADC}&=180^\circ-81^\circ=99^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bisector condition concerns the angle at $A$. In the smaller triangle $ABD$, the angle at $B$ remains $63^\circ$ because $D$ lies on $BC$. Subtracting those two angles from the interior angle sum gives $81^\circ$ at $D$. The linear pair at $D$ therefore measures $81^\circ$ and $99^\circ$, both different from $90^\circ$. An altitude would have to be perpendicular to $BC$, a condition that splitting the vertex angle alone does not guarantee.

The third angle of the original triangle is $45^\circ$. The two base angles differ, so their opposite sides differ as well.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The exterior angle of $ABC$ at $C$ measures $128^\circ$, and $\widehat A=42^\circ$. Find the angles at $B$ and $C$. Classify the triangle by its angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat C&=180^\circ-128^\circ=52^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat B&=128^\circ-42^\circ=86^\circ,\\[6pt]
42^\circ+86^\circ+52^\circ&=180^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The exterior angle at $C$ forms a linear pair with the interior angle at $C$, so they add to $180^\circ$. The same exterior angle equals the sum of the interior angles at $A$ and $B$. All three resulting angles are positive and below $90^\circ$, so the triangle is acute. The measure above $90^\circ$ belongs to the exterior sector. Using it to classify the triangle would confuse the sector on the extension with the one between the sides.

The exterior measure enters both the local calculation at $C$ and the angle sum at the other two vertices.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In triangle $ABC$, $\widehat B=118^\circ$ and $\widehat C=27^\circ$. The altitude from $A$ meets line $BC$ at $H$. Where on line $BC$ does $H$ lie, and what are $\widehat A$ and $\widehat{BAH}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{ABH}&=180^\circ-118^\circ=62^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat{BAH}&=90^\circ-62^\circ=28^\circ,\\[6pt]
\widehat A&=180^\circ-118^\circ-27^\circ=35^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If $H$ were on the ray from $B$ through $C$, the angle at $B$ of right triangle $ABH$ would measure $118^\circ$. Together with the right angle it would already exceed $180^\circ$. The case $H=B$ is also impossible, since then $AB$ would be perpendicular to $BC$. The foot is therefore on the extension past $B$. Angles $\widehat{ABH}$ and $\widehat{ABC}$ form a linear pair. Once the first is known, the right triangle gives its complement at $A$.

Find the entire angle at $A$ separately using the interior angles of $ABC$. The exterior altitude defines a different sector.

</div>
