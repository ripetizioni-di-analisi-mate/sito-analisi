## Exercise 1

An angle bisector divides an angle of $64^\circ$ into two parts. How large is each part?

$$
\beta=\frac{64^\circ}2=32^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An angle bisector is the ray that divides an angle into two congruent angles. The two parts add to the original angle. Each part is therefore half the given angle.

The result is $32^\circ$.

The two $32^\circ$ angles share a vertex and a side. Their interiors are disjoint and their sum is $64^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two angles of a triangle measure $44^\circ$ and $52^\circ$. What is the measure of the third angle?

$$
\gamma=180^\circ-(44^\circ+52^\circ)=84^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interior angles of a triangle sum to one hundred eighty degrees. First add the two known measures. Subtract this sum from the measure of a straight angle to find the remaining angle.

The result is $84^\circ$.

The known angles sum to $96^\circ$. The third angle is therefore acute, just like the first two. Indeed, $44^\circ+52^\circ+84^\circ=180^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An exterior angle of a triangle measures $134^\circ$. One of its two nonadjacent interior angles measures $43^\circ$. What is the measure of the other one?

$$
\beta=134^\circ-43^\circ=91^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An exterior angle equals the sum of the two nonadjacent interior angles. Subtract the known interior angle from the exterior angle. The result is the measure of the other interior angle.

The result is $91^\circ$.

The interior angle adjacent to the exterior angle measures $46^\circ$ because they are supplementary. The three interior measures are therefore $46^\circ$, $43^\circ$, and $91^\circ$, summing to $180^\circ$.

</div>
