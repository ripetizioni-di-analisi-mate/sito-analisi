## Exercise 1

Let $p$ be “$15$ is odd” and $q$ be “$15$ is divisible by $4$.” Find the truth values of $p\lor q$, $p\land q$, and $p\land\neg q$.

$$
15=2\cdot7+1,\qquad 15=4\cdot3+3.
$$

With $p$ true and $q$ false, the results are **true**, **false**, **true**, in that order.

<div class="spiegazione">

The remainders show that $p$ is true and $q$ is false. The **disjunction** only needs $p$ to be true, so $p\lor q$ is true. The **conjunction** requires both statements to be true. The second requirement fails and makes $p\land q$ false. Before evaluating the last conjunction, find $\neg q$, which is true. Both statements in $p\land\neg q$ are now true, so this conjunction is also true.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Use a truth table to verify that $\neg(p\land q)$ is equivalent to $\neg p\lor\neg q$. Find a row where $\neg(p\land q)$ and $\neg p\land\neg q$ differ.

| $p$ | $q$ | $\neg(p\land q)$ | $\neg p\lor\neg q$ | $\neg p\land\neg q$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | F | F | F |
| T | F | T | T | F |
| F | T | T | T | F |
| F | F | T | T | T |

<div class="spiegazione">

Evaluate $p\land q$ before negating it. This conjunction is true only in the first row, so its negation is false only there. For the other expression, first negate $p$ and $q$ separately. At least one of these negations is true in every row except the first. **Equivalence** follows from agreement in all rows. The second row disproves the conjunction of the negations, since it has one true statement and one false one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Consider the integer statement “if $a$ and $b$ are both multiples of $3$, then $ab$ is a multiple of $9$.” Prove the implication, decide whether the converse holds, and write the contrapositive.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a=3k,\ b=3h&\ \Longrightarrow\ ab=9kh,\quad k,h\in\mathbb Z,\\[5pt]
9\mid(2\cdot 9),&\qquad 3\nmid2,\\[5pt]
9\nmid ab&\ \Longrightarrow\ (3\nmid a)\lor(3\nmid b).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying the two expressions shows that dividing $ab$ by $9$ gives the integer $kh$. The converse starts from a product divisible by $9$. When $a=2$ and $b=9$, this hypothesis holds, but the first factor fails to be a multiple of $3$, so the converse is false. The new hypothesis in the **contrapositive** is that $ab$ is not a multiple of $9$. The conclusion negates “both are multiples of $3$,” so at least one factor is not a multiple of $3$.

</div>
