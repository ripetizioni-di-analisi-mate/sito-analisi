## Exercise 1

Proposition $p$ states that $8$ is greater than $7$. Proposition $q$ states that $8$ is less than $6$. What is the truth value of $p\land q$?

- True, because one true proposition is enough
- False, because a conjunction requires both propositions to be true
- True, because p is true
- The information given is insufficient

<div class="spiegazione">

The comparisons show that $8>7$ is true and $8<6$ is false. The conjunction requires both conditions to be true, so it is false here. With the same truth values, the inclusive disjunction $p\lor q$ would hold because $p$ is true.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For a real number $x$, what is the negation of $x>9$?

- $x<9$
- $x\ge 9$
- $x\le 9$
- $x\ne 9$

$$
\neg(x>9)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x\le9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The real numbers that do not exceed $9$ are all the numbers less than $9$, along with $9$ itself. The negation is therefore $x\le9$. The choice $x<9$ misses $x=9$, which makes $x>9$ false and must therefore make its negation true.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the negation of “All $10$ students in the group submitted the assignment”?

- No student in the group submitted the assignment
- All the students submitted the assignment late
- Exactly one student in the group submitted the assignment
- At least one student in the group did not submit the assignment

<div class="spiegazione">

The universal statement holds only if all ten students submitted the assignment. One missing assignment makes it false. The number of students who failed to submit can therefore be anywhere from one to ten. “At least one” includes all these cases.

</div>
