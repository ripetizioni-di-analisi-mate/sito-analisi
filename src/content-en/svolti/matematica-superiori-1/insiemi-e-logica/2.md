## Exercise 1

Let $A=\{0;2;4\}$. Find its complement first in the universal set $U=\{0;1;2;3;4\}$ and then in $V=\{0;1;2;3;4;5;6\}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
U\setminus A&=\{1;3\},\\[5pt]
V\setminus A&=(U\setminus A)\cup\{5;6\}=\{1;3;5;6\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The **complement** starts with the stated universal set. In the first calculation remove $0$, $2$, and $4$ from $U$. In the second the starting set is $V$, which also contains $5$ and $6$. Both are outside $A$, so they remain in the new complement. To check, take the union of $A$ and its complement. This recovers $U$ in the first case and $V$ in the second. In each calculation the two parts are disjoint.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In $U=\{1;2;3;4;5;6;7;8\}$, let $A=\{1;2;3;4\}$ and $B=\{3;4;5;6\}$. Find $(A\cap B)^c$ and check the result by computing $A^c\cup B^c$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A\cap B&=\{3;4\}, & (A\cap B)^c&=\{1;2;5;6;7;8\},\\[5pt]
A^c&=\{5;6;7;8\}, & B^c&=\{1;2;7;8\},\\[5pt]
A^c\cup B^c&=\{1;2;5;6;7;8\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{tipo="due" zone="a,b,u" universo=1 elementi="a:1,2;ab:3,4;b:5,6;u:7,8" titolo="The complement excludes only the overlap"}

<div class="spiegazione">

First identify the overlap. Taking the **complement** means removing just that region from $U$. To check, reason by region. An element only in $A$ is outside $B$, and an element only in $B$ is outside $A$. An element outside both belongs to both complements. The three regions therefore make up $A^c\cup B^c$. This is **De Morgan’s law**. The diagram also shows why $A^c\cap B^c$ would give only $\{7;8\}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In $U=\{0;1;2;3;4;5;6;7;8\}$, let $A=\{0;2;4;6;8\}$, $B=\{1;2;3;6\}$, and $C=\{4;5;6;7\}$. Find $A\cap(B\cup C)$ and identify the regions containing its elements.

$$
\begin{aligned}
B\cup C&=\{1;2;3;4;5;6;7\},\\[5pt]
A\cap(B\cup C)&=\{2;4;6\},\\[5pt]
(A\cap B)\cup(A\cap C)&=\{2;6\}\cup\{4;6\}\\[5pt]
&=\{2;4;6\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{tipo="tre" zone="ab,ac,abc" universo=1 elementi="a:0,8;ab:2;abc:6;ac:4;b:1,3;c:5,7" titolo="The shaded regions lie in $A$ and in at least one of $B$ and $C$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The parentheses require us to take the union of $B$ and $C$ before selecting the elements in $A$. All the odd numbers on the first line are removed in the second. The **distributive law** allows the other order. Find the two overlaps with $A$ first and then take their union. The number $2$ lies in the overlap of $A$ and $B$ outside $C$, $4$ in the overlap of $A$ and $C$ outside $B$, and $6$ in the centre. The fact that $6$ occurs in both overlaps meets the requirement to be in at least one of $B$ and $C$.

</div>
