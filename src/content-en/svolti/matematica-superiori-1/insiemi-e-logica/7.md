## Exercise 1

What is the negation of “All $8$ students in the group submitted the assignment”?

- No student in the group submitted the assignment
- All the students submitted the assignment late
- At least one student in the group did not submit the assignment
- Exactly one student in the group submitted the assignment

<div class="spiegazione">

The statement says that every student meets the same condition of having submitted the assignment. It is false if one or more students failed to meet that condition. A student who turned in the assignment, even late, is consistent with the statement. The negation therefore requires at least one missing assignment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the negation of “There is an integer greater than $9$ with property P”?

- Every integer greater than 9 has property P
- Exactly one integer greater than 9 has property P
- There is an integer less than 9 with property P
- No integer greater than 9 has property P

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain contains only integers greater than $9$. The statement is true as soon as one such integer has property $P$. To make it false, every integer in that domain must lack the property. Statements about integers less than $9$ concern a different domain.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Proposition $p$ states that $10$ is greater than $9$. Proposition $q$ states that $10$ is less than $8$. What is the truth value of $p\land q$?

- False, because a conjunction requires both propositions to be true
- True, because one true proposition is enough
- True, because p is true
- The information given is insufficient

<div class="spiegazione">

Proposition $p$ is true because $10>9$. Proposition $q$ is false because $10$ is greater than $8$. Both parts of the conjunction $p\land q$ must hold, so $q$ being false makes the conjunction false. The row of the truth table with values true and false shows the same result.

</div>
