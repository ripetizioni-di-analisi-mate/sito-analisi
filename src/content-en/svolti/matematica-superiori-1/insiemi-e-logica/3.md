## Exercise 1

Two disjoint sets have $16$ and $24$ elements, respectively. How many elements are in their union?

$$
|A\cup B|=16+24=40
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the sets are disjoint, every element is counted in only one group. The intersection therefore has cardinality zero. The union formula gives $16+24-0=40$. Disjointness lets us use the sum alone, even without listing the elements.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a group of $45$ students, $27$ study English, $18$ study French, and $9$ study both languages. How many study at least one of the two languages?

$$
27+18-9=36
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $27$ English students and the $18$ French students both include the $9$ students who study both languages. Adding the group sizes gives $45$ and counts those students twice. Subtracting the $9$ shared students once gives $36$ distinct people. To check, $18$ students study only English and $9$ only French, for a total of $18+9+9=36$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a class of $60$ students, $40$ swim, play tennis, or do both. How many do neither?

$$
60-40=20
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $40$ students are the union of the swimmers and tennis players. A student who does neither sport is in the complement of that union. There are $60$ students in the class, so the complement has $20$ students. The check is $40+20=60$, which counts each student once.

</div>
