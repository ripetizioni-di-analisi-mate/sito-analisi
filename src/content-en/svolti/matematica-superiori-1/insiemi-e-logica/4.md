## Exercise 1

With $A=\{\varnothing;4\}$, decide which of $\varnothing\in A$, $\varnothing\subseteq A$, $\{4\}\in A$, and $\{4\}\subseteq A$ are true.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\varnothing &\in A, & \varnothing &\subseteq A,\\[5pt]
\{4\}&\notin A, & \{4\}&\subseteq A.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $\in$, compare the whole object with the elements listed inside $A$. The elements are $\varnothing$ and the number $4$. The set $\{4\}$ is absent from that list. With $\subseteq$, check every element of the set on the left. The only element of $\{4\}$ is $4$, which lies in $A$, while the empty set has no element that could violate inclusion. The two tests can yield different results even for the same object on the left.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $A$ be the even integers from $-3$ to $5$, inclusive. Let $B=\{-2;0;2;4\}$. Prove $A=B$ by proving inclusion in both directions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x\in A
&\ \Longrightarrow\ x=2k,\ k\in\mathbb Z,\quad -3\le2k\le5\\[5pt]
&\ \Longrightarrow\ -\tfrac32\le k\le\tfrac52
 \ \Longrightarrow\ k\in\{-1;0;1;2\}\\[5pt]
&\ \Longrightarrow\ x\in B,\\[5pt]
x\in B&\ \Longrightarrow\ x=2k,\quad k\in\{-1;0;1;2\}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ x\in A.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To show $A\subseteq B$, translate “even” into $x=2k$. Dividing both bounds by $2>0$ preserves the direction of the inequalities. The only integer values of $k$ lead to the four elements of $B$, so no element of $A$ lies outside $B$. In the other direction each of the four numbers is twice one of the displayed integer values and lies between the specified endpoints. Equality follows from **inclusion in both directions**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $A=\{1;5\}$ and $B=\{1;2;5;8\}$. What does $A\cap B=A$ tell us about the inclusion of $A$ in $B$? Decide whether it is proper.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x\in A&\ \Longrightarrow\ x\in A\cap B
 \ \Longrightarrow\ x\in B,\\[5pt]
2\in B\setminus A&\ \Longrightarrow\ A\ne B,\\[5pt]
A&\subsetneq B.
\end{aligned}
$$

::venn{tipo="incluso" nomi="B,A" zone="a" universo=0 elementi="a:2,8;ab:1,5" titolo="The number $2$ shows that $B$ also contains elements outside $A$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The first step holds for any element of $A$. Since $A$ equals the intersection, that element also meets the requirement of belonging to $B$. The argument works in the other direction at the set level. If $A\subseteq B$, every element of $A$ already satisfies both requirements for the intersection, so $A\cap B=A$. **Proper inclusion** also requires an element of $B$ outside $A$. The number $2$ supplies that evidence.

</div>
