## Exercise 1

Let $A=\left\{8, 9, 10\right\}$ and $B=\left\{10, 11\right\}$. What is $A\cup B$?

$$
A\cup B=\{8;9;10;11\}
$$

::venn{tipo="due" zone="a,ab,b" universo=0 elementi="a:8,9;ab:10;b:11" titolo="The union contains the elements in all three shaded regions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The element $10$ belongs to both $A$ and $B$. It appears once in the union because the union collects distinct elements. The numbers $8$ and $9$ belong only to $A$, and $11$ belongs only to $B$. The diagram separates these three regions, whose union has four elements.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $A=\left\{9, 10, 11\right\}$ and $B=\left\{11, 12\right\}$. What is $A\cap B$?

$$
A\cap B=\{11\}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Look for a number that appears in both $A$ and $B$. The number $12$ is excluded because it belongs only to $B$. The numbers $9$ and $10$ are excluded because they belong only to $A$. This leaves $11$, so the intersection has one element.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Let $A=\left\{10, 11, 12\right\}$ and $B=\left\{12, 13\right\}$. What is $A\setminus B$?

$$
A\setminus B=\{10;11\}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference $A\setminus B$ starts with set $A$. Remove $12$ because it also belongs to $B$. The numbers $10$ and $11$ remain because they occur only in $A$. Reversing the order of the sets would leave only $13$.

</div>
