## Exercise 1

Let $A=\{1;2;4\}$ and $B=\{2;3;5\}$. How many pairs in $A\times B$ have an even sum? How many have equal coordinates?

$$
\begin{aligned}
R&=\{(1;3);(1;5);(2;2);(4;2)\}, & |R|&=4,\\[5pt]
A\cap B&=\{2\}, & \{(a;b)\in A\times B\mid a=b\}&=\{(2;2)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A sum is even when both addends are even or both are odd. The two even elements of $A$ pair with the only even element of $B$, giving two pairs. The only odd element of $A$ pairs with the two odd elements of $B$, giving two more. The two families are disjoint. For **equal coordinates**, the shared number must belong to both sets, so find $A\cap B$. Only $2$ yields a pair with equal coordinates, which also occurs among the even sums.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a class of $25$, $12$ students choose photography, $11$ choose drawing, and $4$ choose both. Form an ordered pair with one representative who chose photography only and one who chose drawing only. How many pairs are possible, and how many students appear in none?

$$
\begin{aligned}
|F\setminus D|&=12-4=8,& |D\setminus F|&=11-4=7,\\[5pt]
|(F\setminus D)\times(D\setminus F)|&=8\cdot7=56,\\[5pt]
|U|-|(F\setminus D)\cup(D\setminus F)|&=25-(8+7)=10.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The word “only” requires removing those who chose both from each list before forming pairs. For each of the eight photography representatives there are seven choices for the drawing representative, so use a **Cartesian product**. The two sets are disjoint, so the same person cannot be assigned both roles. The ten excluded students are the four who chose both and the six who chose neither, because $12+11-4=19$ students chose at least one activity.

</div>

## Exercise 3

How many functions from $A=\{1;2;3\}$ to $B=\{0;1\}$ have image $B$? Explain why requiring all outputs to be different would give an incorrect count.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\{(f(1);f(2);f(3))\mid f:A\to B\}|&=2\cdot2\cdot2=8,\\[5pt]
(0;0;0),\ (1;1;1)&\ \Longrightarrow\ \textstyle{8-2=6}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each function is described by the three outputs in the order of inputs $1$, $2$, and $3$. These are independent choices, each between two values. Among the eight triples, remove only the two **constant triples**, since every other triple contains at least one $0$ and at least one $1$. Three distinct outputs would be impossible with a codomain of two values. A function requires each input to have a single output and allows different inputs to share one output.

</div>
