## Exercise 1

Calculate $2^7\cdot2^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^7\cdot2^2&=2^{7+2} \\[6pt]
&=2^9 \\[6pt]
&=512
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The power $2^7$ contains seven factors of $2$. The product adds the two factors from $2^2$, bringing the count to nine. The result can be checked by calculating $2^7=128$ and $2^2=4$ separately. The product is $128\cdot4=512$, which agrees with $2^9$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $3^9:3^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^9:3^2&=3^{9-2} \\[6pt]
&=3^7 \\[6pt]
&=2187
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the quotient as a fraction to identify the common factors. The numerator has nine factors equal to $3$ and the denominator has two. After canceling, seven factors remain in the numerator. To check, the product $3^7\cdot3^2$ again has nine factors equal to $3$, so it matches the dividend.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $(2^9)^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2^9)^2&=2^{2\cdot9} \\[6pt]
&=2^{18} \\[6pt]
&=262144
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outer exponent $2$ gives two factors equal to $2^9$. Each factor contains nine copies of $2$, so the product contains eighteen. This explains why the two exponents are multiplied. Calculating $(512)^2=262144$ directly gives the same value.

</div>
