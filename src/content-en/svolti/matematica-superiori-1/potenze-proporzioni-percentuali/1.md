## Exercise 1

Evaluate $2^7\cdot2^2$ and check the result using a second method.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^7\cdot2^2&=2^{7+2}\\[6pt]
&=2^9\\[6pt]
&=512.\\[6pt]
128\cdot4&=512.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first group contains seven factors of $2$ and the second contains two, giving nine in the product. The law applies because the bases agree and the operation between the powers is multiplication. For a separate check, evaluate $2^7=128$ and $2^2=4$. The second factor means doubling $128$ twice, which gives $256$ and then $512$. The two methods give the same result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $3^9:3^2$ and the quotient with numerator and denominator interchanged.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3^9}{3^2}&=3^{9-2}\\[6pt]
&=3^7=2187.\\[6pt]
\frac{3^2}{3^9}&=\frac{1}{3^{9-2}}=\frac{1}{2187}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since the base $3$ is nonzero, the two factors in the denominator can cancel with two of the nine in the numerator. Seven factors of $3$ therefore remain in the numerator. In the other quotient the nine factors are in the denominator, so $3^7$ remains there after cancellation. The two quotients are reciprocals. Multiplying $2187$ by $3^2$ restores the nine factors and checks the first result.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Evaluate $\dfrac{(3^2)^3\cdot 3}{3^3}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3^2)^3&=3^{2\cdot3}=3^6,\\[6pt]
3^6\cdot3&=3^7,\\[6pt]
\frac{3^7}{3^3}&=3^4=81.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.35 xmax=3.7 ymin=-0.45 ymax=8.4 nomi="s,n" punti="0,2,pieno;1,6,pieno;2,7,pieno;3,4,pieno" linee="0,2 1,6 2,7 3,4" titolo="The exponent $n$ takes the values $2$, $6$, $7$, and $4$ over the four steps $s$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The outer exponent $3$ counts three copies of the group $3^2$. There are six factors in all, so the nested power has exponent $6$. The separate factor $3$ is $3^1$, which gives seven factors in the numerator. Three of them cancel with the denominator. To check the order of the operations, note that $(3^2)^3=(3^3)^2$, so the quotient of those two parts is $3^3$. Multiplying by the remaining $3$ again gives $81$.

</div>
