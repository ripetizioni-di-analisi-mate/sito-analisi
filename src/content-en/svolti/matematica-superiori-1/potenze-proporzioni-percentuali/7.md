## Exercise 1

A straight-line distance measures $24\,\mathrm{cm}$ on a map at a scale of $1:50000$. Find the real distance. How long would a $15\,\mathrm{km}$ route measure on the map?

$$
\begin{aligned}
1\,\mathrm{cm}
&\;\longleftrightarrow\;50000\,\mathrm{cm}
=0.5\,\mathrm{km},\\[6pt]
24\cdot0.5\,\mathrm{km}&=12\,\mathrm{km},\\[6pt]
\frac{15}{0.5}\,\mathrm{cm}&=30\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A scale of $1:50000$ compares lengths in the same units. Since a kilometre contains $100000$ centimetres, each centimetre on the map represents half a kilometre. From the map length to the real length, multiply by $0.5$ to get kilometres. In the other direction, count how many half kilometres fit into the real distance of $15$ kilometres. There are $30$ of them, giving $30$ centimetres on the map. The greater real distance needs the greater map length, as the results confirm.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two people share $40$ euros in the ratio $2:3$. How much does the second person get, how much more than the first, and what percentage of the total is the difference?

$$
\begin{aligned}
q&=\frac{40}{2+3}=8,\\[6pt]
A&=2q=16,\qquad B=3q=24,\\[6pt]
B-A&=8,\qquad \frac{8}{40}\cdot100\%=20\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total contains five equal shares, so one share is worth $8$ euros. The two people receive two and three shares respectively, so the second receives exactly one extra share. When the difference is compared with the total, the one share out of five is $20\%$. Compared with the first amount, the same difference is $50\%$, since $8$ euros is half of $16$ euros. The question specifies the total as the basis for comparison.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangular drawing of $2\,\mathrm{cm}\times3\,\mathrm{cm}$ is enlarged without distortion. Its new short side measures $20\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the new long side and the ratio of the new area to the original area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac23&=\frac{20}{x},\qquad x>0,\\[6pt]
x&=\frac{3\cdot20}{2}=30,\\[6pt]
\frac{A_1}{A_0}
&=\frac{20\cdot30\,\mathrm{cm}^2}{2\cdot3\,\mathrm{cm}^2}
=100.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Enlargement without distortion preserves the ratio of the short side to the long side. The proportion can be read using the length factor $\dfrac{20}{2}=10$, so the long side goes from $3$ to $30$ centimetres. Area depends on both sides and each is multiplied by $10$. The area factor is therefore $10^2=100$. To confirm this, divide the $600$ square centimetres of the enlarged drawing by the $6$ square centimetres of the original drawing.

</div>
