## Exercise 1

A print shop charges a setup fee of $12$ euros and $2$ euros per copy. Calculate the prices of $4$ and $8$ copies and determine whether total cost is directly proportional to the number of copies.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C(n)&=12+2n,\\[6pt]
C(4)&=12+2\cdot4=20,\\[6pt]
C(8)&=12+2\cdot8=28,\\[6pt]
\frac{C(4)}4&=5,\qquad \frac{C(8)}8=3.5.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.8 xmax=9.5 ymin=-3 ymax=34 nomi="n,C" linee="0,12 8,28;0,0 6.4,32" punti="0,12,pieno;4,20,pieno;8,28,pieno" etichette="6.3,17,C(n);3.4,28,C(4)n/4" titolo="The actual cost and the direct proportion that would match the cost of four copies"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The bill is the sum of the setup fee and the variable cost. From four to eight copies the variable portion doubles, from $8$ to $16$ euros. The total rises by just $8$ euros, so it fails the doubling test for direct proportionality. The two different average charges, $5$ euros and $3.5$ euros per copy, confirm this. Dividing the difference between the total bills by the four additional copies recovers the marginal price of $2$ euros per copy.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Five workers at the same rate need $12$ hours to complete a job. Two workers leave the team after the first $4$ hours. How much working time remains?

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_\mathrm{tot}&=5\cdot12=60,\\[6pt]
L_\mathrm{rest}&=60-5\cdot4=40,\\[6pt]
t_\mathrm{rest}&=\frac{40}{3}\,\mathrm h
=13\,\mathrm h+20\,\mathrm{min}.
\end{aligned}
$$

| Phase | Workers | Duration | Person-hours |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First phase | 5 | 4 h | 20 |
| Second phase | 3 | 13 h 20 min | 40 |
| Entire job | — | 17 h 20 min | 60 |

<div class="spiegazione">

Measure total work in person-hours. At a constant individual rate, five workers for twelve hours complete $60$ person-hours. In the first phase, five workers complete $20$ person-hours in four hours. Divide the remaining $40$ person-hours among the three workers still present. The quotient is $13$, with one person-hour left over. The three workers finish that last person-hour in a third of an hour, or $20$ minutes. Including the first phase, the total duration is $17$ hours and $20$ minutes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A $750\,\mathrm m$ path measures $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ on the map. Determine the scale and the map length of a path measuring $1.25\,\mathrm{km}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
750\,\mathrm m&=75000\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\frac{3}{75000}&=\frac1{25000},\\[6pt]
1.25\,\mathrm{km}&=125000\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\frac{125000}{25000}\,\mathrm{cm}&=5\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale ratio is formed after converting the two distances to the same units. The real path is $75000$ centimetres long, so the simplified scale is $1:25000$. Each centimetre on the map therefore represents $250$ metres in reality. The second path covers $1250$ metres, or five lengths of $250$ metres, so it is drawn five centimetres long. As a check, the ratio of the real lengths is $\dfrac{1250}{750}=\dfrac53$, matching the ratio of the map lengths.

</div>
