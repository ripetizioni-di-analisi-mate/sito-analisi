## Exercise 1

A print shop charges $12$ euros for setup and $2$ euros per copy. Calculate the cost of $4$ copies and of $8$ copies. Is the cost directly proportional to the number of copies?

$$
\begin{aligned}
C(4)&=12+2\cdot4=20,\\[6pt]
C(8)&=12+2\cdot8=28.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
28\ne2\cdot20.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fixed charge is paid once, so it applies to four copies and to eight copies. Doubling the number of copies doubles only the variable cost, from $8$ to $16$ euros. Thus the total rises from $20$ to $28$ euros. Direct proportionality would require the total cost to double too, so these costs are not directly proportional.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Five workers working at the same rate take $12$ hours to finish a job. After the first $4$ hours, two workers leave. How many more hours will the job take?

$$
\begin{aligned}
5\cdot12 &= 60,\\[6pt]
5\cdot4 &= 20,\\[6pt]
\frac{60-20}{3} &= \frac{40}{3}.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\frac{40}{3}\,\mathrm h=13\,\mathrm h+20\,\mathrm{min}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Measure the job in hours of work by one worker to distinguish the finished work from the remaining work. Five workers over four hours have already completed $20$ of the $60$ hours needed. Since three workers remain, dividing $40$ by $3$ gives the time left. The fractional part is one third of an hour, or $20$ minutes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A trail of $750\,\mathrm m$ takes up $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ on a map. Find the scale and the length on the map of a trail that is $1.25\,\mathrm{km}$ long.

$$
\begin{aligned}
750\,\mathrm m&=75000\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
3:75000&=1:25000.
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.25\,\mathrm{km}&=125000\,\mathrm{cm},\\[6pt]
\frac{125000\,\mathrm{cm}}{25000}&=5\,\mathrm{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio that defines the scale compares lengths in the same unit. One centimeter on the map therefore represents $25000$ actual centimeters, or $250$ meters. The $1.25\,\mathrm{km}$ trail takes up five centimeters on the map. Indeed, at that scale, $5\cdot250=1250$ meters is the actual distance.

</div>
