## Exercise 1

An item priced at $160$ euros is discounted by $20\%$. Find the discount in euros, the final price, and the fraction of the price paid.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s&=\frac{20}{100}\cdot160=32,\\[6pt]
P_1&=160-32=128,\\[6pt]
\frac{P_1}{P_0}&=\frac{128}{160}=\frac45.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The discount gives the percentage to subtract from the original price. Since $20\%$ is one fifth, divide $160$ euros into five equal parts of $32$ euros. One part is the discount and the other four make up the amount paid. The factor $\dfrac45=0.8$ gives the final price in one calculation. Doubling the original price would double both the discount in euros and the amount paid, since the factor would stay the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An item costs $72$ euros after a $20\%$ discount. Find the original price and the discount in euros.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac45P_0&=72,\\[6pt]
P_0&=72\cdot\frac54=90,\\[6pt]
s&=90-72=18.
\end{aligned}
$$

::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="0,0 4,0 4,0.85 0,0.85" linee="0,0 5,0 5,0.85 0,0.85 0,0;1,0 1,0.85;2,0 2,0.85;3,0 3,0.85" etichette="2,1.08,72 €;4.5,1.08,18 €" titolo="Four known fifths, one missing fifth and five fifths for the full price"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The known price comes after the discount and represents four fifths of the original price. Each fifth is worth $72:4=18$ euros. Adding the missing fifth to $72$ euros therefore gets back to $90$ euros. The calculation $72\cdot1.20=86.40$ would use the discounted price as its baseline. The exact inverse factor of $\dfrac45$ is $\dfrac54$, equivalent to a $25\%$ increase over the discounted price. As a check, $20\%$ of $90$ is $18$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A deposit of $1000$ euros rises by $10\%$ and then falls by $10\%$ of the new amount. Find its final value and the overall percentage change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_1&=1000\cdot1.10=1100,\\[6pt]
P_2&=1100\cdot0.90=990,\\[6pt]
\frac{P_2-P_0}{P_0}\cdot100\%
&=\frac{990-1000}{1000}\cdot100\%=-1\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

| Stage | Baseline | Change | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Start | — | — | €1000 |
| After the increase | €1000 | +€100 | €1100 |
| After the decrease | €1100 | −€110 | €990 |

<div class="spiegazione">

The two changes use different baselines. The increase is $10\%$ of $1000$, or $100$ euros. The decrease is $10\%$ of $1100$, or $110$ euros. The changes differ by $10$ euros, leaving an overall loss of $1\%$. The product of the factors $1.10\cdot0.90=0.99$ gives the same information directly. Returning to the original value immediately after the increase would require a decrease of $\dfrac{100}{1100}\cdot100\%\approx9.09\%$.

</div>
