## Exercise 1

An item costs $160$ euros and is discounted by $20\%$. What is the final price?

$$
160\left(1-\frac{20}{100}\right)=128
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original price represents $100\%$. After subtracting $20\%$, the remaining share is $80\%$, so the factor is $\dfrac45$. The amount paid is $160\cdot\dfrac45=128$ euros. To check, calculate the $32$-euro discount separately and subtract it from the original price.

</div>

## Exercise 2

After a discount of $20\%$, the price is $72$ euros. What was the original price?

$$
P\cdot\frac45=72,\qquad P=72\cdot\frac54=90
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $72$ euros paid are four fifths of the original price $P$. Dividing $72$ by $4$ gives one fifth, or $18$ euros. The full price is therefore $5\cdot18=90$ euros. Taking $20\%$ of $90$ gives a discount of $18$ euros and a difference of $72$ euros.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An initial value of $1000$ increases by $10\%$ and then decreases by $10\%$ of the new value. What is the final value?

$$
1000\cdot\frac{11}{10}\cdot\frac9{10}=990
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first increase is $10\%$ of $1000$, bringing the value to $1100$. The decrease applies to $1100$ and equals $110$. Therefore the final value is $1100-110=990$. Both changes use the same percentage in absolute terms, while the second changes the value by more because its base is larger.

</div>
