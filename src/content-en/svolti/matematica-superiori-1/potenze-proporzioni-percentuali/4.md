## Exercise 1

$8$ notebooks cost $24$ euros. Find the cost of $40$ notebooks at the same unit price in two ways.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac{24}{8}=3,\\[6pt]
C&=3\cdot40=120.\\[6pt]
40&=5\cdot8,\\[6pt]
5\cdot24&=120.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unit price is the ratio of cost to the number of notebooks. The data give a ratio of $3$ euros per notebook. Since it stays constant, the cost of $40$ notebooks is $120$ euros. The second method uses the fact that $40$ notebooks are five groups of eight, each costing $24$ euros. The methods agree because the cost grows by the same factor as the item count. A fixed fee would change the ratio.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three identical machines take $36\,\mathrm h$ to complete a job. Find the duration with six identical machines sharing the same job, all working at the same rate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W&=3\cdot36=108,\\[6pt]
6t&=108,\\[6pt]
t&=18\,\mathrm h.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="108/x" xmin=1.6 xmax=8.5 ymin=-1 ymax=43 nomi="m,t" legenda="t=\dfrac{108}{m}" punti="3,36,pieno;6,18,pieno" segmenti="3,0,3,36;6,0,6,18" titolo="The duration $t$ is in hours. The number $m$ of machines is an integer"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Measure the total work in hours of one machine. Since three machines work for $36\,\mathrm h$ each, the total is $108$ machine-hours. With six machines it is split into six equal shares, so each works for $18\,\mathrm h$. The relationship has constant product $mt=108$. On the graph, $(3,36)$ and $(6,18)$ lie on the same curve. Doubling the machine count halves the duration because total work and the individual work rate are fixed.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A service charges a fixed fee plus the same price per visit. Two visits cost $14$ euros in total and four visits cost $20$ euros. Find the cost of six visits and determine whether the total cost is directly proportional.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k&=\frac{20-14}{4-2}=3,\\[6pt]
F&=14-2\cdot3=8,\\[6pt]
C(6)&=8+6\cdot3=26,\\[6pt]
C(0)&=8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the observed costs because both contain the same fixed fee. The difference of $6$ euros corresponds to two additional visits, so a visit costs $3$ euros. Subtracting the cost of two visits from the first total gives a fixed fee of $8$ euros. Six visits cost $26$ euros. The fee gives an intercept of $8$ euros, whereas direct proportionality would give a zero intercept. The ratios $\dfrac{14}{2}=7$ and $\dfrac{20}{4}=5$ also confirm that the average price varies.

</div>
