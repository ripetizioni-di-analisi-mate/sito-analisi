## Exercise 1

A tank with a capacity of $160$ litres is $25\%$ full. How many litres does it contain, and how many are needed to fill it?

$$
\begin{aligned}
25\%&=\frac{25}{100}=\frac14,\\[6pt]
\frac14\cdot160\,\mathrm L&=40\,\mathrm L,\\[6pt]
\left(1-\frac14\right)\cdot160\,\mathrm L
&=\frac34\cdot160\,\mathrm L=120\,\mathrm L.
\end{aligned}
$$

::struttura{riferimento=0 poligoni="0,0 1,0 1,1 0,1" linee="0,0 4,0 4,1 0,1 0,0;2,0 2,1;3,0 3,1" etichette="0.5,0.5,40 L;2.5,1.22,120 L empty" titolo="Each part represents one quarter of the 160-litre capacity"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The capacity of the tank is the starting total. A quarter of the capacity is $40$ litres, the volume already in the tank. The missing volume uses the complementary percentage, $100\%-25\%=75\%$. The same partition into quarters lets us count the three empty parts. The units and the total provide a check, since both volumes are given in litres and $40+120=160$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In the first class, $9$ of $45$ students attend a workshop. In the second class, $10$ of $20$ attend. Find the percentage in the first class and the overall percentage.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{9}{45}\cdot100\%&=20\%,\\[6pt]
\frac{10}{20}\cdot100\%&=50\%,\\[6pt]
\frac{9+10}{45+20}\cdot100\%
&=\frac{19}{65}\cdot100\%\approx29.23\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator counts all people in the group under consideration. The first class has $45$ students, so its $9$ participants make up one fifth, or $20\%$. To calculate the overall percentage, count all $19$ participants and all $65$ students. The simple average of $20\%$ and $50\%$ would give $35\%$, giving both classes the same weight. In the overall calculation every student carries the same weight, so the larger class contributes more and the result is closer to $20\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Initially $100$ out of $200$ students pass an exam. The number passing increases by $10\%$, with the same number of students taking it. Find the new number passing and the increase in pass rate in percentage points.

$$
\begin{aligned}
100\left(1+\frac{10}{100}\right)&=110,\\[6pt]
\frac{100}{200}\cdot100\%&=50\%,\\[6pt]
\frac{110}{200}\cdot100\%&=55\%,\\[6pt]
55-50&=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $10\%$ increase is measured against the original $100$ passes. Adding $10$ passes gives a new total of $110$. The denominator for the pass rate is the full set of $200$ students, which stays constant. Against this baseline, the ten additional passes are worth five percentage points. As a check, the rate goes from $50\%$ to $55\%$, and its relative increase is $\dfrac{55-50}{50}=0.10$, the same as for the number of passes.

</div>
