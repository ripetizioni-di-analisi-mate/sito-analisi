## Exercise 1

Calculate $25\%$ of $160$.

$$
\frac{25}{100}\cdot160=40
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The notation $25\%$ means twenty-five parts out of a hundred. The fraction $\dfrac{25}{100}$ reduces to $\dfrac14$, so the required part is $160:4=40$. Four parts of this size give the whole again, since $4\cdot40=160$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a class of $45$ students, $9$ take part in a workshop. What percentage of the class takes part?

$$
\frac{9}{45}\cdot100=20
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The reference group is the entire class, so the denominator is $45$. The nine participants make up $\dfrac9{45}=\dfrac15$ of the class. One fifth is $\dfrac{20}{100}$ in hundredths, which is $20\%$. As a check, $45\cdot\dfrac{20}{100}=9$ gives the number of participants.

</div>

## Exercise 3

An initial quantity of $100$ increases by $10\%$. What is the new value?

$$
100+\frac{10}{100}\cdot100=110
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The starting value $100$ is the base of the comparison. The amount added is $\dfrac{10}{100}\cdot100=10$, so the final value is $100+10=110$. Multiplying by $1+\dfrac{10}{100}=1.1$ gives the same result.

</div>
