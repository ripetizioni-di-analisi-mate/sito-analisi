## Exercise 1

Find the opposite of $P(x)=-3x^2+x-6$ and verify it by addition.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-P(x)&=(-1)(-3x^2+x-6)\\[6pt]
&=3x^2-x+6,\\[6pt]
P(x)+(-P(x))&=(-3+3)x^2+(1-1)x+(-6+6)=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The opposite is the polynomial that gives zero when added to $P$. Multiplying every term by $-1$ produces that polynomial because opposite coefficients cancel within each group of like terms. The original linear coefficient is $1$, so the corresponding term in the opposite is $-x$. The quadratic and constant coefficients, originally negative, both become positive. The check adds the three groups separately and establishes the equality for every value of $x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Let $P=x^2-x+1$, $Q=2x^2+3$ and $R=x^2+2x+2$. Simplify $P-(Q-R)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q-R&=x^2-2x+1,\\[6pt]
P-(Q-R)&=x^2-x+1-x^2+2x-1\\[6pt]
&=x.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First calculate $Q-R$ to distinguish the internal signs from the subtraction sign in front of that difference. The linear term in the result is $-2x$. When the entire expression is subtracted, that term contributes $+2x$. The quadratic and constant terms cancel. The linear term with coefficient $-1+2=1$ remains. The calculation can be checked in reverse by adding $Q-R$ to $x$ to recover $P$. This gives $x+x^2-2x+1=x^2-x+1$. An equivalent calculation begins with $P-Q+R$. Its linear terms are $-x$ and $2x$, because the linear coefficient of $Q$ is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find $Q$ given that $P=4x^2-x+5$ and $P-Q=-2x^2+3x-1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=-2x^2+3x-1,\\[6pt]
Q&=P-D\\[6pt]
 &=4x^2-x+5+2x^2-3x+1\\[6pt]
 &=6x^2-4x+6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $D$ denote the given difference. From $P-Q=D$, it follows that $P=D+Q$, so the polynomial sought is $P-D$. Therefore reverse the signs of the terms of $D$ before combining like terms. The constant term of $Q$ is $6$, because the constant term of $D$ is $-1$. To verify all the coefficients, recalculate the difference $P-Q$. The contributions are $(4-6)x^2+(-1+4)x+(5-6)$, which gives exactly $D$. Adding $Q$ to both sides of $P-Q=D$ gives $P=D+Q$. Subtracting $D$ from both sides then gives a unique expression for $Q$.

</div>
