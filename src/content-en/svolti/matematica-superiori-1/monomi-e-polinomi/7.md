## Exercise 1

What is the additive inverse of the polynomial $x^2-8x+1$?

$$
-(x^2-8x+1)=- x^{2} + 8 x - 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The additive inverse of a polynomial is obtained by multiplying all its terms by minus one. This changes the sign of each coefficient. Adding the polynomial and its additive inverse gives the zero polynomial.

The result is $- x^{2} + 8 x - 1$.

The original linear term is $-8x$, so the corresponding term in the additive inverse is $8x$. The constant term $1$ also changes sign. To check all the signs together, add the result to $x^2-8x+1$ and combine the three groups of like terms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $(9 x^{2} + 3 x + 1)-(2 x^{2} - x + 9)$.

$$
(9 x^{2} + 3 x + 1)-(2 x^{2} - x + 9)=7 x^{2} + 4 x - 8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting a polynomial changes the sign of each of its terms. After changing the signs, combine like terms. The constant term of the polynomial being subtracted changes sign too.

The result is $7 x^{2} + 4 x - 8$.

To check the calculation, recover the polynomial from which the other one was subtracted. Adding $2x^2-x+9$ to the result gives $(7+2)x^2+(4-1)x+(-8+9)=9x^2+3x+1$. This recovers all three coefficients of the original polynomial.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $(\dfrac{3 x^{2}}{2} + 8 x)-(x^{2} + x)$.

$$
\left(\frac32-1\right)x^2+(8-1)x=\frac{x^{2}}{2} + 7 x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtract the coefficients of the quadratic terms and those of the linear terms. Subtracting one from three halves requires a common denominator. The two variable parts remain separate.

The result is $\dfrac{x^{2}}{2} + 7 x$.

For the quadratic terms, the difference of the coefficients is $\dfrac32-\dfrac22=\dfrac12$. The linear term has coefficient $8-1=7$. At $x=2$, the two original polynomials evaluate to $22$ and $6$, so their difference is $16$, the same as the simplified expression.

</div>
