## Exercise 1

What is the total degree of the monomial $-8x^7y^3$?

$$
(7)+3=10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The total degree of a nonzero monomial is the sum of the exponents of its variables. The numerical coefficient does not contribute to the degree. Add the two exponents.

The result is $10$.

The degree in $x$ is $7$, and the degree in $y$ is $3$. The sum $10$ counts the variable factors when each power is written as a product. The coefficient $-8$ affects the value of the monomial after substitution.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the constant term of the polynomial $P(x)=9 x^{3} - 2 x^{2} + 10 x - 7$?

$$
P(0)=-7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The constant term is the term without the variable. It is also the value of the polynomial when the variable is zero. All other terms vanish at that value.

The result is $-7$.

At $x=0$, the terms $9x^3$, $-2x^2$, and $10x$ are all zero because each has a factor of $x$. The constant $-7$ is therefore the only contribution to $P(0)$. A constant has the same value when $x$ changes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the degree of $P(x)=10x^4+3x^2-9x^4+x$ after simplifying?

$$
P(x)=x^4+3x^2+x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Determine the degree after combining like terms. The difference between the two fourth-degree coefficients is one, so the fourth-degree term remains. The highest remaining exponent is four.

The result is $4$.

To find the degree, start with the coefficients of terms with the same exponent. Here $10-9=1$, so the power $x^4$ remains. If the second coefficient were $-10$, simplifying would leave $3x^2+x$ and the degree would be $2$.

</div>
