## Exercise 1

Find the degree of $-7x^4y^2$ in each variable and its total degree.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\deg_x(-7x^4y^2)&=4,\\[6pt]
\deg_y(-7x^4y^2)&=2,\\[6pt]
\deg(-7x^4y^2)&=4+2=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The degree in $x$ counts only the four factors of $x$. The degree in $y$ counts the two factors of $y$. The total degree is therefore six. The coefficient $-7$ determines the numerical multiplier and the sign of the formula, while the exponents determine the degree. Setting $x=0$ gives the monomial the value zero. Its expression still has degree six because the substitution fixed one particular value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Simplify $P(x,y)=x^2y+x^5-x^5+y^4+2$ and find its degree in $x$, its degree in $y$, and its total degree.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x,y)&=x^2y+y^4+2,\\[6pt]
\deg_x P&=2,\qquad \deg_y P=4,\\[6pt]
\deg P&=\max\{3,4,0\}=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two fifth-degree contributions are opposites, so they cancel before the maxima are calculated. The term $x^2y$ has degree three and determines the degree in $x$. The term $y^4$ has degree four and determines the degree in $y$. The constant $2$ has degree zero. For the total degree, compare the degrees of the individual remaining terms. The maxima for $x$ and $y$ arise from different terms, so adding them would give a degree that no term reaches.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine the degree of $P_k(x)=(k-1)x^4+(k+2)x^2+(3-k)x+2$ as the real parameter $k$ varies.

::grafico{f="-x^4+2*x^2+3*x+2;3*x^2+2*x+2" xmin=-2.35 xmax=2.4 ymin=-13 ymax=24 punti="0,2" legenda="P_0(x);P_1(x)" titolo="The fourth-degree term occurs in $P_0$ and vanishes in $P_1$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
k\ne1&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \deg P_k=4,\\[6pt]
P_1(x)&=3x^2+2x+2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \deg P_1=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First examine the coefficient of the highest power before making any substitution for $x$. If $k-1$ is nonzero, the term $x^4$ is present in the simplified form, so the degree is four. The only value that removes it is $k=1$. For that value, check the next coefficient, which is $k+2=3$. Since it is nonzero, the degree is two. The two graphs represent the case $k=1$ and one example with $k\ne1$.

</div>
