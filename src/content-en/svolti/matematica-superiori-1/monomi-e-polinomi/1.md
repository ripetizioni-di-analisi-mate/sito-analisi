## Exercise 1

Simplify $8x^2y+16x^2y-2x^2y+5xy^2$ and find the coefficient of $x^2y$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
8x^2y+16x^2y-2x^2y+5xy^2
&=(8+16-2)x^2y+5xy^2\\[6pt]
&=22x^2y+5xy^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first three addends share the variable part $x^2y$, so that factor can be taken out and their coefficients added. The exponents of the two variables are reversed in $5xy^2$, so that term stays separate. To check which terms were grouped, substitute $x=2$ and $y=1$. Then $x^2y=4$ and $xy^2=2$, so the two contributions to the simplified sum are $88$ and $10$. The two terms have the same total degree, but they contribute different values.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Add $P(x)=9x^2+2x+1$ and $Q(x)=8x-3$. State the constant term of the sum as well.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x)+Q(x)&=(9+0)x^2+(2+8)x+(1-3)\\[6pt]
&=9x^2+10x-2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polynomial $Q$ has no quadratic term. Its coefficient of $x^2$ is therefore zero, and the sum retains the quadratic contribution from $P$. The linear terms have the same variable part, so the coefficient of their sum is $2+8=10$. The constant term is $-2$. This can be checked separately at $x=0$, since every term containing $x$ vanishes. The original expression also gives $P(0)+Q(0)=1-3=-2$. The sum of the coefficients can also be checked at $x=1$. The original expressions give $P(1)+Q(1)=12+5=17$, and the simplified form gives $9+10-2=17$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the difference between $P(x)=2x^2-3x+7$ and $Q(x)=2x^2+x-1$. Determine where the difference is zero.

::grafico{f="2*x^2-3*x+7;2*x^2+x-1" xmin=-0.6 xmax=2.7 ymin=-3 ymax=17 punti="1,6;1,2;2,9,pieno,I" legenda="P(x);Q(x)" titolo="At $I$ the two polynomials have the same value"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x)-Q(x)&=2x^2-3x+7-2x^2-x+1\\[6pt]
&=-4x+8,\\[6pt]
-4x+8&=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting takes the opposite of the whole second polynomial. Its constant term is $-1$, so $+1$ appears in the difference. The two quadratic contributions cancel, leaving a first-degree polynomial. At $x=2$, the difference is zero, so the vertical coordinates of $P$ and $Q$ coincide. The figure marks that point as $I$. At $x=1$, we have $P(1)=6$ and $Q(1)=2$, so the difference is $4$, in agreement with $-4(1)+8$. The difference can be written as $4(2-x)$. It is positive to the left of $2$ and negative to the right. The vertical order of the curves reverses at their intersection.

</div>
