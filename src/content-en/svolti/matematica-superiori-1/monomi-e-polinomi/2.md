## Exercise 1

Calculate $(8x^6y)\cdot(-2xy^2)$.

$$
8\cdot(-2)=-16,\qquad x^6x=x^7,\qquad yy^2=y^3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply the coefficients and the variable parts separately. For each variable, add its exponents in the two factors. The coefficient is negative because the original coefficients have opposite signs.

The result is $- 16 x^{7} y^{3}$.

The factor $x$ has exponent $1$, and the factor $y$ in the first monomial also has exponent $1$. The product therefore has degree $(6+1)+(1+2)=10$. This is also the sum of the original monomials' degrees, $7$ and $3$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Expand $9x(x^2-2x+3)$.

$$
9x(x^2-2x+3)=9 x^{3} - 18 x^{2} + 27 x
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

By the distributive property, multiply every term of the polynomial by the monomial. In each product, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents of x. The signs of the terms inside the polynomial are preserved.

The result is $9 x^{3} - 18 x^{2} + 27 x$.

The term $-18x^2$ comes from $9x(-2x)$. Its coefficient is $9\cdot(-2)$ and its variable power is $x\cdot x=x^2$. The term $27x$ comes from the constant term of the trinomial. Its degree is one because the factor $3$ has degree zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Expand $(x+10)(x+2)$.

$$
(x+10)(x+2)=x^2+2x+10x+20=x^{2} + 12 x + 20
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each term of the first factor multiplies both terms of the second. This gives four products. The two linear terms are like terms and can be added.

The result is $x^{2} + 12 x + 20$.

The products $x\cdot2$ and $10\cdot x$ are both multiples of $x$. Their sum is $12x$. The constant term $20$ is the product $10\cdot2$. At $x=0$, the value of the original product equals that constant term.

</div>
