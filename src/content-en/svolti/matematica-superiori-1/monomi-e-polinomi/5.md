## Exercise 1

Calculate $P(-3)$ for $P(x)=2x^2+5x-1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-3)&=2(-3)^2+5(-3)-1\\[6pt]
&=18-15-1\\[6pt]
&=2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use $-3$ for every occurrence of $x$. In the quadratic term, first calculate $(-3)^2=9$ and then multiply by $2$. The quadratic contribution is therefore positive. The linear term has a positive coefficient and a negative factor $x$, so it contributes $-15$. The constant $-1$ retains its value. The two negative contributions total $-16$, so adding them to the quadratic contribution gives $2$. If $x=3$ is substituted instead, the linear contribution becomes $+15$ and the other two stay the same. This gives $P(3)=32$, which exceeds $P(-3)$ by $30$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the constant term $c$ in $P(x)=3x^2-2x+c$, given that $P(-1)=5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-1)&=3(-1)^2-2(-1)+c\\[6pt]
&=5+c,\\[6pt]
5+c=5&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The datum $P(-1)=5$ specifies the value at a single point. Two contributions at that point are already known, namely $3$ from the square and $2$ from the linear term. These contributions total $5$, so the constant term must be zero. Substituting $c=0$ gives $P(x)=3x^2-2x$. This polynomial satisfies the given condition and also has $P(0)=0$. The second equality reads off the constant term and checks the interpretation of $c$. The constant term also enters the value at $-1$ with coefficient one. That single evaluation is therefore enough to determine $c$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Determine whether there is a value of $a$ for which $a(x+2)+3x=5x+7$ for every real $x$.

::grafico{f="6.5*x+7;5*x+7" xmin=-1.7 xmax=1.7 ymin=-3 ymax=17 punti="0,7" legenda="y=\dfrac{13}{2}x+7;y=5x+7" titolo="Same constant term and different linear coefficients"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(x+2)+3x&=(a+3)x+2a,\\[6pt]
\begin{cases}
a+3=5\\
2a=7
\end{cases}
&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
a=2\\
a=\dfrac72.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

An identity between two linear expressions requires the same coefficient of $x$ and the same constant term. The first condition gives $a=2$, and the second gives $a=\dfrac72$. Since the values differ, the two conditions cannot be satisfied together. The figure uses $a=\dfrac72$, the value for which the two sides agree at $x=0$. The first line then has linear coefficient $\dfrac{13}2$, whereas the second has coefficient $5$. The equality at $x=0$ holds at that point alone.

</div>
