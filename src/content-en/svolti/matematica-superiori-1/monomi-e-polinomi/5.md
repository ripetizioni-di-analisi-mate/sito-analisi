## Exercise 1

Given $P(x)=2 x^{2} - 3 x + 8$, calculate $P(-6)$.

$$
P(-6)=2\cdot(-6)^2-3\cdot(-6)+8=98
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substitute the same value for every occurrence of the variable. The square of a negative number is positive. The linear term with a negative coefficient becomes positive at the negative input.

The result is $98$.

The contributions can be evaluated separately. The quadratic term gives $72$ because $(-6)^2=36$. The linear term gives $18$ as a product of two negative factors. The constant term adds $8$, so $P(-6)=72+18+8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For which value of $c$ are the monomials $(11-c)x^2$ and $2x^2$ equal for every $x$?

$$
11-c=2,\qquad c=9
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Two monomials with the same variable part are identical when their coefficients match. Set the unknown coefficient equal to two. The resulting equation determines c.

The result is $9$.

The equality must hold for all values of the variable. When $x=1$, the factor $x^2$ is one and the equation becomes $11-c=2$. The solution $c=9$ makes the coefficients equal, so substitution verifies the identity for every $x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which monomial must be added to $2x^2y$ to obtain $12x^2y$?

$$
(12-2)x^2y=10 x^{2} y
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The missing monomial must have the same variable part as the required sum. Its coefficient is the final coefficient minus the one already present. To check the result, add the two coefficients again.

The result is $10 x^{2} y$.

The two known terms in the equation are multiples of $x^2y$. Their difference is $(12-2)x^2y$. Substituting the missing monomial into the original sum gives $(2+10)x^2y=12x^2y$ for all values of the variables.

</div>
