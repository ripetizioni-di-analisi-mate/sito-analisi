## Exercise 1

State the domain restrictions and simplify $\dfrac{-6x^3y^2}{3xy^2}$.

$$
x\ne0,\qquad y\ne0.
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{-6x^3y^2}{3xy^2}
&=-2\cdot\frac{x x^2}{x}\cdot\frac{y^2}{y^2}\\[6pt]
&=-2x^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find the allowed values, start with the denominator $3xy^2$. It is nonzero when both variables are nonzero. Under those conditions, $x$ and $y^2$ can be canceled. After simplification, $y$ disappears from the formula, so the quotient's value is independent of $y$ for the allowed values. At $y=0$, the original quotient would have denominator zero. The simplified formula gives the value of the fraction only on the domain established at the start. For example, with $x=2$ the value is $-8$ for every nonzero $y$. The value $y=0$ remains excluded even when $x=2$ is fixed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the monomial $M$ for which $(-2xy^2)M=12x^4y^5$ is an identity.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M&=-6x^{4-1}y^{5-2}=-6x^3y^3,\\[6pt]
(-2xy^2)(-6x^3y^3)&=12x^4y^5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the product rule to recover the coefficient and exponents of the missing factor. The coefficient is $-6$, because multiplying it by $-2$ gives $12$. Reaching exponent four in $x$ requires three more factors of $x$, and reaching exponent five in $y$ likewise requires three more factors of $y$. The final multiplication verifies the identity without any restrictions on the variables. If either variable is zero, both sides of the identity vanish and the equality still holds.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $Q(x,y)=\dfrac{18x^3y}{6xy^2}$. Decide whether its simplified form is a monomial and describe its graph when $y=2$.

::grafico{f="1.5*x^2" xmin=-2.35 xmax=2.35 ymin=-0.75 ymax=8.6 punti="0,0,vuoto;2,6" legenda="Q(x,2)=\dfrac32x^2" titolo="Values for $y=2$, with the origin excluded"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(x,y)&=3x^{3-1}y^{1-2}=\frac{3x^2}{y}
             \qquad (x\ne0,\ y\ne0),\\[6pt]
Q(x,2)&=\frac32x^2 \qquad (x\ne0).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator requires both $x$ and $y$ to be nonzero before cancellation. The remaining exponent of $y$ is $1-2=-1$, so $y$ stays in the denominator. The simplified expression is not a monomial under the chapter's definition. Setting $y=2$ gives a quadratic formula in $x$. The vertex at the origin is marked with an open point because $x=0$ remains excluded. The figure shows only the case $y=2$, so it does not describe how the quotient changes with $y$. The marked point with horizontal coordinate $2$ has vertical coordinate $6$, which is the product of $\dfrac32$ and $2^2$.

</div>
