## Exercise 1

Calculate $(9x^7y)\cdot(-2xy^2)$.

$$
9\cdot(-2)=-18,\qquad x^7x=x^8,\qquad yy^2=y^3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply the coefficients and the variable parts separately. For each variable, add its exponents in the two factors. The coefficient is negative because the original coefficients have opposite signs.

The result is $- 18 x^{8} y^{3}$.

The second factor contains $x$ with exponent $1$. The original degrees are $8$ and $3$, so the product has degree $8+3=11$. The final coefficient is $-18$, from the product $9\cdot(-2)$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$, simplify $\dfrac{24x^8y^3}{8x^2y}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{24}{8}&=3, \\[6pt] x^{8-2}&=x^6, \\[6pt] y^{3-1}&=y^2
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The stated conditions make the denominator nonzero. Divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents of matching variables. The original conditions still apply after simplification.

The result is $3 x^{6} y^{2}$.

Canceling $x^2$ leaves $x^{8-2}=x^6$, and canceling $y$ leaves $y^{3-1}=y^2$. Multiplying the result by $8x^2y$ gives $24x^8y^3$, which recovers the numerator on the domain of the quotient.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Square the monomial $-10x^8y$.

$$
(-10x^8y)^2=100x^{16}y^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square of a product is the product of the squares of its factors. The negative coefficient becomes positive. The exponents in the variable part double.

The result is $100 x^{16} y^{2}$.

The power is the product of two copies of the monomial. The two negative coefficients give $100$, while the exponents on $x$ and $y$ add separately. The final degree is $18$, twice the original degree $9$.

</div>
