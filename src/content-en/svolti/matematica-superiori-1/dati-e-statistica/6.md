## Exercise 1

A data set has a mean of $12$. If 3 is added to every value, what is the new mean?

$$
\overline{x+3}=\bar x+3=12+3=15
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $N$ be the number of observations. The initial sum is $12N$ because the mean is $12$. Increasing each observation by $3$ adds $3N$ to the total. The new sum is $15N$, so the new mean is $15$. The number of observations stays the same.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A set of measurements has a mean of $12$ cm. If each measurement is doubled, what is the new mean?

$$
\overline{2x}=2\bar x=2\cdot12=24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every new measurement is twice the corresponding original measurement. Their sum doubles while the number of measurements stays the same, so the ratio also doubles. The new mean is $2\cdot12=24\,\mathrm{cm}$ and has the original unit.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The mean of 4 observations is $11$. Three observations are $8$, $10$, and $12$. What is the fourth?

$$
x=4\cdot11-(8+10+12)=14
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum of the four observations is $4\cdot11=44$. The three known values sum to $8+10+12=30$, so the fourth value is $44-30=14$. To check, the deviations from $11$ are $-3$, $-1$, $1$, and $3$. They add to zero, as deviations from the mean do.

</div>
