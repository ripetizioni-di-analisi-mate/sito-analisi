## Exercise 1

A group has a mean journey time of $18$ minutes. Everyone adds $6$ minutes of waiting to the journey. What is the new mean time in seconds?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar t^\prime&=(18+6)\,\mathrm{min}=24\,\mathrm{min},\\[5pt]
24\,\mathrm{min}&=24\cdot60\,\mathrm{s}=1440\,\mathrm{s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With $N$ journeys, the number of minutes added to the total is $6N$. Dividing again by $N$ increases the mean by $6$ minutes. To change unit, multiply each duration by $60$. The same factor multiplies the sum and therefore the mean. We can check by converting first. The $18$ minutes give $1080$ seconds and the $6$ minutes give $360$, still totalling $1440$ seconds.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The mean of $5$ measurements is $15$ centimetres. A measurement of $23$ centimetres is removed. What is the new mean?

$$
\begin{aligned}
S&=5\cdot15=75,\\[5pt]
\bar x^\prime&=\frac{75-23}{5-1}=\frac{52}{4}=13.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original mean gives the sum of the five values. Removing the measurement reduces the sum from $75$ to $52$ centimetres and reduces the number of values to four. Both terms of the fraction need updating. Since $23$ is above the original mean, the new mean should be below $15$. Check by adding the removed measurement back, since $4\cdot13+23=75$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the values $2,5,11$, compare the original mean, the mean after duplicating every value, and the mean after duplicating only $11$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{2+5+11}{3}=6,\\[5pt]
\bar x_1&=\frac{2(2+5+11)}{2\cdot3}=6,\\[5pt]
\bar x_2&=\frac{2+5+11+11}{4}=\frac{29}{4}=7.25.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Duplicating all the data doubles the sum and the number of observations, leaving their ratio unchanged. Each value still has a relative weight of one third. Duplicating only $11$ gives that value a weight of two quarters. It adds a value above the original mean, so the result increases. The increase is $\dfrac{11-6}{4}=1.25$, since the five units above the old mean are spread over four observations.

</div>
