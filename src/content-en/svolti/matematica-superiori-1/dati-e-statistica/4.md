## Exercise 1

Of $50$ responses in a survey, $18$ are “yes” and $12$ are “unsure.” All the rest are “no.” Calculate all relative frequencies.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_{\rm no}&=50-18-12=20,\\[5pt]
f_1&=\frac{18}{50}=36\%,\\[5pt]
f_2&=\frac{12}{50}=24\%,\\[5pt]
f_3&=\frac{20}{50}=40\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Every response belongs to one of the three categories, and the total includes “unsure.” First use subtraction to find the number of “no” responses. Then divide all three counts by $50$. A check is $36\%+24\%+40\%=100\%$. Excluding unsure responses would change the reference group. The numerator for “yes” would still be $18$, but the total it is compared with would become $38$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In two separate course groups with no shared members, $18$ of $24$ people and $30$ of $50$ people finish a test. Which group has the higher proportion? What is the combined proportion?

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_1&=\frac{18}{24}=\frac34,& f_2&=\frac{30}{50}=\frac35,\\[5pt]
f&=\frac{18+30}{24+50}=\frac{24}{37}\approx64.86\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second group has a larger absolute number of people finishing the test. To compare proportions, divide each count by its own group size. Three quarters is more than three fifths, so the first group has the higher proportion. Overall there are $48$ finishers out of $74$, with each person counted once because the groups do not overlap. The overall percentage lies between the two and is closer to that of the larger group.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the number of absences, the cumulative counts at $0,1,2,3$ are $5,11,18,20$. How many observations equal $2$, and how many exceed $1$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_2&=N(2)-N(1)=18-11=7,\\[5pt]
n_{>1}&=N(3)-N(1)=20-11=9.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The cumulative count $N(2)$ also includes zero and one absence. To isolate the value $2$, subtract the whole previous cumulative count, $N(1)$. To count observations exceeding one absence, start from the final cumulative count, the total, and subtract $N(1)$ again. That leaves the seven observations with two absences plus the two with value $3$. They add to $7+(20-18)=9$.

</div>
