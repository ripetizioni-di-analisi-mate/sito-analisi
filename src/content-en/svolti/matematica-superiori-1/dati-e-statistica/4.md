## Exercise 1

Among $40$ observations, one category occurs $16$ times. What is its relative frequency?

$$
f=\frac{16}{40}=\frac25
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is $16$, the count for the category, and the denominator is $40$, the total. Dividing both by $8$ gives $\dfrac25$. On average, this corresponds to two occurrences for every five observations. The corresponding percentage is $40\%$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a survey of $90$ people, $30\%$ choose one answer. How many choose it?

$$
n_i=\frac{30}{100}\cdot90=27
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For every ten people, $30\%$ represents three. Ninety people form nine groups of ten, so the absolute frequency is $9\cdot3=27$. Multiplying by $\dfrac{30}{100}$ gives the same result. To check the count, $\dfrac{27}{90}$ simplifies to $\dfrac3{10}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A table shows three categories with frequencies $10$, $20$, and $30$. What is the total number of observations?

$$
N=10+20+30=60
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each of the three frequencies counts a separate category. Since each observation belongs to exactly one category, add $10$, $20$, and $30$. There are $60$ observations in total. Three is the number of categories in the table, while $60$ is the number of observations collected.

</div>
