## Exercise 1

For three potted plants, height in centimetres is recorded every Monday for four weeks, and the number of leaves is recorded at the end. What is the statistical unit when comparing the plants? Classify both variables and count the height measurements.

$$
3\cdot4=12.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To compare plants, keep one row for each plant and associate its four height measurements with the same unit. Height in centimetres is continuous even when recorded after rounding. The number of leaves is discrete, since it counts whole leaves. The twelve height measurements refer to three plants. To compute mean growth, first find one height difference for each plant.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Four students’ ratings are “poor,” “adequate,” “adequate,” and “good.” Find the mean of the codes using $(1,2,3)$ and $(1,2,9)$ and give a comparison that is independent of the encoding.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1+2+2+3}{4}&=2,\\[5pt]
\frac{1+2+2+9}{4}&=\frac{14}{4}=3.5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both encodings preserve the order of the ratings, so both show that three of the four students receive at least “adequate.” The numerical means differ because the second encoding changes the distance between the top two levels. That distance is a choice made by whoever assigns the codes. For ordinal categories we can compare frequencies and ordering, for example by reporting that “adequate” is the most frequent rating.

</div>

## Exercise 3

To estimate how many enrolled students use the gym, $60$ students are interviewed as they leave it. Of those, $54$ report using it every week. What proportion can be calculated? How can the survey be extended to all enrolled students?

$$
\frac{54}{60}=90\%.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The $90\%$ describes weekly use among those interviewed. The location favours people who already enter the gym. Even interviewing many more people at that exit would give students who never use the gym few chances to answer. A sample can be drawn from the enrolment list, with interviews spread across classes and reaching students who do not visit the gym. The question should have the same meaning for everyone and specify the period it refers to.

</div>
