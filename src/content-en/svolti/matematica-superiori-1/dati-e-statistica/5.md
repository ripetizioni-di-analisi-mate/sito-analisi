## Exercise 1

In a pie chart of $35$ responses, category $A$ has angle $72^\circ$. Categories $B$ and $C$ split the remainder equally. Find the three counts.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_A&=\frac{72^\circ}{360^\circ}=\frac15,\qquad n_A=35\cdot\frac15=7,\\[5pt]
n_B&=n_C=\frac{35-7}{2}=14.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1.23 xmax=1.23 ymin=-1.18 ymax=1.18 curve="cos(t),sin(t),0,6.2832" linee="0,0 1,0;0,0 0.30902,0.95106;0,0 -0.80902,-0.58779" poligoni="0,0 1,0 0.98769,0.15643 0.95106,0.30902 0.89101,0.45399 0.80902,0.58779 0.70711,0.70711 0.58779,0.80902 0.45399,0.89101 0.30902,0.95106" etichette="0.40,0.35,A 72°;-0.66,0.26,B 144°;-0.09,-0.6,C 144°" titolo="The shaded sector occupies one fifth of the circle"}

<div class="spiegazione">

A sector angle is compared with a full turn. Category $A$ takes one fifth of the circle, so it represents one fifth of the responses. Remove the seven in $A$ and split the other twenty-eight into two equal groups. The angles provide a check. Both $B$ and $C$ have proportion $\dfrac{14}{35}=\dfrac25$, hence an angle of $144^\circ$. Adding $72^\circ+144^\circ+144^\circ$ gives a full turn.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two days have $78$ and $84$ admissions. A bar chart starts at $75$. Compare the ratio of the visible bar heights with the ratio of the actual counts.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{84-75}{78-75}&=\frac93=3,\\[5pt]
\frac{84}{78}&=\frac{14}{13}.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.3 xmax=3.3 ymin=75 ymax=86.5 nomi="s,n" poligoni="0.73,75 1.27,75 1.27,78 0.73,78;1.73,75 2.27,75 2.27,84 1.73,84" etichette="0.87,78.55,78;1.87,84.65,84" titolo="The visible bars start at 75 admissions"}

<div class="spiegazione">

First read the values reached by the bars, $78$ and $84$. The difference is six admissions. The chart starts at $75$, so the visible parts are only $3$ and $9$ high. Their ratio is $3$, whereas $\dfrac{14}{13}$ includes the first $75$ admissions of each day. The second ratio captures the change relative to the first day’s count.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a histogram of waiting times, the classes $[0,2)$, $[2,5)$, and $[5,9)$ have densities of $5$, $3$, and $4$ waits per minute. How many waits were recorded? How many took less than five minutes?

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_1&=2\cdot5=10,\quad n_2=3\cdot3=9,\\[5pt]
n_3&=4\cdot4=16,\\[5pt]
N&=10+9+16=35,\quad N_{<5}=10+9=19.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.8 xmax=10.2 ymin=-0.35 ymax=6.2 nomi="t,d" poligoni="0,0 2,0 2,5 0,5;2,0 5,0 5,3 2,3;5,0 9,0 9,4 5,4" linee="0,5 2,5 2,0;2,3 5,3 5,0;5,4 9,4 9,0" etichette="0.66,2.5,10;3.27,1.52,9;6.62,1.96,16" titolo="The areas labelled inside the rectangles give the three counts"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Height is measured in waits per minute. Area gives the number of waits because width is measured in minutes. The widest rectangle, with height $4$, contains more waits than the rectangle of height $5$. To count waits of less than five minutes, add the areas of the first two classes. The value $5$ is in the third class, so no wait in that class belongs in that sum.

</div>
