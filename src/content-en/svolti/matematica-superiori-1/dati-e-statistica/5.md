## Exercise 1

The values $1,2,3$ have frequencies $8$, $16$, and $9$, respectively. How many observations are at most $2$?

$$
N_{\le2}=8+16=24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

“At most $2$” includes both the value $1$ and the value $2$. Their respective frequencies are $8$ and $16$, so the cumulative frequency is $8+16=24$. The value $3$ is excluded because it exceeds $2$. Including the nine observations with value $3$ gives the total $33$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A category represents $25\%$ of $36$ observations. What is the angle of its sector in a pie chart?

$$
\alpha=\frac{25}{100}\cdot360^\circ=90^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The share $25\%$ is a quarter of the observations, so the category contains $36:4=9$ cases. The same fraction applies to the chart’s full angle of $360^\circ$. A quarter of $360^\circ$ is $90^\circ$. Either the percentage or the ratio of nine cases to the total therefore gives the angle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A bar chart compares $60$ and $70$ with a vertical axis starting at $59$. Which statement is correct?

- The second value is at least ten times the first
- The difference is 1 because the axis starts close to the values
- The numerical difference is 10, but the truncated axis can make the gap look larger
- The two values must be equal

<div class="spiegazione">

The difference between the bar labels is $70-60=10$. Starting the graph at $59$, however, makes the first bar $60-59=1$ units high and the second $70-59=11$ units high. These heights suggest that the second value is eleven times the first. The actual counts have ratio $\dfrac{70}{60}=\dfrac76$.

</div>
