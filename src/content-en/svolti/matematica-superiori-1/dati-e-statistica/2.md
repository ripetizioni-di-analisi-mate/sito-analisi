## Exercise 1

What is the median of the data $12, 8, 10, 9, 16$?

$$
8<9<10<12<16,\qquad\operatorname{median}=10
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The median depends on increasing order. Sorting the five observations gives $8,9,10,12,16$. The third observation is $10$, with two smaller values and two larger values. Here it also happens to be the third entry in the original list. Sorting is what justifies the choice.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the median of the ordered data $9, 11, 14, 18$?

$$
\operatorname{median}=\frac{11+14}2=\frac{25}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

There are four observations already in order, so the middle positions are the second and third. These contain $11$ and $14$. Their mean is $\dfrac{11+14}{2}=12.5$. The calculated value is between the two middle values and may be absent from the original observations.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the median of the ordered data $12, 12, 13, 13, 32$, which include a value much larger than the others?

$$
\operatorname{median}=13
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In a sorted list of five observations, the median is the third value. Here that value is $13$. The last observation is $32$ and could be larger without changing the order of the first four values. The median would stay $13$, while the sum and the mean would increase.

</div>
