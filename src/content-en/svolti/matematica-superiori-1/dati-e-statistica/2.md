## Exercise 1

Find the median of $12,4,9,4,6$. Recalculate it after removing the largest value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4,4,\boxed{6},9,12 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Med}=6,\\[5pt]
4,\boxed{4},\boxed{6},9 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Med}=\frac{4+6}{2}=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The repeated value $4$ occupies two distinct positions. With five observations the central position is the third, giving $6$. Removing the maximum leaves four observations. The earlier part of the ordering is the same, but the **central positions** change to the second and third. Take the mean of the second $4$ and $6$. The example shows how removing a value far from the centre can change the median by changing the sample size.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The values $1,4,7,9$ occur $2,3,4,1$ times, respectively. Use cumulative frequencies to find the median.

$$
\operatorname{Med}=\frac{x_{(5)}+x_{(6)}}{2}=\frac{4+7}{2}=5.5.
$$

| Value | Frequency | Cumulative count | Positions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $1$ | $2$ | $2$ | $1$–$2$ |
| $4$ | $3$ | $5$ | $3$–$5$ |
| $7$ | $4$ | $9$ | $6$–$9$ |
| $9$ | $1$ | $10$ | $10$ |

<div class="spiegazione">

With ten observations, the central positions are the fifth and sixth. The cumulative count at $4$ is $5$, so the fifth observation is the last one equal to $4$. The count rises from $5$ to $9$ at $7$, which takes positions six through nine. This identifies both central values without writing out the whole list. The result $5.5$ lies between the two central values and is absent from the observations.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The data are $2,5,8,11,k$. For which real values of $k$ is the median $8$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
k<8&\ \Longrightarrow\ N_{<8}=3,\\[5pt]
k\ge8&\ \Longrightarrow\ N_{<8}=2,\quad N_{\le8}\ge3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

With five observations, look for the third in order. If $k<8$, the three values $2$, $5$, and $k$ come before $8$, so the third value is less than $8$. If $k\ge8$, only $2$ and $5$ are less than $8$, and the next value is $8$ itself. The case $k=8$ is allowed because the two equal observations take positions three and four. The condition is **$k\ge8$**.

</div>
