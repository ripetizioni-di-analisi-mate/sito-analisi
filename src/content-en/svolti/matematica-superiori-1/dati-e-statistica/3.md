## Exercise 1

Thirteen students each choose a genre for the next comic they will read. The counts are $3$ for adventure, $5$ for mystery, and $5$ for science fiction. Find the modes and their relative frequency.

$$
n_{\max}=5,\qquad f_{\max}=\frac{5}{13}\ \bigl(\approx38.46\%\bigr).
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.15 xmax=4.15 ymin=-0.7 ymax=6.4 nomi="c,n" poligoni="0.73,0 1.27,0 1.27,3 0.73,3;1.73,0 2.27,0 2.27,5 1.73,5;2.73,0 3.27,0 3.27,5 2.73,5" etichette="0.91,3.45,3;1.91,5.45,5;2.91,5.45,5" titolo="Categories 1, 2, and 3 are adventure, mystery, and science fiction"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Genre is a categorical variable. Changing the order of the bars leaves their counts unchanged. Mystery and science fiction have the tallest bars, each with five votes, so **there are two modes**. The relative frequency calculated is for each category. To find the proportion who chose either one, add the two disjoint counts to obtain $\dfrac{10}{13}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two groups have data $5,7,8,9,13$ and $5,5,5,13,13$. Compare their ranges and medians.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_1=R_2&=13-5=8,\\[5pt]
\operatorname{Med}_1&=8,\qquad \operatorname{Med}_2=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both lists have minimum $5$ and maximum $13$, so they have the same range. For the median, take the third value in each sorted list. It is $8$ in the first list. The first three observations in the second all equal $5$, so its median is $5$. The **range** keeps only the distance between the extremes and cannot show whether values are spread between them or concentrated at the ends.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Five durations in minutes are $4,6,6,9,25$. The value $25$ was a transcription error and is replaced by $8$. How do the mode, median, and range change?

$$
\begin{aligned}
4,6,6,9,25 &: R=25-4=21,\\[5pt]
4,6,6,8,9 &: R=9-4=5.
\end{aligned}
$$

The mode and median are both $6\,\mathrm{min}$ before and after the correction.

<div class="spiegazione">

After the correction the maximum becomes $9$ and the minimum stays at $4$. Recalculate the range using the actual extremes in the new list. The **mode** stays at $6$ because it occurs twice and every other value occurs once. The new $8$ still comes after the two observations equal to $6$. The third position is therefore still occupied by $6$, and the median is unchanged.

</div>
