## Exercise 1

What is the arithmetic mean of $8, 9, 10, 11$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{8+9+10+11}4 \\[6pt]
&=\frac{38}{4}=\frac{19}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum $8+9+10+11$ equals $38$. Dividing by the number of observations, $4$, gives $\dfrac{19}{2}=9.5$. This is between the minimum $8$ and the maximum $11$. Both pairs $8,11$ and $9,10$ are symmetric about $9.5$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The value $9$ occurs 2 times and the value $11$ occurs 4 times. What is the mean of the six observations?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{2\cdot9+4\cdot11}{2+4} \\[6pt]
&=\frac{62}{6}=\frac{31}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The graph shows two observations of $9$ and four observations of $11$. The sum is therefore $2\cdot9+4\cdot11=62$, with six observations altogether. Dividing $62$ by $6$ gives $\dfrac{31}3$. The mean is closer to $11$ because $11$ occurs twice as often as $9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The mean of 4 observations is $13$. Three observations are $10$, $12$, and $14$. What is the fourth?

$$
x=4\cdot13-(10+12+14)=16
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If four observations have a mean of $13$, their sum is $4\cdot13=52$. The three known observations add up to $10+12+14=36$. The missing observation is therefore $52-36=16$. To check, include $16$ with the other three and divide their sum by four again, which gives $13$.

</div>
