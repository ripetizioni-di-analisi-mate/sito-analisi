## Exercise 1

Four journey times in minutes are $6,8,8,14$. Calculate and interpret the mean.

$$
\bar t=\frac{6+8+8+14}{4}\,\mathrm{min}=\frac{36}{4}\,\mathrm{min}=9\,\mathrm{min}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The four journeys take $36$ minutes in total. The **mean** is the time each journey would take if those minutes were divided into four equal parts. The differences from $9$ are $-3,-1,-1,5$ minutes. They add up to zero, so the extra time on the longest trip balances the shortfalls on the shorter ones. The most frequent duration is $8$ minutes. A mean of $9$ minutes is possible even though no journey took exactly that long.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In one week, $4$ students use no new pencils, $3$ use one, and $2$ use four. How many new pencils does each student use on average?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N&=4+3+2=9,\\[5pt]
S&=0\cdot4+1\cdot3+4\cdot2=11,\\[5pt]
\bar x&=\frac{11}{9}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the chart the bar at $x=4$ has height $2$. It represents two students who used four pencils each and contributes eight pencils to the total. The group at $x=0$ contributes no pencils but four students, so it belongs in the denominator. Divide the total number of pencils by the nine students observed. The mean is a little more than one pencil per student. The numerator counts pencils and the denominator counts students.

## Exercise 3

Two disjoint groups of readers have $7$ and $13$ members. The first reads for an average of $14$ minutes a day, while the combined mean is $17.9$ minutes. Find the mean for the second group.

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\rm tot}&=20\cdot 17.9=358,\\[5pt]
S_1&=7\cdot14=98,\\[5pt]
\bar x_2&=\frac{358-98}{13}=\frac{260}{13}=20.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The combined mean is for all the readers, so multiply by $20$ to recover their total of $358$ minutes. The first seven readers contribute $98$. That leaves $260$ minutes for thirteen people. Dividing by $13$ gives **$20$ minutes**. As a check, the combined mean lies between the two group means and is closer to $20$ than to $14$, on the side of the larger group.

</div>
