## Exercise 1

What is the range of the data $9, 16, 10, 12$?

$$
R=16-9=7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The smallest of the four observations is $9$ and the largest is $16$. Their difference is $7$, the width of the interval containing every observation. The range alone gives no information about the exact positions of the intermediate values. Those values lie between $9$ and $16$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A bar chart compares $59$ and $69$ with a vertical axis starting at $58$. Which statement is correct?

- The numerical difference is 10, but the truncated axis can make the gap look larger
- The second value is at least ten times the first
- The difference is 1 because the axis starts close to the values
- The two values must be equal

<div class="spiegazione">

The scale starts at $58$, so the visible heights are $1$ and $11$. The difference between the actual values is $69-59=10$. The truncated axis changes the ratio of heights. Read the actual difference from the numerical labels.

</div>

## Exercise 3

What is the median of the ordered data $11, 11, 12, 12, 31$, which include a value much larger than the others?

$$
\operatorname{median}=12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The median stays at the third value, $12$, even though the maximum $31$ is well separated from the other values. If the maximum were replaced with $13$, the third value would stay at $12$. The range would change from $31-11=20$ to $13-11=2$. This shows which measure is sensitive to the last value.

</div>
