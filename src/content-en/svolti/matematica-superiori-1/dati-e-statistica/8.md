## Exercise 1

In one week, five deliveries take $10,11,12,13,34$ minutes. Calculate the mean and median. Which describes a typical delivery better if the $34$-minute delivery was delayed by an isolated breakdown?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar x&=\frac{10+11+12+13+34}{5}=16,\\[5pt]
\operatorname{Med}&=12.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{xmin=-0.9 xmax=37.4 ymin=-0.55 ymax=3.25 nomi="t," punti="10,1;11,1;12,1;13,1;34,1" segmenti="12,0,12,2.00;16,0,16,2.00" etichette="8.8,2.30,12;15.2,2.30,16;32.8,1.42,34" titolo="The median lies between 10 and 13 minutes and the mean is farther right"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The median is the third value in order and lies among the four similar durations. The mean is outside that group because it gives the $34$-minute delivery the same weight as every other delivery. To describe a typical delivery without the breakdown, $12$ minutes is the more representative figure. To share out the total time actually spent on the five deliveries, use the mean of $16$ minutes.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The class $[0,4)$ contains $5$ durations and the class $[4,10)$ contains $7$. What can be said about the numbers of durations less than $4$ and less than $6$? Which class contains the median?

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{<4}&=5,\qquad 5\le N_{<6}\le12,\\[5pt]
x_{(6)},x_{(7)}&\in[4,10),\\[5pt]
\frac{x_{(6)}+x_{(7)}}{2}&\in[4,10).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The threshold $4$ is the boundary between classes, so the count is exact. The threshold $6$ lies inside the second interval. All or none of the seven durations in that class could be less than $6$. The middle positions in the twelve ordered observations are sixth and seventh, both in the second class. Their mean lies in that class, but its exact value depends on the individual durations.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Of $80$ people invited to take a survey, $60$ respond and $27$ of the respondents are in favour. Find the response rate, the proportion in favour among respondents, and the bounds on that proportion among all invitees.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{60}{80}&=75\%,& \frac{27}{60}&=45\%,\\[5pt]
\frac{27}{80}&=33.75\%,& \frac{27+20}{80}&=58.75\%.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{assi=0 xmin=-1.5 xmax=81.5 ymin=-0.4 ymax=3.6 linee="0,0 80,0 80,2 0,2 0,0;27,0 27,2;60,0 60,2" poligoni="0,0 27,0 27,2 0,2" etichette="4,0.9,27;35,0.9,33;66,0.9,20" titolo="The 27 in favour and 33 other respondents are separate from the 20 non-respondents"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The response rate uses all invitees as the denominator. The observed proportion in favour uses only the sixty respondents. Extending it to all invitees requires accounting for the twenty non-respondents. If none is in favour, the count stays at twenty-seven. If all are in favour, it reaches forty-seven. These bounds show how much information is missing. The $45\%$ is exact for respondents and does not by itself determine the proportion of invitees.

</div>
