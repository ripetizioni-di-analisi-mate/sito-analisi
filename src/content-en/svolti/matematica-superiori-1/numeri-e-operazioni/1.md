## Exercise 1

Calculate $-8-4+16$.

::grafico{xmin=-14 xmax=7 ymin=-1 ymax=2 uguale=1 assi=0 vettori="-13.5,0,6,0;-8,0.55,-12,0.55;-12,1.35,4,1.35" punti="-12,0,pieno;-8,0,pieno;4,0,pieno" segmenti="-12,0,-12,1.35;-8,0,-8,0.55;4,0,4,1.35" etichette="-12.4,-0.56,−12;-8.3,-0.56,−8;3.85,-0.56,4;-10.44,0.91,−4;-4.55,1.68,+16;6.18,0.05,x" titolo="The moves go from −8 to −12 and then to 4"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
-8-4+16&=-12+16\\[6pt]
&=4.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From position $-8$, subtracting $4$ means moving four units to the left, to $-12$. Adding $16$ then takes us to the right. Twelve of those sixteen units are needed to reach zero, leaving four, so the final coordinate is $4$. One check uses the overall change. The second change offsets the first and adds another $12$ units, since $16-4=12$. Starting at $-8$ therefore gives $-8+12=4$. The net move is $12$ and the final position is $4$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $9-(-27+3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
9-(-27+3)&=9+27-3\\[6pt]
&=36-3\\[6pt]
&=33.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The number being subtracted is the value of the entire sum $-27+3$. Taking its opposite means multiplying both addends by $-1$. Thus $-27$ becomes $+27$ and $+3$ becomes $-3$. One check calculates the sum being subtracted first, giving $-24$. Then $9-(-24)=9+24=33$. The result is $24$ units larger than the first term because the value subtracted is negative. Once that value is known, its effect on $9$ is clear before the final addition.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which integer $z$ makes the equation $-10+z=11$ true?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-10+z)+10&=11+10\\[6pt]
z&=21.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value of $z$ is the move from the starting position $-10$ to the final position $11$. Since the destination is to the right, the move must be positive. To find it, add $10$ to both sides of the equation. By associativity and commutativity, $-10$ and $10$ on the left add to zero, leaving $z$. The right side gives $21$. On the number line, this means ten units to zero and eleven beyond the origin. Substitution confirms the result since $-10+21=11$.

</div>
