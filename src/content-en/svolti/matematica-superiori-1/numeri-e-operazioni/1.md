## Exercise 1

Calculate $-8-4+16$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-8-4+16&=-12+16 \\[6pt]
&=4
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

From $-8$ on the number line, subtracting $4$ means moving four units to the left, to $-12$. Adding $16$ then means moving to the right. Twelve of those sixteen units take us to zero, so the remaining four take us to $4$. This gives both the sign and the magnitude of the result.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $9-(-27+3)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
9-(-27+3)&=9+27-3 \\[6pt]
&=33
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The entire sum $-27+3$ is being subtracted. Subtracting a sum is equivalent to adding its opposite, so $-27$ becomes $+27$ and $+3$ becomes $-3$. To check the calculation, start from $-27+3=-24$. This gives $9-(-24)=9+24=33$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which integer makes the equation $-10+z=11$ true?

$$
z=11+10=21
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Add $10$ to both sides to eliminate $-10$. The left-hand side is then $z$ and the right-hand side is $11+10=21$. Substitution into the original equation gives $-10+21=11$, so $21$ satisfies the equation. On the number line, $21$ is also the distance traveled to the right from $-10$ to $11$.

</div>
