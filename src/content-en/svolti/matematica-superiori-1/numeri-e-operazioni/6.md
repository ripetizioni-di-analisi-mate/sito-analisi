## Exercise 1

What fraction in lowest terms represents the decimal $1.0$?

$$
1.0=1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The digit after the decimal point counts tenths. In $1.0$ that digit is zero, so all ten tenths come from the whole unit. Write the value as $\dfrac{10}{10}$ and divide the numerator and denominator by $10$ to obtain $\dfrac11=1$. This is the fraction in lowest terms, with the denominator $1$ usually left implicit.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What fraction equals $0.\overline{8}$?

$$
z=0.\overline{8},\quad10z-z=8,\quad z=\frac{8}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying $z=0.\overline8$ by $10$ gives $10z=8.\overline8$. The two numbers have the same repeating part, so subtracting eliminates every digit after the decimal point and leaves $9z=8$. Dividing by $9$ gives $\dfrac89$. Like the original decimal, the fraction is less than $1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which number lies exactly halfway between $\dfrac{10}{11}$ and $1$?

$$
\frac12\left(\frac{10}{11}+1\right)=\frac{21}{22}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $1=\dfrac{11}{11}$, the endpoints form an interval of length $\dfrac{1}{11}$. The midpoint lies $\dfrac{1}{22}$ from the left endpoint. In twenty-seconds, moving from $\dfrac{20}{22}$ therefore gives $\dfrac{21}{22}$. The remaining distance from $1=\dfrac{22}{22}$ is again $\dfrac{1}{22}$.

</div>
