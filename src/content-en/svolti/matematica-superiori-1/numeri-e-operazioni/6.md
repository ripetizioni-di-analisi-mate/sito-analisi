## Exercise 1

Write $2.175$ as a fraction in lowest terms. What property of the denominator explains why its decimal expansion terminates?

$$
\begin{aligned}
2.175&=\frac{2175}{1000}
=\frac{2175:25}{1000:25}=\frac{87}{40},\\[6pt]
40&=2^3\cdot5,\qquad 87=3\cdot29.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three digits after the decimal point mean we are counting thousandths. The number contains two thousand one hundred and seventy-five thousandths. Dividing both numerator and denominator by $25$ leaves $\dfrac{87}{40}$, which is in lowest terms because neither $2$ nor $5$ is a prime factor of $87$. The denominator contains only factors of $2$ and $5$, so it divides a power of ten. Here $40\cdot25=1000$. Neither $10$ nor $100$ is a multiple of $40$, so in this case exactly three decimal places are needed.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Convert $0.\overline8$ to a fraction and check it using the first step of long division.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x&=0.\overline8,\qquad 10x=8.\overline8,\\[6pt]
10x-x&=8,\\[6pt]
x&=\frac89.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The repeating block has one digit. After multiplying by $10$, an $8$ appears in the whole part and the decimal tail is identical to that of $x$. Subtracting the two numbers cancels those tails, so $9x=8$. The division $8:9$ confirms the value. To produce the first digit, divide $80$ by $9$, giving $8$ with remainder $8$. Back in the starting situation, we repeat the same step and produce only the digit $8$. The quotient lies between zero and one.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Use long division to find the non-repeating decimal digits and the repeating block of $\dfrac{11}{30}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
110&=3\cdot30+20,\\[6pt]
200&=6\cdot30+20,\\[6pt]
\frac{11}{30}&=0.3\overline6.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $11<30$, the whole part is zero. The initial remainder is $11$. Multiplying it by $10$ produces the digit $3$ and leaves remainder $20$. From then on, the remainder is always $20$, because $200:30$ gives digit $6$ and leaves $20$ again. The $3$ is the non-repeating part and $6$ is the repeating block. The factor $3$ in $30$ does not cancel with $11$, so the reduced denominator divides no power of $10$. This confirms that the remainders eventually repeat and never reach zero.

</div>
