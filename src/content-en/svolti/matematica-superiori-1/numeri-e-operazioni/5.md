## Exercise 1

What is the greatest common divisor of $48$ and $72$? Check it using division with remainder.

$$
\begin{aligned}
48&=2^4\cdot3,\qquad 72=2^3\cdot3^2,\\[6pt]
\gcd(48,72)&=2^3\cdot3=24,\\[6pt]
72&=48+24,\qquad 48=2\cdot24.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Any common divisor can contain at most three factors of $2$ and one factor of $3$, since it must also divide the number in which the corresponding prime power has the smaller exponent. The product $2^3\cdot3$ divides both and reaches both limits. We can also reach $24$ without factoring. A divisor of $72$ and $48$ divides their difference $24$. Conversely, a divisor of $48$ and $24$ divides their sum $72$. Finally, $24$ divides $48$ exactly, so it is the largest common divisor.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two signals repeat every $36$ and $54$ seconds and start together. After how many seconds do they next occur together?

$$
\begin{aligned}
36&=2^2\cdot3^2,\qquad 54=2\cdot3^3,\\[6pt]
\operatorname{lcm}(36,54)&=2^2\cdot3^3=108.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A meeting time must contain a whole number of periods of each signal. A multiple of $36$ contains at least two factors of $2$, and a multiple of $54$ contains at least three factors of $3$. The product of these powers is the smallest positive time that meets both requirements. The diagram shows the events after the start and before $108$: $36$ and $72$ for the first signal, $54$ for the second. The starting time is shared but equals zero. The next meeting occurs after $108\,\mathrm{s}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Each pack contains $4$ red pens and $3$ blue pens. Make as many packs as possible using $40$ red pens and $29$ blue pens. After giving away two packs, how many pens are left altogether?

$$
\begin{aligned}
40&=10\cdot4,\qquad 29=9\cdot3+2,\\[6pt]
40-9\cdot4&=4,\\[6pt]
(9-2)\cdot(4+3)+4+2&=55.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The red pens would cover ten packs, whereas the blue pens can complete only nine. A tenth pack would need three blue pens and only two remain. After packing, four red pens and two blue pens are loose. Giving away two packs leaves seven full packs of seven pens plus the six loose pens. To check the total, start with the original $40+29=69$ pens and subtract the $14$ given away. This also gives $55$.

</div>
