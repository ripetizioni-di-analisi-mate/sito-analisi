## Exercise 1

Which number lies exactly halfway between $\dfrac{8}{9}$ and $1$?

$$
\frac12\left(\frac{8}{9}+1\right)=\frac{17}{18}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance between $\dfrac89$ and $1=\dfrac99$ is $\dfrac19$. The midpoint is half that distance, $\dfrac{1}{18}$, from each endpoint. Adding this amount to $\dfrac89=\dfrac{16}{18}$ gives $\dfrac{17}{18}$. Subtracting it from $1=\dfrac{18}{18}$ reaches the same point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the distance on the number line between $-9$ and $14$?

$$
|14-(-9)|=23
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To go from $-9$ to $14$, travel nine units to zero and another fourteen beyond zero. The distance is therefore $9+14=23$. The absolute-value formula gives the same sum because subtracting $-9$ is equivalent to adding $9$. Reversing the endpoints changes the sign of the difference, while its absolute value remains $23$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Calculate $\dfrac1{10}-\dfrac1{11}$.

$$
\frac1{10}-\frac1{11}=\frac{11-10}{110}=\frac{1}{110}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $10$ and $11$ are relatively prime, their least common multiple is $110$. The first term is eleven parts out of one hundred and ten, while the second is ten. One part out of one hundred and ten remains. The sign can be checked in advance. When the numerators are equal, the fraction with the smaller denominator is larger, which here is $\dfrac1{10}$.

</div>
