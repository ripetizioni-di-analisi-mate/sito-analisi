## Exercise 1

Find the midpoint between $\dfrac89$ and $1$.

::grafico{xmin=0.851 xmax=1.049 ymin=-0.056 ymax=0.079 uguale=1 assi=0 vettori="0.865,0,1.032,0" punti="0.888888889,0,pieno;0.944444444,0,pieno,M;1,0,pieno" segmenti="0.888888889,0.002,0.888888889,0.042;0.944444444,0.002,0.944444444,0.042;1,0.002,1,0.042" linee="0.888888889,0.042 1,0.042" etichette="0.881,-0.022,8⁄9;0.931,-0.022,17⁄18;0.997,-0.022,1;0.899,0.058,1⁄18;0.956,0.058,1⁄18;1.033,0.011,x" titolo="The midpoint is one eighteenth from either endpoint"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
1-\frac89&=\frac19\\[6pt]
\frac89+\frac12\cdot\frac19
&=\frac{16}{18}+\frac1{18}\\[6pt]
&=\frac{17}{18}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The distance between $\dfrac89$ and $1=\dfrac99$ is one ninth. To find the midpoint, start at the smaller endpoint and move half that distance, or $\dfrac1{18}$. Writing the first endpoint in eighteenths makes it possible to add one part. The resulting number is also $\dfrac1{18}$ from the right endpoint, which equals $\dfrac{18}{18}$. The arithmetic mean gives another check since $\dfrac12\left(\dfrac89+1\right)=\dfrac{17}{18}$. The coordinate lies strictly between the endpoints, and the two distances coincide.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the distance between $-9$ and $14$?

::grafico{xmin=-11 xmax=16.3 ymin=-1.7 ymax=2.6 uguale=1 assi=0 vettori="-10.4,0,15.45,0;-9,1.43,0,1.43;0,1.43,14,1.43" punti="-9,0,pieno;0,0,pieno;14,0,pieno" segmenti="-9,0,-9,1.43;0,0,0,1.43;14,0,14,1.43" etichette="-9.34,-0.62,−9;-0.12,-0.62,0;13.68,-0.62,14;-4.66,1.98,9;6.65,1.98,14;15.59,0.18,x" titolo="The two segments on either side of zero measure 9 and 14"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
|14-(-9)|&=|14+9|\\[6pt]
&=23.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Going from $-9$ to $14$ crosses zero. The first nine units reach the origin and another fourteen reach the second endpoint, so the distance is $23$. Algebraically, crossing zero appears when $14-(-9)$ becomes $14+9$. Starting at $14$ and going to $-9$ instead gives a coordinate change of $-23$. Its absolute value is still $23$ because distance measures the length of the path independently of the direction in which it is travelled.

</div>

## Exercise 3

How far is $\dfrac1{10}$ from $\dfrac1{11}$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\frac1{10}-\frac1{11}\right|
&=\left|\frac{11-10}{110}\right|\\[6pt]
&=\frac1{110}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To measure the distance, subtract the smaller coordinate from the larger one. With the same positive numerator, a smaller denominator gives larger parts. So $\dfrac1{10}$ is greater than $\dfrac1{11}$ and comes first in the positive difference. Since $10$ and $11$ are coprime, their least common multiple is $110$. The endpoints become eleven and ten parts out of one hundred ten, separated by just one such part. A check starts at the smaller coordinate and adds the distance, giving $\dfrac1{11}+\dfrac1{110}=\dfrac1{10}$.

</div>
