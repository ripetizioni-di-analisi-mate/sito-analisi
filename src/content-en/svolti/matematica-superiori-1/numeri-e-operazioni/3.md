## Exercise 1

Calculate $(-8)\cdot(9)$.

$$
(-8)\cdot9=-(8\cdot9)=-72
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying $8$ by $9$ gives $72$, the absolute value of the product. Since $-8$ is negative and $9$ is positive, the product is $-72$. The same calculation is the sum of nine copies of $-8$. Each term moves eight units to the left, so nine terms take us to $-72$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Calculate $3\cdot 9-2\cdot(9+1)$.

$$
3\cdot 9-2\cdot 10=27-20=7
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expression $9+1$ is the factor multiplying $2$. Since that factor equals $10$, the second product is $20$. The first product is independent of the second and equals $27$. Subtracting these two values gives $7$. The result is positive because the first product is larger.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Express $3+\dfrac2{10}$ as a single fraction.

$$
3+\frac2{10}=\frac{30+2}{10}=\frac{16}{5}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To write $3$ in tenths, each whole unit is counted as ten tenths. Three units are thirty tenths, so adding the two tenths already present gives thirty-two tenths. Divide the numerator and denominator by $2$. The result $\dfrac{16}{5}$ is slightly greater than $3$ because $3=\dfrac{15}{5}$.

</div>
