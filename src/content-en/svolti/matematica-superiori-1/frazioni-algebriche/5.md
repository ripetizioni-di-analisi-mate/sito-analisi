## Exercise 1

For $x\ne 8$ and $x\ne -8$, calculate $\dfrac{x-8}{x+8}\cdot\dfrac{x+8}{2(x-8)}$.

$$
\frac{(x-8)(x+8)}{2(x+8)(x-8)}=\frac12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators. The two common linear factors are nonzero under the given conditions. They cancel, leaving only the numerical factor.

The result is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The product of the denominators is $2(x+8)(x-8)$. The numerator has the same two linear factors, which can be canceled throughout the given domain. The only denominator left is $2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x\ne 9$ and $x\ne -9$, calculate $\dfrac{x-9}{x+9}:\dfrac{2(x-9)}{x+9}$.

$$
\frac{x-9}{x+9}\cdot\frac{x+9}{2(x-9)}=\frac12
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The divisor is defined and nonzero on the specified domain. Replace division by multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor. The common factors cancel, leaving the ratio of the coefficients.

The result is $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

The divisor fraction has denominator $x+9$ and numerator $2(x-9)$. Both are nonzero, so the reciprocal is defined. In the product, the two linear factors cancel, leaving $\dfrac12$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which conditions make the expression $\dfrac1{x+8}:\dfrac{x-1}{x+8}$ defined?

- $x\ne -8$
- $x\ne1$
- $x\ne -8\;\text{and}\;x\ne1$
- $x\ne 8\;\text{and}\;x\ne-1$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators of both fractions must be nonzero. The divisor must also be nonzero, so its numerator must not vanish either. Both conditions must hold at the same time.

The result is $x\ne -8\;\text{and}\;x\ne1$.

The value $x=-8$ is excluded by both denominators. The value $x=1$ is excluded because it makes the divisor zero. Every other real input gives two defined fractions and permits division.

</div>
