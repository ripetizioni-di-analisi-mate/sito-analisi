## Exercise 1

What is the condition for $\dfrac{x+1}{x-8}$ to be defined over the real numbers?

- $x\ne 8$
- $x\ne -1$
- $x>8$
- $x\ne -8$

$$
x-8=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x=8,\qquad D=\mathbb R\setminus\{8\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator vanishes at $x=8$ because $8-8=0$. The value $x=-1$ makes only the numerator zero, so the fraction equals zero. The only value excluded from the domain is therefore $8$.

The denominator may have either sign. For example, $x=0$ is admissible and gives $-\dfrac18$, while $x=9$ gives $10$. These values are on opposite sides of $8$, and the operation exists in both cases. This distinguishes a nonzero restriction from a positivity restriction.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which values are excluded from $\dfrac{x+2}{x^2-81}$?

- $x=-2\;\text{or}\;x=9$
- $x=-9\;\text{or}\;x=9$
- $x=9$
- $x=-9$

$$
x^2-81=(x-9)(x+9),\qquad D=\mathbb R\setminus\{-9,9\}.
$$

::grafico{f="x^2-81" xmin=-11 xmax=11 ymin=-90 ymax=35 punti="-9,0,pieno;9,0,pieno" titolo="The denominator is zero exactly at −9 and 9"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator is a difference of squares, $(x-9)(x+9)$. Each factor makes the product zero at a different value. The conditions $x\ne9$ and $x\ne-9$ must both hold.

The two roots appear where the graph of $y=x^2-81$ meets the horizontal axis. The denominator is negative between $-9$ and $9$, but each interior point is admissible. In particular, $x=-2$ gives a fraction whose numerator is zero and whose denominator is $-77$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which conditions make the expression $\dfrac1{x+10}:\dfrac{x-1}{x+10}$ defined?

- $x\ne -10$
- $x\ne1$
- $x\ne -10\;\text{and}\;x\ne1$
- $x\ne 10\;\text{and}\;x\ne-1$

$$
\begin{cases}x+10\ne0\\ x-1\ne0\end{cases}\qquad D=\mathbb R\setminus\{-10,1\}.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At $x=-10$, both fractions have denominator zero. At $x=1$, the fractions are defined but the divisor is zero. Both values are excluded for different reasons.

As a check, at $x=0$ the dividend equals $\dfrac1{10}$ and the divisor equals $-\dfrac1{10}$, so the quotient is $-1$. A negative divisor is admissible. Once both denominators have been checked, only a zero divisor would make the division undefined.

</div>
