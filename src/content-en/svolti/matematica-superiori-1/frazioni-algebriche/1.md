## Exercise 1

What is the condition for $\dfrac{x+1}{x-8}$ to be defined over the real numbers?

- $x\ne 8$
- $x\ne -1$
- $x>8$
- $x\ne -8$

<div class="spiegazione">

An algebraic fraction is defined whenever its denominator is nonzero. Its numerator can be zero. Only the zero of the denominator is excluded.

The result is $x\ne 8$.

The denominator vanishes at $x=8$ because $8-8=0$. The value $x=-1$ makes only the numerator zero, so the fraction equals zero. The only value excluded from the domain is therefore $8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which values are excluded from $\dfrac{x+2}{x^2-81}$?

- $x=-2\;\text{or}\;x=9$
- $x=-9\;\text{or}\;x=9$
- $x=9$
- $x=-9$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator factors into two linear terms. A fraction is undefined if either factor in its denominator vanishes. Both opposite values are therefore excluded.

The result is $x=-9\;\text{or}\;x=9$.

The denominator is a difference of squares, $(x-9)(x+9)$. Each factor makes the product zero at a different value. The conditions $x\ne9$ and $x\ne-9$ must both hold.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Which conditions make the expression $\dfrac1{x+10}:\dfrac{x-1}{x+10}$ defined?

- $x\ne -10$
- $x\ne1$
- $x\ne -10\;\text{and}\;x\ne1$
- $x\ne 10\;\text{and}\;x\ne-1$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominators of both fractions must be nonzero. The divisor must also be nonzero, so its numerator must not vanish either. Both conditions must hold at the same time.

The result is $x\ne -10\;\text{and}\;x\ne1$.

At $x=-10$, both fractions have denominator zero. At $x=1$, the fractions are defined but the divisor is zero. Both values are excluded for different reasons.

</div>
