## Exercise 1

Evaluate $\dfrac{x+1}{x-8}$ at $x=10$.

$$
\frac{10+1}{10-8}=\frac{11}{2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The input $10$ gives denominator $10-8=2$, so the quotient can be evaluated. At the same input, the numerator is $11$. The quotient is $\dfrac{11}{2}$.

The quotient can be checked by multiplying the result by the denominator already evaluated. Indeed, $2\cdot\dfrac{11}{2}=11$, which is the numerator obtained by substitution. This check works while keeping the numerator and denominator exact.

</div>

## Exercise 2

At what value of $x$ is the fraction $\dfrac{x-9}{x+1}$ equal to zero?

$$
x-9=0\Rightarrow x=9,\qquad 9+1\ne0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At $x=9$ the numerator is zero and the denominator is $10$. The point $(9,0)$ is therefore on the graph. The denominator’s zero, $-1$, is excluded from the domain.

For $-1<x<9$ the denominator is positive and the numerator is negative, so the fraction is negative. Just after $9$ both are positive. The graph crosses the axis at $9$ because only the numerator changes sign there.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the zeros of $\dfrac{(x-3)(x+2)}{x-3}$ and state the domain.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}\\
(x-3)(x+2)&=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=3\ \lor\ x=-2\\
&\hspace{9pt} x=-2\in D .
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x+2" xmin=-5 xmax=6 ymin=-3 ymax=8 punti="-2,0,pieno;3,5,vuoto" titolo="One admissible zero and one excluded point"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The product in the numerator suggests two candidates. At $x=3$, the denominator is zero, so that candidate must be excluded before identifying the zeros. At $x=-2$, the denominator is $-5$. The quotient is defined and equals zero. On the domain, simplification gives the line $y=x+2$. Its intersection with the horizontal axis represents the actual zero. The point on the line with horizontal coordinate $3$ is shown as an open circle because cancellation removed the factor that required the exclusion.

</div>
