## Exercise 1

For $x\ne8$, calculate $\dfrac2{x-8}+\dfrac3{x-8}$.

$$
\frac2{x-8}+\frac3{x-8}=\frac5{x-8}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The sum is $\dfrac{2+3}{x-8}$. It gives $5$ at $x=9$, where both addends have denominator one. At $x=8$, both fractions are undefined.

To the left of $8$ the denominator is negative, so both terms are negative. For instance, at $x=7$ the sum is $-2-3=-5$, which agrees with the final fraction. The check keeps the common numerator and the sign of the denominator separate.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x\ne9$, calculate $\dfrac{x+2}{x-9}-\dfrac{x-1}{x-9}$.

$$
\frac{x+2-(x-1)}{x-9}=\frac3{x-9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting the numerator $x-1$ gives $x+2-x+1=3$. For example, at $x=10$ the two numerators are $12$ and $9$ and the denominator is one. Their difference is $3$.

Applying the minus sign only to $x$ would give a numerator of $1$. Substituting $x=0$ checks the signs without using the rearrangement. The original fractions give $-\dfrac29-\dfrac19=-\dfrac13$, while a numerator of $1$ would give $-\dfrac19$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $x\ne10$, calculate $\dfrac{2x}{x-10}-2$.

$$
\frac{2x-2(x-10)}{x-10}=\frac{20}{x-10}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write the number $2$ as $\dfrac{2(x-10)}{x-10}$. The minus sign changes the new numerator to $-2(x-10)=-2x+20$, leaving $20$. At $x=11$, the original difference is $22-2=20$.

The final numerator is always positive. The denominator determines the sign. It is negative to the left of $10$ and positive to the right. At $x=0$ the original expression equals $-2$, and the final fraction gives $\dfrac{20}{-10}=-2$, as expected.

</div>
