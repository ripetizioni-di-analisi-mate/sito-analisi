## Exercise 1

For $x\ne 8$, simplify $\dfrac{x^2-64}{x-8}$.

$$
\frac{(x-8)(x+8)}{x-8}=x+8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is $(x-8)(x+8)$. The given conditions allow $x-8$ to be cancelled, leaving $x+8$. At $x=8$, this formula would give $16$, but the original quotient is undefined.

For example, $x=7$ gives $15$ in both the original fraction and the linear formula. Substituting $x=8$ into the same formula gives $16$, but that value belongs to the full line. The point must be excluded because the original expression would give $\dfrac00$ there.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x\ne0$, simplify $\dfrac{9x^2+18x}{9x}$.

$$
\frac{9x(x+2)}{9x}=x+2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring the numerator gives $9x(x+2)$. At $x=1$, the quotient evaluates to $\dfrac{27}{9}=3$, the same as $x+2$. Although the remaining polynomial is $2$ at zero, the original quotient requires $x\ne0$.

Expanding $9x(x+2)$ recovers both original terms and checks the factorisation. At $x=-2$ the new numerator is zero. The original denominator is $-18$, so $-2$ is a zero of the fraction and belongs to its domain.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The fraction $\dfrac{x-10}{x-10}$ simplifies to $1$. Which statement correctly preserves its domain?

- It equals $1$ for every real number, including $x=10$
- It equals $0$ when $x=10$
- It is defined only for $x>10$
- It equals $1$ for every $x\ne 10$

$$
\frac{x-10}{x-10}=1\quad (x\ne10).
$$

::grafico{f="1" xmin=6 xmax=14 ymin=-1 ymax=3 punti="10,1,vuoto" titolo="The fraction equals 1 with only x = 10 excluded"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Before simplifying, substituting $x=10$ would give a quotient with denominator zero. At $x=11$, both parts of the quotient are $1$ and the quotient is $1$. The only excluded point is $10$.

At $x=9$ the fraction is a quotient of two equal negative numbers, so it is $1$. The same happens on the right of $10$, where the numbers are positive. The open circle distinguishes the constant formula from the set of points where it represents the original fraction.

</div>
