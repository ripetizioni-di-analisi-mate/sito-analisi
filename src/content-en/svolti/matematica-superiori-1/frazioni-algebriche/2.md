## Exercise 1

For $x\ne 8$, simplify $\dfrac{x^2-64}{x-8}$.

$$
\frac{(x-8)(x+8)}{x-8}=x+8
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor the numerator as a difference of squares. The common factor can be canceled because it is nonzero on the stated domain. The original condition still applies.

The result is $x + 8$.

The numerator is $(x-8)(x+8)$. The given conditions allow $x-8$ to be canceled, leaving $x+8$. At $x=8$, this formula would give $16$, but the original quotient is undefined.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x\ne0$, simplify $\dfrac{9x^2+18x}{9x}$.

$$
\frac{9x(x+2)}{9x}=x+2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factor out the common factor $9 x$ from the numerator. The denominator equals that common factor. After cancellation, the polynomial in the second factor remains.

The result is $x + 2$.

Factoring the numerator gives $9x(x+2)$. At $x=1$, the quotient evaluates to $\dfrac{27}{9}=3$, the same as $x+2$. Although the remaining polynomial is $2$ at zero, the original quotient requires $x\ne0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The fraction $\dfrac{x-10}{x-10}$ simplifies to $1$. Which statement correctly preserves its domain?

- It equals $1$ for every real number, including $x=10$
- It equals $0$ when $x=10$
- It is defined only for $x>10$
- It equals $1$ for every $x\ne 10$

<div class="spiegazione">

Simplification requires the canceled factor to be nonzero. The value excluded by the original denominator remains excluded. The simplified formula represents the same fraction only on that domain.

The fraction equals $1$ for every $x\ne 10$.

Before simplifying, substituting $x=10$ would give a quotient with denominator zero. At $x=11$, both parts of the quotient are $1$ and the quotient is $1$. The only excluded point is $10$.

</div>
