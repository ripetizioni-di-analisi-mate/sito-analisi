## Exercise 1

Find the domain and simplify $\dfrac{1+\dfrac1x}{1-\dfrac1x}-1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{0,1\}\\[6pt]
\frac{1+\frac1x}{1-\frac1x}-1
&=\frac{x+1}{x-1}-1\\[6pt]
&=\frac{x+1-(x-1)}{x-1}
=\frac2{x-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two inner fractions require $x\ne0$. The outer denominator must also be nonzero, and its form $\dfrac{x-1}{x}$ requires $x\ne1$. The factor $x$ is cancelled only after these checks. To subtract the integer $1$, write it with the same denominator and subtract the entire numerator $x-1$. The final formula shows the exclusion of $1$, while the exclusion of $0$ must remain in the separately stated domain.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Evaluate $\dfrac1{(x-2)^2}-\dfrac1{x^2-4}$ and check the result at $x=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-2,2\}\\[6pt]
\frac1{(x-2)^2}-\frac1{x^2-4}
&=\frac{(x+2)-(x-2)}{(x-2)^2(x+2)}\\[6pt]
&=\frac4{(x-2)^2(x+2)}\\[6pt]
\frac14-\left(-\frac14\right)&=\frac12=\frac4{4\cdot2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The second denominator factors as $(x-2)(x+2)$. It shares the factor $x-2$ with the first denominator, but with a different multiplicity. The common denominator needs two copies of $x-2$ and one copy of $x+2$. The factors added to the two numerators are different. Subtraction applies to the entire second factor, leaving $4$. At $0$, the original expression subtracts a negative number from a positive number, so the check helps catch a sign error.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the values of $x$ for which $\dfrac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ equals $3x-3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}\\[6pt]
\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}&=3x-3\qquad(x\in D)\\[6pt]
x+3&=3x-3\\[6pt]
2x&=6.
\end{aligned}
$$

::grafico{f="x+3;3*x-3" xmin=-2 xmax=6 ymin=-7 ymax=14 punti="3,6,vuoto" titolo="The formulas would meet at the point excluded from the fraction"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring gives a linear equation once the exclusion of $3$ has been recorded. The calculation gives $x=3$ as the only candidate. The original fraction has a zero denominator at exactly that point, so the solution set is empty. Geometrically, the two full lines would intersect at $(3,6)$. The graph of the fraction is the line with that point excluded, so the intersection of the full lines yields no solution of the original equation.

</div>
