## Exercise 1

For $x\ne 8$ and $x\ne -8$, calculate $\dfrac1{x-8}+\dfrac1{x+8}$.

$$
\frac{x+8+x-8}{(x-8)(x+8)}=\frac{2x}{x^2-64}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product of the two linear factors is a common denominator. The new numerators are x plus $8$ and x minus $8$, respectively. Their constant terms cancel when added.

The result is $\dfrac{2 x}{x^{2} - 64}$.

The first fraction is missing the factor $x+8$. The second is missing $x-8$. The new numerators add to $2x$. At $x=0$, the original fractions are opposites and the result is zero too.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x\ne 9$ and $x\ne -9$, calculate $\dfrac1{x-9}-\dfrac1{x+9}$.

$$
\frac{(x+9)-(x-9)}{x^2-81}=\frac{18}{x^2-81}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Use the product of the two denominators as a common denominator. Subtract the entire second numerator. The terms in x cancel and the two numerical terms add.

The result is $\dfrac{18}{x^{2} - 81}$.

For the subtraction, the numerators become $x+9$ and $x-9$. The second is subtracted, so $x+9-x+9=18$. At $x=0$, the expression gives $-\dfrac19-\dfrac19=-\dfrac29$, as does the final fraction.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $x\ne10$, rewrite $1+\dfrac{10}{x-10}$ as a single fraction.

$$
1+\frac{10}{x-10}=\frac{x-10+10}{x-10}=\frac x{x-10}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Write one as the denominator divided by itself. Add the two numerators. Minus $10$ and plus $10$ cancel.

The result is $\dfrac{x}{x - 10}$.

The number $1$ contributes $x-10$ to the combined numerator. Adding $10$ leaves $x$. At $x=0$, the fraction equals zero, as does the sum $1+\dfrac{10}{-10}$.

</div>
