## Exercise 1

For $x\ne-8$, simplify $\dfrac{-x-8}{x+8}$.

$$
\frac{-(x+8)}{x+8}=-1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator is $-(x+8)$. Cancel the factor $x+8$ within the domain to leave $-1$. At $x=0$ the original expression equals $\dfrac{-8}{8}=-1$.

At $x=-9$ the numerator is $1$ and the denominator is $-1$, so the quotient is again $-1$. Changing the denominator’s sign also changes the opposite numerator’s sign. The quotient therefore remains constant on the two intervals separated by $-8$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $x\ne -9$, calculate $\dfrac{x^2-81}{(x+9)^2}$.

$$
\frac{(x-9)(x+9)}{(x+9)^2}=\frac{x-9}{x+9}
$$

::grafico{f="(x-9)/(x+9)" xmin=-15 xmax=14 ymin=-5 ymax=6 punti="9,0,pieno" asintoti="x=-9;y=1" titolo="One factor remains in the denominator after cancellation"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The numerator has one copy of the factor $x+9$. The denominator has two, so one remains below the fraction bar after cancellation. The exclusion of $-9$ is still visible in the final formula.

The input $9$ makes the numerator zero and leaves the denominator equal to $18$. On $-9<x<9$ the numerator is negative and the denominator positive. To the right of $9$ both are positive, so the ratio passes from negative to positive through the allowed zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Simplify $\dfrac{-(x-4)^2}{(x-4)(x+2)}$ on its domain and evaluate it at $x=0$ and $x=6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{-(x-4)^2}{(x-4)(x+2)}&=-\frac{x-4}{x+2}\qquad(x\ne4,-2)\\[6pt]
-\frac{0-4}{0+2}&=2\\[6pt]
-\frac{6-4}{6+2}&=-\frac14 .
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The original denominator excludes $4$ and $-2$. One copy of $x-4$ cancels and the minus sign remains in front of the fraction. At $0$ the ratio before that minus sign is negative, so the result is positive. At $6$ both terms of the ratio are positive and the leading sign makes the result negative. The value $4$ would be a zero of the new numerator, but it remains excluded because it made the original denominator zero.

</div>
