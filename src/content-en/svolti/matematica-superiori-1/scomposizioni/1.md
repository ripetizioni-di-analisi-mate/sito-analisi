## Exercise 1

Factor out the greatest common factor with positive coefficient from $8x^2+16x$. For $x>0$, interpret the product as the area of a rectangle.

$$
8x^2+16x=8x\cdot x+8x\cdot2=8x(x+2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The greatest common divisor of $8$ and $16$ is $8$. The smaller exponent on $x$ is $1$, which gives the common factor $8x$. Read the quotients as the widths of two adjacent rectangles with a shared height of $8x$. Joining them gives a total width of $x+2$. At $x=3$, the area is $24\cdot5=120$, which also equals $72+48=120$. The factorisation remains an identity for $x\le 0$, where the area interpretation no longer applies.

Trying to factor out $8x^2$ would require a width of $\dfrac{2}{x}$ for the second piece and therefore a quotient that is not a polynomial.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $-12x^3+18x^2-30x$ by extracting a negative monomial. Check the signs of the quotients.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-12x^3+18x^2-30x&=(-6x)(2x^2)+(-6x)(-3x)+(-6x)\cdot5\\
&=-6x(2x^2-3x+5).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficients have greatest common divisor $6$, and every term contains at least one factor of $x$. Extracting $-6x$ makes the leading coefficient inside the brackets positive. The term $18x^2$ comes from $(-6x)(-3x)$, so the sign of $-3x$ matters. At $x=-1$, the outer factor is $6$ and the expression in brackets is $2+3+5=10$. The original polynomial gives the same value, $12+18+30=60$. The three products in the first line hold for every $x$.

Choosing $6x$ as the outer factor would reverse every sign inside the brackets. The polynomial would stay the same because the product would still contain a single factor of $-1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the greatest common factor with positive coefficient of $15x^3yz-25x^2y^2+10x^2y$. Explain where the variable $z$ stays.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{gcd}(15,25,10)&=5,\\
\min(3,2,2)&=2,\quad \min(1,2,1)=1,\quad \min(1,0,0)=0,\\
15x^3yz-25x^2y^2+10x^2y&=5x^2y(3xz-5y+2).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Read the exponent of each variable across all three terms. For $z$ the exponents are $1,0,0$, so $z$ stays only in the first quotient $3xz$. The second quotient is $-5y$ because one factor of $y$ is already outside. The third quotient is the number $2$ because the third term has exactly the same variable part as the common factor. That constant term also shows that no further common factor containing a variable can be extracted from the brackets.

Setting $z=0$ removes only the first term of the original expression. In the factored form, it removes exactly the contribution $3xz$ inside the brackets.

</div>
