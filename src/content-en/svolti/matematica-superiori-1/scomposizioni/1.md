## Exercise 1

Which product equals $8x^2+16x$?

$$
8x^2+16x=8x(x+2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both terms contain the given numerical factor $8$ and an x. Dividing each term by the common factor gives x and two. The distributive property recovers the original polynomial.

The result is $8 x \left(x + 2\right)$.

Dividing the two terms by $8x$ gives $x$ and $2$. In the final product, the factor $x$ explains why the polynomial vanishes at zero. The distributive property recovers both original terms.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $-9x^2-18x$.

$$
-9x^2-18x=-9x(x+2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The common factor may be chosen negative. Dividing both terms by minus $9 x$ gives x and two. Applying the distributive property gives two negative terms again.

The result is $- 9 x \left(x + 2\right)$.

With $-9x$ factored out, the ratio of the coefficients in the second term is $(-18):(-9)=2$. Both coefficients of the remaining binomial are positive. Multiplying by $-9x$ recovers the two negative terms.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $x^2y+10xy^2$.

$$
x^2y+10xy^2=xy(x+10y)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Both terms contain an x and a y. After taking out their product, the first term leaves x and the second leaves $10 y$. Multiply the common factor by the result to verify the factorization.

The result is $x y \left(x + 10 y\right)$.

The variable $x$ has exponents $2$ and $1$, while $y$ has exponents $1$ and $2$. Use the smaller exponent of each, so the common factor is $xy$. The remaining terms are $x$ and $10y$.

</div>
