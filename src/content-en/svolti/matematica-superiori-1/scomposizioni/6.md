## Exercise 1

Find the GCD of $42x^3y^2$ and $70x^2y^4z$. Verify that it divides both monomials.

$$
\begin{aligned}
42&=2\cdot3\cdot7,\qquad 70=2\cdot5\cdot7,\\
D&=14x^2y^2,\\
42x^3y^2&=D\cdot3x,\qquad 70x^2y^4z=D\cdot5y^2z.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The common prime factors of the coefficients are $2$ and $7$. The minimum exponents of $x$, $y$, and $z$ are $2,2,0$, respectively. These choices give $14x^2y^2$. The remaining monomials $3x$ and $5y^2z$ have coprime coefficients and no variable in common. There is therefore no further variable factor or integer greater than one that can be moved into $D$ while keeping both equalities.

For example, $14x^3y^2$ divides the first monomial but fails to divide the second, in which $x$ is only squared. This rules out the higher exponent.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the LCM of $16x^2yz$ and $24xy^3$. Find the multiplier needed to reach it from each monomial.

$$
\begin{aligned}
16&=2^4,\qquad 24=2^3\cdot3,\qquad L=2^4\cdot3\,x^2y^3z,\\
(16x^2yz)\cdot3y^2&=48x^2y^3z,\\
(24xy^3)\cdot2xz&=48x^2y^3z.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each prime factor of the coefficient, take the largest exponent. Applying the same rule to the variables gives $x^2$, $y^3$, and $z$. The first monomial already has $x^2$ and $z$, but it still needs the numerical factor $3$ and two factors of $y$. The second needs a numerical factor $2$, one $x$, and one $z$. Lowering any exponent in the result would prevent at least one of the two monomials from dividing it.

The monomial $24x^2y^3z$ would already be a multiple of $24xy^3$, but $16$ does not divide its coefficient. This is why $2^4$ is required.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The monomial $M=18x^3y$ and a monomial $N$ with positive integer coefficient have GCD $D=6x^2y$ and LCM $L=90x^3y^2$. Recover $N$ and verify the data.

$$
\begin{aligned}
DL&=MN,\\
N&=\frac{(6x^2y)(90x^3y^2)}{18x^3y}=30x^2y^2,\\
\operatorname{gcd}(18,30)&=6,\qquad \operatorname{lcm}(18,30)=90.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For each prime factor and each variable, the minimum and maximum exponents add to the same sum as the two original exponents. This gives $DL=MN$, which lets us find $N$ by monomial division. To check the variable parts, the exponents of $x$ in $M,N$ are $3,2$, and those of $y$ are $1,2$. The minimum and maximum pairs give $x^2y$ and $x^3y^2$. Checking the coefficients in the final line confirms both givens.

You could also work backward one variable at a time. The maximum exponent of $y$ is $2$, and since $M$ contains only one factor of $y$, the exponent $2$ must come from $N$.

</div>
