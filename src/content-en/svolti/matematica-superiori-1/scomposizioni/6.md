## Exercise 1

What is the greatest common monomial divisor of $48x^3y^2$ and $72x^2y^4$ with a positive integer coefficient?

$$
\gcd=24x^2y^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient is the greatest common divisor of the numerical coefficients. For each shared variable, take the smaller of the exponents. The resulting monomial divides both given monomials.

The result is $24 x^{2} y^{2}$.

The greatest common divisor of $48$ and $72$ is $24$. The smaller exponents of $x$ and $y$ are both $2$. Dividing the two monomials by $24x^2y^2$ gives $2x$ and $3y^2$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

What is the least common monomial multiple of $18x^2y$ and $27xy^3$ with a positive integer coefficient?

$$
\operatorname{lcm}=54x^2y^3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The coefficient is the least common multiple of the numerical coefficients. For each variable that occurs, take its greatest exponent. The result contains the factors needed to be a multiple of both.

The result is $54 x^{2} y^{3}$.

The least common multiple of $18$ and $27$ is $54$. The greater exponents are $2$ on $x$ and $3$ on $y$. The result is the first monomial times $3y^2$ and the second times $2x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For $x\ne0$, what quotient results when $10x$ is factored out of $10x^2+30x$?

$$
\frac{10x^2+30x}{10x}=x+3
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring out a term means dividing each term by that factor. The first term gives x and the second gives three. The remaining polynomial is their sum.

The result is $x + 3$.

The factor $10x$ multiplies $x$ in the first term and $3$ in the second. Therefore $10x(x+3)=10x^2+30x$ is an identity. To calculate the quotient, require $x\ne0$.

</div>
