## Exercise 1

Which product is a factorization of $x^2-81$?

$$
x^2-81=(x-9)(x+9)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polynomial is a difference of squares. Its factorization uses the sum and the difference of the two bases. When the factors are multiplied, the mixed products cancel.

The result is $\left(x - 9\right) \left(x + 9\right)$.

Since $81=9^2$, the constant term comes from the product $9\cdot(-9)$. The sum of $9x$ and $-9x$ is zero, so the expanded polynomial has only the quadratic term and the constant term.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which product is a factorization of $4x^2-81y^2$?

$$
4x^2-81y^2=(2x-9y)(2x+9y)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases of the two squares are twice x and $9 y$. Factor the difference of squares into the product of the sum and the difference of those bases. Take the square root of the coefficient four too.

The result is $\left(2 x - 9 y\right) \left(2 x + 9 y\right)$.

The coefficient $4$ is the square of $2$, and $81$ is the square of $9$. The full bases are therefore $2x$ and $9y$. On expansion, the mixed products $18xy$ and $-18xy$ cancel.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $x^4-4096$ completely.

$$
x^4-4096=(x^2-64)(x^2+64)=(x-8)(x+8)(x^2+64)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First apply the difference of squares with bases x squared and $8$ squared. The factor containing the minus sign is itself a difference of squares. Factor it further.

The result is $\left(x - 8\right) \left(x + 8\right) \left(x^{2} + 64\right)$.

We have $4096=64^2$ and $64=8^2$. After the first step, the factor $x^2-64$ can be factored further. The factor $x^2+64$ remains positive for every real $x$.

</div>
