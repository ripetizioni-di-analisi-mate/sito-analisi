## Exercise 1

Factor $36x^2-25y^2$. Set $y=1$, find where the product is zero, and connect the zeros to the graph.

$$
\begin{aligned}
36x^2-25y^2&=(6x-5y)(6x+5y),\\
y=1:\quad (6x-5)(6x+5)=0&\iff x\in\left\{-\frac56,\frac56\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases of the two squares are $6x$ and $5y$. The binomials have the same $6x$ term and opposite terms in $y$, so the cross products $30xy$ and $-30xy$ cancel. When $y=1$, the first factor vanishes at $5/6$ and the second at $-5/6$. The parabola is symmetric about the vertical axis because $x$ occurs only squared. The two marked intersections are therefore the same distance from the origin.

Setting $y=0$ instead makes both factors equal to $6x$. The two zeros coincide at $0$, and the graph touches the axis at a single point.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $(x+1)^2-(2x-3)^2$. Check the coefficient of the linear term.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A-B&=(x+1)-(2x-3)=4-x,\\
A+B&=(x+1)+(2x-3)=3x-2,\\
A^2-B^2&=(4-x)(3x-2)=-3x^2+14x-8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Here the bases are whole binomials, $A=x+1$ and $B=2x-3$. In $A-B$, the minus sign applies to both $2x$ and $-3$, so the constant becomes $1+3=4$. To check the linear term, expanding the squares gives $2x-(-12x)=14x$. Multiplying the factors gives $12x+2x=14x$ as well. The linear coefficients agree because the entire second binomial was subtracted.

As a numerical check, both original squares equal $25$ at $x=4$. Their difference is zero, in agreement with the factor $4-x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $x^4-1296$ over the reals until at most a quadratic remains. Justify where you stop.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^4-1296&=(x^2)^2-36^2\\
&=(x^2-36)(x^2+36)\\
&=(x-6)(x+6)(x^2+36).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first difference of squares produces the factors $x^2-36$ and $x^2+36$. The same identity applies again to the first factor. In the second, $x^2\ge 0$ for every real $x$, so $x^2+36\ge 36$. A quadratic with a real linear factor $ax+b$, where $a\ne 0$, would have the zero $-b/a$. The inequality rules that out for $x^2+36$. The other remaining factors already have degree one.

You can verify the two real zeros $6$ and $-6$ directly. Their fourth powers agree because the exponent is even, and both equal $1296$.

</div>
