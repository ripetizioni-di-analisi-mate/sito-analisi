## Exercise 1

Check the proposed factorisation $15x^2+11x+2=(3x+1)(5x+2)$ by comparing every coefficient.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3x+1)(5x+2)&=15x^2+6x+5x+2\\
&=15x^2+11x+2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The quadratic term comes from $(3x)(5x)$ and the constant term from $1\cdot2$. For the linear term, add both cross products, $(3x)\cdot2=6x$ and $1\cdot(5x)=5x$. This step identifies the correct placement of the constants. Swapping $1$ and $2$ gives $(3x+2)(5x+1)$, which still has leading term $15x^2$ and constant term $2$, but the middle coefficient becomes $3+10=13$. Comparing all three coefficients verifies the identity.

At $x=0$, both arrangements of the constants would give $2$. To distinguish between the two proposals by substitution, choose a nonzero value.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The polynomials $P(x)=x^2-4x+3$ and $Q(x)=-2x+3$ agree at $0$ and $2$. Check those values and find where they differ.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(0)=Q(0)&=3,\qquad P(2)=Q(2)=-1,\\
P(x)-Q(x)&=x^2-2x=x(x-2),\\
P(1)&=0,\qquad Q(1)=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

After checking the two values, subtract one polynomial from the other. Factoring out $x$ shows that the difference vanishes exactly when $x=0$ or $x=2$. The graph displays this difference. At every other point the polynomial values differ. At $x=1$, for example, the difference is $-1$. Two pointwise checks can therefore agree even when the polynomials have different coefficients.

The sign of the difference also tells us where $P$ is less than $Q$. This happens between $0$ and $2$, where $x$ and $x-2$ have opposite signs.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Suppose $6x^2+bx+c=(2x+a)(3x-2)$ for every $x$ and the polynomial equals $-10$ at $0$. Find $a$, $b$, and $c$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
c&=-2a=-10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=5,\\
(2x+a)(3x-2)&=6x^2+(3a-4)x-2a,\\
b&=3\cdot5-4=11,\qquad c=-10.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At $x=0$, every term containing $x$ vanishes, so the given value fixes the constant term and gives $-2a=-10$. After finding $a=5$, add the cross products $3ax$ and $-4x$ to obtain the linear coefficient. The quadratic term is already fixed by $(2x)(3x)$. The resulting polynomial $6x^2+11x-10$ can be checked by multiplying $(2x+5)(3x-2)$, which recovers all three coefficients.

The factored form gives another check. At $x=\dfrac{2}{3}$ the factor $3x-2$ is zero, and substituting that value into the expansion gives $\dfrac{8}{3}+\dfrac{22}{3}-10=0$.

</div>
