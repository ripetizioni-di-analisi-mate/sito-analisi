## Exercise 1

Factor $xy+8x+2y+16$.

$$
xy+8x+2y+16=x(y+8)+2(y+8)=(x+2)(y+8)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Group the first two terms and the last two terms. Factor each group to leave the same binomial. Then factor out the common binomial.

The result is $\left(x + 2\right) \left(y + 8\right)$.

The first two terms leave $y+8$ after factoring out $x$. The other two leave $y+8$ after factoring out $2$. The remaining binomial occurs twice and is factored out in the second step.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $9x^3-9x$.

$$
9x^3-9x=9x(x^2-1)=9x(x-1)(x+1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First factor out the common monomial. The remaining factor is a difference of squares. Factor it too to complete the calculation.

The result is $9 x \left(x - 1\right) \left(x + 1\right)$.

Factoring out $9x$ leaves $x^2-1$. This factor vanishes at $1$ and $-1$, so it factors as $(x-1)(x+1)$. The value $0$ zeros the product too, through the original factor $x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $x^4-10000$ completely.

$$
x^4-10000=(x^2-100)(x^2+100)=(x-10)(x+10)(x^2+100)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

First apply the difference of squares with bases x squared and $10$ squared. The factor containing the minus sign is itself a difference of squares. Factor it further.

The result is $\left(x - 10\right) \left(x + 10\right) \left(x^{2} + 100\right)$.

Since $10000=100^2$, the first step gives the factors $x^2-100$ and $x^2+100$. Only the first has real zeros, $10$ and $-10$. The second is at least $100$ for every real $x$.

</div>
