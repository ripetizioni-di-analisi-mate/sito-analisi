## Exercise 1

Factor $ab-4a+3b-12$ by grouping and interpret the result as an area when $a>0$ and $b>4$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
ab-4a+3b-12&=(ab-4a)+(3b-12)\\
&=a(b-4)+3(b-4)=(a+3)(b-4).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Grouping the terms containing $a$ gives $a(b-4)$. Factoring out $3$ from the other two terms leaves the same expression in brackets. In the diagram, the strips have heights $a$ and $3$. Removing a strip of width $4$ from each leaves the common width $b-4$. For instance, with $a=2$ and $b=7$, the two remaining areas are $6$ and $9$. The joined rectangle has area $5\cdot3=15$.

Grouping by columns gives $b(a+3)-4(a+3)$. The common factor would then be $a+3$, and the same product would result in reverse order.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $2x(1-x)+3(x-1)$. Identify which term changes sign when the binomials are rewritten to match.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1-x&=-(x-1),\\
2x(1-x)+3(x-1)&=-2x(x-1)+3(x-1)\\
&=(x-1)(3-2x).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two binomials are opposites. Rewriting $1-x$ as $-(x-1)$ moves the factor $-1$ into the coefficient of the first product, which becomes $-2x$. Now $x-1$ can be factored out. A check at $x=0$ gives $-3$ in both the original expression and the product. For a complete check, distribution recovers $3(x-1)-2x(x-1)$, and the final term is equal to $2x(1-x)$.

Checking the degree is another way to support the algebra. The quadratic term must be $-2x^2$ because it comes from $2x\cdot(-x)$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $10x^4-40x^2$ completely using two techniques. State how many times each linear factor appears.

$$
\begin{aligned}
10x^4-40x^2&=10x^2(x^2-4)\\
&=10x^2(x-2)(x+2),\\
x^2(x-2)(x+2)&=0\iff x\in\{-2,0,2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The common factor $10x^2$ appears before any special product is applied. What remains is $x^2-4$, the difference of the squares of $x$ and $2$. The factors $x-2$ and $x+2$ each appear once. The factor $x$ appears twice because its exponent is $2$. The number $10$ is a nonzero constant. There are therefore three distinct zeros, and $0$ corresponds to a repeated factor.

For $-2<x<2$ and $x\ne0$, the factor $10x^2$ is positive and $(x-2)(x+2)$ is negative. The graph lies below the axis on both sides of $0$.

</div>
