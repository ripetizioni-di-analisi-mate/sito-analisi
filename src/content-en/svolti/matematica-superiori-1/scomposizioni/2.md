## Exercise 1

Which product is a factorization of $x^2-64$?

$$
x^2-64=(x-8)(x+8)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The polynomial is a difference of squares. Its factorization uses the sum and the difference of the two bases. When the factors are multiplied, the mixed products cancel.

The result is $\left(x - 8\right) \left(x + 8\right)$.

The bases of the squares are $x$ and $8$. The product $(x-8)(x+8)$ has constant term $-64$, and the two mixed terms $8x$ and $-8x$ cancel.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $x^2+18x+81$.

$$
x^2+18x+81=(x+9)^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The first and third terms are squares. The middle term is twice the product of the bases with a positive sign. The polynomial is therefore the square of their sum.

The result is $\left(x + 9\right)^{2}$.

The bases are $x$ and $9$. The middle term is $2\cdot x\cdot9=18x$. The form $(x+9)^2$ also shows that the polynomial vanishes at $x=-9$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $x^2-20x+100$.

$$
x^2-20x+100=(x-10)^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The outside terms are squares and the middle term is the negative of twice the product. The trinomial is therefore the square of a difference. The constant term stays positive in the expansion.

The result is $\left(x - 10\right)^{2}$.

The middle term is $-20x$, so the signed numerical base is $-10$. The binomial square vanishes at $x=10$. Substituting into the trinomial gives $100-200+100=0$.

</div>
