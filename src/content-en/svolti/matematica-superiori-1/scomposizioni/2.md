## Exercise 1

Factor $x^2-64$. For $x=10$, use the product to find the area left after removing a square of side $8$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2-64&=(x-8)(x+8),\\
(10-8)(10+8)&=2\cdot18=36.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Since $64=8^2$, the two squares give the bases $x$ and $8$. When the conjugate binomials are multiplied, the terms $8x$ and $-8x$ cancel. To see the area at $x=10$, put the smaller square in a corner of the larger one. The remaining area splits into one rectangle with sides $2$ and $10$ and one with sides $2$ and $8$. Rotate the second piece by a quarter turn so the rectangles can join along their common side of length $2$. The result is a rectangle with sides $2$ and $18$.

The diagram uses positive lengths. Expanding $(x-8)(x+8)$ shows that the cross terms cancel, proving the algebraic identity for the other values of $x$ as well.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Determine whether $4x^2+12xy+9y^2$ is the square of a binomial. Interpret the term $12xy$ for $x,y>0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x^2&=(2x)^2,\qquad 9y^2=(3y)^2,\\
2(2x)(3y)&=12xy,\\
4x^2+12xy+9y^2&=(2x+3y)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The square terms suggest $2x$ and $3y$. Multiplying $(2x+3y)$ by itself gives two copies of $(2x)(3y)$, both positive. A square of side $2x+3y$ splits into four pieces when you mark off lengths $2x$ and $3y$ along each side. The two rectangular pieces each have area $6xy$. This accounts for the coefficient $12$ and for the variable part $xy$, in which both variables have exponent one.

Replacing the sum with $2x-3y$ would preserve the squared terms in the algebra, while the middle term would become $-12xy$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $25t^2-30t+9$ and find where it equals zero. Describe what the graph does at that point.

$$
\begin{aligned}
25t^2-30t+9&=(5t)^2-2(5t)\cdot3+3^2=(5t-3)^2,\\
(5t-3)^2=0&\iff t=\frac35.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The doubled product is $2\cdot5t\cdot3=30t$. The minus sign in the middle term indicates the difference between the bases, which gives $(5t-3)^2$. The square equals zero exactly when its base does. For any $t$ other than $3/5$, the base is nonzero and the square is positive. The graph touches the horizontal axis at $(3/5,0)$ and lies above it on both sides.

The vertical intercept can be found from the constant term $9$ or by setting $t=0$ in the factored form, which gives $(-3)^2$.

</div>
