## Exercise 1

For $P(x)=(2x-3)(x+4)$, find the zeros and the intervals where $P$ is positive or negative.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x)=0&\iff x\in\left\{-4,\frac32\right\},\\
P(x)>0&\iff x<-4\ \text{or}\ x>\frac32,\\
P(x)<0&\iff -4<x<\frac32.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $2x-3$ goes from negative to positive at $3/2$, while $x+4$ changes sign at $-4$. To the left of $-4$ both factors are negative and the product is positive. Between the two zeros, $x+4$ is positive and $2x-3$ is negative. To the right of $3/2$ both are positive. The graph crosses the axis at each zero and lies below the axis only between those crossings.

To check the three intervals numerically, take $x=-5$, $0$, and $2$. The corresponding values are $13$, $-12$, and $6$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For $P(x)=-2(x+1)^2(x-3)$, determine at which zero the graph crosses the axis and at which zero it touches it.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x)=0&\iff x\in\{-1,3\},\\
P(x)>0&\iff x<3\ \text{and}\ x\ne -1,\\
P(x)<0&\iff x>3.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Near $-1$, the square $(x+1)^2$ is positive on both sides and vanishes only at $-1$. Also, $-2(x-3)$ is positive for every $x<3$. The product therefore stays above the axis before and after touching it at $-1$. At $3$, by contrast, the square is positive and the factor $-2(x-3)$ changes sign from positive to negative. This is where the graph crosses the axis.

The outer factor $-2$ determines which side of the axis the curve is on. Replacing it with $2$ would preserve the zeros and reverse the signs.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A quadratic polynomial vanishes at $-2$ and $5$, and equals $30$ when $x=0$. Write its factored and expanded forms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(x)&=a(x+2)(x-5),\\
30=P(0)&=-10a\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=-3,\\
P(x)&=-3(x+2)(x-5)=-3x^2+9x+30.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product $(x+2)(x-5)$ has exactly the required zeros. If $a$ is the leading coefficient of $P$, the difference between $P$ and $a(x+2)(x-5)$ has degree at most one and vanishes at both points. A linear expression with two distinct zeros is identically zero, so the first line holds for the whole polynomial. The value at $0$ determines the factor $a$ and matches the constant term in the expanded form.

The negative outer coefficient makes the parabola open downward. The two zeros still come from the binomials because that coefficient is nonzero.

</div>
