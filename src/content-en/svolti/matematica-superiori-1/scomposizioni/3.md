## Exercise 1

Factor $x^2+10x+16$ by listing the positive factor pairs of $16$. Read the zeros from the product.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1+16&=17,\quad 2+8=10,\quad 4+4=8,\\
x^2+10x+16&=(x+2)(x+8),\\
(x+2)(x+8)=0&\iff x=-2\ \text{or}\ x=-8.
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A positive product and a positive sum require two positive integers. The factor pairs $1,16$, $2,8$, and $4,4$ cover every candidate up to order. The pair $2,8$ gives both the constant term $16$ and the coefficient $10$ of $x$. Each binomial vanishes when $x$ is the opposite of its constant term. On the graph, the parabola meets the horizontal axis at $-8$ and $-2$ and lies below it between those values.

To check the sign in the middle interval, use its midpoint $x=-5$: the factors are $-3$ and $3$, with product $-9$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Factor $x^2-x-12$. Use the sign of the constant term to narrow down the choice of integers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
12-1&=11,\quad 6-2=4,\quad 4-3=1,\\
(-4)+3&=-1,\qquad (-4)\cdot3=-12,\\
x^2-x-12&=(x-4)(x+3).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The product $-12$ requires opposite signs. The sum is $-1$, so the number with greater absolute value must be negative. The positive factor pairs of $12$ are $1,12$, $2,6$, and $3,4$. To obtain a sum of absolute value $1$ with opposite signs, the two absolute values must differ by $1$. This selects $3$ and $4$ and assigns the minus sign to $4$. As a check, the cross terms are $3x$ and $-4x$, which add to $-x$.

Assigning the minus sign to the smaller absolute value would preserve the constant term but give $4-3=1$, making the middle term $+x$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Factor $6x^2+x-2$ by splitting the middle term in two. Check the coefficient of $x$ in the product.

$$
\begin{aligned}
ac&=6\cdot(-2)=-12,\quad 4+(-3)=1,\quad 4\cdot(-3)=-12,\\
6x^2+x-2&=6x^2+4x-3x-2\\
&=2x(3x+2)-(3x+2)=(3x+2)(2x-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the trinomial has leading coefficient $6$, the two numbers used to split its middle term must have product $6\cdot(-2)$ and sum $1$. The pair $4,-3$ makes the binomial $3x+2$ appear in both groups. Expanding the final product gives cross terms $-3x$ and $4x$, which add to $x$. The quadratic term is $6x^2$ and the constant term is $-2$. The zeros of the product are $-\dfrac{2}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$.

For comparison, $2$ and $-1$ also add to $1$. Their product is $-2$, however, so splitting by that pair would lose the common binomial.

</div>
