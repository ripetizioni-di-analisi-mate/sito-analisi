## Exercise 1

Which product equals $x^2+10x+16$?

$$
8+2=10,\quad 8\cdot2=16,\quad x^2+10x+16=(x+8)(x+2)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Find two numbers whose sum is the coefficient of x and whose product is the constant term. The two numbers are the given number and two. Multiplying the two binomials recovers the trinomial.

The result is $\left(x + 2\right) \left(x + 8\right)$.

The positive factor pairs of $16$ are $1,16$, $2,8$, and $4,4$. Only $2$ and $8$ add to $10$. The term in $x$ is therefore the sum of $2x$ and $8x$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Which product equals $x^2-10x+9$?

$$
x^2-10x+9=(x-9)(x-1)
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The positive constant term and negative linear coefficient indicate two negative numbers. Minus $9$ and minus one have the required product and sum. These two numbers give the linear factors.

The result is $\left(x - 9\right) \left(x - 1\right)$.

The product of the two numbers is positive and their sum is negative, so both numbers must be negative. The possible pairs are $-1,-9$ and $-3,-3$. The first pair has sum $-10$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For what value of $c$ does the identity $x^2+13x+c=(x+10)(x+3)$ hold?

$$
(x+10)(x+3)=x^2+13x+30,\quad c=30
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Expand the product of the two binomials. The constant term comes from multiplying their constant terms. The identity requires c to equal that product.

The result is $30$.

At $x=0$, the identity gives $c=10\cdot3=30$. The mixed products on the right are $10x$ and $3x$. Their sum is $13x$, so the linear coefficient already matches.

</div>
