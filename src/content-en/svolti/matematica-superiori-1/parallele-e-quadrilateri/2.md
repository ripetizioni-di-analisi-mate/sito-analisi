## Exercise 1

Two same-side interior angles measure $(2x+15)^\circ$ and $(3x+5)^\circ$. Find the value of $x$ for which the lines are parallel.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    (2x+15)+(3x+5)&=180,\\
    5x&=160\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=32,\\
    79^\circ+101^\circ&=180^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The configuration puts both angles inside the two lines and on the same side of the transversal. If the lines are parallel, the measures must add to $180^\circ$. The equation has one solution. Substitution gives $79^\circ$ and $101^\circ$, both positive and less than $180^\circ$. Because the angles are supplementary and same-side interior, they satisfy the converse test. That final step shows the value found is also sufficient to make the lines parallel.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two distinct lines $r$ and $s$ are both perpendicular to $t$. Prove that they are parallel using the angles formed by $t$.

$$
\alpha=90^\circ=\beta\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad r\parallel s.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Perpendicularity creates four right-angle sectors at each intersection. At both intersections choose the sector above the respective line and to the right of the transversal, as shown. Their matching positions identify two corresponding angles. Because their measures agree, the converse test makes the two lines parallel. Alternatively, use the two interior angles on the same side of $t$. Both are right angles, so they add to $180^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A transversal is perpendicular to $r$, but makes a $45^\circ$ angle with $s$ in the corresponding sector. Do two opposite right angles at the intersection with $r$ imply $r\parallel s$?

$$
90^\circ\ne45^\circ\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad r\not\parallel s.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two right angles named in the question come from the same intersection. They are equal because they are vertical angles, regardless of the direction of line $s$. To test for parallelism, compare angles in matching positions at the two intersections. Their measures here are $90^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. These angles would be equal if the lines were parallel. The different measures give a counterexample to the proposed inference and a direct check on the directions of the lines.

</div>
