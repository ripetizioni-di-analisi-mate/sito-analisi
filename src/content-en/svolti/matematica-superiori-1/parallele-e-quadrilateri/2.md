## Exercise 1

Two lines are cut by a transversal, and a pair of alternate interior angles both measure $59^\circ$. What follows?

- The lines must be perpendicular
- The two lines are parallel
- The two lines must coincide
- The transversal is parallel to both lines

<div class="spiegazione">

The converse of the alternate interior angle theorem also holds. A congruent pair of alternate interior angles establishes that the two lines are parallel. The particular angle measure does not imply perpendicularity.

The two lines are parallel.

Here the given measures are both $59^\circ$, and parallelism follows. The direct theorem starts with lines already known to be parallel and derives angle congruence.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Ray $OC$ lies inside the convex angle $AOB$. Suppose that $AOC=41^\circ$ and $COB=49^\circ$. What is the measure of $AOB$?

$$
\widehat{AOB}=41^\circ+49^\circ=90^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The interior ray divides the angle into two nonoverlapping parts. The total measure is therefore the sum of the two measures. The result is a right angle, so the lines containing OA and OB are perpendicular.

The result is $90^\circ$.

The result is $\widehat{AOB}=41^\circ+49^\circ=90^\circ$. The fact that $OC$ is inside the angle ensures that the two measures add along the convex angle.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A transversal forms same-side interior angles with degree measures $3x+15$ and $2x+20$ on two lines. For which $x$ are the lines parallel?

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3x+15)+(2x+20)&=180 \\[6pt]
5x+35&=180 \\[6pt]
x&=29
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Because the angles are same-side interior angles, the lines are parallel exactly when the measures sum to $180^\circ$. For $x=29$, the angles measure $102^\circ$ and $78^\circ$. Both measures are positive, less than $180^\circ$, and supplementary, so the criterion applies.

</div>
