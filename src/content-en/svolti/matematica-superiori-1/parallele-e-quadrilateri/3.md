## Exercise 1

Three angles of a convex quadrilateral measure $76^\circ$, $86^\circ$, and $90^\circ$. What is the fourth angle?

$$
\delta=360^\circ-76^\circ-86^\circ-90^\circ=108^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A diagonal divides a convex quadrilateral into two triangles. Its four interior angles therefore sum to two straight angles. Subtract the sum of the known angles from three hundred sixty degrees.

The result is $108^\circ$.

The known angles sum to $252^\circ$, leaving $108^\circ$ to reach $360^\circ$. The resulting measure is less than $180^\circ$, as required by convexity.

</div>

## Exercise 2

One interior angle of a parallelogram measures $71^\circ$. What is the measure of the adjacent interior angle?

$$
\beta=180^\circ-71^\circ=109^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The opposite sides of a parallelogram are parallel. The two consecutive angles are therefore same-side interior angles with respect to a side acting as a transversal. Their sum is one hundred eighty degrees.

The result is $109^\circ$.

The check against the given angle is $71^\circ+109^\circ=180^\circ$. In a parallelogram this same pair of measures also occurs at the opposite vertices.

</div>

## Exercise 3

One angle of a parallelogram measures $71^\circ$. What is the measure of the opposite angle?

$$
\gamma=71^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each of the two opposite angles is supplementary to the same adjacent angle. They are therefore congruent. The opposite angle has the given measure.

The result is $71^\circ$.

Both opposite angles under consideration combine with the adjacent $109^\circ$ angle to make $180^\circ$. The fourth angle repeats $109^\circ$, and all four measures sum to $360^\circ$.

</div>
