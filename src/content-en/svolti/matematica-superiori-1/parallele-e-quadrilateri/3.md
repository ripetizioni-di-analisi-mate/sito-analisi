## Exercise 1

In a convex quadrilateral $ABCD$, the angles at $A,B,C$ measure $83^\circ,96^\circ,107^\circ$. Find the angle at $D$ and decide whether the quadrilateral can be a parallelogram.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \angle D&=360^\circ-(83^\circ+96^\circ+107^\circ)=74^\circ,\\
    \angle A&\ne\angle C.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Drawing a diagonal splits the interior angles between two triangles. Their total is $360^\circ$, so subtract the three given angles from that total. The remaining angle is positive, and adding all four angles checks the computation. Opposite angles in a parallelogram must have the same measure. Here the opposite angles at $A$ and $C$ differ. The pair at $B$ and $D$ differs too, though either difference is enough to rule out a parallelogram.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two consecutive angles of a parallelogram are in the ratio $2:3$. Find the measures of all four angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    2k+3k&=180\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=36,\\
    (\angle A,\angle B,\angle C,\angle D)&=(72^\circ,108^\circ,72^\circ,108^\circ).
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio means that the first two measures are $2k$ and $3k$ for the same positive quantity $k$. Along the side shared by those vertices, the opposite parallel sides form a pair of same-side interior angles. That gives the $180^\circ$ total. The other two angles equal their respective opposite angles. You can check the total of $360^\circ$ and the ratio $72:108=2:3$ separately. The ratio alone would leave infinitely many pairs of possible measures.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a convex quadrilateral, $\angle A=\angle C=(2x+10)^\circ$ and $\angle B=\angle D=(3x-10)^\circ$. Find $x$ and prove the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    2(2x+10)+2(3x-10)&=360\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=36,\\
    \angle A+\angle B&=82^\circ+98^\circ=180^\circ,\\
    \angle B+\angle C&=98^\circ+82^\circ=180^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The angle total gives two equal opposite measures, $82^\circ$ and $98^\circ$. The pair at $A,B$ is supplementary and consists of same-side interior angles with transversal $AB$ crossing $AD$ and $BC$. The converse test gives the first pair of parallel sides. Using $B,C$ and transversal $BC$ the same way gives $AB\parallel CD$. The conclusion uses both pairs of angles and their positions, as required to establish both pairs of parallel sides.

</div>
