## Exercise 1

The diagonals of the quadrilateral shown are perpendicular, with $BO=OD=3$, $AO=4$, and $OC=2$. Explain whether these data identify a rhombus.

$$
BO=OD,\qquad AO\ne OC.\qquad AC\perp BD.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Diagonal $BD$ is split into halves at $O$. Diagonal $AC$ meets $O$ in segments of lengths $4$ and $2$. In a parallelogram each diagonal would bisect the other, so the second comparison rules out that classification. Every rhombus is a parallelogram, so the quadrilateral in the diagram is not a rhombus. This gives a precise counterexample: perpendicular diagonals and a midpoint on just one still leave the general classification open.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An isosceles trapezoid has bases of different lengths. Prove that its diagonals are congruent. Does this property make the trapezoid a rectangle?

$$
AB=BA,\qquad BC=AD,\qquad \angle ABC=\angle BAD
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad AC=BD.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Triangles $ABC$ and $BAD$ share base $AB$. The legs are congruent by the isosceles assumption, and the base angles at $B$ and $A$ are congruent. SAS congruence gives equality of the remaining sides, which are the diagonals. A rectangle would have both pairs of opposite sides parallel and its bases would have equal lengths. The unequal bases rule out a rectangle here. Equal diagonals identify a rectangle once the shape is already known to be a parallelogram.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two trapezoids have bases of $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. In each, one leg is $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. In one trapezoid it is perpendicular to the bases. In the other it is slanted and its projection onto the base is $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. Compare the areas.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    h_1&=5\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad h_2=\sqrt{5^2-3^2}=4\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    A_1&=\frac{(7+4)\cdot5}{2}=\frac{55}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\
    A_2&=\frac{(7+4)\cdot4}{2}=22\,\mathrm{cm}^2.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In the first trapezoid the perpendicular leg is the height. In the second, the leg of length $5$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with horizontal projection $3$. By the Pythagorean theorem the other leg is $4$, which is the height. The bases agree, so the area is proportional to the height, and the areas differ by $\dfrac{11}{2}\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. This example shows why a leg length and the two bases alone leave the area undetermined without information such as a projection or an angle.

</div>
