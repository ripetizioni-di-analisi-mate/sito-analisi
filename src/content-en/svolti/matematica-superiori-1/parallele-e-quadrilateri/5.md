## Exercise 1

The diagonals of a parallelogram meet at $O$. If $AC=48$ cm, what is the length of $AO$?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=6.5 ymin=-1 ymax=3.5 linee="0,0 4,0 5.5,2.5 1.5,2.5 0,0" segmenti="0,0,5.5,2.5;4,0,1.5,2.5" punti="0,0;4,0;5.5,2.5,pieno,C;1.5,2.5,pieno,D;2.75,1.25" etichette="-0.2,-0.35,A;4.2,-0.35,B;2.75,1.70,O" titolo="O is the midpoint of AC and BD"}

$$
AO=\frac{AC}2=\frac{48}2=24
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. O is therefore the midpoint of AC. The length AO is half the length of the diagonal.

The result is $24\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The lengths $AO$ and $OC$ add to $48\,\mathrm{cm}$ and are equal. Diagonal $BD$ may have a different length, so this computation gives information only about $AC$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a rectangle, diagonal $AC$ measures $23$ cm. What is the length of diagonal $BD$?

$$
BD=AC=23
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A rectangle is a parallelogram with right angles. Its diagonals are congruent, as follows from the congruence of the right triangles formed by its sides. The second diagonal therefore has the same length as the first.

The result is $23\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Triangles $ABC$ and $BAD$ share $AB$, have $BC=AD$, and have right included angles. They are congruent by SAS, with $AC$ corresponding to $BD$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The diagonals of convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ meet at $O$, where $AO=OC=10$ cm and $BO=OD=12$ cm. Which conclusion is always justified?

- The quadrilateral must be a rectangle
- The quadrilateral must be a rhombus
- The quadrilateral is a parallelogram
- The diagonals must be perpendicular

<div class="spiegazione">

The equalities show that the diagonals bisect each other. This property is a criterion for recognizing a parallelogram. Alone it does not imply either right angles or four congruent sides.

The quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

In this example, $AC=20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $BD=24\,\mathrm{cm}$. The difference rules out a rectangle because the diagonals of a rectangle are congruent.

</div>
