## Exercise 1

The diagonals of a parallelogram $ABCD$ meet at $O$. If $AO=7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $BD=20\,\mathrm{cm}$, find $AC$, $BO$, and $OD$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    AC&=AO+OC=2\cdot 7=14\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    BO&=OD=BD/2=10\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The midpoint property applies to each diagonal separately. On the first diagonal, doubling the half $AO$ gives $AC$. On the second, the full diagonal is known, so halve it to obtain $BO$ and $OD$. In the diagram, the two halves to compare lie on the same line. The results $7$ and $10$ differ because they come from different diagonals. The data also rule out a rectangle, which would have congruent diagonals.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a parallelogram, $AO=3x-1$, $OC=x+7$, and $BO=5x-3$, all in centimetres. Find $x$ and the diagonal lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    3x-1&=x+7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=4,\\
    AC&=2(3\cdot4-1)=22\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    BD&=2(5\cdot4-3)=34\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The expressions to equate are those for $AO$ and $OC$, because both lie on diagonal $AC$. That condition determines $x$. Substitution gives $11$ for each of those halves and $17$ for half the other diagonal. Check by recalculating $OC=4+7$ and comparing it with $AO$. The two diagonal lengths are positive and distinct. Equating $AO$ with $BO$ would require the additional property of congruent diagonals, which the problem does not assume.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The diagonals of a convex quadrilateral meet at $O$, with $AO=OC=4\,\mathrm{cm}$, $BO=OD=3\,\mathrm{cm}$, and $\angle AOB=60^\circ$. Classify the quadrilateral.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    AO=OC, BO=OD&\ \Longrightarrow\ ABCD\ \text{is a parallelogram},\\
    AC=8\,\mathrm{cm}&\ne BD=6\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad 60^\circ\ne90^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Angles $AOB$ and $COD$ are vertically opposite and hence congruent. The triangles also have two congruent pairs of sides, so SAS congruence gives equal alternate interior angles and $AB\parallel CD$. Applying the same construction to the other pair of triangles gives $AD\parallel BC$. Thus the shape is a parallelogram. The different diagonal lengths rule out a rectangle. The $60^\circ$ crossing rules out a rhombus. Since a square belongs to both families, that case is ruled out as well.

</div>
