## Exercise 1

In an isosceles trapezoid, one angle adjacent to the longer base measures $68^\circ$. What is the measure of the other angle adjacent to the same base?

$$
\beta=68^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In an isosceles trapezoid, angles adjacent to the same base are congruent. Both angles are on the stated base. Their measures are therefore equal.

The result is $68^\circ$.

The two angles at the longer base therefore each measure $68^\circ$. The angles at the shorter base each lie next to a $68^\circ$ angle along a leg and measure $112^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

In a trapezoid, one angle on the longer base measures $74^\circ$. What is the angle on the shorter base adjacent to the same leg?

$$
\beta=180^\circ-74^\circ=106^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bases are parallel and the leg is a transversal. The specified angles are same-side interior angles. They are supplementary even when the trapezoid is not isosceles.

The result is $106^\circ$.

The computation gives $180^\circ-74^\circ=106^\circ$. Equality of the angles on the same base would also require an isosceles trapezoid, which is not assumed here.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The bases of an isosceles trapezoid measure $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The projection of the shorter base onto the longer base leaves two equal outer segments. How long is each segment?

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=15 ymin=-1.2 ymax=5.3 linee="0,0 14,0 11,4 3,4 0,0;3,0.3 3.3,0.3 3.3,0;11,0.3 11.3,0.3 11.3,0" segmenti="3,4,3,0;11,4,11,0" etichette="7,4.57,8 cm;7,-0.57,14 cm;1.5,0.35,x;12.5,0.35,x" titolo="The longer base measures 14 cm and the shorter one 8 cm"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x&=(14-8)\,\mathrm{cm}=6\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
x&=3\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The projection keeps the length of the shorter base because the bases are parallel. The two outer segments complete the longer base. Since they are equal, each accounts for half of the remaining $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Indeed, $3+8+3=14$.

</div>
