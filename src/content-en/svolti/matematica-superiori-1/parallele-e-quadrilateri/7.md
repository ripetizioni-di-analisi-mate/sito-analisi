## Exercise 1

In isosceles trapezoid $ABCD$, $AB$ is the longer base, and the angle at $A$ is $45^\circ$. Find the other three angles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \angle B&=45^\circ,\\
    \angle D&=180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ,\\
    \angle C&=135^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In an isosceles trapezoid, the angles on the same base are congruent, so $B$ has the same measure as $A$. To go from $A$ to $D$, regard leg $AD$ as a transversal to the parallel bases. The interior angles on the same side of $AD$ are then supplementary. On the shorter base the two angles have the same measure, so $C=D$. You can also obtain $C$ by using $BC$ as a transversal. Finally, two angles of $45^\circ$ and two of $135^\circ$ add to $360^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The bases of trapezoid $ABCD$ are $AB$ and $CD$. If $\angle A=64^\circ$ and $\angle B=82^\circ$, find $\angle C$ and $\angle D$ and decide whether the trapezoid is isosceles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \angle D&=180^\circ-64^\circ=116^\circ,\\
    \angle C&=180^\circ-82^\circ=98^\circ,\\
    \angle A&\ne\angle B.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each leg picks out a pair of supplementary interior angles. Leg $AD$ connects $A$ with $D$, while $BC$ connects $B$ with $C$. The vertex order determines which angle receives each supplement. The two angles at base $AB$ differ, so a necessary property of an isosceles trapezoid fails. To check the computation, add each pair along a leg to obtain $180^\circ$. The total of all four angles alone would fail to detect a swap between $C$ and $D$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In an isosceles trapezoid, an angle on the shorter base is $18^\circ$ greater than an angle on the longer base. Find the two measures.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \alpha+(\alpha+18^\circ)&=180^\circ,\\
    \alpha&=81^\circ,\qquad\alpha+18^\circ=99^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $\alpha$ be the measure at each endpoint of the longer base. An angle at the shorter base is $\alpha+18^\circ$. Choose two vertices on the same leg to get same-side interior angles, which add to $180^\circ$. The result gives $81^\circ$ on the longer base and $99^\circ$ on the shorter one. The check uses both conditions: $99^\circ-81^\circ=18^\circ$ and $81^\circ+99^\circ=180^\circ$. Each measure occurs twice because the two legs are symmetrically placed.

</div>
