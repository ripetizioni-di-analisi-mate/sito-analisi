## Exercise 1

A parallelogram has perimeter $44\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its longer side is $4\,\mathrm{cm}$ longer than its shorter side. Find the side lengths.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    2(a+a+4)&=44,\qquad 2a+4=22,\\
    a&=9\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad a+4=13\,\mathrm{cm}.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Each side has an opposite side of the same length. The perimeter therefore includes two copies of the shorter side and two of the longer. Halving the perimeter leaves one of each. Subtracting the four-centimetre difference leaves the length of two shorter sides, which total $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. The solution checks both givens: $13-9=4$ and $2(13+9)=44$. The calculation determines the side lengths even though the slant of the parallelogram is unspecified.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rectangle has base $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $10\,\mathrm{cm}$. A parallelogram has the same consecutive side lengths and a height of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ to its $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ base. Compare the perimeters and areas.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    P_1=P_2&=2(12+10)=44\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    A_1&=12\cdot10=120\,\mathrm{cm}^2,\\
    A_2&=12\cdot6=72\,\mathrm{cm}^2.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Perimeter measures the boundary, so it depends only on the side lengths. In both figures, the sides of length $12$ and $10$ each occur twice. Area depends on the perpendicular distance between the base lines. In the parallelogram that distance is $6$, while the slanted side still has length $10$. The areas therefore differ by $48\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. The diagram shows how changing the slant can retain all the boundary lengths while changing the enclosed area.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A parallelogram has perimeter $48\,\mathrm{cm}$, a base of $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a perpendicular height of $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ to that base. Find the slanted side and the area.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    a&=48/2-15=9\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    A&=15\cdot8=120\,\mathrm{cm}^2.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The unknown length belongs to the perimeter. Halving $48$ gives the sum of the base and the slanted side, so subtract $15$. The area uses a different relationship. Its second factor is the perpendicular height already given. The slanted side of $9$ does not replace the height of $8$. The units provide another check: the side is in centimetres, while the area is a product of two lengths and is given in square centimetres.

</div>
