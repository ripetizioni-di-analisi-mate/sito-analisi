## Exercise 1

A parallelogram has consecutive sides of lengths $16$ cm and $24$ cm. What is its perimeter?

$$
P=2(16+24)=80
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Opposite sides of a parallelogram are congruent. Each given length appears twice on the boundary. The perimeter is therefore twice the sum of the consecutive side lengths.

The result is $80\,\mathrm{cm}$.

Following the sides in order adds $16+24+16+24$ centimeters. The factor $2$ groups the two pairs of opposite sides.

</div>

## Exercise 2

A rhombus has a side of $13$ cm. What is its perimeter?

$$
P=4\cdot13=52
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A rhombus has four congruent sides. To find the perimeter, add four copies of the same length. The angle measures are unnecessary for this computation.

The result is $52\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The perimeter is a length, so its unit remains the centimeter. The computation $4\cdot13=52$ uses all four sides, even when the rhombus has angles of different sizes.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A parallelogram has a side of $13$ cm and perimeter $70$ cm. What is the length of the consecutive side?

$$
b=\frac{70}2-13=22
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Half the perimeter is the sum of two consecutive side lengths. Divide the perimeter by two, then subtract the known side length. The side found and its opposite complete the boundary.

The result is $22\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The two $13\,\mathrm{cm}$ sides account for $26\,\mathrm{cm}$ of the perimeter. This leaves $44\,\mathrm{cm}$ for the remaining two sides, each of which measures $22\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
