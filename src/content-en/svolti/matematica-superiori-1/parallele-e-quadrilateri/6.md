## Exercise 1

A rhombus has perimeter $60\,\mathrm{cm}$ and an angle of $70^\circ$. Find the side length and decide whether it is a square.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4l&=60\,\mathrm{cm} \\[6pt]
l&=15\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The perimeter is four times the side length because all four sides are congruent. The given angle is acute, so the definition of a square is not satisfied. Its adjacent angle measures $110^\circ$, and the opposite angle measures $70^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

An interior angle of a rhombus measures $78^\circ$. The diagonal from that vertex bisects the angle. What is the measure of each half?

$$
\beta=\frac{78^\circ}2=39^\circ
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A diagonal of a rhombus divides the angle at its endpoint into two congruent parts. These parts sum to the original angle. Each is therefore half the given measure.

The result is $39^\circ$.

Two parts of $39^\circ$ total $78^\circ$. The diagonal bisects the angle at its endpoint, while the consecutive angle measures $102^\circ$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Quadrilateral $EFGH$ has four congruent sides and four right angles. How is it classified under the inclusive definitions?

- It is a rectangle but not a rhombus
- It is a rhombus but not a rectangle
- It is neither a rectangle nor a rhombus
- It is both a rectangle and a rhombus

<div class="spiegazione">

Four right angles satisfy the definition of a rectangle. Four congruent sides satisfy the definition of a rhombus. Both classifications apply, and the quadrilateral is a square.

It is both a rectangle and a rhombus.

A single name can capture both properties at once. The definition of a square requires four congruent sides and four right angles, and both conditions are given.

</div>
