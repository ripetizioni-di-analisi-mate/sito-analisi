## Exercise 1

A rhombus has diagonals of lengths $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find its area by adding the areas of four triangles.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    AO=OC&=4\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad BO=OD=3\,\mathrm{cm},\\
    A&=4\cdot\frac{4\cdot3}{2}=24\,\mathrm{cm}^2.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A rhombus is a parallelogram, so its diagonals bisect each other. They are also perpendicular in a rhombus, giving each of the four triangles a right angle at their common vertex. Each triangle has perpendicular legs of lengths $4$ and $3$, which can serve as its base and height. Adding four areas of $6\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ gives $24\,\mathrm{cm}^2$. The argument also gives the general formula: each leg is half a diagonal, so the total area is half the product of the diagonals.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rhombus $ABCD$ has $\angle A=68^\circ$. Use angle bisectors $AC$ and $BD$ to find the three angles of triangle $AOB$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \angle B&=180^\circ-68^\circ=112^\circ,\\
    \angle BAO&=34^\circ,\qquad \angle ABO=56^\circ,\\
    \angle AOB&=180^\circ-(34^\circ+56^\circ)=90^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Angles $A$ and $B$ are consecutive in a parallelogram, so they are supplementary. Diagonal $AC$ splits angle $A$ in half, and one half belongs to triangle $AOB$. Diagonal $BD$ does the same at $B$. The third angle completes the triangle total of $180^\circ$. The two halves add to $90^\circ$ because the full angles add to $180^\circ$. The calculation thus recovers the perpendicularity of the diagonals from their angle-bisector properties.

</div>

## Exercise 3

In a convex quadrilateral, each diagonal is $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. The diagonals bisect each other and are perpendicular. What is the most specific classification guaranteed by these facts?

$$
AO=BO=CO=DO=5\,\mathrm{cm},\qquad AC\perp BD.
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The diagonals bisect each other, so start by identifying a parallelogram. Because its diagonals are congruent, this parallelogram is a rectangle. Because the diagonals are also perpendicular, it is a rhombus. It therefore has both four right angles and four congruent sides. The most specific classification is a square. The measure $10$ fixes the size of the drawing, while knowing that the two diagonal lengths are equal would already suffice for the classification.

</div>
