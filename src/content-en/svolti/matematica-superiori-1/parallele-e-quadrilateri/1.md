## Exercise 1

The line through $E,A$ is parallel to the line through $G,B,F$. As in the diagram, $\angle EAB=45^\circ$. Find $\angle GBA$ and $\angle ABF$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \angle GBA&=\angle EAB=45^\circ,\\
    \angle ABF&=180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Rays $AE$ and $BG$ follow the parallel lines in opposite directions, so $EAB$ and $GBA$ are alternate interior angles. For the second angle, notice that $BG$ and $BF$ are opposite rays, while $BA$ forms a boundary of each sector at $B$. These two adjacent sectors make a straight angle. The same result follows directly from $EAB$ and $ABF$ being same-side interior angles. The diagram shows the pairs through the positions of the rays.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Two corresponding angles on parallel lines measure $(2x+16)^\circ$ and $(3x-14)^\circ$. Find $x$ and the measure of an adjacent angle to the first.

$$
\begin{aligned}
    2x+16&=3x-14\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=30,\\
    2(30)+16&=3(30)-14=76,\\
    180^\circ-76^\circ&=104^\circ.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Parallelism is given, so apply the direct property for corresponding angles. Set the two expressions equal. Substituting the solution gives two acute angles and checks that both measures lie between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$. A linear-pair angle adjacent to the first shares one ray and extends the other in the opposite direction, completing the total of $180^\circ$. Adding the corresponding angles themselves to $180^\circ$ would apply a relationship meant for a different pair.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Two parallel lines meet a transversal, and one angle is $118^\circ$. How many angles have this measure, and what are the remaining measures?

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \beta &= 180^\circ-118^\circ=62^\circ,\\
    2+2&=4.
    \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

At the intersection with the given angle, its vertical angle also measures $118^\circ$. The other two sectors are adjacent to the given angle and measure $62^\circ$. The four corresponding angles at the second intersection retain those respective measures. There are therefore four obtuse angles and four acute angles. Check the count at each intersection by adding $2\cdot118^\circ+2\cdot62^\circ=360^\circ$. Two measures determine all eight angles once the position of the first sector is known.

</div>
