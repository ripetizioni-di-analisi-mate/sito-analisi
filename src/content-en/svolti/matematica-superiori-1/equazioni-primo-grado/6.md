## Exercise 1

Find $x$ in $x:4=8:2$ and check by cross-multiplying.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         \frac x4&=\frac82=4\\[6pt]
         x&=4\cdot4=16\\[6pt]
         16\cdot2&=8\cdot4=32.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The ratio on the right says that $8$ is four times $2$. On the left we therefore need a number that is four times $4$. Multiplying both sides by $4$ gives $x=16$. We can also multiply the original proportion by $8$, a common multiple of the denominators. This gives $2x=4\cdot8$, the result of cross-multiplying. Substitution verifies equality of both the quotients and the cross-products.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Rearrange $s=3t+9$ to make $t$ the subject and evaluate it at $s=27$. How does $t$ change if $s$ increases by three units?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         s-9&=3t\\[6pt]
         t&=\frac{s-9}{3}\\[6pt]
         t&=\frac{27-9}{3}=6.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The value of $s$ includes a constant contribution of $9$ and three times $t$. Subtract $9$ first to isolate the product, then divide by $3$. With $s=27$, the difference is $18$, so $t=6$ and $3\cdot6+9=27$. An increase of three units in $s$ adds three to the numerator of the rearranged formula. The denominator stays $3$, so $t$ increases by one. Moving from $s=27$ to $s=30$ indeed changes $t=6$ to $t=7$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Rearrange $P=2b+2h$ for $h$, with $P=34\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $b=11\,\mathrm{cm}$. Could a width of $17\,\mathrm{cm}$ work with the same perimeter?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         P-2b&=2h\\[6pt]
         h&=\frac{P-2b}{2}=\frac P2-b\\[6pt]
         h&=(17-11)\,\mathrm{cm}=6\,\mathrm{cm}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $2b$ from the perimeter leaves the contribution of the two heights. Dividing by $2$ gives the difference between the half-perimeter and the width. With the given perimeter, the adjacent sides add to $17\,\mathrm{cm}$. A width of $11\,\mathrm{cm}$ leaves $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ for the height, and $2(11+6)=34$ checks the measure. A width of $17\,\mathrm{cm}$ would use the entire half-perimeter and leave height zero. The formula gives that value, but a shape with a zero-length side is not a rectangle.

</div>
