## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x-8}{x+1}=\dfrac{2}{11}$ over the real numbers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
11(x-8)&=2(x+1),\quad x\ne -1 \\[6pt]
11x-88&=2x+2 \\[6pt]
9x&=90 \\[6pt]
x&=10
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $x+1$ is nonzero when $x\ne-1$. Multiplication by $11(x+1)$ is reversible on this domain. The solution $10$ belongs to the domain and gives $\dfrac{10-8}{10+1}=\dfrac2{11}$ on the left.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the solution set of $\dfrac{x-9}{x-9}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x-9}{x-9}&=1,\quad x\ne9 \\[6pt]
x-9&=x-9 \\[6pt]
S&=\mathbb R\setminus\{9\}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The domain excludes $9$ because the denominator is zero there. Every remaining value gives an equal and nonzero numerator and denominator, so the quotient is $1$. The simplified identity holds only on the original domain.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{2x-6}{x-3}=0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x-6&=0,\quad x\ne3 \\[6pt]
x&=3 \\[6pt]
S&=\varnothing
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For the fraction to be zero, the numerator must be zero while the denominator remains nonzero. Only $x=3$ makes the numerator zero, and that value also makes the denominator zero. The candidate is therefore excluded and the equation has no solution. For any admissible value, the fraction simplifies to $2$.

</div>
