## Exercise 1

Solve $\dfrac{x-8}{x+1}=\dfrac2{11}$ over the reals and identify the excluded value and the solution separately.

::grafico{xmin=-3.4 xmax=12.2 ymin=-0.7 ymax=0.85 uguale=1 assi=0 vettori="-3.06,0,11.64,0" punti="-1,0,vuoto;10,0,pieno" etichette="-1.17,-0.41,−1;9.71,-0.41,10;11.79,0.13,x" titolo="−1 is excluded from the domain and 10 is the only solution"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{-1\}\\[6pt]
         11(x-8)&=2(x+1)\\[6pt]
         9x&=90\\[6pt]
         x&=10.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The denominator $x+1$ vanishes only at $x=-1$. Multiplying by $11(x+1)$ is therefore valid on the entire allowed domain. The new equation has integer coefficients. Move the term $-88$ to the right by adding $88$ to both sides. The candidate $10$ differs from $-1$ and checks as $\dfrac{10-8}{10+1}=\dfrac2{11}$. On the number line, the open point is a value that cannot be substituted, and the filled point is the only value that makes the sides equal.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the solution set of $\dfrac{x-9}{x-9}=1$. Is it an identity on all the reals?

::grafico{xmin=-0.5 xmax=14 ymin=-0.45 ymax=2.1 f="1" punti="9,1,vuoto" segmenti="9,0,9,1" titolo="The graph of the left side has a missing point at (9, 1)"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{9\}\\[6pt]
         \frac{x-9}{x-9}&=1\quad (x\in D)\\[6pt]
         S&=\mathbb R\setminus\{9\}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The fraction divides a number by itself. For every $x\ne9$, the number $x-9$ is nonzero and the quotient equals $1$. At $x=9$ we would get $0:0$, which has no defined value. So the horizontal line for the left side has a missing point. The right side is defined at that point too. The identity requires both sides to be defined and equal, so it holds exactly on $D$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{2x-6}{x-3}=0$. Why is the zero of the numerator not a solution?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         D&=\mathbb R\setminus\{3\}\\[6pt]
         2x-6&=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=3\notin D\\[6pt]
         S&=\varnothing.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

A fraction can be zero when the numerator vanishes and the denominator remains nonzero. Here the numerator vanishes only at $3$, which also makes the denominator zero. The candidate therefore cannot be substituted. Another check factors $2$ out of $2x-6$. Within the domain the quotient is $\dfrac{2(x-3)}{x-3}=2$. Every allowed value therefore gives $2$ instead of zero, and the equation has no solution.

</div>
