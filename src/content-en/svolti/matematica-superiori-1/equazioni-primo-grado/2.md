## Exercise 1

Solve $2(x-8)=11$, keeping the expression in parentheses intact in the first step.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         x-8&=\frac{11}{2}\\[6pt]
         x&=\frac{11+16}{2}=\frac{27}{2}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The entire expression in parentheses is multiplied by $2$. Dividing both sides by $2$ first immediately gives the value of $x-8$. Then add $8=\dfrac{16}{2}$. The result exceeds $8$, as it must because the expression in parentheses is positive. To check, subtract $8$ from the solution to get $\dfrac{11}{2}$. Multiplying by $2$ gives $11$ again. Expanding first would give $2x-16=11$, which leads to the same quotient.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-\dfrac{x-3}{4}=\dfrac{x+1}{2}$ and check the common value of the two sides.

::grafico{f="-(x-3)/4;(x+1)/2" xmin=-2 xmax=3 ymin=-0.8 ymax=2.2 punti="0.333333333,0.666666667,pieno,P" titolo="The intersection has horizontal coordinate one third and vertical coordinate two thirds"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         -(x-3)&=2(x+1)\\[6pt]
         -x+3&=2x+2\\[6pt]
         1&=3x\\[6pt]
         x&=\frac13.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Multiplying both sides by $4$ clears the first denominator and leaves a factor of $2$ on the right. On the left the minus sign applies to all of $x-3$, changing $-3$ to $+3$. Add $x$ and subtract $2$ to get $1=3x$. At $\dfrac13$, the fraction before applying its outer sign equals $-\dfrac23$, so the left side is $\dfrac23$. The fraction on the right is also $\dfrac23$, the vertical coordinate of the intersection.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x-1}{2}-\dfrac{x+2}{3}=1$. After multiplying by $6$, why is the constant on the left $-7$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         3(x-1)-2(x+2)&=6\\[6pt]
         3x-3-2x-4&=6\\[6pt]
         x-7&=6\\[6pt]
         x&=13.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The least common multiple of $2$ and $3$ is $6$. Multiply every term, including the right side. The first group contributes constant $-3$. The second group has multiplier $-2$ and gives $(-2)\cdot2=-4$. The two constants therefore add to $-7$. Substituting $13$ into the original expression gives $\dfrac{12}{2}-\dfrac{15}{3}=6-5=1$. This also checks the sign of the subtraction between the quotients.

</div>
