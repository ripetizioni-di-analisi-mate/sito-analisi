## Exercise 1

Solve $2(x-8)=11$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x-8 &= \frac{11}{2} \\[6pt]
x &= \frac{11}{2}+8 \\[6pt]
  &= \frac{11+16}{2}=\frac{27}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The factor $2$ multiplies the whole binomial $x-8$. Dividing both sides by $2$ keeps the binomial intact. To isolate $x$, add $8$, which is $\dfrac{16}{2}$. Substitution gives $2\left(\dfrac{27}{2}-\dfrac{16}{2}\right)=11$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Solve $-\dfrac{x-3}{4}=\dfrac{x+1}{2}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-(x-3) &= 2(x+1) \\[6pt]
-x+3 &= 2x+2 \\[6pt]
1 &= 3x \\[6pt]
x &= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The common multiple $4$ clears both denominators. A factor of $2$ remains on the right because $4$ is twice its denominator $2$. On the left, the minus sign applies to both $x$ and $-3$. With $x=\dfrac13$, the left-hand side and the right-hand side both equal $\dfrac23$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Solve $\dfrac{x-1}{2}-\dfrac{x+2}{3}=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(x-1)-2(x+2) &= 6 \\[6pt]
3x-3-2x-4 &= 6 \\[6pt]
x &= 6+7=13
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The least common multiple of the denominators is $6$. Multiplying the right-hand side by $6$ as well preserves the equality. The whole expression $2(x+2)$ is subtracted, so its expansion gives $-2x-4$. Substituting $13$ makes the left-hand side $\dfrac{12}{2}-\dfrac{15}{3}=6-5=1$.

</div>
