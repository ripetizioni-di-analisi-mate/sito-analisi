## Exercise 1

Two consecutive integers have sum $35$. What is the smaller one? Could such a pair have sum $36$?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         n+(n+1)&=35\\[6pt]
         2n&=34\\[6pt]
         n&=17,\qquad n+1=18.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If $n$ is the smaller integer, adding one gives the larger. Their sum must therefore be $2n+1$. With $35$, we find $n=17$ and check $17+18=35$. Both values satisfy the integer restriction. A sum of $36$ would instead give $2n=35$, so $n=\dfrac{35}{2}$. This is not an integer and cannot make an allowed pair. More generally $2n$ is even, so the sum of two consecutive integers is always odd.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three numbers have mean $11$. The first two are $9$ and $10$. Find the third and compare it with the mean.

::grafico{xmin=-1 xmax=15.6 ymin=0 ymax=3.37 assi=0 poligoni="0,2.5 9,2.5 9,2.87 0,2.87;0,1.52 10,1.52 10,1.89 0,1.89;0,0.54 14,0.54 14,0.91 0,0.91" segmenti="11,0.22,11,3.04" etichette="9.1,2.63,9;10.05,1.65,10;14.08,0.67,14;10.75,3.18,11" titolo="The first two values are 9 and 10, and the third is three units above the mean"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         \frac{9+10+x}{3}&=11\\[6pt]
         9+10+x&=33\\[6pt]
         x&=33-19=14.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Three numbers with mean $11$ have sum $33$, the same as three copies of $11$. The first number is two units below that level and the second is one below. So the third number must compensate by three units and equals $11+3=14$. The diagram compares each value with the vertical line for the mean. The equation confirms the balance, and the check $\dfrac{9+10+14}{3}=11$ returns to the original requirement.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four consecutive even integers have mean $21$. Find them and check that the value you obtain is even.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         \frac{x+(x+2)+(x+4)+(x+6)}{4}&=21\\[6pt]
         4x+12&=84\\[6pt]
         x&=18.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If the smallest integer is $x$, we obtain the others by adding $2$, $4$ and $6$. Their sum is four times the mean, or $84$. Solving gives $18$, which is even. The integers are $18$, $20$, $22$ and $24$. A second approach uses symmetry: the endpoints are three units from the mean, and the middle values are one unit away. Indeed $18+24=42$ and $20+22=42$. Each pair has mean $21$.

</div>
