## Exercise 1

Two consecutive integers have sum $35$. What is the smaller integer?

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+(x+1)&=35 \\[6pt]
2x&=35-1=34 \\[6pt]
x&=17
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Let $x$ be the smaller integer. Since the integers are consecutive, the larger is $x+1$. The stated sum is therefore twice the smaller integer plus $1$. The smaller integer is $17$ and the larger is $18$. They are consecutive and $17+18=35$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Three numbers have mean $11$. The first two are $9$ and $10$. What is the third?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{9+10+x}{3}&=11 \\[6pt]
19+x&=33 \\[6pt]
x&=33-19=14
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The mean is the sum divided by the number of data values. The sum must therefore be $33$. The first two numbers already account for $19$ of that total. The third contributes the remaining $14$, and $\dfrac{9+10+14}{3}=11$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Four consecutive even integers have mean $21$. What is the greatest integer?

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x+(x+2)+(x+4)+(x+6)}{4}&=21 \\[6pt]
4x+12&=84 \\[6pt]
x&=\frac{84-12}{4}=18 \\[6pt]
x+6&=24
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Consecutive even integers differ by $2$. If $x$ is the least, then $x+6$ is the greatest. The sum of the four integers is four times their mean, so it is $84$. The calculation gives $18$ as the least integer, and $18$ is even. The four numbers are $18$, $20$, $22$, and $24$. The first and last sum to $42$, as do the middle two.

</div>
