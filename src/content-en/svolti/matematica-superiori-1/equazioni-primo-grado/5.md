## Exercise 1

Today a mother is three times as old as her son. In $8$ years she will be twice his age. How old is her son today?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         3x+8&=2(x+8)\\[6pt]
         3x+8&=2x+16\\[6pt]
         x&=8.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

If the son is $x$ years old today, the mother is $3x$. The passage of eight years adds $8$ to both ages. The doubling applies to the son's whole future age, so write $2(x+8)$. With $x=8$, their ages today are $8$ and $24$, and in eight years they will be $16$ and $32$. The difference remains $16$ today and in the future even though the ratio changes from three to two.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Entry costs $5$ euros and each activity costs $3$ euros. How many activities were purchased for a total of $59$ euros? Could someone pay exactly $60$ euros?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         5+3n=59&\iff 3n=54\iff n=18,\\[6pt]
         5+3n=60&\iff 3n=55\iff n=\frac{55}{3}.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The entry fee is counted once. The remaining euros come in groups of $3$, one for each activity. Subtracting $5$ from $59$ leaves $54$ euros, giving $18$ activities, with check $5+3\cdot18=59$. For a total of $60$ euros the quotient would be $\dfrac{55}{3}=18+\dfrac13$. Since $n$ must be a nonnegative integer, that total cannot be reached. Eighteen activities cost $59$ euros, while nineteen cost $62$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A rectangle has perimeter $46\,\mathrm{cm}$. Its width exceeds its height by $5\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find both side lengths.

::grafico{xmin=-1.8 xmax=15.1 ymin=-1.33 ymax=10.2 uguale=1 assi=0 poligoni="0,0 14,0 14,9 0,9" segmenti="9,0,9,9" etichette="-1,4.5,9;4.35,-0.68,9;11.35,-0.68,5;6.6,9.45,14" titolo="Sides in cm. The width of 14 splits into 9 and 5"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         b+h&=\frac{46}{2}=23\\[6pt]
         h+5+h&=23\\[6pt]
         h&=9,\qquad b=14.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The lengths are measured in centimetres. The half-perimeter is $23$ and counts one width and one height. The width can be split into a part as long as the height and five extra centimetres. Subtract those five from the half-perimeter to leave two equal parts. The height is $9\,\mathrm{cm}$, so the width is $14\,\mathrm{cm}$. The diagram splits the longer side into $9+5$. The lengths satisfy both the stated difference and the perimeter check $2(9+14)=46$.

</div>
