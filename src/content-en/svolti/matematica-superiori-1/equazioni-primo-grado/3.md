## Exercise 1

Find the real solution set of $2(x+8)=2x+17$. What does the vertical gap between the two lines represent?

::grafico{f="2*x+16;2*x+17" xmin=-4 xmax=4 ymin=-0.7 ymax=26 punti="0,16,pieno;0,17,pieno" segmenti="0,16,0,17" titolo="The vertical coordinates always differ by one unit"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         2x+16&=2x+17\\[6pt]
         16&=17,\qquad S=\varnothing.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Expanding the left side gives the constant $16$. Both sides contain $2x$, so the right side is one unit greater at every $x$. Subtracting $2x$ exposes the same contradiction for every choice of the unknown. The graphs are parallel lines with the same slope. Their vertical gap of one unit is the value of $(2x+17)-(2x+16)$, which stays the same as $x$ changes. They have no point in common.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Find the real solution set of $3(x-9)=3x-27$. Does checking $x=0$ establish that outcome?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         3(x-9)&=3x-27\\[6pt]
         3x-27&=3x-27\\[6pt]
         0&=0,\qquad S=\mathbb R.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Distributing $3$ gives exactly the right side, including the constant $-27$. Both sides are defined for every real number. Subtracting them gives $0=0$ for any $x$. Substituting $x=0$ does give $-27$ on both sides, but covers just that point. Distributivity establishes the result for every real number. To distinguish those checks, observe that $3x-27=-27$ also holds at $x=0$, yet has only one solution.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Classify $cx+10=2x+10$ as the real parameter $c$ varies. Why is $x=0$ a solution in every case?

$$
\begin{aligned}
         (c-2)x&=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\mathbb R\quad (c=2)\\[6pt]
         S&=\{0\}\quad (c\ne2).
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Subtracting $10$ and $2x$ on both sides exposes the coefficient $c-2$. If $c=2$, division by it is impossible and the equation becomes $0=0$. If $c\ne2$, division is reversible and gives $x=0$ as the only solution. In the original equation, at $x=0$ both products involving the unknown vanish. Both sides equal $10$ for any $c$. Knowing the solution shared by all parameter values still leaves the question of uniqueness.

</div>
