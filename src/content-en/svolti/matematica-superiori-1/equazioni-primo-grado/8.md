## Exercise 1

Check that $x=8$ solves $5x-8=2x+16$ and decide whether it is the only solution.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         5\cdot8-8&=32,\qquad 2\cdot8+16=32\\[6pt]
         5x-8=2x+16&\iff 3x=24\\[6pt]
         &\iff x=8.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Substitution compares the original expressions and shows both equal $32$ when $x=8$. That establishes $8$ as a solution. To determine how many solutions there are, subtract $2x$ and add $8$ on both sides to get $3x=24$. The coefficient $3$ is nonzero, so division gives a single value. The first check establishes that the candidate belongs to the solution set, and the second determines the whole set.

</div>

## Exercise 2

When do the sides of $4x-6=x+3$ differ by $3$, with the left side greater? What if the right side is greater?

::grafico{xmin=-0.6 xmax=5.55 ymin=-7 ymax=15 f="4*x-6;x+3" punti="2,2,pieno;2,5,pieno;3,6,pieno;4,7,pieno;4,10,pieno" segmenti="2,2,2,5;4,7,4,10" titolo="The vertical distance is 3 in both cases. The higher line switches"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
         (4x-6)-(x+3)&=3x-9,\\[6pt]
         3x-9=3&\iff x=4,\\[6pt]
         3x-9=-3&\iff x=2.
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Measure the difference by subtracting the right side from the left. This gives $3x-9$. When the left side is greater by three, the difference is positive, so set $3x-9=3$. At $x=4$, the values are $10$ and $7$. When the right side is greater by three, the difference is negative, so set $3x-9=-3$. At $x=2$, the values are $2$ and $5$. The graph shows the two vertical gaps on either side of the intersection.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Classify $(k-4)x=2k-8$ for every real parameter $k$. Find the solution set for the exceptional parameter.

$$
\begin{aligned}
         (k-4)x&=2(k-4)\\[6pt]
         (k-4)(x-2)&=0\\[6pt]
         S&=\mathbb R\quad (k=4)\\[6pt]
         S&=\{2\}\quad (k\ne4).
         \end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Factoring $2$ from the right side exposes the same factor $k-4$ as on the left. If $k=4$, it vanishes on both sides and the equation holds for every real number. If $k\ne4$, the product vanishes only when $x-2=0$, so $x=2$. The check $2(k-4)=2k-8$ shows $2$ solves the equation for any parameter. To determine when it is the only value, use the condition on the factor $k-4$.

</div>
