## Exercise 1

The beam shown is fixed at $A$. Its two segments are connected at $B$ by a parallel-link connection with horizontal links. The right segment rests at $C$ on a roller moving on a horizontal surface. Determine whether the beam is statically determinate.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,7,0" aste="A-B;B-C" interni="B,doppio-pendolo,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180;C,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,3 m"}

$$
n = 2 ,
\qquad
s = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
\text{body } 1 \ (AB) : \ \text{fixed support}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{stationary}
$$
$$
\text{body } 2 \ (BC) : \quad C_2 = X_\infty \ \text{(parallel-link support)} ,
\qquad
C_2 \in r_C : \ x = 7\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{stationary}
$$
$$
l = 0 ,
\qquad
i = 0
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
\begin{cases}
u_2 = 0 \\
\varphi_2 = 0 \\
v_2 + 3\varphi_2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
u_2 = v_2 = \varphi_2 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Internal parallel-link connection equals $2$ scalar constraints, so $s = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 = 3n$. The section $AB$ is fixed and is stationary. For observation on the **Still body acting like the ground**, the internal parallel-link connection acts on the segment $BC$ as an external parallel-link support with the horizontal links: it prevents rotation and horizontal translation and leaves vertical translation free. The center of $BC$ is therefore the point at infinity in the horizontal direction $X_\infty$. The roller support at $C$ has the vertical reaction, and the vertical through $C$ has as its point at infinity $Y_\infty$, other than $X_\infty$. The section $BC$ has two distinct centers and is stationary. The beam is **statically determinate**.

In verification the reference point of the body $2$ is at $B$. Parallel-link support, with the body $1$ stationary, imposes $u_2 = 0$ and $\varphi_2 = 0$; roller support, at $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from the reference point, imposes $v_2 + 3\varphi_2 = 0$, so also $v_2 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The beam shown is the same as in the previous exercise, but the roller support at $C$ moves on a vertical plane. Classify the beam and describe its movements.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,7,0" aste="A-B;B-C" interni="B,doppio-pendolo,0" vincoli="A,incastro,180;C,carrello,0" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,3 m"}

$$
n = 2 ,
\qquad
s = 6 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
C_2 = X_\infty ,
\qquad
r_C : \ y = 0 ,
\qquad
X_\infty \in r_C
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the segment } BC \text{ translates vertically}
$$
$$
l = 1 ,
\qquad
i = 1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
(u_2, v_2, \varphi_2) = (0, v, 0) :
\qquad
u_2 = 0 ,
\qquad
\varphi_2 = 0 ,
\qquad
u_C = u_2 - \varphi_2 \cdot 0 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The count is the same as the preceding exercise. The section $AB$ is stationary and the internal parallel-link connection leaves the section $BC$ the only vertical translation, with center $X_\infty$. Vertical roller support has the horizontal reaction, and its line of action, the axis of the beam, has $X_\infty$ as its point at infinity. The conditions are compatible, so the segment $BC$ moves vertically: the parallel-link support pin-ended links and the roller support reaction are parallel, and no constraint prevents the segment from moving vertically. The beam is **unstable and statically indeterminate**, with $l = i = 1$. The excess constraint concerns horizontal forces: on the segment $BC$ both parallel-link support and roller support exert a horizontal force, and the horizontal translation equation alone is not enough to separate them.

In the check, the vertical $(0, v, 0)$ translation of the segment $BC$ meets the two conditions of parallel-link support and roller support, which prevents only horizontal displacement of $C$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The cover in the figure is a three-hinged arch formed by the $AC$ and $CB$ inclined rods, connected at $C$ by an internal hinge. At $A$ there is a pin support and at $B$ a roller support that moves along a horizontal plane. A horizontal tie $DE$ connects the midpoints of the two rods. Determine whether the structure is statically determinate.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,2,1.5;C,4,3;E,6,1.5;B,8,0" aste="A-D;D-C;C-E;E-B;D-E" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" cerniere="C;D;E" tratteggi="0,3 4,3" quote="A,B,8 m,0.9;0 0,0 3,3 m,-0.7"}

$$
n = 2 ,
\qquad
s = 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
\text{hinge } C : \ C_{12} = C = (4, 3)\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\text{tie } DE : \ C_{12} \in \text{line } y = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
C \notin DE
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{no relative displacement}
$$
$$
\text{forms one body with } C_1 = A ,
\quad
C_1 \in r_B : \ x = 8\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{stationary} ,
\qquad
l = i = 0
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
\begin{cases}
-3\varphi_1 = u_2 - 3\varphi_2 \\
4\varphi_1 = -4\varphi_2 \\
(u_2 - 1.5\varphi_2) - (-1.5\varphi_1) = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_2 = -\varphi_1 ,
\quad
u_2 = -6\varphi_1 ,
\quad
-3\varphi_1 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tie is an internal two-force link between the two rods and equals $1$ scalar constraint, hence $s = 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6$, with the pin support, internal hinge, roller support and tie. You first look for the relative center $C_{12}$. For the **relative center imposed by an internal constraint** theorem, the hinge $C$ imposes $C_{12} = C$ and the tie requires $C_{12}$ to stand on its axis, the line $y = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}$. The point $C$ is at $3\,\mathrm{m}$ elevation, so the two conditions are incompatible and the relative displacement is zero: the two rods with the tie behave as one body, the triangle $ACB$ stiffened by the tie. This body has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$ with the vertical reaction, which does not pass through $A$; So it's still. The structure is **statically determinate**.

In verification the body $1$ reference point is at $A$, with zero displacement, and the body $2$ reference point is at $B$, where the roller support cancels the vertical displacement and leaves $u_2$ free. The first two equations equal the displacements of $C$ in the two bodies; the third requires that the horizontal tie does not change length, i.e. that $D$ and $E$ have the same horizontal displacement. Substituting the first two in the third results in $-3\varphi_1 = 0$, so all parameters are zero.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The portal frame in the figure consists of two bodies: the column $AD$ with the crossbeam section on the left and the column $BG$ with the crossbeam section on the right, connected in the middle of the crossbeam by an internal parallel-link connection with the horizontal links. At $A$ and $B$ there are two pin supports. Determine whether the portal frame is statically determinate and, if it is unstable, describe the mechanism.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,0,4;M,3,4,-;G,6,4;B,6,0" aste="A-D;D-M;M-G;G-B" interni="M,doppio-pendolo,0" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9;B,G,4 m;D,M,3 m,-0.7"}

$$
n = 2 ,
\qquad
s = 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
C_1 = A ,
\qquad
C_2 = B ,
\qquad
C_{12} = X_\infty ,
\qquad
\varphi_1 = \varphi_2
$$
$$
\text{line } AB : \ y = 0 \ \text{horizontal}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A ,\ B ,\ X_\infty \ \text{collinear}
$$
$$
l = 1 ,
\qquad
i = 1
$$
$$
\text{check, at } (3, 4)\,\mathrm{m} : \quad
\vec{s}_1 = \varphi \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\vec{s}_2 = \varphi \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 3 - 6 \end{pmatrix}\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
\vec{s}_2 - \vec{s}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -6\varphi\,\mathrm{m} \end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Pin supports give the absolute centers $C_1 = A$ and $C_2 = B$. Internal parallel-link connection with horizontal links prevents relative rotation, hence $\varphi_1 = \varphi_2$, and horizontal relative displacement; the relative displacement is therefore a vertical translation and the relative center is the horizontal $X_\infty$ point at infinity. For the **first theorem of kinematic chains** $C_1$, $C_2$ and $C_{12}$ must be aligned, i.e. the line through $A$ and $B$ must have the horizontal direction of $X_\infty$. Line $AB$ is horizontal, so the condition is satisfied and the portal frame is unstable. The two columns rotate through the same angle about $A$ and $B$, respectively, and the two crossbeam segments slide vertically relative to each other. You have $l = 1$ and, from the $3n - s = l - i$ report, $i = 1$.

With vertical links, $C_{12}$ would be the point at infinity in the vertical direction, the horizontal line $AB$ would not contain it and the portal frame would be statically determinate.

In the check, we calculate the displacements of the point of the crossbeam where there is parallel-link support, $(3, 4)\,\mathrm{m}$, as the point of the body $1$, which rotates $\varphi$ around $A$, and as the point of the body $2$, which rotates the same $\varphi$ around $B = (6, 0)\,\mathrm{m}$. The difference has zero horizontal component, allowed by the horizontal links, and vertical component $-6\varphi\,\mathrm{m}$, i.e. the relative sliding that the parallel-link support leaves free.

</div>
