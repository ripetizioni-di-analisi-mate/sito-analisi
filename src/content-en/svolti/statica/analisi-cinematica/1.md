## Exercise 1

The beam shown has length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, a pin at $A$, and a roller at $B$ on a horizontal surface. Is it unstable, statically determinate, or statically indeterminate?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" quote="A,B,6 m"}

$$
n = 1 ,
\qquad
s = 2 + 1 = 3 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
C_1 = A = (0, 0) ,
\qquad
C_1 \in r_B : \ x = 6\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
A \notin r_B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
l = 0 ,
\qquad
i = l - (3n - s) = 0
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
\det A = \det \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 6 \end{pmatrix} = 6 \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The beam is a single rigid body, so it has $3$ degrees of freedom. The pin imposes $2$ scalar restraints and the roller imposes $1$. Thus $s = 3$, and the count gives $3n - s = 0$, a necessary condition for static determinacy. To check stability, suppose the beam can move. The pin requires the center of rotation $C_1$ to coincide with $A$. The roller requires $C_1$ to lie on its reaction line, the vertical $r_B$ through $B$. The vertical through $B$ does not pass through $A$, so no center satisfies both conditions and the beam remains stationary. Therefore $l = 0$, and the relation $3n - s = l - i$ also gives $i = 0$. The beam is **statically determinate**.

For an independent check, form the kinematic matrix using reference point $A$ and parameters $u_A$, $v_A$, and $\varphi$. The pin gives rows $u_A = 0$ and $v_A = 0$, and the roller gives row $v_B = v_A + 6\varphi = 0$. The determinant is nonzero, so the only compatible displacement is zero, in agreement with the center-of-rotation argument.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The cantilever shown has length $4\,\mathrm{m}$, a fixed support at $A$, and a roller at $B$ on a horizontal surface. Find its degrees of kinematic instability and static indeterminacy.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180;B,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m"}

$$
n = 1 ,
\qquad
s = 3 + 1 = 4 ,
\qquad
3n - s = -1
$$
$$
\text{fixed support at } A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
l = 0 ,
\qquad
i = l - (3n - s) = 0 - (-1) = 1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
r = 3 ,
\qquad
l = 3 - 3 = 0 ,
\qquad
i = 4 - 3 = 1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Fixed support equals $3$ scalar constraints and $1$ roller support, so $s = 4$ exceeds the degrees of freedom by one. The count only says that $i \ge 1$; to know $i$ serves $l$. Fixed support alone prevents any movement of the beam, therefore $l = 0$, and the $3n - s = l - i$ relationship gives $i = 1$, so the cantilever is **statically indeterminate to degree one**. The roller support is the excess constraint, because the cantilever would be statically determinate even without it.

In verification the kinematic matrix, with the reference point at $A$, has four lines: $u_A = 0$, $v_A = 0$, $\varphi = 0$ from fixed support and $v_A + 4\varphi = 0$ from roller support. The first three lines are independent and the fourth is the sum of the second and four times the third, so the rank is $3$ and the rank formula confirms $l = 0$ and $i = 1$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown has rollers on horizontal surfaces at $A$ and $B$. A two-force link at end $C$ of the overhang slopes downward to the left at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. Is the beam statically determinate?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0;C,7,0" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,carrello;B,carrello;C,pendolo,225" quote="A,B,5 m;B,C,2 m"}

$$
n = 1 ,
\qquad
s = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
r_A : \ x = 0 ,
\qquad
r_B : \ x = 5\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
r_C : \ y = x - 7\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
r_A \parallel r_B ,
\qquad
r_C \not\parallel r_A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
r_A \cap r_B \cap r_C = \emptyset
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
l = i = 0
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
\det \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 5 \\ 1 & 1 & 7 \end{pmatrix}
= 0 \cdot (7 - 5) - 1 \cdot (0 \cdot 7 - 5 \cdot 1) + 0 = 5 \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The beam has three scalar constraints, so the **body with three scalar constraints** theorem applies: the beam is statically determinate if and only if the lines of action of the three reactions do not pass by the same point. Roller support reactions are vertical, so $r_A$ and $r_B$ are the vertical lines through $A$ and $B$; the reaction of the two-force link is directed along its axis, the line through $C$ tilted by $45^\circ$. The two verticals are parallel and meet only at the point at infinity in the vertical direction. The line $r_C$ is not vertical, so it does not pass through that point, and the three lines have no common points. The beam is **statically determinate**.

In verification the reference point is at $A$. The roller support at $A$ gives $v_A = 0$, the one at $B$ gives $v_A + 5\varphi = 0$. The two-force link requires that the displacement of $C$ along its axis, direction $(1, 1)$, is zero, i.e. $u_C + v_C = 0$ with $u_C = u_A$ and $v_C = v_A + 7\varphi$; the line was written without the $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ factor of the unit vector, which does not change the determinant to less than a non-zero factor.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The L-shaped frame shown has a column $AB$ of height $4\,\mathrm{m}$ rigidly connected at $B$ to a crossbeam $BC$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$. At $A$ it has a parallel-link support with vertical links and at $C$ a roller on a vertical surface. Is the frame statically determinate?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,4,4" aste="A-B;B-C" vincoli="A,doppio-pendolo;C,carrello,0" quote="B,C,4 m,-0.7;A,B,4 m,-0.7"}

$$
n = 1 ,
\qquad
s = 2 + 1 = 3 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
\text{parallel-link support at } A : \ C_1 = Y_\infty ,
\qquad
\text{roller at } C : \ C_1 \in r_C : \ y = 4\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
Y_\infty \notin r_C
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
l = i = 0
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
\det \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & -4 \end{pmatrix}
= -1 \cdot (0 \cdot (-4) - 1 \cdot 1) = 1 \neq 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Joint $B$ is rigid, so the column and beam act as one body. The two vertical parallel links prevent rotation and vertical translation. Only horizontal translation is possible, placing the instantaneous center at the point at infinity in the vertical direction, $Y_\infty$. The roller at $C$ runs on a vertical surface and exerts a horizontal reaction, so $r_C$ is the horizontal line through $C$. That line contains only the horizontal point at infinity. No center satisfies both conditions, so the frame cannot move. The frame is **statically determinate**. The three-restraint criterion gives the same result because the two vertical link axes and the horizontal line through $C$ have no common point.

In verification, with the reference point at $A$, the lines are $v_A = 0$ and $\varphi = 0$ for parallel-link support and $u_C = u_A - 4\varphi = 0$ for roller support, because $C$ stands $4\,\mathrm{m}$ above the reference point.

</div>
