## Exercise 1

The Gerber beam shown consists of segments $AC$ and $CD$ joined by internal hinge $C$. There is a pin support at $A$ and rollers on horizontal surfaces at $B$ and $D$. Determine whether the beam is statically determinate.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,6,0;D,10,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;D,carrello" cerniere="C" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,2 m;C,D,4 m"}

$$
n = 2 ,
\qquad
s = 2 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 6 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
\text{body } 1 \ (AC) : \quad C_1 = A ,
\quad
C_1 \in r_B : \ x = 4\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{stationary}
$$
$$
\text{body } 2 \ (CD) : \quad C_2 = C ,
\quad
C_2 \in r_D : \ x = 10\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{stationary}
$$
$$
l = 0 ,
\qquad
i = 0
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
\begin{cases}
u_1 = 0 \\
v_1 = 0 \\
v_1 + 4\varphi_1 = 0 \\
u_2 - u_1 = 0 \\
v_2 - (v_1 + 6\varphi_1) = 0 \\
v_2 + 4\varphi_2 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
u_1 = v_1 = \varphi_1 = u_2 = v_2 = \varphi_2 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The internal hinge separates the beam into two bodies, then $3n = 6$; scalar constraints are $2$ of the pin support, $1$ for each roller support and $2$ of the internal hinge, therefore $s = 6$. It proceeds in steps, starting from the part bound to the ground in a statically determinate way. The segment $AC$ has the pin at $A$ and the roller support at $B$, whose vertical reaction does not pass through $A$: its center should stand in two distinct points, so it is stationary. It is the beam *supporting member*. The point $C$ is then stationary and the internal hinge is, for the segment $CD$, a pin support: the center of $CD$ should be $C$ and stand on the vertical through $D$, which does not pass through $C$. $CD$, the beam *suspended span*, is also stationary. Therefore $l = 0$ and, from the relation $3n - s = l - i$, $i = 0$, so the beam is **statically determinate**.

In the check, the six conditions of the constraints with the reference points at $A$ for the body $1$ and at $C$ for the body $2$ are written. The first three give $\varphi_1 = 0$; the internal hinge requires that $C$ has the same displacement in the two bodies, then $u_2 = 0$ and $v_2 = 0$, and the last condition gives $\varphi_2 = 0$. The only solution is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The Gerber beam shown has a pin at $A$, internal hinges at $C$ and $D$, and rollers on horizontal surfaces at $B$, $E$, and $F$. Segment $CD$ is suspended between cantilevers $AC$ and $DF$. Determine whether the beam is statically determinate.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;C,5,0;D,7,0;E,8,0;F,11,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;E-F" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello;E,carrello;F,carrello" cerniere="C;D" quote="A,B,4 m;B,C,1 m;C,D,2 m;D,E,1 m;E,F,3 m"}

$$
n = 3 ,
\qquad
s = 2 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 9 ,
\qquad
3n - s = 0
$$
$$
\text{body } 1 \ (AC) : \quad C_1 = A ,
\quad
C_1 \in r_B
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{stationary},
\qquad
C_2 = C
$$
$$
\text{body } 3 \ (DF) : \quad C_3 \in r_E \cap r_F = Y_\infty ,
\qquad
C_{23} = D
$$
$$
C_2 ,\ C_{23} ,\ C_3 \ \text{collinear} \iff \text{the line } CD \text{ is vertical}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{bodies } 2 \text{ and } 3 \text{ stationary}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
\begin{cases}
u_3 = 0 \\
v_3 = 2\varphi_2 \\
v_3 + \varphi_3 = 0 \\
v_3 + 4\varphi_3 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_3 = 0 ,
\quad
v_3 = 0 ,
\quad
\varphi_2 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bodies are three, $AC$, $CD$ and $DF$, and the scalar constraints are $9$, so $3n - s = 0$. The section $AC$, with the pin at $A$ and the roller support at $B$, is stationary as in the previous exercise. The point $C$ is therefore stationary and the internal hinge $C$ imposes $C_2 = C$. The segment $DF$ has two roller supports with vertical reactions, so its center $C_3$ lies on two distinct verticals and is their common point, the $Y_\infty$ point at infinity in the vertical direction; the internal hinge $D$ gives $C_{23} = D$.

If the $2$ and $3$ bodies moved both, for the **first theorem of kinematic chains** $C_2$, $C_{23}$ and $C_3$ would be aligned, i.e. the line through $C$ and $D$ would have the vertical direction of $C_3$; the line $CD$ is horizontal. If only one of the two bodies moved, the point $D$ would remain still and the other body would have two distinct centers: $C$ and $D$ for the body $2$, $D$ and $Y_\infty$ for the body $3$. In any case the two bodies are stationary, so $l = 0$, $i = 0$ and the beam is **statically determinate**. The $AC$ and $DF$ cantilever are the load-bearing beams and the section $CD$ is the beam suspended span.

In verification the body $1$ is already stationary, so the $C$ reference point of the body $2$ has zero displacement. With the body $3$ reference point at $D$, the $D$ internal hinge imposes $u_3 = 0$ and $v_3 = 2\varphi_2$, displacement of $D$ as the body $2$ point; Roller supports at $E$ and $F$, $1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $D$, give the last two conditions. Subtracting them gives you $3\varphi_3 = 0$, then $v_3 = 0$ and finally $\varphi_2 = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown has a pin at $A$, two internal hinges $B$ and $C$ in its first span, and rollers on horizontal surfaces at $D$, $E$, and $F$. Verify $s = 3n$ and determine whether the beam is statically determinate. If it is unstable, describe the mechanism.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0;C,4,0;D,5,0;E,8,0;F,11,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D;D-E;E-F" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello;E,carrello;F,carrello" cerniere="B;C" quote="A,B,2 m;B,C,2 m;C,D,1 m;D,E,3 m;E,F,3 m"}

$$
n = 3 ,
\qquad
s = 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 9 = 3n
$$
$$
C_1 = A ,
\qquad
C_{12} = B ,
\qquad
C_{23} = C ,
\qquad
C_3 \in r_D \cap r_E \cap r_F = Y_\infty
$$
$$
C_2 \in AB : \ y = 0 ,
\qquad
C_2 \in \text{line through } C \text{ vertical} : \ x = 4\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_2 = (4, 0)\,\mathrm{m} = C
$$
$$
C_2 = C_{23}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C \text{ stationary} ,
\qquad
\text{body } 3 \text{ stationary}
$$
$$
\varphi_1\,\overrightarrow{AB} = \varphi_2\,\overrightarrow{C_2 B}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_1 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{m} = \varphi_2 \cdot (-2\,\mathrm{m})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_2 = -\varphi_1
$$
$$
l = 1 ,
\qquad
i = l - (3n - s) = 1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad
v_B = \varphi_1 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
v_B = \varphi_2 \cdot (2 - 4)\,\mathrm{m} = 2\varphi_1\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
v_C = \varphi_2 \cdot (4 - 4)\,\mathrm{m} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The count gives $s = 3n$, but the two internal hinges are in the same span, between the pin support and the first roller support. The segment $CF$ has three roller supports with vertical reactions, so it can at most translate horizontally and its center is $Y_\infty$. The section $AB$ rotates about $A$. The center of the section $BC$ is with the **first theorem of kinematic chains** applied twice: it lies on the line through $C_1 = A$ and $C_{12} = B$, that is, on the axis of the beam, and on the line through $C_{23} = C$ with the direction of $C_3$, that is, on the vertical through $C$. The intersection is the point $C$ itself.

The segment $BC$ then rotates about $C$, and the point $C$ is stationary. The section $CF$ then has two distinct centers, $C$ and $Y_\infty$, and is stationary. One mechanism remains: $AB$ rotates about $A$ and $BC$ rotates about $C$ in the opposite direction, with $\varphi_2 = -\varphi_1$, while the hinge $B$ moves vertically. The beam is **unstable and statically indeterminate**, with $l = i = 1$: it is unstable in the first span, while the segment $CF$, stationary and with three roller supports, has an excess constraint. With the second internal hinge in another span, for example between $E$ and $F$, the beam would be statically determinate.

In verification, the vertical displacement of $B$ calculated as the point of $AB$, which rotates about $A$, coincides with that calculated as the point of $BC$, which rotates about $C$; $C$ displacement is zero, compatible with the stationary $CF$ segment. Horizontal shifts are zero, because all centers stand on the axis of the beam.

</div>
