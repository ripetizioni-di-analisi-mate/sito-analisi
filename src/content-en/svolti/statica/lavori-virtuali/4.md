## Exercise 1

The simply supported beam in the figure has $AB = 8\,\mathrm{m}$ span and brings the vertical forces $F_1 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, and $F_2 = 14\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $D$, $6\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, both downwards. Calculate with the principle of virtual work the shear force in the midpoint $E$ of the beam.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;E,4,0;D,6,0;B,8,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-1.6,F_1;D,0,-1.6,F_2" quote="A,C,2 m,0.9;C,E,2 m,0.9;E,D,2 m,0.9;D,B,2 m,0.9" titolo="Simply supported beam with $F_1 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$ and $F_2 = 14\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $D$"}

Introduce a parallel-link connection at $E$. Segments $AE$ and $EB$ rotate through the same angle $\delta\varphi$ about $A$ and $B$, respectively.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;E,4,0;D,6,0;B,8,0" aste="A-B" interni="E,doppio-pendolo,0" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-1.6,F_1;D,0,-1.6,F_2" tratteggi="0,0 4,1;4,-1 8,0" titolo="With a parallel-link connection at $E$ the two segments remain parallel. Dashed lines show the displacement for $\delta\varphi > 0$"}

$$
\delta v_s = 4\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_d = (4 - 8)\,\delta\varphi = -4\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\Delta v = \delta v_d - \delta v_s = -8\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
\delta v_C = 2\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_D = (6 - 8)\,\delta\varphi = -2\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
\delta L = -10 \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi - 14 \cdot (-2\,\delta\varphi) = 8\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
T_E\,\Delta v + \delta L = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T_E = -\frac{8\,\delta\varphi}{-8\,\delta\varphi} = 1\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Verification: from the rotation of the whole beam around $B$, $-8\,V_A + 10 \cdot 6 + 14 \cdot 2 = 0$, then $V_A = 11\,\mathrm{kN}$; vertical translation of section $AE$:
$$
V_A - F_1 - T_E = 11 - 10 - 1 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For shear force it fits into $E$ an **parallel-link support** with pin-ended links parallel to the axis: transmits the axial force and the moment and leaves the relative vertical scroll free. The parallel-link connection prevents relative rotation, so both segments rotate through the same angle; the left segment around $A$, the right segment around $B$. The point $E$ then has two different shifts, $4\,\delta\varphi$ upwards on the left and $4\,\delta\varphi$ downwards on the right, and the relative scroll is $\Delta v = -8\,\delta\varphi$. For $\delta\varphi > 0$ the force $F_1$ gets up and does negative work, the force $F_2$ gets down and does positive work. The result is $T_E = 1\,\mathrm{kN}$, positive: on the face of the segment $AE$ the shear force is directed downwards. The check uses vertical translation of the segment $AE$, on which $V_A$, $F_1$ act and shear force.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The beam shown has a pin at $A$, a roller at $B$ with $AB = 6\,\mathrm{m}$, and an overhang $BC = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. The $q = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ uniform load acts on the whole beam and at $C$ the force $F = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downwards. Calculate with the principle of virtual work the shear force immediately to the left and immediately to the right of the $B$ support.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,8,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,C,0.6,0.6,q" forze="C,0,-1.8,F" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9;B,C,2 m,0.9" titolo="Overhanging beam with $q = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on $AC$ and $F = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$"}

Immediately to the left of $B$: the right section includes the support and rotates about $B$, the left section around $A$, both of $\delta\varphi$.
$$
\delta v_s = 6\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_d = 0 ,
\qquad
\Delta v = -6\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
\delta L = -24 \cdot 3\,\delta\varphi - 8 \cdot 1\,\delta\varphi - 8 \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi = -96\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
T_B^{-} = -\frac{-96\,\delta\varphi}{-6\,\delta\varphi} = -16\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Immediately to the right of $B$: the section $AB$ is bound by the hinge and roller support and remains stationary, so even the right section does not rotate and transposes vertically of $\delta v_d$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;S,6.4,0,-;C,8,0" aste="A-C" interni="S,doppio-pendolo,0" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,C,0.6,0.6,q" forze="C,0,-1.8,F" tratteggi="6.4,-0.6 8,-0.6" titolo="With a parallel-link connection just to the right of $B$, only the overhang moves, translating vertically"}

$$
\Delta v = \delta v_d ,
\qquad
\delta L = -8\,\delta v_d - 8\,\delta v_d = -16\,\delta v_d
$$
$$
T_B^{+} = -\frac{-16\,\delta v_d}{\delta v_d} = 16\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Verification: from the rotation of the whole beam around $A$, $6\,R_B - 32 \cdot 4 - 8 \cdot 8 = 0$, then $R_B = 32\,\mathrm{kN}$; vertical translation of the beam element over the support:
$$
T_B^{-} + R_B - T_B^{+} = -16 + 32 - 16 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Above the support the shear force has a jump equal to the reaction, so the two sections are distinguished. With parallel-link support immediately to the left of $B$ the roller support belongs to the right section, which rotates about $B$; the load on $AB$ replaces with the resultant $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $x = 3$, the one on the overhang with $8\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $x = 7$. All loads rise for $\delta\varphi > 0$ and the work is negative. You get $T_B^- = -16\,\mathrm{kN}$. With parallel-link support immediately to the right of $B$, the section $AB$ remains stationary, and parallel-link support imposes the same null rotation on the overhang: the overhang can only move vertically. The work is that of the overhang loads for common displacement, and $T_B^+ = 16\,\mathrm{kN}$, i.e. the total load that stands to the right of the section. In the check the beam element over the support receives the shear force $T_B^-$ upwards on the left face, the reaction $R_B$ and the shear force $T_B^+$ downwards on the right face, and the sum is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The simply supported beam shown has span $AB = 6\,\mathrm{m}$ and carries a triangular load that increases from $0$ at $A$ to $q_0 = 9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$. Calculate with the principle of virtual work the shear force at the midpoint $S$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;S,3,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0,1.2,q_0" quote="A,S,3 m,0.9;S,B,3 m,0.9" titolo="Simply supported beam with a triangular load varying from $0$ to $q_0 = 9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$"}

The load equals $q(x) = 1.5\,x$ (in $\mathrm{kN/m}$, with $x$ in $\mathrm{m}$). The left and right parts of $S$:
$$
Q_s = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 4.5 = 6.75\,\mathrm{kN} \ \text{in}\ x = 2 ,
\qquad
Q_d = \frac{4.5 + 9}{2} \cdot 3 = 20.25\,\mathrm{kN} \ \text{in}\ x = \frac{14}{3}
$$
$$
x_d = \frac{1}{Q_d}\int_3^6 1.5\,x^2\,dx = \frac{1.5 \cdot 63}{20.25} = \frac{14}{3}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

Parallel-link support at $S$, $\delta\varphi$ rotation of the two segments around $A$ and $B$:
$$
\delta v_s = 3\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_d = -3\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\Delta v = -6\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
\delta v(2) = 2\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v\!\left( \frac{14}{3} \right) = \left( \frac{14}{3} - 6 \right) \delta\varphi = -\frac{4}{3}\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
\delta L = -6.75 \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi + 20.25 \cdot \frac{4}{3}\,\delta\varphi = -13.5\,\delta\varphi + 27\,\delta\varphi = 13.5\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
T_S = -\frac{13.5\,\delta\varphi}{-6\,\delta\varphi} = 2.25\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Verification: the total load is $27\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $x = 4$, and from rotation about $B$, $-6\,V_A + 27 \cdot 2 = 0$, then $V_A = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$; vertical translation of section $AS$:
$$
V_A - Q_s - T_S = 9 - 6.75 - 2.25 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangular load is divided into section $S$: on the left remains a triangle, with result equal to the area and applied to two thirds of the base from $A$, in $x = 2$; to the right remains a trapezoid, of which area and centroid are calculated, even with the integral. The two parts stand on different segments of the mechanism and have opposite-sign shifts: the triangle rises with the segment $AS$, the trapezoid lowers with the segment $SB$. For this the overall work is the difference of two terms, and the shear force in the middle is small compared to the reactions. The result is $T_S = 2.25\,\mathrm{kN}$. The shear force cancels further to the right, where the bending moment is maximum. The check uses the vertical translation of the segment $AS$, with the reaction $V_A$ calculated from the rotation of the entire beam around $B$.

</div>
