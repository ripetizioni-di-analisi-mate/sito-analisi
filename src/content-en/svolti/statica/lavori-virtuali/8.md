## Exercise 1

The beam shown is formed by the section $AB$, with a pin support at $A$, and the section $BD$, connected to the first by the internal hinge $B$ and resting on a roller support at $C$; the horizontal coordinates are $A = 0$, $B = 3\,\mathrm{m}$, $C = 6\,\mathrm{m}$, $D = 8\,\mathrm{m}$. At $E$, at $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, the force $F_1 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. Check that the structure is unstable and calculate the vertical force $F_2$ to be applied at $D$ for it to be in equilibrium.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;E,1,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,8,0" aste="A-B;B-D" cerniere="B" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello" forze="E,0,-1.6,F_1;D,0,-1.6,F_2" quote="A,E,1 m,1;E,B,2 m,1;B,C,3 m,1;C,D,2 m,1" titolo="Two-body beam with $F_1 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $E$ and unknown force $F_2$ at $D$"}

Kinematic analysis: $n = 2$, $s = 2 + 2 + 1 = 5$, then $3n - s = 1$. The $C_1 = A$, $C_{12} = B$ and $C_2 = C$ centers are aligned on the axis, and the structure is a mechanism.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;E,1,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;D,8,0" aste="A-B;B-D" cerniere="B" vincoli="A,cerniera;C,carrello" forze="E,0,-1.6,F_1;D,0,-1.6,F_2" tratteggi="0,0 3,0.9 6,0 8,-0.6" titolo="Mechanism for $\delta\varphi_1 > 0$, showing hinge $B$ rising and overhang $CD$ descending"}

$$
\delta v_B = 3\,\delta\varphi_1 = (3 - 6)\,\delta\varphi_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta\varphi_2 = -\delta\varphi_1
$$
$$
\delta v_E = \delta\varphi_1 ,
\qquad
\delta v_D = (8 - 6)\,\delta\varphi_2 = -2\,\delta\varphi_1
$$
$$
-10 \cdot \delta\varphi_1 - F_2 \cdot (-2\,\delta\varphi_1) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_2 = 5\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check with the static method. Rotation of $AB$ around $A$, with $Y_B$ vertical force that $AB$ receives from $BD$: $3\,Y_B - 10 \cdot 1 = 0$, then $Y_B = \dfrac{10}{3}\,\mathrm{kN}$. On $BD$ acts at $B$ the force $\dfrac{10}{3}\,\mathrm{kN}$ downwards; rotation of $BD$ around $C$:
$$
-\frac{10}{3} \cdot (3 - 6) - F_2 \cdot (8 - 6) = 10 - 5 \cdot 2 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Constraint counting gives one degree of freedom, and the alignment of the three centers on the axis confirms that the mechanism exists. For an unstable structure the **principle of virtual work** says that there is balance only if the work of the loads is zero for the displacement of the mechanism; with only one degree of freedom this is an equation that binds the loads. For $\delta\varphi_1 > 0$ the segment $AB$ rises and the segment $BD$ rotates clockwise around $C$, so the overhang lowers. The force $F_1$ does negative work, and the force at $D$ must do positive work equal and opposite, so it is directed downward: **$F_2 = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$, downward**. With a different value of $F_2$ the structure is not in equilibrium. The check obtains the same value by disassembling the beam in the hinge $B$ and writing the rotation of each segment around its own constraint.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The four-bar linkage in the figure is formed by the column $AD$, with $A = (0, 0)$ and $D = (0, 4)$, the crossbeam $DE$, with $E = (6, 4)$, and the $EB$ inclined column, with $B = (8, 0)$ (coordinates in meters). At $A$ and $B$ there are pin supports, at $D$ and at $E$ internal hinges. At point $G = (3, 4)$ of the crossbeam, the force $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downward. Calculate the horizontal force $F$ to be applied at $D$ for the structure to be in equilibrium.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,0,4;G,3,4;E,6,4;B,8,0" aste="A-D;D-E;E-B" cerniere="D;E" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="G,0,-1.6,P;D,1.6,0,F" quote="D,G,3 m,-0.6;G,E,3 m,-0.6;A,D,4 m,0.7" titolo="Four-bar linkage with $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $G$ and unknown horizontal force $F$ at $D$"}

Kinematic analysis: $n = 3$, $s = 2 \cdot 4 = 8$, then $3n - s = 1$. The centers are $C_1 = A$, $C_3 = B$, $C_{12} = D$, $C_{23} = E$; $C_2$ lies on line $AD$, $x = 0$, and line $BE$, $(x, y) = (8, 0) + \lambda\,(-2, 4)$, then $C_2 = (0, 16)$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,0,4;G,3,4;E,6,4;B,8,0" aste="A-D;D-E;E-B" cerniere="D;E" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" forze="G,0,-1.6,P;D,1.6,0,F" tratteggi="0,4 0,16 6,4" punti="0,16,C_2,centro" titolo="The center of crossbeam $C_2 = (0, 16)$ is the intersection of the column axes"}

$$
D:\quad \delta u_D = -4\,\delta\varphi_1 = -\delta\varphi_2\,(4 - 16) = 12\,\delta\varphi_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta\varphi_2 = -\frac{1}{3}\,\delta\varphi_1
$$
$$
E:\quad \delta u_E = 12\,\delta\varphi_2 = -4\,\delta\varphi_1 ,
\quad
\delta v_E = 6\,\delta\varphi_2 = -2\,\delta\varphi_1 ;
\qquad
\delta u_E = -4\,\delta\varphi_3 ,
\quad
\delta v_E = (6 - 8)\,\delta\varphi_3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta\varphi_3 = \delta\varphi_1
$$
$$
\delta v_G = (3 - 0)\,\delta\varphi_2 = -\delta\varphi_1
$$
$$
F \cdot (-4\,\delta\varphi_1) - 12 \cdot (-\delta\varphi_1) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F = 3\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check with static method, with $F$ applied to crossbeam at $D$. The $AD$ and $EB$ columns are unloaded and hinged at the ends, so they transmit direct forces along their columns to the crossbeam, both of which pass through $C_2$. Crossbeam rotation around $C_2 = (0, 16)$:
$$
(3 - 0) \cdot (-P) - (4 - 16) \cdot F = -36 + 12 \cdot 3 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The four-bar linkage has one degree of freedom. The center of the crossbeam is found with the first theorem of kinematic chains: it lies on the line that joins $C_1 = A$ and $C_{12} = D$, that is, on the prolonged left-hand column, and on the line that joins $C_3 = B$ and $C_{23} = E$, that is, on the prolonged inclined column. The two lines meet in $C_2 = (0, 16)$, $12\,\mathrm{m}$ above the crossbeam. The displacement of $D$ on the two bodies binds $\delta\varphi_2$ to $\delta\varphi_1$, and the displacement of $E$ binds $\delta\varphi_3$; the two components of moving $E$ give the same value, and it is the control of the calculations. Since $C_2$ lies on the vertical through $D$, the point $G$ moves vertically by $-\delta\varphi_1$ and the load $P$ does positive work for $\delta\varphi_1 > 0$; the force $F$ must perform an equal and opposite work. The result is **$F = 3\,\mathrm{kN}$, to the right**. In checking the moment about $C_2$ it eliminates the forces of the two columns and leaves only $P$ and $F$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown is formed by three segments: $AB$, with a pin support at $A$; $BC$, connected to $AB$ by the internal hinge $B$; $CD$, connected to $BC$ by the internal hinge $C$ and resting on a roller support at $D$. The axes are $A = 0$, $B = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, $C = 6\,\mathrm{m}$, $D = 8\,\mathrm{m}$. At the midpoint $E$ of $BC$ the force $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts downwards. Calculate the vertical forces $P_B$ and $P_C$, applied in the $B$ and $C$ hinges and positive upwards, which make the structure in equilibrium.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0;E,4,0;C,6,0;D,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello" forze="E,0,-1.6,P;B,0,1.2,P_B;C,0,1.2,P_C" quote="A,B,2 m,1.7;B,E,2 m,1.7;E,C,2 m,1.7;C,D,2 m,1.7" titolo="Three-segment beam with $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $E$ and unknown forces $P_B$ and $P_C$ at the hinges"}

Kinematic analysis: $n = 3$, $s = 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 7$, then $3n - s = 2$. Two independent mechanisms are chosen.

First mechanism, $AB$ segment stationary: $BC$ rotates about $B$, $CD$ around $D$.
$$
\delta v_C = 4\,\delta\varphi_2 = (6 - 8)\,\delta\varphi_3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta\varphi_3 = -2\,\delta\varphi_2 ,
\qquad
\delta v_E = 2\,\delta\varphi_2 ,
\qquad
\delta v_B = 0
$$
$$
P_C \cdot 4\,\delta\varphi_2 - 12 \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi_2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_C = 6\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Second mechanism, segment $CD$ stationary: $BC$ rotates about $C$, $AB$ around $A$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,0;E,4,0;C,6,0;D,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,carrello" forze="E,0,-1.6,P;B,0,1.2,P_B;C,0,1.2,P_C" tratteggi="0,0 2,0.8 6,0" titolo="Second mechanism. Segment $CD$ remains stationary, $BC$ rotates about $C$, and $AB$ rotates about $A$. Dashed lines show the displacement for $\delta\varphi_1 > 0$"}

$$
\delta v_B = 2\,\delta\varphi_1 = (2 - 6)\,\delta\varphi_2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta\varphi_2 = -\frac{1}{2}\,\delta\varphi_1 ,
\qquad
\delta v_E = (4 - 6)\,\delta\varphi_2 = \delta\varphi_1 ,
\qquad
\delta v_C = 0
$$
$$
P_B \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi_1 - 12\,\delta\varphi_1 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_B = 6\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check with the static method. Rotation of $AB$ around $A$: The vertical force that $AB$ receives from $BC$ at $B$ is $-P_B$, so $BC$ receives the force $P_B = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ acting upwards at $B$; Similarly, from the rotation of $CD$ around $D$, $BC$ receives at $C$ the force $P_C = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ upwards. $BC$ rotation around $B$:
$$
-12 \cdot (4 - 2) + 6 \cdot (6 - 2) = -24 + 24 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The structure has two degrees of freedom, so compatible shifts are the combinations of two independent mechanisms, and balance requires that the work of the loads be zero for each of the two. Two mechanisms are chosen in which only one of the two unknown forces does work: holding the segment $AB$, the hinge $B$ does not move and $P_B$ does not do work; holding the segment $CD$, it is $C$ that does not move. Each mechanism thus gives an equation with only one unknown. You get **$P_B = P_C = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward**: the segment $BC$ behaves like a simply supported beam in the two hinges, and the $AB$ and $CD$ segments transmit the applied forces without charging. The check uses the moment equilibrium of the segment $BC$ around $B$ with the forces it receives from the other two segments.

</div>
