## Exercise 1

The beam shown has a pin at $A$ and a roller at $B$, with span $AB = 5\,\mathrm{m}$. A downward force $F = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $C$, a distance $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Use virtual work to find the support reactions.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="C,0,-1.3,F" quote="A,C,2 m,0.9;C,B,3 m,0.9" titolo="Simply supported beam under force $F = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$"}

Set $A = (0, 0)$. To find $V_B$, remove the roller and let the beam rotate through $\delta\varphi$ about $A$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,2,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera" reazioni="B,0,1.3,V_B" forze="C,0,-1.3,F" tratteggi="0,0 5,-1" titolo="With the roller removed, the beam rotates about $A$. Dashed lines show the displacement for $\delta\varphi < 0$"}

$$
\delta v_C = 2\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_B = 5\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
V_B \cdot 5\,\delta\varphi - 20 \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_B = \frac{40}{5} = 8\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

To find $V_A$, replace the pin with a roller that permits vertical motion. The beam then rotates about $B$.
$$
\delta v_A = (0 - 5)\,\delta\varphi = -5\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_C = (2 - 5)\,\delta\varphi = -3\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
V_A \cdot (-5\,\delta\varphi) - 20 \cdot (-3\,\delta\varphi) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_A = \frac{60}{5} = 12\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

To find $H_A$, replace the pin with a roller that permits horizontal motion. The beam then translates horizontally by $\delta u$.
$$
H_A\,\delta u = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H_A = 0
$$

Check by taking moments about $C$.
$$
-V_A \cdot 2 + V_B \cdot 3 = -12 \cdot 2 + 8 \cdot 3 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

To find each reaction, release the scalar restraint that produces it. This turns the beam into a mechanism with one degree of freedom, and the **principle of virtual work** gives an equation containing only the reaction sought. Removing the roller at $B$ leaves the pin at $A$, so the beam can only rotate about $A$. The vertical displacement of each point equals the rotation times its signed distance from $A$. Reaction $V_B$, taken as positive upward, does work $V_B\,\delta v_B$. The downward force $F$ does work $-F\,\delta v_C$. To find $V_A$, keep only the horizontal restraint at $A$. The center of rotation is where the horizontal through $A$ intersects the vertical through $B$, namely $B$. Then $C$ moves down when $\delta\varphi > 0$, and $F$ does positive work. To find $H_A$, translate the beam horizontally. No load has a horizontal component, so $H_A = 0$. The results are **$V_A = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, both upward**. For an independent check, take moments about $C$. Their sum is zero.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The beam shown has a pin at $A$, a roller at $B$, and an overhang $BC$, with $AB = 6\,\mathrm{m}$ and $BC = 2\,\mathrm{m}$. A uniform load $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts over the entire beam, and a downward force $F = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $C$. Use virtual work to find the support reactions.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,8,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,C,0.6,0.6,q" forze="C,0,-2,F" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9;B,C,2 m,0.9" titolo="Overhanging beam with $q = 3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on $AC$ and $F = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $C$"}

The distributed load lies on one body and has resultant $Q = 3 \cdot 8 = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $x = 4\,\mathrm{m}$.

$R_B$ reaction: removes the roller support, rotation around $A$.
$$
\delta v_B = 6\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v(4) = 4\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_C = 8\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
R_B \cdot 6\,\delta\varphi - 24 \cdot 4\,\delta\varphi - 9 \cdot 8\,\delta\varphi = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_B = \frac{96 + 72}{6} = 28\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

$V_A$ reaction: vertical roller support instead of the hinge, rotation around $B$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,8,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,carrello,180;B,carrello" reazioni="A,0,1.4,V_A" carichi="A,C,0.6,0.6,q" forze="C,0,-2,F" tratteggi="0,-1.2 8,0.4" titolo="Releasing $A$ vertically lets the beam rotate about $B$. Point $A$ moves down and the overhang rises. Dashed lines show the displacement for $\delta\varphi > 0$"}

$$
\delta v_A = -6\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v(4) = (4 - 6)\,\delta\varphi = -2\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_C = (8 - 6)\,\delta\varphi = 2\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
V_A \cdot (-6\,\delta\varphi) - 24 \cdot (-2\,\delta\varphi) - 9 \cdot 2\,\delta\varphi = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_A = \frac{48 - 18}{6} = 5\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

$H_A$ reaction: no horizontal load, hence $H_A = 0$.

Check vertical force equilibrium:
$$
V_A + R_B - Q - F = 5 + 28 - 24 - 9 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

For distributed load work the resultant applied in the centroid of the diagram is used, because on the rigid body vertical displacement is a first-degree function of $x$. After the roller support is removed, the beam rotates about $A$ and all loads lower when the beam lowers, so their work contributions have the same sign and **$R_B = 28\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward**. For $V_A$ the beam rotates about $B$: the center of the load, to the left of $B$, lowers when $A$ lowers, while the overhang rises. For this reason the distributed load and force at $C$ appear with opposite signs, and the load on the overhang reduces the reaction at $A$. The result is **$V_A = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward**. Splitting the load into the left and right parts of $B$ gives the same virtual work: $-18 \cdot (-3\,\delta\varphi) - 6 \cdot \delta\varphi = 48\,\delta\varphi$. The check uses the vertical force equilibrium equation, which was not used in the calculation.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown has length $9\,\mathrm{m}$ and two overhangs. Pin $A$ lies $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from end $D$, and roller $B$ lies $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from end $E$. A downward force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $D$, a uniform load $q = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts on $AB$, and a counterclockwise couple $\mathcal{M} = 15\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ acts at $E$. Use virtual work to find the support reactions.

::struttura{nodi="D,0,0;A,2,0;B,7,0;E,9,0" aste="D-E" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="D,0,-1.8,F" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,q" coppie="E,1,ℳ" quote="D,A,2 m,0.9;A,B,5 m,0.9;B,E,2 m,0.9" titolo="Beam with two overhangs, under $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $D$, $q = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on $AB$, and counterclockwise couple $\mathcal{M} = 15\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at $E$"}

Place the origin at $D$, then $A = (2, 0)$, $B = (7, 0)$, $E = (9, 0)$. The distributed load has resultant $Q = 4 \cdot 5 = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ in $x = 4.5\,\mathrm{m}$.

$R_B$ reaction: rotation around $A$, $\delta v = (x - 2)\,\delta\varphi$.

::struttura{nodi="D,0,0;A,2,0;B,7,0;E,9,0" aste="D-E" vincoli="A,cerniera" reazioni="B,0,1.5,R_B" forze="D,0,-1.8,F" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,q" coppie="E,1,ℳ" tratteggi="0,0.4 9,-1.4" titolo="Removing the roller at $B$ lets the beam rotate about $A$. Dashed lines show the displacement for $\delta\varphi < 0$"}

$$
\delta v_D = -2\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v(4.5) = 2.5\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_B = 5\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
R_B \cdot 5\,\delta\varphi - 10 \cdot (-2\,\delta\varphi) - 20 \cdot 2.5\,\delta\varphi + 15\,\delta\varphi = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_B = \frac{50 - 20 - 15}{5} = 3\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

$V_A$ reaction: rotation around $B$, $\delta v = (x - 7)\,\delta\varphi$.
$$
\delta v_D = -7\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v(4.5) = -2.5\,\delta\varphi ,
\qquad
\delta v_A = -5\,\delta\varphi
$$
$$
V_A \cdot (-5\,\delta\varphi) - 10 \cdot (-7\,\delta\varphi) - 20 \cdot (-2.5\,\delta\varphi) + 15\,\delta\varphi = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V_A = \frac{70 + 50 + 15}{5} = 27\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

$H_A$ reaction: no horizontal load, hence $H_A = 0$.

Check moment equilibrium about $D$:
$$
V_A \cdot 2 + R_B \cdot 7 - Q \cdot 4.5 + \mathcal{M} = 27 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 7 - 20 \cdot 4.5 + 15 = 54 + 21 - 90 + 15 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The couple does work $\mathcal{M}\,\delta\varphi$, where $\delta\varphi$ is the rotation of the body on which it acts; the point of application is immaterial because all points of a rigid body rotate through the same angle. In rotation about $A$ the point $D$ stands to the left of the center, then lowers when $B$ rises, and the force $F$ does positive work for $\delta\varphi > 0$; This is why the load on the left overhang reduces $R_B$. The counterclockwise couple also reduces $R_B$, because its work has the same sign as that of $F$. In rotation about $B$ all loads and couple do positive work for $\delta\varphi > 0$, so they add up. The results are **$V_A = 27\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $R_B = 3\,\mathrm{kN}$, both upwards**, and $H_A = 0$. The check uses the moment equilibrium about the end $D$, which does not appear in the two calculations; the sum gives zero, and the sum of vertical reactions, $30\,\mathrm{kN}$, is equal to $F + Q$.

</div>
