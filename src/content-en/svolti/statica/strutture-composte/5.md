## Exercise 1

The frame shown is formed by the body $ABC$, hinged to the ground in $A$, and the column $CD$, hinged to the body $ABC$ in $C$ and to the ground in $D$. The $AB$ and $CD$ are $4\,\mathrm{m}$ high, the crossbeam $BC$ is $6\,\mathrm{m}$ long. The crossbeam carries the uniform load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$; at joint $B$ the horizontal force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts to the right. Calculate support reactions and force in the column $CD$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,6,4;D,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" carichi="B,C,0.6,0.6,q" forze="B,1.5,0,F" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8;B,C,6 m,-1" titolo="Unsymmetrical frame with load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on the crossbeam and force $F = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,6,4;D,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" forze="3 4,0,-1.4,30 kN;B,1.5,0,F" reazioni="A,1.2,0,H_A;A,0,1.2,V_A;D,0,1.2,V_D" titolo="Free-body diagram of the entire structure showing the vertical reaction at $D$"}

$$
n = 2, \qquad 3n = 6, \qquad s = 2 + 2 + 2 = 6
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
H_A + F = 0 \\[4pt]
6\,V_D - 30 \cdot 3 - 4\,F = 0 \\[4pt]
V_A + V_D - 30 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
H_A = -12\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad V_D = \frac{90 + 48}{6} = 23\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad V_A = 7\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
N_{CD} = -V_D = -23\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check, rotation of the whole structure around $D$:
$$
-6\,V_A - 4\,F + 30 \cdot 3 = -42 - 48 + 90 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The column $CD$ is hinged at the two extremes and does not carry loads. For the **unloaded member hinged at the two extremes** theorem the forces it receives at $C$ and $D$ are directed along the line $CD$, which is vertical; therefore the reaction of the hinge $D$ has horizontal component zero. The frame is an **unsymmetrical frame**: the body $ABC$ is supported by the hinge $A$ and, through the column $CD$, by a two-force vertical link in $C$. The remaining unknowns are $H_A$, $V_A$ and $V_D$, and the three equations of the whole structure are sufficient.

In horizontal translation the only horizontal reaction is $H_A$, which balances the force $F$ alone. About $A$, the load resultant $30\,\mathrm{kN}$ at a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and force $F$ at elevation $4\,\mathrm{m}$ produce clockwise moments. Force $V_D$, with moment arm $6\,\mathrm{m}$, produces a counterclockwise moment. The results are: $H_A = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left, $V_A = 7\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward, $V_D = 23\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward, $H_D = 0$.

The column $CD$ receives from the ground the force of $23\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward in $D$ and from the body $ABC$ the opposite force at $C$, so it is **in compression**: $N_{CD} = -23\,\mathrm{kN}$. In the check around $D$ the reaction $V_A$ and the force $F$ have clockwise moment, the resultant of the load, $3\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left of $D$, has counterclockwise moment.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Consider the frame from the preceding exercise, with columns $AB$ and $CD$ of height $4\,\mathrm{m}$, beam $BC$ of length $6\,\mathrm{m}$, and hinges at $A$, $C$, and $D$. Its beam carries a uniform load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. A uniform horizontal load of $3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts to the left over the full height of column $CD$, as shown. Calculate the support reactions and the force at hinge $C$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,6,4;D,6,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" carichi="B,C,0.6,0.6,q;D,C,0.6,0.6,3 kN/m,180" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8;B,C,6 m,-1" titolo="Frame with column $CD$ under a uniform horizontal load of $3\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ to the left"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C1,6,4,-;C2,10,4,-;D,10,0" aste="A-B;B-C1;C2-D" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" forze="3 4,0,-1.4,30 kN;10 2,-0.8,0,12 kN;C1,-0.8,0,X_C;C1,0,-1,Y_C" reazioni="A,1.2,0,H_A;A,0,1.2,V_A;D,1.2,0,H_D;D,0,1.2,V_D;C2,0.8,0,X_C;C2,0,1,Y_C" etichette="5.8,4.3,C;10.2,4.3,C" punti="6,4;10,4" titolo="Free-body diagrams of body $ABC$ and column $CD$ showing resultant $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ of the horizontal load"}

Whole structure and auxiliary equation for the column $CD$:
$$
\begin{cases}
H_A + H_D - 12 = 0 \\[4pt]
V_A + V_D - 30 = 0 \\[4pt]
6\,V_D - 30 \cdot 3 + 12 \cdot 2 = 0 \\[4pt]
4\,H_D - 12 \cdot 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
V_D = \frac{90 - 24}{6} = 11\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad V_A = 19\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad H_D = 6\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad H_A = 6\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Force in the hinge, from the balance of the $CD$ Right:
$$
\begin{cases}
X_C + H_D - 12 = 0 \\[4pt]
Y_C + V_D = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
X_C = 6\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad Y_C = -11\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check, moment equilibrium of body $ABC$ about $C$:
$$
-6\,V_A + 4\,H_A + 30 \cdot 3 = -114 + 24 + 90 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The column $CD$ carries a load, so the forces in its hinges are not directed along its axis and the frame should be treated as a two-body structure. The external reactions are four: the three equations of the whole structure are added the **auxiliary equation** in $C$ for the $CD$ part. The horizontal load has resultant $3 \cdot 4 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left, applied in the middle of the column, at $2\,\mathrm{m}$ elevation.

In the rotation around $A$ the resultant of the horizontal load, directed to the left at $2\,\mathrm{m}$ elevation, has counterclockwise moment $12 \cdot 2 = 24\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, and $H_D$ has zero moment arm. In the auxiliary equation for the column $CD$ around $C$, $V_D$ has zero moment arm because it is applied on the vertical line through $C$; $H_D$, applied $4\,\mathrm{m}$ under $C$, has counterclockwise moment if positive; the resultant of the load, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ below $C$ and headed to the left, has clockwise moment. It follows that $H_D = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$: the hinge $D$ absorbs half the horizontal load, as if the column was a simply supported beam in $C$ and $D$ loaded by wind.

The results are: $H_A = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $H_D = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right, $V_A = 19\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_D = 11\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward. The body $ABC$ exerts on the column, in $C$, a force of $6\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right and $11\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward; the horizontal component, absent in the previous year, is due to the load on the column.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Beam $AB$ in the figure has length $4\,\mathrm{m}$. It is pinned to the wall at $A$ and supported at its tip $B$ by tie rod $BD$. The rod is pinned to the beam at $B$ and to the wall at $D$, a height $3\,\mathrm{m}$ above $A$. The beam carries a uniform load $q = 6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ and, at its tip $B$, a downward force $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$. Find the tension in the tie rod and the support reactions at $A$ and $D$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;D,0,3" aste="A-B;B-D" cerniere="B" vincoli="A,cerniera,180;D,cerniera,180" carichi="A,B,0.5,0.5,q" forze="B,0,-1.2,P" quote="A,B,4 m,0.8;A,D,3 m,0.8" titolo="Beam pinned to the wall and supported by tie rod $BD$, with load $q = 6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ and force $P = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera,180" forze="2 0,0,-1.2,24 kN;B,0,-1.2,P" reazioni="A,1,0,H_A;A,0,1,V_A;B,-0.8,0.6,N" tratteggi="4,0 0,3" titolo="Free-body diagram of the beam showing the tie-rod force along $BD$"}

$$
\cos\alpha = \frac{4}{5}, \qquad \sin\alpha = \frac{3}{5}, \qquad \overline{BD} = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
H_A - \dfrac{4}{5}\,N = 0 \\[8pt]
V_A + \dfrac{3}{5}\,N - 24 - P = 0 \\[8pt]
4 \cdot \dfrac{3}{5}\,N - 24 \cdot 2 - 4\,P = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
N = \frac{5 \cdot 96}{12} = 40\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad H_A = 32\,\mathrm{kN}, \quad V_A = 36 - 24 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
H_D = -32\,\mathrm{kN}, \qquad V_D = 24\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

Check, rotation of the whole structure around $D$:
$$
3\,H_A - 24 \cdot 2 - 4\,P = 96 - 48 - 48 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tie $BD$ member is a hinged unloaded member at the two extremes, so for the **unloaded member hinged at the two extremes** theorem it behaves like a two-force link: the force exerting on the beam in $B$ is directed along $BD$. $N$ is the axial force of tie, positive in tension. A tension pull pull pulls the beam $B$ towards $D$, i.e. to the left and up, with $-\dfrac{4}{5}\,N$ and $\dfrac{3}{5}\,N$ components, because $BD$ has $4\,\mathrm{m}$ projections horizontally and $3\,\mathrm{m}$ vertically over an $5\,\mathrm{m}$ length.

Apply horizontal and vertical force equilibrium to the beam and take moments about $A$. Taking moments about $A$ eliminates $H_A$ and $V_A$. Only the vertical component of the tie force contributes, with moment arm $4\,\mathrm{m}$. The horizontal component acts along the beam axis through $A$. The result $N = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$ is positive, confirming that the rod is **in tension**. At $A$ the reactions are $H_A = 32\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the right, since the beam pushes against the wall, and $V_A = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward.

In $D$ the tie pulls the wall towards $B$, so the wall reaction on the tie is the force opposite: $H_D = -32\,\mathrm{kN}$, i.e. $32\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left, and $V_D = 24\,\mathrm{kN}$ upward. In the check around $D$, $H_A$ is applied $3\,\mathrm{m}$ under $D$ and has counterclockwise moment; $V_A$ has zero moment arm; the resultant of the load and $P$ have clockwise moment.

</div>
