## Exercise 1

The rectangular section shown has outside dimensions $24 \times 16\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a rectangular hole of dimensions $8 \times 6\,\mathrm{cm}$, positioned as indicated in the figure. Find the centroid of the section.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 24,0 24,16 0,16" fori="12,6 20,6 20,12 12,12" frecce="0,0,26.5,0,x;0,0,0,18.5,y" quote="0 0,24 0,24 cm,1.2;24 0,24 16,16 cm,1.2;12 12,20 12,8 cm,-0.9;20 6,20 12,6 cm,0.9;0 6,12 6,12 cm,-0.9;12 0,12 6,6 cm,-0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangle of dimensions $24 \times 16\,\mathrm{cm}$ with a hole of dimensions $8 \times 6\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
A_1 = 24 \cdot 16 = 384\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad (x_1, y_1) = (12, 8)\,\mathrm{cm};
\qquad
A_2 = -8 \cdot 6 = -48\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad (x_2, y_2) = (16, 9)\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
A = 384 - 48 = 336\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{384 \cdot 12 - 48 \cdot 16}{336}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{3840}{336}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{80}{7}\,\mathrm{cm} = 11.43\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{384 \cdot 8 - 48 \cdot 9}{336}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{2640}{336}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{55}{7}\,\mathrm{cm} = 7.86\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
384 \left( 12 - \frac{80}{7} \right) - 48 \left( 16 - \frac{80}{7} \right) = \frac{1536}{7} - \frac{1536}{7} = 0,
\qquad
384 \left( 8 - \frac{55}{7} \right) - 48 \left( 9 - \frac{55}{7} \right) = \frac{384}{7} - \frac{384}{7} = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The section with the hole is the solid rectangle $A_1$ minus the hole $A_2$, so the hole enters the formula of the **centroid of a composite figure** with negative area. The hole occupies $12 \leq x \leq 20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $6 \leq y \leq 12\,\mathrm{cm}$, so its centroid is $(16, 9)\,\mathrm{cm}$. The centroid of the solid rectangle is its center $(12, 8)\,\mathrm{cm}$.

The hole stands to the right and up from the center of the rectangle, so removing it moves the centroid to the left and down: in fact $x_G < 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $y_G < 8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The check calculates the first moments of area about the centroidal axes found, with the exact fractions, and finds them null. The centroid is $G = \left( \dfrac{80}{7}, \dfrac{55}{7} \right)\mathrm{cm} = (11.43;\ 7.86)\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The rectangular section shown measures $20 \times 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and has a circular hole of radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$. The center of the hole lies $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the left edge and $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the bottom edge. Find the centroid of the section.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 20,0 20,12 0,12" cerchi="14,6,3,foro" punti="14,6" tratteggi="0,6 20,6" frecce="0,0,22.5,0,x;0,0,0,14.5,y" quote="0 0,20 0,20 cm,1.1;20 0,20 12,12 cm,1.1;0 6,14 6,14 cm,-4.5;14 6,17 6,r = 3 cm,1.2" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangle of dimensions $20 \times 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ with a circular hole of radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
y_G = 6\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
A_1 = 20 \cdot 12 = 240\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad x_1 = 10\,\mathrm{cm};
\qquad
A_2 = -\pi \cdot 3^2 = -9\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2} = -28.27\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad x_2 = 14\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{240 \cdot 10 - 9\pi \cdot 14}{240 - 9\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{2400 - 126\pi}{240 - 9\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{2004.16}{211.73}\,\mathrm{cm} = 9.47\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
10 - x_G = \frac{9\pi \cdot (14 - 10)}{240 - 9\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{113.10}{211.73}\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.53\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal line at elevation $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ is an axis of symmetry of both the rectangle and the hole, so it is an axis of symmetry of the section and, by the theorem on **centroid of a symmetrical figure**, $y_G = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$. For the horizontal coordinate the formula of **centroid of a composite figure** is used, with the negative area hole $-\pi r^2$ and centroid in its center.

The check calculates the displacement of the centroid relative to the center of the rectangle in another way. It takes the center of the rectangle as the origin of the axes: the rectangle has zero first moment of area, and the hole has first moment of area $9\pi \cdot 4\,\mathrm{cm^3}$, which subtracts; dividing by area $240 - 9\pi$ results in a shift of $0.53\,\mathrm{cm}$ to the left, on the side opposite the hole. The centroid is $G = (9.47;\ 6)\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

Find the centroid of the right trapezoid shown. Its longer base measures $12\,\mathrm{cm}$, its shorter base $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, and its height $8\,\mathrm{cm}$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 12,0 6,8 0,8" tratteggi="6,0 6,8" frecce="0,0,14,0,x;0,0,0,10,y" quote="0 0,12 0,12 cm,1;0 8,6 8,6 cm,-0.9;0 0,0 8,8 cm,-1" riferimento=0 titolo="Right trapezoid split into a rectangle and a right triangle"}

$$
A_1 = 6 \cdot 8 = 48\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad (x_1, y_1) = (3, 4)\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
A_2 = \frac{6 \cdot 8}{2} = 24\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad (x_2, y_2) = \left( 6 + \frac{6}{3},\ \frac{8}{3} \right)\mathrm{cm} = \left( 8,\ \frac{8}{3} \right)\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{48 \cdot 3 + 24 \cdot 8}{72}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{336}{72}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{14}{3}\,\mathrm{cm} = 4.67\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{48 \cdot 4 + 24 \cdot \dfrac{8}{3}}{72}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{256}{72}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{32}{9}\,\mathrm{cm} = 3.56\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
A_1' = 96\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (6, 4);
\quad
A_2' = -24\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ \left( 10, \frac{16}{3} \right);
\quad
x_G = \frac{576 - 240}{72} = \frac{14}{3},
\quad
y_G = \frac{384 - 128}{72} = \frac{32}{9}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The dashed vertical segment divides the trapezoid into the rectangle $A_1$ of $6 \times 8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the right triangle $A_2$ with the legs of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The centroid of the triangle is found with the theorem on **triangle and semicircle centroids**: it is one third of the horizontal leg from the vertical leg, which stands at $x = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$, and one third of the vertical leg from the base. The formula of the **centroid of a composite figure** gives $G = \left( \dfrac{14}{3}, \dfrac{32}{9} \right)\mathrm{cm}$.

As a check the trapezoid is seen as the rectangle of $12 \times 8\,\mathrm{cm}$ minus the upper-right triangle, of vertices $(12, 0)$, $(12, 8)$ and $(6, 8)$. The centroid of this triangle is the average of the coordinates of the vertices, $\left( 10, \dfrac{16}{3} \right)$, and the triangle enters with negative area; the result coincides. The centroid is $G = (4.67;\ 3.56)\,\mathrm{cm}$, closer to the longer base, where there is more area.

</div>

## Exercise 4

The section shown consists of a rectangle of width $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and height $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ topped by a semicircle of radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. The diameter of the semicircle lies along the rectangle's upper edge. Find the centroid.

::struttura{poligoni="-6,0 6,0 6,10 6,10 5.8,11.55 5.2,13 4.24,14.24 3,15.2 1.55,15.8 0,16 -1.55,15.8 -3,15.2 -4.24,14.24 -5.2,13 -5.8,11.55 -6,10" tratteggi="-6,10 6,10" frecce="-7.5,0,8.5,0,x;0,-0.8,0,18,y" quote="-6 0,6 0,12 cm,1;6 0,6 10,10 cm,1;0 10,-6 10,r = 6 cm,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangle of dimensions $12 \times 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ topped by a semicircle of radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
x_G = 0
$$
$$
A_1 = 12 \cdot 10 = 120\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad y_1 = 5\,\mathrm{cm};
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{\pi \cdot 6^2}{2} = 18\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 56.55\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad y_2 = 10 + \frac{4 \cdot 6}{3\pi} = 10 + \frac{8}{\pi} = 12.55\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{120 \cdot 5 + 18\pi \left( 10 + \dfrac{8}{\pi} \right)}{120 + 18\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{744 + 180\pi}{120 + 18\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{1309.49}{176.55}\,\mathrm{cm} = 7.42\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad 120 \cdot (5 - 7.417) + 56.55 \cdot (12.546 - 7.417) = -290.0 + 290.0 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis $y$ is the axis of symmetry of both the rectangle and the semicircle, hence $x_G = 0$. For the **triangle and semicircle centroids** theorem the centroid of the semicircle lies on the symmetry axis at $\dfrac{4r}{3\pi} = \dfrac{8}{\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = 2.55\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the diameter; the diameter is at elevation $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, therefore $y_2 = 12.55\,\mathrm{cm}$. In the $18\pi \cdot \dfrac{8}{\pi} = 144\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ product, the $\pi$ factor is simplified, and the numerator becomes $600 + 180\pi + 144 = 744 + 180\pi$.

With the formula of the **centroid of a composite figure** you get $y_G = 7.42\,\mathrm{cm}$. The value is greater than $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, the height of the center of the rectangle, because the semicircle adds area at the top. Verification, done with three decimal places to avoid accumulating rounding errors, shows that the first moment of area about the centroidal axis is zero. The centroid is $G = (0;\ 7.42)\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>
