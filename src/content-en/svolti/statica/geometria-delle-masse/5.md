## Exercise 1

The L-section shown has legs of lengths $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, both of thickness $2\,\mathrm{cm}$. Find the centroid, the centroidal second moments of area, and the centroidal product of inertia $I_{x_G y_G}$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 6,0 6,2 2,2 2,8 0,8" frecce="0,0,7.5,0,x;0,0,0,9.5,y" quote="0 0,6 0,6 cm,0.8;0 0,0 8,8 cm,-0.8;6 0,6 2,2 cm,0.7;0 8,2 8,2 cm,-0.7" riferimento=0 titolo="L-section with leg lengths $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and thickness $2\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
A_1 = 6 \cdot 2 = 12\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (3, 1);
\qquad
A_2 = 2 \cdot 6 = 12\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (1, 5);
\qquad
x_G = \frac{36 + 12}{24} = 2\,\mathrm{cm},
\quad
y_G = \frac{12 + 60}{24} = 3\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
(x_1 - x_G,\ y_1 - y_G) = (1, -2)\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
(x_2 - x_G,\ y_2 - y_G) = (-1, 2)\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{x_G} &= \left( \frac{6 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 12 \cdot 2^2 \right) + \left( \frac{2 \cdot 6^3}{12} + 12 \cdot 2^2 \right) = 52 + 84 = 136\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_{y_G} &= \left( \frac{2 \cdot 6^3}{12} + 12 \cdot 1^2 \right) + \left( \frac{6 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 12 \cdot 1^2 \right) = 48 + 16 = 64\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_{x_G y_G} &= 12 \cdot 1 \cdot (-2) + 12 \cdot (-1) \cdot 2 = -48\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
I_{xy} = 12 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 + 12 \cdot 1 \cdot 5 = 96\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_{x_G y_G} + A\,x_G\,y_G = -48 + 24 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 = 96\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Divide the section into the full horizontal leg $A_1$ and the portion of the vertical leg above it, $A_2$, of height $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ with its center at $(1, 5)\,\mathrm{cm}$. The formula for the **centroid of a composite area** gives $G = (2, 3)\,\mathrm{cm}$. Each rectangle has sides parallel to the axes. Its local centroidal axes are therefore axes of symmetry and its product of inertia about them is zero. By the **parallel-axis theorem**, the product of inertia of each part about the centroidal axes of the full section reduces to $A_i\,(x_i - x_G)(y_i - y_G)$, with the signs of the coordinates retained.

The horizontal flange stands at the bottom right of $G$ and the vertical part at the top left, so both make a negative contribution. The check calculates the product of inertia relative to the $x$ and $y$ axes along the outer edges, where all coordinates are positive, and compares it to the transport of $I_{x_G y_G}$. The results are $G = (2, 3)\,\mathrm{cm}$, $I_{x_G} = 136\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{y_G} = 64\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = -48\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The Z-section shown has overall height $18\,\mathrm{cm}$. The web and flanges have thickness $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, and each flange has overall length $8\,\mathrm{cm}$, including the web thickness. Calculate $I_{x_G}$, $I_{y_G}$, and $I_{x_G y_G}$.

::struttura{poligoni="-7,-9 1,-9 1,7 7,7 7,9 -1,9 -1,-7 -7,-7" frecce="-9,0,9.5,0,x;0,-10.5,0,11,y" quote="-1 9,7 9,8 cm,-1;-7 -9,1 -9,8 cm,1;7 -9,7 9,18 cm,4;7 7,7 9,2 cm,0.7;-1 0,1 0,2 cm,4.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Z-section of height $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ with web and flange thickness $2\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_1 &= 2 \cdot 14 = 28\,\mathrm{cm^2}, & (x_1, y_1) &= (0, 0) \\[4pt]
A_2 &= 8 \cdot 2 = 16\,\mathrm{cm^2}, & (x_2, y_2) &= (3, 8)\,\mathrm{cm} \\[4pt]
A_3 &= 8 \cdot 2 = 16\,\mathrm{cm^2}, & (x_3, y_3) &= (-3, -8)\,\mathrm{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_{x_G} = \frac{2 \cdot 14^3}{12} + 2\left( \frac{8 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 16 \cdot 8^2 \right) = 457.33 + 2\,(5.33 + 1024) = 2516\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
I_{y_G} = \frac{14 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 2\left( \frac{2 \cdot 8^3}{12} + 16 \cdot 3^2 \right) = 9.33 + 2\,(85.33 + 144) = 468\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
I_{x_G y_G} = 0 + 16 \cdot 3 \cdot 8 + 16 \cdot (-3) \cdot (-8) = 384 + 384 = 768\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
A_1' = 2 \cdot 18 = 36\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (0, 0);
\quad
A_2' = 12\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (4, 8);
\quad
A_3' = 12\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (-4, -8);
\quad
I_{x_G y_G} = 12 \cdot 32 + 12 \cdot 32 = 768\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The Z-section has no axis of symmetry, but it is centrally symmetric about the center of its web. For every point $(x, y)$ in the section, there is a corresponding point $(-x, -y)$. With the origin at the web center, paired elements therefore make canceling contributions to $S_x$ and $S_y$. The centroid is at the origin, and axes $x$ and $y$ shown in the figure are centroidal. After excluding the overlap with the web, the flanges are rectangles measuring $8 \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$. The upper flange runs from $x = -1$ to $x = 7\,\mathrm{cm}$ with its center at $(3, 8)\,\mathrm{cm}$. The remaining web height is $18 - 2 \cdot 2 = 14\,\mathrm{cm}$.

With the **parallel-axis theorem** the flanges are transported; the web has the centroid in the origin and has no parallel-axis terms. The product of inertia is positive because the flanges are in the first and third quadrants. The check uses another decomposition: the high web $18\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the two flange parts that protrude, of $6 \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$, with the centers at $(\pm 4, \pm 8)\,\mathrm{cm}$; the product of inertia coincides. The results are $I_{x_G} = 2516\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{y_G} = 468\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = 768\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the right trapezoid in the figure, with the larger base of $9\,\mathrm{cm}$, the smaller base of $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the height of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, calculate the centroid and moments $I_{x_G}$, $I_{y_G}$, $I_{x_G y_G}$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 9,0 3,6 0,6" tratteggi="3,0 3,6" frecce="0,0,10.5,0,x;0,0,0,7.5,y" quote="0 0,9 0,9 cm,0.8;0 6,3 6,3 cm,-0.7;0 0,0 6,6 cm,-0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Right trapezoid split into a rectangle of dimensions $3 \times 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and a right triangle"}

$$
A_1 = 3 \cdot 6 = 18\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (1.5;\ 3);
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{6 \cdot 6}{2} = 18\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ \left( 3 + \frac{6}{3},\ \frac{6}{3} \right) = (5, 2)
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{18 \cdot 1.5 + 18 \cdot 5}{36}\,\mathrm{cm} = 3.25\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
y_G = \frac{18 \cdot 3 + 18 \cdot 2}{36}\,\mathrm{cm} = 2.5\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
(x_1 - x_G,\ y_1 - y_G) = (-1.75;\ 0.5)\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
(x_2 - x_G,\ y_2 - y_G) = (1.75;\ -0.5)\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{x_G} &= \left( \frac{3 \cdot 6^3}{12} + 18 \cdot 0.5^2 \right) + \left( \frac{6 \cdot 6^3}{36} + 18 \cdot 0.5^2 \right) = 58.5 + 40.5 = 99\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_{y_G} &= \left( \frac{6 \cdot 3^3}{12} + 18 \cdot 1.75^2 \right) + \left( \frac{6 \cdot 6^3}{36} + 18 \cdot 1.75^2 \right) = 68.625 + 91.125 = 159.75\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_{x_G y_G} &= 18 \cdot (-1.75) \cdot 0.5 + \left( -\frac{6^2 \cdot 6^2}{72} + 18 \cdot 1.75 \cdot (-0.5) \right) = -15.75 - 18 - 15.75 = -49.5\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
I_{xy} = \frac{3^2 \cdot 6^2}{4} + \left( -18 + 18 \cdot 5 \cdot 2 \right) = 81 + 162 = 243\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_{x_G y_G} + A\,x_G\,y_G = -49.5 + 36 \cdot 3.25 \cdot 2.5 = 243\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The trapezoid divides into the rectangle $A_1$ of $3 \times 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the right triangle $A_2$ with the legs of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$, which has the right angle in $(3, 0)$, at the bottom left. The **centroid of a composite figure** is $G = (3.25;\ 2.5)\,\mathrm{cm}$. For axial moments the triangle contributes with $\dfrac{b h^3}{36}$ about each of its centroidal axes parallel to the legs, where $h$ is the leg perpendicular to the axis; the **parallel-axis theorem** adds transport.

In the product of inertia the rectangle has contributed just zero, while the triangle has the centroidal product of inertia $-\dfrac{b^2 h^2}{72} = -18\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, because the right angle stands at the bottom left; to this is added transport. The check calculates the product of inertia relative to the $x$ and $y$ axes for the lower left vertex: for the rectangle with a vertex in the origin it equals $\dfrac{b^2 h^2}{4}$, for the triangle you transport the centroidal product of inertia. The results are $G = (3.25;\ 2.5)\,\mathrm{cm}$, $I_{x_G} = 99\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{y_G} = 159.75\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = -49.5\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>
