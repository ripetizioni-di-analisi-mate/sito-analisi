## Exercise 1

The section in the figure is bounded by the axis $x$, the line $x = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the $y = \dfrac{x^2}{6}$ parabola (lengths in $\mathrm{cm}$). Calculate the area, centroid and second moments of area $I_x$, $I_y$, $I_{x_G}$, $I_{y_G}$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 0.25,0.01 0.5,0.04 0.75,0.09 1,0.17 1.25,0.26 1.5,0.38 1.75,0.51 2,0.67 2.25,0.84 2.5,1.04 2.75,1.26 3,1.5 3.25,1.76 3.5,2.04 3.75,2.34 4,2.67 4.25,3.01 4.5,3.38 4.75,3.76 5,4.17 5.25,4.59 5.5,5.04 5.75,5.51 6,6 6,0" tratteggi="4,0 4,2.67;4.3,0 4.3,3.08" frecce="0,0,7.5,0,x;0,0,0,7,y" etichette="4.15,-0.45,dx" quote="0 0,6 0,6 cm,1;6 0,6 6,6 cm,0.8" riferimento=0 titolo="Area under the parabola $y = \dfrac{x^2}{6}$ for $0 \leq x \leq 6\,\mathrm{cm}$, showing a vertical strip of width $dx$"}

$$
A = \int_0^6 \frac{x^2}{6}\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^3}{18} \right]_0^6 = 12\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$
$$
S_y = \int_0^6 x \cdot \frac{x^2}{6}\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^4}{24} \right]_0^6 = 54\,\mathrm{cm^3},
\qquad
S_x = \int_0^6 \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{x^2}{6} \right)^2 dx = \left[ \frac{x^5}{360} \right]_0^6 = 21.6\,\mathrm{cm^3}
$$
$$
x_G = \frac{54}{12} = 4.5\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
y_G = \frac{21.6}{12} = 1.8\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
I_x = \int_0^6 \frac{1}{3}\left( \frac{x^2}{6} \right)^3 dx = \left[ \frac{x^7}{4536} \right]_0^6 = \frac{432}{7}\,\mathrm{cm^4} = 61.71\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_y = \int_0^6 x^2 \cdot \frac{x^2}{6}\,dx = \left[ \frac{x^5}{30} \right]_0^6 = 259.2\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
I_{x_G} = 61.71 - 12 \cdot 1.8^2 = 61.71 - 38.88 = 22.83\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_{y_G} = 259.2 - 12 \cdot 4.5^2 = 259.2 - 243 = 16.2\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
S_x = \int_0^6 y\,\big( 6 - \sqrt{6y} \big)\,dy = 108 - \sqrt{6} \cdot \frac{2}{5} \cdot 6^{\frac{5}{2}} = 108 - 86.4 = 21.6\,\mathrm{cm^3}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The figure is divided into vertical strips $dx$ wide; the strip at horizontal coordinate $x$ is a thin rectangle with the base on the axis $x$ and $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2}{6}$ height, of $f(x)\,dx$ area. Each strip size is written with the formulas of the rectangle, and then integrates from $0$ to $6$. For $S_y$ all points in the strip have $x$ horizontal coordinate, so the contribution is $x\,f(x)\,dx$. For $S_x$ the strip centroid stands at $\dfrac{f(x)}{2}$, so the contribution is $\dfrac{f(x)}{2} \cdot f(x)\,dx$. For $I_x$ the strip is a basic rectangle $dx$ with the base on the axis, and for **second moments of area of rectangle, triangle, circle and crown** its contribution is $\dfrac{dx\,f(x)^3}{3}$. For $I_y$ all points in the strip are $x$ away from the axis $y$, and the contribution is $x^2 f(x)\,dx$.

The centroidal moments are obtained from the **parallel-axis theorem**, subtracting $A\,y_G^2$ and $A\,x_G^2$. The check calculates $S_x$ with horizontal strips: the strip at $y$ elevation goes from the parabola, i.e. from $x = \sqrt{6y}$, to the line $x = 6$, and $\displaystyle\int_0^6 y^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dy = \dfrac{2}{5} \cdot 6^{\frac{5}{2}}$, so $\sqrt{6} \cdot \dfrac{2}{5} \cdot 6^{\frac{5}{2}} = \dfrac{2}{5} \cdot 6^3 = 86.4$. The result coincides. The results are $A = 12\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, $G = (4.5;\ 1.8)\,\mathrm{cm}$, $I_x = 61.71\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_y = 259.2\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{x_G} = 22.83\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{y_G} = 16.2\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the semicircle of radius $r = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ in the figure, with the diameter on the axis $x$, calculate for integration the position of the centroid and the second moment of area about the diameter; then derive the centroidal moments $I_{x_G}$ and $I_{y_G}$.

::struttura{poligoni="6,0 5.98,0.52 5.91,1.04 5.8,1.55 5.64,2.05 5.44,2.54 5.2,3 4.91,3.44 4.6,3.86 4.24,4.24 3.86,4.6 3.44,4.91 3,5.2 2.54,5.44 2.05,5.64 1.55,5.8 1.04,5.91 0.52,5.98 0,6 -0.52,5.98 -1.04,5.91 -1.55,5.8 -2.05,5.64 -2.54,5.44 -3,5.2 -3.44,4.91 -3.86,4.6 -4.24,4.24 -4.6,3.86 -4.91,3.44 -5.2,3 -5.44,2.54 -5.64,2.05 -5.8,1.55 -5.91,1.04 -5.98,0.52 -6,0" tratteggi="-4.47,4 4.47,4;-4.8,3.6 4.8,3.6" frecce="-7.5,0,7.5,0,x;0,0,0,7.5,y" etichette="2.3,4.3,dy" quote="0 0,6 0,r = 6 cm,0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Semicircle of radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ with a horizontal strip of width $2\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 6^2}{2} = 18\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 56.55\,\mathrm{cm^2},
\qquad
x_G = 0
$$
$$
S_x = \int_0^6 y \cdot 2\sqrt{36 - y^2}\,dy = \left[ -\frac{2}{3}\,(36 - y^2)^{\frac{3}{2}} \right]_0^6 = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 216 = 144\,\mathrm{cm^3},
\qquad
y_G = \frac{144}{18\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{8}{\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = 2.55\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_x &= \int_0^6 y^2 \cdot 2\sqrt{36 - y^2}\,dy = 2 \cdot 6^4 \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sin^2 t \cos^2 t\,dt \\[6pt]
&= 2592 \cdot \frac{\pi}{16} = 162\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4} = 508.94\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_{x_G} = 162\pi - 18\pi \left( \frac{8}{\pi} \right)^2 = 162\pi - \frac{1152}{\pi} = 508.94 - 366.69 = 142.25\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_{y_G} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\pi \cdot 6^4}{4} = 162\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4} = 508.94\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad I_x = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\pi r^4}{4} = \frac{1296\pi}{8} = 162\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
\frac{4r}{3\pi} = \frac{24}{3\pi} = \frac{8}{\pi}\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis $y$ is an axis of symmetry, hence $x_G = 0$. Horizontal strips are used: at $y$ elevation the strip goes from $x = -\sqrt{36 - y^2}$ to $x = \sqrt{36 - y^2}$, it has $2\sqrt{36 - y^2}$ width and all its points are $y$ away from the axis $x$. The integral of $S_x$ is calculated with the primitive of $y\sqrt{36 - y^2}$, which is $-\dfrac{1}{3}(36 - y^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}$. For $I_x$ you replace $y = 6\sin t$, with $dy = 6\cos t\,dt$ and $\sqrt{36 - y^2} = 6\cos t$; because $\sin^2 t\cos^2 t = \dfrac{\sin^2 2t}{4}$, the integral from $0$ to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ is $\dfrac{\pi}{16}$.

With the **parallel-axis theorem** you switch from $I_x$ at the moment about the center axis from parallel to the diameter. The axis $y$ passes through $G$ and is symmetry axis of the whole circle, which the diameter divides into two equal semicircles; therefore $I_{y_G}$ is half the moment of the circle. The check obtains $I_x$ in the same way, as half the moment of the circle about the diameter, and compares $y_G$ with the formula of the theorem on **triangle and semicircle centroids**. The results are $y_G = 2.55\,\mathrm{cm}$, $I_x = 508.94\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{x_G} = 142.25\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{y_G} = 508.94\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the quarter circle of radius $r = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ in the figure, with the extreme rays on the positive axes $x$ and $y$, calculate the centroid, the product of inertia $I_{xy}$ about the axes $x$, $y$ and the product of inertia centroidal $I_{x_G y_G}$.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 6,0 5.98,0.52 5.91,1.04 5.8,1.55 5.64,2.05 5.44,2.54 5.2,3 4.91,3.44 4.6,3.86 4.24,4.24 3.86,4.6 3.44,4.91 3,5.2 2.54,5.44 2.05,5.64 1.55,5.8 1.04,5.91 0.52,5.98 0,6" tratteggi="3,0 3,5.2;3.3,0 3.3,5.01" frecce="0,0,7.5,0,x;0,0,0,7.5,y" etichette="3.15,-0.45,dx" quote="0 0,0 6,r = 6 cm,-0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Quarter circle of radius $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ with a vertical strip of height $\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}$"}

$$
A = \frac{\pi \cdot 6^2}{4} = 9\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 28.27\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$
$$
S_y = \int_0^6 x\sqrt{36 - x^2}\,dx = \left[ -\frac{1}{3}\,(36 - x^2)^{\frac{3}{2}} \right]_0^6 = 72\,\mathrm{cm^3},
\qquad
x_G = y_G = \frac{72}{9\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{8}{\pi}\,\mathrm{cm} = 2.55\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
I_{xy} = \int_0^6 x \cdot \frac{36 - x^2}{2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\left[ 18x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_0^6 = \frac{1}{2}\,(648 - 324) = 162\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
I_{x_G y_G} = I_{xy} - A\,x_G\,y_G = 162 - 9\pi \cdot \frac{64}{\pi^2} = 162 - \frac{576}{\pi} = 162 - 183.35 = -21.35\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
I_{xy} = \int_0^6 y \left( \int_0^{\sqrt{36 - y^2}} x\,dx \right) dy = \int_0^6 y \cdot \frac{36 - y^2}{2}\,dy = 162\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
x_G = \frac{4r}{3\pi} = \frac{8}{\pi}\,\mathrm{cm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The bisector of the first quadrant is an axis of symmetry of the quarter circle, so $x_G = y_G$. The vertical strip at horizontal coordinate $x$ extends from $y = 0$ to $y = \sqrt{36 - x^2}$, and its first moment of area about the $y$-axis is $x\sqrt{36 - x^2}\,dx$. To find the product of inertia, note that all points on the strip have horizontal coordinate $x$, while their vertical coordinates give $\displaystyle\int_0^{\sqrt{36 - x^2}} y\,dy = \dfrac{36 - x^2}{2}$. The contribution of the strip is therefore $x \cdot \dfrac{36 - x^2}{2}\,dx$.

The **parallel-axis theorem** for the product of inertia subtracts $A\,x_G\,y_G$, with the coordinates of $G$ positive. Centroidal product of inertia results negative. About the centroidal axes, the parts of the quarter circle along the axis $x$ and along the axis $y$, far from the origin, stand in the fourth and second quadrants, and their negative contributions exceed the positive ones of the part near the origin, in the third quadrant, and the part near the arch, in the first. The check calculates $I_{xy}$ with the horizontal strips and compares $x_G$ with the centroid distance of the semicircle from the diameter, because the quarter circle is half of the semicircle that has the axis $y$ by diameter. The results are $G = (2.55;\ 2.55)\,\mathrm{cm}$, $I_{xy} = 162\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{x_G y_G} = -21.35\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>
