## Exercise 1

The section in the figure is a tube with an outer diameter of $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and an inner diameter of $16\,\mathrm{cm}$. Calculate the area, the second moment of area about a diameter, the polar moment about the center and the radius of gyration. Compare the second moment of area with that of a full circular section of the same area.

::struttura{cerchi="0,0,10;0,0,8,foro" frecce="-12,0,12.5,0,x_G;0,-10.7,0,12.5,y_G" quote="-10 0,10 0,20 cm,11.2;-8 0,8 0,16 cm,-1.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Tube with outside diameter $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ and inside diameter $16\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
A = \pi\,(R^2 - r^2) = \pi\,(10^2 - 8^2) = 36\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 113.10\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$
$$
I_{x_G} = \frac{\pi\,(R^4 - r^4)}{4} = \frac{\pi\,(10\,000 - 4096)}{4} = 1476\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4} = 4636.99\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
I_p = I_{x_G} + I_{y_G} = 2 \cdot 1476\pi = 2952\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4} = 9273.98\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\rho = \sqrt{\frac{I_{x_G}}{A}} = \sqrt{\frac{1476\pi}{36\pi}}\,\mathrm{cm} = \sqrt{41}\,\mathrm{cm} = 6.40\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
r_{\text{solid}} = \sqrt{36}\,\mathrm{cm} = 6\,\mathrm{cm},
\qquad
I_{\text{solid}} = \frac{\pi \cdot 6^4}{4} = 324\pi\,\mathrm{cm^4} = 1017.88\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
\frac{I_{x_G}}{I_{\text{solid}}} = \frac{1476}{324} = 4.56
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad \rho^2 = \frac{R^4 - r^4}{4\,(R^2 - r^2)} = \frac{R^2 + r^2}{4} = \frac{100 + 64}{4} = 41\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The tube is a annulus with $R = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $r = 8\,\mathrm{cm}$. The second moment of area about a diameter is read between **second moments of area of rectangle, triangle, circle and crown**; is the same for each diameter, because the crown is symmetrical with respect to each. The polar moment about the center is the sum of the moments about two orthogonal diameters, therefore double. **radius of gyration** is the root of the ratio of second moment of area to area.

A circle full of $36\pi\,\mathrm{cm^2}$ area has $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ radius, and its second moment of area is about $4.6$ times smaller: with the same amount of material the tube takes the area away from the center. The check obtains $\rho^2$ in literal form, breaking down $R^4 - r^4 = (R^2 - r^2)(R^2 + r^2)$, and finds $41\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. The results are $A = 113.10\,\mathrm{cm^2}$, $I_{x_G} = 4636.99\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_p = 9273.98\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $\rho = 6.40\,\mathrm{cm}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The section in the figure is a rectangle $20\,\mathrm{cm}$ wide and $30\,\mathrm{cm}$ high with two circular holes of $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ radius, whose centers stand on the horizontal axis of the rectangle at $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the vertical axis, from opposite parts. Calculate $I_{x_G}$ and $I_{y_G}$.

::struttura{poligoni="-10,-15 10,-15 10,15 -10,15" cerchi="-5,0,3,foro;5,0,3,foro" frecce="-12,0,11.5,0,x_G;0,-15.6,0,17.5,y_G" quote="-10 -15,10 -15,20 cm,1.2;10 -15,10 15,30 cm,4.8;0 -4.5,5 -4.5,5 cm,0.1;5 0,8 0,r = 3 cm,-3.6" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangle of dimensions $20 \times 30\,\mathrm{cm}$ with two symmetrically placed holes of radius $3\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
A = 20 \cdot 30 - 2 \cdot 9\pi = (600 - 18\pi)\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 543.45\,\mathrm{cm^2}
$$
$$
I_{x_G} = \frac{20 \cdot 30^3}{12} - 2 \cdot \frac{\pi \cdot 3^4}{4} = 45\,000 - 40.5\pi = 45\,000 - 127.23 = 44\,872.77\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{y_G} &= \frac{30 \cdot 20^3}{12} - 2\left( \frac{\pi \cdot 3^4}{4} + 9\pi \cdot 5^2 \right) \\[6pt]
&= 20\,000 - 2\,(63.617 + 706.858) = 20\,000 - 1540.95 = 18\,459.05\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{check:}\quad I_p &= \left( \frac{20 \cdot 30^3}{12} + \frac{30 \cdot 20^3}{12} \right) - 2\left( \frac{\pi \cdot 3^4}{2} + 9\pi \cdot 5^2 \right) \\[6pt]
&= 65\,000 - 531\pi = 63\,331.81\,\mathrm{cm^4} = I_{x_G} + I_{y_G}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The horizontal and vertical axes of the rectangle are symmetry axes for the couple of holes as well, so they are the centroidal axes of the section and the centroid is the center of the rectangle. Subtract the second moment of area of each hole, using the values from **second moments of area of rectangle, triangle, circle and crown** and transferring them to the required axis with **parallel-axis theorem**. About $x_G$ the hole centers stand on the axis, so there is no transport; about $y_G$ they are $5\,\mathrm{cm}$ apart, and each hole also takes $9\pi \cdot 5^2\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ off.

The holes reduce $I_{x_G}$ less than the $0.3\%$, because they stand near the axis $x_G$, and $I_{y_G}$ about the $8\%$, because they are further away from $y_G$. The check calculates the polar moment about the center independently, with the polar moment $\dfrac{\pi r^4}{2}$ of each hole transported by $5\,\mathrm{cm}$, and finds the sum $I_{x_G} + I_{y_G}$. The second moments of area are $I_{x_G} = 44\,872.77\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{y_G} = 18\,459.05\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The section in the figure is formed by a rectangle of $12 \times 8\,\mathrm{cm}$ and an isosceles triangle with the base of $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ on the upper side of the rectangle and height of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$. Determine the centroid and second moments of area centroidal.

::struttura{poligoni="-6,0 6,0 6,8 0,14 -6,8" tratteggi="-6,8 6,8" frecce="-7.5,0,8.5,0,x;0,-0.8,0,16,y" quote="-6 0,6 0,12 cm,1;6 0,6 8,8 cm,1;6 8,6 14,6 cm,1" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangle of dimensions $12 \times 8\,\mathrm{cm}$ with an isosceles triangle of height $6\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
A_1 = 96\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad y_1 = 4\,\mathrm{cm};
\qquad
A_2 = \frac{12 \cdot 6}{2} = 36\,\mathrm{cm^2},\quad y_2 = 8 + \frac{6}{3} = 10\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
y_G = \frac{96 \cdot 4 + 36 \cdot 10}{132}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{744}{132}\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{62}{11}\,\mathrm{cm} = 5.64\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{x_G} &= \left( \frac{12 \cdot 8^3}{12} + 96 \left( 4 - \frac{62}{11} \right)^2 \right) + \left( \frac{12 \cdot 6^3}{36} + 36 \left( 10 - \frac{62}{11} \right)^2 \right) \\[6pt]
&= (512 + 257.06) + (72 + 685.49) = 1526.55\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_{y_G} = \frac{8 \cdot 12^3}{12} + 2 \cdot \frac{6 \cdot 6^3}{12} = 1152 + 216 = 1368\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{check:}\quad I_{\text{base}} &= \frac{12 \cdot 8^3}{3} + \left( 72 + 36 \cdot 10^2 \right) = 2048 + 3672 = 5720\,\mathrm{cm^4} , \\[6pt]
I_{x_G} + A\,y_G^2 &= 1526.55 + 132 \cdot \left( \frac{62}{11} \right)^2 = 1526.55 + 4193.45 = 5720\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axis $y$ is an axis of symmetry, hence $x_G = 0$. The centroid of the triangle is one-third of the height from the base, which is at elevation $8\,\mathrm{cm}$; the formula of **centroid of a composite figure** gives $y_G = \dfrac{62}{11}\,\mathrm{cm}$. For $I_{x_G}$ the rectangle contributes with $\dfrac{b h^3}{12}$ and the triangle with $\dfrac{b h^3}{36}$, its moment about the center axis from parallel to the base; the **parallel-axis theorem** adds transport, with distances $\dfrac{18}{11}\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\dfrac{48}{11}\,\mathrm{cm}$.

By $I_{y_G}$ the isosceles triangle divides, along the axis $y$, into two rectangular triangles with the legs of $6\,\mathrm{cm}$; each has a leg on the axis $y$, which acts as a base, and about it the second moment of area is $\dfrac{b h^3}{12}$, with the base $b = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ and the $h = 6\,\mathrm{cm}$ height measured perpendicular to the axis. The check calculates the moment about the base of the rectangle without passing through $G$ and compares it with the transport of $I_{x_G}$. The results are $G = (0;\ 5.64)\,\mathrm{cm}$, $I_{x_G} = 1526.55\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{y_G} = 1368\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>
