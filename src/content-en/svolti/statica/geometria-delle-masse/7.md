## Exercise 1

For the rectangle in the figure, $6\,\mathrm{cm}$ wide and $12\,\mathrm{cm}$ high, calculate $I_\xi$, $I_\eta$ and $I_{\xi\eta}$ relative to the $\xi$ and $\eta$ centroidal axes rotated $30^\circ$ counterclockwise relative to the axes parallel to the sides.

::struttura{poligoni="-3,-6 3,-6 3,6 -3,6" frecce="0,0,5,0,x_G;0,0,0,7.5,y_G;0,0,4.76,2.75,ξ;0,0,-2.4,4.16,η" linee="1.6,0 1.6,0.08 1.59,0.17 1.58,0.25 1.57,0.33 1.55,0.41 1.52,0.49 1.49,0.57 1.46,0.65 1.43,0.73 1.39,0.8" etichette="2,0.45,30°" quote="-3 -6,3 -6,6 cm,0.9;3 -6,3 6,12 cm,4.5" riferimento=0 titolo="Rectangle of dimensions $6 \times 12\,\mathrm{cm}$ and centroidal axes $\xi$ and $\eta$ rotated through $30^\circ$"}

$$
I_{x_G} = \frac{6 \cdot 12^3}{12} = 864\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_{y_G} = \frac{12 \cdot 6^3}{12} = 216\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_{x_G y_G} = 0
$$
$$
C = \frac{864 + 216}{2} = 540\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
D = \frac{864 - 216}{2} = 324\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
2\alpha = 60^\circ
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_\xi &= 540 + 324 \cos 60^\circ - 0 = 540 + 162 = 702\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_\eta &= 540 - 324 \cos 60^\circ + 0 = 540 - 162 = 378\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_{\xi\eta} &= 324 \sin 60^\circ + 0 = 162\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{cm^4} = 280.59\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
I_\xi + I_\eta = 1080 = I_{x_G} + I_{y_G},
\qquad
(I_\xi - C)^2 + I_{\xi\eta}^2 = 162^2 + 3 \cdot 162^2 = 324^2 = D^2 + I_{x_G y_G}^2
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The axes parallel to the sides are axes of symmetry of the rectangle, so they are principal and the product of inertia relative to them is zero. Formulas of **second moments of area about rotated axes** with $\alpha = 30^\circ$ are simplified because the term with $I_{x_G y_G}$ is missing. The moment about $\xi$ is between $216$ and $864\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, as it must be, because the principal moments are the minimum and maximum of the centroidal moments.

Compared to the rotated axes the product of inertia is different from zero, so the $\xi$ and $\eta$ axes are not main. The check checks that the sum of the two axial moments has not changed and that the point $(I_\xi, I_{\xi\eta})$ lies on the **Mohr's circle** of center $(540, 0)$ and radius $324\,\mathrm{cm^4}$. The results are $I_\xi = 702\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_\eta = 378\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and $I_{\xi\eta} = 280.59\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

For the L-section in the figure, with the flanges of $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ thick $2\,\mathrm{cm}$, calculate the centroidal moments, construct the circle of Mohr and derive from it the principal moments and the direction of the principal axes.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 14,0 14,2 2,2 2,16 0,16" frecce="0,0,16,0,x;0,0,0,18,y" quote="0 0,14 0,14 cm,1;0 0,0 16,16 cm,-1;14 0,14 2,2 cm,0.9;0 16,2 16,2 cm,-0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="L-section with leg lengths $14\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $16\,\mathrm{cm}$ and thickness $2\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
A_1 = 28\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (7, 1);
\quad
A_2 = 28\,\mathrm{cm^2},\ (1, 9);
\qquad
G = (4, 5)\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{x_G} &= \left( \frac{14 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 28 \cdot 4^2 \right) + \left( \frac{2 \cdot 14^3}{12} + 28 \cdot 4^2 \right) = 457.33 + 905.33 = 1362.67\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_{y_G} &= \left( \frac{2 \cdot 14^3}{12} + 28 \cdot 3^2 \right) + \left( \frac{14 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 28 \cdot 3^2 \right) = 709.33 + 261.33 = 970.67\,\mathrm{cm^4} \\[6pt]
I_{x_G y_G} &= 28 \cdot 3 \cdot (-4) + 28 \cdot (-3) \cdot 4 = -672\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
X = (1362.67;\ -672),
\qquad
Y = (970.67;\ 672),
\qquad
C = \left( \frac{1362.67 + 970.67}{2};\ 0 \right) = (1166.67;\ 0)
$$
$$
R = \sqrt{(1362.67 - 1166.67)^2 + 672^2} = \sqrt{196^2 + 672^2} = 700\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
I_1 = 1166.67 + 700 = 1866.67\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_2 = 1166.67 - 700 = 466.67\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
\tan 2\alpha_0 = \frac{672}{196} = \frac{24}{7},
\qquad
2\alpha_0 = 73.74^\circ,
\qquad
\alpha_0 = 36.87^\circ
$$

$$
\text{check:}\quad
I_\xi(\alpha_0) = 1166.67 + 196 \cdot \frac{7}{25} - (-672) \cdot \frac{24}{25} = 1166.67 + 54.88 + 645.12 = 1866.67\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The centroid and the centroidal moments are found with the **Procedure for the Compound Sections**: the two parts have equal area, the centroid $G = (4, 5)\,\mathrm{cm}$ is the midpoint of their centroids, and the distances from $G$ are $(3, -4)\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $(-3, 4)\,\mathrm{cm}$. The product of inertia is negative because the parts are in the fourth and second quadrants.

For the **Mohr's circle**, the point $X$ is marked, representing the axis $x_G$, and the point $Y$, representing the axis $y_G$; the center $C$ is the midpoint of $XY$, on the horizontal axis, and the radius is the distance $CX$. The intersections of the circle with the horizontal axis are the principal moments. The point $X$ is below the horizontal axis, so to bring it to point $I_1$ you have to rotate the radius $CX$ counterclockwise of the angle $2\alpha_0$, whose components follow from the legs $672$ and $196$ in the right triangle with the hypotenuse $CX$. Since on the circle the angles are double and with the same direction, the maximum moment axis is the axis $x_G$ rotated by $36.87^\circ$ counterclockwise.

The check replaces $\alpha_0$ in the axis rotation formula, with $\cos 2\alpha_0 = \dfrac{7}{25}$ and $\sin 2\alpha_0 = \dfrac{24}{25}$, and finds $I_1$. The results are $I_1 = 1866.67\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ along the inclined axis of $36.87^\circ$ and $I_2 = 466.67\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ along the perpendicular axis.

</div>

## Exercise 3

For the asymmetric I-section in the figure (bottom flanges of $14 \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$, web of $2 \times 16\,\mathrm{cm}$, top flanges of $6 \times 2\,\mathrm{cm}$) calculate the mid-point moments $I_{x_G}$ and $I_{y_G}$. With Mohr's circle determine the maximum absolute value of the product of inertia about a pair of orthogonal centroidal axes and the centroidal axes against which the second moment of area equals $2862\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

::struttura{poligoni="-7,0 7,0 7,2 1,2 1,18 3,18 3,20 -3,20 -3,18 -1,18 -1,2 -7,2" frecce="-8.5,0,8.5,0,x;0,-0.8,0,21.3,y" quote="-7 0,7 0,14 cm,1.1;-3 20,3 20,6 cm,-2.2;7 0,7 20,20 cm,1.4;1 2,1 18,16 cm,2.6;-7 0,-7 2,2 cm,-0.9;-3 18,-3 20,2 cm,-0.9" riferimento=0 titolo="Asymmetric I-section of height $20\,\mathrm{cm}$"}

$$
y_G = \frac{28 \cdot 1 + 32 \cdot 10 + 12 \cdot 19}{72}\,\mathrm{cm} = 8\,\mathrm{cm}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{x_G} &= \left( \frac{14 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 28 \cdot 7^2 \right) + \left( \frac{2 \cdot 16^3}{12} + 32 \cdot 2^2 \right) + \left( \frac{6 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 12 \cdot 11^2 \right) \\[6pt]
&= 1381.33 + 810.67 + 1456 = 3648\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
I_{y_G} = \frac{2 \cdot 14^3}{12} + \frac{16 \cdot 2^3}{12} + \frac{2 \cdot 6^3}{12} = 457.33 + 10.67 + 36 = 504\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
I_{x_G y_G} = 0
$$
$$
C = \frac{3648 + 504}{2} = 2076\,\mathrm{cm^4},
\qquad
R = \frac{3648 - 504}{2} = 1572\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$
$$
|I_{\xi\eta}|_{\max} = R = 1572\,\mathrm{cm^4}
\quad \text{for} \quad \alpha = \pm 45^\circ
$$
$$
2076 + 1572 \cos 2\alpha = 2862
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\cos 2\alpha = \frac{786}{1572} = \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha = \pm 30^\circ
$$
$$
\text{check:}\quad
I_{x_G} = I_{\text{base}} - A\,y_G^2 = \left( \frac{14 \cdot 2^3}{3} + \frac{2 \cdot 16^3}{12} + 32 \cdot 10^2 + \frac{6 \cdot 2^3}{12} + 12 \cdot 19^2 \right) - 72 \cdot 8^2 = 8256 - 4608 = 3648\,\mathrm{cm^4}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The centroid lies on the vertical symmetry axis, at $8\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the bottom edge. The distances of the centroids of the parts from the axis $x_G$ are $7$, $2$ and $11\,\mathrm{cm}$, and the **parallel-axis theorem** gives $I_{x_G} = 3648\,\mathrm{cm^4}$; compared to $y_G$ all parts have centroid on the axis. Since $y_G$ is an axis of symmetry, the $x_G$ and $y_G$ axes are main, and on the **Mohr's circle** points $X$ and $Y$ stand on the horizontal axis: the center is $C = 2076\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ and the radius is the semi-difference of the moments, $1572\,\mathrm{cm^4}$.

The product of inertia is the order of the points of the circle, so its maximum absolute value is the radius, reached at the highest and lowest points, to $90^\circ$ from point $X$ on the circle; to the axes corresponds half of the angle, i.e. the inclined axes of $\pm 45^\circ$. The axis $2862\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ exceeds the center of $786\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, half the radius: the points of the circle with this axis form with $X$ angles in the center of $\pm 60^\circ$, and the axes correspond rotations of $\pm 30^\circ$. The check recalculates $I_{x_G}$ carrying from the bottom edge. The results are $I_{x_G} = 3648\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $I_{y_G} = 504\,\mathrm{cm^4}$, $|I_{\xi\eta}|_{\max} = 1572\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ for $\pm 45^\circ$ axes, and $I_\xi = 2862\,\mathrm{cm^4}$ for $\pm 30^\circ$ inclined axes compared to $x_G$.

</div>
