## Exercise 1

The $AB$ inclined beam shown has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$ on a horizontal surface; the horizontal projection is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ and the $3\,\mathrm{m}$ level difference, so the length is $5\,\mathrm{m}$. It carries a uniform vertical load $q = 2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ per unit length of the horizontal projection, such as snow load on a ground. Plot diagrams of $N$, $T$ and $M$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,3" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,q" quote="A,4 0,4 m,0.7;4 0,B,3 m,0.7" tratteggi="0.09,-0.12 4.09,2.88" titolo="Inclined beam with vertical load $q$ per unit of horizontal projection"}

$$
\cos\alpha = \frac{4}{5} = 0.8 , \qquad \sin\alpha = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6 , \qquad x = s\cos\alpha = 0.8\,s
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
H_A = 0 \\[4pt]
V_A + V_B - 8 = 0 \\[4pt]
4\,V_B - 8 \cdot 2 = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
V_A = V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
R^{\mathrm{left}}_x = 0 , \qquad R^{\mathrm{left}}_y = 4 - 2x = 4 - 1.6\,s
$$
$$
N(s) = -R^{\mathrm{left}}_y \sin\alpha = -2.4 + 0.96\,s ,
\qquad
T(s) = R^{\mathrm{left}}_y \cos\alpha = 3.2 - 1.28\,s
$$
$$
M = 4x - x^2 = 3.2\,s - 0.64\,s^2 , \qquad M_{\max} = M(2.5) = 4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
N(0) = -2.4\,\mathrm{kN} , \quad N(5) = 2.4\,\mathrm{kN} , \qquad T(0) = 3.2\,\mathrm{kN} , \quad T(5) = -3.2\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The load is referred to the horizontal projection, so its resultant value is $q \cdot 4 = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ and it is applied on the vertical line through the midpoint, $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ horizontally. In equilibrium equations horizontal distances count, because the forces are vertical; You get $V_A = V_B = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $H_A = 0$.

The member runs from $A$ to $B$, with $s$ measured along the axis and the reference fibers under the member. The part preceding the section contains $V_A$ and the load on the $x$ projection segment, so its resultant is vertical and equals $4 - 2x$. For **Inclined members** observation the component along the axis gives the axial force and the orthogonal one gives the shear. Axial force varies linearly from $-2.4\,\mathrm{kN}$, compression, in $A$ to $2.4\,\mathrm{kN}$, tension, in $B$, because roller support at $B$ pushes up a member that rises to the right, and the component along the axis of its reaction tends the top. The moment depends only on horizontal distances and is the same as a horizontal beam of span $4\,\mathrm{m}$, so the maximum is $\dfrac{q \cdot 4^2}{8} = 4\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ in the middle of the member, where the shear vanishes.

The check uses moment equilibrium about $B$, with the horizontal distance of $A$ from $B$ as the arm of $V_A$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The three-hinged arch shown is formed by the straight $AC$ and $CB$ members, connected by the hinge $C$ and hinged to the ground in $A$ and $B$; $A$ and $B$ are $6\,\mathrm{m}$ apart and the hinge $C$ is $4\,\mathrm{m}$ higher, on the vertical line through the midpoint. The $F = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward force acts in $D$, midpoint of $AC$. Calculate reactions and plot diagrams of $N$, $T$ and $M$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,1.5,2;C,3,4;B,6,0" aste="A-C;C-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,cerniera" cerniere="C" forze="D,0,-1.4,F" tratteggi="0.25,0 3,3.65 5.75,0" quote="A,B,6 m,0.8;A,0 4,4 m,-0.7" titolo="Three-hinged arch with straight members and force $F$ at the midpoint of $AC$"}

$$
\begin{cases}
H_A + H_B = 0 \\[4pt]
V_A + V_B - F = 0 \\[4pt]
6\,V_B - 1.5\,F = 0 \\[4pt]
3\,V_B + 4\,H_B = 0
\end{cases}
\qquad
V_B = 2\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
H_B = -1.5\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
V_A = 6\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
H_A = 1.5\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
\text{member } AC: \quad \cos\alpha = 0.6 , \quad \sin\alpha = 0.8 ; \qquad \text{member } CB: \quad \hat{t} = (0.6 ;\, -0.8)
$$
$$
\text{segment } AD,\ 0 < s < 2.5: \qquad N = -(1.5 \cdot 0.6 + 6 \cdot 0.8) = -5.7\,\mathrm{kN} , \qquad T = -1.5 \cdot 0.8 + 6 \cdot 0.6 = 2.4\,\mathrm{kN} , \qquad M(s) = 2.4\,s
$$
$$
\text{segment } DC,\ 2.5 < s < 5: \qquad N = -(1.5 \cdot 0.6 - 2 \cdot 0.8) = 0.7\,\mathrm{kN} , \qquad T = -1.2 - 1.2 = -2.4\,\mathrm{kN} , \qquad M(s) = 6 - 2.4\,(s - 2.5)
$$
$$
\text{member } CB: \qquad N = -\sqrt{1.5^2 + 2^2} = -2.5\,\mathrm{kN} , \qquad T = 0 , \qquad M = 0
$$
$$
M_D = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

$$
\text{check:} \quad \sum M_C^{\,AC} = 4\,H_A - 3\,V_A + 1.5\,F = 6 - 18 + 12 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The arch has two bodies and $2 + 2 + 2 = 6$ scalar constraints, so it is statically determinate if the three hinges are not aligned, as in this case. There are four unknowns: at the three equilibrium equations of the entire arch (horizontal translation, vertical translation, rotation around $A$) the auxiliary equation of moment equilibrium of member $CB$ about $C$ is added. The member $CB$ is an **member unloaded between two hinges**, so the reaction at $B$ is directed along $CB$: in fact, $\dfrac{H_B}{V_B} = -\dfrac{3}{4}$, the same ratio between the components of the vector $\overrightarrow{BC} = (-3, 4)$. The thrust equals $1.5\,\mathrm{kN}$, to the right in $A$ and to the left in $B$.

The members run from $A$ to $C$ and from $C$ to $B$, with the reference fibers within the arch. In the member $AC$ the part preceding the section contains $H_A$ and $V_A$, and after $D$ also $F$; by the observation on **Inclined members**, project the resultants onto the unit vector along the member and the unit vector perpendicular to it. The section $AD$ is in compression and the section $DC$ is slightly in tension. The moment is $-M^{\mathrm{left}}_S$: the clockwise moment of $V_A$ with arm $0.6\,s$ minus the counterclockwise moment of $H_A$ with arm $0.8\,s$ gives $3.6\,s - 1.2\,s = 2.4\,s$. The maximum $M_D = 6\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ is under the force, with the inner fibers in tension out, and the moment vanishes in the **hinge $C$**. The member $CB$ has $T = 0$ and $M = 0$ and is in compression by the force $\sqrt{H_B^2 + V_B^2} = 2.5\,\mathrm{kN}$.

The check uses the zero moment condition in $C$ for the member $AC$, which was not used: about $C$, $H_A$ has a moment arm of $4\,\mathrm{m}$, $V_A$ $3\,\mathrm{m}$ moment arm and $F$ $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ moment arm.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The three-hinged frame shown is pinned to the ground at its bases $A$ and $B$. Columns $AD$ and $BE$ have height $3\,\mathrm{m}$, and beam $DE$ has length $6\,\mathrm{m}$ and an internal hinge $C$ at midspan. The uniform load $q = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts only on segment $DC$, one half of the beam. Calculate the reactions and draw the diagrams for $N$, $T$, and $M$, taking the inner fibers as the reference fibers.

H_A + H_B = 0 \\[4pt]
V_A + V_B - 12 = 0 \\[4pt]
6\,V_B - 12 \cdot 1.5 = 0 \\[4pt]
3\,V_B + 3\,H_B = 0

\qquad

$$

$$
\text{check:} \quad \sum M_C^{\,ADC} = 3\,H_A - 3\,V_A + 12 \cdot 1.5 = 9 - 27 + 18 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The portal frame has two bodies and six scalar constraints. The rotation of the entire portal frame around $A$ gives $V_B$, because $H_B$ passes through $A$ at the same elevation. The auxiliary equation is the rotation around $C$ of the right side $CEB$, which is unloaded: about $C$, $V_B$ has $3\,\mathrm{m}$ arm and $H_B$ has $3\,\mathrm{m}$ arm, so $H_B = -V_B$. The thrust equals $3\,\mathrm{kN}$, to the right in $A$ and to the left in $B$. The $CEB$ part is loaded only in the $C$ and $B$ hinges, so the reaction at $B$ is directed along the $BC$ conjunct, inclined by $45^\circ$.

You go through the portal frame from $A$ to $B$, with the fibers dashed inside. In column $AD$ the part preceding the section contains $H_A$ and $V_A$; Therefore, for the observation on the inclined members with $\alpha = 90^\circ$, $N = -V_A$ and $T = -H_A$, and the moment is $-3s$, with the **outer fibers in tension**. In crossbeam the thrust gives the $N = -3\,\mathrm{kN}$ axial force; the moment starts from $-9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ in $D$, as the theorem on **Hard joint** requires, has the maximum $\dfrac{9}{8}\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ where the shear vanishes and vanishes in the hinge $C$. At section $CE$, unloaded, the moment is linear and returns to $-9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ in $E$; moving down column $EB$ from its top, the moment varies from $-9\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ to zero. The moment diagram on unloaded segment $CEB$ puts the outer fibers in tension, as in a portal frame under thrust alone.

The check uses the zero moment condition in $C$ for the left side $ADC$: about $C$, $H_A$ has a moment arm of $3\,\mathrm{m}$, $V_A$ $3\,\mathrm{m}$ moment arm and the resultant $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ moment arm load.

</div>
