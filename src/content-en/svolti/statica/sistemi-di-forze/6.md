## Exercise 1

On the beam $AB$ shown, of length $7\,\mathrm{m}$, three vertical loads down act: $P_1 = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$ a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, $P_2 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ a distance $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and $P_3 = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$ a distance $6\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$. Determine the resultant and its line of action.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,7,0" aste="A-B" forze="1 0,0,-0.8,P_1;4 0,0,-1.2,P_2;6 0,0,-2,P_3" quote="A,1 0,1 m,0.8;1 0,4 0,3 m,0.8;4 0,6 0,2 m,0.8;6 0,B,1 m,0.8" titolo="Three vertical loads on beam $AB$"}

$$
R = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 = 8 + 12 + 20 = 40\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
x_C = \frac{P_1 x_1 + P_2 x_2 + P_3 x_3}{P_1 + P_2 + P_3} = \frac{8 \cdot 1 + 12 \cdot 4 + 20 \cdot 6}{40} = \frac{176}{40} = 4.4\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad M_B = (1 - 7) \cdot (-8) + (4 - 7) \cdot (-12) + (6 - 7) \cdot (-20) = 48 + 36 + 20 = 104\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
(4.4 - 7) \cdot (-40) = 104\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The loads are parallel and all point in the same direction. Taking unit vector $\hat{u}$ downward therefore gives positive signs for the load magnitudes $P_i$. For **center of parallel forces** the resultant is the sum of the intensities and passes through the mean of the horizontal coordinates weighed with the intensities. The center lies to the right of the simple mean of the three axes, $\dfrac{1 + 4 + 6}{3} = 3.67\,\mathrm{m}$, because the largest load, $P_3$, is the furthest from $A$.

The check compares the moment against $B$ of the three loads with that of the resultant applied at $C$. All loads stand to the left of $B$ and have counterclockwise moment, and the two sums coincide. Loads are equivalent to **$40\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward, $4.4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$**.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Beam $AB$ has length $7\,\mathrm{m}$ and carries a downward force $P_1 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $A$, an upward force $P_2 = 4\,\mathrm{kN}$ at a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, and a downward force $P_3 = 6\,\mathrm{kN}$ at $B$. Determine the resultant and the center of parallel forces.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,7,0" aste="A-B" forze="A,0,-1.5,P_1;3 0,0,0.9,P_2;B,0,-1.2,P_3" quote="A,3 0,3 m,1.3;3 0,B,4 m,1.3" titolo="Three vertical forces acting in opposite directions"}

$$
F_1 = 10 ,
\qquad
F_2 = -4 ,
\qquad
F_3 = 6
\qquad (\mathrm{kN},\ \text{positive downward})
$$
$$
R = 10 - 4 + 6 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
x_C = \frac{10 \cdot 0 + (-4) \cdot 3 + 6 \cdot 7}{12} = \frac{-12 + 42}{12} = \frac{30}{12} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad M_B = (0 - 7) \cdot (-10) + (3 - 7) \cdot 4 + 0 = 70 - 16 = 54\,\mathrm{kN\,m} ,
\qquad
(2.5 - 7) \cdot (-12) = 54\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

In **center of parallel forces** the magnitudes $F_i$ are signed relative to the common unit vector. Here the unit vector points downward, so the upward force $P_2$ enters with $F_2 = -4\,\mathrm{kN}$. The sum is positive, so the resultant is directed downward. The $P_1$ and $P_3$ loads alone have the center in $x = \dfrac{6 \cdot 7}{16} = 2.625\,\mathrm{m}$. The force $P_2$ enters with negative intensity, so it moves the center from the opposite side to its $x = 3\,\mathrm{m}$ line of action, i.e. towards $A$.

In verifying moments about $B$ are calculated with the formula in components, with $F_y = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$ for $P_1$ and $F_y = +4\,\mathrm{kN}$ for $P_2$; force at $B$ has zero moment. The system is equivalent to **$12\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward, $2.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$**.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The overhead crane beam $AB$ has length $6\,\mathrm{m}$. Its trolley runs on two wheels carrying vertical loads $P_1 = 30\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $P_2 = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$. The more heavily loaded wheel is a distance $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, as shown. At what distance $a$ from $A$ must the other wheel be placed so that the resultant passes through beam midpoint $E$?

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;E,3,0" aste="A-B" forze="1 0,0,-1.5,P_1;4.5 0,0,-1,P_2" quote="A,1 0,1 m,0.8;A,4.5 0,a,1.5;A,E,3 m,-0.6" titolo="Two trolley wheel loads, with the position $a$ of the second wheel unknown"}

$$
x_C = \frac{P_1 \cdot 1 + P_2\,a}{P_1 + P_2} = 3\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
30 \cdot 1 + 20\,a = 50 \cdot 3
$$
$$
20\,a = 150 - 30 = 120
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a = 6\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad M_E = (1 - 3) \cdot (-30) + (6 - 3) \cdot (-20) = 60 - 60 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The resultant of the two loads is $50\,\mathrm{kN}$ down and passes through the **center of parallel forces**; by requiring the center's horizontal coordinate to be $3\,\mathrm{m}$ a first-degree equation is obtained in $a$. The less charged wheel must stay further away from the center, because the arms are inversely proportional to the loads: $30 \cdot 2 = 20 \cdot 3$.

The resultant passes through $E$, so the moment of the two loads about $E$ is zero, and verification checks it. The load $P_1$ stands to the left of $E$ and has counterclockwise moment, $P_2$ stands to the right and has clockwise moment, and the two moments cancel out. The second wheel must be at **$a = 6\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$**, i.e. on the $B$ support.

</div>

## Exercise 4

On the three vertices of the triangular plate in the figure, $P_1 = (0, 0)$, $P_2 = (2, 3)$ and $P_3 = (6, 1)$ in meters, three forces act parallel to the vector $(3, -4)$, with the same direction and intensity $F_1 = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$, $F_2 = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $F_3 = 5\,\mathrm{kN}$. Determine the center of parallel forces, the resultant and the resultant moment about $P_1$. Then verify that, if the three forces were vertical downwards with the same intensity, the line of action of the resultant would pass through the same center.

::struttura{poligoni="0,0 2,3 6,1" nodi="P_1,0,0;P_2,2,3;P_3,6,1" forze="0.36 -0.48,0.36,-0.48,F_1;2.72 2.04,0.72,-0.96,F_2;6.36 0.52,0.36,-0.48,F_3" titolo="Triangular plate under three forces parallel to vector $(3, -4)$"}

$$
\hat{u} = \Big( \frac{3}{5},\, -\frac{4}{5} \Big) ,
\qquad
F_1 + F_2 + F_3 = 20\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
x_C = \frac{5 \cdot 0 + 10 \cdot 2 + 5 \cdot 6}{20} = \frac{50}{20} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
y_C = \frac{5 \cdot 0 + 10 \cdot 3 + 5 \cdot 1}{20} = \frac{35}{20} = 1.75\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\vec{R} = 20\,\hat{u} = (12, -16)\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
M_{P_1} = x_C\,R_y - y_C\,R_x = 2.5 \cdot (-16) - 1.75 \cdot 12 = -40 - 21 = -61\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad M_{P_1} = \underbrace{0}_{F_1} + \underbrace{2 \cdot (-8) - 3 \cdot 6}_{F_2 = (6,\,-8)} + \underbrace{6 \cdot (-4) - 1 \cdot 3}_{F_3 = (3,\,-4)} = -34 - 27 = -61\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{vertical forces:} \quad M_{P_1} = 2 \cdot (-10) + 6 \cdot (-5) = -50\,\mathrm{kN\,m} ,
\qquad
x = \frac{-50}{-20} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m} = x_C
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three forces have positive magnitudes and share the same unit vector $\hat{u}$, obtained by dividing $(3, -4)$ by its magnitude $5$. For **center of parallel forces** the coordinates of $C$ are the average of the coordinates of the application points weighed with the intensities, and the resultant is $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ in the direction of $\hat{u}$. The moment about $P_1$, which is the origin, is that of the resultant applied at $C$.

The first check adds the moments of all three forces using their individual components. Force $F_1$ acts at the moment center. The second part verifies that $C$ is independent of the force direction. With vertical forces, the resultant is $20\,\mathrm{kN}$ downward. By **reduction of a planar force system**, its line of action is the vertical line at horizontal coordinate $\dfrac{M_{P_1}}{R_y} = 2.5\,\mathrm{m}$, which passes through $C$. The center is $C = (2.5;\, 1.75)\,\mathrm{m}$, the resultant is **$\vec{R} = (12, -16)\,\mathrm{kN}$ with magnitude $20\,\mathrm{kN}$**, and the moment about $P_1$ is **$-61\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, clockwise**.

</div>
