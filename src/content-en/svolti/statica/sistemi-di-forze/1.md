## Exercise 1

The vertical force $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ shown acts downward at $P = (4, 3)$, whose coordinates are in meters. Calculate its moments about $O = (0, 0)$, $A = (6, 0)$, and $B = (4, 0)$.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;A,6,0;B,4,0;P,4,3" forze="P,0,-1.5,F" tratteggi="4,4.8 4,-0.4" quote="O,B,4 m,1;B,A,2 m,1;B,P,3 m,-0.6" punti="0,0;6,0;4,0;4,3" titolo="Vertical force $F$ at $P$ and moment reference points $O$, $A$, and $B$"}

$$
F_x = 0 ,
\qquad
F_y = -10\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
M_O = (x_P - x_O)\,F_y - (y_P - y_O)\,F_x = 4 \cdot (-10) - 3 \cdot 0 = -40\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
M_A = (4 - 6) \cdot (-10) - (3 - 0) \cdot 0 = 20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
M_B = (4 - 4) \cdot (-10) - (3 - 0) \cdot 0 = 0
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad M_A = M_O + (x_O - x_A)\,F_y - (y_O - y_A)\,F_x = -40 + (0 - 6) \cdot (-10) - 0 = 20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The downward force has components $F_x = 0$ and $F_y = -10\,\mathrm{kN}$. Use the component formula for the **moment of a force**. Its line of action, the vertical line $x = 4$, lies $4\,\mathrm{m}$ from $O$. A downward force to the right of a reference point produces clockwise rotation, so $M_O = -40\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The same line is $2\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left of $A$ and therefore produces a counterclockwise moment, $M_A = 20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. Point $B$ lies on the line of action, so the **moment–arm relation** gives zero moment even though $B$ is a distance $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from the point of application $P$.

As a check, use the change-of-reference-point formula to obtain $M_A$ from $M_O$. The resultant is the applied force itself, and the result agrees with the direct calculation. The moments are **$M_O = -40\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ clockwise**, **$M_A = 20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ counterclockwise**, and $M_B = 0$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The force $F = 13\,\mathrm{kN}$ shown acts at $P = (3, 4)$, with coordinates in meters, and points upward and to the left, parallel to the vector $(-5, 12)$. Find its moment about the origin $O$ and its moment arm. Check by sliding the force along its line of action to the $x$ axis.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,3,4" forze="2.42 5.38,-0.58,1.38,F" tratteggi="5.05,-0.9 2.15,6.05;3,4 3,0" quote="O,3 0,3 m,1" punti="0,0;3,4;4.67,0,P_1" titolo="Force $F$ at $P$, its line of action, and its intersection $P_1$ with the $x$ axis"}

$$
F_x = 13 \cdot \frac{-5}{13} = -5\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
F_y = 13 \cdot \frac{12}{13} = 12\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
M_O = x_P\,F_y - y_P\,F_x = 3 \cdot 12 - 4 \cdot (-5) = 36 + 20 = 56\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
b = \frac{|M_O|}{F} = \frac{56}{13}\,\mathrm{m} = 4.31\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
P_1 = P + t\,(-5, 12) \;\text{ with }\; 4 + 12t = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = -\frac{1}{3} ,
\qquad
P_1 = \Big( \frac{14}{3},\, 0 \Big)
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad M_O = x_{P_1}\,F_y - 0 \cdot F_x = \frac{14}{3} \cdot 12 = 56\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Vector $(-5, 12)$ has magnitude $\sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$, so the unit vector along the force is $\Big( \dfrac{-5}{13}, \dfrac{12}{13} \Big)$. Multiplying it by $13\,\mathrm{kN}$ gives the components. by **Varignon's theorem** the moment of force is the sum of the moments of the two components applied at $P$. The vertical component $F_y = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$, upwards to $3\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right of $O$, has counterclockwise moment $36\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$; the horizontal component, of $5\,\mathrm{kN}$ to the left at $4\,\mathrm{m}$ above $O$, also has counterclockwise moment, $20\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The moment arm is the ratio of the moment magnitude to the intensity of the force.

As a check the force travels along its line of action, and the moment about $O$ remains the same for the moment and moment arm theorem. At point $P_1$ of the line of action that lies on the axis $x$ the horizontal component has zero moment arm, so the moment is given by the vertical component alone and still holds $56\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The moment is **$M_O = 56\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, counterclockwise**, with a moment arm of $b = 4.31\,\mathrm{m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

A force of magnitude $F = 10\,\mathrm{kN}$ acts at $P = (4, 3)$, with coordinates in meters, and makes an angle $\alpha$ measured counterclockwise from the $x$ axis, as shown. Express its moment about the origin $O$ in terms of $\alpha$. Find the values of $\alpha$ between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ for which the moment is zero and for which it is greatest.

::struttura{nodi="O,0,0;P,4,3" forze="4.75 4.3,0.75,1.3,F" tratteggi="4,3 5.8,3;0,0 4,3" etichette="4.95,3.3,α" punti="0,0;4,3" titolo="Force $F$ at $P$ makes angle $\alpha$ with the $x$ axis"}

$$
F_x = 10\cos\alpha ,
\qquad
F_y = 10\sin\alpha
$$
$$
M_O(\alpha) = 4 \cdot 10\sin\alpha - 3 \cdot 10\cos\alpha = 40\sin\alpha - 30\cos\alpha
$$
$$
M_O(\alpha) = 50\Big( \frac{4}{5}\sin\alpha - \frac{3}{5}\cos\alpha \Big) = 50\sin(\alpha - \beta) ,
\qquad
\cos\beta = \frac{4}{5} ,\;\; \sin\beta = \frac{3}{5} ,\;\; \beta = 36.87^\circ
$$
$$
M_O = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha = \beta = 36.87^\circ \;\text{ or }\; \alpha = \beta + 180^\circ = 216.87^\circ
$$
$$
M_O = 50\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha = \beta + 90^\circ = 126.87^\circ
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad \alpha = 126.87^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; F_x = -6\,\mathrm{kN} ,\;\; F_y = 8\,\mathrm{kN} ,\qquad M_O = 4 \cdot 8 - 3 \cdot (-6) = 50\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The force components depend on the angle, and the **moment of a force** in components gives a combination of $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$. The coefficients $40$ and $30$ satisfy $\sqrt{40^2 + 30^2} = 50$, so by collecting $50$ the coefficients of $\sin\alpha$ and $\cos\alpha$ become the cosine and sine of an angle $\beta$, and the formula of sine subtraction brings the moment into the form $50\sin(\alpha - \beta)$. The angle $\beta$ is what the segment $OP$ forms with the axis $x$, because $\tan\beta = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

The moment is zero when $\alpha - \beta$ is $0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, i.e. when the force is parallel to $OP$; then the line of action passes through $O$, as the theorem on **moment and moment arm** requires. The moment is maximum, $50\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, when $\alpha - \beta = 90^\circ$; in this case the force is perpendicular to $OP$, and the moment arm is the distance $|OP| = 5\,\mathrm{m}$, the largest possible value. The minimum, $-50\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, is for $\alpha = 306.87^\circ$, with the force perpendicular to $OP$ and opposite. The check replaces the components for $\alpha = 126.87^\circ$, i.e. $(\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha) = (-0.6;\, 0.8)$, and finds $50\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The moment is **null for $\alpha = 36.87^\circ$ and $\alpha = 216.87^\circ$**, and **maximum for $\alpha = 126.87^\circ$**.

</div>
