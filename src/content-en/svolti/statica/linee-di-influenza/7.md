## Exercise 1

On the simply supported beam shown, of span $9\,\mathrm{m}$, a uniform $q = 12\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ load passes along $c = 3\,\mathrm{m}$. Find the position of the load that makes the maximum bending moment at section $S$ to $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge $A$, and the maximum value.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;S,3,0;B,9,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="4.5 0,7.5 0,0.6,0.6,q" quote="A,S,3 m,0.9;S,B,6 m,0.9;4.5 0,7.5 0,c = 3 m,-1.3" titolo="Simply supported beam with moving load $q = 12\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$ in an arbitrary position"}

$$
\eta_M(\xi) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2}{3}\,\xi & \text{if } 0 \le \xi \le 3 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{9 - \xi}{3} & \text{if } 3 \le \xi \le 9
\end{cases}
\qquad
\eta_M(3) = 2\,\mathrm{m}
$$

$$
\eta_M(z) = \eta_M(z + 3)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{2}{3}\,z = \frac{9 - z - 3}{3}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
2z = 6 - z
\;\Longrightarrow\;
z = 2\,\mathrm{m}
$$

$$
\mathcal{A} = \frac{\frac{4}{3} + 2}{2} \cdot 1 + \frac{2 + \frac{4}{3}}{2} \cdot 2 = \frac{5}{3} + \frac{10}{3} = 5\,\mathrm{m^2} ,
\qquad
M_{S,\max} = q\,\mathcal{A} = 12 \cdot 5 = 60\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$

::grafico{f="min(2*x/3,(9-x)/3)+0*sqrt(x*(9-x))" xmin=-0.5 xmax=9.8 ymin=-0.3 ymax=2.6 area="min(2*x/3,(9-x)/3);0;2;5" segmenti="2,0,2,1.3333;5,0,5,1.3333;3,0,3,2" punti="3,2,pieno" nomi="ξ,η" titolo="Influence line for the moment at $S$ and a load extending from $2\,\mathrm{m}$ to $5\,\mathrm{m}$, with end ordinates $\dfrac{4}{3}$"}

$$
\text{check (load from } 2 \text{ to } 5\,\mathrm{m}\text{):} \quad
V_A = \frac{36 \cdot 5.5}{9} = 22\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
M_S - V_A \cdot 3 + 12 \cdot 1 \cdot 0.5 = 60 - 66 + 6 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moment influence lines in $S$ is a triangle with the $\dfrac{3 \cdot 6}{9} = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ vertex. With the load on the segment $[z, z + 3]$ the moment equals $q$ for the area of the line on that segment. For the **allocated load of length** theorem the area is maximum when the ordinate at the ends of the load are equal; the length $c = 3\,\mathrm{m}$ is less than the base of the triangle, so this position exists and is unique, with the load straddling the vertex.

The left end is on the $\dfrac{2}{3}\,\xi$ branch, the right end is on the $\dfrac{9 - \xi}{3}$ branch; Equalizing them is $z = 2\,\mathrm{m}$, so the load occupies the section from $2\,\mathrm{m}$ to $5\,\mathrm{m}$, $1\,\mathrm{m}$ to the left of $S$ and $2\,\mathrm{m}$ to the right. The area is divided into the two trapezoids separated by vertical by $S$. The maximum moment is $M_{S,\max} = 60\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

Check recalculates the moment with balance. The resultant of the load, $36\,\mathrm{kN}$, is $3.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ and then $5.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $B$; moment equilibrium about $B$ gives $V_A$. On the segment $AS$ act $V_A$ and the load between $2\,\mathrm{m}$ and $3\,\mathrm{m}$, resulting in $12\,\mathrm{kN}$ $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $S$. With the load centered on $S$, from $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$ to $4.5\,\mathrm{m}$, a smaller value would be obtained, $58.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

On the simply supported beam shown, of span $12\,\mathrm{m}$, a load train of three forces passes down, $P_1 = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$, $P_2 = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $P_3 = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$, in this order from left to right, at a distance of $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from each other. Find the maximum value of the bending moment at section $S$ to $3\,\mathrm{m}$ from the hinge $A$ and the position of the load train that produces it.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;S,3,0;B,12,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" forze="5 0,0,-2,P_1;7 0,0,-3,P_2;9 0,0,-3,P_3" quote="A,S,3 m,0.9;S,B,9 m,0.9;5 0,7 0,2 m,-3.9;7 0,9 0,2 m,-3.9" titolo="Train of three forces $P_1 = 20\,\mathrm{kN}$, $P_2 = P_3 = 40\,\mathrm{kN}$ at spacing $2\,\mathrm{m}$, shown in an arbitrary position"}

$$
\eta_M(\xi) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{3}{4}\,\xi & \text{if } 0 \le \xi \le 3 \\[8pt]
\dfrac{12 - \xi}{4} & \text{if } 3 \le \xi \le 12
\end{cases}
\qquad
\eta_M(3) = \frac{9}{4}\,\mathrm{m}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_1 \text{ in } S\!: \quad & 20 \cdot \frac{9}{4} + 40 \cdot \eta_M(5) + 40 \cdot \eta_M(7) = 45 + 40 \cdot \frac{7}{4} + 40 \cdot \frac{5}{4} = 45 + 70 + 50 = 165\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \\[6pt]
P_2 \text{ in } S\!: \quad & 20 \cdot \eta_M(1) + 40 \cdot \frac{9}{4} + 40 \cdot \eta_M(5) = 20 \cdot \frac{3}{4} + 90 + 40 \cdot \frac{7}{4} = 15 + 90 + 70 = 175\,\mathrm{kN\,m} \\[6pt]
P_3 \text{ in } S\!: \quad & 20 \cdot 0 + 40 \cdot \eta_M(1) + 40 \cdot \frac{9}{4} = 0 + 30 + 90 = 120\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
M_{S,\max} = 175\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
\qquad (P_1 \text{ in } \xi = 1\,\mathrm{m},\; P_2 \text{ in } S,\; P_3 \text{ in } \xi = 5\,\mathrm{m})
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;S,3,0;B,12,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,3,0,(3/4)*s;3,0,12,0,9/4+(-1/4)*s" segni=1 titolo="Influence line for the moment at $S$ ($\mathrm{m}$), with peak ordinate $\dfrac{9}{4}$; with $P_2$ at $S$, the other forces are at $\xi = 1\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\xi = 5\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\text{check (forces at } 1, 3, 5\,\mathrm{m}\text{):} \quad
V_A = \frac{20 \cdot 11 + 40 \cdot 9 + 40 \cdot 7}{12} = \frac{215}{3}\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
M_S - V_A \cdot 3 + 20 \cdot 2 = 175 - 215 + 40 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The moment at $S$ as a function of the position of the load train is the sum of the three forces for the ordinate of the line at their points, and is linear at times. For observation on the **load load train** the maximum is when one of the forces is on the vertex of the line, i.e. in $S$, or when a force enters or exits the beam; at the ends of the beam the line equals $0$, so just try the three positions with each force on $S$.

With $P_3$ in $S$ the force $P_1$ would fall into $\xi = -1\,\mathrm{m}$, out of the beam, and does not contribute. Between the three positions the maximum is $M_{S,\max} = 175\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, with $P_2$ on the section. The maximum is with the two forces from $40\,\mathrm{kN}$ on the vertex and right branch, because the right branch drops by $\dfrac{1}{4}$ per meter, while the left branch drops by $\dfrac{3}{4}$ per meter.

The check calculates $V_A$ with the rotation around $B$ and the moment at $S$ with the segment $AS$, on which $V_A$ and $P_1$ to $2\,\mathrm{m}$ act from $S$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown has a pin at $A$, a roller at $B$ a distance $6\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, and overhang $BC$ of length $2\,\mathrm{m}$. A uniform $q = 8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ load along $c = 3\,\mathrm{m}$ passes through it. Find the maximum value of $V_B$ and the minimum value of $V_A$, with the position of the load.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,8,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="1.5 0,4.5 0,0.5,0.5,q" quote="A,B,6 m,0.9;B,C,2 m,0.9;1.5 0,4.5 0,c = 3 m,-1.2" titolo="Overhanging beam with moving load $q = 8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ of length $3\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\eta_{V_B}(\xi) = \frac{\xi}{6} ,
\qquad
\eta_{V_A}(\xi) = \frac{6 - \xi}{6}
\qquad (0 \le \xi \le 8)
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dz}\,V_B(z) = q\,\big(\eta_{V_B}(z + 3) - \eta_{V_B}(z)\big) = 8 \cdot \frac{3}{6} = 4\,\mathrm{kN/m} > 0
\qquad (0 \le z \le 5)
$$

$$
z = 5\,\mathrm{m}\!: \quad
V_{B,\max} = 8 \int_5^8 \frac{\xi}{6}\,d\xi = \frac{8}{12}\,(64 - 25) = 26\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
V_{A,\min} = 8 \int_5^8 \frac{6 - \xi}{6}\,d\xi = 8 \cdot \frac{18 - 19.5}{6} = -2\,\mathrm{kN}
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,8,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,(1/6)*s;6,0,8,0,1+(1/6)*s" segni=1 titolo="Influence line for $V_B$, increasing along the entire beam, so the load is placed at end $C$"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0;C,8,0" aste="A-C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="5 0,C,0.5,0.5,q" titolo="Load position for $V_{B,\max}$ and $V_{A,\min}$, extending from $5\,\mathrm{m}$ to $8\,\mathrm{m}$"}

$$
\text{check (load from } 5 \text{ to } 8\,\mathrm{m}\text{, vertical force balance):} \quad
V_A + V_B - 8 \cdot 3 = -2 + 26 - 24 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The influence lines of the two reactions are straight across the beam. For $V_B$ the derivative of the value with respect to the $z$ position of the load is the difference of the ordinate at the ends, multiplied by $q$; because the line is increasing, the difference is always positive and the value grows as long as the load moves to the right. The maximum is then with the load at the end of the race, leaning at the end $C$, from $5\,\mathrm{m}$ to $8\,\mathrm{m}$, and there is no internal position with the ordinate extremes equal. Similarly $V_A$ has descending line and its minimum is in the same position.

You get **$V_{B,\max} = 26\,\mathrm{kN}$, Upward**, and **$V_{A,\min} = -2\,\mathrm{kN}$, i.e. $2\,\mathrm{kN}$ down**: with the load largely on the overhang the beam tends to rise in $A$. The same result is achieved with equilibrium: the resultant $24\,\mathrm{kN}$ is at $6.5\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$, then $V_B = \dfrac{24 \cdot 6.5}{6} = 26\,\mathrm{kN}$. Check uses vertical translation.

</div>
