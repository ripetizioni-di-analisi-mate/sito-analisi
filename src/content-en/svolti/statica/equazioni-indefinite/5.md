## Exercise 1

The beam shown has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$, span $6\,\mathrm{m}$, and carries a trapezoidal load that equals $2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ in $A$ and $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ in $B$. Get shear and moment with the differential equilibrium equations, reactions, position and maximum moment value.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.25,1,q" quote="A,B,6 m,1" etichette="0,0.55,2 kN/m;6,1.3,8 kN/m" titolo="Simply supported beam with a trapezoidal load varying from $2\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ to $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$"}

$$
q(x) = 2 + (8 - 2)\,\frac{x}{6} = 2 + x
$$
$$
T(x) = c_1 - 2x - \frac{x^2}{2} ,
\qquad
M(x) = c_2 + c_1 x - x^2 - \frac{x^3}{6}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
M(0) = c_2 = 0 \\
M(6) = c_2 + 6\,c_1 - 36 - 36 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_2 = 0 ,
\qquad
c_1 = 12\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
T(x) = 12 - 2x - \frac{x^2}{2} ,
\qquad
M(x) = 12x - x^2 - \frac{x^3}{6}
$$
$$
V_A = T(0) = 12\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
V_B = -T(6) = -(12 - 12 - 18) = 18\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
12 - 2x - \frac{x^2}{2} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x^2 + 4x - 24 = 0 ,
\qquad
x = -2 + 2\sqrt{7} \approx 3.29\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
M_{\max} = M(-2 + 2\sqrt{7}) = \frac{56\sqrt{7} - 80}{3} \approx 22.72\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{check, vertical force balance:} \quad V_A + V_B - \frac{2 + 8}{2} \cdot 6 = 12 + 18 - 30 = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,12-2*s-s^2/2" segni=1 titolo="Shear diagram $T$ ($\mathrm{kN}$), a parabola"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,12*s-s^2-s^3/6" verso=-1 titolo="Bending moment diagram $M$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$), a cubic with a maximum at $x \approx 3.29\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The load varies linearly from $2$ to $8\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on $6\,\mathrm{m}$, so it grows by $1\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ per meter and is given by $q(x) = 2 + x$. The load is first degree, therefore the shear is a parabola and the moment a cubic. The constants are determined with the **boundary conditions** of the simply supported beam, $M(0) = 0$ and $M(6) = 0$.

The reactions are **$V_A = 12\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B = 18\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward**, in accordance with trapezoidal load formulas: $\dfrac{(2 \cdot 2 + 8) \cdot 6}{6} = 12$ and $\dfrac{(2 + 2 \cdot 8) \cdot 6}{6} = 18$.

The shear vanishes in the positive root of the second degree equation, $x = -2 + 2\sqrt{7} \approx 3.29\,\mathrm{m}$, beyond the midspan, on the side of the most intense load. Substituting in the expression of the moment and using $x^2 = 24 - 4x$ to lower powers results in the **maximum $\dfrac{56\sqrt{7} - 80}{3} \approx 22.72\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$**. Check is vertical translation, with the resultant trapezoid, area of the load diagram.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The cantilever shown is fixed at $A$ and has length $3\,\mathrm{m}$. Its parabolic load $q(x) = x^2$ ($\mathrm{kN/m}$, with $x$ measured in $\mathrm{m}$ from the fixed end) increases from zero at $A$ to $9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at the free end $B$. Determine the shear force, bending moment, and fixed-end reactions.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" carichi="A,1 0,0,0.07;1 0,2 0,0.07,0.27;2 0,B,0.27,0.6,q" quote="A,B,3 m,1" titolo="Cantilever under parabolic load $q(x) = x^2$, equal to $9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$"}

$$
T(3) = 0 ,
\qquad
T(x) = \int_x^3 t^2\,dt = \frac{27 - x^3}{3} = 9 - \frac{x^3}{3}
$$
$$
M(3) = 0 ,
\qquad
M(x) = -\int_x^3 \left(9 - \frac{t^3}{3}\right) dt = -\left(27 - \frac{81}{12}\right) + 9x - \frac{x^4}{12} = -\frac{81}{4} + 9x - \frac{x^4}{12}
$$
$$
V_A = T(0) = 9\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
M_A = -M(0) = \frac{81}{4} = 20.25\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{check, moments about } A: \quad M_A - \int_0^3 x^2 \cdot x\,dx = 20.25 - \frac{81}{4} = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" diagramma="0,0,3,0,9-s^3/3" segni=1 titolo="Shear diagram $T$ ($\mathrm{kN}$), a cubic curve"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,incastro,180" diagramma="0,0,3,0,-81/4+9*s-s^4/12" verso=-1 titolo="Bending moment diagram $M$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$), a fourth-degree polynomial"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The free end $B$ is unloaded, so the boundary conditions are $T(3) = 0$ and $M(3) = 0$, and it is convenient to the **integral form** of the differential equilibrium equations between section $x$ and the free end. The $x$ shear is the resultant of the load to the right of the section. The load is second grade, so the shear is third grade and the moment of quarter.

The vertical reaction is the resultant of the load, **$V_A = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ Upward**, i.e. the area under the parabola, $\dfrac{3^3}{3} = 9$. The reaction moment is **$M_A = 20.25\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ counterclockwise**. The resultant of the load is applied in the centroid of the load diagram, which for the $q = x^2$ parabola on $[0, 3]$ is distant $\dfrac{3}{4} \cdot 3 = 2.25\,\mathrm{m}$ from the fixed support, and in fact $9 \cdot 2.25 = 20.25$.

The shear is positive throughout the cantilever, so the moment grows from $-20.25\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ at the fixed end to zero in the free end, and is negative everywhere. In the free end the moment diagram has horizontal tangent, because $T(3) = 0$. The check calculates the moment of load about $A$ with the integral $\displaystyle\int_0^3 q(x)\,x\,dx$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown has a pin at $A$ and a roller support at $B$, span $6\,\mathrm{m}$, and carries the sine load $q(x) = 5\sin\dfrac{\pi x}{6}$ ($\mathrm{kN/m}$), null in the supports and equal to $5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at midspan. Get shear and moment, reactions and maximum moment.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,1 0,0,0.3;1 0,2 0,0.3,0.52;2 0,3 0,0.52,0.6;3 0,4 0,0.6,0.52;4 0,5 0,0.52,0.3;5 0,B,0.3,0,q" quote="A,B,6 m,1" titolo="Simply supported beam under sinusoidal load $q(x) = 5\sin\dfrac{\pi x}{6}$"}

$$
T(x) = c_1 - \int 5\sin\frac{\pi x}{6}\,dx = c_1 + \frac{30}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi x}{6}
$$
$$
M(x) = c_2 + c_1 x + \frac{180}{\pi^2}\sin\frac{\pi x}{6}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
M(0) = c_2 = 0 \\
M(6) = c_2 + 6\,c_1 + \dfrac{180}{\pi^2}\sin\pi = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_2 = 0 ,
\qquad
c_1 = 0
$$
$$
T(x) = \frac{30}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi x}{6} ,
\qquad
M(x) = \frac{180}{\pi^2}\sin\frac{\pi x}{6}
$$
$$
V_A = T(0) = \frac{30}{\pi} \approx 9.55\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
V_B = -T(6) = \frac{30}{\pi} \approx 9.55\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
T(3) = 0 ,
\qquad
M_{\max} = M(3) = \frac{180}{\pi^2} \approx 18.24\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{check, vertical force balance:} \quad V_A + V_B - \int_0^6 5\sin\frac{\pi x}{6}\,dx = \frac{60}{\pi} - \frac{60}{\pi} = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,30/pi*cos(pi*s/6)" segni=1 titolo="Shear diagram $T$ ($\mathrm{kN}$), a cosine curve"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,180/pi^2*sin(pi*s/6)" verso=-1 titolo="Bending moment diagram $M$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$), a sine curve with its maximum at midspan"}

<div class="spiegazione">

An $\sin\dfrac{\pi x}{6}$ primitive is $-\dfrac{6}{\pi}\cos\dfrac{\pi x}{6}$, and an $\cos\dfrac{\pi x}{6}$ primitive is $\dfrac{6}{\pi}\sin\dfrac{\pi x}{6}$. These integrate $\dfrac{dT}{dx} = -q$ and $\dfrac{dM}{dx} = T$. The **boundary conditions** $M(0) = 0$ and $M(6) = 0$ cancel both constants, because the sine vanishes in $0$ and $\pi$.

The moment has the same shape as the load, and the load is symmetrical about the midspan; therefore the reactions are equal, **$V_A = V_B = \dfrac{30}{\pi} \approx 9.55\,\mathrm{kN}$, Upward**. The shear vanishes at midspan, where the cosine equals zero, and there the moment has the **maximum $\dfrac{180}{\pi^2} \approx 18.24\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$**.

The resultant load is $\dfrac{60}{\pi} \approx 19.10\,\mathrm{kN}$. A uniform load with the same resultant, i.e. $\dfrac{10}{\pi}\,\mathrm{kN/m}$, would give the maximum moment $\dfrac{10}{\pi} \cdot \dfrac{36}{8} \approx 14.32\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, less, because the sine load is more concentrated towards the midspan. Check is vertical translation.

</div>
