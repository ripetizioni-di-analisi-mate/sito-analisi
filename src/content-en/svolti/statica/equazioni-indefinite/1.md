## Exercise 1

The beam shown has a pin at $A$, a roller support at $B$, $L = 6\,\mathrm{m}$ span and carries the uniform load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$. Get with the differential equilibrium equations the expressions of the shear and the bending moment, the support reactions and the maximum moment, and draw the diagrams.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0.6,0.6,q" quote="A,B,6 m,1" titolo="Simply supported beam under uniform load $q = 5\,\mathrm{kN/m}$"}

$$
\frac{dT}{dx} = -q = -5
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T(x) = c_1 - 5x
$$
$$
\frac{dM}{dx} = T
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M(x) = c_2 + c_1 x - \frac{5}{2}\,x^2
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
M(0) = c_2 = 0 \\
M(6) = c_2 + 6\,c_1 - 90 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_2 = 0 ,
\qquad
c_1 = 15\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
T(x) = 15 - 5x ,
\qquad
M(x) = 15x - \frac{5}{2}\,x^2
$$
$$
V_A = T(0) = 15\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
V_B = -T(6) = -(15 - 30) = 15\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
H_A = 0
$$
$$
T(x) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = 3\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
M_{\max} = M(3) = 45 - 22.5 = 22.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{check, moments about } A: \quad V_B \cdot 6 - q \cdot 6 \cdot 3 = 15 \cdot 6 - 5 \cdot 6 \cdot 3 = 90 - 90 = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,15-5*s" segni=1 titolo="Shear diagram $T$ ($\mathrm{kN}$)"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,15*s-2.5*s^2" verso=-1 titolo="Bending moment diagram $M$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The beam acts as a single rigid body. The pin at $A$ and the roller at $B$ supply three independent restraints, so the beam is statically determinate. Take the origin at $A$ and the positive $x$-axis to the right. The load is uniform, and no point forces act between the supports, so one interval covers the entire span. The **differential equilibrium equations** give the derivative of the shear force as $-q$ and the derivative of the bending moment as $T$. Successive integration gives a linear shear diagram and a parabolic moment diagram, with two integration constants.

Constants are determined with boundary conditions. End supports do not transmit moment, so $M(0) = 0$ and $M(6) = 0$; the two conditions, to be met together, form the system written above. Reactions are read in the shear jumps. In $A$ the shear goes from $0$ to $T(0) = V_A$, in $B$ it goes from $T(6)$ to $0$, then $V_B = -T(6)$. Both reactions equal **$15\,\mathrm{kN}$, Upward**. There are no horizontal loads, so $H_A = 0$.

The moment is stationary where the shear vanishes, i.e. at midspan; since $q > 0$, there the moment has the maximum, $M_{\max} = 22.5\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$, equal to $\dfrac{qL^2}{8}$. The check uses moment equilibrium about $A$, which was not used in the calculation in the calculation. The resultant load, $30\,\mathrm{kN}$, is applied at midspan and its clockwise moment is balanced by that of $V_B$.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The simply supported beam shown, with a pin at $A$ and roller support at $B$, has a span of $L = 6\,\mathrm{m}$ and carries a triangular load that is zero at $A$ that grows linearly up to $q_0 = 9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ in $B$. Get shear and bending moment with differential equilibrium equations, support reactions, position and maximum moment value.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,B,0,0.9,q_0" quote="A,B,6 m,1" titolo="Simply supported beam under a triangular load of intensity $q_0 = 9\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ at $B$"}

$$
q(x) = q_0\,\frac{x}{L} = \frac{9x}{6} = \frac{3}{2}\,x
$$
$$
T(x) = c_1 - \int \frac{3}{2}\,x\,dx = c_1 - \frac{3}{4}\,x^2 ,
\qquad
M(x) = c_2 + c_1 x - \frac{x^3}{4}
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
M(0) = c_2 = 0 \\
M(6) = c_2 + 6\,c_1 - 54 = 0
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
c_2 = 0 ,
\qquad
c_1 = 9\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
T(x) = 9 - \frac{3}{4}\,x^2 ,
\qquad
M(x) = 9x - \frac{x^3}{4}
$$
$$
V_A = T(0) = 9\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
V_B = -T(6) = -(9 - 27) = 18\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
9 - \frac{3}{4}\,x^2 = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x^2 = 12 ,
\qquad
x = 2\sqrt{3} \approx 3.46\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
M_{\max} = 9 \cdot 2\sqrt{3} - \frac{(2\sqrt{3})^3}{4} = 18\sqrt{3} - 6\sqrt{3} = 12\sqrt{3} \approx 20.78\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{check, moments about } B: \quad -V_A \cdot 6 + \frac{q_0 L}{2} \cdot \frac{L}{3} = -9 \cdot 6 + 27 \cdot 2 = -54 + 54 = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,9-0.75*s^2" segni=1 titolo="Shear diagram $T$ ($\mathrm{kN}$), a parabola that vanishes at $x = 2\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,6,0,9*s-s^3/4" verso=-1 titolo="Bending moment diagram $M$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$), a cubic with a maximum at $x = 2\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The triangular load equals zero in $x = 0$ and $q_0$ in $x = L$, so its law is the line $q(x) = q_0\,\dfrac{x}{L}$. The load is a polynomial of the first degree, so for the **degree of curves** the shear is a parabola and the moment a cubic. The two integration constants are derived, as with any simply supported beam, from conditions $M(0) = 0$ and $M(6) = 0$.

Reactions are the shear in the supports, **$V_A = 9\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B = 18\,\mathrm{kN}$, both upward**. The reaction at $B$ is double that in $A$, because the resultant of the load, $\dfrac{q_0 L}{2} = 27\,\mathrm{kN}$, is applied in the triangle centroid, $\dfrac{L}{3} = 2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $B$.

The shear vanishes in $x = 2\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{m}$, beyond the midspan and then towards the most loaded extreme; at that point $q > 0$, so the moment has the maximum $12\sqrt{3} \approx 20.78\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. The result coincides with the formula $\dfrac{q_0 L^2}{9\sqrt{3}}$. The rotation around $B$ is used as a check, where the clockwise moment of $V_A$ balances the counterclockwise moment of the load resultant.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown is pinned at $A$ and supported by a roller at $B$ over a span of $6\,\mathrm{m}$. A uniform load $q = 6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ acts only on segment $AC$ of length $4\,\mathrm{m}$. Integrate the differential equilibrium equations piecewise to determine the shear force and bending moment. Find the support reactions and maximum moment, and draw the diagrams.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" carichi="A,C,0.6,0.6,q" quote="A,C,4 m,1;C,B,2 m,1" titolo="Simply supported beam with uniform load $q = 6\,\mathrm{kN/m}$ on segment $AC$"}

$$
\text{segment } AC,\ 0 \le x \le 4: \qquad
T_1(x) = a_1 - 6x ,
\qquad
M_1(x) = b_1 + a_1 x - 3x^2
$$
$$
\text{segment } CB,\ 4 \le x \le 6: \qquad
T_2(x) = a_2 ,
\qquad
M_2(x) = b_2 + a_2 x
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
M_1(0) = b_1 = 0 \\
M_2(6) = b_2 + 6\,a_2 = 0 \\
T_1(4) = T_2(4): \quad a_1 - 24 = a_2 \\
M_1(4) = M_2(4): \quad b_1 + 4\,a_1 - 48 = b_2 + 4\,a_2
\end{cases}
$$
$$
b_1 = 0 ,
\qquad
a_1 = 16\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
a_2 = -8\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
b_2 = 48\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
T_1(x) = 16 - 6x ,
\qquad
M_1(x) = 16x - 3x^2 ,
\qquad
T_2(x) = -8 ,
\qquad
M_2(x) = 48 - 8x
$$
$$
V_A = T_1(0) = 16\,\mathrm{kN} ,
\qquad
V_B = -T_2(6) = 8\,\mathrm{kN}
$$
$$
T_1(x) = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x = \frac{8}{3} \approx 2.67\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
M_{\max} = 16 \cdot \frac{8}{3} - 3 \cdot \frac{64}{9} = \frac{128}{3} - \frac{64}{3} = \frac{64}{3} \approx 21.33\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
M(4) = 16 \cdot 4 - 3 \cdot 16 = 16\,\mathrm{kN\,m}
$$
$$
\text{check, moments about } A: \quad V_B \cdot 6 - q \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 8 \cdot 6 - 6 \cdot 4 \cdot 2 = 48 - 48 = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,4,0,16-6*s;4,0,6,0,-8" segni=1 titolo="Shear diagram $T$ ($\mathrm{kN}$)"}

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;B,6,0" aste="A-B" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,4,0,16*s-3*s^2;4,0,6,0,16-8*s" verso=-1 titolo="Bending moment diagram $M$ ($\mathrm{kN\,m}$), parabolic over $AC$ and linear over $CB$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The loading changes at $C$, so **piecewise integration** requires separate expressions for the loaded segment $AC$ and the unloaded segment $CB$. On $AC$ the shear varies linearly and the moment varies quadratically. On $CB$ the load intensity is zero, so the shear is constant and the moment varies linearly. There are four integration constants, $a_1$, $b_1$, $a_2$ and $b_2$.

The four conditions, to be met together, are the zero moment in the two supports and the continuity of shear and moment at $C$. In $C$ no forces or concentrated couples act, so for the rule of jumps neither the shear nor the moment have discontinuities. $a_1 = 16\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $a_2 = -8\,\mathrm{kN}$ are derived from the system, and the reactions are **$V_A = 16\,\mathrm{kN}$ and $V_B = 8\,\mathrm{kN}$, upward**.

The shear vanishes in the loaded section, in $x = \dfrac{8}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$, where the moment has the maximum $\dfrac{64}{3} \approx 21.33\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$. In the segment $CB$ the shear is negative and constant, so the moment decreases linearly from $16\,\mathrm{kN\,m}$ to zero. The moment diagram in $C$ has no corners, because the shear is continuous. The check is the rotation around $A$, in which the resultant of the load, $24\,\mathrm{kN}$, is applied $2\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$.

</div>
