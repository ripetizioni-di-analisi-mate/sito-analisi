## Exercise 1

The beam shown is formed by the body $1 = AC$, with a pin support at $A$, and the body $2 = CD$, connected to the first by the internal hinge $C$ and resting at $B$ on a roller support that moves along a horizontal plane; the segment $BD$ is one overhang. Draw the vertical displacement diagram as a function of $\varphi_1$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;B,6,0;D,8,0" aste="A-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" quote="A,C,4 m;C,B,2 m;B,D,2 m" titolo="Two-body beam with an overhang"}

$$
C_1 = A = (0, 0) ,
\qquad
C_{12} = C = (4, 0) ,
\qquad
C_2 = B = (6, 0)
$$
$$
\varphi_1 \cdot (4 - 0)\,\mathrm{m} = \varphi_2 \cdot (4 - 6)\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_2 = -2\,\varphi_1
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v(x) &= \varphi_1\,x & &(0 \le x \le 4\,\mathrm{m}) \\[4pt]
v(x) &= -2\varphi_1\,(x - 6) & &(4\,\mathrm{m} \le x \le 8\,\mathrm{m})
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
v_C = 4\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1 ,
\qquad
v_B = 0 ,
\qquad
v_D = -2\varphi_1 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{m} = -4\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad v_C^{(1)} - v_C^{(2)} = 4\varphi_1 - \big( -2\varphi_1\,(4 - 6) \big) = 4\varphi_1 - 4\varphi_1 = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;C,4,0;B,6,0;D,8,0" aste="A-C;C-D" cerniere="C" vincoli="A,cerniera;B,carrello" diagramma="0,0,4,0,s;4,0,8,0,4-2*s" segni=1 titolo="Vertical displacement diagram $\dfrac{v}{\varphi_1}$ ($\mathrm{m}$)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The centers are found as in the chains of two bodies: $C_1 = A$ for the pin support, $C_{12} = C$ for the internal hinge, and $C_2$ on the vertical for the roller support and on the line $C_1 C_{12}$, then $C_2 = B$. From the first theorem of kinematic chains $\varphi_2 = -2\varphi_1$.

For the **Vertical Shifts of a Body** theorem, $v$ depends only on the horizontal coordinate, so on each body the diagram is a segment that cancels on the vertical of the center and has slope equal to the rotation. The first segment starts from zero at $A$ and climbs with $\varphi_1$ slope up to $4\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1$ at $C$; the second descends with $-2\varphi_1$ slope, passes through zero at $B$ and reaches **$-4\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1$ at the end of overhang**. The diagram is continuous in the hinge $C$, and verification checks it by calculating $v_C$ with the two bodies.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The beam shown is formed by the body $1 = AB$, fixed at $A$, the body $2 = BC$ and the body $3 = CD$, connected by the $B$ and $C$ internal hinges; the body $3$ is resting at $E$ on a roller support that moves along a horizontal plane, and $ED$ is one overhang. Find the centers of rotation and draw the diagram of vertical shifts as a function of the rotation $\varphi_2$ of the body $2$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;E,8,0;D,10,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,incastro,180;E,carrello" quote="A,B,3 m;B,C,3 m;C,E,2 m;E,D,2 m" titolo="Three-body beam with a fixed support"}

$$
\varphi_1 = 0 ,
\qquad
\vec{s}_1 = \vec{0}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
C_2 = C_{12} = B = (3, 0)
$$
$$
C_{23} = C = (6, 0) ,
\qquad
C_3 = E = (8, 0)
$$
$$
\varphi_2 \cdot (6 - 3)\,\mathrm{m} = \varphi_3 \cdot (6 - 8)\,\mathrm{m}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_3 = -\frac{3}{2}\,\varphi_2
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v(x) &= 0 & &(0 \le x \le 3\,\mathrm{m}) \\[4pt]
v(x) &= \varphi_2\,(x - 3) & &(3\,\mathrm{m} \le x \le 6\,\mathrm{m}) \\[4pt]
v(x) &= -\frac{3}{2}\varphi_2\,(x - 8) & &(6\,\mathrm{m} \le x \le 10\,\mathrm{m})
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
v_C = 3\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_2 ,
\qquad
v_D = -\frac{3}{2}\varphi_2 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{m} = -3\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_2
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad v_C^{(2)} - v_C^{(3)} = 3\varphi_2 - \left( -\frac{3}{2}\varphi_2 \cdot (-2) \right) = 3\varphi_2 - 3\varphi_2 = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,3,0;C,6,0;E,8,0;D,10,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,incastro,180;E,carrello" diagramma="3,0,6,0,s;6,0,10,0,3-1.5*s" segni=1 titolo="Vertical displacement diagram $\dfrac{v}{\varphi_2}$ ($\mathrm{m}$)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

Fixed support prevents any displacement of the body $1$, which remains stationary. The body $2$ is connected to the stationary body by the hinge $B$, so it can only rotates about $B$: $C_2 = B$. That is why the natural parameter is the rotation $\varphi_2$. The center $C_3$ lies on the vertical for the roller at $E$ and, for the first theorem of kinematic chains, on the line $C_2 C_{23}$, the axis of the beam; then $C_3 = E$.

The point $C$ is internal to the segment $C_2 C_3$, so the bodies $2$ and $3$ rotate in opposite directions, and the distance ratio gives $\varphi_3 = -\dfrac{3}{2}\varphi_2$. The diagram is zero on the encased body, rises linearly from $B$ to $C$ up to $3\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_2$, then descends, cancels at $E$ and reaches **$-3\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_2$ at $D$**. Verification controls continuity at $C$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The beam shown is formed by the bodies $1 = AB$, $2 = BC$, $3 = CD$, connected by the internal hinges $B$ and $C$. At $A$ there is a pin support, at $E$ and at $D$ two roller supports that travel on horizontal surfaces. Find the centers of rotation, including $C_{13}$, and draw the vertical displacement diagram as a function of $\varphi_1$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;E,5,0;C,7,0;D,9,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;E,carrello;D,carrello" quote="A,B,4 m;B,E,1 m;E,C,2 m;C,D,2 m" titolo="Three-body beam supported by a pin and two rollers"}

$$
C_1 = A = (0, 0) ,
\quad
C_{12} = B = (4, 0) ,
\quad
C_2 = E = (5, 0) ,
\quad
C_{23} = C = (7, 0) ,
\quad
C_3 = D = (9, 0)
$$
$$
\varphi_1 \cdot 4 = \varphi_2 \cdot (4 - 5)
\ \Rightarrow \
\varphi_2 = -4\,\varphi_1 ,
\qquad
\varphi_2 \cdot (7 - 5) = \varphi_3 \cdot (7 - 9)
\ \Rightarrow \
\varphi_3 = -\varphi_2 = 4\,\varphi_1
$$
$$
\varphi_1\,x_{C_{13}} = \varphi_3\,(x_{C_{13}} - 9)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x_{C_{13}} = 4\,(x_{C_{13}} - 9)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_{13} = (12, 0)
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
v(x) &= \varphi_1\,x & &(0 \le x \le 4\,\mathrm{m}) \\[4pt]
v(x) &= -4\varphi_1\,(x - 5) & &(4\,\mathrm{m} \le x \le 7\,\mathrm{m}) \\[4pt]
v(x) &= 4\varphi_1\,(x - 9) & &(7\,\mathrm{m} \le x \le 9\,\mathrm{m})
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
v_B = 4\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1 ,
\quad
v_C = -8\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad v_1(12) - v_3(12) = 12\,\varphi_1 - 4\varphi_1\,(12 - 9) = 12\,\varphi_1 - 12\,\varphi_1 = 0
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,4,0;E,5,0;C,7,0;D,9,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;E,carrello;D,carrello" diagramma="0,0,4,0,s;4,0,7,0,4-4*s;7,0,9,0,4*s-8" segni=1 titolo="Vertical displacement diagram $\dfrac{v}{\varphi_1}$ ($\mathrm{m}$)"}

<div class="spiegazione">

All the centers stand on the axis of the beam. The $C_1$, $C_{12}$, $C_{23}$ centers are given by the hinges; $C_2$ lies on the vertical for the roller at $E$ and on the line $C_1 C_{12}$, then $C_2 = E$; $C_3$ also lies on the vertical through $D$ and on the line $C_2 C_{23}$, hence $C_3 = D$. Rotations are obtained by proceeding from one body to the next with the relation of the first theorem of kinematic chains: $\varphi_2 = -4\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_3 = 4\varphi_1$.

The **Second theorem of kinematic chains** says that $C_{13}$ lies on the line $C_{12} C_{23}$, but here this line coincides with the line $C_1 C_3$, and the alignment is not enough to pinpoint the point. The relationship between the rotations of the $1$ and $3$ bodies is then used, which gives $C_{13} = (12, 0)$, out of the beam. In the diagram, $C_{13}$ is the point at which the prolongations of the segments of the $1$ and $3$ bodies meet: there the two bodies, extended, would have the same displacement, and verification controls it. The body $2$, short and with the center near the hinge $B$, rotates a lot, and the hinge $C$ drops by $8\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1$.

</div>
