## Exercise 1

The portal frame shown consists of bodies $1 = AB$, $2 = BC$, and $3 = CD$, joined by internal hinges $B$ and $C$. At $A$ and $D$ are two fixed pins at different elevations. Locate all the absolute and relative centers of rotation and express the rotations $\varphi_2$ and $\varphi_3$ in terms of $\varphi_1$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,6,4;D,6,1" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" quote="B,C,6 m,-0.7;A,B,4 m,-0.8;D,C,3 m,0.8" titolo="Three-body portal frame with pin supports at different elevations"}

$$
C_1 = A = (0, 0) ,
\qquad
C_3 = D = (6, 1) ,
\qquad
C_{12} = B = (0, 4) ,
\qquad
C_{23} = C = (6, 4)
$$
$$
C_2 \in C_1 C_{12} \ (x = 0) ,
\qquad
C_2 \in C_3 C_{23} \ (x = 6)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_2 = \text{point at infinity in the vertical direction} ,
\quad
\varphi_2 = 0
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y = \dfrac{x}{6} & \text{(line } C_1 C_3) \\[4pt]
y = 4 & \text{(line } C_{12} C_{23})
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_{13} = (24, 4)
$$
$$
\varphi_1\,\overrightarrow{C_1 C_{13}} = \varphi_3\,\overrightarrow{C_3 C_{13}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_1 \begin{pmatrix} 24 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \varphi_3 \begin{pmatrix} 18 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_3 = \frac{4}{3}\,\varphi_1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad u_B = -\varphi_1\,(4 - 0)\,\mathrm{m} = -4\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1 ,
\qquad
u_C = -\varphi_3\,(4 - 1)\,\mathrm{m} = -\frac{4}{3}\varphi_1 \cdot 3\,\mathrm{m} = -4\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The pin supports give the absolute centers of the columns, $C_1 = A$ and $C_3 = D$, and the internal hinges give the relative centers $C_{12} = B$ and $C_{23} = C$. For **first theorem of kinematic chains** applied to $1$ and $2$ bodies, the center $C_2$ of the crossbeam lies on the line $AB$; Applied to bodies $2$ and $3$, it lies on line $DC$. The two lines are the vertical $x = 0$ and $x = 6$, parallel, so $C_2$ is the point at infinity in the vertical direction, therefore the crossbeam **translates horizontally** and $\varphi_2 = 0$.

The relative center of the two columns, which are not directly connected, is found with the **Second theorem of kinematic chains**: $C_{13}$ lies on the line $C_{12} C_{23}$, i.e. $y = 4$, and, for the first theorem applied to bodies $1$ and $3$, on the line $C_1 C_3$. The point $C_{13} = (24, 4)$ is external to the segment $C_1 C_3$, so the two column rotate in the same direction, and the relationship between the vectors gives $\varphi_3 = \dfrac{4}{3}\varphi_1$. The column $CD$ is shorter than $AB$ and must give the hinge $C$ the same horizontal displacement that $AB$ gives to $B$, so it rotates more. The check just checks that $B$ and $C$, extremes of the crossbeam it translates, have the same horizontal displacement.

</div>

## Exercise 2

The portal frame in the figure is formed by the bodies $1 = AB$, $2 = BC$, $3 = CD$, connected by the internal hinges $B$ and $C$; at $A$ and $D$ there are two pin supports and the column $CD$ is tilted. Find the $C_2$ and $C_{13}$ rotation centers, the rotations as a function of $\varphi_1$ and the displacement of the hinge $C$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,4,4;D,6,2" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" quote="B,C,4 m,-0.7;A,B,4 m,-0.8;C,6 4,2 m,-0.6;D,6 4,2 m,0.8" titolo="Three-body portal frame with one inclined column"}

$$
C_1 = A = (0, 0) ,
\qquad
C_3 = D = (6, 2) ,
\qquad
C_{12} = B = (0, 4) ,
\qquad
C_{23} = C = (4, 4)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x = 0 & \text{(line } C_1 C_{12}) \\
y = 8 - x & \text{(line } C_3 C_{23})
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_2 = (0, 8)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
y = \dfrac{x}{3} & \text{(line } C_1 C_3) \\[4pt]
y = 4 & \text{(line } C_{12} C_{23})
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_{13} = (12, 4)
$$
$$
\varphi_1 \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \varphi_2 \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 4 - 8 \end{pmatrix}
\ \Rightarrow \
\varphi_2 = -\varphi_1 ,
\qquad
\varphi_1 \begin{pmatrix} 12 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} = \varphi_3 \begin{pmatrix} 12 - 6 \\ 4 - 2 \end{pmatrix}
\ \Rightarrow \
\varphi_3 = 2\,\varphi_1
$$
$$
\vec{s}_C = \big( -\varphi_3\,(4 - 2),\ \varphi_3\,(4 - 6) \big) = (-4, -4)\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad \vec{s}_C \text{ with body } 2 = \big( -\varphi_2\,(4 - 8),\ \varphi_2\,(4 - 0) \big) = (-4, -4)\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1
$$

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,4;C,4,4;D,6,2" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;D,cerniera" punti="0,8,C_2,centro" tratteggi="0,4 0,8.4;6,2 -0.4,8.4;0,0 7,2.33" titolo="Center $C_2$ is the intersection of the column axes. Point $C_{13} = (12, 4)$ lies outside the figure on the dashed extension of line $AD$"}

<div class="spiegazione">

The absolute centers of the columns and the relative centers are given by the hinges, as in the four-bar linkage. For the **first theorem of kinematic chains** the center $C_2$ of the crossbeam lies on the line of the column $AB$ and the line of the column $DC$, which has slope $-1$ and passes through $D = (6, 2)$; The two lines meet in $C_2 = (0, 8)$. For the **second theorem** the relative center of the column $C_{13}$ lies on the line $BC$, i.e. $y = 4$, and on the line $AD$, of $\dfrac{1}{3}$ slope; then $C_{13} = (12, 4)$.

The point $B$ is the midpoint of $C_1 C_2$, so the crossbeam rotates in the opposite direction to the column $AB$ and with the same amplitude. The point $C_{13}$ is outside the segment $C_1 C_3$ and is twice as far from $C_1$ as from $C_3$, so the two columns rotate in the same direction and $\varphi_3 = 2\varphi_1$. The hinge $C$ moves perpendicular to the $DC$ RIGHT, of $(-4, -4)\,\mathrm{m} \cdot \varphi_1$. The check recalculates the displacement of $C$ as the crossbeam point, with $\varphi_2 = -\varphi_1$ and the center $C_2$.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The arch in the figure is formed by the bodies $1 = AB$, $2 = BC$, $3 = CD$, connected by the internal hinges $B$ and $C$. At $A$ there is a pin support, at $D$ a roller support that moves along a horizontal plane, and the crossbeam $BC$ is supported at point $E$ by a two-force vertical link. Find rotation centers and rotations as a function of $\varphi_1$.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,2,3;E,4,3;C,6,3;D,8,0" aste="A-B;B-C;C-D" cerniere="B;C" vincoli="A,cerniera;E,pendolo;D,carrello" quote="A,2 0,2 m;2 0,6 0,4 m;6 0,D,2 m;B,2 0,3 m,0.6" titolo="Three-body arch with a link on its crossbeam"}

$$
C_1 = A = (0, 0) ,
\qquad
C_{12} = B = (2, 3) ,
\qquad
C_{23} = C = (6, 3)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x = 4 & \text{(axis of the link)} \\[2pt]
y = \dfrac{3}{2}\,x & \text{(line } C_1 C_{12})
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_2 = (4, 6)
$$
$$
\begin{cases}
x = 8 & \text{(vertical line through } D) \\[2pt]
y = 6 - \dfrac{3}{2}\,(x - 4) & \text{(line } C_2 C_{23})
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_3 = (8, 0) = D
$$
$$
C_{13} \in C_1 C_3 \ (y = 0) ,
\qquad
C_{13} \in C_{12} C_{23} \ (y = 3)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
C_{13} = \text{point at infinity in the horizontal direction} ,
\quad
\varphi_3 = \varphi_1
$$
$$
\varphi_1 \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \varphi_2 \begin{pmatrix} 2 - 4 \\ 3 - 6 \end{pmatrix}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi_2 = -\varphi_1
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad \varphi_2\,\overrightarrow{C_2 C_{23}} = -\varphi_1 \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} = \varphi_1 \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
\varphi_3\,\overrightarrow{C_3 C_{23}} = \varphi_1 \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

Hinges give $C_1 = A$, $C_{12} = B$ and $C_{23} = C$. The vertical two-force link allows $E$ only horizontal shifts, so $C_2$ lies on the vertical $x = 4$; for the **first theorem of kinematic chains** also lies on the line $AB$, and the intersection is $C_2 = (4, 6)$. The center $C_3$ lies on the vertical for the roller at $D$ and, for the first theorem applied to bodies $2$ and $3$, on the line $C_2 C_{23}$; This line passes right through $D$, then $C_3 = D$.

For the **Second theorem of kinematic chains** $C_{13}$ it lies on the line $BC$, $y = 3$; It also lies on line $C_1 C_3$, $y = 0$. The two lines are parallel, so $C_{13}$ is at infinity and the $1$ and $3$ bodies have the same rotation. The point $B$ is the midpoint of $C_1 C_2$, therefore $\varphi_2 = -\varphi_1$. The check applies the relation of the first theorem to the pair of $2$ and $3$ bodies: the two vectors are equal, so the found rotations are compatible in the hinge $C$.

</div>
