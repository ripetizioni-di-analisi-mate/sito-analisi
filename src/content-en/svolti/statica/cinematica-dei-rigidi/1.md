## Exercise 1

The L-shaped beam $ABD$ shown has points $A = (0, 0)$, $B = (0, 3)$, and $D = (4, 3)$, with coordinates in meters. In an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement, point $A$ has displacement components $u_A = 3\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $v_A = -4\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the beam rotates counterclockwise through $\varphi = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$. Find the displacements of $B$ and $D$ and locate the center of rotation.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,0,3;D,4,3" aste="A-B;B-D" frecce="0,0,0.6,-0.8,s_A" quote="A,B,3 m,-0.8;B,D,4 m,-0.7" titolo="L-shaped beam free to move in the plane. The arrow shows the prescribed displacement of $A$"}

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_B &= u_A - \varphi\,(y_B - y_A) = 3\,\mathrm{mm} - 0.002 \cdot 3\,\mathrm{m} = 3\,\mathrm{mm} - 6\,\mathrm{mm} = -3\,\mathrm{mm} \\[4pt]
v_B &= v_A + \varphi\,(x_B - x_A) = -4\,\mathrm{mm} + 0.002 \cdot 0 = -4\,\mathrm{mm} \\[4pt]
u_D &= u_A - \varphi\,(y_D - y_A) = 3\,\mathrm{mm} - 6\,\mathrm{mm} = -3\,\mathrm{mm} \\[4pt]
v_D &= v_A + \varphi\,(x_D - x_A) = -4\,\mathrm{mm} + 0.002 \cdot 4\,\mathrm{m} = -4\,\mathrm{mm} + 8\,\mathrm{mm} = 4\,\mathrm{mm}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
x_C = x_A - \frac{v_A}{\varphi} = 0 - \frac{-0.004\,\mathrm{m}}{0.002} = 2\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
y_C = y_A + \frac{u_A}{\varphi} = 0 + \frac{0.003\,\mathrm{m}}{0.002} = 1.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad u_D = -\varphi\,(y_D - y_C) = -0.002 \cdot 1.5\,\mathrm{m} = -3\,\mathrm{mm} ,
\qquad
v_D = \varphi\,(x_D - x_C) = 0.002 \cdot 2\,\mathrm{m} = 4\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The displacement of $A$ and the rotation determine the displacement of the entire beam because a planar rigid body has three degrees of freedom. With reference point $A$, the **infinitesimal rigid-body displacement** formulas give the displacements of $B$ and $D$. Multiplying lengths in meters by the rotation in radians gives displacements in meters, which we then convert to millimeters. Points $B$ and $D$ have the same vertical coordinate and therefore the same horizontal displacement $-3\,\mathrm{mm}$. Points $A$ and $B$ have the same horizontal coordinate and therefore the same vertical displacement $-4\,\mathrm{mm}$.

Since $\varphi \neq 0$, exactly one point has zero displacement. The **center of rotation** formulas give $C = (2;\ 1.5)\,\mathrm{m}$. Recalculating the displacement of $D$ as a rotation about $C$ reproduces the previous values. Also, $|\vec{s}_D| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}\,\mathrm{mm} = 5\,\mathrm{mm}$ equals $\varphi\,\overline{CD} = 0.002 \cdot 2.5\,\mathrm{m} = 5\,\mathrm{mm}$, as required for a rotation.

</div>

## Exercise 2

Beam $AB$ has points $A = (0, 0)$ and $B = (5, 0)$, with coordinates in meters, and undergoes an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement. Given $u_A = 1\,\mathrm{mm}$, $v_A = 2\,\mathrm{mm}$, and $v_B = -8\,\mathrm{mm}$, find the beam rotation, the horizontal displacement of $B$, and the center of rotation.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;B,5,0" aste="A-B" frecce="0,0,0.5,1,s_A;5,0,5,-1.6,v_B" quote="A,B,5 m" titolo="Free beam with prescribed displacements"}

$$
v_B = v_A + \varphi\,(x_B - x_A)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = \frac{v_B - v_A}{x_B - x_A} = \frac{-0.008 - 0.002}{5}\,\mathrm{rad} = -0.002\,\mathrm{rad}
$$
$$
u_B = u_A - \varphi\,(y_B - y_A) = 1\,\mathrm{mm} - \varphi \cdot 0 = 1\,\mathrm{mm}
$$
$$
x_C = x_A - \frac{v_A}{\varphi} = -\frac{0.002\,\mathrm{m}}{-0.002} = 1\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
y_C = y_A + \frac{u_A}{\varphi} = \frac{0.001\,\mathrm{m}}{-0.002} = -0.5\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad v_B = \varphi\,(x_B - x_C) = -0.002 \cdot 4\,\mathrm{m} = -8\,\mathrm{mm} ,
\qquad
u_B = -\varphi\,(y_B - y_C) = 0.002 \cdot 0.5\,\mathrm{m} = 1\,\mathrm{mm}
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The two vertical shifts differ by $\varphi$ by the horizontal distance between the points, so the rotation is obtained by dividing the difference of the vertical shifts by the span. You get $\varphi = -0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$: the beam **wheel in clockwise**, as seen also by the fact that $A$ goes up and $B$ moves down. The horizontal displacement of $B$ is equal to that of $A$, because the two points stand on the same horizontal and, by the theorem on **travel along the conjunct**, have the same component as the displacement along the beam.

The center of rotation lies at $1\,\mathrm{m}$ from $A$ horizontally and $0.5\,\mathrm{m}$ below the beam, then out of the body. The check recalculates the displacements of $B$ with the center formulas and finds the assigned or calculated values.

</div>

## Exercise 3

The triangular plate $ADB$ shown has vertices $A = (0, 0)$, $D = (3, 0)$, and $B = (3, 4)$, with coordinates in meters. During an infinitesimal rigid-body displacement, vertex $A$ has displacement components $u_A = 4\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $v_A = -2\,\mathrm{mm}$, and vertex $B$ has vertical displacement $v_B = 4\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find the rotation, the horizontal displacement of $B$, the displacement of $D$, and the center of rotation.

::struttura{nodi="A,0,0;D,3,0;B,3,4" poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,4" frecce="0,0,0.8,-0.4,s_A;3,4,3,4.8,v_B" quote="A,D,3 m;D,B,4 m" titolo="Triangular plate with prescribed displacements"}

$$
v_B = v_A + \varphi\,(x_B - x_A)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varphi = \frac{0.004 - (-0.002)}{3}\,\mathrm{rad} = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}
$$
$$
u_B = u_A - \varphi\,(y_B - y_A) = 4\,\mathrm{mm} - 0.002 \cdot 4\,\mathrm{m} = -4\,\mathrm{mm}
$$
$$
u_D = u_A - \varphi\,(y_D - y_A) = 4\,\mathrm{mm} ,
\qquad
v_D = v_A + \varphi\,(x_D - x_A) = -2\,\mathrm{mm} + 0.002 \cdot 3\,\mathrm{m} = 4\,\mathrm{mm}
$$
$$
x_C = 0 - \frac{-0.002\,\mathrm{m}}{0.002} = 1\,\mathrm{m} ,
\qquad
y_C = 0 + \frac{0.004\,\mathrm{m}}{0.002} = 2\,\mathrm{m}
$$
$$
\text{check:} \quad (\vec{s}_B - \vec{s}_A) \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = (-4 - 4) \cdot 3 + \big( 4 - (-2) \big) \cdot 4 = -24 + 24 = 0
$$

<div class="spiegazione">

The three $u_A$, $v_A$ and $v_B$ data identify the rigid-body displacement, because the plate has three degrees of freedom. From the formula of the vertical displacement of $B$ with the reference point at $A$ is obtained the rotation, **$\varphi = 0.002\,\mathrm{rad}$ counterclockwise**. With the known rotation, the formulas of the rigid-body displacements infinitesimal give the horizontal displacement of $B$, $-4\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the displacement of $D$, $(4, 4)\,\mathrm{mm}$. The point $D$ has the same order as $A$ and thus the same horizontal displacement, and has the same horizontal coordinate as $B$ and thus the same vertical displacement.

The rotation center formulas give $C = (1, 2)\,\mathrm{m}$. On the vertical line $x = 1\,\mathrm{m}$, the plate extends as far as the ordinate $\dfrac{4}{3}\,\mathrm{m}$ of side $AB$, so the center lies outside the plate. The check checks that the displacements of $A$ and $B$ have the same component along the conjunct $AB$, i.e. that the distance between the two vertices remains unchanged at the first order; products are expressed in $\mathrm{mm} \cdot \mathrm{m}$.

</div>
