## What understanding a passage means

:::definizione{#comprensione titolo="Text, question and answer"}
**Reading comprehension** consists of reconstructing the information and relationships expressed by a passage. An answer is supported when it follows from what the text states or from a justified connection between its parts. It may be true in the world and still fail to answer the question if the passage does not provide the evidence needed to derive it.

The wording of the question defines the task. “According to the author” requires reconstructing the author’s position; “which fact is reported” requires explicit information; “which conclusion follows” requires a deduction; “which statement contradicts the text” requires a conflict with its facts. The correct answer therefore depends on both passage and question, rather than on the option’s plausibility alone.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="A survey and what it measures"}
A report describes evening entries to a library and specifies that reading time was not measured. A higher entry count means that more entries were recorded. It does not mean that each person reads for longer: there might be more visitors or more short visits by the same person.

If the question asks for the observed fact, the answer concerns entries. If it asks which variable should be measured to assess reading duration, the answer concerns time spent reading. The two questions use the same passage but select different information and reasoning.
:::

:::osservazione
The practice passages in this chapter are original and are not official CISIA questions. The cases, data and organizations described serve the reading exercise. Answers depend on the conditions stated in each passage; any real rules of a library, museum or laboratory are not added to the text.
:::

## Explicit, implicit and absent information

:::definizione{#informazioni titolo="Three relationships with the text"}
**Explicit** information is stated directly, even if the answer restates it using different words. **Implicit** information requires connecting facts already present. **Absent** information is not determined, although it may be compatible with the passage. This distinction prevents a possibility from being turned into a fact.

If the museum opens Tuesday through Sunday and the text says it is closed on Monday, Monday’s closure is explicit. If a visit is scheduled for Monday and can take place only while the museum is open, its impossibility under that schedule is a deduction. The expected number of visitors remains absent if no sentence provides it.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="A deduction joining two facts"}
The archive has digitized every map published after 1950. A map in its catalog was published in 1962. Since 1962 follows 1950, that map belongs to the completely digitized group. The conclusion uses only the rule and the date.

For a map published in 1930, however, digitization would remain undetermined if the text said that *some* earlier maps had also been digitized. The existence of digitized old maps does not identify which ones they are. The difference between a fully described set and a partially described one changes the strength of the answer.
:::

:::attenzione
“The text does not prove that a procedure works” does not mean “the text proves that the procedure does not work”. The first sentence leaves the issue open; the second asserts a negative outcome. For example, a test on only two samples may be insufficient for generalization even if those two cases produced favorable results.
:::

## Thesis, structure and examples

:::definizione{#tesi titolo="Topic and thesis"}
The **topic** is what the text discusses; the **thesis** is the position it supports on that topic. A passage can discuss maps without claiming that every more detailed map is better. It might instead argue that the choice of details should depend on the intended use.

Structure connects opening, development and conclusion. A text may introduce a problem, compare two responses and justify a preference; it may describe a phenomenon and then limit its interpretation; it may recount an episode and use it as an example of a broader transformation. Identifying these functions allows a summary to preserve the argument.
:::

:::definizione{#esempi titolo="The function of examples"}
An example makes a concept concrete, demonstrates a possible case or provides a counterexample to an overgeneral rule. Its function depends on its connection with the other sentences. A favorable case may illustrate a thesis without automatically proving that the thesis always holds.

An author argues that an urban landscape also includes sounds and habits. The author then recounts how a district is perceived differently after night shifts end, although its buildings remain unchanged. The episode demonstrates a perceptual transformation without a change in buildings. It does not make every industrial district identical to the one described.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="A proportionate title"}
A passage compares maps for pedestrians with maps for maintenance workers, explaining that the former highlight accessible routes and the latter utility systems. “Maps change with their purpose” summarizes both parts. “The district’s pipes” concerns only a detail of the second use. “Maps are always incomplete and useless” adds an absent judgment.

An appropriate title therefore has a scope similar to the thesis. Wording that is too narrow loses part of the reasoning; wording that is too broad introduces a claim the text has not supported.
:::

## Connectives and logical relationships

:::definizione{#connettivi titolo="Causes, consequences and contrasts"}
**Connectives** make relationships between sentences explicit. “Since” may introduce a cause or reason; “therefore” introduces a consequence; “however” signals a contrast with what might have been expected; “moreover” adds information; “that is” introduces a restatement or explanation.

The relationship is identified by considering both parts. In “the project arrived on time; however, a signature is missing”, the second fact limits the favorable outcome that might be associated with punctuality. The missing signature does not make the project late: it introduces a separate requirement. Replacing “however” with “therefore” would make the missing signature appear to result from punctuality, changing the meaning.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Concession and contrast"}
“Although the room is smaller, the voices are more distinguishable” accepts the reduced size while asserting a potentially unexpected result. The sentence does not deny the room’s size or say that the voices are distinguishable *because* the room is small.

“While in winter the boat departs only in the morning, in summer it also runs an evening service” uses “while” to compare two arrangements. In “while the boat was leaving, the caretaker was closing the gate”, the same word indicates simultaneity. A word’s function is therefore inferred from the actual relationship between the clauses.
:::

:::definizione{#condizioni titolo="Conditions and exceptions"}
“Provided that” introduces a condition. If a notice permits courtyard access provided that the side passage is used, authorized access depends on using that passage. “Except” and “with the exception of” limit a general rule: plaster casts remain in the room except those sent for restoration.

A condition may be necessary, sufficient or both. “An application is accepted only if signed” makes the signature necessary; “a signature is enough for acceptance” makes it sufficient. Reading must preserve the direction of the relationship indicated by the text. Similar words alone do not make the two sentences equivalent.
:::

## References, words and viewpoint

:::definizione{#riferimenti titolo="Pronouns and textual references"}
Pronouns and expressions such as “this”, “those”, “the former” and “in this way” refer back to something already introduced. The reference may concern a noun, an entire sentence or a procedure. Gender and number help identify candidates, but the meaning must make the whole sentence coherent.

In “the tower houses two bells and three clocks; the latter have been synchronized”, “the latter” refers to the clocks. In “the original code is retained in a separate field; in this way the source image can be identified”, the reference concerns retaining the code, rather than just the field’s name.
:::

:::definizione{#lessico titolo="Meaning in context"}
A word may have several general meanings. Its **contextual meaning** is the one consistent with the sentence and the passage’s development. A “previously unpublished” photograph may have been taken many years earlier and be shown for the first time: the term concerns publication, not the age of the photograph.

A correct paraphrase preserves the relevant property. “Recycled” describes material containing matter recovered from previous products; “recyclable” describes the possibility of recovery after use. Although the words share a root, they do not convey the same information. The context may assign an explicit definition that takes precedence over vague associations.
:::

:::osservazione
**Viewpoint** identifies who observes, remembers or judges. A narrator recounting a memory does not automatically coincide with the book’s author. If a text reports a colleague’s objection, that statement should be attributed to the colleague; attributing the same position to the author also requires an indication of endorsement, not merely discussion. Attributing a voice to the wrong person can reverse the passage’s overall position.
:::

## Quantities, times and the scope of claims

:::definizione{#ambito titolo="The scope of a conclusion"}
Words indicating quantity delimit the set being described. “Some” requires at least one case, “all” includes the entire set, and “only” excludes what lies outside the stated class. “Often” does not mean “always”, and “may” does not mean “must”. A faithful summary preserves these differences.

Time also limits a conclusion. A survey conducted only in August can describe summer preferences without determining winter ones. A comparison lasting one week does not guarantee that its result will persist for a year. Caution may already be expressed by the authors, in which case it must also appear in the answer.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Sample and population"}
A newsroom interviews people entering the campus through the north gate on Monday morning. Among these people, the bus is the most frequent mode of transport. The result concerns those interviewed at that place and time. People entering through other gates, attending on other days or absent at the time might have different habits.

“Among the interviewees, the bus predominates” preserves the scope. “All campus students take the bus” changes both quantifier and population. “Nobody uses other transport” is even stronger and does not follow even from the bus being the most frequent mode in the sample.
:::

:::definizione{#cronologia titolo="Temporal order and duration"}
An event sequence is reconstructed by distinguishing starting date, duration, deadline and any conditions. “After the check” establishes an order; “by the end of the day” establishes a deadline; “for three months” establishes a duration. “First” may concern time or priority within an argument, depending on context.

If a procedure returns to checking after additional information is supplied, supplying it does not replace the check. A complete sequence includes receipt, checking, requesting missing data, supplying them and checking again. Omitting the final step changes the procedure even if every activity name appears in the answer.
:::

## Data, tables and graphs

:::definizione{#dati titolo="Labels and units"}
A table associates each row with an object and each column with a quantity or property. A value’s meaning depends on its heading, unit and any notes. A “return” column may indicate departure time from the station, rather than arrival time at campus.

A symbol may indicate missing data, an unfinished check or a category. Zero and an empty cell are not interchangeable if the key distinguishes them. Zero anomalies may mean that a check was performed and found none; an empty cell may mean that the check was not performed.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Applying two thresholds together"}
A team qualifies if it obtains at least $15$ points and takes at most $30$ minutes.

| Team | Points | Minutes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Iris | 15 | 30 |
| Cedro | 16 | 31 |
| Pino | 14 | 27 |

Iris satisfies both thresholds, which include equality. Cedro exceeds the point threshold but takes too long; Pino meets the time limit but does not reach the required points. Combining the requirements therefore selects only Iris. Comparing one column in isolation would not answer the question.
:::

:::osservazione
A comparison may concern counts, differences or ratios. If A has $18$ passes out of $20$ and B has $24$ out of $30$, B has more passes in absolute terms, but A has the higher proportion, $90\%$ versus $80\%$. The question determines which comparison is relevant. Percentages with different denominators cannot simply be added to obtain an overall percentage.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Reading a comparison between two costs"}
A service offers Flex at two euros per journey and Plan with a twenty-euro fixed charge plus one euro per journey. In the graph, $n$ denotes journeys and $C$ total cost in euros; $L$ and $P$ distinguish the two fares. Cost is a vertical value, while journey count is the horizontal input.

::grafico{f="2*x;20+x" xmin="0" xmax="40" ymin="0" ymax="85" nomi="n,C" punti="20,40" legenda="L=2n;P=20+n" titolo="At twenty journeys the lines intersect: both fares cost forty euros."}

Plan’s line starts at twenty euros and rises less quickly. Before the intersection it is higher, so it indicates greater spending; after the intersection it is lower. The price per journey alone does not determine which fare is cheaper. Interpreting the graph also requires the initial charge and journey count: a statement such as “Plan costs less for everyone” extends the result beyond the region where it applies.
:::

## Comparisons, causes and limits

:::definizione{#causalita titolo="Association and causal explanation"}
Two phenomena are associated when they vary together in observed data. Attributing an effect on one to the other requires further evidence. A common cause might exist, or the relationship might operate in the opposite direction. A passage can propose a causal hypothesis without claiming it has been proved.

If people enabling reminders complete more exercises, reminders might help. However, greater initial motivation might favor both enabling them and practicing. Participants’ free choice leaves this alternative explanation open. A faithful answer distinguishes the observed result from its interpretation.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Changing one condition"}
Two groups grow seeds. The first receives more light and more water, the second less light and less water. If more seeds germinate in the first group, the comparison does not isolate the effect of light because water also changes. A comparison with identical watering and different light would be more suitable for studying light while keeping other relevant conditions comparable.

This reading requires no additional botanical knowledge. The limitation follows directly from the described comparison’s structure. When a question asks which measurement or comparison is missing, the answer must address that specific limitation rather than add an interesting fact unrelated to the conclusion.
:::

:::osservazione
A decrease in the mean does not require a decrease for every individual. If some waits fall substantially and others rise slightly, the mean can fall while some users wait longer. Similarly, lower consumption per streetlight can accompany stable total consumption if the number of lights increases. A text can compare different levels of description without contradicting itself.
:::

## Summaries, alternatives and checking answers

:::definizione{#sintesi titolo="Faithful paraphrases and summaries"}
A **paraphrase** expresses the same content in other words. A **summary** reduces details while retaining the thesis and the relationships needed to understand it. Both preserve subject, quantity, time, conditions and degree of certainty. Turning a proposed trial into a final decision changes the content even when the topic stays the same.

A good summary of “the mobile library will be tested for three months, and the final choice will depend on visits and costs” preserves the experimental stage and the evaluation criteria. Saying only “the mobile library will start in spring” may be a true fact but miss the decisive part when the question asks for the whole text’s thesis.
:::

:::definizione{#distrattori titolo="Why an option fails to answer"}
An option may contradict a sentence, add information, generalize limited data, reverse a condition, swap cause and consequence, or repeat a detail in place of the thesis. Comparison with the passage reveals which transformation has occurred.

Using the same words does not suffice to make an answer correct. “The procedure reduces errors” and “errors reduce the procedure” contain similar words but assign different roles. Even a more elegant or more general answer may be less faithful than simple, carefully limited wording.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="A complete check"}
The text says a project will start in June if the material arrives by May and the room is available. The material has arrived, but there is no information about the room. “Confirmation that the room is available is missing” preserves the unresolved requirement. “The project will certainly start” adds availability; “the project is canceled” turns absent information into a negative outcome.

The check therefore links the answer to two precise parts of the text: the compound requirement and the one requirement already established. This connection explains the choice and identifies the alternatives’ errors without referring to their positions in the list.
:::
