## Quantities and measurements

:::definizione{#grandezza-fisica titolo="Physical quantity"}
The length of a table, the mass of an object, and the duration of a trip are properties that can be measured. A measurable property of an object or phenomenon is called a **physical quantity**.

Measuring a quantity means comparing it with a quantity of the same kind chosen as the **unit of measurement**. A length is measured using a unit of length, such as the meter. If a meter fits twice along one side of a table, that side is $2\,\mathrm{m}$ long.

A measurement is written as a number followed by a unit. In the expression
$$
\ell=1.5\,\mathrm{m},
$$
the letter $\ell$, pronounced “ell,” denotes length, $1.5$ is the numerical value, and $\mathrm{m}$ is the symbol for the meter. The value represents one meter plus half a meter. The letter refers to the same quantity even when its value changes.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="The same length in two units"}
A ribbon that is $2\,\mathrm{m}$ long is also $200\,\mathrm{cm}$ long, since each meter contains $100$ centimeters. The length stays the same while the number used to express it changes. The number $200$ is greater than $2$ because the centimeter is a smaller unit than the meter.

:::

:::osservazione{titolo="Direct and indirect measurements"}
A **direct measurement** compares the quantity with the scale of a suitable instrument. Reading the length of a line segment on a ruler is an example of a direct measurement. An **indirect measurement** uses a formula to obtain the quantity from other measurements.

To find the area of a rectangular tabletop, measure its length and width with a meterstick and multiply the two measurements. The lengths are measured directly, while the area is found indirectly. This method requires the rectangle used in the calculation to describe the shape of the table. The choice of formula therefore depends on the physical situation being observed.
:::

## The International System of Units

:::definizione{#sistema-internazionale titolo="Base units"}
Common units are needed to compare measurements made by different people. The **International System of Units**, abbreviated SI, defines seven base units. The first three are enough to describe many phenomena in mechanics.

| Quantity | Unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length | meter | $\mathrm{m}$ |
| Mass | kilogram | $\mathrm{kg}$ |
| Time | second | $\mathrm{s}$ |
| Thermodynamic temperature | kelvin | $\mathrm{K}$ |
| Electric current | ampere | $\mathrm{A}$ |
| Amount of substance | mole | $\mathrm{mol}$ |
| Luminous intensity | candela | $\mathrm{cd}$ |

The symbol follows the number, with a space between them. For example, $5\,\mathrm{kg}$ represents a mass of five kilograms. Unit symbols stay the same in the plural. The expression $12\,\mathrm{s}$ means twelve seconds.

A quantity obtained by combining other quantities is called a **derived quantity**. The area of a rectangle is the product of two lengths, so its SI unit is the square meter, $\mathrm{m^2}$. The symbol $\mathrm{m^2}$ means a meter multiplied by a meter.
:::

:::osservazione{titolo="The number and what the measurement means"}
Two quantities can have the same numerical value and different meanings. The expression $3\,\mathrm{m}$ describes a length, and $3\,\mathrm{s}$ describes a duration. The units therefore also identify what was measured.

The instrument and the method used limit the precision of a measurement of a real object. The chapter on measurements, formulas, and graphs introduces a way to express this limitation. Conversion factors between units, such as $1\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{cm}$, are exact.
:::

## Multiples, submultiples, and lengths

:::definizione{#prefissi titolo="Unit prefixes"}
A **prefix** placed before the name of a unit indicates a multiple or submultiple of that unit. For example, “kilo” means one thousand, so one kilometer contains one thousand meters. “Milli” means one thousandth, so one thousand millimeters make one meter.

| Prefix | Symbol | Value relative to the unit |
|---|---|---|
| kilo | $\mathrm{k}$ | $1000$ |
| hecto | $\mathrm{h}$ | $100$ |
| deka | $\mathrm{da}$ | $10$ |
| deci | $\mathrm{d}$ | $0.1$ |
| centi | $\mathrm{c}$ | $0.01$ |
| milli | $\mathrm{m}$ | $0.001$ |

The symbol $\mathrm{m}$ on its own stands for the meter. Before another unit symbol, it stands for the prefix milli. For example, $\mathrm{mm}$ means millimeter and $\mathrm{mg}$ means milligram. The meaning depends on the entire symbol.
:::

:::teorema{#conversioni-lunghezze titolo="Converting lengths"}
To express a length in a new unit, replace the original unit with its value in the required unit. Since $1\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{cm}$, a length of $1.8\,\mathrm{m}$ is equal to
$$
\ell=1.8\cdot100\,\mathrm{cm}=180\,\mathrm{cm}.
$$
To convert in the opposite direction, since $100\,\mathrm{cm}=1\,\mathrm{m}$, divide the number of centimeters by $100$. For example,
$$
\ell=\frac{75}{100}\,\mathrm{m}=0.75\,\mathrm{m}.
$$
Replacing the unit shows both which operation to use and which factor to apply. From meters to millimeters, the factor is $1000$ because one meter contains one thousand millimeters.

A conversion can be checked by converting back to the original unit. For example, multiplying $0.75$ meters by $100$ gives $75$ centimeters again. Changing to a smaller unit increases the numerical value, and changing to a larger unit decreases it. In both cases the quantity stays the same.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Adding lengths"}
Two stretches of a route are $1.2\,\mathrm{km}$ and $350\,\mathrm{m}$ long. To add the lengths, express both in meters. This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
1.2\,\mathrm{km}&=1200\,\mathrm{m},\\[6pt]
\ell&=1200\,\mathrm{m}+350\,\mathrm{m}\\[6pt]
&=1550\,\mathrm{m}=1.55\,\mathrm{km}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using a common unit ensures that both terms count amounts of the same quantity using the same reference.
:::

## Time and mass

:::definizione{#tempo titolo="Time interval"}
A **time interval** is the time between two instants. A stopwatch measures, for example, the time between a runner's start and finish. The second is the SI unit of time. The minute and the hour are units used alongside the SI.
$$
1\,\mathrm{min}=60\,\mathrm{s},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{h}=60\,\mathrm{min}=3600\,\mathrm{s}.
$$
The symbol $\mathrm{min}$ stands for the minute and $\mathrm{h}$ stands for the hour. A duration of $2\,\mathrm{min}$ and $15\,\mathrm{s}$ equals $2\cdot60\,\mathrm{s}+15\,\mathrm{s}=135\,\mathrm{s}$. Converting makes it possible to compare durations originally expressed in different ways.
:::

:::attenzione{titolo="Decimal hours and minutes"}
Half an hour is $0.5\,\mathrm{h}=30\,\mathrm{min}$. Therefore, $1.5\,\mathrm{h}$ means one hour and thirty minutes. One hour and fifty minutes, on the other hand, is $1+\dfrac{50}{60}$ hours, or about $1.83\,\mathrm{h}$. Decimal fractions of an hour are converted using $60$ minutes per hour.
:::

:::definizione{#massa titolo="Mass and units of mass"}
**Mass** is a property of a body that is measured in kilograms and appears in the laws of motion. A balance and a standard mass are used to measure mass by comparison. The chapter on dynamics explores the role of mass in motion.

Conversions use the relationships
$$
1\,\mathrm{kg}=1000\,\mathrm{g},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{g}=1000\,\mathrm{mg}.
$$
The symbol for the gram is $\mathrm{g}$, and the symbol for the milligram is $\mathrm{mg}$. An object with a mass of $250\,\mathrm{g}$ therefore has a mass of $0.250\,\mathrm{kg}$. The metric ton, symbol $\mathrm{t}$, equals $1000\,\mathrm{kg}$, so a mass of $1.4\,\mathrm{t}$ equals $1400\,\mathrm{kg}$.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Comparing durations"}
One activity lasts $90\,\mathrm{min}$ and another lasts $1.25\,\mathrm{h}$. Expressing the second duration in minutes gives $1.25\cdot60=75\,\mathrm{min}$. The first activity therefore lasts $15\,\mathrm{min}$ longer. Comparing only the numbers $90$ and $1.25$ without their units would not show how the two durations are related.
:::

## Areas

:::definizione{#area titolo="Area of a rectangle"}
**Area** measures the extent of a surface. A square meter is the area of a square with sides one meter long. For a rectangle with base $b$ and height $h$, multiplying the two lengths expressed in the same unit gives the number of unit squares that cover the surface. Therefore,
$$
A=b\,h.
$$
The letter $A$ denotes area. For a rectangle with base $3\,\mathrm{m}$ and height $2\,\mathrm{m}$, the area is $A=3\cdot2\,\mathrm{m^2}=6\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.8 xmax=3.8 ymin=-0.8 ymax=2.7 poligoni="0,0 3,0 3,2 0,2" linee="1,0 1,2;2,0 2,2;0,1 3,1" etichette="1.5,-0.45,3 m;-0.45,1,2 m" titolo="The rectangle contains six squares, each with an area of $1\,\mathrm{m^2}$."}
:::

::::teorema{#conversioni-aree titolo="Converting areas"}
To convert areas, multiply the length conversion factor by itself. Since $1\,\mathrm{m}=100\,\mathrm{cm}$, it follows that
$$
1\,\mathrm{m^2}=10\,000\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
$$
Consequently, $0.3\,\mathrm{m^2}=3000\,\mathrm{cm^2}$. This rule makes it possible to compare areas expressed in different units.

:::dimostrazione
A square with sides one meter long also has sides $100\,\mathrm{cm}$ long. Calculating its area in square centimeters gives
$$
A=100\,\mathrm{cm}\cdot100\,\mathrm{cm}=10\,000\,\mathrm{cm^2}.
$$
The length conversion factor $100$ therefore appears twice, once for each side.
:::
::::

## Volumes and liters

:::definizione{#volume titolo="Volume"}
**Volume** measures the space an object occupies. A cubic meter is the volume of a cube with edges one meter long. A rectangular prism with length $a$, width $b$, and height $h$ has volume
$$
V=a\,b\,h.
$$
The letter $V$ denotes volume. The product $a\,b$ gives the area of the base, and multiplying it by the height accounts for all the space occupied. A box with internal dimensions $4\,\mathrm{dm}$, $3\,\mathrm{dm}$, and $2\,\mathrm{dm}$ has a capacity of $24\,\mathrm{dm^3}$.
:::

:::teorema{#conversioni-volumi titolo="Converting volumes"}
In volume conversions, the length conversion factor appears three times. Since $1\,\mathrm{m}=10\,\mathrm{dm}$, it follows that
$$
1\,\mathrm{m^3}=10\cdot10\cdot10\,\mathrm{dm^3}=1000\,\mathrm{dm^3}.
$$
Similarly, $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=1000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ and $1\,\mathrm{m^3}=1\,000\,000\,\mathrm{cm^3}$. For example, $250\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ equals $0.250\,\mathrm{dm^3}$ because the conversion requires dividing by $1000$.
:::

:::definizione{#litro titolo="Liter and milliliter"}
The **liter**, symbol $\mathrm{L}$, is the volume of a cube with edges one decimeter long. Therefore,
$$
1\,\mathrm{L}=1\,\mathrm{dm^3},
\qquad
1\,\mathrm{mL}=1\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
$$
A liter contains $1000$ milliliters. A $750\,\mathrm{mL}$ container has an internal volume of $0.750\,\mathrm{L}$, while $2\,\mathrm{m^3}$ corresponds to $2000\,\mathrm{L}$. The liter is a unit of volume used, for example, to express the capacity of a container.

::grafico{assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-0.7 xmax=3.6 ymin=-0.8 ymax=3.5 linee="0,0 2,0 2,2 0,2 0,0;0,2 0.9,2.9 2.9,2.9 2.9,0.9 2,0;2,2 2.9,2.9" segmenti="0,0,0.9,0.9;0.9,0.9,2.9,0.9;0.9,0.9,0.9,2.9" etichette="1,-0.4,1 dm;-0.4,1,1 dm;2.9,0.25,1 dm" titolo="A cube with edges of length $1\,\mathrm{dm}$ has volume $1\,\mathrm{dm^3}=1\,\mathrm{L}$. The drawing shows the cube in perspective."}
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Measuring the volume of an object by immersion"}
A graduated container initially holds $120\,\mathrm{mL}$ of water. When a small, solid object is completely submerged, the reading rises to $155\,\mathrm{mL}$. Assume that the object does not absorb water, dissolve, or trap bubbles, and that no liquid spills out.

The increase in the volume reading corresponds to the space the object occupies, so its volume is
$$
V=(155-120)\,\mathrm{mL}=35\,\mathrm{mL}=35\,\mathrm{cm^3}.
$$
This method can also measure the volume of an irregularly shaped object. The final reading includes both the water and the space occupied by the submerged object, which is why the initial reading is subtracted.
:::

## Mass, volume, and density

:::definizione{#densita titolo="Density of a body"}
To compare the masses of bodies with different volumes, consider the mass per unit of volume. The **average density** of a body is the ratio of its mass $m$ to its volume $V$, where $V>0$.
$$
\rho=\frac{m}{V}.
$$
The Greek letter $\rho$ is read “rho.” In the SI, density is measured in $\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. A density of $800\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ means that one cubic meter of that material has a mass of $800\,\mathrm{kg}$ if the material is homogeneous and the conditions are the same.

A sample with mass $54\,\mathrm{g}$ and volume $20\,\mathrm{cm^3}$ has density $\rho=\dfrac{54}{20}\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}=2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$. Dividing the mass by the volume gives the mass of one cubic centimeter.
:::

:::osservazione{titolo="Volume and mass of a liquid"}
The mass of one liter depends on the density of the liquid. Assuming a density of $1.0\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ for water, one liter of water has a mass of about one kilogram. An oil with a density of $0.80\,\mathrm{kg/L}$ has a mass of $0.80\,\mathrm{kg}$ per liter. The two liquids occupy the same volume but have different masses.

Since $1\,\mathrm{g}=0.001\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=0.000001\,\mathrm{m^3}$, it follows that $1\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}=1000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Therefore, a density of $2.7\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}$ is equivalent to $2700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.
:::

## Writing numbers in scientific notation

:::definizione{#notazione-scientifica titolo="Powers of ten"}
The expression $10^3$ represents the product of three factors equal to ten, so $10^3=1000$. The exponent indicates how many factors there are. By definition, $10^0=1$. Negative exponents give the reciprocals of the corresponding positive powers. For example, $10^{-3}=\dfrac{1}{1000}=0.001$.

A positive number is written in **scientific notation** when it has the form $a\cdot10^n$, where $a$ is at least $1$ and less than $10$, and $n$ is an integer. The factor $a$ is called the coefficient. This notation makes very large and very small values easier to read.
:::

:::esempio{titolo="Large and small numbers"}
A length of $4800\,\mathrm{m}$ is written as $4.8\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{m}$ because $4.8\cdot1000=4800$. A thickness of $0.00025\,\mathrm{m}$ is written as $2.5\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}$ because $2.5$ divided by $10\,000$ gives $0.00025$.

The unit still follows the number. Going from $4800\,\mathrm{m}$ to $4.8\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{m}$ changes only the way the number is written. Going from $4800\,\mathrm{m}$ to $4.8\,\mathrm{km}$ also changes the unit.
:::
