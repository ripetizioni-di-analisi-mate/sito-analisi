## The decimal metric system

:::definizione{#sistema-metrico titolo="The decimal metric system"}
In the **decimal metric system**, each quantity has a reference unit. Other units are obtained by multiplying or dividing it by $10$, $100$, or $1000$. The reference unit for length is the meter (m), for mass the gram (g), and for capacity the liter (l). In the International System of Units the unit of mass is the kilogram, but prefixes are applied to the gram. The names of multiples and submultiples use prefixes that have the same meaning for every quantity.

- kilo (k) represents $1000$, hecto (h) represents $100$, and deca (da) represents $10$.
- deci (d) represents $\dfrac{1}{10}$, centi (c) represents $\dfrac{1}{100}$, and milli (m) represents $\dfrac{1}{1000}$.

Thus $1$ km $= 1000$ m, $1$ hg $= 100$ g, and $1$ ml is one thousandth of a liter. Other units of mass include the metric ton, $1\ \text{t} = 1000\ \text{kg}$, and the metric quintal, $1\ \text{q} = 100\ \text{kg}$. Knowing the prefixes gives the value of even an unfamiliar unit. For example, a decagram is $10$ g.
:::

:::teorema{#conversioni-lunghezze titolo="Converting lengths, masses, and capacities"}
Arranged from largest to smallest, the units of length, mass, or capacity form a scale in which each unit is $10$ times the next. To convert to a smaller unit, multiply by $10$ per step. To convert to a larger unit, divide by $10$ per step. Multiplying by $10$ moves the decimal point one place to the right, so a conversion moves the decimal point as many places as there are steps.

- There are three steps from km to m, so $3.7\ \text{km} = 3700\ \text{m}$.
- There are two steps up from cm to m, so $450\ \text{cm} = 4.5\ \text{m}$.
- There are three steps from kg to g, so $0.25\ \text{kg} = 250\ \text{g}$.

Multiply by $10$ for each step down and divide by $10$ for each step up."}

:::

## Area, volume, and capacity

:::teorema{#conversioni-superfici titolo="Converting units of area"}
A square meter ($\text{m}^2$) is the area of a square with sides $1$ m long. Since $1\ \text{m} = 10\ \text{dm}$, this square contains $10 \cdot 10 = 100$ squares with sides $1$ dm long, so $1\ \text{m}^2 = 100\ \text{dm}^2$. Each step on the scale of area units therefore represents a factor of $100$, and the decimal point moves *two* places per step. For example,
$$
2.5\ \text{m}^2 = 250\ \text{dm}^2 = 25\,000\ \text{cm}^2 .
$$
Land area is often measured in **hectares** (ha). A hectare is the area of a square with sides $100$ m long, or $1\ \text{ha} = 1\ \text{hm}^2 = 10\,000\ \text{m}^2$. A field of $2.5$ hectares therefore has an area of $25\,000\ \text{m}^2$.

::grafico{linee="0,0 10,0 10,10 0,10 0,0;1,0 1,10;2,0 2,10;3,0 3,10;4,0 4,10;5,0 5,10;6,0 6,10;7,0 7,10;8,0 8,10;9,0 9,10;0,1 10,1;0,2 10,2;0,3 10,3;0,4 10,4;0,5 10,5;0,6 10,6;0,7 10,7;0,8 10,8;0,9 10,9" poligoni="0,0 1,0 1,1 0,1" etichette="3.2,10.7,1 m = 10 dm;0.2,-0.9,1 dm" assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-1 xmax=11 ymin=-1.8 ymax=11.8 titolo="A square with sides $1$ m long is divided into $10 \cdot 10 = 100$ squares with sides $1$ dm long, so $1\ \text{m}^2 = 100\ \text{dm}^2$."}

:::

:::teorema{#conversioni-volumi titolo="Converting units of volume"}
A cubic meter ($\text{m}^3$) is the volume of a cube with edges $1$ m long. It contains $10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 1000$ smaller cubes with edges $1$ dm long. Thus $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{dm}^3$. Each step on the scale of volume units represents a factor of $1000$, so the decimal point moves *three* places per step. For example, $0.75\ \text{m}^3 = 750\ \text{dm}^3$ and $4500\ \text{cm}^3 = 4.5\ \text{dm}^3$.
:::

::::teorema{#litri-e-decimetri-cubi titolo="Liters and cubic decimeters"}
The volume of a container and its capacity are related by the equations
$$
1\ \text{l} = 1\ \text{dm}^3,
\qquad
1\ \text{ml} = 1\ \text{cm}^3,
\qquad
1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{l} .
$$
These equations give the number of liters a container holds when its dimensions are known. A rectangular tank with edges of $2$ m, $1.5$ m, and $0.8$ m has a volume of $2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 0.8 = 2.4\ \text{m}^3$, so it holds $2400$ liters. Expressing the dimensions in decimeters from the start simplifies the calculation because the numerical value of the volume in $\text{dm}^3$ is also the capacity in liters.

::grafico{linee="0,0 10,0 10,4 0,4 0,0;0,4 4,7 14,7 10,4;14,7 14,3 10,0" segmenti="0,0,4,3;4,3,14,3;4,3,4,7" etichette="3.5,-1,20 dm;14.3,1.2,15 dm;-3.6,1.8,8 dm;5,2,V = 2400 dm³" assi=0 uguale=1 xmin=-4.5 xmax=15.5 ymin=-2 ymax=8 titolo="A tank with edges of $20$ dm, $15$ dm, and $8$ dm has a volume of $20 \cdot 15 \cdot 8 = 2400\ \text{dm}^3 = 2400$ liters."}

:::dimostrazione
A liter is defined as the capacity of a cube with edges $1$ dm long, so $1\ \text{l} = 1\ \text{dm}^3$. A milliliter is one thousandth of a liter, and a cubic centimeter is one thousandth of a cubic decimeter, so $1\ \text{ml} = 1\ \text{cm}^3$. Finally, $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1000\ \text{dm}^3 = 1000\ \text{l}$.
:::
::::

:::attenzione
For area and volume, the decimal point does *not* move just one place per step. A rug with an area of $3\ \text{m}^2$ measures $30\,000\ \text{cm}^2$ rather than $300\ \text{cm}^2$, because there are two steps from square meters to square centimeters and each represents a factor of $100$. Likewise, $1\ \text{m}^3 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$, because there are two steps from cubic meters to cubic centimeters, each representing a factor of $1000$. In quizzes, incorrect options are often off by a factor of $10$ or $100$ because of this error.
:::

## Units of time

:::definizione{#misure-di-tempo titolo="Units of time"}
Units of time do not follow the decimal system. An hour contains $60$ minutes, and a minute contains $60$ seconds, so
$$
1\ \text{h} = 60\ \text{min} = 3600\ \text{s} .
$$
To express a duration in decimal hours, divide the number of minutes by $60$. To convert the fractional part of an hour back to minutes, multiply it by $60$. For example, $45$ minutes is $\dfrac{45}{60} = 0.75$ h, and $2.4$ h is $2$ hours and $0.4 \cdot 60 = 24$ minutes. Decimal hours are useful in speed formulas, where time is expressed as a single number.
:::

:::esempio{#operazioni-ore-minuti titolo="Adding and subtracting durations"}
To add durations, add the hours and minutes separately, then convert each group of $60$ minutes to an hour.
$$
3\ \text{h}\ 45\ \text{min} + 2\ \text{h}\ 30\ \text{min} = 5\ \text{h}\ 75\ \text{min} = 6\ \text{h}\ 15\ \text{min} .
$$
When subtracting, if there are more minutes to subtract than are available, regroup one hour as $60$ minutes. A train leaves at $8{:}35$ and arrives at $14{:}10$. Since $10$ minutes is less than $35$, write $14\ \text{h}\ 10\ \text{min} = 13\ \text{h}\ 70\ \text{min}$. The trip therefore lasts
$$
13\ \text{h}\ 70\ \text{min} - 8\ \text{h}\ 35\ \text{min} = 5\ \text{h}\ 35\ \text{min} .
$$

::grafico{linee="8.583,0 14.167,0" punti="8.583,0,pieno;14,0,pieno;14.167,0,pieno" segmenti="8.583,0.6,14,0.6;14,0.6,14.167,0.6;8.583,0.3,8.583,0.9;14,0.3,14,0.9;14.167,0.3,14.167,0.9" etichette="8.3,-0.8,8:35;13.6,-0.8,14:00;14.1,-1.5,14:10;10.5,1.2,5 h 25 min;14.2,1.2,10 min" assi=0 xmin=7.8 xmax=14.8 ymin=-2 ymax=2 titolo="The trip from $8{:}35$ to $14{:}10$ consists of $5\ \text{h}\ 25\ \text{min}$ up to $14{:}00$, followed by another $10$ minutes, for a total of $5\ \text{h}\ 35\ \text{min}$."}

:::

:::attenzione{#ore-decimali}
A decimal number of hours must *not* be read as hours and minutes. A duration of $2.30$ h is $2$ hours and $0.30 \cdot 60 = 18$ minutes, while $2$ hours and $30$ minutes is $2.5$ h. Reading $2.30$ h as $2$ hours and $30$ minutes introduces an error of $12$ minutes. Quizzes often include the result of this misreading among the options.
:::

## Average speed

:::definizione{#velocita-media titolo="Average speed"}
The **average speed** of an object that travels a distance $s$ in a time $t$ is the ratio
$$
v = \frac{s}{t} .
$$
The same formula gives the distance, $s = v \cdot t$, and the time, $t = \dfrac{s}{v}$. A car that travels $210$ km in $2$ hours and $30$ minutes, or $2.5$ h, has an average speed of $\dfrac{210}{2.5} = 84$ km/h. If an object moves at constant speed, the distance traveled is directly proportional to time. Proportional quantities are covered in the topic on proportions and percentages.
:::

:::teorema{#conversione-km-h-m-s titolo="Converting between km/h and m/s"}
To convert from km/h to m/s, divide by $3.6$. To convert from m/s to km/h, multiply by $3.6$. Indeed, $1$ km/h corresponds to $1000$ meters in $3600$ seconds, or
$$
\frac{1000}{3600}\ \text{m/s} = \frac{1}{3.6}\ \text{m/s} .
$$
Thus $72$ km/h is $\dfrac{72}{3.6} = 20$ m/s, and $15$ m/s is $15 \cdot 3.6 = 54$ km/h. The numerical value of a positive speed in m/s is always smaller than its value in km/h. This gives a way to check the direction of the conversion.
:::

::::teorema{#velocita-media-andata-ritorno titolo="Average speed on a round trip"}
If the outward journey is made at an average speed of $v_1$ and the return journey at an average speed of $v_2$, the average speed for the entire trip is
$$
v = \frac{2\, v_1\, v_2}{v_1 + v_2} .
$$
A car that travels from one city to another at $60$ km/h and returns at $40$ km/h has an average speed of $\dfrac{2 \cdot 60 \cdot 40}{100} = 48$ km/h rather than $50$ km/h. The result is less than the arithmetic mean of the two speeds because the car spends more time traveling at the slower speed on the return trip.

:::dimostrazione
Let $d$ be the one-way distance. The total distance is $2d$ and the total time is $\dfrac{d}{v_1} + \dfrac{d}{v_2} = \dfrac{d\,(v_1 + v_2)}{v_1\, v_2}$. Dividing distance by time gives the formula, in which $d$ cancels.
:::
::::

## Motion problems involving meeting or catching up

:::teorema{#incontro titolo="Meeting when traveling in opposite directions"}
Two objects start at the same time from points a distance $d$ apart and move toward each other at constant speeds $v_1$ and $v_2$. Each hour the distance between them decreases by $v_1 + v_2$, so they meet after a time
$$
t = \frac{d}{v_1 + v_2} .
$$
Two trains leave at $9{:}00$ from stations $300$ km apart, one traveling at $70$ km/h and the other at $80$ km/h. The distance between them decreases by $150$ km each hour, so they meet after $2$ hours, at $11{:}00$. The first train has traveled $70 \cdot 2 = 140$ km and the second $160$ km. The sum of the two distances is $300$ km, which confirms the result.

::grafico{f="70*x+0*sqrt(x)+0*sqrt(4.28-x);300-80*x+0*sqrt(x)+0*sqrt(3.75-x)" punti="2,140,pieno" segmenti="2,0,2,140" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.5 ymin=-40 ymax=360 nomi="t,s" legenda="s = 70\,t;s = 300 - 80\,t" titolo="Distance $s$ in km from the first station as a function of time $t$ in hours. The lines intersect at $t = 2$, a distance of $140$ km from the first station."}

:::

:::teorema{#inseguimento titolo="Catching up"}
When a second object starts moving, a first object is already $d$ kilometers ahead on the same road, traveling in the same direction. If their constant speeds are $v_1$ for the first object and $v_2 > v_1$ for the second, the gap decreases by $v_2 - v_1$ each hour. The second object therefore catches up after a time
$$
t = \frac{d}{v_2 - v_1} .
$$
A truck leaves at $8{:}00$ traveling at $60$ km/h. At $9{:}00$ a car leaves from the same point at $80$ km/h. By $9{:}00$ the truck has traveled $60$ km, which is its head start. The car gains $20$ km each hour, so it catches the truck after $\dfrac{60}{20} = 3$ hours, at $12{:}00$, a distance of $240$ km from the starting point.

::grafico{f="60*x+0*sqrt(x);80*(x-1)+0*sqrt(x-1)" punti="4,240,pieno" segmenti="4,0,4,240" xmin=-0.5 xmax=5.5 ymin=-40 ymax=360 nomi="t,s" legenda="s = 60\,t;s = 80\,(t - 1)" titolo="Distance $s$ in km as a function of the hours $t$ elapsed since $8{:}00$. The car leaves one hour later and catches the truck at $t = 4$, that is, at $12{:}00$."}

:::

:::osservazione{#tempo-da-un-istante titolo="Clock times and travel times"}
The formulas for meeting and catching up give a duration measured from the instant when *both* objects are moving. To find the clock time, add this duration to the later departure time. In the truck example, the duration is $3$ hours starting at $9{:}00$, so the meeting time is $12{:}00$. Adding $3$ hours to $8{:}00$ would give the wrong time.
:::

## Work-rate and tank-filling problems

:::definizione{#rendimento titolo="Hourly work rate"}
If a person or machine working at a constant rate completes a job in $T$ hours, the fraction completed in one hour is $\dfrac{1}{T}$. This fraction is the **hourly work rate**. A worker who paints a room in $4$ hours paints $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the room each hour. A tap that fills a tank in $12$ hours fills $\dfrac{1}{12}$ of the tank each hour. Rates are useful because the rates of people working together can be added, whereas their times cannot.
:::

:::teorema{#lavori-insieme titolo="Time to complete a job together"}
If two people or machines can each complete the same job in $T_1$ and $T_2$ hours, respectively, then working together at their own constant rates they complete it in the time $T$ satisfying
$$
\frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{T_1} + \frac{1}{T_2},
\qquad\text{that is}\qquad
T = \frac{T_1\, T_2}{T_1 + T_2} .
$$
In one hour they complete the sum of the fractions each would complete alone. Two taps can fill a $600$-liter tank in $4$ hours and $12$ hours, respectively. Together they fill $\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ of it per hour, so they fill it in $3$ hours. The time required when both work together is always shorter than the time for the faster one alone, which rules out many answer choices.

::grafico{f="150*x+0*sqrt(x)+0*sqrt(4-x);200*x+0*sqrt(x)+0*sqrt(3-x)" punti="3,600,pieno;4,600,pieno" segmenti="0,600,4,600;3,0,3,600;4,0,4,600" xmin=-0.5 xmax=4.8 ymin=-75 ymax=675 nomi="t,V" legenda="V = 150\,t;V = 200\,t" titolo="Volume $V$ in liters in the tank as a function of time $t$ in hours. The first tap alone delivers $150$ liters per hour and fills the tank in $4$ hours. With the second tap delivering $50$ liters per hour, the tank fills in $3$ hours."}

:::

:::teorema{#rubinetto-e-scarico titolo="Opening a tap and a drain together"}
If a tap fills a tank in $T_1$ hours and a drain empties it in $T_2$ hours, each at a constant rate, with $T_2 > T_1$, opening them together fills the empty tank in the time $T$ satisfying
$$
\frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{T_1} - \frac{1}{T_2} .
$$
Subtract the drain’s rate because it removes a fraction $\dfrac{1}{T_2}$ of the tank’s capacity every hour. With $T_1 = 4$ hours and $T_2 = 12$ hours, the fraction of the tank filled each hour is $\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{1}{6}$, so the tank is full after $6$ hours.

::grafico{f="150*x+0*sqrt(x)+0*sqrt(4-x);100*x+0*sqrt(x)+0*sqrt(6-x)" punti="4,600,pieno;6,600,pieno" segmenti="0,600,6,600;4,0,4,600;6,0,6,600" xmin=-0.5 xmax=6.8 ymin=-75 ymax=675 nomi="t,V" legenda="V = 150\,t;V = 100\,t" titolo="Volume $V$ in liters in the tank as a function of time $t$ in hours. The tap alone delivers $150$ liters per hour and fills the tank in $4$ hours. With the drain removing $50$ liters per hour, the net inflow is $100$ liters per hour and the tank takes $6$ hours to fill."}

:::

:::attenzione
The times needed to do a job must *not* be added and must *not* be averaged. For two taps that fill a tank in $4$ and $12$ hours, both the sum of $16$ hours and the average of $8$ hours exceed the time for the faster tap alone. Opening a second tap, however, fills the tank sooner. The correct time, $3$ hours, is obtained by adding the rates.
:::

## Mixtures and concentrations

:::teorema{#prezzo-miscela titolo="The price of a mixture"}
If you mix $q_1$ kilograms of a product with unit price $p_1$ with $q_2$ kilograms of a product with unit price $p_2$, the price per kilogram of the mixture is the **weighted average** of the prices, with the quantities as weights.
$$
p = \frac{p_1\, q_1 + p_2\, q_2}{q_1 + q_2} .
$$
The numerator is the total cost and the denominator is the total quantity. Mixing $6$ kg of coffee priced at $10$ euros per kilogram with $4$ kg priced at $15$ euros per kilogram costs $60 + 60 = 120$ euros for $10$ kg, so the blend costs $12$ euros per kilogram. The mixture price lies between the two prices and is closer to the price of the ingredient present in the larger amount.
:::

:::osservazione{#regola-della-miscela titolo="Quantities to mix for a given price"}
Given the prices $p_1 < p_2$ and the mixture price $p$, the quantities to mix are inversely proportional to how far their prices are from $p$.
$$
q_1 : q_2 = (p_2 - p) : (p - p_1) .
$$
To obtain this relation, multiply the mixture price formula by $q_1 + q_2$ and collect terms. To make $20$ kg of a mixture priced at $4$ euros per kilogram from two grades priced at $3$ and $7$ euros per kilogram, the price differences are $7 - 4 = 3$ and $4 - 3 = 1$. The ratio of the less expensive grade to the more expensive grade is therefore $3 : 1$. Dividing $20$ kg into $4$ parts of $5$ kg gives $15$ kg at $3$ euros per kilogram and $5$ kg at $7$ euros per kilogram. Indeed, $\dfrac{45 + 35}{20} = 4$.

:::

:::definizione{#concentrazione titolo="Percent concentration"}
In a solution, such as salt in water or alcohol in water, the **concentration** is the ratio of the quantity of dissolved substance to the total quantity of solution, usually expressed as a percentage.
$$
c = \frac{\text{quantity of solute}}{\text{quantity of solution}} \cdot 100 .
$$
A $600$ g solution containing $30$ g of salt has a concentration of $\dfrac{30}{600} \cdot 100\% = 5\%$. When two solutions are mixed, the concentration of the mixture is the weighted average of their concentrations, just as for prices. For percentages by mass, use the masses of the solutions as weights. For percentages by volume, use their volumes, assuming the solution volumes add.
:::

:::teorema{#diluizione titolo="Dilution"}
When water is added to a solution, the quantity of dissolved substance stays the same while the quantity of solution increases and the concentration decreases. Thus, if $V_1$ and $c_1$ are the values before dilution and $V_2$ and $c_2$ the values after it,
$$
c_1 \cdot V_1 = c_2 \cdot V_2 .
$$
Five liters of a $12\%$ solution contain $0.12 \cdot 5 = 0.6$ liters of solute. To bring the concentration to $8\%$, the solution must have a volume of $\dfrac{0.6}{0.08} = 7.5$ liters, so $2.5$ liters of water must be added. Concentration and volume are inversely proportional, and the water *added* is the difference between the two volumes.

Before and after dilution, the product stays at $c \cdot V = 60$, corresponding to $0.6$ liters of solute."}

:::

## Density and currency exchange

:::definizione{#densita titolo="Density"}
The **density** of a material is the ratio of the mass $m$ of an object made from it to the object’s volume $V$.
$$
d = \frac{m}{V} .
$$
Density is measured in $\text{g/cm}^3$, $\text{kg/dm}^3$, or $\text{kg/m}^3$. The first two give the same numerical value because $1\ \text{kg} = 1000\ \text{g}$ and $1\ \text{dm}^3 = 1000\ \text{cm}^3$. Water has a density of $1\ \text{kg/dm}^3$, so one liter of water has a mass of $1$ kg. The same formula gives the mass, $m = d \cdot V$, and the volume, $V = \dfrac{m}{d}$. A block of aluminum with a volume of $100\ \text{cm}^3$ and a mass of $270$ g has a density of $2.7\ \text{g/cm}^3$. A liter of oil with a density of $0.92\ \text{kg/dm}^3$ has a mass of $0.92$ kg.
:::

:::definizione{#cambio-valuta titolo="Currency conversion"}
An **exchange rate** gives the number of units of one currency corresponding to one unit of another. For example, $1$ euro $= 1.08$ dollars. The amounts in the two currencies are directly proportional, so

- To convert euros to dollars, multiply by $1.08$, so $250$ euros is equivalent to $250 \cdot 1.08 = 270$ dollars.
- To convert dollars to euros, divide by $1.08$, so $54$ dollars is equivalent to $\dfrac{54}{1.08} = 50$ euros.

A percentage fee is deducted from the amount exchanged in the same way as a discount. Percentages are covered in the topic on proportions and percentages.
:::

:::osservazione{#costo-per-unita titolo="Chains of ratios"}
Many questions connect several quantities through known ratios, such as fuel consumption in liters per $100$ km and price in euros per liter. Convert from one quantity to the next one ratio at a time, checking the units. For a car that uses $5$ liters per $100$ km, a trip of $300$ km requires $\dfrac{300}{100} \cdot 5 = 15$ liters. At a fuel price of $1.80$ euros per liter, the cost is $15 \cdot 1.80 = 27$ euros.
:::

## Quick strategies for quizzes

:::osservazione{#metodi-rapidi titolo="Checking units and orders of magnitude"}
In questions about quantities and units, incorrect options nearly always come from a few recurring mistakes. Several checks help identify them.

- **Count the steps** in a conversion, remembering that each step represents a factor of $100$ for area and $1000$ for volume. A factor of $10$ between two choices can indicate a one-step error in a conversion of length, mass, or capacity.
- **Check the direction**. For a positive quantity, a smaller unit gives a larger numerical value. A positive speed has a smaller numerical value in m/s than in km/h. A job completed by two people together takes less time than it takes the faster person alone.
- **Write minutes as fractions of an hour**. For example, $15$ min $= \dfrac{1}{4}$ h, $20$ min $= \dfrac{1}{3}$ h, $40$ min $= \dfrac{2}{3}$ h, and $45$ min $= \dfrac{3}{4}$ h.
- **Estimate the average**. The price or concentration of a mixture lies between the two original values and is closer to the value for the larger quantity. For a round trip over equal distances, the average speed is less than the arithmetic mean when the two speeds differ and equals it when they are the same.

These checks often leave just one or two possible answers. The exact calculation then serves only to choose between them.
